# Audit: all-depth frame toric fibre, quartet lattice, and Markov obstruction

Date: 2026-07-26

Source audited:
`MATH_THEOREM_ALL_DEPTH_FRAME_TORIC_FIBRE_AND_QUARTET_MARKOV_OBSTRUCTION_20260726.md`.

Method: pure mathematics only. No finite search, computation, solver, or
web input is used.

## 0. Verdict

The four requested claims are correct:

1. in one fixed ordered-core/two-slot chart, every phasewise interval-load
   vector factors through the tournament score sequence;
2. directed triangle reversals connect every tournament fibre with fixed
   scores;
3. the lattice generated by directed four-cycle reversals has index two
   in the integral circulation lattice; and
4. the dominance-separated double triangle is parity-neutral, lies in
   that quartet lattice, and has two quartet-isolated endpoints.

Thus physical quartets are not a Markov basis for the all-depth frame
configuration.  The first obstruction in the fixed-core chart is not a
new rational defect or higher torsion: it is failure of a lattice
decomposition to remain in the nonnegative tournament fibre.

There is no contradiction with
`MATH_THEOREM_QUARTET_RECTANGLE_LATTICE_SATURATION_AND_TEMPLATE_GATE_20260726.md`.
That theorem concerns the **untwisted top-count lattice**, whose columns
have unsigned point-incidence margins.  The fixed-core tournament theorem
concerns **orientation changes**, whose margins are signed scores.  On
one four-cycle the two descriptions can be made identical by orienting
its bipartition, but there is no single such orientation on the complete
outside-label graph: its triangles are exactly the residual
\(\mathbb Z/2\) class.

Two scope qualifications in the source are essential and correct.

- A universal Markov basis must connect every fibre, so the fifteen-top
  fibre is a decisive counterexample even though it is not the particular
  full top-margin-one fibre sought for coefficient one.
- The double triangle is a bounded local obstruction.  Since its binomial
  lies in the saturation of the quartet ideal, bounded extra
  multiplicity can act as a catalyst.  It is not by itself an
  \(\Omega(W)\) exceptional-incidence obstruction.

## 1. Exact fixed-core configuration

Fix an \((M-2)\)-set \(C\), two positional slots \(A,B\), one common
rooted positional word on \(C\cup\{A,B\}\), and a set \(Z\) of outside
labels.  For each pair \(\{u,v\}\subset Z\), the top is

\[
U_{uv}=C\cup\{u,v\},
\]

and its two chart columns are denoted \(u\to v\) and \(v\to u\),
according to which label occupies slot \(A\).  With top margin one on
each pair top, an integral table is exactly a tournament \(T\) on \(Z\).

### Proposition 1.1 (score factorization)

For every common phase, interval length, retained-phase set, and nested
phase-to-depth schedule, the aggregate physical target load of the chart
depends only on the score vector

\[
d_T(u)=|\{v:u\to v\text{ in }T\}|.
\]

If at least one retained phase interval contains slot \(A\) but not
slot \(B\), the phase-refined load vector recovers every \(d_T(u)\).

#### Audit proof

Fix a positional interval and let \(K\subseteq C\) be its ordinary-label
part.

- If it contains neither special slot, its image is \(K\), independent
  of every pair orientation.
- If it contains both, the top \(\{u,v\}\) contributes
  \(K\cup\{u,v\}\), again independently of orientation.
- If it contains only \(A\), the multiplicity of
  \(K\cup\{u\}\) is exactly \(d_T(u)\).
- If it contains only \(B\), that multiplicity is
  \(|Z|-1-d_T(u)\).

This proves the factorization separately at every phase and length, so
common deletions and common tag weights preserve it.  Promotion
root-form targets cause no problem: a root-form target is the global
complement of the corresponding direct interval, after one common phase
shift, and global complementation is a fixed permutation of target rows.
No root term was omitted.

## 2. Triangle connectivity

Let \(T,T'\) have the same scores.  Direct every edge on which they
differ as it is directed in \(T\).  Equal scores say that the resulting
difference digraph has equal indegree and outdegree at every vertex.  It
therefore decomposes into directed cycles.

### Proposition 2.1 (applicable triangle decomposition)

Every directed cycle reversal in a tournament is a sequence of
applicable directed triangle reversals.  Consequently triangle reversals
connect every fixed-score tournament fibre.

#### Audit proof

For a directed cycle

\[
v_1\to v_2\to\cdots\to v_r\to v_1,
\]

inspect the chord \(v_1v_3\).  If \(v_3\to v_1\), first reverse the
directed triangle \(v_1v_2v_3v_1\); the remaining directed cycle has
length \(r-1\).  If \(v_1\to v_3\), reverse that length-\((r-1)\)
cycle first; this creates the required directed triangle, which is then
reversed.  In both cases the chord is reversed twice and every original
cycle edge once.  Induction proves the assertion.  Processing the
Eulerian cycle decomposition one cycle at a time connects \(T\) to
\(T'\).  All intermediate moves are literal three-top common-frame
exchanges.

The directed-triangle fibre on three outside labels has exactly two
states, so the cubic triangle move is indispensable in this chart.

## 3. Exact quartet quotient in a fixed chart

Choose arbitrary reference orientations for the edges of \(K_Z\), let

\[
\partial:\mathbb Z^{E(K_Z)}\to\mathbb Z^Z
\]

be signed incidence, and put

\[
\mathcal L_{\rm cyc}=\ker_{\mathbb Z}\partial.
\]

The difference of two equal-score tournaments lies in
\(\mathcal L_{\rm cyc}\).  A physical quartet is exactly reversal of a
directed four-cycle; let \(\mathcal L_4\) be the lattice generated by
all signed simple four-cycle vectors.

### Theorem 3.1 (index two, no higher chart torsion)

For \(|Z|\ge4\),

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal L_4
=\left\{z\in\mathcal L_{\rm cyc}:
\sum_{e\in E(K_Z)}z_e\equiv0\pmod2\right\},
\qquad
\mathcal L_{\rm cyc}/\mathcal L_4\cong\mathbb Z/2.}
\]

#### Audit proof

Fix a vertex \(0\).  The star-triangle vectors

\[
\tau_{ij}=e_{0i}+e_{ij}-e_{0j}
\]

generate the circulation lattice: subtract them to clear every nonstar
edge, after which zero divergence clears the star.  Four-cycle vectors
on \(\{0,i,j,k\}\) identify all \(\tau_{ij}\) modulo
\(\mathcal L_4\) and give \(2\tau_{ij}\in\mathcal L_4\).  Hence the
quotient has order at most two.  Edge-coefficient parity annihilates
every four-cycle but is one on a triangle, so the triangle class is
nonzero and the quotient is exactly \(\mathbb Z/2\).  This proves both
the index and the absence of odd or higher \(2\)-power torsion in this
chart.

## 4. Double-triangle endpoint audit

Partition six outside labels into

\[
A_0=\{a_0,a_1,a_2\},\qquad B_0=\{b_0,b_1,b_2\}.
\]

Orient each triple as a directed triangle and every cross edge from
\(A_0\) to \(B_0\).  Let \(T'\) reverse both internal triangles and
leave all cross edges fixed.

### Theorem 4.1 (parity-neutral isolated fibre pair)

The two associated frame tables have identical top rows and identical
all-depth physical target loads, their exponent difference belongs to
\(\mathcal L_4\), every affine top-parity syndrome agrees, and neither
endpoint admits a quartet.

#### Audit proof

Every \(a_i\) has score four and every \(b_j\) score one.  Reversing a
directed triangle preserves one internal win at each vertex, so the
score vectors and hence all loads agree by Proposition 1.1.

The difference is the sum of two triangle circulation vectors.  It has
even edge parity and therefore belongs to \(\mathcal L_4\) by Theorem
3.1.  For an affine mod-two top character

\[
w(U)=\alpha_0+\sum_{x\in U}\alpha_x,
\]

the six changed pair tops contribute every core label six times and
every outside label twice; the total is zero modulo two.

Finally, every cross edge points from \(A_0\) to \(B_0\).  A directed
walk that enters \(B_0\) cannot return to \(A_0\), so every directed
cycle lies inside one three-vertex block.  There is no directed
four-cycle at either endpoint.  Since chart quartets are precisely
directed four-cycle reversals, both monomials are isolated.

No hidden quartet with another core is available.  The fibre has top
margin one only on the fifteen tops \(C\cup\{u,v\}\) and zero outside.
Any four occupied pair tops forming a physical top rectangle have common
intersection exactly \(C\) and their outside pairs form a four-cycle in
\(K_Z\).  Thus every applicable physical quartet would already be one
of the directed four-cycles just excluded.

## 5. Reconciliation with top-index rectangle saturation

The saturation theorem uses the lattice

\[
\mathcal R
=\left\langle
e_{C+a_0+b_0}-e_{C+a_0+b_1}
-e_{C+a_1+b_0}+e_{C+a_1+b_1}
\right\rangle
\]

in the free group on **unoriented tops**, and proves

\[
\mathcal R=\ker_{\mathbb Z}A,
\qquad
(Az)_x=\sum_{U\ni x}z_U.
\]

This is an unsigned point-margin statement.  In the fixed-core chart it
becomes the kernel of the unsigned vertex-edge incidence matrix of
\(K_Z\).  By contrast, tournament score preservation is the kernel of
the signed incidence matrix \(\partial\).

For one four-cycle, orienting all four edges from one bipartition class
to the other turns its signed circulation vector into the alternating
checkerboard vector.  Such a choice cannot be made simultaneously on
all of \(K_Z\), because \(K_Z\) contains triangles.  Equivalently,
the unsigned and signed incidence matrices are diagonally equivalent
exactly on bipartite support graphs.  Their difference on an odd cycle
is the triangle parity found in Theorem 3.1.

The ranks make the distinction visible.  For \(|Z|=r\),

\[
\operatorname{rank}\ker(\text{unsigned incidence of }K_r)
=\binom r2-r,
\]

whereas

\[
\operatorname{rank}\ker(\text{signed incidence of }K_r)
=\binom r2-r+1.
\]

The first lattice is saturated by the top-index theorem.  Four-cycle
circulations have full rank in the second but index two.  Therefore
“top rectangles are saturated” and “quartet circulations have index
two” are simultaneously exact.

There is a third, orientation-twisted global projection: normalized
rooted permutation parity.  A physical quartet maps there to

\[
e_C\wedge(e_{a_0}-e_{a_1})\wedge(e_{b_0}-e_{b_1}).
\]

For the unrestricted library its Smith cokernel is
\((\mathbb Z/2)^n\), as proved in
MATH_THEOREM_L_QUARTET_INTEGER_QUOTIENT_DENSE_MARKOV_NOGO_20260726.md.
This again has the same affine mod-two characters, but it is neither the
free untwisted quotient nor the one-chart score quotient.  Keeping these
three coefficient conventions separate removes the apparent conflict.

## 6. Markov and constant-one boundary

The audited source proves a universal Markov-basis no-go, not a
coefficient-one obstruction.

- Lattice membership of the double-triangle difference says that some
  signed sum of quartets realizes it after negative intermediate
  multiplicities are allowed.
- Endpoint isolation says no such decomposition is conformal to the
  two nonnegative monomials.
- Multiplying by a sufficiently large common catalyst monomial clears
  all negative intermediate exponents, so the obstruction is bounded
  and asymptotically cheap in one fixed chart.

Accordingly quartets alone cannot justify a general integral
common-permutation rounding theorem.  A positive constant-one route must
either add triangle/higher placeholder moves, or prove that a bounded
exceptional catalyst can be transported through the particular full
top-margin-one fibre with an \(o(W)\) literal incidence ledger.  Neither
conclusion follows from top-index saturation.
