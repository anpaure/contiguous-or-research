## 6. Why the reversed serial order was invalid

The previous tentative construction processed \(D_3\) first and \(D_4\)
second, then treated the \(D_4\) word as if it were a contiguous
parent-boundary block. The \(X/Y\) exactness of that serial product was
correct, but the collar conclusion was not.

For a seven-step path obtained by processing \(D_3\) and then \(D_4\), the
global oriented exchange word has the form

\[
 a^{(3)}_1a^{(3)}_2a^{(3)}_3\ |
 a^{(4)}_1\cdots a^{(4)}_4\ |
 b^{(3)}_1b^{(3)}_2b^{(3)}_3\ |
 b^{(4)}_1\cdots b^{(4)}_4\ |
 \infty.                                                \tag{6.1}
\]

The marked \(D_4\) insertion \(b^{(4)}_1\) has \(b^{(4)}_2\) immediately
on one side. On the other side it has only the three \(D_3\) insertion
slots before the next \(D_4\) slot is met. Hence a cyclic interval whose
\(D_4\)-intersection is exactly \(\{b^{(4)}_1\}\) has length at most
four. It cannot be the protected parent window of length at least nine
used by the hereditary \(D_4\) collar theorem.

Thus no projection of the form “arbitrary exterior \(K\) plus the marked
\(b_1\) singleton” follows in that order. Reversing the slabs as in
Section 3 moves the \(D_4\) action to the genuine global first-insertion
boundary, where Theorem 4.1 is literal.
