## 0. Outcome

Put

\[
 n=2m,\qquad M=m+H,
\]

and consider literal words on the rank-\(M\) tops.  The exact array
shadow of the collar-neutral three-top packet is an alternating
six-cycle in the incidence graph between rank-\((M-1)\) seams and
rank-\(M\) tops.  The two-seam projection of the Johnson-square
four-top re-root is an alternating eight-cycle; the complete physical
shift has additional column action audited below.  The following
statements are exact.

1. For a connected set of active rooted positions, the integer lattice
   generated by all three-top six-cycle trades is

   \[
   \boxed{
   \begin{aligned}
   {\cal L}_{\rm hex}=\{z:\;&
      \sum_j z_{j,B,U}=0 &&(B\subset U),\\
    & \sum_{B\subset U}z_{j,B,U}=0 &&(j,U),\\
    & \sum_{U\supset B}z_{j,B,U}=0 &&(j,B)\}.
   \end{aligned}}
   \tag{0.1}
   \]

   Thus the six-cycle lattice is saturated.

2. The usual position--label histogram equations are consequences of
   the last two rows of (0.1).  However, the seam--position counts

   \[
      D_{j,B}=\#\{U:\text{the letter in position }j
                    \text{ on }U\text{ is }U\setminus B\}
   \tag{0.2}
   \]

   are extra invariants not determined by the position--label histogram.
   Hence the physical six-cycle lattice, although saturated, is a proper
   sublattice of the lattice obtained by fixing only position--label
   counts.

3. Every four-top Johnson-square Latin shadow belongs already to
   \({\cal L}_{\rm hex}\): its octagon is the sum of two literal
   hexagon vectors.  Therefore adjoining those four-top shadows does
   not enlarge the integer lattice and does not remove (0.2).

4. There are two literal word tables with the same top rows and the same
   position--label histogram but different seam histograms.  A two-top
   intercalate gives the smallest example.  No composition of three-top
   packets and four-top Latin shadows joins them.

5. Even after (0.2) is fixed, hexagons and Johnson-square octagons are
   not a Markov basis.  For every \(r\ge5\) in the available ground-set
   range there is an induced \(2r\)-cycle whose two alternating Latin
   states form a two-point fibre.  Neither endpoint admits a conformal
   six- or eight-cycle move, although their difference lies in the
   saturated lattice (0.1).  The failure is positivity/Markov failure,
   not a further lattice character.

6. The complete physical four-top move

   \[
                  (p_0,p_1,p_2,p_3)
          \longmapsto(Sp_0,Sp_1,Sp_2,Sp_3)
   \tag{0.3}
   \]

   is not itself a move in a fixed position--label histogram fibre.
   In fact no simultaneous one-step shift of four nonempty length-\(M\)
   words can preserve that histogram when \(M>4\).  Its octagon is only
   the seam shadow of (0.3), not the full array derivative.

Consequently the local re-root theorems do not yet give a fixed-fibre
Markov routing theorem.  The precise surviving primitive would be a
column-balanced composition of physical four-top shifts whose net
position--label boundary is zero but whose net seam histogram is
nonzero.  No such literal composition is supplied by the current local
theorems.

## 1. Literal words as an edge-colouring array

Let

\[
 {\cal B}=\binom{[n]}{M-1},\qquad
 {\cal U}=\binom{[n]}M,
\]

and let \(G=G_{n,M-1}\) be the bipartite inclusion graph with shores
\({\cal B},{\cal U}\) and edge set

\[
 {\cal E}=\{(B,U):B\subset U\}.
\tag{1.1}
\]

The edge \((B,U)\) has the unique ground label

\[
                         \lambda(B,U)=U\setminus B.
\tag{1.2}
\]

A word \(p_U=(p_U(1),\ldots,p_U(M))\) on \(U\) assigns position
\(j\) to the incidence edge

\[
                         (U-p_U(j),U).
\tag{1.3}
\]

Thus a table of one word per top is a colouring of every edge of \(G\)
by one rooted position, with every colour appearing exactly once at each
top.  Write

\[
 x_{j,B,U}={\bf1}_{\{p_U(j)=U\setminus B\}}.
\tag{1.4}
\]

The literal word equations are

\[
 \sum_jx_{j,B,U}=1\quad(B\subset U),\qquad
 \sum_{B\subset U}x_{j,B,U}=1\quad(j,U).
\tag{1.5}
\]

The two relevant histograms are

\[
 C_{j,v}=\sum_{\substack{B\subset U\\U\setminus B=\{v\}}}
                    x_{j,B,U},
 \qquad
 D_{j,B}=\sum_{U\supset B}x_{j,B,U}.
\tag{1.6}
\]

The first is the column histogram found in the independent audit of the
three-top conveyor.  The second remembers the rank-\((M-1)\) seam left
after deleting the letter occupying that position.

For an integer difference \(z\), define

\[
 T_{j,U}(z)=\sum_{B\subset U}z_{j,B,U},\quad
 S_{j,B}(z)=\sum_{U\supset B}z_{j,B,U},\quad
 L_{j,v}(z)=\sum_{\lambda(B,U)=v}z_{j,B,U}.
\tag{1.7}
\]

There is an exact dependence among these rows:

\[
 \boxed{
 L_{j,v}(z)=
 \sum_{U\ni v}T_{j,U}(z)-
 \sum_{B\ni v}S_{j,B}(z).}
\tag{1.8}
\]

Indeed an edge \((B,U)\) has coefficient
\({\bf1}_{v\in U}-{\bf1}_{v\in B}\), which is one precisely when
\(U\setminus B=\{v\}\).  Hence top balance and seam balance imply
position--label balance.  The converse is false.

## 2. The three-top packet is a hexagon

Fix an \((M-2)\)-set \(C\) and distinct labels
\(a,b,c\notin C\).  Put

\[
 B_a=C+a,\quad B_b=C+b,\quad B_c=C+c,
\]

\[
 U_{ab}=C+a+b,\quad U_{bc}=C+b+c,\quad U_{ca}=C+c+a.
\tag{2.1}
\]

These vertices form the inclusion hexagon

\[
 B_a-U_{ab}-B_b-U_{bc}-B_c-U_{ca}-B_a.
\tag{2.2}
\]

Orient it and let

\[
 \gamma(C;a,b,c)=
 (e_{B_b,U_{ab}}-e_{B_a,U_{ab}})
 +(e_{B_c,U_{bc}}-e_{B_b,U_{bc}})
 +(e_{B_a,U_{ca}}-e_{B_c,U_{ca}}).
\tag{2.3}
\]

For two active positions \(s,t\), the Latin trade is

\[
                   (e_s-e_t)\otimes\gamma(C;a,b,c).
\tag{2.4}
\]

At position \(s\) the three labels are cyclically routed, and at
position \(t\) the opposite routing occurs.  This is exactly the array
form of the audited three-top packet: each of the three tops swaps its
two placeholder letters, every top remains a permutation, and the label
multiset at each active position is unchanged.  Formula (2.3) also
shows coefficientwise that every seam count is unchanged.

The physical theorem certifies a star of pairs \((s,t)\): the near
placeholder is rooted at \(s=3\), and \(t\) may be any certified remote
position.  The lattice statement below only needs the graph of allowed
position pairs to be connected.

## 3. Hexagons generate the complete inclusion-graph cycle lattice

Orient every edge of \(G\) from \({\cal B}\) to \({\cal U}\), and let
\(\partial\) be its signed incidence map.

### Lemma 3.1 (integral hexagon generation)

For \(1\le M-1\le n-1\),

\[
 \boxed{
 \ker_{\mathbb Z}\partial
 =\left\langle\gamma(C;a,b,c)\right\rangle_{\mathbb Z}.}
\tag{3.1}
\]

#### Proof

Every integral circulation of a graph is an integral sum of oriented
simple cycles.  A cycle of \(G\) has the form

\[
 B_0,U_0,B_1,U_1,\ldots,B_{r-1},U_{r-1},B_0,
\tag{3.2}
\]

where \(B_i,B_{i+1}\) are adjacent in the Johnson graph
\(J(n,M-1)\) and \(U_i=B_i\cup B_{i+1}\).  It is therefore the lift
of an integral Johnson-graph cycle.

The Johnson graph is the one-skeleton of the hypersimplex

\[
 \Delta(n,M-1)=\{x\in[0,1]^n:\sum_i x_i=M-1\}.
\]

The hypersimplex is convex, so its cellular first homology is zero.
Its two-faces are triangles or squares: a triangle is one exchange
among three coordinates, while a square is two commuting exchanges.
Consequently every integral Johnson cycle is an integral sum of
oriented triangle and square boundaries.  This conclusion is integral,
because it is the equality
\(\ker\partial_1=\operatorname{im}\partial_2\) in the cellular chain
complex of a contractible polytope.

There are two kinds of Johnson triangle.  If its three \((M-1)\)-sets
are facets of one common \(M\)-set, its lift is zero: all three Johnson
edges use the same top, and the three incidence-edge differences
cancel.  If its three vertices have a common \((M-2)\)-core, its lift
is exactly (2.3).

It remains to lift a square.  Write its seam vertices as

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 B_0&=K+a+b,&B_1&=K+b+c,\\
 B_2&=K+c+d,&B_3&=K+d+a,
 \end{aligned}
\tag{3.3}
\]

where \(|K|=M-3\), and put \(B_*=K+a+c\).  Its lifted octagon is the
sum of the two lifted up-triangles

\[
 \boxed{
 \gamma_4(B_0,B_1,B_2,B_3)
 =\gamma_3(B_0,B_*,B_3)+
  \gamma_3(B_*,B_1,B_2).}
\tag{3.4}
\]

The two incidences at \(B_*\) cancel, and the four remaining top
differences are precisely the square lift.  Thus every lifted
triangle or square belongs to the hexagon lattice, proving (3.1).
\(\square\)

### Theorem 3.2 (exact multi-position lattice and saturation)

Let \(P_*\) be a connected active-position graph.  The lattice generated
by all trades (2.4) on its edges is exactly (0.1), with \(j\in P_*\)
and with zero coordinates outside \(P_*\).

#### Proof

Every generator satisfies the three displayed rows in (0.1).  Conversely,
let \(z\) satisfy them and fix a root position \(s\in P_*\).  For every
position \(j\), the vector

\[
                         z_j=(z_{j,B,U})_{B\subset U}
\]

has zero top and seam boundary, hence is a graph circulation.  By
Lemma 3.1 it is an integral sum of hexagons.  The first row of (0.1)
gives

\[
                         z_s=-\sum_{j\ne s}z_j
\tag{3.5}
\]

edge by edge.  Route the hexagon decomposition of each \(z_j\) from
\(j\) to \(s\) along any path in the active-position graph.  Internal
positions cancel, position \(j\) receives \(z_j\), and (3.5) gives
the required vector at \(s\).  Thus \(z\) is generated by (2.4).

The right side of (0.1) is the kernel of a homomorphism between free
abelian groups.  Such a kernel is primitive.  Therefore
\({\cal L}_{\rm hex}\) is saturated. \(\square\)

By (1.8), every vector in (0.1) has zero position--label derivative.
Equation (0.1), however, contains the strictly stronger seam rows.

## 4. The four-top shadow adds no lattice direction

The four facets in (3.3), together with their four pairwise unions,
are exactly the Johnson-square seam pattern used by the physical
four-top re-root theorem.  If one records only the two active seam
positions, its idealized Latin shadow is

\[
                   (e_s-e_t)\otimes\gamma_4.
\tag{4.1}
\]

Equation (3.4) proves

\[
 \boxed{{\cal L}_{\langle 3\text{-top},4\text{-top shadow}\rangle}
       ={\cal L}_{\rm hex}.}
\tag{4.2}
\]

Thus the four-top square is useful conformally—it supplies one legal
octagon in one step—but it supplies no new element of the integer
lattice and breaks no linear seam invariant.

## 5. A literal invariant beyond position--label histograms

Choose distinct tops \(U,V\) containing two common labels \(x,y\),
and choose active positions \(s,t\).  Consider the two local tables

\[
 \begin{array}{c|cc}
 &s&t\\ \hline
 U&x&y\\
 V&y&x
 \end{array}
 \qquad\longleftrightarrow\qquad
 \begin{array}{c|cc}
 &s&t\\ \hline
 U&y&x\\
 V&x&y.
 \end{array}
\tag{5.1}
\]

Keep every other position and every other top unchanged.  Both shores
are literal word tables, and their position--label histograms agree:
each of \(x,y\) occurs once in each of \(s,t\).  Their seam multisets
at position \(s\), however, are

\[
             \{U-x,V-y\}\qquad\text{and}\qquad
             \{U-y,V-x\}.
\tag{5.2}
\]

The four seams are distinct when \(U\ne V\) and \(x,y\in U\cap V\).
Hence (5.2) gives different vectors \((D_{s,B})_B\).

### Corollary 5.1 (failure on the raw histogram fibre)

The three-top and four-top-shadow lattice is a proper saturated
sublattice of the integer difference lattice obtained by fixing only
the top rows and the position--label histogram.  The two shores (5.1)
belong to different components under these fixed-fibre hexagon and
octagon moves.

This is stronger than a parity obstruction: (0.2) is a complete integer
ledger.  It survives arbitrary compositions and arbitrary changes of
the common core used by individual hexagons.

### The fixed-core two-slot exception

There is no contradiction with the positive tournament theorem in one
fixed common-core chart.  Fix an \((M-2)\)-set \(C\), let
\(Q=[n]\setminus C\), retain only the pair tops

\[
                         U_{uv}=C+u+v,
\]

and allow only \(u,v\) in the two active slots.  A state is an
orientation of every edge \(uv\) of \(K_Q\).  The position--label
histogram is its score vector.  Moreover

\[
 D_{s,C+u}=(|Q|-1)-C_{s,u},
\tag{5.3}
\]

up to the choice of which active slot is called \(s\).  Hence in this
chart the seam histogram is already determined by the label histogram.

The three-top packet reverses a directed triangle.  If two tournaments
have the same scores, orient every edge on which they differ as in the
first tournament.  The difference digraph is Eulerian and decomposes
into directed cycles.  A directed cycle

\[
 v_1\to v_2\to\cdots\to v_r\to v_1
\]

can be reversed by applicable triangle reversals: inspect the chord
\(v_1v_3\), reverse either the first triangle or the shorter remaining
cycle first according to its orientation, and induct on \(r\).  Thus:

\[
 \boxed{\text{in one fixed pair chart, the three-top packets are a
 Markov basis for every fixed-score fibre.}}
\tag{5.4}
\]

The intercalate (5.1) necessarily uses tops whose nonactive parts are
not one common \(C\), or uses common labels outside the two-slot pair
chart.  Global chart mixing is exactly where the new seam ledger becomes
independent.

## 6. Saturation is not Markov connectivity

The seam-refined lattice is saturated, but bounded six- and eight-cycle
moves still do not form a Markov basis.

Fix \(r\ge5\), choose an \((M-3)\)-set \(K\), and choose distinct
\(x_0,\ldots,x_{r-1}\notin K\).  With indices modulo \(r\), put

\[
 B_i=K+x_i+x_{i+1},\qquad
 U_i=K+x_i+x_{i+1}+x_{i+2}.
\tag{6.1}
\]

Then

\[
 B_0-U_0-B_1-U_1-\cdots-B_{r-1}-U_{r-1}-B_0
\tag{6.2}
\]

is an induced \(2r\)-cycle of \(G\).  Indeed the only consecutive
two-subsets of the cyclic list contained in
\(\{x_i,x_{i+1},x_{i+2}\}\) are
\(\{x_i,x_{i+1}\}\) and
\(\{x_{i+1},x_{i+2}\}\).

At position \(s\), take either one of the two alternating perfect
matchings of (6.2), and at position \(t\) take the other.  Swap the two
matchings to obtain the second state.  At each top the two used labels
are \(x_i,x_{i+2}\).  At each position the label multiset is
\(\{x_0,\ldots,x_{r-1}\}\), and every seam \(B_i\) occurs once.
Thus the two states have identical top, edge, label, and seam margins.

Give every vertex of (6.2) margin one and every incidence vertex outside
(6.2) margin zero.  Since the cycle is induced, this fibre consists of
exactly its two alternating matchings.  For \(r\ge5\) its support
contains no six- or eight-cycle.  Hence neither state admits an
applicable three- or four-top Latin move.

On the other hand their signed difference is an integral circulation,
so Lemma 3.1 places it in the hexagon lattice.  Its hexagon decomposition
necessarily uses intermediate incidence edges absent from both endpoint
supports.  Therefore the obstruction is nonconformal and proves:

\[
 \boxed{\text{lattice saturation does not imply literal chronology.}}
\tag{6.3}
\]

The construction proves failure of a universal bounded Markov basis for
the induced carrier catalogues.  In the full all-top table, extra positive
rows may act as catalysts; (6.2) alone does not rule that out.  The seam
invariant of Section 5, by contrast, is a genuine obstruction in the
full raw histogram fibre.

## 7. The complete four-top shift leaves the fibre

Let \(p_0,\ldots,p_3\) be the four words in a physical four-top
re-root packet, and let \(S\) be left cyclic shift.  Put

\[
 A_{j,v}=\#\{i\in\{0,1,2,3\}:p_i(j)=v\}.
\tag{7.1}
\]

The exact position--label derivative of the physical packet is

\[
 \boxed{\Delta C_{j,v}=A_{j+1,v}-A_{j,v}.}
\tag{7.2}
\]

If this derivative vanished, then \(A_{j,v}\) would be independent of
\(j\) for every \(v\).  Summing over all \(M\) positions would give

\[
 r_v:=\#\{i:v\in U_i\}=M A_{j,v}.
\tag{7.3}
\]

For every label used by the four tops, \(1\le r_v\le4\).  When
\(M>4\), (7.3) is impossible.  Therefore

\[
 \boxed{(p_i)_i\mapsto(Sp_i)_i
 \text{ is never a fixed-column-histogram move for }M>4.}
\tag{7.4}
\]

This applies both to the common-\((M-2)\)-core four-cycle recharge,
where each special label occurs twice, and to the Johnson-square
four-top theorem, where each of its four special labels occurs three
times.  The trace-collar cancellation in those theorems is exact, but
it is a different set of rows from the column histogram (1.6).

Accordingly one may use the octagon (4.1) as the **shadow** of the
physical four-top packet, but one may not insert it as a literal
fixed-fibre transition without separately balancing all the other
position columns.

## 8. Exact implication boundary

Proved:

1. the exact edge-colouring model for literal top words;
2. the three-top packet is an alternating inclusion hexagon;
3. inclusion hexagons generate the complete integral circulation lattice;
4. the exact saturated multi-position lattice (0.1);
5. the seam--position histogram as an invariant beyond ordinary column
   histograms;
6. the four-top Johnson-square shadow is the sum of two hexagons;
7. an explicit same-column-histogram intercalate separated by the seam
   invariant;
8. induced long-cycle fibres proving bounded-move Markov failure; and
9. the exact nonzero column derivative of every complete physical
   four-top shift.

Not proved:

1. a column-balanced composition of complete four-top physical shifts;
2. a catalyst theorem connecting the full seam-refined all-top fibre;
3. prefixwise middle-owner capacity during a nonconformal hexagon
   decomposition; or
4. coefficient one.

The sharp routing gate is therefore no longer integer-lattice
saturation.  It is to construct a literal, column-balanced re-root
macro which changes the seam histogram, or else to enlarge the move
library to the unbounded induced-cycle Latin trades and prove that their
physical collars and owner prefixes remain legal.
