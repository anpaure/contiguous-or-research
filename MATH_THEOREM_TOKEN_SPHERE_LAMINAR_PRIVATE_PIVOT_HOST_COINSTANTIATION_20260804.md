# Token-sphere seam selection and laminar private co-instantiation in a
# one-pivot host

**Date:** 2026-08-04  
**Status:** exact conditional composition theorem, an unconditional
two-stage regular-factor router, and a sharp abstract correlation
obstruction.  The theorem does not construct the required Boolean host and
does not prove `PPC(1)`, `PCC(0)`, or an all-dimensional upper bound.

## 0. Result

Fix a child odd host on a ground set of size `2R-1`, an incoming
rank-`(R-1)` aperture token `H`, and depth `D`, where

\[
                         2\le D\le R-2.                 \tag{0.1}
\]

Let `F` be a coordinate bank which is forbidden only in the **exported
token**.  Put

\[
 a=|F\cap H|,\qquad b=|F-H|.                           \tag{0.2}
\]

The exact number of one-aperture outputs avoiding `F` is

\[
 N_F(H)=
 {R-1-a\choose R-D}{R-b\choose D-1}.                 \tag{0.3}
\]

It is positive exactly when

\[
                         a\le D-1,
 \qquad                    b\le R-D+1.                \tag{0.4}
\]

Suppose a `q1`-exact cyclic carrier is available, so its lower seam
colours run once through the whole rank-`(R-1)` shore.  Every token counted
by (0.3) is then the colour of a unique possible opening seam.  If fewer
than `N_F(H)` of these seams fail the **joint** lower/upper/router host
test, one surviving token can be chosen and the aperture can be planted at
its seam.

There is an exact way to certify that joint test.  Encode all lower-path,
upper occurrence-section/forest/connector, cap, compensation, prefix, and
diamond choices as finite relations on their shared physical variables.  If
the relation scopes have a running-intersection tree (in particular, if
they are laminar), their natural join is nonempty exactly when the usual
separator messages admit a root tuple.  Thus a seam is bad for a precise
reason, not because separately existing witnesses were informally
intersected.

For a surviving joined tuple, the three modules coexist literally under
the following private-footprint rule:

* their finite-capacity interiors are disjoint after the frozen
  compensation footprint is deleted;
* every overlap is either the same read-only interval fact or a displayed
  interface occurrence whose combined roles are explicitly accepted;
* the only nontrivial boundary identification is one complete bundle at
  the opened seam, shared by the omitted `q1` colour, the aperture output,
  and the missing wrap diamond; and
* all objects use one word, cap, guard, occurrence, and terminal-type
  state.

Under the exact lower, upper, and router rows stated in Section 4, gluing
introduces no new omitted target.  If the three modules already exhaust the
target inventory, the resulting odd scaffold has

\[
                     \mathcal H=\varnothing,
 \qquad               \tau=1.                         \tag{0.5}
\]

The one unit in (0.5) is the pivot letter; the boundary bundle is an
existing occurrence role, not a second source position.

The router row can be weaker than full-port suffix rank.  A left
`h`-regular/right-at-most-`h` private claim--port factor followed by a
left-`q`-regular private port--typed-sink catalogue routes every claim when
its exact degree-weighted sink load is at most `hq`.  Uniform weight
`1/(hq)` on every length-two incidence chain is a full fractional flow, and
integrality makes it literal.  Right degree at most `q` is a simple
sufficient specialization.  At `h=q=2`, two coexisting occurrence-lifted
factors suffice.  This does not say that two abstract Middle-Levels factors
possess the required physical lifts.

Finally, running intersection is a real correlation hypothesis.  Three
binary relations with scopes `{x,y}`, `{y,z}`, `{z,x}` can be individually
feasible, have full common-coordinate projections, and be feasible in
every pair, while their triple join is empty.  Hence pairwise
co-instantiation and pairwise-disjoint private interiors do not imply one
pivot host.

## 1. The exact restricted token sphere

Let

\[
 \mathcal S_D(H)=
 \left\{J\in{[2R-1]\choose R-1}:|H\cap J|=R-D\right\} \tag{1.1}
\]

be the exact one-aperture transition sphere, and define

\[
 \mathcal S_D(H;F)=\{J\in\mathcal S_D(H):J\cap F=\varnothing\}.
                                                               \tag{1.2}
\]

### Lemma 1.1 (restricted-sphere count)

Equations (0.3)--(0.4) hold.  More explicitly,

\[
 |\mathcal S_D(H;F)|=
 {R-1-|F\cap H|\choose R-D}
 {R-|F-H|\choose D-1}.                                \tag{1.3}
\]

#### Proof

Every output has the unique decomposition

\[
 J=(H\cap J)\mathbin{\dot\cup}(J-H),
 \qquad |H\cap J|=R-D,
 \qquad |J-H|=D-1.                                   \tag{1.4}
\]

To avoid `F`, the first part is chosen from the `R-1-a` elements of
`H-F`, and the second from the `R-b` elements of `H^c-F`.  This gives
(1.3).  The two binomial coefficients are positive exactly when

\[
 R-1-a\ge R-D,
 \qquad R-b\ge D-1,
\]

which is (0.4).  \(\square\)

The lemma counts output-token avoidance only.  The relay uses the entire
incoming token `H`; therefore it does not imply that a bank meeting `H` is
absent from the current pivot block.  It says that the bank need not remain
in the next token.

### Corollary 1.2 (exact seam reservoir)

Assume a cyclic `q1`-exact owner chronology whose lower colours

\[
 P_i=T_i\cap T_{i+1}
\]

run once through `binom([2R-1],R-1)`.  Let `mathcal B` be any family of
seam colours forbidden by additional host correlations.  If

\[
 |\mathcal B\cap\mathcal S_D(H;F)|<N_F(H),            \tag{1.5}
\]

then some aperture output `J` avoids `F`, does not belong to
`mathcal B`, and there is a unique seam `i_*` with `P_(i_*)=J`.

A convenient sufficient condition is

\[
                         |\mathcal B|<N_F(H).          \tag{1.6}
\]

#### Proof

Lemma 1.1 gives `N_F(H)` eligible tokens.  Inequality (1.5) leaves one
outside `mathcal B`.  Exactness of the lower palette gives existence and
uniqueness of its seam.  \(\square\)

The threshold is sharp for an arbitrary correlated host family: if
\(\mathcal B=\mathcal S_D(H;F)\), every coordinate-eligible relay is
rejected.
The point of later structural work is to prove that the actual bad set is
smaller, not to assume independence.

## 2. Parameterized running-intersection composition

Let `z` be the seam-colour variable and let `mathcal V` be all remaining
finite host-choice variables.  These may include

* the owner paths and named lower assignment;
* the upper occurrence section, retained witnesses, forest deletions,
  connector choices, and absorbers;
* the common cap and frozen compensation linkage;
* claim--port prefixes, suffix bundles, terminal types, and shared-capacity
  allocation; and
* the pivot and boundary-bundle occurrences.

Let `(mathcal R_i)_(i in mathcal I)` be finite relations, with scopes
`S_i subseteq {z} union mathcal V`.  A tuple in their natural join is one
literal common host choice.  Equal variable names mean equal physical
choices; they are not independently renamed copies.

Assume there is a tree `T` on `mathcal I` such that, for every variable
`v`, the nodes

\[
                         \{i:v\in S_i\}               \tag{2.1}
\]

induce a connected subtree.  Root `T`.  For a fixed token `J`, restrict
`z=J`.  For an oriented edge `i->j`, let `M^J_(i->j)` be the assignments
on `S_i cap S_j` which extend to every relation in the component of
`T-ij` containing `i`.  Equivalently, these messages obey the leaf-to-root
recursion

\[
 M^J_{i\to j}=\pi_{S_i\cap S_j}
 \left\{x\in\mathcal R_i[z=J]:
 x|_{S_i\cap S_k}\in M^J_{k\to i}
 \text{ for }k\in N_T(i)-\{j\}\right\}.             \tag{2.2}
\]

### Theorem 2.1 (exact laminar host test)

For fixed `J`, the natural join

\[
                         \Join_i\mathcal R_i[z=J]     \tag{2.3}
\]

is nonempty if and only if the root relation has a tuple compatible with
all incoming messages.  Consequently the joint bad-seam set

\[
 \mathcal B_{\rm join}=
 \left\{J:\Join_i\mathcal R_i[z=J]=\varnothing\right\}             \tag{2.4}
\]

is determined exactly by separator tests.  A laminar scope family admits
such a tree.

#### Proof

For an edge `ij`, running intersection makes `S_i cap S_j` the complete
set of variables shared by the two sides of `T-ij`.  Induction from the
leaves therefore shows that (2.2) is precisely the projection of all
assignments extendible over the `i`-side.  A compatible root tuple can be
extended down the tree, and extensions on different child subtrees agree
on every shared variable.  They glue to (2.3).  Conversely, restriction of
any global tuple belongs to every message.  The laminar family uses its
inclusion forest, joining disjoint roots with empty separators.  \(\square\)

### Corollary 2.2 (local exceptional sets)

Suppose there are certified seam-exception sets
`mathcal B_1,...,mathcal B_t` such that the separator recursion succeeds
for every

\[
             J\notin\mathcal B_1\cup\cdots\cup\mathcal B_t.       \tag{2.5}
\]

If

\[
       \sum_{u=1}^t|\mathcal B_u|<N_F(H),             \tag{2.6}
\]

then some `J in mathcal S_D(H;F)` has a complete common host tuple.

#### Proof

The union in (2.5) has size at most the left side of (2.6).  Apply
Corollary 1.2 with this union as `mathcal B`, then Theorem 2.1.  \(\square\)

The union bound is only a sufficient way to use the exact bad set.  The
sharp condition is

\[
 |\mathcal B_{\rm join}\cap\mathcal S_D(H;F)|<N_F(H).              \tag{2.7}
\]

No theorem currently bounds this set for the complete Boolean host.

## 3. A two-stage regular-factor router

The full-port suffix condition

\[
                         r_\Gamma(P)=|P|               \tag{3.1}
\]

is convenient but stronger than the claim-level Rado condition.  The next
theorem gives a structural replacement suited to private-footprint
composition.

Let `B=(G,P;E_B)` be a bipartite multigraph satisfying

\[
 \deg_B(g)=h\quad(g\in G),
 \qquad \deg_B(p)\le h\quad(p\in P).                 \tag{3.2}
\]

Let `K=(P,S;E_K)` be a second bipartite multigraph satisfying

\[
                         \deg_K(p)=q\quad(p\in P).    \tag{3.3}
\]

Degrees count parallel edge occurrences.  Put `d_p=deg_B(p)` and assume
the degree-weighted sink condition

\[
 \boxed{
 \sum_{f=ps\in E_K}d_p\le hq
 \qquad(s\in S),}                                    \tag{3.4}
\]

where the sum is over incident edge occurrences.  The simpler condition
`deg_K(s)<=q` implies (3.4).

Fix a literal prefix `Q_e:g leadsto p` for each edge `e=gp` of `B` and a
correctly typed suffix bundle `R_f:p leadsto s` for each edge `f=ps` of
`K`.
Parallel edges are distinct physical bundle records.  Assume:

1. all objects lie in one fixed residual state after a frozen compensation
   linkage and its sinks have been deleted;
2. distinct prefix interiors are private, except for their displayed claim
   or port endpoints, and avoid all nonincident displayed endpoints;
3. distinct suffix interiors are private, except for their displayed port
   or sink endpoints, and avoid all nonincident displayed endpoints;
4. a prefix and suffix meet only at their common displayed port; and
5. for every incidence chain `g-p-s`, sink `s` has a legal terminal type
   for claim `g`.

### Theorem 3.1 (degree-weighted factor cascade)

Every claim in `G` has a pairwise vertex-disjoint route to a distinct legal
sink in `S`.

#### Proof

For every incidence chain

\[
                         g-p-s                        \tag{3.5}
\]

send `1/(hq)` units along `Q_eR_f`.  A claim has `hq` such chains,
so its source load is one.  A fixed prefix has load

\[
                         q/(hq)=1/h.                  \tag{3.6}
\]

At port `p` the load is

\[
                   {\deg_B(p)q\over hq}
                   ={\deg_B(p)\over h}\le1.          \tag{3.7}
\]

A fixed suffix has load

\[
                         {\deg_B(p)\over hq}\le1/q.   \tag{3.8}
\]

Finally the load at sink `s` is

\[
 {1\over hq}\sum_{f=ps\in E_K}\deg_B(p)\le1         \tag{3.9}
\]

by (3.4).  Privacy accounts for every internal capacity.  Thus (3.5) gives a feasible
fractional flow of value `|G|` in the node-split integral network.  Integral
max flow gives a flow of the same value.  Every unit claim arc is saturated
and every sink has unit capacity, so flow decomposition gives the asserted
routes.  \(\square\)

### Corollary 3.2 (two lifted factors)

If `deg_K(s)<=q`, then (3.4) is automatic.  In particular, at `h=q=2`, two
private occurrence-lifted left-2-regular/right-at-most-2 factors give a full
typed claim router.  The second factor may be a
factor-diamond suffix catalogue when every displayed diamond is a complete
residual bundle and its internal owner is either private or only a semantic
record as in the owner-bypassing diagonal bundle.

This corollary does not follow from two abstract factors.  Compensation
survival, occurrence addresses, terminal type, common cap, and the privacy
conditions of Theorem 3.1 remain hypotheses.  Nor does it resolve a second
physical occurrence coordinate or cross-coordinate product closure.

### Corollary 3.3 (strictly below full-port rank)

Theorem 3.1 can apply when the complete port bank is not suffix-linkable.
Take one claim `g`, two ports `p_1,p_2`, and the two prefix incidences
`gp_1,gp_2`, so `h=2` and `d_(p_1)=d_(p_2)=1`.  Put `q=1` and give both
ports a private bundle to the same unit sink `s`.  Then

\[
             \sum_{f=ps}d_p=2=hq,                    \tag{3.10}
\]

so the claim is routable, while the suffix rank of `{p_1,p_2}` is one.
Thus the degree-weighted cascade is genuinely a claim-level certificate,
not a disguised assumption of (3.1).

## 4. Literal private-footprint gluing

Fix a pre-insertion word `A` of length `B(k)` and one pivot insertion,
giving `Z` of length `B(k)+1`.  A **private co-instantiation datum** in `Z`
consists of the following joined choices.

### L. Lower bank

There is one saturated Boolean path per owner, with the balanced
floor/ceiling visitation multiplicities.  On this same bank, the required
named lower inventory is assigned to visiting paths with owner load at most
`D`.  Thus the exact multisocket cuts vanish.  If the intended certificate
requires the anonymous residual nonadjacency pattern, its stronger
zero--one system is included explicitly; balance and the capacity-only cuts
are not used to infer it.

Every selected named target has a literal interval witness in `Z`.  Paths
may meet as Boolean chains; they are not silently treated as disjoint
capacity routes.

### U. Upper forest and path

On one occurrence-labelled factor, one protected rainbow witness bank and
one upper-exact rooted forest satisfy the blocker/occurrence-section and
component-omission conditions.  A direct ordered connector forest, or
one-defect component Hall plus protected serial accessibility, gives a
Hamilton owner path.  Every selected higher witness is retained or
explicitly redelivered, so the result is all-width exact.  The aperture
transition is the actual opened seam.

### R. Residual typed router

One cap/guard/occurrence/type state and one frozen compensation linkage are
fixed first.  After their capacities and sink slots are deleted, the claim
prefixes and suffix bundles satisfy either

* the exact claim-level Rado inequalities;
* the full factor-diamond linkage; or
* the regular-factor cascade of Theorem 3.1.

For a cyclic native diagonal, the upper-turn terminal `q_i` may retain its
upper-witness role and terminate its diamond ticket only when that exact
dual role and terminal type are accepted in the common state.

### P. Physical pushout

Let `C_L,C_U,C_R,C_P` be the sets of finite unit-capacity resources used by
the lower, upper, router, and pivot/boundary modules after compensation
deletion.  Their disjoint union is quotiented only by the following
displayed interfaces.

1. The same owner or interval occurrence may be read by two modules when
   both roles assert the identical value at the identical address and no
   rule consumes it twice.
2. A port or terminal may be identified only when one complete combined
   bundle certifies all roles and charges its unit capacity once.
3. At the opened seam, the lower `q1` boundary, pivot output, and missing
   wrap diamond are one bundle with one entry value `J` and one accepted
   upper-turn terminal.

After removing these certified interfaces, the four capacity sets are
pairwise disjoint.  In particular, the compensation footprint and reserved
prefix interiors avoid every selected suffix bundle.  Overlap of source
positions by two interval addresses is not a capacity collision unless the
chosen model explicitly node-prices those positions.

### C. Completeness and one-star rebuilding

The monotone insertion condition holds at the pivot cut, so every old
interval-OR witness survives.  The lower bank, owner row, upper witnesses,
pivot singleton and rays, transported background, and terminal router form
an exhaustive partition of the certificate obligations.  The final replay
finds no target outside these rows.  If the datum is used recursively, the
child is rebuilt with one active star; it is not a concatenation of two
already charged child words.

### Theorem 4.1 (private pivot-host co-instantiation)

Assume:

1. (0.4) holds;
2. the parameterized host relations of Section 2 have running intersection;
3. their joint bad-seam set satisfies (2.7); and
4. every tuple in a surviving seam fibre is a private co-instantiation
   datum satisfying L, U, R, P, and C.

Then there is one outgoing token `J`, one opened seam, and one literal word
`Z` in which the balanced lower bank, protected all-width upper forest and
Hamilton path, sharp pivot, and residual typed router coexist.  Gluing the
modules introduces no omitted target.  Under the zero-omission clause in C,

\[
                         \tau(Z)=1.                   \tag{4.1}
\]

#### Proof

Lemma 1.1 and (2.7) choose

\[
 J\in\mathcal S_D(H;F)-\mathcal B_{\rm join}.        \tag{4.2}
\]

The one-aperture relay realizes `J`.  The complete lower `q1` palette has a
unique seam of colour `J`; open there.  Theorem 2.1 supplies one tuple of
all lower, upper, router, cap, and occurrence choices for that same seam.

Take the union of its four literal modules.  Condition P assigns every
finite resource either to one private interior or to one certified combined
interface.  Hence no unit capacity is overused.  All semantic occurrences
belong to the same word and state.  The lower assignments therefore remain
literal, the protected upper witness bank survives the forest and connector
choices, and the router remains in the residual network formed after the
fixed compensation deletion.  Its service is supplied by the selected
Rado, factor-diamond, or regular-cascade certificate.

At the opened seam, boundary coalescence identifies the sole missing lower
colour, the aperture output, and the missing wrap entry.  The accepted
complete bundle services them once.  Monotone insertion preserves every
old interval value, and C lists every new obligation.  Thus gluing creates
no new omitted target.  In the zero-omission case the scaffold excess is
the one pivot position and the repair length is zero, proving (4.1).
\(\square\)

### Corollary 4.2 (flushing fixed coordinate banks without charge growth)

Consider a same-parity sequence with

\[
                         D\longrightarrow\infty,
 \qquad                    R-D\longrightarrow\infty. \tag{4.3}
\]

Suppose `|F_R|<=f_0` and the number of jointly bad eligible seam colours is
at most `b_0`, where `f_0,b_0` are absolute constants.  Then, for all
sufficiently large `R`, an output token avoids `F_R` and all joint bad
seams.

If every selected output is an accepted next state and every child is
rebuilt with one star, iteration retains charge one at every odd terminal;
the charges are not summed down the spine.

#### Proof

For fixed `f_0`, both available banks in (1.3) grow without bound under
(4.3), uniformly over

\[
             |F_R\cap H|+|F_R-H|\le f_0.
\]

Hence \(N_{F_R}(H)>b_0\) eventually.  Apply
Theorem 4.1 at each accepted state.  One-star rebuilding and zero omission
give charge one anew at each terminal rather than adding the previous
charge.  \(\square\)

This corollary flushes only a coordinate bank from the exported token.  It
does not flush a growing occurrence footprint, repair the fixed-fibre
component deficiency, or make a compensation path private from the suffix
catalogue.

## 5. Sharp correlation obstructions

### Proposition 5.1 (pairwise-compatible correlation triangle)

Let `x,y,z` be binary variables and put

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathcal R_L&=\{(x,y):x=y\},\\
 \mathcal R_U&=\{(y,z):y=z\},\\
 \mathcal R_R&=\{(z,x):z\ne x\}.
 \end{aligned}                                       \tag{5.1}
\]

Every relation is nonempty.  Every projection onto a shared one-variable
separator is `{0,1}`.  Every pairwise join is nonempty.  Nevertheless

\[
                  \mathcal R_L\Join\mathcal R_U
                  \Join\mathcal R_R=\varnothing.     \tag{5.2}
\]

The three scopes do not have a running-intersection tree.

#### Proof

The first two relations force `x=y=z`, while the third forces `z!=x`, so
(5.2) is empty.  Any tree on the three two-variable scopes omits one of the
three pairwise adjacencies.  The variable belonging to that omitted pair
then occurs at the two ends of the tree path but not at its middle node,
violating running intersection.  \(\square\)

The example can be read as lower, upper, and router choices of one common
binary cap/orientation state.  Their private resource interiors may be
empty, so physical disjointness does not cure the correlation.  It proves
that individual cuts, full shared-variable projections, and pairwise
coexistence are insufficient.

### Proposition 5.2 (token coverage is the exact remaining seam obstruction)

For fixed `H,F`, let a host family accept precisely a set `A` of seam
colours.  A token-compatible opening exists if and only if

\[
                         A\cap\mathcal S_D(H;F)\ne\varnothing.    \tag{5.3}
\]

Therefore the numerical condition `|mathcal B|<N_F(H)` in Corollary 1.2 is
best possible when only the size of the rejected set is known.

#### Proof

The one-aperture theorem gives exactly `mathcal S_D(H)`, avoidance cuts it
to `mathcal S_D(H;F)`, and `q1` exactness gives one seam for each colour.
Intersecting with the accepted set is therefore necessary and sufficient.
Taking `A=emptyset`, equivalently taking the rejected set equal to the
entire restricted sphere of size `N_F(H)`, shows sharpness.  \(\square\)

There remains an independent physical obstruction: two marginal routers
may use one common terminal bank of insufficient total capacity.  Neither
running intersection on abstract choices nor token avoidance removes that
cut.  It must be resolved in the common state by capacity separation,
allocation, or legal role coalescence.

## 6. Exact scope relative to `PPC(1)`

The theorem proves a genuine composition statement under two transparent
structural hypotheses:

1. **laminar/running-intersection choice scopes**, which turn global
   correlation into exact separator messages; and
2. **private physical footprints with certified interfaces**, which let a
   joined abstract tuple paste into one literal capacity-feasible object.

It also enlarges the router menu: a second bounded-degree physical factor
can replace full-port suffix rank.  The exact token sphere supplies a large
seam reservoir and flushes any fixed-size coordinate bank from the exported
token.

None of the following is proved here:

* that the Boolean lower, upper, cap, and router relations actually have
  running-intersection scopes;
* that their joint bad-seam set is smaller than (0.3);
* existence of a balanced lower path bank satisfying every multisocket cut,
  still less the residual-nonadjacency zero--one lift;
* existence of the protected all-width rainbow forest/Hamilton host;
* occurrence lifting and compensation privacy for either factor in
  Corollary 3.2;
* the second occurrence coordinate, transported-background product closure,
  residence renewal, even taps, or an infinite accepted spine; or
* any new bound on `nu(k)`.

Thus Theorem 4.1 is a sufficient co-instantiation theorem and Proposition
5.1 is a sharp warning against replacing it by pairwise compatibility.  It
does not promote the exact projection theorems to `PPC(1)`.
