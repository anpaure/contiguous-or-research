## 1. The complete quartet frame graph

Let \({\mathfrak M}\) be the set of perfect matchings of
\(\Omega=[2m]\).  A \(B_4\) switch acts on four distinct coordinates
\(\{a,b,c,d\}\) by replacing one of

\[
                         ab\mid cd,\qquad
                         ac\mid bd,\qquad
                         ad\mid bc                 \tag{1.1}
\]

with another, while leaving all other matching edges unchanged.  These
are exactly the three shores of the local quartet associator.

### Lemma 1.1 (quartet switches connect all frames)

The graph on \({\mathfrak M}\) generated by the switches (1.1) is
connected.

#### Proof

Fix \(M,N\in{\mathfrak M}\).  Their symmetric difference is a disjoint
union of even alternating cycles.  On a cycle

\[
 x_1x_2\ldots x_{2r}x_1,                            \tag{1.2}
\]

where the \(M\)-edges and \(N\)-edges alternate, a quartet switch on
\(\{x_1,x_2,x_3,x_4\}\) replaces the two current \(M\)-edges
\(x_1x_2,x_3x_4\) by \(x_2x_3,x_1x_4\).  The first new edge agrees with
\(N\), and the remaining alternating disagreement cycle has length
\(2r-2\).  Repeating reduces the component to agreement.  Apply this
independently to every symmetric-difference cycle.  Thus \(M\) can be
transformed into \(N\).  \(\square\)

Since every coordinate pair occurs in some perfect matching, the union of
all frame edges is \(K_{2m}\).  The exclusion-component theorem therefore
gives:

### Corollary 1.2 (one common-owner component)

For the complete quartet atlas, the common-owner overlap has the single
component

\[
                         {\cal U}=\binom{\Omega}{m}.              \tag{1.3}
\]

The local \(24\)-owner reservoir lift does not refine this component: it
implements the switches (1.1) on equal-dimensional cells but generates
the same exclusion edges.

Accordingly the component dual (2.4) of the source note has one maximum
over a global option \(o\).  Componentwise adaptation has disappeared.

## 2. One common all-depth option orbit

Let \(o_0\) be one legal whole option based on a cyclically ordered frame,

\[
 I_{o_0,q}^{\epsilon}\subseteq{\cal T}_q^\epsilon             \tag{2.1}

\[
                         |I_{o_0,q}^{\epsilon}|=G.              \tag{2.2}

Let

\[
                         {\cal O}=\{\sigma o_0:\sigma\in S_{2m}\}.          \tag{2.3}

The same permutation \(\sigma\) conjugates the frame, packet selector,

\(\sigma o_0\) is one legal common option across all depths.

## 3. Exact Johnson margin

For \(q\ge1\),

\[
 {N_q\over W}
 ={m!^2\over(m-q)!(m+q)!}
 =\prod_{j=0}^{q-1}{m-j\over m+j+1}.                \tag{3.1}
\]

Every factor after the first is smaller than one, so

\[
                         {N_q\over W}\le {m\over m+1},
 \qquad
                         W-N_q\ge {W\over m+1}.       \tag{3.2}
\]

Equation (3.2) is the exact first Johnson-shadow margin below the middle
layer.  Combining it with (0.2) gives

\[
                         G>N_q\qquad(1\le q\le H).   \tag{3.3}
\]

No depth-dependent estimate is needed: the smallest surplus occurs at
\(q=1\).

## 4. Simultaneous weighted component expansion

Let \(o\) range over the menu (2.3), and define its joint weighted score

\[
 S_y(o)=
 \sum_{q=1}^H\sum_{\epsilon\in\{-,+\}}
 \sum_{T\in I_{o,q}^{\epsilon}}y_{q,T}^{\epsilon}.   \tag{4.1}
\]

The weights may be arbitrary in \([0,1]\); the argument works for
arbitrary nonnegative weights.

### Theorem 4.1 (exact common-option expansion)

For every joint weight family \(y\),

\[
 \max_{o\in{\cal O}}S_y(o)
 \ge
 \sum_{q=1}^H\sum_{\epsilon}
 {G\over N_q}\sum_{T\in{\cal T}_q^\epsilon}
 y_{q,T}^{\epsilon}.                                \tag{4.2}
\]

In particular (0.4) holds.

#### Proof

Choose one uniform \(\sigma\in S_{2m}\) and use the single option
\(\sigma o_0\) at every depth.  The symmetric group is transitive on
each rank \(\binom{\Omega}{m\pm q}\).  Since \(o_0\) has exactly \(G\)
occurrences at the specified signed depth, every fixed target is hit by a
uniform conjugate with expected multiplicity

\[
                         {G\over N_q}.               \tag{4.3}
\]

Linearity of expectation, without any independence between depths, gives

\[
 {\mathbb E}_{\sigma}S_y(\sigma o_0)
 =
 \sum_{q,\epsilon}{G\over N_q}
        \sum_Ty_{q,T}^{\epsilon}.                    \tag{4.4}
\]

Some one value of \(\sigma\) attains at least its expectation, proving
(4.2).  Equation (3.3) then proves (0.4).  \(\square\)

This is stronger than a depthwise expansion theorem.  It chooses one
coordinate conjugate after seeing the entire weight tensor
\((y_{q,T}^{\epsilon})_{q,\epsilon,T}\), and that conjugate serves every
depth and both signs together.

### Corollary 4.2 (the joint fractional dual vanishes)

#### Proof

The bracket in the joint dual (2.4) is at most zero by (0.4), while the

The proof uses no product distribution of traces and no independent

## 5. Why there is no universal dual obstruction

For a fixed matching \(M\), the known pair-type weights give a positive
Gaussian Hall margin against every \(M\)-internal option.  Those weights
depend on \(M\).  Under a conjugate \(\sigma\), the witness is conjugated
to the pair-type weight for \(\sigma M\).

Averaging such witnesses over all \(\sigma\) produces a target weight
constant on each rank, because \(S_{2m}\) acts transitively there.  A
rank-constant weight cannot separate the option orbit: at signed depth
\(q\), its total demand is the weight times \(N_q\), whereas every option
has \(G>N_q\) weighted occurrences.

Thus the family of fixed-frame cuts is an *adaptive integral certificate*:
after an integral matching is selected, use its own pair-type witness.
It is not one feasible dual vector with positive margin against the convex
hull of all frames.  Theorem 4.1 proves that no such vector exists.

The same observation applies to a frozen coordinate subset \(R\).
Averaging its suffix-type witness over the full symmetric group again
gives a rank-constant weight and loses the margin.  This is why fixed
suffix and fixed-pair obstructions coexist with zero fractional deficit.

## 6. Integral options remain fixed-frame

The fractional programme may mix the whole options in (2.3).  An integral
choice on the single component (1.3) selects exactly one whole option.
Within the moving-perfect-matching/status-cell architecture, such an
option selects one global perfect matching \(M\), possibly assembled from
different local quartet shores on disjoint coordinate quartets.

Every protected window remains inside one \(M\)-status cell.  Therefore
its lower trace preserves the owner's number of full \(M\)-pairs and its
upper trace preserves the owner's number of empty \(M\)-pairs.

For \(0\le f\le\lfloor(m-q)/2\rfloor\), let

\[
\begin{aligned}
 V_f&={m!\,2^{m-2f}\over f!^2(m-2f)!},\\
 T_{f,q}&={m!\,2^{m-2f-q}\over
             f!(f+q)!(m-2f-q)!}.
\end{aligned}                                        \tag{6.1}
\]

There are \(V_f\) middle owners of type \(f\), while the corresponding
lower target family—and, dually, the upper target family—has size
\(T_{f,q}\).  One owner supplies at most one occurrence of either sign at
the fixed depth.  Charge each of the \(L\) quarantined owners
optimistically as one arbitrary extra occurrence of each sign.  Hence
every integral option obeys

\[
 M_q^-\ge\sum_f(T_{f,q}-V_f)_+-L,\qquad
 M_q^+\ge\sum_f(T_{f,q}-V_f)_+-L.                   \tag{6.2}
\]

At \(q=\lfloor A\sqrt m\rfloor\), the exact local central-limit evaluation
is

\[
 {1\over W}\sum_f(T_{f,q}-V_f)_+
 \longrightarrow
 \delta_A=e^{-A^2}\Phi(A/2)-\Phi(-3A/2)>0.           \tag{6.3}
\]

This proves (0.6).

### Theorem 6.1 (linear integrality gap)

Assume \(H\ge\lfloor A\sqrt m\rfloor\).  For the complete multiframe
quartet atlas:

\[
 \eta_H^{\rm frac}=0,\qquad
 \eta_H^{\rm int}\ge(2\delta_A-o(1))W.               \tag{6.4}
\]

#### Proof

Corollary 4.2 proves the fractional equality.  Every integral solution
chooses one option on the unique owner component, and (6.2)--(6.3) give
the two signed deficits at the one protected Gaussian depth.  The
all-depth objective is at least its contribution from that depth.
\(\square\)

Thus dependent rounding, discrepancy rounding, and absorption cannot
repair the symmetric fractional point while remaining inside this
one-component status-cell option model: the desired integral point does
not exist.

## 7. Exact escape boundary

The simultaneous weighted expansion problem and the integral problem have
opposite answers:

1. **Fractional:** full \(S_{2m}\) frame symmetry gives exact common-option
   expansion and zero joint dual value.
2. **Integral:** quartet recoupling connects the owner overlap, forcing one
   global frame and a linear Gaussian deficit.

Boolean/Johnson isoperimetry enters through the exact middle-to-adjacent
rank margin (3.1)--(3.3); no stronger isoperimetric theorem can cure the
integral obstruction because it is a statewise fixed-frame capacity cut.

The smallest possible positive successor must violate at least one premise
of Theorem 6.1.  It must use either:

* a literal cycle whose protected windows change ambient perfect matching
  along the cycle;
* an exterior-moving packet whose owner-overlap relation is not generated
  by matching-edge exclusion; or
* an arbitrary owner-disjoint subcube matching which selects proper faces
  from several frame partitions rather than one whole component shore; or
* a bounded-overlap owner recycling scheme not representable by one exact
  component option.

The third item is a genuine escape, not a synonym for component switching:
the exact \(J(4,2)\) mosaic and its valid-dimensional tensor lift are
audited in
MATH_AUDIT_ARBITRARY_MULTIFRAME_SUBCUBE_HYPERMATCHING_ESCAPE_20260726.md.

while merging the owner components more completely.  That is the precise
