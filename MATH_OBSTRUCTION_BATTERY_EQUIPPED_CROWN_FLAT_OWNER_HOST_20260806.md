# The explicit eight-port battery is not a flat owner/q1 host

**Date:** 2026-08-06  
**Method:** unavoidable depth-window rank audit  
**Status:** unconditional obstruction to planting the currently displayed
lower-plus-dual battery block as source letters of a flat central carrier.
It does not rule out a redesigned buffered/basis-term battery, a fragmented
owner collar, or an external absorber with separately priced positions.

## 0. Verdict

The three-sheet crown colouring solves the **owner multiplicity ledger**,
and the bi-battery solves the **formal lower/upper current ledger**.  They
cannot simply be concatenated and handed to the polynomial protected-factor
extension theorem.

At depth `h>=1`, the lower battery contains an internal owner window of
rank only `h+1`, while the saturated dual battery contains an internal
owner window of rank at least `r+1`.  A flat carrier requires rank exactly
`r` at both addresses.  These are literal internal windows; no exterior
factor completion can change their values.

Thus option B still needs a new **owner-flat battery realization** before
protected-factor extension becomes applicable.

## 1. Parameters

Work on `k=2m+1` coordinates.  The central owner rank is

\[
                              r=m+1.                   \tag{1.1}
\]

Let the factor depth be `h`, and put `p=m-2`.  In the all-dimensional
range of interest,

\[
                         1\le h\le p,\qquad h+1<r.    \tag{1.2}
\]

A flat depth-`h` source word has the property that every internal source
interval of length `h+1` has union rank `r`.

## 2. The lower battery is rank-deficient

One lower port has the literal form

\[
       H,\ \{z\},\ \{q_1\},\ldots,\{q_p\},\ H,
       \qquad H=\{a,d\},                               \tag{2.1}
\]

where `z` is one of `a,d` and the `q_i` are distinct rail coordinates,
disjoint from the active pair.

### Theorem 2.1 (unavoidable low owner)

The length-`h+1` interval beginning at the pivot in (2.1) has value

\[
                         \{z,q_1,\ldots,q_h\}          \tag{2.2}
\]

and rank `h+1<r`.  Hence no displayed lower battery port can occur at an
internal position of a flat depth-`h`, rank-`r` carrier.

#### Proof

The inequality `h<=p` makes (2.2) an internal interval of the port.  Its
letters are distinct singletons, so its rank is exactly `h+1`.  Equation
(1.2) gives the contradiction. \(\square\)

The shield does not help: it lies immediately **before** the pivot and is
not contained in the interval (2.2).  Nor can an exterior collar help,
because the interval is wholly internal to the port.

## 3. The saturated dual battery is rank-excessive

In the corrected dual port, the pivot has the form

\[
                         P=C\cup\{z\},qquad |P|=r,    \tag{3.1}
\]

and is followed by an anchor singleton `{w}` with `w notin P`, before the
reversed rail.

### Theorem 3.1 (unavoidable high owner)

Every length-`h+1` interval beginning at `P` contains `P union {w}` and
therefore has rank at least `r+1`.  Hence no displayed saturated dual port
can occur at an internal position of a flat depth-`h`, rank-`r` carrier.

#### Proof

Since `h>=1`, the interval contains the first two port letters `P,{w}`.
The anchor was removed from the pivot core, so `w notin P`.  Their union
already has rank `r+1`; adding later letters cannot decrease rank.
\(\square\)

The anchor refinement was necessary to keep each **letter** at rank at
most `r`, but letterwise rank is weaker than flat-window legality.

## 4. Why protected-factor extension cannot repair this

The polynomial protected two-factor theorem starts with a valid simple
owner-path forest: every protected owner already has rank `r`, every
protected q1 incidence is legal, and the theorem only fills the residual
middle-level graph.

Theorems 2.1 and 3.1 show that the explicit battery block does not induce
such a protected forest.  Its failure occurs before Hall, exposure, or
component completion.  Therefore statements of the form

\[
 \text{battery support is }O(r)
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \text{polynomial extension plants it}                \tag{4.1}
\]

are invalid.  Small support does not repair a wrong-rank source window.

## 5. What the owner/q1 theorem does supply conditionally

Suppose a redesigned battery/crown macro is first realized as a simple
protected owner-path forest `P` with

\[
 |E(P)|=r^{O(1)},qquad
 \alpha(P),\beta(P)\le(1/2-\epsilon)r.                \tag{5.1}
\]

Then the existing polynomial protected-factor theorem extends `P` to a
spanning owner/q1 two-factor for all sufficiently large `r`.  If its two
oriented incidence colours are required separately, the protected-matching
theorem extends each role to a perfect matching, yielding a directed cycle
cover.

This is a real residual-completion result, but (5.1) is an input.  The
three-sheet colouring by itself is only an occurrence-addressed crown
ledger, and the present battery is only an interval-current gadget.  Neither
one supplies the valid protected forest demanded in (5.1).

## 6. Sharp next constructions

There are now three proof-safe escape routes.

1. **Depth-buffered battery.**  Replace each problematic port by an
   owner-flat pivot collar whose complete extra prefix currents cancel in a
   branched bank.
2. **Basis-term ports.**  Realize the individual basis terms of the rail
   current in separately shielded, owner-flat collars instead of one long
   one-sided ray.
3. **Masked crown gluing.**  Use the three-sheet crown factorization only
   for owner detachment and let a correlated masked-core branch absorb the
   profile changes, bypassing the explicit battery source block.

The factor-extension theorem will close the residual owner/q1 Hall row
after any one of these supplies a simple protected bank.  It cannot supply
that bank itself.
