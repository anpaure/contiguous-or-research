# Raw-answer symmetry census (`k=1,...,14`)

## Scope and reproducibility

This audit reads **only** `answers/k01.word,...,answers/k14.word`.  It does not
read certificates, search logs, solver metadata, or `MATHEMATICAL_HANDOFF.md`.
The exact analyzer is

```

SHA-256 30937433d606616c7bca761a5ce0ba9c570089af2f0d8bd5be1fded6a615ef38
```

and its full machine-readable output is

```

SHA-256 7a9ab729e2b1336b712d05c2dd2cf461ef38ff70cae60728b1edf0df626651ba
```

All coordinate maps below are zero-based.  Position symmetries are checked by
literal replay, and carrier automorphism groups are obtained by an exhaustive
weighted-transition-matrix backtrack followed by literal directed-edge-set
replay.  No symmetry is inferred from a heuristic score.

## The recurring structure that is actually present

For every stored answer from `k=6` through `k=14`:

1. The OR-derivative row at depth `d = length - binom(k,ceil(k/2))` is a
   bijection onto the entire central layer.  The tiny `k=4,5` answers are the
   only non-flat exceptions.
2. Every **interior** coordinate-residence run in that central row has length
   at least `d+1`, and the global interior minimum is exactly `d+1`.  Thus the
   residence inequality is sharp in every one of these nine answers.  Shorter
   runs seen at the two endpoints are genuine boundary truncations, not
   residence defects.
3. Every upper union shadow of the central row is complete, at every possible
   depth.  Equivalently, for rank `r+q`, the largest shortest-witness depth is
   exactly `d+q`.
4. The central ordering is a literal Johnson path for `k=6,7,9,10,11,12,13,14`.
   The only exception is the stored `k=8` answer, with two non-Johnson seams.

At the immediate lower shadow, the stable boundary pattern begins at `k=9`:
the stored even answers `k=10,12,14` have zero missing `(r-1)`-colours, while
the stored odd answers `k=9,11,13` have exactly one.  (`k=6` already has zero;
`k=7,8` have two and one respectively.)  Deeper lower shadows are not governed
by one parity-only rule, so they should not be promoted to an invariant.

The complete raw words themselves have no useful global symmetry: for every
`k>=5`, the only cyclic/dihedral position action realizable by a coordinate
permutation is the identity.  The symmetry lives in the central carrier, and
the lower compiler destroys it.  This distinction is important.

## Exact hidden group structure at `k=11`

The depth-three central row has 462 vertices and 461 directed Johnson edges.
Under the standard coordinate rotation `x -> x+1 mod 11`, its edge-orbit
occupancies are

```
41 orbits of occupancy 11, and 1 orbit of occupancy 10.
```

The missing edge is exactly the end-to-start closure edge.  Adding it produces
one 462-cycle whose directed edge set has coordinate automorphism group
**exactly** `C_11` (all 11 translations and no others).

The chronology itself is equivariant: shifting the cycle by 42 positions maps
coordinates by `x -> x+2 mod 11`.  Consequently the whole carrier is generated
by one quotient template of length 42, repeated through the 11 coordinate
translations.  The analyzer independently sees all eleven length-42 blocks as
one coordinate-relabel class.

## Exact hidden group and seam structure at `k=13`

The depth-three central row has 1716 vertices and 1715 directed Johnson edges.
Its standard `C_13` edge-orbit occupancies are

```
130 orbits of occupancy 13,
  2 orbits of occupancy 12,
  1 orbit of occupancy 1.
```

The unique occupancy-one edge is at path index 1546.  Removing it splits the
path into segments of lengths

```
1547 = 119 * 13,    169 = 13 * 13.
```

Each segment's end-to-start closure is a missing edge in one of the two
occupancy-12 orbits.  Adding both closures therefore replaces the singleton
seam by two full coordinate orbits.  The reconstructed two-cycle carrier has
coordinate automorphism group **exactly** `C_13`.

Both cycles are individually equivariant (their unequal lengths already force
the group to preserve them):

* on the 1547-cycle, position shift 119 is coordinate translation `+5`;
* on the 169-cycle, position shift 13 is coordinate translation `+6`.

Thus the raw `k=13` answer is generated by two quotient templates of sizes 119
and 13, totaling 132 quotient vertices, then opened once in each orbit-cycle
and joined by one seam.  This is not a retrospective reading of a certificate;
it is forced by the raw directed-edge multiset.

## What does *not* recur

* Standard cyclic coordinate-orbit concentration is absent in the stored even
  answers and in `k=6,...,10,12,14`; their edge occupancies are diffuse.
* Equal consecutive block templates beyond the trivial one-/two-cell Johnson
  type occur globally only at `k=11`.  At `k=13` the two templates become
  visible only after the unique seam is inferred and removed.
* Entry-rank histograms of the raw compiler words vary substantially and do
  not define a stable odd/even family.
* Deeper lower-shadow hole counts vary (`k=12` even has one depth-three hole,
  while `k=13,14` do not), so only the immediate lower boundary flag is stable.

## Theorem-level construction conjecture suggested by the raw data

The `k=11` and `k=13` answers support a precise, falsifiable template:

> For odd `k=2r-1`, seek a `C_k`-invariant Johnson 2-factor on the central
> layer, specified by `binom(k,r)/k` quotient edges, whose cycles can each be
> opened once and concatenated.  The number of sacrificed lower colours is the
> number of cycles minus one; residence and all upper shadows should be imposed
> on the quotient templates before lifting.

What is proved by this audit is only that this template describes the stored
`k=11` and `k=13` carriers exactly.  The all-odd-`k` existence statement is a
conjecture.  The evidence is substantially stronger than visual regularity:
the completed carrier automorphism groups are exhaustively determined to be
exactly `C_11` and `C_13`, and the quotient sizes and seam locations are
reconstructed without certificate data.
