## 0. Result

Let `d>=2`, let

\[
 F=\{f_0,f_1,\ldots,f_d,f_{d+1}\},\qquad
 F^\circ=F\setminus\{f_0,f_{d+1}\},
\]

and let `z,a_0,a_1,a_2,a_3` and the fillers be distinct.  Suppress a fixed
core `K`.  Put

\[
 X=\{z,a_1,a_3\}.
\]

There is an active boundary rethread of the folded C8 with the following
properties.

1. Before the final binary refinement, the rethread is length neutral and
   has exactly the old rank-`r` owner chronology.
2. Refining the clipped endpoint `X` exposes, in the old phase, the complete
   ray

   \[
                         S_1(j+1),\qquad1\le j<d,
   \]

   and in the new phase the complete ray

   \[
                         S_0(j+1),\qquad1\le j<d.
   \]

   Each phase uses `d-1` pairwise-distinct physical interval cells.
3. In the canonical terminal convention

   \[
   L_0={\cal P}_0,\quad L_+={\cal P}_1,\quad
   R_0={\cal S}_0,\quad R_+={\cal S}_1,
   \]

   the old phase physically supplies the `R_+` shore and the new phase the
   `R_0` shore.  The complementary `L_0=P_0` and `L_+=P_1` occurrence banks
   are not supplied by this endpoint module.
4. Every old target-labelled background matching transports injectively.
   Its transported cells are automatically disjoint from the new suffix-ray
   cells.
5. A length-neutral three-edge surgery on the folded owner factor preserves
   all sixteen local upper support values and produces two resident paths,
   of sizes `d+2` and `7d+22`, on all `8d+24` owner vertices.  All
   `8d+22` remaining lower edge colours are distinct.

The last statement is **support**, which is the native upper requirement of
the contiguous-OR problem.  It preserves every already witnessed upper mask.
It is not a strict upper-occurrence or upper-rainbow theorem; a later
recursive selector that consumes named upper occurrences must add those
occurrences to its protected address state.

This closes one complete physical **one-ray** actuator.  It does not close a
cross matching: the complementary prefix occurrences and their cap/guard
compatible pairing remain the exact local gate left by the zero-block
reduction.

## 1. The nested boundary factorization

Choose an active endpoint label `c` and let `b` be the other member of
`{a_1,a_3}`.

At the clean endpoint orient the active owner ear so that its maximal source
prefix is `E_0,...,E_d` and has the nested form

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 E_0&=X\cup F[1,d],\\
 E_i&=\{z,c\}\cup F[1,d-i+1]
                  &&(1\le i\le d).
 \end{aligned}                                             \tag{1.1}
\]

The phase-`epsilon` endpoint rethread replaces this prefix by

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 Q_0&=X,\\
 Q_i&=\{z,c,f_{d-i+1}\}
                  &&(1\le i\le d).
 \end{aligned}                                             \tag{1.2}
\]

Equivalently, in the literal folded realization one takes the maximal screen
at position `i` inside

\[
       \{z,c,f_{d-i+1}\}.
\]

The load-bearing identity is

\[
                         E_i=\bigcup_{t=i}^d Q_t
                 \qquad(0\le i\le d).                 \tag{1.3}
\]

Consequently replacing (1.1) by (1.2) does not change any owner window.  It
is an active rethread, not a passive recut, but it costs no source position.
The finite folded audit constructs the actual factors by maximal screens and
checks the suffix-factorization identity and owner equation directly in both
phases; it does not assume that the two phase prefixes have one identical
closed display before relabelling.

Now refine the boundary letter

\[
                    Q_0=X=(\{z,b\})\cup(\{z,c\})       \tag{1.4}
\]

in the displayed far-to-near order.  This is a genuine full-block
refinement.  It adds one source position unless the split socket is already
present.  The old authenticated phase uses `c=a_3`; the relabelled module
uses `c=a_1`.  In the actual paired folded seam the second phase is checked
directly by maximal screens rather than inferred from a common closed form.

## 2. Literal addresses of the transported ray

Index the split word from zero.  Its generic beginning is

\[
 \{z,b\},\ \{z,c\},\ \{z,c,f_d\},\ldots,
 \{z,c,f_2\},\{z,c,f_1\}.                              \tag{2.1}
\]

For `1<=j<d`, put

\[
                         I^S_j=[1,d-j+1].                \tag{2.2}
\]

Then

\[
            \operatorname{OR}(I^S_j)
                 =\{z,c\}\cup F[j+1,d].                \tag{2.3}
\]

The `d-1` cells in (2.2) are pairwise distinct.  In the old phase
`c=a_3`, so they realize `S_1(j+1)`; in the new phase `c=a_1`, so they
realize `S_0(j+1)`.  Thus the old phase supplies the complete `R_+` shore
and the new phase the complete `R_0` shore of the zero-block cross gate.

The algebraic partners are still

\[
        P_0(j)\longleftrightarrow S_1(j+1),\qquad
        P_1(j)\longleftrightarrow S_0(j+1).             \tag{2.4}
\]

Their intersection and union caps are constant in `j`, but that algebra
does not supply the missing physical `P_0` and `P_1` occurrence banks.
Those must be returned from the exterior, or supplied by a second compatible
socket, before either cross matching is complete.

## 3. Exact transport of the old background matching

The active rethread is stronger than support containment.  It transports a
target-labelled matching.

Let an old interval be `[i,j]`.  On the part affected by (1.1)--(1.2), map
it first to

\[
 \phi(i,j)=
 \begin{cases}
   [i,d],&j\le d,\\
   [i,j],&j>d.
 \end{cases}                                             \tag{3.1}
\]

Identity (1.3) gives

\[
                   \operatorname{OR}_E(i,j)
                    =\operatorname{OR}_Q(\phi(i,j)).    \tag{3.2}
\]

The only collisions of `phi` come from intervals with one common start
`i` and endpoints at most `d`.  All those old intervals have the same value
`E_i`.  Therefore `phi` is injective on every target-to-cell matching:
two distinct target vertices cannot occupy two equal-valued cells.

The full-block split (1.4) has the standard injective lift

\[
 \psi(i,j)=
 \begin{cases}
   [0,j+1],&i=0,\\
   [i+1,j+1],&i>0.
 \end{cases}                                             \tag{3.3}
\]

and preserves every OR value.  Thus `psi o phi` transports the complete old
background matching.

The suffix cells `I^S_j` use the new near half of (1.4), without its far
half, and are outside the full-block image.  Their values are the
opposite-phase suffix ray and are absent from the old local source support.
Every transported background edge is therefore disjoint from all cells in
(2.2), without a new Hall calculation.

This proves the background statement at target-matching level.  It does not
preserve an arbitrary multiplicity of protected addressed occurrences: the
collisions deliberately forgotten in (3.1) have equal set value but
different old endpoints.  Such occurrence guards must be added to the
state, or their cells included in the residual finite halo.

## 4. The upper-support-safe owner forest

The active endpoint must be planted in an upper-support-safe owner factor;
a better cut of the old cycle was proved insufficient.  The required owner
surgery is explicit.

Let

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 U&=\{z,a_1,a_3\}\cup F^\circ,\\
 V&=\{z,a_1\}\cup(F\setminus\{f_0\}),\\
 W&=\{z,a_3\}\cup(F\setminus\{f_0\}),\\
 Y&=\{z,a_3\}\cup(F\setminus\{f_1\}),\\
 A&=\{z,a_0\}\cup(F\setminus\{f_{d+1}\}),\\
 B&=\{z,a_0\}\cup(F\setminus\{f_d\}).
 \end{aligned}                                             \tag{4.1}
\]

In each upper-complete folded phase, delete the three common edges

\[
                           UV,\quad WY,\quad AB            \tag{4.2}
\]

and add the edge `VW`.  All are Johnson edges.  The first deletion opens the
clean host at `U`; the second deletion creates the endpoint `Y`; and `VW`
restores exactly the upper colour lost at `UV`, because

\[
                            U\cup V=V\cup W.             \tag{4.3}
\]

The upper colour at `WY` is a sweep colour of multiplicity `d+2`; the edge
`AB` also lies in a repeated sweep colour.  Hence the operation preserves
all sixteen local upper support values.

Directly following the two resulting components gives path sizes

\[
                              d+2,\qquad7d+22.           \tag{4.4}
\]

They partition all `8d+24` owner vertices.  Every internal coordinate run
contains a full filler sweep of length `d+2`; shorter runs meet a path
endpoint.  Both paths are therefore depth-`d` resident.

The lower edge colours are distinct.  Inside one filler sweep the
intersection records its active two-label base and the adjacent omitted
filler pair.  At a connector it records a singleton/triple active signature
and an endpoint filler omission.  These types are disjoint, and the two
cuts remove the only repeated connector types.  There are `8d+22` remaining
edges and `8d+22` resulting lower colours.

The operation is length neutral at owner level.  Its two components export
one topology socket; joining them to an ambient forest is a separate global
forest-extension step, not an upper-support casualty.

## 5. Charge and exact remaining gate

The owner rethread (1.2) and owner surgery (4.2) are length neutral.  The
binary refinement (1.4) has raw charge

\[
                                  \chi=1.                \tag{5.1}
\]

One use therefore gives a literal `B+1`-scale actuator.  Serial reuse has
bounded total charge only if the split socket is retained and regenerated;
creating a fresh split for every comparator would accumulate charge.  Exact
`B` additionally needs one certified contraction elsewhere.

After this theorem, one shore of the local zero-block gate is physical.  The
remaining gate is exactly:

1. return the complementary prefix shore and put each resulting cross
   matching in one complete cap/guard state;
2. attach the two-path owner forest to the ambient chronology while
   preserving its protected external witnesses; and
3. regenerate the same split socket with bounded global `chi`.

No strict upper-rainbow condition is needed for the OR word itself.  It is
only an additional gate for recursive architectures that explicitly spend
upper occurrences.
