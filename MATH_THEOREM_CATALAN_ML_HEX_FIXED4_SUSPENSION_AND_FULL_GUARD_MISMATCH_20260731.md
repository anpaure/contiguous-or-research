# The Middle-Levels hex has a fixed-4 suspension, but not a fully private rooted/compiler absorber

Date: 2026-07-31  
Status: exact dimension-uniform local suspension and unit-absorber theorem;
exact catalogue degree, common-basis marginal-survival, root-budget, and
pointwise-cap obstructions.  No zero-defect cover-down theorem is claimed.

## 0. Verdict

The standard Boolean-incidence hexagon does have a genuine, very small
embedding in the fixed-`Q` side-slot host.  Adjoining one fixed upper
coordinate turns its two alternating matchings into a balanced
`3 <-> 3` exchange which uses the same three lower colours, the same three
upper colours, and the same six physical-owner slots in both phases.
Deleting one old atom gives a literal native `2 -> 3` unit absorber.  After
orienting every physical edge from the spoke bank to the triangle bank, the
same identity is an ordered-four-transversal identity.

This positive statement is strictly weaker than transporting the
**transparent `ML(7)` actuator**.  Middle-Levels transparency stores turn
colours computed from the external neighbours of the six ports and the
marked boundary order of retained fragments.  Those data are not resources
of the four-uniform side host.  In the displayed `ML(7)` fixture, either
possible six-coordinate suspension deletes a coordinate actually used by
an external hex port.  Moreover, the fixed-4 exchange changes the pointwise
cap at all three edited lower colours.  It therefore preserves the
existential Boolean common cap after a successful exact exchange, but it
cannot preserve a pointwise protected cap or an unverified downstream
compiler trace.

There is also a sharp obstruction to the strongest private-rooted reading.
A completely owner-private on-state consists of three isolated physical
edges and hence needs at least three seam anchors.  Pairwise owner-disjoint
gadgets therefore number at most `C/3=O(P/n)`, where
`C=Cat_(n+1)` is the entire anchor bank.  Requiring both the off and on
states to be rooted raises the local minimum to four.  Thus a linear
fully-private rooted atlas cannot exist.

What balanced common-basis marginals *do* give is exact three-row survival.
The unpunctured host contains a resource-disjoint local-hex atlas of order
at least `N/72`, which is \(\Omega(P)\).  Some synchronized common basis kills at most

\[
                 3{C\over N}|\mathcal A|=O(P/n)                 \tag{0.1}
\]

of its gadgets.  Conditional on a bulk state already exposing the off
phases and designated target boundaries, this reduces an atlas-indexed
linear defect bank to Catalan scale.  The Delcourt--Postle colouring does
not supply that alignment, the roots, or the compiler trace, so (0.1) is
not itself a cover-down theorem.

The exact remaining two-scale theorem is therefore a Catalan-size family of
possibly long, bulk-attached ears satisfying the contracted graphic/root,
slot, protected-cap and compiler-blocker rows jointly.  Since

\[
 {P\over n}={n-1\over n+2}\operatorname {Cat}_n,                 \tag{0.2}
\]

this is literally a Catalan-scale finish.

## 1. The fixed-`Q` side host

Let the ground set have order `2n`, with `n>=3`.  A side atom is

\[
 e=(D,V,(x,i),(y,j)),                                      \tag{1.1}
\]

where

\[
 |D|=n,\quad |V|=n+2,\quad D\subset V,\quad
 x\cap y=D,\quad x\cup y=V.                               \tag{1.2}
\]

The two owner slots have capacities one at seam anchors and two elsewhere.
The allowed lower bank is the rank-`n` layer with the puncture image of the
fixed common basis `Q` deleted; every rank-`(n+2)` upper colour is present.

Put

\[
 N={2n\choose n-1},\qquad P={2n\choose n-2},\qquad
 C=\operatorname {Cat}_{n+1}.                              \tag{1.3}
\]

The balanced common-basis distribution has marginal

\[
 q={C\over N}={2(2n+1)\over n(n+2)}.                        \tag{1.4}
\]

The local theorem below first lives in the unpunctured catalogue.  It is a
legal fixed-`Q` packet exactly when its three displayed lower resources
survive the puncture.

## 2. Dimension-uniform suspension of the incidence hex

Choose

\[
 H\in{[2n]\choose n-1},\qquad a,b,c,z\notin H              \tag{2.1}
\]

with `a,b,c,z` distinct.  Use indices modulo three, with
`r_0=a,r_1=b,r_2=c`, and define

\[
\begin{aligned}
 D_i&=H+r_i,\\
 V_i&=H+r_i+r_{i+1}+z,\\
 X_i&=H+r_i+z,\\
 Y_i&=H+r_i+r_{i+1}.
\end{aligned}                                             \tag{2.2}
\]

There are two phases

\[
 E^0=\{(D_i,V_i;X_i,Y_i):i\in\mathbb Z_3\},                \tag{2.3}
\]

\[
 E^1=\{(D_i,V_{i-1};X_i,Y_{i-1}):i\in\mathbb Z_3\}.       \tag{2.4}
\]

Here a semicolon suppresses the fixed occurrence-slot name at each owner.

### Theorem 2.1 (fixed-4 hex suspension)

Both `E^0` and `E^1` are three-atom matchings of the side host.  They use
exactly the same host resources:

\[
 \{D_0,D_1,D_2\},\quad \{V_0,V_1,V_2\},\quad
 \{X_0,X_1,X_2,Y_0,Y_1,Y_2\}.                       \tag{2.5}
\]

At every owner choose one legal slot and use that same slot in both phases.
The exchange then preserves every occurrence-labelled slot, including a
capacity-one anchor slot.  Orienting every edge `X_i -> Y_j` also preserves
the three tail resources and three head resources separately.  Each phase
is a three-edge physical matching and therefore a forest.

#### Proof

For (2.3),

\[
 X_i\cap Y_i=H+r_i=D_i,\qquad
 X_i\cup Y_i=H+r_i+r_{i+1}+z=V_i.                   \tag{2.6}
\]

For (2.4),

\[
 X_i\cap Y_{i-1}=H+r_i=D_i,\qquad
 X_i\cup Y_{i-1}=H+r_{i-1}+r_i+z=V_{i-1}.           \tag{2.7}
\]

The three active one-sets in the `D` bank and the three active
`{r_i,r_(i+1),z}` sets in the `V` bank are distinct.  The `X` owners contain
`z`, the `Y` owners do not, and each of the two triples is distinct.  Hence
each phase is a host matching.  Equations (2.3)--(2.4) visibly permute the
same `D,V,X,Y` resources.  The slot, ordered-role and physical assertions
follow.  `square`

The outer incidence edges in (2.3)--(2.4) are precisely a directed triangle
exchange in the sense of the fixed-fibre `C6` theorem.  This is the correct
palette-neutral side object; it is not the asymmetric physical `C6` whose
old half repeats a palette colour.

### Corollary 2.2 (native unit absorber)

Designate `e_0^0` as a target atom and put

\[
 B^-=E^0\setminus\{e_0^0\},\qquad B^+=E^1.           \tag{2.8}
\]

Then, in the free abelian group on the four host resource classes,

\[
             \operatorname {inc}(B^+)-\operatorname {inc}(B^-)
                        =\operatorname {inc}(e_0^0).             \tag{2.9}
\]

Thus `B^- -> B^+` is an exact `2 -> 3` absorber for one complete side atom.
Both states are locally slot-safe and acyclic.  If their six owners are
disjoint from the retained physical scaffold, both unions with that
scaffold are forests.

#### Proof

Theorem 2.1 gives
`inc(E^0)=inc(E^1)`.  Subtract `inc(e_0^0)` from the left phase.  `square`

This is a native fixed-4 analogue of a `2 -> 3` actuator.  It is **not** an
incidence-preserving image of the AGCF actuator of Section 6 below.

## 3. The literal `ML(7)` transparent fixture does not transport as a decorated state

The repaired `ML(7)` fixture uses

\[
 H=\{0,5\},\qquad(a,b,c)=(3,4,6),                    \tag{3.1}
\]

with lower ports `41,49,97` and upper ports `57,113,105`.  The two unused
Middle-Levels coordinates are `1,2`.  Choosing `z=1` gives the literal
six-coordinate side suspension

\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
D&41,49,97\\
V&59,115,107\\
X&43,51,99\\
Y&57,113,105.
\end{array}                                             \tag{3.2}
\]

Choosing `z=2` gives the isomorphic other suspension.  Thus the local
incidence `C6` really embeds.

However, the external neighbours of the six ports in the displayed source
cycle are

\[
\begin{array}{c|rrrrrr}
v&41&49&57&97&105&113\\ \hline
v_{\rm ext}&45&51&56&99&73&112\\
\text{changed coordinate}&2&1&0&1&5&0.
\end{array}                                             \tag{3.3}
\]

If `z=1`, coordinate `2` is absent from the side ground and the external
port at `41` is lost.  If `z=2`, coordinate `1` is absent and the external
ports at `49,97` are lost.  These external neighbours are exactly part of
the turn-colour and retained-fragment boundary data in the transparent-hex
criterion.  Therefore:

### Proposition 3.1 (local incidence is not transparent-state functoriality)

The literal `ML(7)` hex has a six-coordinate fixed-4 suspension as an
unmarked incidence circuit, but neither suspension is an embedding of its
full fixed-decoration transparent state.  In particular the ML palette and
boundary-alternation certificate cannot be used as a side compiler/root
certificate.

The obstruction is a type obstruction, not a failed search: a turn colour
at a hex vertex is computed from both of its cycle neighbours, while a
fixed-4 side atom records only its own `D,V` and two owner slots.

## 4. A linear unrooted atlas and exact marginal survival

Count oriented tuples `(H,a,b,c,z)` as in (2.1).  Their number is

\[
 T=N(n+1)n(n-1)(n-2).                                 \tag{4.1}
\]

The exact numbers of oriented tuples through one fixed resource are

\[
\begin{aligned}
 d_D&=3n^2(n-1)(n-2),\\
 d_V&=3(n+2)(n+1)n(n-2),\\
 d_X&=6n(n+1)(n-1)(n-2).
\end{aligned}                                           \tag{4.2}
\]

For `n>=4`, `d_X` is the maximum (with equality `d_X=d_V` at `n=4`).

### Proposition 4.1 (constant-support private atlas)

For `n>=4`, the unpunctured side catalogue contains at least

\[
                         \left\lceil {N\over72}\right\rceil       \tag{4.3}
\]

hex supports pairwise disjoint in all three lower resources, all three
upper resources, and all six physical owners.  At `n=3` it contains at
least one.

#### Proof

Each support has twelve typed resources.  Greedily selecting one support
deletes at most `12 max(d_D,d_V,d_X)` oriented rows, including all duplicate
descriptions of the same support.  Therefore the greedy family has order at
least

\[
 {T\over12d_X}={N\over72}                              \tag{4.4}
\]

for `n>=4`.  The same calculation with `d_V` gives a positive row at
`n=3`.  `square`

This is a host-catalogue packing.  Reserving all of it before running the
Delcourt--Postle theorem would cost linear mass; the protected-reserve
theorem only makes an `o(P)` preloaded subatlas harmless.

### Theorem 4.2 (balanced-marginal three-row survival)

Fix any atlas \(\mathcal A\) from Proposition 4.1 before choosing the
synchronized common basis.  Under the balanced common-basis distribution,
let `K(Q)` be the number of atlas gadgets for which at least one of their
three lower resources is punctured.  Then

\[
               \mathbb E K(Q)\le3{C\over N}|\mathcal A|.          \tag{4.5}
\]

Consequently some common basis satisfies the same deterministic upper
bound.  Since `C/N=O(1/n)`, all but `O(P/n)` members of a linear atlas
survive.

#### Proof

The puncture map from the common-basis ground to the physical lower layer
is injective.  A lower resource in its image is punctured with exact
marginal `C/N`; a lower resource outside the image has probability zero.
For one gadget the union bound over its three lower resources is therefore
at most `3C/N`.  Sum over the atlas and average.  No pairwise or higher
negative dependence is used.  `square`

### Conditional corollary 4.3 (what the marginal theorem would buy)

Suppose, in addition, a bulk construction is already **atlas-aligned**:
for every member of \(\mathcal A\) it contains the off state, exposes exactly
the designated target boundary when requested, and all packet phases pass
the common contracted graphic and guard rows.  Then after choosing the
common basis from Theorem 4.2, all but `O(P/n)` designated defects can be
absorbed by the surviving on states.

Atlas alignment is the missing hypothesis.  A full conflict-free colouring
does not force its chosen bulk colour to contain the two off atoms of every
surviving gadget, nor does it force its leave to equal the designated target
columns.

## 5. Exact root and cap obstructions to a fully private atlas

Let the whole six-owner support of a gadget be disjoint from the retained
bulk/scaffold owners.  In the on state its physical graph is three disjoint
edges.  Hence every component contains a seam anchor only if at least three
of the six owners are anchors.  In the off state of Corollary 2.2, two
owners are isolated and the remaining four form two edges.  Requiring both
states to be rooted needs at least four anchors, and four suffice.

### Theorem 5.1 (private-root budget)

In any owner-disjoint atlas on a fixed common basis,

\[
 \#\{\text{final-rooted gadgets}\}\le\left\lfloor {C\over3}\right\rfloor,
                                                                    \tag{5.1}
\]

and if every off/on packet boundary must itself be rooted,

\[
 \#\{\text{two-state-rooted gadgets}\}\le
                         \left\lfloor {C\over4}\right\rfloor.     \tag{5.2}
\]

Since

\[
 {C\over P}={2(2n+1)\over n(n-1)},                              \tag{5.3}
\]

both bounds are `O(P/n)`.  Thus no \(\Omega(P)\) completely owner-private
atlas can simultaneously provide the rooted/no-empty row.

This does not obstruct gadgets attached through free second slots to an
existing rooted bulk.  Such gadgets cease to be owner-private and must pass
the contracted graphic/Higgs-root condition jointly with that bulk.

There is an independent cap obstruction.  The two phase cap maps are

\[
             \pi_0(D_i)=V_i,\qquad \pi_1(D_i)=V_{i-1}.             \tag{5.4}
\]

All three values change.  By the protected-cap rigidity theorem, the packet
is incompatible with a pointwise cap pin at any edited `D_i`.  Corollary
2.2 changes the incumbent caps at its two off-state lower colours as well.
Therefore:

* the **existential local Boolean cap** is automatic once the terminal
  outer matching, slots and forest are exact;
* a **pointwise protected cap** forbids this packet on its support; and
* the later maximal-envelope/compiler cap needs a separate trace-guard or
  blocker-deadline audit.

The transparent `ML(7)` turn-colour equality proves none of the latter
trace statements.

## 6. Why the AGCF `2 -> 3` actuator is a different host object

One AGCF candidate is a `(3n+1)`-resource column: it consumes `n+1` middle
states and `n` resources on each turn shore.  The authenticated suspended
`2 -> 3` actuator has boundary `R_n` of shore sizes `(n+1,n,n)` and that
boundary is explicitly **not** an AGCF candidate.  A side atom instead has
resource signature `(1,1,2)`.

An incidence-preserving, matching-faithful map from one whole AGCF candidate
to one side atom would have to keep the `3n+1` distinct resources of that
candidate distinct; four host vertices cannot do so.  Atomizing an AGCF
path into Johnson steps does give fixed-4 atoms, but then the
one-complement-reset-per-component condition is extra global state.  The
AGCF actuator also carries no fixed-`Q` puncture, slot, root or compiler
certificate.

Thus Corollary 2.2 is a genuine native side analogue, not a transport of the
AGCF actuator.  Conversely the AGCF degree/endpoint theorem cannot be cited
as a fixed-side graphic/root theorem.

## 7. The exact Catalan-scale long-ear finish still needed

Let `M` be a side matching of order `P-h`, let `Z` be the protected old
bank, and let `F_fix` be the frozen central/seam forest with its incidences
deducted from the residual slots.  Suppose marginal survival and a
bulk-aligned constant packet layer have reduced the uncovered bank to
target columns

\[
                         T=\{t_1,\ldots,t_h\},
                 \qquad h=O(P/n)=O(\operatorname {Cat}_n).        \tag{7.1}
\]

The missing long-ear theorem must produce packets `(R_i,A_i)` and an order
with all of the following literal properties.

1. **Boundary and gain:**

   \[
   \operatorname {inc}(A_i)-\operatorname {inc}(R_i)
                         =\operatorname {inc}(t_i),
   \qquad |A_i|=|R_i|+1.                                  \tag{7.2}
   \]

   The union of the chosen `A_i` is a host matching, the old neighbourhoods
   are the declared `R_i`, and no `R_i` meets `Z`.
2. **Slots and palettes:** every whole prefix is a legal fixed-`Q` slot
   matching and the final prefix saturates both outer palettes after the
   affine slot baseline has been removed.
3. **Graphic/root row:** after deleting the chosen old shores, contract
   `F_fix` and the retained bulk.  The added physical supports are loopless
   and acyclic, obey maximum degree two, admit the required ordered
   tail/head orientation, and leave no anchor-free terminal component.
4. **Protected cap/compiler row:** every protected pointwise cap is avoided;
   all migrating caps lie in one explicitly certified compiler trace face.
   Equivalently, every pulled-back minimal compiler blocker obeys the exact
   packet deadline/self-breaking inequality at every prefix.
5. **Serialization:** every physical circuit, host collision, DP
   short-cycle blocker and compiler blocker satisfies the common deadline
   order from the protected-ear theorem.

If these rows hold, the existing protected-cover-down theorem gives a
literal size-`P` rooted physical forest with the declared cap guard.  The
new content is their all-`n` Boolean supply and correlation with the bulk.
An `O(n)`-support ear for each of `Theta(P/n)` targets has total support
`Theta(P)`, so this second layer cannot simply be preloaded as the `o(P)`
reserve of the Delcourt--Postle theorem.  It must be an integrated long-ear
cover-down or a laminar/shared-corridor construction.
