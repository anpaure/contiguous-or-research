# Complete fixed-ledger \(q=1\) trade image: point margins are the only algebraic invariants

Date: 2026-07-26

Method: pure mathematics only.

## 0. Outcome and exact boundary

Let \(2\le k\le n-2\), and put

\[
 {\cal L}=\binom{[n]}{k-2},\qquad
 {\cal M}=\binom{[n]}{k-1},\qquad
 {\cal U}=\binom{[n]}k .
\]

An edge of \(J(n,k-1)\) is a rank-two Boolean interval
\((L,U)\), with \(L\in{\cal L}\), \(U\in{\cal U}\).  Let
\(B_0,B_-,B_+\) record, respectively, its two middle endpoints, its
lower colour \(L\), and its upper colour \(U\).  Define

\[
 {\cal T}_{n,k}:=\ker_{\mathbb Z}B_0\cap\ker_{\mathbb Z}B_- .
\]

If \(P_k\) is point-versus-\(k\)-set incidence, then

\[
 \boxed{B_+({\cal T}_{n,k})=\ker_{\mathbb Z}P_k.}
 \tag{0.1}
\]

Thus the upper point margins are the complete integral algebraic
invariants under fixed-owner, fixed-lower trades.  There is no additional
linear, parity, or congruence invariant.

The proof is constructive at the signed-lattice level.  The integral
kernel of \(P_k\) is generated by upper quadrilaterals, and every such
quadrilateral has a direct four-edge preimage inside one lower-colour
clique.  It is also the difference of two coordinate-conjugates of the
complemented rooted pentagon trade when \(k\ge4\).

This does not prove connectivity of the legal Hamilton-cycle fibre.  A
signed vector \(z\in{\cal T}_{n,k}\) is legal at a cycle \(C\) only when
\(x_C+z\) is binary, two-regular, and connected.  The quadrilateral
construction need satisfy none of these conformality conditions at a
prescribed \(C\).

The exact \(q=1\) consequences are:

1. on even ground, every full-owner lower-complete Hamilton cycle has in
   its signed trade fibre an upper load with no holes and minimum possible
   repeat excess;
2. on odd ground, the fibre contains the perfectly upper-rainbow load if
   and only if the repeated-lower family is point-regular;
3. for an even-ground exact lower-rainbow saturating core, point balance
   of its owner set is necessary and algebraically sufficient for an exact
   upper-rainbow load.

The remaining gate is therefore nonlinear: conformal support,
Hamiltonicity, and availability of ported switches.

## 1. The diamond identity

Write an edge as

\[
 e=(L,U),\qquad |L|=k-2,\quad |U|=k,\quad L\subset U.
\]

If \(U\setminus L=\{a,b\}\), its endpoints are
\(X=L\cup\{a\}\) and \(Y=L\cup\{b\}\).  Define

\[
 B_0e=e_X+e_Y,\qquad B_-e=e_L,\qquad B_+e=e_U.
 \tag{1.1}
\]

For \(j\ge1\), let \(P_j\) be point-versus-\(j\)-set incidence.

### Lemma 1.1

\[
 P_{k-2}B_-+P_kB_+=P_{k-1}B_0.
 \tag{1.2}
\]

Consequently,

\[
 B_+({\cal T}_{n,k})\subseteq\ker_{\mathbb Z}P_k.
 \tag{1.3}
\]

#### Proof

For every point \(v\),

\[
 {\bf1}_{v\in L}+{\bf1}_{v\in U}
 ={\bf1}_{v\in X}+{\bf1}_{v\in Y}.
\]

This proves (1.2) column by column.  Apply it to a vector annihilated by
\(B_0\) and \(B_-\). \(\square\)

## 2. Integral generation of the point kernel

For \(A\in\binom{[n]}{k-2}\) and distinct
\(a,b,c,d\notin A\), define

\[
 q(A;a,b,c,d)
 :=e_{Aab}+e_{Acd}-e_{Aac}-e_{Abd}.
 \tag{2.1}
\]

Every point has signed multiplicity zero in (2.1).  Let \(Q_{n,k}\) be
the integral lattice generated by these quadrilaterals.

### Lemma 2.1 (square equations)

Let \(\mathbb F\) be any field.  If
\(f:\binom{[n]}k\to\mathbb F\) satisfies

\[
 f(Aab)+f(Acd)-f(Aac)-f(Abd)=0
 \tag{2.2}
\]

for every admissible choice, then

\[
 f(S)=\gamma+\sum_{i\in S}\alpha_i
 \tag{2.3}
\]

for suitable \(\gamma,\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_n\in\mathbb F\).
The space of such functions has dimension exactly \(n\), in every
characteristic.

#### Proof

For distinct \(i,j\), and a \((k-1)\)-set \(T\) avoiding them, put

\[
 \Delta_{ij}(T)=f(Ti)-f(Tj).
\]

If \(T=Aa\) and \(T'=Ab\) are adjacent in
\(J([n]\setminus\{i,j\},k-1)\), apply (2.2) with the ordered active
quadruple \((a,i,j,b)\).  It gives

\[
 f(Aai)-f(Aaj)=f(Abi)-f(Abj).
\]

The Johnson graph is connected because \(1\le k-1\le n-3\).
Therefore \(\Delta_{ij}(T)\) is independent of \(T\); call it \(d_{ij}\).

For distinct \(i,j,\ell\), choose one \((k-1)\)-set avoiding all three.
This is possible because \(k\le n-2\), and it gives

\[
 d_{ij}+d_{j\ell}=d_{i\ell}.
\]

Hence \(d_{ij}=\alpha_i-\alpha_j\) for suitable \(\alpha_i\).  Subtract
\(\sum_{i\in S}\alpha_i\) from \(f(S)\).  The resulting function is
constant on every Johnson edge, hence constant on \(J(n,k)\).  This proves
(2.3).

The parameter map \((\gamma,\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_n)\mapsto f\) has
one-dimensional kernel: all \(\alpha_i=t\) and \(\gamma=-kt\).
Its image therefore has dimension \(n\), over every field. \(\square\)

### Theorem 2.2 (integral quadrilateral generation)

For \(2\le k\le n-2\),

\[
 \boxed{Q_{n,k}=\ker_{\mathbb Z}P_k.}
 \tag{2.4}
\]

#### Proof

Let \(N=\binom nk\), \(K=\ker_{\mathbb Z}P_k\), and \(Q=Q_{n,k}\).
Over \(\mathbb Q\), the \(n\) rows of \(P_k\) are independent: a
relation \(\sum_{i\in S}\alpha_i=0\) for every \(k\)-set implies by
one-point swaps that all \(\alpha_i\) are equal, and then
\(k\alpha_i=0\).  Thus

\[
 \operatorname{rank}K=N-n.
 \tag{2.5}
\]

Fix a prime \(p\).  Lemma 2.1 says that the annihilator over
\(\mathbb F_p\) of the reduced quadrilaterals has dimension \(n\).
Their span consequently has dimension \(N-n\).

The lattice \(K\) is saturated in \(\mathbb Z^N\): if \(rx\in K\),
then \(rP_kx=0\) in torsion-free \(\mathbb Z^n\), so \(x\in K\).
Therefore \(K/pK\) embeds in \(\mathbb F_p^N\) and also has dimension
\(N-n\).  Since the quadrilateral span lies inside it, the two spaces
coincide.  Equivalently,

\[
 K=Q+pK
 \tag{2.6}
\]

for every prime \(p\).  Indeed, if \(x=q+pw\), with \(x\in K\) and
\(q\in Q\), then \(pP_kw=0\), hence \(w\in K\).

The quotient \(K/Q\) is finite by (2.5) and Lemma 2.1 over
\(\mathbb Q\).  If it were nonzero, choose a prime \(p\) dividing its
order.  Equation (2.6) would make multiplication by \(p\) surjective on
that finite group, which is impossible.  Hence \(K=Q\). \(\square\)

The all-prime step is essential: rational generation alone would not
exclude a finite-index parity obstruction.

## 3. Direct quadrilaterals and the one-copy pentagon distinction

There is a much shorter algebraic lift of (2.1) than the pentagon.

### Lemma 3.1 (direct four-edge lift)

For every quadrilateral \(q(A;a,b,c,d)\), define

\[
\begin{aligned}
 z_A(a,b,c,d)
 &:=e_{(A,Aab)}+e_{(A,Acd)}\\
 &\quad-e_{(A,Aac)}-e_{(A,Abd)}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3.1}
\]

Then

\[
 z_A(a,b,c,d)\in{\cal T}_{n,k},\qquad
 B_+z_A(a,b,c,d)=q(A;a,b,c,d).
\tag{3.2}
\]

#### Proof

All four edges have lower colour \(A\), and their signed coefficients
sum to zero.  Their middle endpoints are

\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
\text{edge}&\text{middle endpoints}\\ \hline
(A,Aab)&A+a,\ A+b\\
(A,Acd)&A+c,\ A+d\\
(A,Aac)&A+a,\ A+c\\
(A,Abd)&A+b,\ A+d.
\end{array}
\]

Each of the four owners has one positive and one negative incidence.
Thus \(B_-z_A=B_0z_A=0\), while its four upper coefficients are exactly
(2.1). \(\square\)

Combining Lemma 3.1 with Theorem 2.2 already proves the complete image
theorem for \(2\le k\le n-2\).  The direct lift has an important physical
defect: its negative shore consists of two edges with the same lower
colour \(A\).  It therefore cannot be conformal at a cycle in which every
lower colour occurs exactly once.

The complemented pentagon is relevant because both of its shores use
each affected lower colour once.  We record its relation to the same
quadrilateral lattice.

On \(D=[6]\), the two complemented rooted-pentagon path factors are

\[
\begin{array}{c|cccc}
1&456&245&235&123\\
2&356&345&234&124\\
3&346&236&126&125\\
4&246&146&136&135\\
5&256&156&145&134
\end{array}
\qquad
\begin{array}{c|cccc}
1&456&345&234&123\\
2&356&156&126&124\\
3&346&146&145&125\\
4&246&245&235&135\\
5&256&236&136&134.
\end{array}
\tag{3.3}
\]

Both tables enumerate all twenty local owners once, have the same row
ports, and use every local two-subset once as a lower intersection.  Their
upper signed difference, new minus old, is

\[
 e_{\widehat{25}}+e_{\widehat{36}}+e_{\widehat{14}}
 -e_{\widehat{15}}-e_{\widehat{34}}-e_{\widehat{26}},
\tag{3.4}
\]

where \(\widehat{ij}=D\setminus\{i,j\}\).  These facts follow by listing
the three adjacent intersections and unions in each of the five rows.
Thus the edge difference of the tables belongs to \({\cal T}_{6,4}\).

If \(M,N\) are disjoint perfect matchings of \(K_D\), define

\[
 p_D(M,N)=
 \sum_{e\in M}e_{D\setminus e}
 -\sum_{e\in N}e_{D\setminus e}.
\tag{3.5}
\]

The union of two disjoint perfect matchings on six vertices is an
alternating six-cycle.  The symmetric group is transitive on ordered such
pairs.  Hence a coordinate relabelling of (3.3), interchanging the old
and new tables if needed, realizes every \(p_D(M,N)\) as the upper image
of an integral fixed-owner, fixed-lower pentagon trade.

### Lemma 3.2 (two-pentagon formula)

Assume \(4\le k\le n-2\).  Every quadrilateral (2.1) belongs to the
upper image of the integral span of embedded complemented-pentagon trades.

#### Proof

Choose distinct \(x,y\in A\), possible because \(k\ge4\), and put

\[
 K=A\setminus\{x,y\},\qquad D=\{a,b,c,d,x,y\}.
\]

Adjoin \(K\) to every state of the local pentagon.  Its middle states now
have size \((k-4)+3=k-1\), as required.

Consider

\[
 M_1=\{ab,cd,xy\},\qquad
 M_2=\{ac,bd,xy\},\qquad
 N=\{ad,bx,cy\}.
\tag{3.6}
\]

The matching \(N\) is disjoint from both \(M_1\) and \(M_2\).
Moreover, \(M_1\cup N\) is the six-cycle

\[
 a,b,x,y,c,d,a,
\]

and \(M_2\cup N\) is the six-cycle

\[
 a,c,y,x,b,d,a.
\]

Thus \(p_D(M_1,N)\) and \(p_D(M_2,N)\), with \(K\) adjoined, are
upper images of pentagon trades.  Their difference equals

\[
\begin{aligned}
 p_D(M_1,N)-p_D(M_2,N)
 &=e_{K(D\setminus cd)}+e_{K(D\setminus ab)}\\
 &\quad-e_{K(D\setminus bd)}-e_{K(D\setminus ac)}\\
 &=e_{Aab}+e_{Acd}-e_{Aac}-e_{Abd}.
\end{aligned}
\]

This is \(q(A;a,b,c,d)\).  The difference of the two signed pentagon
trades remains in \({\cal T}_{n,k}\). \(\square\)

### Theorem 3.3 (complete upper-image theorem)

For \(2\le k\le n-2\), equation (0.1) holds.

#### Proof

Lemma 1.1 gives one inclusion.  The direct lift in Lemma 3.1 puts every
quadrilateral in the opposite image, and Theorem 2.2 says these
quadrilaterals generate the whole integral point kernel. \(\square\)

### Corollary 3.4 (the abstract pentagon catalogue has full upper span)

Assume \(4\le k\le n-2\), and let \({\cal P}_{n,k}\) be the integral
span of all embedded, coordinate-relabelled complemented-pentagon trade
vectors.  Then

\[
 B_+({\cal P}_{n,k})=\ker_{\mathbb Z}P_k.
 \tag{3.7}
\]

#### Proof

Every pentagon is a fixed-owner, fixed-lower trade, so Lemma 1.1 gives
one inclusion.  Lemma 3.2 expresses every upper quadrilateral as the
upper image of a difference of two pentagons.  Theorem 2.2 supplies the
reverse inclusion. \(\square\)

Thus even the one-copy-conformal pentagon catalogue has no global upper
linear or modular invariant beyond point margins.  Its unresolved defect
is statewise: a prescribed Hamilton cycle may contain few or no eligible
negative pentagon tables.

## 4. Complete linear and modular invariant statement

The image of point incidence is

\[
 P_k(\mathbb Z^{\cal U})
 =\left\{a\in\mathbb Z^n:
              \sum_{i=1}^na_i\equiv0\pmod k\right\}.
 \tag{4.1}
\]

Every column has coordinate sum \(k\).  Conversely, differences
\(e_i-e_j\) are obtained by subtracting two \(k\)-set columns with a
common \((k-1)\)-subset, and one column supplies total sum \(k\).

Theorem 3.3 and the first isomorphism theorem give

\[
 \boxed{
 \mathbb Z^{\cal U}/B_+({\cal T}_{n,k})
 \cong
 \left\{a\in\mathbb Z^n:\sum_i a_i\equiv0\pmod k\right\}.}
 \tag{4.2}
\]

The group on the right is free abelian of rank \(n\).  Hence no torsion
or modular invariant remains after the point margins.  Equivalently, for
any abelian group \(A\), every homomorphism
\(\Phi:\mathbb Z^{\cal U}\to A\) which vanishes on all fixed-ledger upper
trade directions factors uniquely through \(P_k\).

## 5. Consequences for cycle histograms

Let \(C\) have edge indicator \(x_C\), middle degree ledger
\(d=B_0x_C\), lower histogram \(\ell=B_-x_C\), and upper histogram
\(u=B_+x_C\).

### Corollary 5.1 (signed-fibre criterion)

For \(u^*\in\mathbb Z^{\cal U}\), the following are equivalent:

1. \(B_+z=u^*-u\) for some \(z\in{\cal T}_{n,k}\);
2. \(P_ku^*=P_ku\).

#### Proof

Necessity is Lemma 1.1; sufficiency is Theorem 3.3. \(\square\)

This describes the integral affine hull of the fixed \((d,\ell)\)-fibre,
not its binary Hamilton points.

### 5.1 Even-ground full-owner Hamilton cycles

Take \(n=2m\), \(k=m+1\), and let \(C\) use all

\[
 W=\binom{2m}m
\]

middle owners.  Put

\[
 N=\binom{2m}{m-1},\qquad E=W-N,
\]

and suppose

\[
 \ell=\mathbf1+h,\qquad h\ge0,\qquad \sum_Sh(S)=E.
 \tag{5.1}
\]

Writing \(h_v=\sum_{S\ni v}h(S)\), the diamond identity gives

\[
 P_{m+1}(u-\mathbf1)=(E-h_v)_{v\in[2m]}.
 \tag{5.2}
\]

There is a nonnegative integral upper vector \(g\) of mass \(E\) with

\[
 P_{m+1}g=(E-h_v)_v.
 \tag{5.3}
\]

Indeed, set \(q_v=E-h_v\).  Then

\[
 0\le q_v\le E,\qquad \sum_vq_v=(m+1)E.
\]

The Gale--Ryser inequalities for left degrees \((q_v)\) and \(E\) right
degrees all equal to \(m+1\) are immediate: the sum of the \(s\) largest
left degrees is at most \(sE\) for \(s\le m+1\), and at most
\((m+1)E\) for \(s\ge m+1\).  The resulting right neighbourhoods are
\(E\) possibly repeated \((m+1)\)-sets and define \(g\).

Set \(u^*=\mathbf1+g\).  By (5.2), (5.3), and Corollary 5.1, a signed
fixed-owner, fixed-lower trade sends \(u\) to \(u^*\).  This load has no
upper holes and

\[
 \sum_U(u^*(U)-1)_+=\sum_Ug(U)=E,
 \tag{5.4}
\]

the minimum forced by \(W=N+E\).  Thus the full even-ground \(q=1\)
problem is closed in the signed trade lattice, but not in the Hamilton
fibre.

### 5.2 Odd-ground full-owner Hamilton cycles

Take \(n=2m+1\), \(k=m+1\).  Here \(|{\cal U}|=W=|E(C)|\).  With the
same notation \(\ell=\mathbf1+h\),

\[
 P_{m+1}(u-\mathbf1)=(\eta-h_v)_v,\qquad
 \eta=\frac{E(m-1)}{2m+1}.
 \tag{5.5}
\]

Consequently a signed trade to exact upper rainbowness exists if and only
if

\[
 h_v=\eta\qquad(v\in[2m+1]).
 \tag{5.6}
\]

Necessity follows from (5.5), and sufficiency from Corollary 5.1.  Thus
point-regularity is the complete algebraic obstruction on odd ground.

### 5.3 Even-ground exact lower-rainbow core

Let \(C\) instead have length

\[
 N=\binom{2m}{m-1}=|{\cal U}|
\]

and use every lower colour exactly once.  If \(r_v\) counts used middle
owners containing \(v\), then

\[
 P_{m+1}(u-\mathbf1)
 =2\left(r_v-\frac N2\right)_v.
 \tag{5.7}
\]

Therefore a signed fixed-owner, lower-rainbow trade to exact upper
rainbowness exists if and only if

\[
 r_v=N/2\qquad(v\in[2m]).
 \tag{5.8}
\]

The known point-balance condition is thus algebraically sufficient, though
not sufficient for a legal Hamilton switch.

When \(m=2^a-1\), the Catalan number
\(\operatorname{Cat}_m=W/(m+1)\) is odd and
\(N=m\operatorname{Cat}_m\) is odd.  Condition (5.8) is then impossible.
Thus the known Mersenne parity obstruction is already contained in the
point margin; Theorem 3.3 produces no further congruence obstruction.

## 6. Legal motion and the surviving gate

Call \(z\in{\cal T}_{n,k}\) legal at a Hamilton cycle \(C\) when
\(x_C+z\) is again a binary connected two-factor.  In particular, the
negative support of \(z\) must occur in \(C\), and its toggle must preserve
one cycle.  These conditions are absent from the ledger equations.

### Proposition 6.1 (one-copy conformal normal form)

Let \(C,C'\) be binary cycle factors with the same middle degree ledger,
and suppose both use every lower colour exactly once.  Cancel their common
edges and colour \(E(C)\setminus E(C')\) red and
\(E(C')\setminus E(C)\) blue.  Then:

1. at every middle owner, red degree equals blue degree;
2. every touched lower colour occurs on exactly one red and one blue edge;
3. the red-blue overlay decomposes into alternating closed trails.

Conversely, toggling any binary collection with properties 1 and 2
preserves the owner and exact lower ledgers.  It gives another cycle
factor precisely when its blue edges together with the retained edges are
two-regular, and it gives a Hamilton cycle precisely when that factor is
connected.

#### Proof

The equality of owner ledgers gives Item 1 after common edges are
cancelled.  Equality of the two all-one lower histograms gives Item 2.
At every vertex, pair its incident red half-edges bijectively with its
incident blue half-edges.  Traversing an edge and then the paired
opposite-colour half-edge partitions all edge occurrences into alternating
closed trails.  The converse ledger statements follow by summing the
signed incidences, while two-regularity and connectedness are exactly the
factor and Hamilton conditions. \(\square\)

Thus the legal exact-rainbow trade space is an alternating-circuit space
with a one-copy lower-colour constraint.  This constraint is not present
in the full lattice \({\cal T}_{n,k}\).

The direct lift (3.1) is a two-edge matching switch inside the lower-colour
clique on the owners containing \(A\): it replaces the negative matching
\[
 \{(A+a)(A+c),(A+b)(A+d)\}
\]
by the positive matching
\[
 \{(A+a)(A+b),(A+c)(A+d)\}.
\]
It can be conformal only when the current cycle uses two edges of lower
colour \(A\).  Hence it is unavailable at every exact lower-rainbow cycle,
where the load of \(A\) is one.  Even when both negative edges occur, the
chosen reconnection must still be checked not to split the Hamilton cycle.

### Proposition 6.2 (legal repeated-colour two-opt)

Suppose a Hamilton cycle contains two nonadjacent edges of the same lower
colour \(A\), with four distinct middle owners

\[
 A+a,\quad A+b,\quad A+c,\quad A+d.
\]

Then one of the two alternative perfect matchings on these four owners
replaces the old two edges and produces another Hamilton cycle.  The switch
preserves every lower load and changes the upper histogram by a nonzero
quadrilateral.

#### Proof

Deleting two nonadjacent cycle edges leaves two disjoint paths whose four
ends are the displayed owners.  Of the two perfect matchings different
from the deleted one, exactly one joins the two paths into one cycle; the
other closes them separately.  All six pairs of displayed owners are
Johnson edges with common lower colour \(A\), because each owner is
\(A\) plus one point.  Hence the Hamiltonian reconnection is legal and
preserves the lower histogram.  Its old and new upper colours are the two
perfect matchings of pairs among \(a,b,c,d\), so their signed difference
is a quadrilateral of the form (2.1), which is nonzero. \(\square\)

Thus union defects are legally mobile already through a four-owner move
wherever a repeated lower colour occurs on disjoint cycle edges.  This
mechanism is absent from an exact lower-rainbow core and is not a descent
theorem: the nonlinear repeat excess may increase or decrease according
to the four current upper loads.  More exactly, if \(D_2\) is the old
two-target set and \(A_2\) the new one, then

\[
 \Delta R^+
 =|\{U\in A_2:u(U)\ge1\}|
  -|\{U\in D_2:u(U)\ge2\}|.
 \tag{6.1}
\]

The pentagon (3.3) is a genuine legal switch whenever its five old rows
occur as pairwise vertex-disjoint ported subpaths of \(C\).  The new rows
have the same port pairs and repartition the same owners, so the global
cycle is preserved.  Its upper difference (3.4) is nonzero: union defects
are not legally invariant.

If \(A\) and \(D\) are the positive and negative upper supports in (3.4),
then the exact repeat-excess change is

\[
 \Delta R^+
 =|\{U\in A:u(U)\ge1\}|
  -|\{U\in D:u(U)\ge2\}|.
 \tag{6.2}
\]

This follows because deleting one unit lowers \((u-1)_+\) exactly at a
repeated target, while adding one raises it exactly at an occupied target.

However, Lemma 3.2 realizes a quadrilateral as

\[
 z(M_1,N)-z(M_2,N),
 \tag{6.3}
\]

the difference of two signed pentagon trades on one local owner fibre.
It need not be conformal to a prescribed cycle, and the two occurrences
with common upper matching \(N\) need not share a common intermediate path
factor.  Thus (6.3) is not one certified legal Hamilton move.

The exact remaining statement is:

> **Fixed-ledger Hamilton Markov gate.**  Given a lower-complete Hamilton
> cycle \(C\) and a nonnegative target upper load \(u^*\) with
> \(P_ku^*=P_ku\), find conformal ported trades which remain in the binary
> Hamilton fibre and reach \(u^*+o(W)\), or exhibit a nonlinear component
> invariant of that legal transition graph.

Theorem 3.3 proves that no invariant of the **full signed trade lattice**
survives beyond point margins.  It does not exclude an additional linear
or modular invariant of a restricted legal move library if that library
spans a proper sublattice.  Any such invariant must arise from the
one-copy/conformal support restriction or Hamilton connectivity, rather
than from the unrestricted fixed-ledger equations.

## 7. Coefficient-one boundary

The exact proved theorem is

\[
 \text{fixed owner ledger and fixed lower ledger}
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \text{upper signed directions}=\ker_{\mathbb Z}P_{m+1}.
\]

No legal fibre-connectivity theorem, dense pentagon occurrence theorem, or
two-sided Hamilton construction is proved here.  No coefficient-one claim
is made.
