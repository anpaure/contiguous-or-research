# The saturated pivot exposes two common-root flags, but the strict private stutter needs a separate repeated-owner sidecar

**Date:** 2026-08-01  
**Status:** exact lower-face physical ray and matching; exact full-lift
incompatibility; explicit strict-stutter companion; exact conditional
strict-gammoid direct-funnel theorem.  The common physical chronology and
deletion-robust matching supply remain open.  No additive-constant bound is
claimed.

## 0. Verdict

The collared monotone pivot comes closer to the item2543L physical lift than
a set-level audit suggests, but it does not itself close that lift.

1. On the saturated aperture face \(B=X\), the pivot letter has a literal
   same-neighbour, same-type alternative

   \[
                     B\longmapsto B-\{x_R\}+\{\rho _1\}.        \tag{0.1}
   \]

   Its \(h\) lower/compiler cells are two ordered flags of one common
   rank-\(r\) root.  Thus the target-side flag alignment is exact and the
   physical cells really have paired old/new occurrence labels.

2. There is a genuine occurrence-labelled compiler positive on the
   **lower-only** face.  At a rank-saturated monotone insertion, transport
   an old strict-lower matching and retain the old providers of every fan
   target instead of releasing those targets onto the new fan.  The
   transported matching avoids the complete fan, hence avoids all \(h\)
   changed cells below, and it matches \(Q^-\) externally.  This is one
   physical coindependent ray in one fixed lower cap state.  It requires
   every retained fan value (including \(B=X\), when required) to have its
   old provider; it is not a rankwise-Hall inference.

3. This is nevertheless an **owner rethread**, not the owner- and upper-transparent
   private pull-clock stutter.  It changes one owner, one immediate-lower
   colour, and one immediate-upper colour.  The displayed pivot collar has
   no earlier reset containing both exchanged labels, so longer intervals
   ending at the pivot can change as well.

4. The same physical cells carry only the two displayed flag choices, not
   the complete Boolean containment neighbourhood required by the
   root-slot lift of item2543L.  The ordered-shift endpoint order is also
   different from the physical flag order.  Hence protected rankwise Hall
   and the sharp \(m+1\)-root reserve cannot be invoked on these cells.  A
   complete refresh-table audit also shows that no other center with full
   history inside the native \(4h+1\)-letter tight collar has a nontrivial
   owner-preserving terminal pull-clock fibre.

5. The exact missing local object can nevertheless be written explicitly.
   A separate repeated-owner sidecar using the same flag labels realizes the
   two flags as a genuine fixed-neighbour private stutter.  It changes
   exactly the \(h\) flag cells, returns every longer prefix/suffix and every
   owner/upper row, and lies in one literal two-phase cap.

6. For \(H\) such sidecars, the exact deletion-robust **adaptive-rematching**
   direct-funnel
   condition is

   \[
       D\cup F\in{\cal I}(M_\theta^*)
       \qquad\text{for every allowed protected deletion }F,   \tag{0.2}
   \]

   where \(D\) is the complete ray union and \(M_\theta^*\) is the strict
   gammoid of safe compiler-cell deletions for the **full** target shore.
   Assuming that full shore is matchable before deletion, (0.2) and
   nonadjacent sidecars give a height-one rematching funnel on their
   declared terminal bank: first externally match every terminal, then
   unmatch and toggle any requested subset.  This is
   \(\forall F\,\exists M_F\), not item2543L's one-fixed-\(M\)
   \({\rm BD}(H,1,B)\).  A fixed matching follows from the stronger test
   that \(D\cup\bigcup_{F\in{\cal F}_B}F\) is coindependent.

The transport-only matching proves the lower ray of the weak pivot rethread
is physically free.  It does **not** prove (0.2) for the auxiliary sidecar,
whose repeated-owner collar is absent from the pivot chronology.  The
protected complete-atlas theorem quarantines the corresponding flags only
abstractly.  Thus the precise remaining theorem is now cohabitation of the
explicit repeated-owner sidecars with that quarantining matching in one
chronology and cap—not discovery of the flag pair or of a lower-face
provider.

## 1. Tight split-pivot notation

Use the authoritative tight split-pivot source at depth \(h\) and owner
rank \(r\).  Write

\[
              B=C\mathbin{\dot\cup}X_L\mathbin{\dot\cup}X_R,
              \qquad X=X_L\mathbin{\dot\cup}X_R,                \tag{1.1}
\]

with \(x_L\in X_L\), \(x_R\in X_R\), and disjoint moving banks

\[
 \Lambda=(\lambda _1,\ldots,\lambda _h),\qquad
 P=(\rho _1,\ldots,\rho _h).                                  \tag{1.2}
\]

The saturated aperture face is

\[
                             C=\varnothing,\qquad B=X,
                             \qquad |B|=r-h.                    \tag{1.3}
\]

Around the pivot letter, the tight source contains

\[
       (B-\{x_R\})+\{\lambda _h\},\quad
       B,\quad
       (B-\{x_L\})+\{\rho _1\}.                                \tag{1.4}
\]

Put

\[
\begin{aligned}
 q&=M_0=B\cup\{\lambda _1,\ldots,\lambda _h\},\\
 s&=M_1=B\cup\{\rho _1,\lambda _2,\ldots,\lambda _h\}
       =q-\{\lambda _1\}+\{\rho _1\},\\
 p&=L_{h-1}=q-\{x_R\}+\{\eta\},
\end{aligned}                                                  \tag{1.5}
\]

where \(\eta=q^-\) is the left seam label.  The protected owner path
contains

\[
                             p\longrightarrow q\longrightarrow s. \tag{1.6}
\]

Every displayed owner has rank \(r\), and the full tight collar is a simple
Johnson path.

## 2. The hidden same-neighbour owner rethread

### Theorem 2.1 (literal central rethread)

On the saturated face (1.3), replace only the pivot source letter by

\[
                       \widehat B=B-\{x_R\}+\{\rho _1\}.         \tag{2.1}
\]

This is a literal same-neighbour, same-type alternative.  Its middle owner
is

\[
                       q'=q-\{x_R\}+\{\rho _1\},                \tag{2.2}
\]

and the protected local path rethreads as

\[
                       p\longrightarrow q'\longrightarrow s.  \tag{2.3}
\]

Both transitions in (2.3) are Johnson edges.

#### Proof

The exchanged labels \(x_R,\rho _1\) are absent from every predecessor age
block through depth \(h\): the tight left seam and near-left letter
deliberately omit \(x_R\), while \(\rho _1\) first appears to the right of
the pivot.  Hence replacing one by the other preserves the pivot-letter
size and every predecessor intersection count.

The next source letter in (1.4) contains both \(x_R\) and \(\rho _1\).
It therefore masks their difference in the outgoing recurrence.  This is
exactly the same-neighbour/same-type criterion.

Finally,

\[
 p=q-\{x_R\}+\{\eta\},\qquad
 q'=q-\{x_R\}+\{\rho _1\},
\]

so the first edge exchanges \(\eta\) for \(\rho _1\).  Also

\[
 s=q-\{\lambda _1\}+\{\rho _1\},
\]

so the second exchanges \(\lambda _1\) for \(x_R\).  \(\square\)

One local two-phase cap suffices at the changed source position:

\[
                    L_t=B-\{x_R\},\qquad
                    C_t=B\cup\{\rho _1\},                       \tag{2.4}
\]

with every other local source position fixed.  The old and new phase
letters in (2.1) both lie between this lower bound and cap.  This is a
local mixed common/private cap state; all ambient rows and guards must still
be added to that same state.

## 3. Exact occurrence labels: two flags of the common root \(s\)

For \(1\le j\le h\), define

\[
\begin{aligned}
 T_j
   &=s-\{\rho _1,\lambda _2,\ldots,\lambda _j\}
     =B\cup\{\lambda _{j+1},\ldots,\lambda _h\},\\
 \widehat T_j
   &=s-\{x_R,\lambda _2,\ldots,\lambda _j\}
     =T_j-\{x_R\}+\{\rho _1\}.
\end{aligned}                                                  \tag{3.1}
\]

At \(j=1\), the displayed \(\lambda\)-range is empty.  At \(j=h\),
\(T_h=B\) and \(\widehat T_h=B-\{x_R\}+\{\rho _1\}\).

### Theorem 3.1 (literal two-flag ray)

Let \(c_j\) be the physical interval ending at the pivot and having source
length \(h-j+1\).  Then

\[
          \operatorname{OR}_{\rm old}(c_j)=T_j,\qquad
          \operatorname{OR}_{\rm new}(c_j)=\widehat T_j
          \qquad(1\le j\le h).                                \tag{3.2}
\]

In particular the longest lower/compiler port is the same physical cell
\(c_1\), with

\[
        Q^-=T_1=s-\{\rho _1\},\qquad
        Q^+=\widehat T_1=s-\{x_R\}.                            \tag{3.3}
\]

Thus the lower target pair consists of two coatoms of the **common** owner
\(s\), and the whole ray consists of two ordered \(s\)-flags which differ
only in their first deleted coordinate:

\[
 (\rho _1,\lambda _2,\ldots,\lambda _h)
 \quad\longleftrightarrow\quad
 (x_R,\lambda _2,\ldots,\lambda _h).                           \tag{3.4}
\]

#### Proof

The source positions immediately before the pivot contribute, from near to
far, \(\lambda _h,\lambda _{h-1},\ldots,\lambda _2\), while their
core-bearing near letter contributes \(B-\{x_R\}\).  Union with the old
pivot letter restores \(B\); union with the new letter instead adds
\(\rho _1\) and continues to omit \(x_R\).  This gives (3.1)--(3.2).
Equation (3.3) is the case \(j=1\).  \(\square\)

If \(C\ne\varnothing\), Theorem 2.1 still gives a same-neighbour rethread,
but the one-letter cell has value \(X\), of rank strictly below
\(r-h=|B|\).  The ray is then not the full triangular depth-\(h\) flag of
one rank-\(r\) root.  Saturation (1.3) is therefore necessary for the
rooted-flag identification (3.4).

### Theorem 3.2 (one fixed lower matching avoids the physical ray)

Let \(M_0\) be an old strict-lower target matching at the rank-saturated
cut, and let \(\Phi(M_0)\) be its monotone-insertion transport.  Assume the
inserted source and both rethread phases are admitted in one lower cap/guard
state using (2.4).  Do **not**
release the logical fan targets onto the insertion-born cells; retain their
old providers.  Assume every required fan value, including the singleton
\(B=X\) when it belongs to the target shore, already has its \(M_0\)-edge.
Then \(\Phi(M_0)\):

1. saturates the same strict-lower target shore;
2. avoids the complete insertion fan and hence every occurrence
   \(c_1,\ldots,c_h\) in (3.2); and
3. matches \(Q^-=T_1\) at an occurrence outside that ray.

The same matching is valid in both phases of Theorem 2.1 on the lower
compiler band.

#### Proof

The monotone insertion theorem identifies the complete fan as the
complement of the transported old-cell image.  The only old width-\(h\)
crossings expelled from the band have middle rank \(r\), so a strict-lower
matching uses none of them.  Therefore every edge of \(M_0\) transports and
the image matching is disjoint from the entire fan.  The old-phase cells
\(c_j\) are precisely its singleton/prefix-ray part, so they are all free,
while the retained old provider of \(Q^-\) transports outside the fan.

Changing the pivot source letter alters exactly those \(h\) lower-band
addresses.  Since the matching uses none of them, all its target--cell
equalities survive the alternative phase. \(\square\)

This theorem is occurrence-labelled and stronger than separate rankwise
Hall.  Its scope is also exact: if \(B=X\) is a genuinely new required
target with no old provider, the singleton fan cell cannot be quarantined;
and the theorem says nothing about the owner, upper, or longer-prefix rows
changed in the next section.

## 4. Exact owner and palette collateral

The rethread is not transparent.  Its adjacent lower colours change as

\[
\begin{array}{rclcrcl}
 p\cap q&=&q-\{x_R\},&&p\cap q'&=&q-\{x_R\},\\
 q\cap s&=&q-\{\lambda _1\},&&q'\cap s
   &=&q-\{x_R,\lambda _1\}+\{\rho _1\},
\end{array}                                                    \tag{4.1}
\]

and its adjacent upper colours change as

\[
\begin{array}{rclcrcl}
 p\cup q&=&q+\{\eta\},&&p\cup q'
   &=&q-\{x_R\}+\{\eta,\rho _1\},\\
 q\cup s&=&q+\{\rho _1\},&&q'\cup s&=&q+\{\rho _1\}.
\end{array}                                                    \tag{4.2}
\]

Hence its literal signed interface is exactly:

* owner current \(e_{q'}-e_q\);
* lower current \(e_{Q^+}-e_{Q^-}\);
* upper current
  \[
  e_{q-\{x_R\}+\{\eta,\rho _1\}}-e_{q+\{\eta\}}.               \tag{4.3}
  \]

All other immediate rows at the two-edge rethread are unchanged.  Since the
tight pivot's owner, lower, and upper local palettes are injective, the
three deleted values in this interface have no second provider inside the
packet.  An ambient carrier must preserve or recreate them explicitly.

There is also no left reset containing both \(x_R\) and \(\rho _1\) in the
displayed pivot collar.  Both labels are absent from all \(h\) source
positions before the pivot.  Consequently intervals ending at the pivot
and longer than the lower/compiler ray can continue to change until an
external occurrence of both labels is met.  The local cap (2.4) therefore
does not by itself return arbitrary-width prefix guards.

This is the precise distinction:

\[
\boxed{\text{same-neighbour lower flag rethread}
       \ne
       \text{owner-/upper-/boundary-transparent private stutter}.} \tag{4.4}
\]

## 5. Why the Boolean root-slot theorem still does not apply

Definition 3.1 of the item2543L root-slot lift requires one physical cell
\(\iota_1(s)\) adjacent to **every** coatom of \(s\).  On the same-type,
rank-\((r-1)\) trace slice, the minimal cap (2.4) gives the physical port
\(c_1\) only the two values in (3.3):

\[
 N_\theta(c_1)\cap\binom{[k]}{r-1}
       =\{s-\{\rho _1\},\,s-\{x_R\}\}.                         \tag{5.1}
\]

For example \(s-\{\lambda _2\}\) is absent: the source interval \(c_1\)
contains the fixed coordinate \(\lambda _2\).  Therefore

\[
                          c_1\ne\iota_1(s).                    \tag{5.2}
\]

The deeper cells similarly realize the two flags (3.4), not all
rank-restricted subsets of \(s\).  Thus the ray is a physical
**two-flag column**, not a Boolean root-slot column.

There is a second order mismatch.  The owner portal

\[
                       q\longrightarrow q'=q-\{x_R\}+\{\rho _1\}
                                                                    \tag{5.3}
\]

would use an ordered-shift flag at \(q\) whose first deleted label is
\(x_R\).  The actual old physical ray at the pivot is the \(q\)-flag whose
first deleted label is \(\lambda _1\).  Equivalently, when viewed at the
common root \(s\), the two physical orders begin with \(\rho _1\) and
\(x_R\).  Since \(x_R\in B\) and \(\lambda _1\in\Lambda\) are distinct,
the robust ordered-endpoint theorem and the physical ray do not refer to
the same root/order/occurrence.

This is an occurrence-labelled failure.  Matching the same target masks at
other roots does not repair (5.1)--(5.3).

### Proposition 5.1 (the tight collar has no internal strict pull-clock site)

The obstruction is not confined to the central pivot letter.  Under the
standard hypothesis that all moving, donor, and seam labels are mutually
distinct and lie outside \(B\), index the \(4h+1\) letters of the
authoritative tight source (4.17) by
\(a_0,\ldots,a_{4h}\).  Abbreviate

\[
\begin{aligned}
 A^-&=(B-x_R)+\lambda_h, & A^+&=(B-x_L)+\rho_1,\\
 P^-&=(B-x_R)+q^-,       & P^+&=(B-x_L)+q^+,\\
 E^-&=(C\cup X_L)+d^-_{h-1},
 &E^+&=(C\cup X_R)+d^+_1.
\end{aligned}                                                   \tag{5.4}
\]

For an internal center \(h\le t\le4h-1\), put

\[
 Z^{t-1}_{h-1}
   =a_{t-h}\setminus\bigcup_{u=t-h+1}^{t-1}a_u,
 \qquad
 S_t=Z^{t-1}_{h-1}\cap a_{t+1}.                              \tag{5.5}
\]

The complete refresh table is

\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
\text{center }a_t&S_t\\ \hline
\lambda_j\ (1\le j\le h-1)&\varnothing\\
A^-&X-\{x_R\}\subseteq A^-\\
X&\varnothing\\
A^+&\varnothing\\
\rho_j\ (2\le j\le h)&\varnothing\\
P^+&C\cup X_R\subseteq P^+\\
E^+,\ d^+_j\text{ when internal}&\varnothing.
\end{array}                                                    \tag{5.6}
\]

In particular every \(S_t\) is empty or is wholly selected by the current
newborn \(a_t\).  A nontrivial owner-preserving terminal pull-clock fibre
would require two distinct equal-cardinality subsets of \(S_t\), equivalently

\[
                     0<|a_t\cap S_t|<|S_t|.                   \tag{5.7}
\]

Therefore no internal center of the tight collar carries such a fibre.

#### Proof

Formula (5.5) is the last-age cell at time \(t-1\) intersected with the
next newborn.  Along the singleton banks the entering and successor labels
are distinct, giving the empty rows.  At \(\lambda_{h-1}\), the raw
intersection \(E^-\cap A^-=C\cup X_L\) has already been refreshed by
\(P^-\).  At \(A^-\), the entering letter is \(P^-\), the intervening
lambda singletons are disjoint, and the successor is \(X\), giving
\(X-x_R\).  The central and \(A^+\) rows are disjoint.  At \(\rho_h\),
the raw intersection \(X\cap P^+=X-x_L\) has already been refreshed by
\(A^+\).  At \(P^+\), the entering letter is \(A^+\), the intervening rho
singletons are disjoint, and the successor is \(E^+\), giving
\(C\cup X_R\).  The remaining right-exterior successors are fresh
singletons.  This proves (5.6), and (5.7) is the fixed-size pull-clock fibre
criterion. \(\square\)

The boundary centers \(0,\ldots,h-1\), the final center \(4h\), and
exterior centers whose histories overlap the block depend on external
letters and are not covered by Proposition 5.1.  The companion in Section 6
is precisely such an auxiliary boundary/repeated-owner construction; it is
not hidden elsewhere in the native tight block.

## 6. The strict private companion sidecar

The target-side alignment (3.4) can be physicalized at a **separate**
repeated-owner occurrence.

### Theorem 6.1 (explicit strict-stutter companion)

Keep the saturated labels above.  At source times \(t-h,\ldots,t+1\), use

\[
\begin{aligned}
 a_{t-h}&=\{x_R,\rho _1\},\\
 a_{t-h+i}&=\{\lambda _{i+1}\}
                 &&(1\le i\le h-1),\\
 a_t&=B
       \quad\longleftrightarrow\quad
       \widehat a_t=B-\{x_R\}+\{\rho _1\},\\
 a_{t+1}&=\{x_R,\rho _1\}.
\end{aligned}                                                  \tag{6.1}
\]

Then the two alternatives at \(t\):

1. have the same predecessor and successor age states and the same type;
2. have the same middle and next owner, both equal to \(s\);
3. change exactly the \(h\) interval cells ending at \(t\) of lengths
   \(1,\ldots,h\), with old/new values \(T_j,\widehat T_j\) from (3.1);
4. leave every longer interval and every interval containing \(t+1\)
   unchanged; and
5. lie in the single local cap (2.4), with all other positions fixed.

Thus (6.1) is a literal owner-, upper-, and boundary-transparent private
stutter carrying the coatom transfer (3.3).

#### Proof

The left reset at \(t-h\) places \(x_R,\rho _1\) in one predecessor age
cell.  Swapping them in the newborn letter preserves every predecessor
intersection count and the owner.  The right reset at \(t+1\) masks the
difference in the outgoing recurrence.

Both owner windows have union

\[
 B\cup\{\rho _1,\lambda _2,\ldots,\lambda _h\}=s,              \tag{6.2}
\]

in either phase.  The suffix singleton order gives exactly the unions
(3.1).  Intervals of length at least \(h+1\) ending at \(t\) contain the
left reset, and intervals continuing past \(t\) contain the right reset;
both resets contain both exchanged labels.  Hence no other interval
changes.  The cap assertion is immediate from (2.4).  \(\square\)

The sidecar is local and literal, but its displayed support occupies an
\((h+2)\)-position collar.  A global carrier must reuse or embed this
collar; appending a fresh copy per task would cost \(\Theta(Hh)\) source
positions and would not prove an additive constant.

## 7. Exact strict-gammoid adaptive direct-funnel criterion

Let \(P_1,\ldots,P_H\) be occurrence-disjoint copies of Theorem 6.1 in one
fixed mixed cap/trace state \(\theta\), with nonadjacent centers and all
external guards returned.  Let \(R_i\) be the \(h\)-cell ray of \(P_i\),
put

\[
                            D=\bigcup_{i=1}^H R_i,              \tag{7.1}
\]

and let \(Q_i^-,Q_i^+\) be their old/new coatom ports.  Require the
\(2H\) port target IDs to be pairwise distinct.  Let \({\cal T}\) be the
full intended target shore, and let \(M_\theta\) be the cell transversal
matroid of the full background compiler.  Assume the baseline compiler is
complete:

\[
                    r_{M_\theta}({\cal C})=|{\cal T}|.         \tag{7.2}
\]

Let \({\cal F}_B\) be a declared family of protected cell-deletion sets,
each of size at most \(B\).

Write
\({\rm BD}^{\rm rem}_{\mathbf Q^+}(H,1,{\cal F}_B)\) for the following
bank-restricted adaptive property: for every \(F\in{\cal F}_B\), one may
choose a target-saturating background matching \(M_F\) in the surviving
cap graph, after which every subset of the declared terminal bank
\(\mathbf Q^+=(Q_1^+,\ldots,Q_H^+)\) has mutually disjoint direct
sidecar augmentations.  The cap state is fixed; the matching may depend on
\(F\).

### Theorem 7.1 (deletion-robust height-one funnel)

Assume every site itself and every returned guard survives every
\(F\in{\cal F}_B\).  Then the \(H\) sidecars form the direct,
deletion-robust rematching funnel

\[
                  {\rm BD}^{\rm rem}_{\mathbf Q^+}
                       (H,1,{\cal F}_B)                        \tag{7.3}
\]

if

\[
 \boxed{\quad
       r_{M_\theta^*}(D\cup F)=|D\cup F|
       \qquad(F\in{\cal F}_B).
       \quad}                                                   \tag{7.4}
\]

#### Proof

By (7.2) and the dual-transversal theorem, (7.4) gives for every \(F\) a
matching of all targets in \({\cal T}\) into cells outside \(D\cup F\).
It therefore matches every \(Q_i^-\) and \(Q_i^+\) externally and leaves
every sidecar port and shorter ray cell free.

For any designated subset \(I\subseteq[H]\), delete from this matching the
external edges of \(Q_i^+\), \(i\in I\).  Toggle precisely those
nonadjacent private sites and assign their distinct new ports to the now
unmatched \(Q_i^+\).  All other targets retain their external edges.
Theorem 6.1 returns the owner, upper, boundary, and cap state, so these are
\(|I|\) disjoint direct paths.  This proves (7.3); no claim is made for
holes outside the bank. \(\square\)

This is an exact strict-gammoid theorem, not an unproved \(O(1)\) statement.
Its quantifiers are \(\forall F\,\exists M_F\).  Item2543L's unqualified
\({\rm BD}(H,L,B)\) instead fixes one matching \(M\) and its network
\(\Gamma_M\) before the deletions, so (7.4) must not be cited as that
stronger property.

If a child construction injects the complete sidecar contexts, cap state,
allowed deletion family, and the coindependence certificates in (7.4), then
the same direct paths prove a zero-stretch lift of the **adaptive** state
with \(L=1\).  The current Pascal/pivot theorems do not supply that
injection.

### Corollary 7.2 (exact replicated finite-bank bound)

Suppose each of \(H\) declared transfers has \(B+1\) occurrence-disjoint
copies of (6.1), and all copy rays together satisfy (7.4).  Allow a broader
resource-deletion set \(G\), \(|G|\le B\), provided its compiler-cell
projection is some \(F(G)\in{\cal F}_B\), every deletable resource vertex
is incident with at most one copy, and every copy not incident with \(G\)
retains all its site/guard data.  Shared immutable cap data are outside this
deletion family.

Then at least one copy of every transfer survives.  Apply Theorem 7.1 with
\(F(G)\) and choose one survivor per transfer to obtain

\[
                  {\rm BD}^{\rm rem}_{\mathbf Q^+}
                       (H,1,{\cal F}_B)
\]

on the declared terminal bank.

This is the sharp elementary replication bound: \(B\) deletions can kill
\(B\) private copies, so \(B\) copies would not suffice without an
additional shared relocation route.  The corollary is still conditional on
placing the \(H(B+1)\) copies and their one-cap coindependence; it is not an
assertion that the pivot chronology contains them.

### Corollary 7.3 (one-fixed-matching strengthening)

Put \(F_\cup=\bigcup_{F\in{\cal F}_B}F\).  If

\[
 r_{M_\theta^*}(D\cup F_\cup)=|D\cup F_\cup|,                \tag{7.5}
\]

then one target-saturating matching avoids \(D\cup F_\cup\) and therefore
survives every allowed deletion.  Using that one matching in the proof of
Theorem 7.1 gives the fixed-matching, declared-terminal-bank property with
height \(L=1\).

Condition (7.5) is exact for the existence of one background matching
avoiding all possibly deleted cells at once.  It remains terminal-bank
restricted; it does not provide item2543L's paths to arbitrary holes.

## 8. What the existing matching theorems do and do not give

The frozen pivot matching theorem admits two different target assignments.
Its advertised complete packet releases the ray targets and possibly \(X\)
onto the insertion-born fan; that assignment does not avoid the ray.  But
Theorem 3.2 uses the equally literal transport-only assignment: retain every
fan target on its old provider and release none of them.  At the
rank-saturated cut this gives one matching avoiding the entire fan in both
lower phases.  If \(X\) is genuinely fresh and has no exterior provider,
the transport-only choice is impossible and deleting the complete fan
already violates Hall on the singleton target \(\{X\}\).

This lower positive belongs to the weak pivot occurrence.  It does not
quarantine the auxiliary sidecar of Section 6, whose ray occupies different
source addresses in a repeated-owner collar, and it does not return the
owner/upper/long-prefix collateral of Section 4.  Those rows must be solved
in the same cap, rather than by taking a union of the two marginal
matchings.

The protected complete-atlas theorem gives the needed sidecar a stronger
**static** statement.
The old flag in (3.4) is one prepared flag at root \(s\).  Up to \(m+1\)
**pairwise-distinct** prepared roots can be quarantined while all named
targets are assigned to flexible-root occurrences.  Hence a bounded bank of the sidecar rays is
coindependent in the static occurrence matroid.  What remains unproved is
that those flexible providers and the literal sidecar source collars coexist
in one physical chronology and the one cap state \(\theta\) required by
(7.4).  Duplicate-root extra occurrences are not supplied by the
one-token-per-root static atlas theorem.

The Boolean root-slot theorem does not bridge this last row because (5.1)
fails.  Rankwise Hall sees the target flags but forgets their physical
occurrence addresses.

## 9. Sharp smallest calibration

Take

\[
               h=2,\qquad r=4,\qquad
               B=X=\{x_L,x_R\}.                               \tag{9.1}
\]

The authoritative tight pivot source has nine letters and seven pairwise
distinct owners.  Put

\[
 q=B+\{\lambda _1,\lambda _2\},\qquad
 s=B+\{\rho _1,\lambda _2\}.                                  \tag{9.2}
\]

The weak central rethread changes

\[
       B\longmapsto \{x_L,\rho _1\},\qquad
       q\longmapsto q-\{x_R\}+\{\rho _1\},                     \tag{9.3}
\]

and its physical longest lower cell changes

\[
              B+\{\lambda _2\}
       \longmapsto
              \{x_L,\rho _1,\lambda _2\}.                      \tag{9.4}
\]

These are \(s-\rho _1\) and \(s-x_R\).  The coatom
\(s-\lambda _2=B+\rho _1\) is absent at that physical cell because
\(\lambda _2\) is forced in its interval.  Thus the root-slot neighbourhood
already fails in the first admissible split-pivot instance.

The strict companion is the four-letter phase pair

\[
 \{x_R,\rho _1\},\quad
 \{\lambda _2\},\quad
 B\ \longleftrightarrow\ \{x_L,\rho _1\},\quad
 \{x_R,\rho _1\}.                                             \tag{9.5}
\]

Its two consecutive depth-two owners are both \(s\), and its two changed
cells are exactly (9.4) together with
\(B\leftrightarrow\{x_L,\rho _1\}\).  This is the minimal literal repair of
the occurrence mismatch.

## 10. Exact remaining theorem

The physical-lift frontier is now:

1. plant a bounded bank of the explicit repeated-owner sidecars (6.1) inside
   the same chronology as the protected pivot packets, reusing rather than
   appending their \(\Theta(h)\)-position collars;
2. realize the static flexible-provider quarantine in that same mixed cap
   state, equivalently prove (7.4) for every protected deletion;
3. preserve the external endpoint, residence, owner, and palette guards
   while embedding the sidecars.

The pivot itself supplies the exact two-flag target algebra **and** one
transported lower matching avoiding that ray, but not the owner/upper return
or the sidecar cohabitation.  Theorem 7.1 shows that once the latter is
supplied, the adaptive-rematching height is already the optimal constant
\(L=1\); Corollary 7.2 gives the literal finite robustness budget
\(H(B+1)\) private copies.  Corollary 7.3 records the stronger union-safe
test needed for one fixed matching.  No further alternating-path theorem is
needed on either direct face, but no unconditional zero-stretch recursion
follows until those copies (or an equivalent root-fibred lift) coexist with
the physical carrier and its fixed cap.
