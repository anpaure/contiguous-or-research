# Quota resets: lossless copy relabelling, the exact reset ledger, and the packet-closure obstruction

Date: 2026-07-26

Method: pure mathematics only.

## 0. Statement of the result

Put

\[
 n=2m,\qquad \mathcal M=\binom{[n]}m,\qquad
 W=|\mathcal M|=\binom{2m}m,
\]

and, for \(0\le q\le H\),

\[
 V_q=\binom{[n]}{m-q},\qquad N_q=|V_q|,
 \qquad \lambda_q={W\over N_q}=c_q+\alpha_q,
\tag{0.1}
\]

where \(c_q=\lfloor\lambda_q\rfloor\) and
\(0\le\alpha_q<1\).

There are three distinct questions which must not be conflated.

1. Can the floor/ceiling target copies be transported through all ranks,
   allowing the top copy to be relabelled whenever \(c_q\) increases?
2. Can the resulting flags be made to obey one common run vector?
3. Can the flags be grouped into an owner-disjoint \(H\)-safe Johnson
   cycle factor?

The first question has a completely positive integral answer.

> **Lossless reset theorem.**  There are exactly \(W\) integral Boolean
> deletion chains
> \[
> X=L_0(X)\supset L_1(X)\supset\cdots\supset L_H(X),
> \qquad X\in\mathcal M,                                      \tag{0.2}
> \]
> one rooted at every middle owner, such that
> \[
> \#\{X:L_q(X)=S\}\in\{c_q,c_q+1\}
> \qquad(S\in V_q)                                           \tag{0.3}
> \]
> simultaneously for every \(q\le H\).  At a quota reset the copy labels
> may be reassigned on the crossing arcs.  This costs no chain and no
> target occurrence.  The analogous upper flags exist by complementation.

Thus a quota reset has **zero intrinsic target cost**.  The old top copy
must be absorbed into the new base copies; it must not be discarded.

There is nevertheless a sharp cost for a restart rule which refuses this
relabeling.  Let

\[
 \mathcal R_H=\{q\le H:c_q>c_{q-1}\}.                           \tag{0.4}
\]

As long as every crossing is a one-level crossing, put

\[
 \delta_q={\lambda_q-\lambda_{q-1}\over\lambda_{q-1}}
          ={2q-1\over m-q+1}.                                  \tag{0.5}
\]

If the identity of the top copy is preserved across a reset, then at
least

\[
 \alpha_{q-1}N_{q-1}-\alpha_qN_q                               \tag{0.6}
\]

old top-copy chains must terminate whenever this number is positive.
Uniformly in the regime

\[
 \lambda_H\to\infty,qquad
 \Delta_H:=\lambda_H\delta_H=o(1),                             \tag{0.7}
\]

the aggregate termination demand is

\[
 (1-o(1))W\log\lambda_H.                                      \tag{0.8}
\]

So literal fresh-copy restart is much worse than \(o(W)\).  Relabelling
is mathematically mandatory.

If instead one charges only the one-bit *fringe* needed to rewrite the
old top/base representation into the new top/base representation, then
the exact reset charge at \(q\in\mathcal R_H\) is

\[
 F_q=(1-\alpha_{q-1})N_{q-1}+\alpha_qN_q.                       \tag{0.9}
\]

In the same one-level regime,

\[
 {W\delta_q\over1+\delta_q}\le F_q\le W\delta_q,                \tag{0.10}
\]

and hence

\[
 \sum_{q\in\mathcal R_H}F_q
 =(1+o(1)){2H\lambda_H\over m}\,W.                            \tag{0.11}
\]

Thus even fringe-discarding is \(o(W)\) exactly in the range

\[
                         H\lambda_H=o(m),                       \tag{0.12}
\]

up to the endpoint assumptions in (0.7).  At the first scale where
\(\lambda_H(2H/m)\asymp1\), namely

\[
 H^2={m\over2}\bigl(\log m-\log\log m+O(1)\bigr),              \tag{0.13}
\]

the accumulated fringe is already \(\Theta(W)\).  A construction through
that scale must transport the fringe; it cannot pay for it as leave.

The second and third questions are not consequences of the lossless
reset theorem.  In fact there is an exact projection obstruction.

> **Reset-state projection theorem.**  Adjoining quota-level, reset, or
> pair-radius labels to safe \(H\)-windows does not enlarge the projection
> of the integral root-and-memory circulation onto physical Johnson
> windows.  Every integral projected solution is still generated by one
> owner permutation \(P\), and its flags still obey
> \[
> R_j=R_1^j,\qquad
> a_t(PX)=a_{t+1}(X),\qquad b_t(PX)=b_{t+1}(X).                  \tag{0.14}
> \]
> For a fixed owner-disjoint packet catalogue, reset labels merely refine
> its columns.  They do not change a packet cycle's physical target
> incidence vector, and therefore cannot repair a Hall deficit, a target
> collision, or a failure of owner-disjoint cycle selection.

This is a genuine strict gap.  For \(m=H=2\), the layered flow (0.2)--(0.3)
exists and arbitrary resets are allowed, but no integral \(2\)-safe owner
permutation exists: safety would force \(P^2X=X^c\), while complementation
on the six middle owners is three transpositions and cannot be a
permutation square.  For \(m=3,H=2\), exact balanced depth-two point
margins are already impossible because every singleton load is
\(10-2R_v\).

Consequently there is no theorem which converts the common-run arithmetic
plus an epochwise pair-radius menu into owner-disjoint packet cycles merely
by adding reset states.  The reset problem itself is solved losslessly;
the remaining missing theorem is the integral physical packet/chronology
projection.  The finite obstructions do not prove an asymptotic
constant-one no-go with \(o(W)\) error, and no such claim is made.

## 1. Exact lossless reset flow

Form the layered Boolean deletion network with vertex set

\[
                         \{s,t\}\cup\bigcup_{q=0}^HV_q.         \tag{1.1}
\]

Split every \(S\in V_q\) into \(S^{\rm in},S^{\rm out}\), join them by
a node arc, and put the integral bounds

\[
 c_q\le f(S^{\rm in},S^{\rm out})\le c_q+1.                    \tag{1.2}
\]

For \(q=0\), these bounds are both one.  Join \(s\) to every
\(X\in V_0=\mathcal M\) with fixed flow one.  For every Boolean deletion
\(T=S-a\), join \(S^{\rm out}\) to \(T^{\rm in}\) with unbounded
capacity.  Join every node at level \(H\) to \(t\), and require total
flow \(W\).

### Theorem 1.1 (integral all-depth floor/ceiling flags)

The network has an integral feasible flow.  Its unit-path decomposition
gives (0.2)--(0.3).

#### Proof

There is a symmetric fractional flow.  Give every node at level \(q\)
the load

\[
                              f_q(S)={W\over N_q}=\lambda_q.     \tag{1.3}
\]

Split this equally among its \(m-q\) deletion arcs.  Thus every arc
\(S\to S-a\) carries

\[
                              {W\over N_q(m-q)}.                 \tag{1.4}
\]

A node \(T\in V_{q+1}\) has \(m+q+1\) parents.  Its incoming flow is

\[
 {W(m+q+1)\over N_q(m-q)}={W\over N_{q+1}},                     \tag{1.5}
\]

because

\[
 N_{q+1}=N_q{m-q\over m+q+1}.                                  \tag{1.6}
\]

The node load (1.3) lies in the interval (1.2).  Hence the fractional
flow is feasible.

After the standard lower-capacity reduction, this is an ordinary directed
network with integral capacities and supplies.  Its matrix is totally
unimodular, so it has an integral feasible flow.  The source arcs have
flow one, and therefore unit-path decomposition gives exactly one path
from every middle owner.  The node bounds give (0.3). \(\square\)

The proof makes no reference to the fractional parts \(\alpha_q\).
Therefore integer crossings of \(\lambda_q\) cause no loss.  On each
integral unit path label the copy at a node of load \(d\) by an element of
\([d]\).  Labels may be reassigned independently on every network arc.
At a reset this absorbs the old top label into the enlarged base alphabet
\([c_q]\) and creates the new optional label \(c_q+1\).  The quota-control
alphabet through depth \(H\) has size at most \(c_H+1\), so this is a
finite-state reset rule for each fixed \((m,H)\).

More precisely, define
\(U_q(X)=[n]\setminus L_q(X^c)\) from the chain rooted at \(X^c\).
These are the corresponding upper flags with the same floor/ceiling
loads.  The lower and upper flags can be paired at their common root,
since the deleted letters lie in \(X\) and the inserted letters lie in
\(X^c\).  Explicitly,

\[
 X_q=L_q(X)\cup\bigl(U_q(X)\setminus X\bigr)                    \tag{1.7}
\]

changes by one deletion from \(X\) and one insertion from \(X^c\) at
each step; neither word repeats a letter.  Thus (1.7) is one safe
length-\(H\) Johnson path from every root.  It does **not** make the
suffix at its first successor equal to the selected path rooted there;
that is the separate memory equation treated in Section 4.

## 2. Exact reset accounting

The exact adjacent ratio is

\[
 {\lambda_q\over\lambda_{q-1}}
 ={N_{q-1}\over N_q}
 ={m+q\over m-q+1}=1+\delta_q,                                  \tag{2.1}
\]

with \(\delta_q\) as in (0.5).  Put

\[
                         \Delta_q=\lambda_q-\lambda_{q-1}
                                  =\lambda_{q-1}\delta_q.       \tag{2.2}
\]

Suppose \(q\) is a reset and \(\Delta_q<1\).  Then exactly one integer is
crossed.  Write

\[
 \alpha_{q-1}=1-\varepsilon_q,qquad
 \alpha_q=\Delta_q-\varepsilon_q,qquad
 0\le\varepsilon_q\le\Delta_q.                                \tag{2.3}
\]

### Lemma 2.1 (top-copy preservation is expensive)

If an old top-labelled chain may continue only as a new top-labelled
chain, at least

\[
 D_q:=\bigl(\alpha_{q-1}N_{q-1}-\alpha_qN_q\bigr)_+            \tag{2.4}
\]

chains terminate at reset \(q\).  If
\(\max_{q\le H}\Delta_q=o(1)\), then uniformly over all resets,

\[
                         D_q=(1-o(1)){W\over k_q},               \tag{2.5}
\]

where \(k_q=c_q\) is the crossed integer.

#### Proof

There are \(\alpha_{q-1}N_{q-1}\) old top copies and only
\(\alpha_qN_q\) new top slots, proving (2.4).  At the crossing,
\(\lambda_{q-1}=k_q+O(\Delta_q)\) and
\(\lambda_q=k_q+O(\Delta_q)\).  Equation (2.3) gives
\(\alpha_{q-1}=1-o(1)\) and \(\alpha_q=o(1)\), uniformly.  Since
\(N_i=W/\lambda_i\), (2.5) follows. \(\square\)

If \(K=\lfloor\lambda_H\rfloor\to\infty\) and
\(\Delta_H=o(1)\), every crossing through \(K\) is a one-level crossing.
Summing (2.5) gives

\[
 \sum_{q\in\mathcal R_H}D_q
 =(1-o(1))W\sum_{k=2}^K{1\over k}
 =(1-o(1))W\log\lambda_H.                                     \tag{2.6}
\]

This proves (0.8).  It is the exact reason that the old top copy must be
renamed as a base copy.

### Lemma 2.2 (fringe identity)

At a one-level reset, the fringe (0.9) satisfies (0.10).

#### Proof

Using (2.3), \(N_i=W/\lambda_i\), and
\(\lambda_q=\lambda_{q-1}(1+\delta_q)\),

\[
 {F_q\over W}
 ={\varepsilon_q\over\lambda_{q-1}}
  +{\Delta_q-\varepsilon_q\over\lambda_q}
 ={\delta_q\over1+\delta_q}
  +{\varepsilon_q\delta_q
     \over\lambda_{q-1}(1+\delta_q)}.                           \tag{2.7}
\]

The second term lies between zero and
\(\delta_q^2/(1+\delta_q)\), because
\(0\le\varepsilon_q\le\Delta_q=\lambda_{q-1}\delta_q\).
This is exactly (0.10). \(\square\)

### Lemma 2.3 (sum over Gaussian crossings)

Assume (0.7).  Then (0.11) holds.

#### Proof

Uniformly for \(q\le H=o(m^{2/3})\),

\[
 \log\lambda_q={q^2\over m}+o(1),\qquad
 \delta_q={2q\over m}(1+o(1)).                                 \tag{2.8}
\]

Let \(q_k\) be the first depth with \(\lambda_{q_k}\ge k\).  Since
\(\Delta_H=o(1)\), the reset depths are exactly the \(q_k\), up to a
bounded initial ambiguity, and

\[
 q_k=(1+o(1))\sqrt{m\log k}.                                   \tag{2.9}
\]

Equations (0.10), (2.8), and \(\delta_H=o(1)\) give

\[
 {1\over W}\sum_{q\in\mathcal R_H}F_q
 =(1+o(1)){2\over\sqrt m}
   \sum_{k\le\lambda_H}\sqrt{\log k}.                         \tag{2.10}
\]

The elementary integral comparison

\[
 \sum_{k\le K}\sqrt{\log k}
 =(1+o(1))K\sqrt{\log K}                                      \tag{2.11}
\]

and \(H=(1+o(1))\sqrt{m\log\lambda_H}\) prove (0.11).
\(\square\)

The threshold (0.13) is obtained from
\(\Delta_H\asymp(2H/m)e^{H^2/m}\asymp1\).  Notice that this
ledger concerns a scheme which *pays* for label rewriting.  The network
flow of Theorem 1.1 transports and relabels the same units, and therefore
pays zero physical target loss.

## 3. Pair-radius menus and the common-run line

Fix a partition of \([2m]\) into \(m\) unordered pairs.  For a target
\(S\), let \(d(S)\) be its number of full pairs.  Inside one quota epoch,
the high family may be chosen as

\[
                         \mathcal H_q(r)=\{S:d(S)\le r_q\},      \tag{3.1}
\]

with nondecreasing \(r_q\).  Deletion never increases \(d(S)\), so these
families are shadow-nested inside the epoch.  At a reset one starts a new
threshold sequence; no nesting is imposed across that boundary.

This is the correct way to use the pair-radius menu.  Keeping one threshold
sequence through a reset is impossible, while preserving the identity of
its top copy has the cost (2.6).  The lossless network instead forgets the
top-copy identity at the reset.

There is an exact arithmetic warning.  Every family (3.1) is point-regular,
because the wreath group of the fixed pairing is transitive on the
coordinates.  If its cardinality is \(r_q^{\rm top}\), then every
coordinate has high-family degree

\[
                         {m-q\over2m}r_q^{\rm top}.              \tag{3.2}
\]

For a physical owner circulation, the common-run identity requires

\[
 h_{q,v}^-={m-q\over2m}r_q^{\rm top}
 -q\left(R_v-{W\over2m}\right).                                \tag{3.3}
\]

Thus an **exact**, unperturbed pair-radius high family at even one positive
depth forces

\[
                         R_v={W\over2m}\quad\hbox{for every }v. \tag{3.4}
\]

In particular, it is impossible unless \(2m\mid W\).  For every odd
prime \(m=p\),

\[
                         \binom{2p}p\equiv2\pmod p,              \tag{3.5}
\]

so (3.4) fails.  Therefore epochwise pair-radius families must be
perturbed if exact point margins are demanded.

This is not an asymptotic obstruction.  If physical \(Q_r\) packet cycles
quantize run counts in multiples of

\[
                         \kappa_r={2^r\over2r},                  \tag{3.6}
\]

then the aggregate all-depth point correction scale remains

\[
                         O(mH^2\kappa_r)=o(W)                    \tag{3.7}
\]

for \(r=o(m)\) and \(H=o(r)\).  Hence the exact symmetry failure can be
absorbed by an \(o(W)\) exceptional target table.  What is not supplied is
a proof that those corrected tables are traces of the same packet cycles.

## 4. Reset labels do not solve memory closure

Let \(\Gamma_H\) be the safe physical Johnson paths

\[
                         \gamma=(X_0,X_1,\ldots,X_H).            \tag{4.1}
\]

An integral owner circulation is a vector \(z_\gamma\in\{0,1\}\) obeying

\[
 \sum_{\gamma:X_0=X}z_\gamma=1                                 \tag{4.2}
\]

for every root, and

\[
 \sum_{\gamma:\operatorname{pre}\gamma=\sigma}z_\gamma
 =
 \sum_{\gamma:\operatorname{suf}\gamma=\sigma}z_\gamma       \tag{4.3}
\]

for every \((H-1)\)-memory \(\sigma\).

Augment a path by arbitrary finite labels recording \(q\), \(c_q\), a
reset bit, a pair-radius cell, and copy labels.  Let
\(\widehat\Gamma_H\) be the resulting labelled path set and let
\(\pi:\widehat\Gamma_H\to\Gamma_H\) forget the labels.

### Theorem 4.1 (reset-state projection)

Suppose the labelled root and memory equations project to (4.2)--(4.3).
Then every integral labelled circulation projects to one owner permutation
\(P\), and its selected physical path at root \(X\) is

\[
                         (X,PX,P^2X,\ldots,P^HX).                \tag{4.4}
\]

Consequently every deletion and insertion word satisfies the shift laws

\[
 a_t(PX)=a_{t+1}(X),\qquad b_t(PX)=b_{t+1}(X)\qquad(t<H).        \tag{4.5}
\]

#### Proof

After summing labelled variables over every fibre of \(\pi\), equations
(4.2)--(4.3) hold for the projected nonnegative integral vector.  The root
row gives one selected outgoing physical path at each owner.  Memory
balance says that its suffix is the selected prefix rooted at its first
successor.  Induction gives (4.4), and reading the deletion and insertion
letters gives (4.5).  Labels never enter this argument. \(\square\)

Thus the reset alphabet does not relax the semigroup equations.  It only
describes a physical solution which must already exist.

There is an equally direct packet version.  Let \(\mathcal C\) be any
catalogue of owner-disjoint candidate packet cycles, and let
\(A(C)\) denote the complete vector of their root and signed target
incidences.  A labelled version of \(C\) has exactly the same physical
vector \(A(C)\).  Therefore the projection of every labelled integral
selection is

\[
                         \sum_{C\in\mathcal C}y_CA(C),
 \qquad y_C\in\{0,1\}.                                      \tag{4.6}
\]

Refining a column into reset-labelled copies does not alter the semigroup
generated by the physical columns.  In particular it cannot turn a
physical target hole into an occurrence, separate two occurrences which
hit the same target, or make intersecting packet owner sets disjoint.

## 5. An exact reset-aware counterexample

The projection loss in Section 4 is strict even when all marginal reset
flows are feasible.

### Theorem 5.1 (the \(m=H=2\) square obstruction)

For \(m=H=2\), Theorem 1.1 supplies exact all-depth floor/ceiling deletion
flags, and the upper flags do likewise.  Nevertheless there is no integral
two-safe owner permutation, with any choice of reset labels.

#### Proof

The middle owners are the six two-subsets of \([4]\).  A safe two-step
Johnson path starting at \(X\) deletes both coordinates of \(X\) and
inserts both coordinates of \(X^c\).  Hence any physical owner permutation
would satisfy

\[
                              P^2(X)=X^c.                         \tag{5.1}
\]

Complementation on the six owners is a product of three transpositions.
In the square of a permutation, transpositions occur in pairs: each pair
comes from one four-cycle.  Thus a permutation square cannot be a product
of an odd number of transpositions.  Equation (5.1) is impossible.
Theorem 4.1 shows that reset labels cannot change (5.1). \(\square\)

There is also a point-margin obstruction at the next dimension.  When
\(m=3,H=2\), \(W=20\), and every depth-two lower target is a singleton.
The common-run identity gives

\[
                         \mu_2^-(\{v\})=10-2R_v.                 \tag{5.2}
\]

Every load is even, whereas the balanced loads are three and four.  They
would all have to be four, giving total load \(24\ne20\).  Again, quota
resets do not affect the identity.

These examples disprove a universal exact theorem asserting that reset
states, common-run-compatible marginal tables, and pair-radius menus
automatically decompose into owner-disjoint packet cycles.  They do not
give a positive-density obstruction in the asymptotic Gaussian window.

## 6. Exact conclusion for the coefficient-one lane

The quota-reset issue is now separated sharply.

1. **Target-copy chronology:** solved exactly and integrally by Theorem
   1.1.  Every reset is lossless if the old top copy is relabelled as a new
   base copy.
2. **Literal restart:** impossible at \(o(W)\) cost once
   \(\lambda_H\to\infty\), by the \(W\log\lambda_H\) termination ledger.
3. **Fringe discard:** has the sharp cost (0.11), and ceases to be
   \(o(W)\) at (0.13).
4. **Common-run arithmetic:** exact pair-radius symmetry can fail, but the
   necessary exceptional correction is only \(o(W)\) in the audited
   \(Q_r\) regime.
5. **Physical chronology:** reset labels do not relax root transversality,
   memory overlap, or packet grouping.  Theorem 4.1 and Theorem 5.1 give
   an exact no-go to obtaining those constraints merely by state
   augmentation.

Accordingly, the next positive theorem cannot be another quota reset or
another marginal table.  It must prove an integral selection statement
for the physical packet incidence columns themselves: one owner-disjoint
selection whose projected signed target histograms are within \(o(W)\) of
the floor/ceiling tables and whose packet cycles obey the common run
vector.  Neither the lossless Boolean reset flow nor the pair-radius menu
implies that selection.
