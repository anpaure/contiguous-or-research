# Almost-equivariant row orbits: exact fractional resolution and the sparse-backbone integral gate

Date: 2026-07-25

Method: pure mathematics only.  No computation, solver, or web input is
used.

## 0. Verdict

Let

\[
 p=2m+1\ \text{be prime},\qquad
 W=\binom p m,\qquad B=W/p=\operatorname {Cat}_m,
 \qquad K=\lceil A\sqrt m\rceil .                 \tag{0.1}
\]

Throughout, \(m\) is sufficiently large that \(K\le m-1\).

This note does **not** prove, and does not refute, the desired integral
almost-equivariant near-factor.  It proves two exact statements which
strictly narrow its remaining gate.

1.  If all translation packets which are rainbow simultaneously at the
    ranks \(m,m-1,\ldots,m-K\) receive their natural common weight, then
    they form a simultaneous **annealed mean-cap** fractional resolution.
    Its quotient middle leave is

    \[
       o(B/\sqrt m),                               \tag{0.2}
    \]

    and its total physical deficit relative to the uniform means
    \(\lambda_q=W/N_q\), through the paired lower/upper window, is
    \(o(W)\).  This does not polarize the mean into the exact floor/ceiling
    quota vector.
    This statement keeps whole literal cyclic rows and one common packet
    weight; it is nevertheless fractional, not an exact near-factor.

2.  The within-rank \(O(1/m)\) packet codegree has a rigid and very sparse
    source.  At the middle rank it is supported on the quotient odd graph,
    of maximum degree at most \(m+1\).  After this odd-graph adjacency is
    removed, every pair involving a nonexceptional necklace has relative
    codegree \(O(1/m^2)\).  At every positive depth
    \(1\le q\le K\), the whole pair codegree is \(O_A(1/m^2)\).  The
    exceptional necklace family has only \(O_A(m^{7/2})\) vertices over
    the entire window.  Its middle part can be charged to the middle leave;
    its positive-depth parts cost only polynomial physical shadow mass.

Thus the remaining integral problem is not same-rank local packet
clustering.  It is an exact bundled coloring/matching problem for a sparse
cycle backbone, the deterministic cross-rank nested flag columns, and an
\(m^{-2}\)-diffuse same-rank remainder.  Section 6 states the precise
sufficient integral lemma.  It is marked **unproved**.

## 1. Rows, packets, and quotient ranks

An underlying row is an unoriented cyclic order on \(\mathbb F_p\), modulo
rotation and reversal.  For an oriented representative \(\pi\), put

\[
 I_i^{(r)}(\pi)
   =\{\pi_i,\pi_{i+1},\ldots,\pi_{i+r-1}\},
 \qquad i\in\mathbb Z_p.                          \tag{1.1}
\]

The translation packet of the row is its orbit under
\(T=(\mathbb F_p,+)\).  A row is **band-rainbow** if, for every
\(0\le q\le K\), its \(p\) intervals of size

\[
 r_q=m-q                                             \tag{1.2}
\]

belong to distinct translation orbits.  A band-rainbow row has a free
translation orbit of size \(p\); its packet consists of \(p\) literal
rows and has \(p\) distinct quotient targets at every controlled rank.
Complementation carries its rank-\((m-q)\) targets to its
rank-\((m+q+1)\) targets, up to a cyclic shift.  Thus every lower-rank
statement below has an identical simultaneous upper-rank statement.

Write

\[
 N_q=\binom p{r_q},\qquad
 \mathcal V_q=\binom{\mathbb F_p}{r_q}/T,
 \qquad |\mathcal V_q|=N_q/p.                    \tag{1.3}
\]

Freeness in (1.3) holds because a nonempty proper subset of
\(\mathbb F_p\) cannot be fixed by a nonzero translation.

There are

\[
 R=\frac{(p-1)!}{2}                               \tag{1.4}
\]

underlying rows, and a fixed rank-\(r_q\) target belongs to exactly

\[
 d_q=\frac{r_q!(p-r_q)!}{2}                       \tag{1.5}
\]

underlying rows.  The identities

\[
 N_qd_q=pR,
 \qquad
 \frac{d_q}{d_0}=\frac W{N_q}=:\lambda_q          \tag{1.6}
\]

will be used repeatedly.  For fixed \(A\), all \(\lambda_q\),
\(q\le K\), are bounded above by a constant depending only on \(A\).

## 2. The band-bad mass is exponentially negligible

We include a proof because only an aggregate estimate, rather than a
pointwise degree assertion, is valid here.

### Lemma 2.1 (exact translation-collision expectation)

For \(1\le r\le m\), a uniformly random oriented cyclic order satisfies

\[
 \Pr\bigl(\text{two length-}r\text{ intervals lie in one }T\text{-orbit}\bigr)
 \le \frac{p^2(p-1)}{\binom p r}.                 \tag{2.1}
\]

#### Proof

Fix \(a\ne0\).  Along the cyclic order generated by addition by \(a\),
let \(t_a(S)\) be the number of one-runs of an \(r\)-set \(S\).  The
standard cyclic-composition count is

\[
 \#\{S:t_a(S)=t\}
 =\frac pt\binom{r-1}{t-1}
              \binom{p-r-1}{t-1}.                \tag{2.2}
\]

If two positional length-\(r\) intervals are separated so that their
intersection has size \(r-t\), conditioning on the first interval gives

\[
 \Pr(I_j^{(r)}=I_i^{(r)}+a)
 =\frac{pt}{r(p-r)\binom p r}.                    \tag{2.3}
\]

For one fixed start, the sum of these positional values of \(t\) over
all nonzero separations is \(r(p-r)\).  Summing (2.3) over the \(p\)
starts and the \(p-1\) nonzero translations gives the exact expected
number

\[
 \frac{p^2(p-1)}{\binom p r}                      \tag{2.4}
\]

of ordered collision triples.  Markov proves (2.1).  Reversal does not
alter the event, so the same bound applies to underlying rows. \(\square\)

Put

\[
 \eta_A(m)=p^2(p-1)\sum_{q=0}^{K}\frac1{N_q}.     \tag{2.5}
\]

By a union bound, at most \(\eta_A(m)R\) underlying rows fail to be
band-rainbow.  Uniformly for \(q\le A\sqrt m+1\),
\(N_q\ge c_AW\) for some \(c_A>0\).  Therefore

\[
 \eta_A(m)=O_A\!\left(\frac{m^{7/2}}W\right),
 \qquad
 (K+1)\eta_A(m)=o(1).                             \tag{2.6}
\]

Translation-fixed arithmetic-progression rows are band-bad already at the
middle rank, so no separate stabilizer exception is hidden in (2.5).

## 3. Exact simultaneous annealed mean-cap resolution

Let \(\mathscr P_A\) be the family of band-rainbow translation packets.
Give every \(P\in\mathscr P_A\) the single common weight

\[
 x_P=1/d_0.                                       \tag{3.1}
\]

For \(v\in\mathcal V_q\), let \(L_q(v)\) be the sum of these weights
over packets containing \(v\).

### Theorem 3.1 (annealed fractional almost-equivariant window theorem)

For every \(0\le q\le K\),

\[
 0\le L_q(v)\le\lambda_q\quad(v\in\mathcal V_q), \tag{3.2}
\]

and

\[
 \boxed{
 \sum_{v\in\mathcal V_q}(\lambda_q-L_q(v))
 \le \eta_A(m)B.}                                \tag{3.3}
\]

Consequently the quotient middle leave is at most \(\eta_A(m)B\), hence
is \(o(B/\sqrt m)\), and the aggregate physical deficit in the paired
lower/upper window is at most

\[
 2p(K+1)\eta_A(m)B=2(K+1)\eta_A(m)W=o(W).         \tag{3.4}
\]

#### Proof

Fix a representative \(S\) of \(v\).  In a band-rainbow packet there is
exactly one translated row containing \(S\), so the number of good packets
through \(v\) equals the number of band-rainbow rows through \(S\).  It
is at most \(d_q\), proving (3.2) by (1.6).

Let \(R_{\rm bad}\) be the number of band-bad underlying rows.  The bad
property is translation-invariant.  Summing the missing row degree over
physical rank-\(r_q\) targets counts \(pR_{\rm bad}\) incidences; after
division by the free orbit size \(p\), summing over quotient targets gives
exactly \(R_{\rm bad}\).  Hence the left side of (3.3) is

\[
 \frac{R_{\rm bad}}{d_0}
 \le\eta_A(m)\frac R{d_0}
 =\eta_A(m)B,                                     \tag{3.5}
\]

where \(R/d_0=B\) follows from (1.6) at \(q=0\).  At the middle rank
\(\lambda_0=1\), so (3.3) is the fractional quotient leave.  A quotient
unit represents \(p\) physical targets.  Summing (3.3) through the lower
window and its complemented upper copy gives (3.4). \(\square\)

Theorem 3.1 is a common-packet statement: weights are not chosen separately
at different depths.  It has two defects relative to the desired theorem:
integrality and exact quota polarization.  If \(\lambda_q\notin\mathbb Z\),
the inequality \(L_q(v)\le\lambda_q\) does not choose only
\(\rho_q/p\) quotient vertices of capacity \(c_q+1\); the uniform load
\(\lambda_q\) lies strictly above the low capacity \(c_q\) everywhere.

The failure of polarization is quantitatively macroscopic at a genuine
Gaussian depth.

### Proposition 3.2 (the natural weighting is far outside every hard quota)

Put

\[
 B_q=|\mathcal V_q|=N_q/p,\qquad
 \lambda_q=c_q+\alpha_q,\quad 0\le\alpha_q<1,
 \qquad h_q=\alpha_qB_q=\rho_q/p\in\mathbb Z.    \tag{3.6}
\]

Thus a quotient balanced quota has a high set \(H\) of size \(h_q\).
For every such \(H\), the hard-cap excess of the natural loads satisfies

\[
 \boxed{
 E_H:=\sum_{v\in\mathcal V_q}
   \bigl(L_q(v)-c_q-\mathbf1_H(v)\bigr)_+
 \ge \alpha_q(1-\alpha_q)B_q-\eta_A(m)B.}         \tag{3.7}
\]

In particular, choose a fixed \(a\in(0,A]\) for which \(e^{a^2}\) is
not an integer and put \(q=\lfloor a\sqrt m\rfloor\).  Then

\[
 \lambda_q\longrightarrow e^{a^2},
 \qquad B_q=\Theta_A(B),                           \tag{3.8}
\]

so, after choosing \(a\) away from an integer boundary,

\[
 \inf_{|H|=h_q}E_H=\Theta_A(B).                   \tag{3.9}
\]

Consequently no perturbation of (3.1) having total packet-weight variation
\(o(B/p)\) can enter the hard-quota polytope.

#### Proof

Theorem 3.1 gives

\[
 \sum_vL_q(v)\ge\lambda_qB_q-\eta_A(m)B.          \tag{3.10}
\]

Also \(L_q(v)\le\lambda_q\).  Since \((x)_+\ge x\),

\[
\begin{aligned}
 E_H
 &\ge\sum_{v\notin H}(L_q(v)-c_q)\\
 &\ge\lambda_qB_q-\eta_A(m)B-|H|\lambda_q
       -c_q(B_q-|H|)\\
 &=\alpha_q(1-\alpha_q)B_q-\eta_A(m)B,
\end{aligned}                                      \tag{3.11}
\]

which proves (3.7).  The standard product expansion
\(W/N_q=\exp(q(q+1)/m+o(1))\) proves (3.8), while (2.6) makes the error
in (3.7) polynomial and hence negligible relative to \(B\).  This proves
(the lower bound in) (3.9); the upper bound follows from
\(E_H\le\sum_vL_q(v)\le\lambda_qB_q=B\).

Changing packet weights by \((\Delta_P)\) changes the rank-\(q\) load
vector in \(\ell^1\) by at most \(p\sum_P|\Delta_P|\), because one packet
has \(p\) quotient targets at that rank.  If \(L'_q\) obeys the fixed hard
cap belonging to \(H\), then \(E_H\le\|L_q-L'_q\|_1\).  Removing the
excess in (3.9)
therefore requires \(\sum_P|\Delta_P|=\Omega_A(B/p)\), proving the last
assertion. \(\square\)

Proposition 3.2 closes a specific nibble architecture: one cannot preselect
integral high sets, start from uniform packet weights, and repair only an
\(o(1)\) fraction of their mass.  It does not rule out rounding the high
sets jointly with the packet matching.  Section 6 gives an exact extended
fractional formulation which does precisely that.

## 4. Exact pair counts for cyclic rows

Fix distinct \(r\)-sets \(S,T\) with \(|S\cap T|=j\), and let
\(D_{r,j}\) be the number of underlying rows containing both.  In a row
through \(S\), every positive intersection size \(1\le j\le r-1\)
occurs at exactly two other cyclic positions, whereas there are
\(p-2r+1\) disjoint positions.  Since the number of \(T\)'s with
intersection \(j\) is

\[
 M_{r,j}=\binom rj\binom{p-r}{r-j},               \tag{4.1}
\]

double counting gives the exact formula

\[
 D_{r,j}=
 \begin{cases}
 \displaystyle
 \frac{(p-2r+1)d_r}{\binom{p-r}{r}},&j=0,\\[3mm]
 \displaystyle
 \frac{2d_r}{\binom rj\binom{p-r}{r-j}},&1\le j<r.
 \end{cases}                                      \tag{4.2}
\]

For quotient vertices \([S]\ne[T]\), the number of band-rainbow packets
containing both is at most

\[
 \sum_{a\in\mathbb F_p}D_{r,|S\cap(T+a)|}.        \tag{4.3}
\]

Indeed, a good packet through \([S]\) has a unique row through \(S\),
and its occurrence from \([T]\) is one uniquely determined translate.

## 5. The sparse-backbone theorem

Call an \(r\)-set \(T\) **two-run exceptional** if, for some \(a\ne0\),

\[
 |T\cap(T+a)|\ge r-2.                             \tag{5.1}
\]

Let \(\mathcal X_q\subseteq\mathcal V_q\) be the corresponding quotient
family.

### Lemma 5.1 (the exceptional family is polynomial)

For every \(q\le K\),

\[
 |\mathcal X_q|
 \le(p-1)\left(1+\frac{(r_q-1)(p-r_q-1)}2\right)
 =O(m^3).                                         \tag{5.2}
\]

Consequently

\[
 \sum_{q=0}^{K}|\mathcal X_q|=O_A(m^{7/2})
 =o(B/\sqrt m).                                   \tag{5.3}
\]

#### Proof

Condition (5.1) says that the number of one-runs along the \(a\)-cycle is
at most two.  Formula (2.2) gives, for a fixed \(a\), exactly

\[
 p+\frac p2(r_q-1)(p-r_q-1)                       \tag{5.4}
\]

sets with one or two runs.  Union bound over \(p-1\) nonzero \(a\)'s
and division by the free translation-orbit size \(p\) prove (5.2).
Equation (5.3) follows from \(K=O_A(\sqrt m)\) and the exponential size
of \(B\). \(\square\)

We require two elementary translation facts.

### Lemma 5.2 (extreme translates)

Let \([S]\ne[T]\).

1. If \(|S|=|T|=m\), at most two translates of \(T\) are disjoint from
   \(S\).
2. If \(T\) is not two-run exceptional, at most two translates of \(T\)
   have intersection at least \(|T|-1\) with \(S\).

#### Proof

For the first assertion, all disjoint translates are \(m\)-subsets of the
\((m+1)\)-set \(S^c\), so two of them intersect in \(m-1\) points.  If
\(|T\cap(T+a)|=m-1\), the binary word of \(T\) along the \(a\)-cycle has
one one-run.  Thus \(T\) is a cyclic interval in that cycle, and the only
nonzero shifts with overlap \(m-1\) are the two unit shifts in the same
step: after writing \(T=\{x,x+a,\ldots,x+(m-1)a\}\), a direct endpoint
comparison gives shifts \(\pm a\) only.  Three
translation offsets would have all pairwise differences among these two
values, which is impossible for \(p\ge5\).

For the second assertion, three translates, each differing from \(S\) in
at most one deleted and one inserted point, would give two whose mutual
intersection is at least \(|T|-2\).  Translating back contradicts the
nonexceptionality of \(T\). \(\square\)

At the middle rank define the simple quotient odd graph \(\mathcal D\) on
\(\mathcal V_0\) by joining \([S]\) and \([T]\) when some translates are
disjoint.  Since a fixed \(S\) has only \(m+1\) physical disjoint
neighbours,

\[
 \Delta(\mathcal D)\le m+1.                       \tag{5.5}
\]

### Theorem 5.3 (sparse \(m^{-1}\) backbone, diffuse \(m^{-2}\) remainder)

Let \(\operatorname{codeg}_q(v,w)\) denote packet codegree in
\(\mathscr P_A\) at quotient rank \(q\).

At \(q=0\), if \(w\notin\mathcal X_0\), then

\[
 \boxed{
 \operatorname{codeg}_0(v,w)
 \le
 \left(\frac{2a(v,w)}{m+1}+\frac{20}{m^2}\right)d_0,}          \tag{5.6}
\]

where \(a(v,w)\in\{0,1,2\}\) is the number of translates of one
representative disjoint from the other.  In particular,

\[
 vw\notin E(\mathcal D)
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \operatorname{codeg}_0(v,w)\le20d_0/m^2.         \tag{5.7}
\]

For every \(1\le q\le K\), if
\(w\notin\mathcal X_q\), then

\[
 \boxed{
 \operatorname{codeg}_q(v,w)\le C_A d_q/m^2}      \tag{5.8}
\]

for a constant depending only on \(A\).

#### Proof

For \(q=0\), Lemma 5.2 gives at most two terms with \(j=0\), each equal
to \(2d_0/(m+1)\), and at most two terms with \(j=m-1\), each equal to
\(2d_0/(m(m+1))\).  For \(1\le j\le m-2\),

\[
 \binom mj\binom{m+1}{j+1}
 \ge \frac{m^2(m+1)}2.                            \tag{5.9}
\]

The remaining at most \(p\) terms in (4.3) therefore contribute less
than \(12d_0/m^2\) for large \(m\), and the two \(j=m-1\) terms less
than \(4d_0/m^2\).  The relaxed constant \(20\) proves (5.6)--(5.7).

Now let \(q\ge1\), \(r=m-q\).  The at most two \(j=r-1\) terms have
total \(O(d_q/m^2)\).  Uniformly for \(1\le j\le r-2\),

\[
 \binom rj\binom{p-r}{r-j}=\Omega(m^3),           \tag{5.10}
\]

because at one endpoint the two factors are at least
\(r\) and \(\binom{p-r}{2}\), while in the interior one factor is at
least \(\binom r2\) and the other at least \(p-r\).  Summing the at most
\(p\) corresponding terms of (4.2) gives \(O(d_q/m^2)\).

For \(j=0\), the total over all translations is at most

\[
 \frac{p(2q+2)}{\binom{m+q+1}{2q+1}}d_q.          \tag{5.11}
\]

At \(q=1\) this is \(O(d_q/m^2)\).  For \(q\ge2\) and
\(q\le A\sqrt m+1\), the denominator is at least
\(\binom{m+q+1}{5}\), so (5.11) is smaller still.  Combining these
bounds proves (5.8). \(\square\)

The \(m^{-1}\) term in (5.6) is unavoidable.  Each good packet is a
\(p\)-cycle in \(\mathcal D\).  Since the number of good packets is
\((1-o(1))R/p\) and \(|E(\mathcal D)|\le B(m+1)/2\), double counting
packet-cycle edges shows that some edge of \(\mathcal D\) has packet
codegree at least

\[
 (1-o(1))\frac{2d_0}{m+1}.                        \tag{5.12}
\]

Here \(R/B=d_0\) was used.  Thus a proof must exploit the sparse cycle
geometry; it cannot replace (5.6) by a global \(O(d_0/m^2)\) estimate.

There is nevertheless a stronger aggregate statement: the large
codegrees are square-summably sparse.

### Proposition 5.4 (exact \(\ell^2\)-diffuseness at the middle rank)

For every \(v\in\mathcal V_0\),

\[
 \boxed{
 \sum_{\substack{w\in\mathcal V_0\\w\ne v}}
 \left(\frac{\operatorname{codeg}_0(v,w)}{d_0}\right)^2
 =O(1/m).}                                        \tag{5.13}
\]

#### Proof

Fix a representative \(X\) of \(v\).  For a quotient orbit \(w=[Y]\)
and \(0\le j\le m-1\), put

\[
 n_{w,j}=|\{a\in\mathbb F_p:|X\cap(Y+a)|=j\}|.    \tag{5.14}
\]

This is independent of the chosen representative of \(w\).  Formula
(4.2) at \(r=m\) gives, even before band-bad packets are removed,

\[
 \operatorname{codeg}_0(v,w)
 \le2d_0\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\frac{n_{w,j}}{M_j},
 \qquad
 M_j=\binom mj\binom{m+1}{j+1}.                  \tag{5.15}
\]

For fixed \(j\), every physical \(m\)-set having intersection \(j\)
with \(X\) occurs once among the translates represented in (5.14), so

\[
 \sum_w n_{w,j}\le M_j.                           \tag{5.16}
\]

Trivially \(n_{w,j}\le p\), while Lemma 5.2 improves this to
\(n_{w,0}\le2\) for \(w\ne v\).  Hence

\[
 \sum_{w\ne v}n_{w,0}^2\le2M_0,
 \qquad
 \sum_{w\ne v}n_{w,j}^2\le pM_j\quad(j\ge1).    \tag{5.17}
\]

The endpoint estimates for \(M_j\) give

\[
 \sum_{j=1}^{m-1}\frac1{M_j}=O(m^{-2}),
 \qquad
 \sum_{j=1}^{m-1}\frac1{\sqrt{M_j}}=O(m^{-1}).   \tag{5.18}
\]

Indeed, the \(j=m-1\) term is respectively \(O(m^{-2})\) and
\(O(m^{-1})\), the \(j=1\) term is smaller, and for
\(2\le j\le m-2\) one has \(M_j=\Omega(m^4)\), so summing the remaining
\(O(m)\) terms gives the displayed bounds.

Apply the triangle inequality in \(\ell^2(w)\) to (5.15).  Equations
(5.17)--(5.18) yield

\[
\begin{aligned}
 \left(\sum_{w\ne v}
   \left(\sum_j\frac{n_{w,j}}{M_j}\right)^2\right)^{1/2}
 &\le \sqrt{\frac2{M_0}}
    +\sqrt p\sum_{j=1}^{m-1}\frac1{\sqrt{M_j}}\\
 &=O(m^{-1/2}).                                   \tag{5.19}
\end{aligned}
\]

Squaring and using (5.15) proves (5.13). \(\square\)

Thus the packet system is quantitatively more diffuse than a projective
plane even before exceptional vertices are discarded.  Proposition 5.4
still supplies a second-moment input, not an integral matching theorem.

## 6. The exact unproved integral lemma

Because \(p\mid W\) and \(p\mid N_q\), the remainder in

\[
 W=c_qN_q+\rho_q,
 \qquad 0\le\rho_q<N_q                              \tag{6.1}
\]

is divisible by \(p\).  Hence one may choose a physical balanced quota in
the following translation-invariant quotient form:

\[
 b_q(v)=c_q+\mathbf1_{H_q}(v),
 \qquad |H_q|=\rho_q/p,
 \qquad \sum_{v\in\mathcal V_q}b_q(v)=B.          \tag{6.2}
\]

There is a useful exact way to postpone the choice of \(H_q\) until the
matching is rounded.

### Theorem 6.1 (joint quota-layer fractional lift)

For each \(q\ge1\), make \(c_q\) labelled base copies

\[
 (q,j,v),\qquad j\in[c_q],\quad v\in\mathcal V_q, \tag{6.3}
\]

one bonus copy \((q,*,v)\) of every \(v\in\mathcal V_q\), and a set
\(\mathcal T_q\) of exactly \(h_q=\rho_q/p\) bonus tokens.  A
**decoration** of a packet assigns each of its \(p\) rank-\(q\)
occurrences either a base label \(j\in[c_q]\), or the bonus label \(*\);
distinct bonus-labelled occurrences are also assigned distinct tokens of
\(\mathcal T_q\).  The decorated hyperedge contains the corresponding
target-layer vertices and all assigned tokens, as well as the packet's
\(p\) middle quotient vertices.  Decorations at different depths belong
to the same hyperedge.

Let \(\widetilde{\mathscr P}_A\) contain all such decorations of all
packets in \(\mathscr P_A\).  Then:

1. every matching of \(t\) decorated edges projects to \(t\) distinct
   whole translation packets and to high sets \(H_q\) for which
   \[
      \mu_q(v)\le c_q+\mathbf1_{H_q}(v),
      \qquad |H_q|=h_q;                           \tag{6.4}
   \]
2. \(\widetilde{\mathscr P}_A\) has a fractional matching of total
   weight
   \[
      \frac{R_{\rm good}}{pd_0}
      \ge(1-\eta_A(m))\frac Bp,                   \tag{6.5}
   \]
   and every expanded resource has load at most one.

#### Proof

For the first assertion, different decorations of the same base packet
share all its middle vertices, so a matching selects at most one.  At
rank \(q\), a target can be used at most once in each of the \(c_q\)
base layers and at most once in the bonus layer.  Token-disjointness bounds
the total number of bonus occurrences by \(h_q\).  Take \(H_q\) to
contain every target used in the bonus layer, adding arbitrary targets if
fewer than \(h_q\) were used.  This proves (6.4).

For the fractional assertion, write \(\lambda_q=c_q+\alpha_q\) and put

\[
 \beta_q=\frac{\alpha_q}{\lambda_q}.               \tag{6.6}
\]

For each base packet, give total weight \(1/d_0\).  Distribute this weight
over decorations as follows, independently at different depths.  Choose a
rotation-symmetric random subset of its \(p\) occurrences whose expected
size is \(p\beta_q\), so that every occurrence has bonus probability
\(\beta_q\).  (Mix the two adjacent subset sizes and then choose uniformly
at that size.)  Assign the remaining occurrences independently and
uniformly among the \(c_q\) base labels.  Since

\[
 \frac{1-\beta_q}{c_q}
 =\frac1{\lambda_q},                               \tag{6.7}
\]

each base label has probability \(1/\lambda_q\).  Assign distinct bonus
tokens uniformly without replacement.  For large \(m\), the selected
bonus subset has size at most \(h_q\); if \(\alpha_q=0\), it is empty.

A middle resource has load at most one.  A base-layer resource at depth
\(q\) has load at most

\[
 \frac{d_q}{d_0}\frac1{\lambda_q}=1,              \tag{6.8}
\]

and a bonus-target resource has load at most

\[
 \frac{d_q}{d_0}\beta_q=\alpha_q\le1.             \tag{6.9}
\]

Finally, the total expected bonus incidence over all weighted packets is
at most

\[
 \frac Bp\,p\beta_q=B\frac{\alpha_q}{\lambda_q}
 =\alpha_qB_q=h_q.                                \tag{6.10}
\]

Uniform injection into the \(h_q\) tokens therefore gives every token
load at most one.  The total base-packet weight is
\(R_{\rm good}/(pd_0)\), and Lemma 2.1 gives (6.5). \(\square\)

Theorem 6.1 resolves the fixed-high-set fractional obstruction from
Proposition 3.2: the fractional bonus load \(\alpha_q\) is a convex mixture
of integral high sets, and the bonus tokens require the eventual integral
matching to polarize that mixture.  It does not perform the integral
rounding.

The following is a clean pointwise sufficient packet statement left by the
present argument.  It is stronger than necessary: aggregate quota excess
\(o(B)\), summed through the window with the appropriate physical factor,
would also suffice.

> **Integral sparse-backbone packet lemma \(\mathrm{ISBP}_A\) (unproved).**
> There are a matching \(\mathcal M\subseteq\mathscr P_A\) at the
> middle quotient rank and quotient high sets \(H_q\), \(1\le q\le K\),
> such that, with \(\mu_q(v)\) the number of packets of \(\mathcal M\)
> containing \(v\),
> \[
>  \ell:=B-p|\mathcal M|=o(B/\sqrt m),             \tag{6.3}
> \]
> and
> \[
>  \mu_q(v)\le b_q(v)\quad
>  (v\in\mathcal V_q,\ 1\le q\le K).             \tag{6.4}
> \]

This lemma is entirely integral and keeps whole translation packets.  It
immediately gives the requested almost-equivariant row-orbit near-factor:
the lifted packets contain \(B-\ell\) rows and leave \(p\ell=o(W/\sqrt m)\)
middle owners.  At every rank the total quotient occurrence count is
\(B-\ell\); (6.2)--(6.4) therefore give exact deficit \(\ell\) and no
overload, where "exact deficit" means
\(\sum_v(b_q(v)-\mu_q(v))=\ell\).  The deficit below the uniform floor
\(c_q\) is only asserted to be at most \(\ell\).  Summing physically
through \(K+1\) ranks costs

\[
 p(K+1)\ell=o(W),                                  \tag{6.5}
\]

and every occurrence is realized by the literal cyclic rows in the chosen
packets.  The complemented upper window obeys the same quotas, so the
paired signed-window cost is \(2p(K+1)\ell=o(W)\).
This is a partial integral row packing.  No extension of its middle leave
to a completed exact factor is asserted.

Theorem 3.1 proves only the annealed mean-cap relaxation, with far smaller
aggregate error; it is not a feasible fractional point for the polarized
hard quotas (6.2).  Theorem 5.3 identifies the only \(m^{-1}\)-scale
dependency graph **within any one controlled rank** as the
degree-\((m+1)\) quotient odd graph at the middle.  Cross-rank incidences
inside one nested cyclic flag remain deliberately bundled in
\(\mathrm{ISBP}_A\); they are not estimated as independent pairs here.
No integral rounding theorem establishing (6.3)--(6.4) is proved.

## 7. Why ordinary codegree rounding is not a proof

At middle rank the packet uniformity is \(p\), while (5.12) gives relative
codegree of order \(1/p\).  A statement using only regularity and the bound
\(\Delta_2/D=O(1/p)\) cannot imply an almost-perfect matching: the lines
of a projective plane of order \(s\) form an \((s+1)\)-uniform,
\((s+1)\)-regular simple hypergraph with pair codegree one and matching
number one.  This comparison does not obstruct \(\mathscr P_A\), because
the projective-plane high-codegree graph is dense whereas (5.5) is sparse.
It does show exactly why a fixed-uniformity nibble or a black-box
degree--codegree assertion cannot be substituted for
\(\mathrm{ISBP}_A\).

## 8. Adversarial audit

1. **Aggregate versus pointwise goodness.**  Lemma 2.1 does not prove that
   every quotient vertex loses few rows.  The proof of Theorem 3.1 uses
   only the exact aggregate bad-incidence identity (3.5).  No false
   minimum-degree claim is made.

2. **Unoriented normalization.**  Both \(R\) and every \(d_q\) contain the
   same factor \(1/2\).  Equations (1.6), (3.5), and all relative
   codegrees are therefore unchanged by orientation conventions.

3. **Exceptional vertices.**  The estimate in Lemma 5.1 is a union bound,
   so it may overcount, but it is an upper bound.  Its polynomial total is
   much smaller than the permitted quotient leave and is not silently
   rounded into a selected factor.

4. **Disjoint-translate seam.**  The \(q=0\) disjoint terms cannot be put
   into the \(m^{-2}\) remainder; (5.12) proves that they genuinely occur
   at order \(d_0/m\).  They are retained explicitly as the quotient
   odd-graph backbone.

5. **Positive-depth estimate.**  Formula (5.11), rather than the middle
   disjoint-set bound, is used for \(q\ge1\).  The case \(q=1\) is checked
   separately before the \(\binom{m+q+1}{5}\) estimate is invoked.

6. **Fractional/hard-quota/integral boundary.**  The weights (3.1) are not
   rows of an exact factor.  Moreover, when \(\lambda_q\) is nonintegral,
   their pointwise mean cap is not the polarized quota (6.2); thus they are
   not even asserted to be a feasible hard-quota fractional solution.
   The projective-plane example explains why the coarser local estimates
   alone would not justify integral rounding.  The only assertion here
   which would produce the integral near-factor is \(\mathrm{ISBP}_A\),
   and it is explicitly unproved.

Accordingly, the coefficient-one conjecture is not claimed in this note.
