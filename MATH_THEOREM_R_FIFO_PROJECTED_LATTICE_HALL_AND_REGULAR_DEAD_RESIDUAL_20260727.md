# FIFO root-cycle packing: projected lattice closure, Hall assignment, and exact regular dead residuals

Date: 2026-07-27

Method: pure hand mathematics only. No computation, finite search,
solver, or web input is used.

## 0. Exact outcome and scope

Put

\[
 n=2m,\qquad k=m-H,\qquad \ell=m+H,
\]

\[
 N=\binom nk,\qquad W=\binom nm,\qquad
 \lambda={W\over N}={k!\ell!\over(m!)^2},\qquad
 r=\lfloor\lambda\rfloor.
\tag{0.1}
\]

At the reduced scale,

\[
 H=\lfloor\sqrt{m\log\log m}\rfloor,\qquad
 r=\log m+O(1).
\tag{0.2}
\]

Assume throughout \(H\ge1\), so \(n>2k\).  Where the genuine
\(F_r\)-owner columns are used, also assume

\[
 \gcd(\ell,r)=1,\qquad H\ge2,\qquad r\ge2,\qquad
 r(2H-1)<\ell-1.
\tag{0.3}
\]

The collar inequality \(tr\ge2H\) is retained with its exact scope:
it is necessary for the previously specified overlapping-collar
recurrence, not sufficient for closure. Under \(\gcd(\ell,r)=1\), the
fixed ordered successor \(F_r\) has exact state period \(n=2m\).
Therefore an \(F_r\)-only closed trajectory has \(t\) divisible by
\(n\); on one lap \(t=n\), so \(tr\ge2H\) is automatic. Any shorter
faithful object must contain a literal queue-changing transition rather
than merely subdividing the fixed successor.

The conclusions are as follows.

1. The integer lattice generated by differences of root interval-orbit
   columns is exactly the kernel of singleton-coordinate incidence.
   Thus every root-only additive invariant, over every abelian target
   group, factors through singleton degrees.  There is no hidden
   root-column parity, modular, or additive design invariant.
2. If FIFO chronology is discarded and one asks only to give each root
   \(r\) globally distinct disjoint middle owners, the resulting
   problem has an exact integral solution by Hall's theorem.
3. Even after restoring the ordered \(r\)-phase path above one fixed
   root, every owner family of density \(O(r/H)=o(1)\) contains a
   literal FIFO bouquet, with maximum local relative codegree
   \(2(r-1)/(rm^2)\). The missing condition is closure of those local
   bouquets under the successor between roots.
4. All exact root/owner cardinality and singleton leave conditions admit
   a root leave of size \(<n\) and an owner leave \(o(W)\).  Therefore
   these conditions cannot force a linear loss.
5. At degree two, the root and owner pair kernels span both copies of
   the nontrivial pair Specht module over \(\mathbb Q\).  Hence no
   rational quadratic/Fourier invariant forces a loss either.
6. One fixed global coordinate cycle supplies only
   \(\varphi(n)/2\) physical root supports, covering \(O(n^2)\) roots;
   moreover a physical root support determines its two reversed
   orientations, so owner bundles cannot be attached post hoc.
7. Despite all this projected mobility, there are exact
   scalar-compatible and coordinate-regular positive-density residuals
   below the \(e^{-1}\) entropy threshold which contain no faithful
   FIFO column, even with all unordered profiles through \(H\)
   asymptotically correct.
8. After conditioning on deadness, every admissible Boolean cylinder
   on at most \(\kappa_\varepsilon nr\) complement-pair variables
   remains asymptotically Bernoulli. Thus regeneration from local
   marginals of even positive linear order is false.

Items 1--6 close projected or fixed-cycle routes; items 7--8 isolate
the opposite regeneration obstruction. Neither side proves or refutes a globally
correlated near-perfect matching of full ordered \(F_r\)-orbit columns.

## 1. The primitive root-column lattice

Let

\[
 {\cal R}_k=\binom{[n]}k
\]

and write \(e_A\) for the standard basis vector of
\(\mathbb Z^{{\cal R}_k}\).  Define singleton incidence

\[
 B:\mathbb Z^{{\cal R}_k}\longrightarrow\mathbb Z^n,
 \qquad Be_A={\bf1}_A.
\tag{1.1}
\]

For an oriented cyclic coordinate order \(\omega\), modulo rotation,
let

\[
 v_\omega=\sum_{i\in\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z}e_{W_i(\omega)},
\tag{1.2}
\]

where \(W_i(\omega)\) is the length-\(k\) consecutive window beginning
at \(i\).

### Theorem 1.1 (primitive interval-column difference theorem)

For \(1\le k<n/2\),

\[
 \boxed{
 \left\langle v_\omega-v_{\omega'}:
          \omega,\omega'\right\rangle_{\mathbb Z}
       =\ker_{\mathbb Z}B.
 }
\tag{1.3}
\]

The equality is integral and primitive; no denominator or exceptional
prime is present.

#### Proof

Every coordinate occurs in exactly \(k\) of the \(n\) cyclic
length-\(k\) windows. Therefore

\[
                         Bv_\omega=k{\bf1}
\tag{1.4}
\]

for every \(\omega\), proving that the left side of (1.3) is contained
in \(\ker_{\mathbb Z}B\).

We next extract literal primitive rectangles. Fix distinct labels
\(x,y\), and let \(A,D\) be disjoint \((k-1)\)-sets avoiding \(x,y\).
Place, in a cyclic order, the labels of \(A\) immediately before the
adjacent pair \(x,y\), and the labels of \(D\) immediately after it.
This uses \(2k\) positions. Since \(n>2k\), the remaining labels fill
the nonempty residual gap. Swapping the adjacent \(x,y\) changes
exactly two \(k\)-windows, and, up to reversing the displayed sign,
gives the literal column difference

\[
 T(A,D;x,y)
 =e_{A+x}+e_{D+y}-e_{A+y}-e_{D+x}.
\tag{1.5}
\]

Now fix \(x,y\). The Kneser graph on the \((k-1)\)-subsets of
\([n]\setminus\{x,y\}\), adjacent when disjoint, is connected because

\[
                         n-2>2(k-1).
\tag{1.6}
\]

For completeness, if \(C'=C-a+b\) differs from \(C\) by one exchange,
the complement of \(C\cup\{b\}\) inside
\([n]\setminus\{x,y\}\) has at least \(k-1\) elements. Choose a
\((k-1)\)-set \(E\) there. Then

\[
                         C\;-\;E\;-\;C'
\]

is a two-edge Kneser path. Repeating exchanges connects arbitrary
vertices.

Let \(L_0\) be the lattice generated by the literal rectangles (1.5),
and put

\[
                         Q=\mathbb Z^{{\cal R}_k}/L_0.
\]

For a \((k-1)\)-set \(A\) avoiding \(x,y\), write in \(Q\)

\[
                         d_A^{xy}=[e_{A+x}-e_{A+y}].
\]

Equation (1.5) gives \(d_A^{xy}=d_D^{xy}\) whenever \(A,D\) are
disjoint. Connectivity makes this class independent of \(A\); denote
it by \(d^{xy}\). If \(x,y,z\) are distinct, choose one
\((k-1)\)-core avoiding all three. Then

\[
                         d^{xy}+d^{yz}=d^{xz}.
\tag{1.7}
\]

Fix a coordinate \(o\) and a base \(k\)-set \(S_0\). Put

\[
                         b=[e_{S_0}],\qquad
                         g_x=d^{xo}\quad(x\ne o).
\tag{1.8}
\]

Replacing elements of an arbitrary \(k\)-set one at a time and using
(1.7) shows that \(Q\) is generated by the \(n\) elements

\[
                         b,\quad g_x\ (x\ne o).
\tag{1.9}
\]

The map \(B\) annihilates \(L_0\), hence induces
\(\bar B:Q\to\operatorname{im}B\). The images of (1.9) are

\[
                         {\bf1}_{S_0},\quad e_x-e_o\ (x\ne o).
\tag{1.10}
\]

They are \(\mathbb Z\)-linearly independent. Indeed, summing all
coordinates in a relation first kills the coefficient of
\({\bf1}_{S_0}\), because its coordinate sum is \(k>0\); the remaining
differences \(e_x-e_o\) are independent. Since (1.9) generates \(Q\),
\(\bar B\) is injective. Consequently

\[
                         L_0=\ker_{\mathbb Z}B.
\]

Every generator of \(L_0\) is a literal column difference, while all
column differences lie in \(\ker B\) by (1.4). This proves (1.3).
\(\square\)

For \(k=1\), \(B\) is the identity and all vectors \(v_\omega\) are
equal, so (1.3) is also immediate. The boundary \(n=2k\) is excluded;
the disjoint-core connectivity argument need not survive there.

### Corollary 1.2 (complete root-only additive invariant classification)

Let \(G\) be any abelian group and
\(\Phi:\mathbb Z^{{\cal R}_k}\to G\) a homomorphism such that
\(\Phi(v_\omega)\) is independent of \(\omega\). Then \(\Phi\)
factors through \(B\):

\[
 \Phi=\psi B
\tag{1.11}
\]

for a homomorphism \(\psi:\operatorname{im}B\to G\).

Thus every additive or modular root-only invariant is already a
singleton-coordinate invariant. In particular there is no parity
invariant attached to the cyclic order. Indeed, because \(n\) is even,
rotation of an ordering by one place is an odd \(n\)-cycle, so the sign
of a representative is not even defined modulo cyclic rotation.

## 2. The projected root-owner assignment is integrally feasible

Make a bipartite graph with root side
\(\binom{[n]}k\), owner side \(\binom{[n]}m\), and

\[
                         A\sim U\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad A\cap U=\varnothing.
\tag{2.1}
\]

Its degrees are

\[
 d_R=\binom{\ell}{m}=\binom{\ell}{H},\qquad
 d_M=\binom m k=\binom mH,
\tag{2.2}
\]

and

\[
                         {d_R\over d_M}=\lambda.
\tag{2.3}
\]

### Theorem 2.1 (exact projected \(r\)-assignment)

For every integer \(0\le r\le\lambda\), one can assign to each root
\(A\) exactly \(r\) distinct owners disjoint from \(A\), with all
\(rN\) assigned owners globally distinct.

#### Proof

Replace every root by \(r\) clones, with each clone having the same
owner neighborhood. Let \(X\) be any set of clones and \(S\) its set
of underlying roots. Then

\[
                         |X|\le r|S|\le\lambda|S|.
\tag{2.4}
\]

Counting edges from \(S\) to its neighborhood gives

\[
 d_R|S|\le d_M|\Gamma(S)|,
\]

and hence, by (2.3),

\[
                         |\Gamma(X)|=|\Gamma(S)|
                         \ge\lambda|S|\ge|X|.
\tag{2.5}
\]

Hall's theorem supplies a matching saturating all root clones.
\(\square\)

This theorem is deliberately projected. It does not make the \(r\)
owners assigned to one root into consecutive \(m\)-windows of one
ordered complement, and it does not couple successive root blocks
around an \(F_r\)-orbit. It proves that every obstruction visible only
in the disjointness graph (2.1), including its Hall cuts, disappears.
The surviving obstruction is the ordered window bundle and FIFO
monodromy.

## 2A. Exact local ordered bouquets are abundant

Fix a root \(A\). Put \(T=[n]\setminus A\), so \(|T|=\ell\).
Every owner \(U\) disjoint from \(A\) is equivalently described by its
\(H\)-hole set

\[
                         Q=T\setminus U\in\binom TH.
\tag{2A.1}
\]

An ordered local \(r\)-bouquet is a sequence
\[
                         Q_0,Q_1,\ldots,Q_{r-1}
\tag{2A.2}
\]
which is a Johnson geodesic:
\[
 Q_i=Q_0-\{x_1,\ldots,x_i\}+\{y_1,\ldots,y_i\},
\tag{2A.3}
\]
where the \(x_i\in Q_0\) are distinct and the
\(y_i\in T\setminus Q_0\) are distinct.

Every such bouquet is a literal \(O\)-phase trajectory over \(A\).
Order the initial \(Q\)-queue with
\(x_1,\ldots,x_{r-1}\) first, and order the initial \(P\)-queue with
\(y_1,\ldots,y_{r-1}\) first. The successive hole sets under
\(O^0,\ldots,O^{r-1}\) are exactly (2A.2).

### Theorem 2A.1 (local FIFO bouquet expansion)

Assume \(2\le r\le H+1\). Let
\({\cal G}\subseteq\binom TH\) have density

\[
                         \alpha={{|{\cal G}|}\over\binom\ell H}.
\]

If

\[
 \alpha>
 \alpha_*:={H+2r\ell\over H(m+1)},
\tag{2A.4}
\]

then \({\cal G}\) contains an ordered local \(r\)-bouquet.
At reduced scale,

\[
                         \alpha_*=O(r/H)=o(1).
\tag{2A.5}
\]

#### Proof

Let \(J(\ell,H)\) be the Johnson graph on the hole sets, and let \(M\)
be the incidence matrix between \(H\)-sets and \((H-1)\)-sets. Its
adjacency matrix is

\[
                         {\cal A}=MM^{\mathsf T}-HI.
\tag{2A.6}
\]

For the indicator \(f=1_{\cal G}\), Cauchy--Schwarz gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
 f^{\mathsf T}{\cal A}f
 &=\|M^{\mathsf T}f\|_2^2-H|{\cal G}|\\
 &\ge {H^2|{\cal G}|^2\over\binom{\ell}{H-1}}
       -H|{\cal G}|.
\end{aligned}
\]

Since
\[
 {\binom\ell H\over\binom\ell{H-1}}={m+1\over H},
\]
the average degree of the induced graph on \({\cal G}\) is at least

\[
                         H(m+1)\alpha-H.
\tag{2A.7}
\]

Condition (2A.4) makes (2A.7) larger than \(2r\ell\).
Iteratively deleting vertices of degree at most \(r\ell\) therefore
leaves a nonempty induced subgraph of minimum degree greater than
\(r\ell\).

Start anywhere in this core and construct a geodesic greedily. After
\(j\) valid steps, a neighbor is invalid only if it removes one of the
\(j\) previously arrived labels or reintroduces one of the \(j\)
previously departed labels. The number of such neighbors is at most

\[
                         jm+Hj-j^2=j\ell-j^2<r\ell.
\tag{2A.8}
\]

The current core degree is larger than this number, so a valid next
neighbor exists. Continue through \(r-1\) steps. \(\square\)

There is also an exact local codegree calculation. Let
\({\cal B}_r\) be the chronological hypergraph whose edge consists of
one root and the \(r\) owners of one local bouquet. Put
\[
                         (a)_t=a(a-1)\cdots(a-t+1).
\]
The two path orientations are counted as distinct chronological edges.
If they are identified, every degree and codegree below is divided by
two and every relative value is unchanged.
Its root and owner degrees are

\[
\begin{aligned}
 D_R^{\rm loc}
   &=\binom\ell H(H)_{r-1}(m)_{r-1},\\
 D_M^{\rm loc}
   &={r\over\lambda}D_R^{\rm loc}
    =r\binom mH(H)_{r-1}(m)_{r-1}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2A.9}
\]

A disjoint root-owner pair has codegree

\[
                         r(H)_{r-1}(m)_{r-1},
\]
and hence relative codegree, normalized by the smaller degree
\(D_M^{\rm loc}\),

\[
                         {1\over\binom mH}.
\tag{2A.10}
\]

Two distinct owners at Johnson distance \(j\) have zero codegree when
\(j\ge r\). For \(1\le j\le r-1\), their codegree divided by
\(D_M^{\rm loc}\) is exactly

\[
                         {2(r-j)\over r\binom mj^2}.
\tag{2A.11}
\]

Indeed, choose the common root in
\(\binom{m-j}{H-j}\) ways. Choose which owner occurs first and its
position in the bouquet in \(2(r-j)\) ways; order the \(j\) forced
departures and arrivals in \((j!)^2\) ways; and extend through the
remaining steps in

\[
                         (H-j)_{r-1-j}(m-j)_{r-1-j}
\]
ways. Division by (2A.9) simplifies to (2A.11). Thus the maximum local
relative codegree is, eventually,

\[
                         {2(r-1)\over rm^2}.
\tag{2A.12}
\]

### Corollary 2A.2 (what local expansion alone guarantees)

Let a maximal matching in \({\cal B}_r\) leave an \(x\)-fraction of
the roots unmatched. If \(\alpha_*<1\), then

\[
                         x\le
 {r\over r+(1-\alpha_*)\lambda}.
\tag{2A.13}
\]

At reduced scale the right side is \(1/2+o(1)\).

#### Proof

For an unmatched root, the unused disjoint owners have local density at
most \(\alpha_*\), or Theorem 2A.1 would supply an augmenting bouquet.
Let \(d_R,d_M\) be the two degrees in the disjointness graph (2.1), so
\(d_R/d_M=\lambda\). Counting disjoint incidences between the \(xN\)
unmatched roots and the \(W-r(1-x)N\) unused owners gives

\[
 d_RxN-d_Mr(1-x)N
 \le\alpha_*d_RxN.
\]

Use \(d_R/d_M=\lambda\) and rearrange. \(\square\)

The corollary is only a guarantee for a maximal local packing, not an
upper bound on the true local matching number. More importantly,
\({\cal B}_r\) does not impose that the exit state of one root bouquet
is the entry state of the next. Conservation under the deterministic
\(F_r\) successor restores complete \(n\)-block orbit columns. Thus
local ordered supply is solved, while global cyclic queue coupling
remains.

## 3. Exact marginal leaves can be \(o(N)\) and \(o(W)\)

Put

\[
                         g=\gcd(n,k),\qquad q={n\over g},
\tag{3.1}
\]

and let \(z_0\) be the least nonnegative residue of \(N\) modulo \(n\).
Because \(n\mid kN\),

\[
                         q\mid z_0.
\tag{3.2}
\]

Write

\[
                         z_0=aq,\qquad 0\le a<g.
\tag{3.3}
\]

Also \(g\mid2H\), because \(g\mid n=2m\) and \(g\mid2k=2m-2H\).
Thus

\[
                         g\le2H,\qquad q\ge {m\over H}\to\infty
\tag{3.4}
\]

at the reduced scale.

### Theorem 3.1 (all scalar and singleton conditions admit a tiny leave)

There is a simple root family \({\cal Z}_R\) with

\[
 |{\cal Z}_R|=z_0<n,\qquad
 \deg_{{\cal Z}_R}(x)={kz_0\over n}\quad(x\in[n]).
\tag{3.5}
\]

Putting

\[
 c={N-z_0\over n},\qquad
 d=W-nrc=(\lambda-r)N+rz_0,
\tag{3.6}
\]

there is a simple owner family \({\cal Z}_M\) with

\[
 |{\cal Z}_M|=d,\qquad
 \deg_{{\cal Z}_M}(x)={d\over2}\quad(x\in[n]).
\tag{3.7}
\]

At reduced scale,

\[
                         z_0=o(N),\qquad d=o(W).
\tag{3.8}
\]

#### Proof

Write \(w=k/g\). Then \(\gcd(q,w)=1\). Consequently every binary word
of length \(q\) and weight \(w\) is primitive: if it were a repetition
of a shorter word, the repetition number would divide both \(q\) and
\(w\). Since \(w/q=k/n\to1/2\), the number

\[
                         {1\over q}\binom qw
\tag{3.9}
\]

of its cyclic necklaces is eventually larger than \(g\).

Choose \(a\) distinct such necklaces. Repeat a representative word
\(g\) times to obtain a binary word of length \(n\) and weight \(k\).
Its orbit under cyclic coordinate shift has exactly \(q\) distinct
\(k\)-sets. Over those \(q\) shifts, every coordinate occurs in
exactly \(w\) sets. Distinct base necklaces give disjoint length-\(q\)
orbits. Their union therefore has size \(aq=z_0\) and coordinate
degree

\[
                         aw={kz_0\over n},
\]

proving (3.5).

The integer \(d\) in (3.6) is even, because \(W=\binom{2m}m\) and
\(nrc\) are even. Choose any \(d/2\) complementary owner pairs

\[
                         \{U,[n]\setminus U\}.
\tag{3.10}
\]

Their union is simple, has size \(d\), and contains each coordinate
once per selected pair, proving (3.7).

Finally \(z_0<n\), and

\[
 0\le d<N+rn=O(N)=o(W),
\tag{3.11}
\]

because \(0\le\lambda-r<1\), \(z_0<n\), and \(W\sim rN\) with
\(r\to\infty\). \(\square\)

These are abstract marginal leaves, not leaves proved reachable from a
joint FIFO packing. The theorem shows exactly that cardinality,
divisibility, and singleton regularity cannot themselves force
\(\Omega(N)\) uncovered roots.

### Theorem 3.2 (one global coordinate cycle supplies only exponentially few physical root columns)

Fix an \(n\)-cycle \(\tau\) on the coordinates and assume

\[
                         2\le k<n/2.
\tag{3.12}
\]

Among all \(\langle\tau\rangle\)-orbits on \(k\)-sets, exactly
\(\varphi(n)/2\) are supports of genuine cyclic interval columns.
They are the arithmetic interval families

\[
 \left\{
   \{x,\tau^e x,\ldots,\tau^{(k-1)e}x\}:x\in[n]
 \right\},
\qquad \gcd(e,n)=1,
\tag{3.12a}
\]

with \(e\) identified with \(-e\).
Consequently all physical interval columns carried by this one global
cycle cover at most

\[
                         {n\varphi(n)\over2}=O(n^2)
                         =e^{-\Omega(m)}N
\tag{3.13}
\]

distinct roots.

#### Proof

For a cyclic coordinate order \(\gamma\), let

\[
 {\cal B}_\gamma
 =\{\{\gamma^i x,\gamma^{i+1}x,\ldots,
             \gamma^{i+k-1}x\}:i\in\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\}
\tag{3.14}
\]

be its \(n\) cyclic \(k\)-intervals. Two blocks of
\({\cal B}_\gamma\) meet in \(k-1\) elements exactly when their start
indices differ by \(1\) or \(-1\), because \(k<n/2\). Thus the
intersection-\((k-1)\) graph on the blocks is the cycle \(C_n\).

Every coordinate permutation preserving \({\cal B}_\gamma\) therefore
induces a dihedral automorphism of this block cycle. The action on
blocks is faithful: if every block is fixed, then each singleton is
fixed because

\[
 B_i\setminus B_{i+1}=\{\gamma^i x\}.
\]

Conversely the rotations and reflection of the coordinate cycle
preserve the block family. Hence

\[
                         \operatorname{Aut}({\cal B}_\gamma)=D_n.
\tag{3.15}
\]

Suppose a full \(\tau\)-orbit is the physical support
\({\cal B}_\gamma\). Then \(\tau\) acts transitively on that support
and belongs to \(D_n\). Since \(\tau\) is an \(n\)-cycle and \(n>2\),
it cannot be a reflection. Therefore

\[
                         \tau=\gamma^a,\qquad \gcd(a,n)=1.
\tag{3.16}
\]

Equivalently \(\gamma\) is a unit power of \(\tau\). There are
\(\varphi(n)\) unit powers, and reversing a cyclic order gives the same
unordered interval support.

To see that there are no further identifications, recover the
undirected coordinate cycle from the interval family: two coordinates
belong together to exactly \(k-1\) blocks precisely when they are
adjacent in that cycle. Hence two unit steps \(e,e'\) give the same
support only if

\[
                         e'\equiv\pm e\pmod n.
\]

For \(n>2\), the units pair freely under sign. Thus there are exactly
\(\varphi(n)/2\) supports. Distinct supports are distinct
\(\tau\)-orbits and hence disjoint. Each contains \(n\) roots, proving
(3.13). \(\square\)

Thus the coordinate-shift orbits in Theorems 3.1 and 5.1 are devices
for constructing regular abstract leaves; almost all of them are not
FIFO root columns. A positive group-resolution construction must vary
the long coordinate cycle across exponentially many choices.

### Corollary 3.3 (a root support leaves only reversal)

Under (3.12), a physical cyclic-interval root support determines its
undirected coordinate cycle. Modulo cyclic rotation, it therefore has
exactly two oriented realizations, which are reversals of one another.

Consequently one cannot first select a root-only interval factor and
then use the large projected Hall or local-bouquet menu independently.
For each selected root column, the complete \(F_r\) owner bundle has
at most the two orientations forced by that root support. Root cycles
and their phase bouquets must be selected simultaneously.

## 4. The joint quadratic moment module is fully mobile over \(\mathbb Q\)

Use the exact \(F_r\)-cycle chart from the companion FIFO report. Let
\(I\) be the length-\(k\) root interval and \(J_a\) the phase-\(a\)
owner position set, \(0\le a<r\). For a cyclic displacement \(d\), set

\[
 R(d)=|I\cap(I+d)|,\qquad
 M(d)=\sum_{a=0}^{r-1}|J_a\cap(J_a+d)|.
\tag{4.1}
\]

Thus \(R(d)\) and \(M(d)\) are the pair-degrees, in one column, of two
coordinate labels whose positions have displacement \(d\).

Assume for this section

\[
 2r\le H,\qquad H+2r<\ell,\qquad rk>m.
\tag{4.2}
\]

These inequalities hold for all sufficiently large \(m\) at the
reduced scale.

### Lemma 4.1 (two exact short-distance values)

\[
\begin{aligned}
 R(1)&=k-1,&\qquad R(2)&=k-2,\\
 M(1)&=r(m-r),& M(2)&=r(m-2r).
\end{aligned}
\tag{4.3}
\]

#### Proof

The root identities are immediate for a length-\(k\) interval.

Write the owner complement as

\[
                         J_a^c=I\sqcup G_a,
\tag{4.4}
\]

where, after multiplying top-chart residues by \(r\), \(G_a\) becomes
the cyclic \(H\)-interval

\[
                         \{-a,-a+1,\ldots,H-1-a\}.
\tag{4.5}
\]

For \(d=1,2\), conditions (4.2) imply

\[
 \{0,\ldots,d-1\}\subseteq G_a,\qquad
 G_a\cap\{\ell-d,\ldots,\ell-1\}=\varnothing,
\tag{4.6}
\]

and

\[
                         |G_a\cap(G_a+d)|=H-rd.
\tag{4.7}
\]

Indeed, for \(0\le x<d\), membership follows by taking
\(s=a+rx\): the worst case is

\[
                         a+r(d-1)\le2r-1\le H-1.
\]

For \(1\le j\le d\), membership of \(\ell-j\) would require
\(s-a=-rj\) or \(s-a=\ell-rj\). The first is below the allowed range
because \(a<r\le rj\), and the second is above it because
\(\ell-2r>H\). Finally multiplication by \(r\) turns translation by
\(d\) into translation by \(rd\) of the interval (4.5), whose overlap
has length \(H-rd\).

In the full \(F_r\)-chart, \(I+d\) wraps through the positions
\(0,\ldots,d-1\), while \(G_a+d\) does not cross into \(I\), by
(4.6). Hence

\[
\begin{aligned}
 |J_a^c\cap(J_a^c+d)|
 &=(k-d)+d+(H-rd)\\
 &=m-rd.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4.8}
\]

Since \(J_a\) and \(J_a^c\) both have size \(m=n/2\), complementation
preserves this autocorrelation:

\[
                         |J_a\cap(J_a+d)|=m-rd.
\tag{4.9}
\]

Summing over \(a\) proves (4.3). \(\square\)

Let \(V\) be the rational permutation module on unordered coordinate
pairs. It decomposes as

\[
 V={\bf1}\oplus S^{(n-1,1)}\oplus S^{(n-2,2)}.
\tag{4.10}
\]

For a fixed base cyclic order, regard \(R\) and \(M\) as vectors of
\(V\). Their coordinate-vertex sums are constant, so after subtracting
their respective means they lie in \(S^{(n-2,2)}\).

### Theorem 4.2 (two independent quadratic directions)

The centered pair vectors \(R^\circ,M^\circ\) are linearly independent.
Consequently the diagonal \(S_n\)-orbit of

\[
                         (R^\circ,M^\circ)
\tag{4.11}
\]

spans both copies of \(S^{(n-2,2)}\) in
\(V\oplus V\).

#### Proof

If the centered vectors were dependent, there would be
\(\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb Q\) with

\[
                         M(d)=\alpha R(d)+\beta
\tag{4.12}
\]

for every \(d\). The two values in (4.3) force

\[
                         \alpha=r^2,\qquad
                         \beta=r(m-rk)<0.
\tag{4.13}
\]

But at the antipodal displacement \(d=m\),

\[
                         R(m)=0,\qquad M(m)\ge0,
\]

contradicting (4.12)--(4.13). Thus the vectors are independent.

The Specht module \(S^{(n-2,2)}\) is absolutely irreducible over
\(\mathbb Q\), and the image of \(\mathbb Q[S_n]\) on it is its full
endomorphism algebra. Given arbitrary targets in two copies, choose an
endomorphism sending the independent vectors \(R^\circ,M^\circ\) to
those targets. Therefore the cyclic submodule generated by (4.11) is
the full direct sum of the two copies. \(\square\)

### Corollary 4.3 (no rational degree-two obstruction)

Every rational additive invariant made from root and owner
coordinate-pair degrees and constant on all relabelled joint columns
reduces to the two cardinality ledgers and the two singleton-coordinate
ledgers. In particular it cannot force a positive-density leave.

This statement is rational. It does not assert saturation of the joint
integer lattice, exclude torsion at exceptional primes, or treat
higher Specht levels.

## 5. Exact coordinate-regular residuals with no FIFO column

The preceding theorems show that projected additive obstructions vanish.
The next result shows why this does not imply regenerative matching.

### Theorem 5.1 (regular dead-residual theorem)

Under (0.2)--(0.3), for every fixed \(\varepsilon>0\) and all
sufficiently large \(m\), there are residual families

\[
 {\cal R}\subseteq\binom{[n]}k,\qquad
 {\cal M}\subseteq\binom{[n]}m
\]

and an integer \(c\) satisfying

\[
\begin{aligned}
 |{\cal R}|&=N-nc,&
 \deg_{\cal R}(x)&={k|{\cal R}|\over n},\\
 |{\cal M}|&=W-nrc,&
 \deg_{\cal M}(x)&={|{\cal M}|\over2}
\end{aligned}
\tag{5.1}
\]

for every coordinate \(x\), such that both residual densities are
bounded away from zero,

\[
                         {|{\cal M}|\over W}<e^{-1-\varepsilon},
\tag{5.2}
\]

and, uniformly for every \(1\le q\le H\) and
\(T\in\binom{[n]}q\),

\[
\begin{aligned}
 \deg_{\cal R}(T)
   &=\bigl(u_R+o(1)\bigr)\binom{n-q}{k-q},\\
 \deg_{\cal M}(T)
   &=\bigl(u_M+o(1)\bigr)\binom{n-q}{m-q},
\end{aligned}
\qquad
 u_R={|{\cal R}|\over N},\quad
 u_M={|{\cal M}|\over W},
\tag{5.2a}
\]

and no complete ordered \(F_r\)-orbit column has all its root and owner
vertices in \({\cal R}\sqcup{\cal M}\).

#### Proof

Put \(u=e^{-1-2\varepsilon}\) and

\[
 c=\left\lfloor{(1-u)W\over nr}\right\rfloor,\qquad
 z=N-nc,\qquad d=W-nrc.
\tag{5.3}
\]

Then

\[
                         z/N=u+O(1/r),\qquad d/W=u+o(1).
\tag{5.4}
\]

For a fixed coordinate \(n\)-cycle \(\tau\), all but \(o(N)\) roots
belong to full length-\(n\) \(\tau\)-orbits. Non-full binary necklaces
have a proper period at most \(n/2\), so together they contain at most

\[
                         n2^{n/2}=o\!\binom nk
\tag{5.5}
\]

labelled words. There are therefore at least \(c\) full orbits for
large \(m\), because

\[
 {c\over N/n}=(1-u){\lambda\over r}+o(1)<1-{u\over2}
\tag{5.5a}
\]

eventually. Choose \(c\) of them uniformly and remove them from the
complete root layer.
Every removed orbit contains each coordinate exactly \(k\) times, so
the root residual has the first line of (5.1).

Partition the middle layer into \(W/2\) complement-pairs
\(\{U,U^c\}\). The integer \(d\) is even. Select uniformly \(d/2\)
of these pairs and let \({\cal M}\) be their union. It has the second
line of (5.1) deterministically.

The no-complement theorem for a genuine \(F_r\)-column says that its
\(nr\) distinct owners belong to \(nr\) distinct complement-pairs.
Thus the probability that all of them lie in \({\cal M}\) is

\[
 { (d/2)_{nr}\over(W/2)_{nr}}
 \le(d/W)^{nr}.
\tag{5.6}
\]

There are \((n-1)!\) oriented columns, and

\[
                         {\log((n-1)!)\over nr}=1+o(1).
\tag{5.7}
\]

By (5.2), the expected number whose owner support survives is
\(\exp[-\Omega_\varepsilon(nr)]<1\). Some exact complement-pair
choice therefore contains no full owner support, and hence the joint
residual catalogue is empty.

It remains to prove that the random choices may simultaneously satisfy
(5.2a). We use the following elementary sampling bound. If \(p\) of
\(M\) blocks are sampled uniformly without replacement, the blocks
carry weights \(a_i\in[0,b]\), and \(X\) is the sum of the sampled
weights, then

\[
 \Pr(|X-\mathbb EX|\ge t)
 \le2\exp\left(-{t^2\over2pb^2}\right).
\tag{5.9}
\]

To see this, reveal the sampled blocks sequentially and use the Doob
martingale for the final sum. After \(j\) revelations its value is the
revealed sum plus \(p-j\) times the mean of the remaining population.
Changing the \(j\)-th revealed weight changes this conditional
expectation by

\[
 |a-a'|\left(1-{p-j\over M-j}\right)\le b.
\]

Azuma's inequality gives (5.9).

For a fixed nonempty \(T\in\binom{[n]}q\), exactly

\[
                         D^M_q=\binom{n-q}{m-q}
\tag{5.10}
\]

complement-pairs contain an owner which contains \(T\), and each such
pair contributes weight one. Thus the owner degree has exact mean
\(u_MD^M_q\), and (5.9) applies with \(b=1\) and \(p=d/2\).

For roots, give a full coordinate-shift orbit weight equal to its
number of members containing \(T\), a number in \([0,n]\). If

\[
                         D^R_q=\binom{n-q}{k-q},
\tag{5.11}
\]

then the total weight over full orbits is \(D^R_q-E_T\), where
\[
                         0\le E_T\le n2^{n/2}.
\]
The number of full orbits is
\[
                         F={N-o(N)\over n},
\]
and
\[
                         {c\over F}=1-u_R+o(1).
\tag{5.12}
\]
Consequently the root residual degree has mean
\(u_RD^R_q+o(D^R_q)\), uniformly for \(q\le H\), and (5.9) applies to
the removed degree with \(b=n\).

Both \(D^R_H\) and \(D^M_H\) are
\(\exp(\Theta(m))\), while \(H=o(m)\). Taking
\(\delta_m=1/\log m\), the union of the bounds (5.9), over all
\[
                         \sum_{q=1}^H\binom nq\le2^n
\]
sets \(T\), is \(o(1)\): the two worst exponents are at least

\[
 {\delta_m^2(D^R_H)^2\over2Nn}
       =\exp(\Theta(m)),
\qquad
 {\delta_m^2(D^M_H)^2\over W}
       =\exp(\Theta(m)).
\tag{5.13}
\]

The exceptional-root contribution is also uniform and negligible,
because

\[
                         n2^{n/2}=o(D^R_H).
\tag{5.14}
\]

Hence (5.2a) holds with probability \(1-o(1)\). Separately, the
probability that any FIFO column survives is at most its expectation
\(\exp[-\Omega_\varepsilon(nr)]=o(1)\). A simultaneous choice exists,
and the proof is complete. \(\square\)

The finite first-moment cutoff exposed by the proof is

\[
 u_*=\bigl((n-1)!\bigr)^{-1/(nr)}
                         =e^{-1+o(1)}.
\tag{5.15}
\]

This is a sufficient cutoff for the complement-pair construction, not
a converse above \(u_*\).

The residual in Theorem 5.1 is not proved reachable as the leave of a
partial matching. Therefore it is not a counterexample to the desired
global near-factor. It proves, exactly, that scalar balance,
congruences, singleton regularity, positive density, all unordered
inclusion profiles through the Gaussian depth \(H\), and favorable
time-zero pair codegrees are insufficient state variables for an
iterative matching proof.

### Theorem 5.2 (deadness is invisible to a linear fraction of admissible local variables)

Retain the notation and random owner construction of Theorem 5.1. Put

\[
 h=nr,\qquad P={W\over2},\qquad Q={d\over2},\qquad
 v={Q\over P}={d\over W},
\tag{5.16}
\]

and let \({\cal D}\) be the event that no faithful \(F_r\)-column has
all its owners retained.  Let

\[
 v_0=e^{-1-2\varepsilon},\qquad
 \alpha_\varepsilon={1\over4}\min(v_0,1-v_0),
\tag{5.17}
\]

\[
 \kappa_\varepsilon
 =\min\left\{{1\over4},
       {\varepsilon\over4\log(1/\alpha_\varepsilon)}\right\}>0.
\tag{5.18}
\]

Condition the uniform \(Q\)-subset of the \(P\) owner complement-pairs
on \({\cal D}\). Then:

1. it extends to an exactly \(S_n\)-invariant law on joint
   scalar-compatible root/owner residuals, every realization of which
   is coordinate-regular and FIFO-dead;
2. uniformly for every \(j\le\kappa_\varepsilon h\), every choice of
   \(j\) distinct complement-pair variables, and every prescribed
   zero-one pattern on them with \(a\) ones and \(b=j-a\) zeros,

   \[
   \Pr(\text{pattern}\mid{\cal D})
      =(1+o(1))v^a(1-v)^b.
   \tag{5.19}
   \]

Thus the absence of a complete ordered owner bundle cannot be detected
from any admissible cylinder involving at most a fixed positive
fraction of the \(h=nr\) pair variables used by one column.

#### Proof

Every column uses \(h\) distinct complement-pairs. By (5.6) and the
union bound,

\[
\begin{aligned}
 \delta_m:=\Pr({\cal D}^c)
 &\le (n-1)!{(Q)_h\over(P)_h}\\
 &\le (n-1)!v^h.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5.20}
\]

Since \(v=v_0+o(1)\) and

\[
                         {\log((n-1)!)\over h}=1+o(1),
\]

we have, for all sufficiently large \(m\),

\[
                         \delta_m\le e^{-\varepsilon h}.
\tag{5.21}
\]

For an unconditioned prescribed pattern on \(j\) distinct pair
variables, fixed-size sampling gives the exact probability

\[
 p_A={ (Q)_a(P-Q)_b\over(P)_j}.
\tag{5.22}
\]

Here \(j=O(h)\), while \(P,Q,P-Q\) are exponential in \(m\).
Consequently, uniformly over all the displayed choices,

\[
 p_A=(1+o(1))v^a(1-v)^b,
\qquad
 p_A\ge\alpha_\varepsilon^j
\tag{5.23}
\]

for sufficiently large \(m\).  The first assertion follows directly
by expanding the three falling-factorial ratios; their total relative
error is

\[
 O\left({j^2\over\min(Q,P-Q)}\right)=o(1).
\]

For the lower bound, \(v\to v_0\), \(1-v\to1-v_0\), and the factor
\(1+o(1)\) is uniform; the conservative factor \(1/4\) in
\(\alpha_\varepsilon\) absorbs it.

For any event \(A\),

\[
\begin{aligned}
 \left|\Pr(A\mid{\cal D})-\Pr(A)\right|
 &\le{\delta_m\over1-\delta_m}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5.24}
\]

Dividing by (5.23), and using \(j\le\kappa_\varepsilon h\), gives

\[
 \left|{\Pr(A\mid{\cal D})\over\Pr(A)}-1\right|
 \le
 {e^{-\varepsilon h}\alpha_\varepsilon^{-j}
  \over1-e^{-\varepsilon h}}
 \le
 2e^{-3\varepsilon h/4}=o(1).
\tag{5.25}
\]

Combining (5.22)--(5.25) proves (5.19).

Uniform fixed-size complement-pair sampling is invariant under every
coordinate permutation. The catalogue of faithful columns and hence
the event \({\cal D}\) are also \(S_n\)-invariant. Conditioning
therefore preserves exact invariance. Every complement-pair union is
coordinate-regular, and \({\cal D}\) is precisely FIFO-deadness. This
proves the owner part of the first assertion.

For the root part, fix any coordinate-regular root residual
\({\cal R}_0\) of size \(z=N-nc\) obtained by removing \(c\) full
coordinate-shift orbits as in Theorem 5.1. Choose a uniformly random
\(\pi\in S_n\) and use \(\pi{\cal R}_0\), independently of the
conditioned owner residual. This root law and the conditioned owner law
are both \(S_n\)-invariant, so their product is invariant. Every
realization has the exact scalar relation and singleton degrees (5.1).
Deadness already follows from the owner side. This proves the joint
assertion. \(\square\)

The variables in Theorem 5.2 are complement-pair variables, not
independent indicators of the two complementary owners. This is the
right admissible class for a faithful column, because the
no-complement theorem says that a column queries at most one owner from
each pair. The theorem does not say that the conditioned residual is a
reachable matching leave.

## 6. Exact proved/conditional boundary

The following obstruction classes are now closed.

1. Root-only additive, modular, parity, and integer-lattice invariants:
   Theorem 1.1 shows that all factor through singleton incidence.
2. Projected root-owner Hall or odd-set cuts: Theorem 2.1 gives an
   exact integral \(r\)-assignment.
3. Local ordered \(r\)-window supply: Theorem 2A.1 finds a literal
   bouquet in every owner family of density above \(O(r/H)=o(1)\),
   with exact local codegrees (2A.10)--(2A.12).
4. Cardinality, divisibility, and singleton leave constraints:
   Theorem 3.1 realizes them with \(o(N)\) and \(o(W)\) leaves.
5. A resolution by orbits of one fixed global coordinate cycle:
   Theorem 3.2 covers only \(O(n^2)\) roots.
6. Post hoc owner assignment after choosing a root factor:
   Corollary 3.3 leaves only the two reversed owner bundles per root
   support.
7. Rational joint degree-two/Fourier invariants: Theorem 4.2 spans both
   nontrivial pair modules.
8. Regeneration based only on those ledgers: Theorem 5.1 supplies an
   exact coordinate-regular positive-density residual with no column,
   even while every unordered inclusion profile through depth \(H\)
   has the correct relative density up to \(o(1)\).
9. Regeneration from local admissible marginals of order
   \(o(nr)\), or even at most \(\kappa_\varepsilon nr\): Theorem 5.2
   makes all such cylinders asymptotically Bernoulli while the full
   column event is identically absent.

What remains is precisely an ordered problem:

> Select \((1-o(1))N/n\) complete \(F_r\)-orbit columns so that their
> \(n\)-root supports and \(nr\)-owner supports are jointly disjoint,
> while retaining the full ordered queue state.

Any positive proof must correlate the selection globally from the
start or introduce a literal queue-changing packet. Any further
negative proof must use the ordered \(r\)-window bundle and cyclic
\(F_r\) coupling, or a genuinely nonlinear or modular invariant above
degree two. No near-perfect packing or coefficient-one theorem is
claimed here.
