# Two-top boundary rectangles are vertically exact but cannot recycle their boundary orbits

Date: 2026-07-27

Method: pure mathematics only. No computation, search, solver, or web
input is used.

## 0. Outcome

Put

\[
 n=2m,\qquad M=m+H,\qquad d=m-3H+1,
\tag{0.1}
\]

and assume \(H\ge3\) and \(m\ge6H+4\). This note independently derives
the nonclosed two-top boundary macro and then audits its chronological
reuse.

1. For every \((m-2)\)-set \(R\) and distinct labels
   \(a,b,z,z'\notin R\), there are two explicit rank-\(M\) tops and one
   first-two-letter swap on each top whose aggregate deck derivative is

   \[
      \Delta_h=0\quad(h\ne H),
   \tag{0.2}
   \]

   while

   \[
    \Delta_H=
    e_{R\cup\{a,z\}}+e_{R\cup\{b,z'\}}
    -e_{R\cup\{a,z'\}}-e_{R\cup\{b,z\}}.
   \tag{0.3}
   \]

   Thus one literal pair of nonclosed boundary swaps leaves exactly one
   four-cell hypersimplex rectangle and has zero collateral at every
   other deletion length.

2. The chronological state of one participating top lies in a
   two-element boundary orbit

   \[
      (u,v,c_1,c_2,\ldots)
      \longleftrightarrow
      (v,u,c_1,c_2,\ldots).
   \tag{0.4}
   \]

   Every rectangle macro toggles two such orbits and never changes an
   orbit itself. Hence an arbitrary chronological composition depends
   only on the parity with which each top orbit is toggled. Consecutive
   reuse of one top alternates a unit transfer with its negative.

3. Consequently, if \(N=\binom{2m}{M}\) tops are available, every
   endpoint displacement generated by these rectangles satisfies

   \[
      \frac12\|\Delta_H\|_1\le N.
   \tag{0.5}
   \]

   If every top path is restored, then \(\Delta_H=0\) exactly. A
   chronology of \(\Theta(W)\) rectangle steps on only
   \(N=(1+o(1))W/m\) tops therefore cancels almost all of its own work;
   it cannot realize a middle displacement of total variation
   \(\Theta(W)\).

4. Changing the declared common \(2H\)-core, changing the decomposition
   of the unchanged tail into filler and protected labels, and re-pairing
   the two tops do not evade this invariant. The boundary orbit is
   intrinsic to the actual top word. A successful dense bank must use a
   separate middle-neutral recharge move which changes that word outside
   the first-two transposition orbit.

5. There is nevertheless an asymptotically maximal one-shot bank. A
   deterministic first-split-pair involution matches all but
   \(e^{-\Omega(m)}N\) rank-\(M\) tops into Johnson-adjacent pairs, and
   every pair supports one explicit macro. Hence

   \[
      \frac{N-o(N)}2
   \tag{0.6}
   \]

   rectangles are simultaneously installable with no repeated top.
   This exhausts the one-shot owner capacity but is still \(o(W)\).

This is an exact parity/chronology obstruction to a recyclable bank made
from the two-top rectangles themselves. It does not rule out an open
bank in which three-top conveyors or a new multi-port associator change
the boundary orbit. The closed-router theorem rules out restoring all
companions around one such recharge, but the fully open changing-core
recharge circuit remains unproved.

## 1. Literal path decks

For a rank-\(M\) top \(U\) and a full injective word

\[
                         p=(p_1,\ldots,p_M)
\tag{1.1}
\]

on \(U\), define, for \(0\le h\le2H\),

\[
 \mathcal D_h(U,p)=
 \sum_{i=1}^{d}
 e_{\,U\setminus\{p_i,p_{i+1},\ldots,p_{i+h-1}\}}.
\tag{1.2}
\]

For \(h=0\), the deleted interval is empty. At \(h=H\), every displayed
target has size \(M-H=m\), so (1.2) is the middle-owner deck.

Let

\[
                         p=(u,v,c_1,c_2,\ldots)
\tag{1.3}
\]

and let \(sp=(v,u,c_1,c_2,\ldots)\). For \(h=0\) the decks are plainly
equal. For \(h=1\), the phase-\(1\) and phase-\(2\) terms are merely
interchanged. For \(h\ge2\), phase \(1\) contains both \(u,v\), every
phase \(i\ge3\) contains neither, and only phase \(2\) changes.

Put

\[
 K_h(U,p)=U\setminus\{u,v,c_1,\ldots,c_{h-1}\}.
\tag{1.4}
\]

Then the exact derivative is

\[
 \boxed{
 \mathcal D_h(U,sp)-\mathcal D_h(U,p)
 =e_{K_h(U,p)\cup\{v\}}-e_{K_h(U,p)\cup\{u\}}}
 \qquad(h\ge2).
\tag{1.5}
\]

No cyclic or asymptotic argument is used here; (1.5) is a phasewise
identity in the retained linear path.

## 2. The explicit two-top rectangle

Choose disjoint data

\[
 |R|=m-2,\qquad
 F=(f_2,\ldots,f_{H-2}),\qquad |F|=H-3,
\tag{2.1}
\]

and distinct labels \(a,b,z,z',x,y\), all outside \(R\cup F\). Let
\(\rho\) be any order of \(R\). Define

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 U_x&=R\cup\{a,b,z,z'\}\cup F\cup\{x\},\\
 U_y&=R\cup\{a,b,z,z'\}\cup F\cup\{y\},
 \end{aligned}
\tag{2.2}
\]

and the two words

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 p_x&=(a,b,x,f_2,\ldots,f_{H-2},z,z',\rho),\\
 p_y&=(b,a,y,f_2,\ldots,f_{H-2},z',z,\rho).
 \end{aligned}
\tag{2.3}
\]

Both words have length \(M\) and list their tops exactly once. Let
\(sp_x,sp_y\) be obtained by swapping their first two letters.
The largest protected interval ends at

\[
                         d+2H-1=m-H.
\tag{2.3a}
\]

Hence the last \(2H\) labels of the common tail \(\rho\) are untouched
at every displayed length and may be declared as one common protected
core on the two tops.

### Theorem 2.1 (all-length cancellation)

For

\[
 \Delta_h=
 \mathcal D_h(U_x,sp_x)+\mathcal D_h(U_y,sp_y)
 -\mathcal D_h(U_x,p_x)-\mathcal D_h(U_y,p_y),
\tag{2.4}
\]

one has (0.2)--(0.3).

#### Proof

The cases \(h=0,1\) were checked directly in Section 1. For \(h\ge2\),
the auxiliary labels \(x,y\) have both been deleted in (1.4). Before
the landmark pair \(z,z'\), the two deleted prefix sets agree. After
both landmarks have been deleted, they again agree. Therefore

\[
 K_h(U_x,p_x)=K_h(U_y,p_y)
 \qquad(2\le h\le2H,\ h\ne H).
\tag{2.5}
\]

At the unique exceptional length,

\[
 K_H(U_x,p_x)=R\cup\{z'\},\qquad
 K_H(U_y,p_y)=R\cup\{z\}.
\tag{2.6}
\]

The first word is oriented \(a\to b\), while the second is oriented
\(b\to a\). Hence (1.5) gives

\[
 \Delta_h=
 \bigl(e_{K_h(U_x,p_x)\cup\{b\}}
       -e_{K_h(U_x,p_x)\cup\{a\}}\bigr)
 -\bigl(e_{K_h(U_y,p_y)\cup\{b\}}
       -e_{K_h(U_y,p_y)\cup\{a\}}\bigr).
\tag{2.7}
\]

Equation (2.5) gives zero for every \(h\ne H\). Substitution of (2.6)
into (2.7) gives

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \Delta_H={}&e_{R\cup\{b,z'\}}-e_{R\cup\{a,z'\}}\\
             &+e_{R\cup\{a,z\}}-e_{R\cup\{b,z\}},
 \end{aligned}
\tag{2.8}
\]

which is exactly (0.3). \(\square\)

The construction uses one old and one new path at each of two distinct
tops. It is therefore squarefree at top ownership. It is nonclosed:
both endpoint paths remain changed.

## 3. The intrinsic boundary orbit

For an actual top word \(p=(u,v,c_1,\ldots,c_{M-2})\), define its
boundary orbit

\[
 \mathcal O(p)=
 \{(u,v,c_1,\ldots,c_{M-2}),
   (v,u,c_1,\ldots,c_{M-2})\}.
\tag{3.1}
\]

This datum is intrinsic to the rooted retained path. It does not mention
the chosen common core, the labels called filler, or the companion top.
The two states of \(\mathcal O\) will be denoted \(p^0,p^1\), and put

\[
 \delta_h(\mathcal O)=
 \mathcal D_h(U,p^1)-\mathcal D_h(U,p^0).
\tag{3.2}
\]

A two-top boundary rectangle toggles two boundary orbits. It never
changes either orbit, because it changes only the order of the first two
letters.

### Theorem 3.1 (parity normal form for every chronology)

Consider any finite chronological sequence of the two-top macros from
Theorem 2.1. The tops, common cores, rectangle data, and pairings may
change from step to step, but no other frame transition is allowed.
For every boundary orbit \(\mathcal O\), let \(t_{\mathcal O}\) be its
number of toggles and let \(\epsilon_{\mathcal O}\in\{0,1\}\) be its
initial state. Then the complete endpoint derivative is

\[
 \boxed{
 \Delta_h^{\rm end}=
 \sum_{\mathcal O:\ t_{\mathcal O}\text{ odd}}
 (-1)^{\epsilon_{\mathcal O}}
 \delta_h(\mathcal O).}
\tag{3.3}
\]

In particular, only the toggle parity of each orbit matters.

#### Proof

Restrict the chronology to one fixed orbit. Its successive contributions
are

\[
 \delta_h(\mathcal O),-\delta_h(\mathcal O),
 \delta_h(\mathcal O),-\delta_h(\mathcal O),\ldots
\tag{3.4}
\]

if the initial state is \(0\), and the negative alternating sequence if
the initial state is \(1\). The sum is zero for an even number of toggles
and \((-1)^\epsilon\delta_h\) for an odd number. Sum independently over
the orbits. \(\square\)

### Corollary 3.2 (closed circuits are null)

If every top path is restored at the endpoint, then

\[
                         \Delta_h^{\rm end}=0
                         \qquad(0\le h\le2H).
\tag{3.5}
\]

Thus no closed circuit of two-top boundary rectangles is a recyclable
load catalyst.

### Corollary 3.3 (endpoint variation bound)

Let \(q\) be the number of boundary orbits toggled oddly. Then

\[
                         \frac12\|\Delta_H^{\rm end}\|_1\le q.
\tag{3.6}
\]

In particular \(q\le N\) in a one-path-per-top table, proving (0.5).

#### Proof

At \(h=H\), equation (1.5) is one Johnson unit transfer and has
\(\ell_1\)-norm two. Apply the triangle inequality to (3.3). A current
top path belongs to one boundary orbit, so at most one oddly toggled
orbit can remain at each top. \(\square\)

The number of chronological rectangle steps does not appear in (3.6).
One may execute \(10^{100}\) steps on the same finite top bank; all
but the final odd toggle at each top cancel in the endpoint ledger.

## 4. Exact changing-core audit

Theorem 3.1 permits the declared core and all rectangle data to change
at every step. This freedom does not move a top from one orbit to
another. Indeed, for a fixed current word, the first unordered pair
\(\{u,v\}\) and the ordered tail \((c_1,\ldots,c_{M-2})\) are physically
recoverable before a core is named.

### Theorem 4.1 (core changes do not recharge a boundary orbit)

Suppose a chronological circuit uses only the literal first-two-letter
swaps from Section 2, but may choose a new protected \(2H\)-core, new
filler names, new landmark names, and a new companion top whenever the
current word admits such a description. Then every top remains in its
initial boundary orbit and the parity normal form (3.3) holds.

#### Proof

Every allowed transition replaces

\[
                         (u,v,c_1,c_2,\ldots)
 \quad\text{by}\quad
                         (v,u,c_1,c_2,\ldots).
\tag{4.1}
\]

Neither side depends on how the unchanged suffix is subsequently
partitioned or which subset of its late labels is declared to be the
protected core. Thus the physical orbit (3.1) is unchanged. Apply
Theorem 3.1. \(\square\)

This theorem is the exact chronology obstruction requested for a
changing-core circuit made from the rectangle macro itself. A genuine
recharge must contain a different physical transition, for example a
middle-neutral conveyor transposition which moves a suffix label into a
boundary position. Such a transition changes (3.1) and is outside the
theorem.

The closed-companion routing theorem shows that an arbitrary interior
occurrence cannot be recharged in this way and then returned while every
companion path is restored. It does not exclude an open multi-top
recharge circuit in which many companion paths remain changed and later
serve as other rectangle endpoints.

## 5. The maximal one-shot bank

The local positive capacity can also be calculated exactly. Fix

\[
                         B=R\cup\{a,b,z,z'\}\cup F,
                         \qquad |B|=M-1.
\tag{5.1}
\]

There are

\[
                         |[2m]\setminus B|=m-H+1
\tag{5.2}
\]

petal tops \(B\cup\{x\}\). Split their auxiliary labels into sets
\(L_0,L_1\). Give tops in \(L_0\) the first word type in (2.3), and tops
in \(L_1\) the second word type. Every matching between \(L_0,L_1\)
is a family of simultaneously applicable, top-disjoint rectangles.

### Proposition 5.1 (sharp petal-star bank)

The largest simultaneous bank in one fixed petal star has size

\[
                         \left\lfloor\frac{m-H+1}{2}\right\rfloor.
\tag{5.3}
\]

It changes exactly twice as many top paths and is collar-neutral at all
\(h\ne H\).

#### Proof

A balanced split and a perfect matching attain (5.3). Every macro uses
two distinct petal tops, so no top-disjoint family can be larger.
The all-length assertion is Theorem 2.1 summed over the matching.
\(\square\)

The petal-star construction can be globalized without a nibble.
Partition the ground coordinates into fixed pairs

\[
 [2m]=\bigsqcup_{j=1}^{m}\{q_{j,0},q_{j,1}\}.
\tag{5.4}
\]

Call a top split at \(j\) if it contains exactly one label of the
\(j\)-th ground pair. For every top having a split pair, let \(j(U)\)
be its first split pair and let \(U^*\) be obtained by exchanging the
two labels of that pair.

### Theorem 5.2 (deterministic near-perfect rectangle bank)

The map \(U\mapsto U^*\) is a fixed-point-free involution on all tops
having a split pair. It partitions them into Johnson-adjacent pairs.
Every pair can be equipped independently with one macro from Theorem
2.1. Consequently there is a simultaneous top-disjoint bank of size

\[
 \frac12\left(
 N-\mathbf 1_{2\mid M}\binom{m}{M/2}
 \right)
 =\frac{N-o(N)}2.
\tag{5.5}
\]

#### Proof

Exchanging the two labels of the first split ground pair preserves all
earlier pair occupancies and preserves the first split index. Thus
\((U^*)^*=U\), and \(U,U^*\) differ by one deletion and one insertion.

Put \(B=U\cap U^*\), so \(|B|=M-1\). Partition \(B\) into four labels
\(a,b,z,z'\), an ordered \((H-3)\)-set \(F\), and the remaining
\((m-2)\)-set \(R\). The two labels in \(U\triangle U^*\) play the
roles of \(x,y\). Equations (2.2)--(2.3) now give one literal rectangle
on this top pair. Different involution pairs are top-disjoint, so all
macros are simultaneously applicable.

A top has no split pair exactly when every ground pair contributes zero
or two labels. Such a top exists only for even \(M\), and is specified
by choosing its \(M/2\) full ground pairs. This gives the exceptional
term in (5.5). Under \(H\le m/6\),
\(\binom{m}{M/2}=e^{-\Omega(m)}N\), proving the asymptotic formula.
\(\square\)

Across any top-disjoint global collection, the elementary owner bound is

\[
                         \#\text{rectangles}\le\frac N2.
\tag{5.6}
\]

This is \(o(W)\) in the calibrated regime. It is enough for a sparse
absorber but not for a displacement whose total variation is
\((1-o(1))W\).

## 6. Dense-bank consequence

Suppose an initial load \(K\) and desired load \(L\) satisfy

\[
                         \frac12\|L-K\|_1=(1-o(1))W.
\tag{6.1}
\]

Any chronology built only from the collar-neutral rectangle macros has,
by Corollary 3.3,

\[
                         \frac12\|\Delta_H^{\rm end}\|_1
                         \le N=(1+o(1))\frac Wm=o(W),
\tag{6.2}
\]

and therefore cannot connect \(K\) to \(L\), regardless of how often
the tops are reused.

This proves a sharper statement than the raw count of available toggles:
even a bank containing \(\Theta(W)\) chronological rectangle occurrences
has only \(O(N)\) net middle action unless it is recharged between uses.

The exact missing primitive is now:

> A squarefree open recharge circuit which changes the intrinsic
> boundary orbit (3.1) on \(\Theta(W)\) rectangle occurrences, has
> average \(\Theta(m)\) recharges per rank-\(M\) top, and closes its
> aggregate nonmiddle trace and owner ledgers without restoring each
> individual companion.

No known parity invariant rules out that open circuit. Conversely, the
abstract tetrahedral permutation monodromy does not construct it because
its canonical source chronology fails at the first mixed face.

## 7. Exact boundary

Proved:

1. the explicit two-top words and top sets;
2. exact cancellation at every deletion length \(h\ne H\);
3. the signed four-cell hypersimplex rectangle at \(h=H\);
4. the intrinsic two-state boundary orbit and the parity normal form for
   every chronology of rectangle macros;
5. nullity of every closed rectangle circuit;
6. the bound \(\frac12\|\Delta_H\|_1\le N\), independent of chronology
   length;
7. persistence of the obstruction under changing declared cores and
   arbitrary re-pairing of the same physical boundary states; and
8. a sharp one-shot bank of \(\lfloor(m-H+1)/2\rfloor\) rectangles in
   one petal star; and
9. an explicit global bank of \((N-o(N))/2\) simultaneously installable
   rectangles, attaining the one-shot owner bound asymptotically.

Not proved:

1. an open changing-core circuit which moves between different boundary
   orbits;
2. simultaneous squarefree installation of \(\Theta(W)\) recharged
   rectangle occurrences;
3. cancellation of the recharge circuit's complete nonmiddle trace; or
4. coefficient one.

The two-top rectangle solves the vertical-collar problem exactly, but it
does not solve reuse. Its chronology is a product of two-cycles. Dense
recycling begins only when a separate physical move changes the boundary
orbit itself.
