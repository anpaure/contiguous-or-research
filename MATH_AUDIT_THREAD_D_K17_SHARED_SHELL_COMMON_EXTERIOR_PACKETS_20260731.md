## 3. Common-exterior packet lemma

Represent the source factor as its middle-levels incidence two-factor.  A
rank-eight row `L_i` is incident with two rank-nine owners.  Select one as
the retained owner

\[
 V_i=L_i\cup\{x\}
\]

and the other as the removed owner `U_i`, where the same exterior coordinate
`x` is used throughout the packet.  Suppose

\[
 L_i\subset U_{i+1}
\]

cyclically and all selected rows and removed owners are distinct.  Replace
the old incidences `L_i U_i` by `L_i U_(i+1)`.

### Lemma 3.1

This replacement is an alternating `C_(2t)` switch.  It preserves every
rank-eight incidence colour rowwise and preserves the complete immediate
upper-turn multiset.  Only the lower turns at the removed owners can change.

### Proof

The old and new incidence at `L_i` both have intersection `L_i`, so the
rank-eight palette is unchanged.  Since `U_(i+1)` has rank nine and contains
`L_i`, write `U_(i+1)=L_i+z_i`.  The new upper turn at `L_i` is

\[
 V_i\cup U_{i+1}=L_i+x+z_i=U_{i+1}+x.
\]

But `U_(i+1)+x` is the old upper turn at the next selected row: the old
incidence there exchanges `x` with its unique coordinate outside that row.
Hence new upper turns are a cyclic permutation of the old ones.  Every
unselected upper turn is unchanged.

At a removed owner `U_i`, let `E_i` be its retained rank-eight incident
row.  Its old and new lower turns are respectively

\[
 E_i\cap L_i,
 \qquad
 E_i\cap L_{i-1}.
\]

No other owner turn changes.  This proves the claim. `square`

The switch is therefore a perfect matching of inseparable joint columns

\[
 (L_i,U_{i+1};\ V_i\cup U_{i+1},\ E_{i+1}\cap L_i),
\]

with the three augmented edges

\[
 \{L_iU_{i+1},\ L_i-(V_i\cup U_{i+1}),\
   (E_{i+1}\cap L_i)-U_{i+1}\}.
\]

The upper identity is automatic in this family, but the lower-turn and
common-core matching rows are not.
