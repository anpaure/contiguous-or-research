# Affine pair-cell traces and the exact voltage criterion for two-trace face glue

**Date:** 2026-08-04  
**Status:** unconditional pure-mathematical theorem.  This note refines the
face-glue gate in
MATH_THEOREM_TOUCH_NUMBER_BINARY_RESIDUE_AND_FACE_GLUE_GATE_20260804.md.
It identifies every pair-cell trace with an affine binary subspace, quotients
the common-pair face directions, and expresses the two-trace face-glue graph
as a complete fibre blow-up of a finite abelian voltage cover.  Connectedness
is equivalent to base connectedness plus spanning of the full cycle-voltage
group.  It does **not** construct a globally disjoint two-touch dicut,
a Hamilton interval, residence collars, a palette, or a compiler.

## 0. Result

Identify an \(m\)-dimensional source pairing cell \(C\) with

\[
                         V=\mathbb F_2^m,
\]

using the \(m\) singleton source pairs as the standard cube directions
\(E=\{e_1,\ldots,e_m\}\).

Every nonempty trace \(T=K\cap C\) of a cell from a second pairing frame is
an affine subspace

\[
                         T=t+L\subseteq V.                 \tag{0.1}
\]

Put

\[
 A(L)=\operatorname{span}\{e_j:e_j\in L\}.                \tag{0.2}
\]

The connected components of the induced cube graph on \(T\) are exactly
the cosets of \(A(L)\) in \(t+L\).  In the overlay language,
\(A(L)\) is the span of the free common-pair directions; the quotient
\(L/A(L)\) is generated by the free nontrivial alternating-cycle flips.

Now let

\[
 T_i=t_i+L_i,\qquad A_i=A(L_i),\qquad i=1,2,              \tag{0.3}
\]

be two disjoint traces.  Set

\[
\begin{aligned}
 A&=A_1+A_2, & Q&=V/A, & \pi&:V\to Q,\\
 H_i&=\pi(L_i),&
 X_i&=\pi(t_i)+H_i,&
 H&=H_1\cap H_2.
\end{aligned}                                             \tag{0.4}
\]

Define the quotient bipartite graph \(\mathcal G\) with shores
\(X_1,X_2\), joining \(x\in X_1\) to \(y\in X_2\) when

\[
                         x+y\in \pi(E).                    \tag{0.5}
\]

Here `pi(E)` is a set and \(\mathcal G\) is the simple adjacency graph.
If two source coordinate directions have the same image in `Q`, they do not
create duplicate edges: the face-glue graph records existence of a cube
adjacency, not its coordinate multiplicity.  Parallel edges appearing later
in \(\mathcal B\) are instead distinct `H`-orbits of simple edges of
\(\mathcal G\).

Then the original face-glue graph is obtained from \(\mathcal G\) by
replacing every shore-\(i\) vertex by

\[
 \left|\frac{L_i\cap A}{A_i}\right|                       \tag{0.6}
\]

twins and every base edge by a complete bipartite graph between its two
fibres.  Since both trace shores are nonempty, this first fibre blow-up is
connected if and only if \(\mathcal G\) is connected.  For disconnected
graphs, an isolated base vertex with a nontrivial twin fibre expands to
several isolated face vertices, so no equality of raw component counts is
claimed at this first quotient.

Translation by \(H\) acts freely on \(\mathcal G\).  Let

\[
                         \mathcal B=\mathcal G/H            \tag{0.7}
\]

be the finite bipartite orbit multigraph.  The graph \(\mathcal G\) is the
regular \(H\)-voltage cover of \(\mathcal B\).  If \(\mathcal B\) is
connected and \(\Omega\le H\) is the subgroup generated by the voltages of
its closed walks, then

\[
 \boxed{
 \#\operatorname{Comp}(\mathcal G)=[H:\Omega].}           \tag{0.8}
\]

Therefore the exact two-trace connectivity criterion is

\[
 \boxed{
 \text{face glue connected}
 \iff
 \mathcal B\text{ connected and }\Omega=H.}               \tag{0.9}
\]

Since all groups are binary vector spaces, \(\Omega=H\) means that the
cycle voltages linearly span \(H\).  In particular, if
\(\mathcal B\) is connected, then necessarily

\[
 |E(\mathcal B)|-|V(\mathcal B)|+1\ge\dim H.              \tag{0.10}
\]

A tree base can glue the two traces only when \(H=0\).

## 1. The affine trace

### Theorem 1.1

Let \(R\) be the source pairing, let \(C\) be an \(R\)-cell, and let
\(K\) be a cell of another perfect pairing.  After identifying the
singleton \(R\)-pairs of \(C\) with the coordinates of \(V=\mathbb F_2^m\),
the nonempty trace \(K\cap C\) is an affine subspace \(t+L\).

Moreover, its induced cube-graph components are precisely the affine cosets
of \(A(L)\) from (0.2).

#### Proof

On an alternating component of the overlay of the two pairings, the cell
records impose equations

\[
 x_u+x_v=\rho_{uv}\qquad(\rho_{uv}\in\{0,1,2\}).          \tag{1.1}
\]

After the coordinates fixed by \(C\) are substituted, a right side zero
or two fixes both endpoint bits, while a right side one becomes an affine
binary equation.  Thus the full solution set, when nonempty, is affine over
\(\mathbb F_2\).

More explicitly, every free common overlay pair contributes one standard
basis direction.  Every free nontrivial alternating overlay cycle
contributes the vector which flips all singleton source-pair choices met by
that cycle.  Different overlay components have disjoint supports, so these
vectors are independent.  This recovers the direction space \(L\).

For any affine subspace \(t+L\), a cube edge

\[
                         x\longleftrightarrow x+e_j
\]

stays in \(t+L\) exactly when \(e_j\in L\).  Hence every cube path inside
the trace changes its vertex only by vectors in \(A(L)\), and every vector
in \(A(L)\) is realized by a sequence of such cube edges.  The connected
components are therefore exactly the cosets of \(A(L)\). \(\square\)

This gives the earlier \(2^b\) copies of an \(a\)-face with

\[
 a=\dim A(L),\qquad b=\dim L-\dim A(L).                   \tag{1.2}
\]

## 2. Quotienting the face fibres

Let \(T_1,T_2\) be as in (0.3).  Their face-component sets are

\[
 \mathcal F_i=(t_i+L_i)/A_i.                              \tag{2.1}
\]

The projection \(\pi:V\to Q=V/(A_1+A_2)\) induces a surjection

\[
 \varphi_i:\mathcal F_i\longrightarrow X_i               \tag{2.2}
\]

with kernel

\[
                         K_i=\frac{L_i\cap A}{A_i}.         \tag{2.3}
\]

### Theorem 2.1 (complete fibre blow-up)

Two face components \(F_1\in\mathcal F_1\) and
\(F_2\in\mathcal F_2\) are adjacent in the source cube if and only if

\[
 \varphi_1(F_1)+\varphi_2(F_2)\in\pi(E).                  \tag{2.4}
\]

For every edge \(xy\) of \(\mathcal G\), every face in
\(\varphi_1^{-1}(x)\) is adjacent to every face in
\(\varphi_2^{-1}(y)\).

Consequently the face-glue graph is connected if and only if
\(\mathcal G\) is connected.

#### Proof

Write

\[
 F_i=t_i+\ell_i+A_i.
\]

If a cube edge joins the two faces, there are \(a_i\in A_i\) and
\(e_j\in E\) such that

\[
 t_1+\ell_1+a_1+t_2+\ell_2+a_2=e_j.                     \tag{2.5}
\]

Projection modulo \(A=A_1+A_2\) gives (2.4).

Conversely, assume (2.4), and choose \(e_j\in E\) which realizes it.  Then

\[
 t_1+\ell_1+t_2+\ell_2+e_j\in A_1+A_2.
\]

Write this vector as \(a_1+a_2\), with \(a_i\in A_i\).  The vertices

\[
 t_1+\ell_1+a_1\in F_1,\qquad
 t_2+\ell_2+a_2\in F_2
\]

differ by \(e_j\), so they form a cube edge.  The argument uses only the
images \(x,y\), and therefore works for every pair of faces in the two
fibres.  Thus each base edge expands to a complete bipartite graph.
This remains true when several coordinate directions have one image under
`pi`: any one realizing direction suffices, and the correction vector for
each chosen pair of faces is absorbed in `A=A_1+A_2`.

Replacing vertices by nonempty twin fibres and edges by complete bipartite
graphs never joins distinct base components.  Every base component
containing an edge expands to one connected component.  An isolated base
vertex may split into several isolated twins, but because both shores are
nonempty this exceptional case cannot occur when \(\mathcal G\) itself is
connected.  The asserted connectedness equivalence follows. \(\square\)

## 3. The finite abelian voltage cover

The common image subgroup

\[
                         H=H_1\cap H_2                    \tag{3.1}
\]

acts on both shores of \(\mathcal G\) by translation.  It preserves every
edge because in characteristic two simultaneous translation adds `2h=0`
to the edge difference, and it acts freely on vertices.  It also acts freely
on edges and has no edge inversions: the two bipartite shores are preserved,
and an edge stabilizer fixes its initial vertex.  Therefore every quotient
edge orbit has exactly one lift incident with each vertex over its initial
endpoint.  Thus \(\mathcal B=\mathcal G/H\) is a quotient multigraph and
\(\mathcal G\) is a regular graph cover with deck group \(H\).

Choose one representative \(s(v)\) in \(\mathcal G\) for every vertex
\(v\) of \(\mathcal B\).  For each oriented edge orbit
\(\bar e:u\to v\), take the unique lifted edge whose initial vertex is
\(s(u)\).  Its terminal vertex has the form

\[
                         s(v)+\omega(\bar e)
\]

for a unique \(\omega(\bar e)\in H\).  This is the edge voltage.  Reversing
an edge negates its voltage; over \(\mathbb F_2\) this is the same voltage.
Changing the section \(s\) changes voltages by a coboundary and does not
change the subgroup generated by closed-walk voltages.

### Theorem 3.1 (component formula)

For each connected component \(\mathcal B_0\) of \(\mathcal B\), let
\(\Omega(\mathcal B_0)\le H\) be the subgroup generated by the voltages of
closed walks in \(\mathcal B_0\).  Its inverse image in \(\mathcal G\) has

\[
                         [H:\Omega(\mathcal B_0)]          \tag{3.2}
\]

connected components.  Hence

\[
 \#\operatorname{Comp}(\mathcal G)
 =
 \sum_{\mathcal B_0\in\operatorname{Comp}(\mathcal B)}
 [H:\Omega(\mathcal B_0)].                                \tag{3.3}
\]

In particular, (0.8)--(0.9) hold.

#### Proof

Identify the vertices above \(v\in V(\mathcal B)\) with pairs
\((v,h)\), \(h\in H\), through the map

\[
                         (v,h)\longmapsto s(v)+h.
\]

An oriented edge \(\bar e:u\to v\) lifts as

\[
                         (u,h)\longrightarrow
                         (v,h+\omega(\bar e)).             \tag{3.4}
\]

Fix a base vertex \(v_0\).  A lifted closed walk starting at
\((v_0,h)\) ends at

\[
                         (v_0,h+\omega(W)),
\]

where \(\omega(W)\) is its total voltage.  Therefore two vertices in the
fibre above \(v_0\) lie in the same component exactly when their labels
differ by an element of \(\Omega(\mathcal B_0)\).  Each coset of
\(\Omega(\mathcal B_0)\) gives one component, and base connectedness lets
every component meet every fibre over \(\mathcal B_0\).  This proves
(3.2)--(3.3). \(\square\)

### Corollary 3.2 (cycle-rank obstruction)

If \(\mathcal B\) is connected and the face-glue graph is connected, then

\[
 \dim H\le
 |E(\mathcal B)|-|V(\mathcal B)|+1.                       \tag{3.5}
\]

#### Proof

Choose a spanning tree of \(\mathcal B\).  Closed-walk voltages are
generated by the voltages of the fundamental cycles of the non-tree edges.
There are
\(|E|-|V|+1\) such edges, while connected face glue requires those voltages
to span \(H\). \(\square\)

Parallel edge orbits are retained in \(\mathcal B\); they can form genuine
two-edge voltage cycles.  They are distinct translation orbits of adjacency
edges, not multiple physical coordinate labels on one adjacency.  This is
why the quotient is naturally a multigraph even though the face-glue graph
records only adjacency.

## 4. Consequence for the two-touch resident selector

Assume the arithmetic hypotheses of the parent touch theorem, in particular
`M>nu_2(W_r)`, a source cell of dimension at least `M`, and
`|U| congruent to W_r modulo 2^M`.  Consider a two-touch exact extension

\[
                         C\setminus U=T_1\mathbin{\dot\cup}T_2. \tag{4.1}
\]

The binary touch theorem already forces

\[
 \operatorname{wt}_2((-W_r)\bmod2^M)\le2.                \tag{4.2}
\]

The present theorem makes the geometric gate exact:

\[
\boxed{
\begin{array}{c}
C\setminus U\text{ connected}\\
\Updownarrow\\
\mathcal B\text{ connected and the cycle voltages span }
H=\pi(L_1)\cap\pi(L_2).
\end{array}}                                               \tag{4.3}
\]

Thus a candidate with disconnected base is impossible.  A candidate with
connected tree base is impossible whenever \(H\ne0\).  More generally, a
base with cycle rank below \(\dim H\) cannot be a two-touch selector,
regardless of the outside compensation cells or residence collars.

When (4.3) holds, the face-glue graph is connected, but this is still only
the connectedness row of the selector.  To obtain one cyclic interval and a
\(D\)-resident source Hamilton cycle one must additionally provide:

1. a Hamilton path through the glued faces, or another exact interval
   certificate;
2. a Hamilton path through \(U\) and two boundary cube edges;
3. literal multi-seam \(D\)-residence collars;
4. disjoint full compensation cells satisfying the global one-way dicut;
   and
5. the owner, macro Hall, palette, and compiler interfaces.

Accordingly, the surviving two-touch gate is now algebraic but still
correlated:

\[
\boxed{
\text{two-bit residue}
+\text{base connectivity}
+\text{cycle-voltage span}
+\text{interval/residence/global dicut}.
}                                                         \tag{4.4}
