## 1. Exact definition of (b_1(P))

\[
 P=X_0(P),X_1(P),X_2(P),X_3(P),X_4(P)=\overline P     \tag{1.1}
\]

be the infinity-cut Johnson geodesic in the row rooted at
(P\in\mathcal D_4).  There are unique deletion and insertion orders

\[
 a_1(P),\ldots,a_4(P)\quad\hbox{and}\quad
 b_1(P),\ldots,b_4(P)                                 \tag{1.2}

such that

\[
 X_t(P)=
 \bigl(P\setminus\{a_1(P),\ldots,a_t(P)\}\bigr)
 \cup\{b_1(P),\ldots,b_t(P)\}.                       \tag{1.3}
\]

Therefore the definition is literally

\[
 \boxed{b_1(P)\text{ is the unique element of }X_1(P)\setminus P.}
                                                               \tag{1.4}

Equivalently, if (Y_0(P)=X_0(P)\cup X_1(P)), then

\[
                         b_1(P)=Y_0(P)\setminus P,     \tag{1.5}

and the child-window identity gives

\[
                   \bigcap_{t=1}^4X_t(P)=\{b_1(P)\}. \tag{1.6}
\]

For an arbitrary factor, (b_1(P)) is **not** defined by the first return
of the word (P).  That description is a special property of the
canonical MSW path.

## 3. Complete ledger conservation laws

For every (T\subseteq[8]), exact (X)- and (Y)-ownership says

\[
 \sum_{P\in\mathcal D_4}\sum_{t=0}^4
       \mathbf1_{\{T\subseteq X_t(P)\}}
   =\binom{8-|T|}{4-|T|},                              \tag{3.1}
\]

for (|T|\le4), and

\[
 \sum_{P\in\mathcal D_4}\sum_{t=0}^3
       \mathbf1_{\{T\subseteq Y_t(P)\}}
   =\binom{8-|T|}{5-|T|},                              \tag{3.2}
\]

for (|T|\le5).  These are the complete containment-moment form of the
two ledgers.  At (|T|=4) and (5), respectively, they are the individual
state equations, so all linear ledger identities are generated by

Fixed complementary ports give, independently,

\[
 \#\{(P,t):a_t(P)=j\}=d_j,qquad
 \#\{(P,t):b_t(P)=j\}=14-d_j,                         \tag{3.3}
\]

where (d_j) is (2.2).  In particular (b_1(P)\ne1), and if

\[
 n_j=\#\{P:b_1(P)=j\},                                \tag{3.4}

then

\[
 n_1=0,qquad \sum_{j=2}^8n_j=14,qquad
 0\le n_j\le14-d_j.                                   \tag{3.5}

The point-margin instance of (3.1) is

\[
 \sum_{P\ni j}d_P(j)
 +\sum_{P\not\ni j}(5-s_P(j))=35,                    \tag{3.6}
\]

where (d_P(j)) is the deletion position of (j), and (s_P(j)) is its
insertion position.  Equivalently,

\[
 \sum_{P\ni j}d_P(j)-\sum_{P\not\ni j}s_P(j)
                         =5d_j-35.                     \tag{3.7}
\]

The (Y)-point margin gives the same equation, not a second one.

There is a useful exact pair law.  For distinct (i,j), subtracting the
(X)-pair margin (inom62=15) from the (Y)-pair margin
(inom63=20) gives

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}

 &+\#\{P:j\in P,i\notin P, s_P(i)\le d_P(j)\}.

Indeed, if both coordinates start on the same side of the port, their
numbers of common (X)- and (Y)-occurrences agree.  If (iin P) and

(j) is inserted no later than (i) is deleted.  Summing proves (3.8).

For (E_1=\{2,3\}), every Dyck port contains at least one member of

\[

## 4. The forced (b_1=8) count

Every port contains (1) and excludes (8).  Write (d_P=d_P(1)) and
(s_P=s_P(8)).  A row contains both (1,8) in exactly

\[
                              (d_P-s_P)_+              \tag{4.1}
\]

of its five (X)-states.  Since exactly fifteen four-subsets of ([8])
contain both coordinates, (3.1) gives

\[
                         \sum_P(d_P-s_P)_+=15.         \tag{4.2}
\]

The pair law (3.8) becomes

\[
                         \#\{P:s_P\le d_P\}=5.        \tag{4.3}
\]

For every row, (1\le s_P,d_P\le4), so a positive summand in (4.2) is
at most three.  Only the five rows in (4.3) can contribute.  Therefore
equality in (4.2) forces all five summands to equal three.  On those five
rows,

\[
                              s_P=1,qquad d_P=4.       \tag{4.4}
\]

Every other row has (d_P<s_P).  Since (s_P=1) is exactly the condition
(b_1(P)=8), this proves (0.3).  It also proves the dual statement that
coordinate (1) is the last deleted coordinate on exactly five rows.

This invariant is forced by the complete (X/Y) margins and the special
universal port pair (1\mid8).  It does not extend to the middle pairs.

## 7. Suffix-context scope

Let (B) be a fixed Dyck word.  For every (P\in\mathcal D_4), the MSW
concatenation identity is

\[
 P(PB)=P(P)B\ \Vert\
       \overline P\bigl(P(B)\setminus\{B\}\bigr).     \tag{7.1}
\]

The first segment is exactly the canonical four-step local path with the
same spectator (B) adjoined.  The second segment depends only on the
common terminal port (overline P), so it is unchanged when the local
factor is replaced by (G).

The rooted context theorem therefore preserves both complete ledgers and
the rowwise endpoints on the fourteen roots (PB).  On every other
(mathcal D_{4+|B|/2})-root, retain the canonical row.  The resulting
factor is exact and anchored.  Its first transition occurs in the first
segment of (7.1), so the local insertion labels in (5.7) and hence the
transfer (0.2) survive literally.

This proves the suffix claim.  It does not justify the same statement for
left concatenation or outer-primitive wrapping, and it does not enlarge
the active eight-coordinate core.
