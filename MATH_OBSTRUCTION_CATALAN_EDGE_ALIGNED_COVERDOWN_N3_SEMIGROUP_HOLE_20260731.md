# Edge-aligned cover-down needs a nonzero-boundary augmentor

Date: 2026-07-31  
Status: exact boundary lemma, smallest literal AGCF lattice-to-matching-
semigroup gap, explicit `2 -> 3` repair, exact capacity-slot affine
obstruction, and exact graphic quotient gate.  No all-parameter cover-down
theorem is claimed, and no cone-normality assertion is made.

## 0. Theorem and verdict

The universal AGCF `2 <-> 2` trade and the arbitrary-`Q`
Delcourt--Postle side-forest theorem do not compose as black boxes.
There are three independent reasons.

1. A balanced trade lies in the kernel of the resource-incidence map and
   therefore cannot change, let alone align, the leave of a near-matching.
   A bank of the one-edge absorbers from handoff `2292AG` covers a leave
   `r` only when `r` is already a nonnegative union of disjoint candidate
   columns.
2. Exact flux and even membership in the full integer candidate lattice do
   not imply that positive decomposition.  The first obstruction occurs at
   `n=3`, while the physical projection is already a rooted disjoint union
   of paths with degree cap two.
3. In the capacity-slot host of handoff `2295ROOT`, the full host leave has
   a forced affine slot surplus.  Moreover future absorber ears must be
   independent after contraction of the retained physical forest; the
   single-shore Delcourt--Postle cycle conflicts do not imply this mixed
   graphic condition.

Thus an exact proof must either choose an aligned leave from the outset or
supply augmenting packets with nonzero incidence boundary.  Alternating
reserve-colour circuits and the existing balanced `2 <-> 2` packets cannot
perform this job.

## 1. Boundary-semigroup lemma

Let \(\partial\) denote the vertex-edge incidence matrix of a hypergraph and
let `M` be a matching.  Its leave vector is

\[
                         r={\bf 1}-\partial{\bf 1}_M.          \tag{1.1}
\]

### Lemma 1.1 (kernel rigidity)

If a packet replaces an edge family `P` by `Q` with

\[
                 \partial{\bf 1}_P=\partial{\bf 1}_Q,         \tag{1.2}
\]

then it leaves `r` unchanged.  Consequently every sequence of balanced
`2 <-> 2` trades preserves the exact residual resource set.

For the `2292AG` absorber identity

\[
                         A+B=C+D,                              \tag{1.3}
\]

the off-to-on switch `B -> C+D` has boundary `A`.  Therefore disjoint such
switches cover a residual vector `r` only if

\[
                         r=\sum_i \chi_{A_i},                  \tag{1.4}
\]

where the target candidates `A_i` are resource-disjoint.  In particular,
membership of `r` in the integer lattice generated by candidate columns is
not enough; it must belong to the positive matching semigroup.

#### Proof

Equation (1.2) makes the covered vector invariant, so (1.1) is invariant.
Equation (1.3) gives

\[
 \partial({\bf 1}_{C}+{\bf 1}_{D}-{\bf 1}_{B})
                         =\partial{\bf 1}_A.
\]

Summing the boundaries of disjoint switches proves (1.4).  Since `r` is a
zero-one vector and every candidate column is nonnegative, two target
columns in (1.4) cannot overlap.  \(\square\)

An alternating reserve-colour **circuit** is another instance of (1.2).
Only an alternating path/tree with exposed boundary can move the leave.

### Theorem 2.1 (flux-correct noncandidate leave)

The leave (2.2) has exactly the resource cardinality and every coordinate
flux of one candidate, but it is not a candidate edge.  Thus `M` is a
maximal matching of size `Cat_3-1=4`.

Its physical projection is four vertex-disjoint `P_4` paths, hence is a
linear forest with degree at most two.  On its covered middle support,
choosing one listed starting endpoint per path gives a protected endpoint
of degree one in every component and a basis of the ordinary root-star
graphic matroid on that support.  This last assertion does not impose a
separately prescribed common contraction or downstream root bank.

#### Proof

For coordinates `0,...,5`, the leave counts `(l_x,m_x,u_x)` are

\[
 (2,3,3),(1,2,2),(1,2,2),(0,1,1),(1,2,2),(1,2,2).       \tag{2.3}
\]

Thus `m_x=u_x=l_x+1` for every coordinate, and the shore sizes are
`(4,3,3)`.

Every parameter-three candidate has two complementary middle endpoints.
The complements of the four residual middle sets are respectively

\[
                         234,145,123,035,                       \tag{2.4}
\]

and none is residual.  Hence no candidate has middle resource set `R_M`,
so (2.2) is not candidate-shaped.  Since exactly one candidate's worth of
resources remains, no further edge is disjoint from `M`.

The physical claim follows directly from the four disjoint middle rows in
(2.1).  \(\square\)

This obstruction is sharp in the parameter.  At `n=2`, relabel any

\[

\]

Its complementary resource set is the candidate

\[
                         ac,ad,bd,                               \tag{2.5}

so every one-edge matching extends.

## 3. The leave is in the integer lattice and has a `2 -> 3` repair

Define three further candidates

\[
\begin{array}{c|cccc}
 F_1&014&015&135&235\\
 F_2&034&023&025&125\\
 F_3&124&024&045&035.
\end{array}                                                     \tag{3.1}
\]

Their masks are

```text
(19,35,42,44), (25,13,37,38), (22,21,49,41).
```

### Theorem 3.1 (exact augmenting boundary)

On all three resource shores,

\[
             \chi_{E_3}+\chi_{E_4}+\chi_R
                =\chi_{F_1}+\chi_{F_2}+\chi_{F_3}.              \tag{3.2}
\]

Consequently `E_1,E_2,F_1,F_2,F_3` form a perfect matching, and

\[
             \chi_R=\chi_{F_1}+\chi_{F_2}+\chi_{F_3}
                       -\chi_{E_3}-\chi_{E_4}.                  \tag{3.3}
\]

The residual vector therefore passes **every** integer linear or modular
divisibility test generated by the candidate incidence matrix.  Its
failure is positive matching-semigroup membership, not a missing flux or
parity row.  Equation (3.3) proves lattice membership; it does not assert
that the residual lies in the rational candidate cone, so no normality
"semigroup hole" conclusion is used.

#### Proof

For the middle shore, the right side of (3.2) consists exactly of the eight
middle states of `E_3,E_4` together with the four states in `R_M`.  The
lower resources of (3.1) are

\[
 \{01,15,35,03,02,25,24,04,05\},
\]

which are the six lower resources of `E_3,E_4` plus `R_L`.  The upper
resources are

\[
 \{0145,0135,1235,0234,0235,0125,0124,0245,0345\},
\]

which are the six upper resources of `E_3,E_4` plus `R_U`.  This proves
(3.2).  Resource disjointness from `E_1,E_2` is visible in the same lists.
Equation (3.3) follows.  \(\square\)

Thus the smallest witness is repairable, but only by a packet with nonzero
boundary.  It is a model for the kind of augmenting packet an iterative
cover-down must supply.

## 4. The capacity-slot leave is affine, not an edge union

Use the notation of `2295ROOT`:

\[
 N=\binom{2n}{n-1},\quad P=\binom{2n}{n-2},\quad
 K=\operatorname {Cat}_n,\quad C=\operatorname {Cat}_{n+1}.
\]

The capacity-slot hypergraph has `P` lower vertices, `P` upper vertices,
and `2N-C` slot vertices.  Every atom consumes one lower, one upper and two
slots.

### Proposition 4.1 (unavoidable slot baseline)

If a matching has `P-t` atoms, then its full host leave has shore signature

\[
                         (t,t,2t+\sigma),                       \tag{4.1}
\]

where

\[
 \sigma=2N-C-2P=C-2K={2(n-1)\over n+2}K>0.                    \tag{4.2}
\]

A disjoint union of `t` four-resource host atoms has signature `(t,t,2t)`.
Therefore the **literal full-vertex** leave can never be a union of
candidate atoms.  The correct cover-down target is affine:

\[
                         r=s_0+\sum_i\chi_{A_i},                 \tag{4.3}
\]

where `s_0` is an unused-capacity vector of size `sigma`.  Its slot
locations must be chosen jointly with ordinary degree two and protected
anchor degree one; counts alone do not choose `s_0`.

#### Proof

A matching of `P-t` atoms leaves `t` vertices in each outer palette and

\[
 (2N-C)-2(P-t)=2t+(2N-C-2P)
\]

slots.  The Catalan identities in (4.2) give the displayed value of the
constant.  \(\square\)

For `n=3,4,5,6`, the exact baseline sizes are respectively

```text
4, 14, 48, 165.
```

This proposition concerns the full auxiliary slot host.  If “leave” is
defined only on the two outer palettes, (4.1) is not an obstruction, but
then the missing slot vector remains a separate degree/anchor feasibility
row and is not supplied by `2292AG`.

## 5. Exact graphic gate after a Delcourt--Postle forest

Let `F` be the retained physical forest after deleting the off-state edges
of all reserved packets.  Contract every component of `F`.  If the
on-state part of packet `i` is a private physical ear, suppress its private
internal vertices and let `Sigma_i` be the menu of possible quotient links.

### Proposition 5.1 (graphic Rado gate)

There is one simultaneous packet choice whose union with `F` is acyclic if
and only if

\[
 r_{\rm gr}\!\left(\bigcup_{i\in J}\Sigma_i\right)\ge |J|
       \qquad\text{for every packet set }J.                     \tag{5.1}
\]

For designated packets, with one quotient link each, this says exactly that
their quotient links contain no loop or circuit.  Palette privacy and the
ordinary/anchor degree bounds remain additional partition-capacity rows.

#### Proof

After contraction and suppression, adding private ears is acyclic exactly
when their quotient links are independent in the graphic matroid.  Rado's
independent transversal theorem gives one independent link from every menu
exactly under (5.1).  \(\square\)

The Delcourt--Postle conflicts in `2295ROOT` forbid cycles internal to one
selected shore matching.  They do not assert (5.1) for cycles alternating
between the retained forest, protected central attachments and future
absorber ears.

This gap is literal in the authenticated parameter-three direct-side
fixture of

```text

```

at retained edge id `1`: both marginal rooted palette-saturating shores
exist, but the only synchronized lower support forms quotient circuits;

explicit four-cycles.  Hence arbitrary `Q`, marginal side forests, and
candidate-local absorption do not imply correlated rooted cover-down.

## 6. Exact remaining positive target

A viable edge-aligned theorem must add both missing rows:

1. a boundary-generating exchange network whose augmenting paths/trees
   move every admissible DP residual into the candidate matching semigroup;
   and
2. the quotient-graphic Rado inequalities (5.1), together with the affine
   slot baseline (4.3), throughout that movement.

The full conflict-free colouring may be useful as an exchange reservoir,
but alternating circuits alone are kernel moves.  What must be proved is
endpoint expansion for nonzero-boundary alternating components, not merely
the existence of many additional forest colour classes.
