# Common-permutation frame tables: a degree-eight cube relation and the failure of quartet Markov connectivity

Date: 2026-07-26

Method: pure mathematics only. No finite search, solver, computation, or web
input is used.

## 0. Result and exact scope

Let \(n=2m\), \(M=m+H\), and \(M\ge 7\).  A rooted frame on an
\(M\)-top \(U\) is a bijection from the cyclic position set
\(\mathbb Z_M\) to \(U\).  For a frame \(\pi\), phase \(s\), and proper
length \(1\le \ell<M\), write

\[
 I_\pi(s,\ell)=\{\pi(s),\ldots,\pi(s+\ell-1)\}.
\tag{0.1}
\]

The physical quartet binomials of
`MATH_THEOREM_PROMOTION_RING_QUARTET_HOLONOMY_EXCHANGE_20260726.md`
do **not** form a Markov basis for the common-permutation frame-table
fibre.  There is an explicit eight-top binomial whose two monomials

* select one literal cyclic frame at each touched top;
* have the same owner rows and the same interval rows, phase by phase,
  for every proper interval length;
* remain distinct after quotienting cyclic rotation and reversal; and
* are both isolated vertices in the graph generated by physical quartet
  moves, after the surrounding frame table is chosen suitably.

The obstruction is not a new lattice character.  Its exponent difference
is an integral sum of four quartet exponent differences.  Thus this is a
**conformal/Markov failure**, not a new character: the required
intermediate monomials are absent.  Integer-lattice saturation alone
cannot prove connectivity.  A degree-six double-triangle obstruction was
already known in a sparse fixed-core fibre; the new point here is a
full-top-margin construction, dense enough to carry an
\(\Omega(W)\) exceptional-incidence ledger.

The construction can be placed on \(N_H-o(N_H)\) tops, partitioned into
disjoint eight-top gadgets, where

\[
 N_H=\binom{2m}{M},\qquad MN_H=(1+o(1))W
\tag{0.2}
\]

in the tuned promotion regime.  Consequently deleting \(o(N_H)\) selected
top frames, equivalently deleting \(o(W)\) middle phase incidences, does not
remove all gadgets.  This is the relevant robust statement when
"exceptional" means discarded/ignored frame rows.  The argument does not
prove a lower bound against a model which permits a small set of arbitrary
temporary frame replacements to act catalytically and then be recycled.

## 1. The three-placeholder cube

Fix an ((M-3))-set (C), and three disjoint pairs of labels outside it,

\[
 \{a_0,a_1\},\qquad \{b_0,b_1\},\qquad \{c_0,c_1\}.
\tag{1.1}
\]

For (\epsilon=(i,j,k)\in\{0,1\}^3), put

\[
 U_\epsilon=C\cup\{a_i,b_j,c_k\}.
\tag{1.2}
\]

Choose a rooted cyclic positional word on
(C\cup\{A,B,D\}), with the three placeholder positions in three
different gaps of the cyclic order induced on (C).  For
\(g\in S_{\{A,B,D\}}\), let \(\pi^g_\epsilon\) be the frame obtained by
placing \(a_i,b_j,c_k\) in the placeholder positions according to \(g\),
while fixing every label of \(C\).  We use the right-action convention:
\(\pi^{gh}\) is obtained from \(\pi^g\) by applying \(h\) to the labels
currently occupying the placeholder positions.  In particular, if \(h\)
is a transposition, these two frames differ by that literal placeholder
transposition.  Let

\[
 \tau=(A\ B\ D).
\tag{1.3}
\]

For every (s,\ell), denote the standard basis vector of the interval in
(0.1) by (v^g_\epsilon(s,\ell)).

### Lemma 1.1 (exact phasewise cube identity)

For every phase (s), every (1\le\ell<M), and hence for every common
real phase/length weighting,

\[
 \boxed{
 \sum_{\epsilon\in\{0,1\}^3}
 (-1)^{i+j+k}
 \bigl(v^{1}_\epsilon(s,\ell)-v^{\tau}_\epsilon(s,\ell)\bigr)=0.}
\tag{1.4}
\]

#### Proof

Fix the positional interval and let (S\subseteq\{A,B,D\}) be the set
of placeholders which it contains.  If (S=\varnothing), its label set
is independent of (i,j,k), so its alternating sum is zero.  If
(S=\{A,B,D\}), the interval contains all three selected labels and is
again independent of their permutation, while its alternating sum is
zero.

If (0<|S|<3), then for each fixed placeholder permutation the interval
label set is independent of at least one of (i,j,k).  Summing first over
that unused bit cancels the two equal basis vectors.  This applies
separately to (g=1) and (g=\tau), and proves (1.4).  Weighted summation
is immediate. \(\square\)

Define two frame selections on the eight tops by

\[
 T_0(\epsilon)=
 \begin{cases}
 \pi^1_\epsilon,&i+j+k\text{ even},\\
 \pi^\tau_\epsilon,&i+j+k\text{ odd},
 \end{cases}
 \qquad
 T_1(\epsilon)=
 \begin{cases}
 \pi^\tau_\epsilon,&i+j+k\text{ even},\\
 \pi^1_\epsilon,&i+j+k\text{ odd}.
 \end{cases}
\tag{1.5}
\]

Equation (1.4) says exactly that (T_0) and (T_1) have identical
aggregate interval tables, phase by phase and length by length.  They also
have the same top rows, since both select one frame at each (U_\epsilon).
Thus the degree-eight binomial

\[
 \prod_\epsilon x_{U_\epsilon,T_0(\epsilon)}
 -
 \prod_\epsilon x_{U_\epsilon,T_1(\epsilon)}
\tag{1.6}
\]

lies in every promotion frame-table toric ideal obtained by retaining any
common subset or weighting of the phase/length rows.

This includes the literal lower/upper promotion rows: root-form middle
targets are global complements of the relevant top intervals, and
entrance targets are intervals themselves.  Complementation merely
permutes the standard basis, while a common deleted phase or nested
phase-to-depth schedule merely retains a common subset of the identities
(1.4).

The gadget also lies in a fixed mechanical support whenever the three
placeholder positions have one common mechanical colour and all six labels
in (1.1) lie on the corresponding physical shore.  Every permutation in
(1.3) then preserves the binary positional word.  Thus the obstruction is
not intrinsically caused by crossing the two mechanical shores.

### Lemma 1.2 (dihedral distinction)

For every (\epsilon), the frames (\pi^1_\epsilon) and
(\pi^\tau_\epsilon) are distinct modulo rotation and reversal.

#### Proof

They put every label of (C) in the same rooted position.  A nonidentity
rotation fixes no position, and a reflection of a cycle fixes at most two
positions.  Since (|C|=M-3\ge4), a dihedral map carrying one frame to the
other would therefore be the identity.  It would then have to fix the
three placeholder labels, contrary to (\tau\ne1). \(\square\)

## 2. Why no quartet starts inside the gadget

Every four-top rectangle contained in the vertex set (1.2) is a
two-dimensional face of the three-cube: it fixes one of (i,j,k) and
varies the other two.  The common ((M-2))-core of that rectangle is (C)
together with the selected label from the fixed pair.

In a physical quartet, the two switched frames differ only by swapping
the two varying placeholder labels.  Consequently their restrictions to
the common ((M-2))-core have exactly the same cyclic order.  On every
face of (1.2), however, the checkerboard selections in (T_0) alternate
between (\pi^1) and (\pi^\tau).  The label from the fixed third pair
moves between two different gaps of the (C)-order under the three-cycle
(\tau).  Hence the two restrictions to the common core differ.

Allowing an independent reversal when frames are represented modulo
dihedral symmetry does not change this conclusion.  Such a reversal would
make the common cyclic order induced on the at least four fixed labels of
(C) equal to its reverse while fixing every label, which is impossible.

It follows that no physical quartet monomial divides the monomial of
(T_0).  The same argument applies to (T_1).

This proves isolation inside the eight-top catalogue.  Section 4 below
chooses the surrounding frame table so that no quartet using tops from
different gadgets is available either.

## 3. The lattice quotient does not see the obstruction

Let (E_{\epsilon,g}) be the standard exponent vector of the variable
(x_{U_\epsilon,\pi^g_\epsilon}), and put

\[
 h_\tau=\sum_\epsilon(-1)^{i+j+k}
             (E_{\epsilon,1}-E_{\epsilon,\tau}).
\tag{3.1}
\]

Choose transpositions (s,t\in S_3) with (\tau=st).  Telescope:

\[
 h_\tau=
 \sum_\epsilon(-1)^{|\epsilon|}(E_{\epsilon,1}-E_{\epsilon,s})
 +
 \sum_\epsilon(-1)^{|\epsilon|}(E_{\epsilon,s}-E_{\epsilon,st}).
\tag{3.2}
\]

In the first sum, (s) swaps two placeholder positions.  Hold the bit of
the remaining label family fixed.  For each of its two values, the inner
four terms are exactly one physical quartet exponent vector; the two
faces occur with opposite signs.  The same statement applies to the
second sum, now to the two label families occupying the positions swapped
by (t) after (s).  Therefore

\[
 \boxed{h_\tau\text{ is an integral signed sum of four quartet vectors}.}
\tag{3.3}
\]

More generally, with (r) binary placeholder families and any
(g\in S_r), choose a transposition word of length (L) for (g).
The (r)-cube alternating relation between (1) and (g) is an integral
sum of (2^{r-2}L) quartet face vectors.  Thus this whole hierarchy of
higher cube identities contributes no new element to the integer lattice
quotient.

There is also an exact unbounded conformal hierarchy.  Fix \(r\ge3\)
with
\[
 r\le \min\{\lfloor M/2\rfloor,n-M\}.
\tag{3.3a}
\]
Take \(g\) to be an \(r\)-cycle, put the \(r\) placeholder positions in distinct gaps of
the common core, and choose (1) versus (g) according to cube parity.
The proof of Lemma 1.1 works verbatim: every proper placeholder subset
misses a binary index.  Every two-face fixes (r-2\ge1) selected labels,
and (g) moves each of them to a different core gap.  Hence no quartet
divides either endpoint monomial.  The resulting exact toric binomial has
degree (2^r) on each side while still lying in the quartet **lattice**.
For each fixed \(r\) satisfying (3.3a), the pair-occupancy construction
of Section 4 partitions \(N_H-o(N_H)\) tops into such \(r\)-cubes
(the tops with fewer than \(r\) single ground pairs number
\(O_r(m^{r-1}2^m)=o(N_H)\)), and hitting one top
per gadget costs

\[
 (2^{-r}-o(1))MN_H=\Omega_r(W)
\tag{3.4}
\]

middle phase incidences.  Thus the degree-eight relation is the first
member of the placeholder-cube hierarchy, not the smallest Markov
obstruction overall.  More generally, adjoining cube moves
of every dimension at most a fixed (R) still misses the ((R+1))-cycle
cube endpoints: each allowed proper subcube fixes a placeholder label
which the long cycle moves.  This last statement concerns the coherent
cube-move hierarchy; it does not classify every unrelated physical move
of comparable algebraic degree.

For fixed \(r\), the global-isolation step also repeats verbatim:
choose the core words of the \(r\)-cube gadgets independently.  On any
cross-gadget top rectangle, compatibility prescribes the unoriented
cyclic order on at least \(M-r-2\) random core labels.  The resulting
factorial denominator dominates the \(\exp(O(m))\) possible rectangles.

Equations (1.6) and (3.3) sharply separate two assertions:

* the quartet **lattice** contains the higher relation;
* the exhibited quartet-lattice decomposition uses intermediate
  transposition frames absent from the endpoint monomials, while the
  independent isolation proof shows that no applicable quartet path of
  any form can start at either endpoint.

In particular, no computation of the saturated integer quotient, even if
it leaves only the already audited affine mod-two characters, can imply
that quartets form a Markov basis.

### 3.1 Exact integral quotient of the untwisted top-rectangle lattice

There is, in fact, no hidden odd torsion in the abstract signed
checkerboard lattice on tops.  Let

\[
 \mathcal V=\binom{[n]}M
\]

and let (L_\square\le\mathbb Z^{\mathcal V}) be generated by all signed
top rectangles

\[
 e_{C+a_0+b_0}-e_{C+a_0+b_1}
 -e_{C+a_1+b_0}+e_{C+a_1+b_1}.
\tag{3.5}
\]

Let (B) record total mass and point degrees:

\[
 Bz=\left(\sum_Uz_U,
           \left(\sum_{U\ni x}z_U\right)_{x\in[n]}\right).
\tag{3.6}
\]

Then, for (2\le M\le n-2),

\[
 \boxed{L_\square=\ker_{\mathbb Z}B.}
\tag{3.7}
\]

In particular \(\mathbb Z^{\mathcal V}/L_\square\cong\operatorname{im}B\)
is free abelian of rank \(n\).  Its mod-two characters are precisely the
affine degree-zero/one margins, and this untwisted signed rectangle
quotient has no torsion at any prime.

To prove (3.7), work first over an arbitrary field (K).  If
(w:\mathcal V\to K) annihilates (3.5), then for distinct (x,y) the
difference

\[
 \Delta_{xy}(S)=w(S+x)-w(S+y)
\tag{3.8}
\]

is unchanged when the ((M-1))-set (S) takes one Johnson-graph step
inside ([n]\setminus\{x,y\}).  That Johnson graph is connected.  Thus
(\Delta_{xy}) is independent of (S).  A common (S) avoiding
(x,y,z) gives the cocycle law
(d_{xy}+d_{yz}=d_{xz}).  It follows, by fixing one label and then using
connectivity of (J(n,M)), that

\[
 w(U)=\alpha_0+\sum_{x\in U}\alpha_x.
\tag{3.9}
\]

This affine function space has dimension (n) over **every** field: its
(n+1) displayed generators have the single relation
(\sum_x1_{x\in U}=M\cdot1), including in characteristics dividing
(M).  Hence the rectangle matrix has rank
(|\mathcal V|-n) over (mathbb Q) and over every (mathbb F_p).
No prime divides the gcd of its maximal minors, so its row lattice is
saturated.  Its rational span is (ker B), which proves (3.7).

This calculation is exact but deliberately limited to the untwisted
top-rectangle model.  Permutation parity itself is intrinsically
\(\mathbb F_2\)-valued: after choosing arbitrary global reference frames,
an integral \(0/1\) lift of a physical quartet can acquire
word-dependent signs at its four tops.  Thus (3.7) is not claimed as a
Smith normal form for the full frame-variable quartet lattice.  The cube
obstruction above is stronger in a different direction: it already has
zero class in the full quartet lattice generated on its coherent
catalogue, yet remains Markov-isolated.

## 4. A global table with isolated endpoints

The local gadgets can be made simultaneous.  Partition the (2m) labels
into (m) fixed pairs.  For an (M)-top (U), call a ground pair full,
empty, or single according as it contributes two, zero, or one labels.
If (f,e,s) are the three counts, then

\[
 f-e=H,\qquad f+e+s=m.
\tag{4.1}
\]

For every fixed full/empty/single pattern with (s\ge3), the choices of
the labels in the single pairs form a cube (\{0,1\}^s).  Fix three of
the single coordinates and partition this cube into its disjoint
three-dimensional coordinate subcubes.  Over all patterns this partitions
every top having at least three single pairs into disjoint gadgets of the
form (1.2).

The number of excluded tops is at most (O(m^2 3^m)).  For (H=o(m)),

\[
 \binom{2m}{m+H}=4^{m-o(m)},
\tag{4.2}
\]

so (O(m^2 3^m)=o(N_H)).  Thus the number of gadgets is

\[
 \frac{N_H-o(N_H)}8.
\tag{4.3}
\]

Choose the positional word of each gadget independently and uniformly,
subject to putting its three placeholders in three prescribed distinct
core gaps.  Give uncovered tops arbitrary frames, independently and
uniformly.  We claim that there is a choice for which neither global
table (T_0) nor (T_1) admits a cross-gadget quartet.

Indeed, the number of possible top rectangles is at most

\[
 \binom{2m}{M-2}(2m)^4=\exp(O(m)).
\tag{4.4}
\]

Consider one which is not a face of one selected gadget.  Its vertices
involve at least two independently chosen positional words.  Conditional
on all but one of them, quartet applicability prescribes the cyclic order
on at least (M-5) of the random core labels of the remaining word.
If that remaining gadget contributes more than one corner, its internal
core restrictions are either already incompatible, or any one compatible
corner still has to satisfy this same prescribed-order condition.
Allowing rotations, reversal, the two tables, and the finitely many
placeholder assignments changes the probability by only a polynomial
factor.  Hence that probability is at most

\[
 \frac{\operatorname{poly}(M)}{(M-6)!}.
\tag{4.5}
\]

The product of (4.4) and (4.5) tends to zero.  Therefore a simultaneous
choice with no cross-gadget quartet exists.  Section 2 excludes the
within-gadget quartets.  Both (T_0) and (T_1) are consequently isolated
vertices of the unrestricted physical quartet graph, while Lemma 1.1 on
each gadget shows that they lie in the same exact all-phase/all-length
frame-table fibre.

For completeness, there is a conditional route to the same density
statement inside a balanced fixed-shore atlas.  It is not used in the
unrestricted theorem.  Pair labels separately within the two shores.
A top for which each shore has at most two single ground pairs belongs to
only \(\operatorname{poly}(m)2^m=o(N_H)\) possibilities.  If every
remaining root type supplies three separated slots of the matching
mechanical colour and the catalogue permits arbitrary within-colour
relabelling, the preceding construction and factorial union bound then go
through.  The parity of the shore sizes, the negligible extreme root
types, and slot availability must be entered explicitly before promoting
this conditional paragraph to a dense fixed-shore theorem.  A smaller
frozen-shore catalogue requires a separate availability audit.

## 5. Exceptional-frame normalization

There are two inequivalent meanings of an "exceptional frame".

1. **Discarded top rows.**  If one deletes or ignores selected top frames,
   then a table with no applicable quartet remains without an applicable
   quartet.  The two global endpoints differ on every covered top.  Thus
   making them agree outside the exceptional set requires
   (N_H-o(N_H)) exceptional top rows.  Even merely hitting every local
   degree-eight gadget requires ((N_H-o(N_H))/8) top rows.

2. **Temporary catalytic replacements.**  If an exceptional operation may
   install an arbitrary frame, use newly enabled quartets, and later recycle
   the exceptional frame, isolation of the initial monomial alone does not
   give an additive lower bound on the number of catalysts.  No such
   recycling lower bound is claimed here.

At the middle row, one selected top frame contributes (M) cyclic phase
occurrences.  Therefore the first, deletion-type obstruction has phase
incidence cost

\[
 M\frac{N_H-o(N_H)}8
 =\left(\frac18-o(1)\right)MN_H
 =\Omega(W)
\tag{5.1}
\]

in the tuned regime (MN_H=(1+o(1))W).  It cannot be removed by an
(o(W)) exceptional-incidence budget.  By contrast, the raw number of all
top frames is (N_H=(1+o(1))W/M=o(W)).  Consequently a statement allowing
"(o(W)) exceptional top frames" without charging their (M) phase
occurrences is vacuous at the promotion scale: it permits discarding the
entire table.

## 6. Exact boundary

Proved:

1. an explicit literal degree-eight common-permutation toric binomial;
2. exact equality phase by phase at every interval length;
3. survival after the rotation/reversal quotient;
4. isolated endpoint monomials for a suitable unrestricted global frame
   table;
5. failure of physical quartets to be a Markov basis;
6. an exact four-quartet integral decomposition of the higher relation,
   proving that the failure is invisible in the lattice quotient; and
7. a dense direct-product construction with an (\Omega(W))
   deletion-type exceptional-incidence toll.

Not proved:

1. a complete Smith normal form of the unrestricted global quartet
   lattice;
2. a higher torsion character beyond the known affine mod-two characters;
3. an (\Omega(W)) lower bound against arbitrary recyclable catalytic
   frame edits; or
4. failure of a larger move library containing the degree-eight cube
   relations.

The mathematically correct next question is therefore not whether the
quartet lattice is saturated.  It is whether adjoining the three-cycle
cube relations (and their conjugates) gives a bounded-degree Markov basis,
or whether the same conformal obstruction iterates to unbounded
placeholder rank.  The general (r)-cube identity above shows where such
an iteration can begin.
