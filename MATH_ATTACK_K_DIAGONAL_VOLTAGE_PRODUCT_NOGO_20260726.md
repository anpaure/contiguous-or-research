# The pentagon product overlay has no diagonal voltage compression

Date: 2026-07-26

Method: pure mathematics only.  No computation, finite search, solver, or
web input is used.

## 0. Result

Consider the old and new five-row rooted pentagon factors in independent
product slots.  Retain every physical product `X/Y` token, including its
slot and endpoint context.  Then a token internal to slot `h` can only join
rows which agree in every coordinate other than `h`.  Thus every overlay
voltage is axis-supported.  A voltage which moves two product coordinates
at once is not the voltage of the same physical token.

The obstruction is already visible in the local edge-coloured graph.  Its
colour monodromy is the full symmetric group `S_5`; it has neither a
nontrivial cyclic quotient carrying the pentagon move nor a nontrivial
row-port quotient.  In `k` full product slots the monodromy is therefore

\[
                              S_5^k,
\]

acting coordinatewise, and a full product skeleton is one overlay
component of size `5^k` on each shore.  A diagonal copy of `S_5` is an
abstract subdirect subgroup, but it is not generated by the independently
labelled product resources and cannot replace their axis generators.

More generally, correlated use of only some complete slot fibres does not
escape the tradeoff.  If a fraction `f` of all axis fibres is active, the
size-biased component moment obeys

\[
                              \boxed{\chi\ge 5^{fk}}.             \tag{0.1}
\]

Consequently a five-row component contains at most one active product
fibre.  No component of bounded size can support a growing number of
product slots.  This is a statewise resource obstruction, before any
shadow projection or cap estimate.

The scope is exact: this closes voltage compression which preserves the
literal Cartesian product ledger.  It does not rule out a new nonproduct
factor with a physical serial connector or an interleaved ledger not
isomorphic to the product ledger.

## 1. The local edge-coloured overlay

Index the old pentagon rows and the new pentagon rows by `1,...,5` in the
order

\[
 123,\quad124,\quad125,\quad135,\quad134.
\]

There are four lower-state colours `x_0,...,x_3` and three adjacent-union
colours `y_0,y_1,y_2`.  For a colour `c`, define `pi_c` by

> the token of colour `c` in old row `i` is owned by new row `pi_c(i)`.

Reading the two exact pentagon tables gives

\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
c&\pi_c\\ \hline
x_0&1\\
x_1&(1\ 4\ 3\ 5\ 2)\\
x_2&(1\ 4\ 5\ 3\ 2)\\
x_3&1\\
y_0&(2\ 4\ 3\ 5)\\
y_1&(1\ 4\ 2)(3\ 5)\\
y_2&(2\ 4\ 5\ 3).
\end{array}                                                    \tag{1.1}
\]

For example, the old `x_1` states

\[
136,126,145,235,234
\]

occur in new rows `4,1,5,3,2`, respectively.  The old first upper states

\[
1236,1246,1245,1235,1234
\]

occur in new rows `1,4,5,3,2`, giving `pi_(y_0)`.  The other entries follow
in the same literal fashion.  Explicitly, the old `y_1` states occur in
new rows `4,1,5,2,3`, and the old `y_2` states occur in new rows
`1,4,2,5,3`; these are precisely the last two permutations in (1.1).
Thus (1.1) is an ownership table, not a row-name relabelling.

### Proposition 1.1 (exact local monodromy)

The colour monodromy of the rooted pentagon overlay is `S_5` in its natural
five-point action.

#### Proof

The identity colour `x_0` lets an alternating two-edge walk realize each
`pi_c` as a permutation of either shore.  Put

\[
 a=\pi_{x_1},\qquad b=\pi_{x_2}.
\]

Both are five-cycles and direct multiplication gives

\[
                           a^{-1}b=(3\ 5\ 4).          \tag{1.2}
\]

Hence `<a,b>` is an even transitive subgroup containing elements of orders
five and three.  It is `A_5`: equivalently, these two elements are a
standard five-cycle/three-cycle generating pair; or, by Lagrange, a proper
alternative would have order `15` or `30`.  A group of order fifteen is
cyclic here and would contain an element of order fifteen, impossible in
`S_5`; an order-thirty subgroup would have index two in the simple group
`A_5`.  Both alternatives are impossible.  Finally `pi_(y_0)` is a
four-cycle and is odd.  The full group is
therefore `S_5`.  \(\square\)

Suspension and any common rooted one-hole context injectively transport
the seven resources.  They may reverse the order in which the colours are
seen, but they do not change their ownership permutations.  Proposition
1.1 therefore applies to the once-suspended packet used in the dense
size-four-slot tensor.

### Corollary 1.2 (local quotient classification)

There is no cyclic five-state voltage presentation preserving all seven
colours.  More generally, every colour-invariant equivalence relation on
the five row ports is either equality or the universal relation.

#### Proof

Translations of `C_5` commute, whereas `a^{-1}b` in (1.2) has order three.
Thus the complete colour table cannot consist of cyclic translations in
any relabelling.  An invariant equivalence relation is a block system for
the natural `S_5` action, which is primitive.  Its only block systems are
the discrete and universal ones.  The universal quotient identifies five
distinct root ports and so is not a rooted exact-factor interface.
\(\square\)

Equivalently, an abelian or cyclic group quotient of the local monodromy
factors through

\[
                         S_5/A_5\cong C_2.             \tag{1.3}
\]

It kills both active five-cycles `a,b`.  The parity quotient consequently
retains no pentagon row transport.

## 2. Product-token addresses are rigid

Take `k` disjoint copies of the suspended pentagon carrier, ordered in the
serial rooted-context product.  A row port is a tuple

\[
                         \mathbf i=(i_1,\ldots,i_k)\in[5]^k.     \tag{2.1}
\]

When the path is internal to slot `h`, every earlier slot is at its
complement port, every later slot is at its root port, and the `h`-th
coordinate contains the transported local state.  Fixed exterior material
is suppressed below.

### Lemma 2.1 (statewise address injectivity)

Let `T` be an internal product `X` or `Y` token in slot `h` and context
`mathbf i`.  If the same physical set `T` occurs in the other product
factor in slot `h'` and context `mathbf j`, then

\[
              h'=h,\qquad j_l=i_l\ (l\ne h),
              \qquad j_h=\pi_c(i_h),                  \tag{2.2}
\]

where `c` is the transported local colour of `T`.

#### Proof

Restrict the physical set to each disjoint coordinate block.  For an
internal `X` token, exactly one block restriction is an internal local
state.  It is neither a root port nor a complement port: exactness already
assigns all root ports and all complement ports to the two endpoint
columns, and an `X` state occurs only once in a factor.  All other block
restrictions are root or complement spectators.  This identifies `h`
from the physical set itself.

For an internal `Y` token the active block is even more directly
identified: it has local cardinality one larger than the endpoint
restrictions.  Thus it also identifies `h`.

In every spectator block, the maps `i_l -> P_(i_l)` and
`i_l -> complement(P_(i_l))` are injective.  Equality of the two physical
sets therefore forces `j_l=i_l` for `l ne h`.  In the active block,
equality is exactly the definition of the local owner matching `pi_c`,
which gives the last equality in (2.2).  \(\square\)

The common connector and boundary tokens do not weaken the lemma.  They
give identity edges or additional port-closure edges and can only merge
components already forced by the internal tokens.

### Theorem 2.2 (axis theorem for every literal product resource)

In the direct ownership overlay of the two `k`-slot product factors, the
edge of colour `(h,c)` is exactly

\[
 (i_1,\ldots,i_h,\ldots,i_k)_-
 \ \longleftrightarrow\
 (i_1,\ldots,\pi_c(i_h),\ldots,i_k)_+.                \tag{2.3}
\]

In particular every allowed voltage is supported on one coordinate axis.
No voltage which changes two row coordinates can preserve the same
physical product token.

#### Proof

Every product token has a unique owner on each exact-factor shore.  Lemma
2.1 identifies those two owners and gives (2.3).  An alternative edge is
not merely a different decomposition of the same ledger: it joins the old
token to a row whose spectator restriction differs, so that row does not
own the token.  The token would then have degree zero at its true new owner
and degree two after the proposed reassignment.  \(\square\)

The global root token has the identity matching on `[5]^k`.  Combining it
with (2.3), the one-shore monodromy contains a copy

\[
                         (S_5)_h
\]

acting on coordinate `h` for every active slot.  These copies commute and
have trivial pairwise intersections.  Hence:

### Corollary 2.3 (exact full-product component and moment)

If all `k` slots carry the old/new pentagon pair, the one-shore monodromy
is

\[
                              S_5^k,                   \tag{2.4}
\]

and is transitive on `[5]^k`.  The product skeleton is one component with

\[
                         s_K=5^k,\qquad \chi_K=5^k.    \tag{2.5}
\]

If exactly `d` coordinate slots are active throughout a Cartesian
subcarrier and the other coordinates are fixed, then every component has
shore size at least `5^d`; for the full `d`-subcube it has size exactly
`5^d`.

The tagged complete depth-one variation of the full `k`-slot component is
also exact.  There are `k5^(k-1)` slot fibres, and each suspended pentagon
fibre has `l_1` variation eighteen.  Therefore

\[
 \mathcal L_{1}^{\rm tag}=18k5^{k-1},\qquad
 \mathcal A_{1}^{\rm tag}=9k5^{k-1}.                  \tag{2.6}
\]

The half-action per product row is `9k/5`.  This tagged identity does not
assert that the untagged physical shadow targets avoid cancellation.

### Corollary 2.4 (the growing Catalan tensor)

In a size-`r` Catalan row, let `k(T)` be the number of marked size-four
fringe slots whose fillings belong to the five suspended-pentagon shapes.
Collapsing all such slots gives skeleton classes of size `5^k`.
Corollary 2.3 makes each class one component, so globally

\[
 \chi_r={1\over C_r}\sum_{\Sigma}25^{k(\Sigma)}
       =\mathbb E_{T\in D_r}5^{k(T)}.                 \tag{2.7}
\]

The exact marked-slot count is

\[
 \sum_{T\in D_r}k(T)
 =5[z^{r-4}](1-4z)^{-1/2}
 =5\binom{2r-8}{r-4},                                \tag{2.8}
\]

and hence

\[
 \mathbb E k(T)=\left({5\over256}+o(1)\right)r.
\]

Jensen applied to (2.7) gives

\[
                         \boxed{
 \chi_r\ge5^{(5/256+o(1))r}.}                        \tag{2.9}
\]

Summing (2.6) over skeletons gives the exact occurrence-tagged totals

\[
 \mathcal L_1^{\rm tag}=18\binom{2r-8}{r-4},
 \qquad
 \mathcal A_1^{\rm tag}=9\binom{2r-8}{r-4}.          \tag{2.10}
\]

With `W_r=(2r+1)C_r`, their densities are

\[
 {\mathcal L_1^{\rm tag}\over W_r}\longrightarrow{9\over256},
 \qquad
 {\mathcal A_1^{\rm tag}\over W_r}\longrightarrow{9\over512}. \tag{2.11}
\]

Each suspended slot occupies a cyclic carrier of at most nine positions.
For a row with `k(T)` disjoint slots, the union-of-blocks two-boundary
argument gives at most `18k(T)` changed starts at any fixed nondegenerate
interval length.  Therefore the untagged endpoint profiles obey the
statewise carrier bound

\[
 {1\over2}\|\mu_q^+-\mu_q^-\|_1
 \le18\sum_Tk(T)
 =90\binom{2r-8}{r-4}
 =\left({45\over256}+o(1)\right)W_r.                 \tag{2.12}
\]

Thus the full tensor reaches critical `Theta(W_r)` tagged action and has
`Theta(r)` active-slot support, but its component moment is exponential.
Physical target aggregation can only reduce the tagged variation, so
(2.10) is not itself a cap-gain statement.

## 3. Why neither a diagonal subgroup nor a group quotient helps

A diagonal subgroup

\[
                 \Delta S_5=\{(g,\ldots,g):g\in S_5\}\le S_5^k       \tag{3.1}
\]

has bounded orbits (at most `120`), and a diagonal five-cycle has orbits
of size at most five.  These are legitimate abstract subdirect actions.
They are not actions of the product ownership ledger.  A colour `(h,c)`
in that ledger supplies the axis element

\[
                 (1,\ldots,1,\pi_c,1,\ldots,1),       \tag{3.2}
\]

not `(pi_c,...,pi_c)`.  Replacing (3.2) by a diagonal element contradicts
Lemma 2.1 in every changed spectator block.

Nor can a group quotient identify the active coordinate groups
diagonally.  The derived subgroup of (2.4) is

\[
                              A_5^k.                   \tag{3.3}
\]

Every normal subgroup of a direct product of centreless nonabelian simple
groups is a product of coordinate factors.  Thus every quotient of (3.3)
either retains a given `A_5` coordinate factor or kills it; it never
identifies two factors as one diagonal `A_5`.  A quotient of `S_5^k` which
kills all the `A_5` factors sees at most the parity cube `(C_2)^k` and,
by (1.3), kills the active five-cycle colours.  Consequently any quotient
which still sees the pentagon routing in `d` slots retains `d` independent
coordinate actions and has a row orbit of size at least `5^d`.

For completeness, the normal-subgroup assertion is elementary here.  If
`N normal A_5^k` contains an element whose `h`-th coordinate is nonidentity,
commuting that element with elements supported only in coordinate `h`
puts nonidentity elements supported only in `h` into `N`.  Their normal
closure is the whole simple factor `(A_5)_h`.  Removing that coordinate
and iterating shows that `N` is precisely a product of coordinate factors.
In particular the diagonal `A_5` is subdirect but is not normal; it cannot
occur as a quotient identification.

This separates two notions which are easy to conflate:

* a diagonal is a possible **subgroup** after one correlates and deletes
  axis generators;
* a resource-preserving product overlay contains every axis generator, so
  its monodromy is the direct product and has no such correlation.

## 4. Arbitrary correlated fibre selection

One might avoid the full product by switching only a correlated set of
slot fibres.  Exactness still forces an entire five-row fibre whenever it
uses the local pentagon trade: on a fixed context fibre, the old/new local
ownership overlay is connected, so the converse component theorem allows
only one complete shore or the other.

Explicitly, let `sigma(a)` record whether root `a` of a fixed fibre uses
the old or new local row segment.  A local resource `t` then occurs

\[
             (1-\sigma(o^-(t)))+\sigma(o^+(t))
\]

times.  Exact one-use is equivalent to
`sigma(o^-(t))=sigma(o^+(t))` on every resource edge.  Proposition 1.1
makes the edge graph connected, so `sigma` is constant on all five roots.
Thus correlation may choose which complete axis lines are active, but it
cannot select a diagonal five-point subset inside any one product fibre.

For a set `C subset [5]^k`, let `L(C)` denote the number of complete
axis-parallel five-point lines contained in `C`.

### Lemma 4.1 (five-ary line entropy inequality)

For every nonempty `C subset [5]^k`,

\[
                         \boxed{5L(C)\le |C|\log_5|C|}.           \tag{4.1}
\]

#### Proof

Let `X` be uniform on `C`.  For coordinate `h`, condition on `X_(-h)`.
The conditional distribution of `X_h` is uniform on the corresponding
coordinate fibre.  If this fibre is a complete five-point line, its
base-five conditional entropy is one; otherwise the entropy is
nonnegative.  Hence

\[
 {5L(C)\over |C|}
 \le \sum_{h=1}^k H_5(X_h\mid X_{-h}).                \tag{4.2}
\]

Han--Shearer gives

\[
 \sum_h H_5(X_h\mid X_{-h})
 =kH_5(X)-\sum_hH_5(X_{-h})
 \le H_5(X)=\log_5|C|.                               \tag{4.3}
\]

Combining (4.2) and (4.3) proves (4.1).  Equality holds for every full
coordinate subcube.  Conversely, equality forces every nonempty
coordinate fibre to have size one or five (otherwise the first entropy
inequality is strict), and equality in Han--Shearer forces the coordinates
of `X` to be independent.  Hence the equality sets are exactly products
whose coordinate factors are singletons or all of `[5]`, namely the full
axis subcubes.  \(\square\)

### Theorem 4.2 (correlated voltage action--fragmentation inequality)

Partition `[5]^k` into the one-shore row sets `C_K` of the port-closed
overlay components of any correlated literal pentagon-product trade.  Let
`L_K` be the number of active complete slot fibres assigned to component
`K`, and put

\[
 L=\sum_KL_K,\qquad
 f={L\over k5^{k-1}},\qquad
 \chi={1\over5^k}\sum_K|C_K|^2.                      \tag{4.4}
\]

Then

\[
                         \boxed{\chi\ge5^{fk}}.        \tag{4.5}
\]

The exact tagged action and its equivalent moment bound are

\[
 \mathcal L_1^{\rm tag}=18L,\qquad
 \mathcal A_1^{\rm tag}=9L,                           \tag{4.6}
\]

and

\[
 \log_5\chi
 \ge {\mathcal L_1^{\rm tag}\over18\,5^{k-1}}
 =   {\mathcal A_1^{\rm tag}\over9\,5^{k-1}}.        \tag{4.7}
\]

#### Proof

Every active fibre lies wholly in one component, by local connectedness.
Apply Lemma 4.1 to each `C_K`:

\[
 5L\le\sum_K |C_K|\log_5|C_K|.                       \tag{4.8}
\]

Divide by `5^k`.  Since `5L/5^k=fk`,

\[
 fk\le\sum_K {|C_K|\over5^k}\log_5|C_K|.             \tag{4.9}
\]

The coefficients `|C_K|/5^k` sum to one.  Weighted arithmetic--geometric
mean now gives

\[
 \chi=\sum_K {|C_K|\over5^k}|C_K|
 \ge \prod_K |C_K|^{|C_K|/5^k}
 \ge5^{fk}.                                           \tag{4.10}
\]

The slot/context tag makes the eighteen local signed units from distinct
active fibres disjoint coordinates of the tagged occurrence tensor, so
(4.6) is exact.  Substitution into (4.5) gives (4.7).  \(\square\)

### Corollary 4.3 (bounded components have vanishing active density)

If every component has shore size at most `B`, then

\[
                         f\le {\log_5B\over k}.        \tag{4.11}
\]

In particular:

* `B=5` permits at most one active axis fibre in any component;
* `B=O(1)` permits only an `O(1/k)` fraction of all product fibres;
* positive active density `f>=epsilon` forces
  `chi>=5^(epsilon k)` and some exponentially large component.

Thus a diagonal five-row orbit cannot distribute the pentagon action over
`Theta(k)` independent slots while preserving their individual `X/Y`
resources.  The proposed escape is rigorously closed for the literal
product carrier.

## 5. Exact surviving boundary

The no-go uses three hypotheses, all forced by the proposed product
voltage lift:

1. coordinate blocks are physically disjoint and their product-token
   addresses are retained;
2. an active fibre uses the complete old/new rooted pentagon ledger; and
3. every root/complement port remains literal, so spectator row labels are
   injectively visible.

A genuinely new construction must violate the product architecture, not
merely correlate its signs.  The audited possibilities are a physical
serial conveyor carrying one port label through many stages, or a new
interleaved exact factor whose tokens no longer have the Cartesian address
of Lemma 2.1.  Such an object would require a fresh complete `X/Y` ledger
and fresh port proof.  It cannot be obtained as a cyclic or diagonal
voltage quotient of the existing pentagon product.

Accordingly this pair is not a viable bounded-`chi` `CRH_A` witness: the
very action density which makes its carrier critical forces exponential
component moment.  The theorem does not claim that an exceptional
directed signing of the original giant-component endpoint cube is
impossible; it proves that diagonal voltage compression supplies no new
states and cannot be the missing CRH rounding mechanism.
