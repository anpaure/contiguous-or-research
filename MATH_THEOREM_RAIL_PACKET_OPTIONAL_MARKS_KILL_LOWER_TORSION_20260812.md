# Optional rail marks eliminate every lower-target congruence

**Date:** 2026-08-12
**Method:** integer-column differences; no computation
**Status:** unconditional for the owner/lower/trace projection of the
unified rail-queue packet family.  This rules out the palette-parity
obstruction in that projection.  It does not prove saturation of the rigid
owner/socket/common-cap coordinates.

## 1. Setup

Fix `k,R,d` and the literal rail-queue component family from
`MATH_THEOREM_UNIFIED_RAIL_QUEUE_FULL_FRACTIONAL_SHADOW_BRAID_20260807.md`.
For a component `C`, write

\[
  o(C)\in\mathbb Z^{\binom{[k]}R}
\]

for its rank-`R` owner incidence vector.  Its complete proper interval deck
is simple.  Let `E(C)` be any set of eligible designated proper suffix
occurrences used by the lower compiler, and let `v(e)` be the Boolean value
of an occurrence `e`.

A mark is a designation of an already existing literal interval.  It does
not change the source word, owner cycle, residence, immediate palettes, or
prefix/suffix trace state.  Consequently, whenever `S subseteq E(C)` is an
admissible marked set, so is every subset of `S`.  In the monotone-rotor
construction, any chosen subset of the strictly nested proper suffixes is
admissible.

For the owner/lower projection define the marked packet column

\[
 a(C,S)=o(C)\oplus\sum_{e\in S}{\bf e}_{v(e)}.
 \tag{1.1}
\]

If a zero trace-boundary coordinate is retained, append it to every column.
Let `L_rail` be the integer lattice generated by all columns (1.1).

The word **packet** in this note means one whole closed rail cycle.  It does
not mean a one-owner atom.  One column therefore contains all `N` owners of
that cycle.  This distinction is essential: a generic one-owner
owner/target/trace hypergraph may contain determinant-two parity boxes, but
such a box is not a subconfiguration of the closed-rail column system merely
because it has the same separate marginals.

## 2. Unit-difference theorem

### Theorem 2.1

If a lower target `T` has one eligible occurrence in one rail component,
then

\[
  0\oplus {\bf e}_T\in L_{\rm rail}.
  \tag{2.1}
\]

Hence every modular character annihilating every marked rail-packet column
has coefficient zero on every eligible lower-target coordinate.

### Proof

Choose `C`, `e in E(C)` with `v(e)=T`, and an admissible marked set `S`
containing `e`.  Downward closure of the marking rule makes
`S-{e}` admissible.  The two packets have identical physical word, owner
cycle, residence, palette, and trace data.  Therefore

\[
 a(C,S)-a(C,S-\{e\})=0\oplus {\bf e}_T,
\]

which proves (2.1).

Now let `y` be a row character over `Z/qZ` satisfying
`y^T a(C,S)=0` for every marked packet.  Apply it to the displayed
difference.  This gives `y_T=0`.  \(\square\)

### Corollary 2.2

In the symmetric fractional rail factor every Ferrers-residual strict-lower
target has positive designated load, and therefore has an eligible literal
occurrence.  Thus the whole lower-target coordinate lattice

\[
 \mathbb Z^{\mathcal L_{\rm residual}}
\]

lies in `L_rail` as a direct coordinate sublattice.

In particular, the three-owner/two-palette parity obstruction in which each
component is forced to contain either two `X` targets or two `Y` targets
cannot occur in this projection: a rail packet is not forced to carry a
fixed parity of designated lower marks.

The same argument applies verbatim to any **optional witness annotation**.
In particular, if an upper interval already exists physically and marking
it as the designated witness changes no compulsory packet resource, then
columns differing only by that mark give the corresponding upper-witness
unit vector.  An equality formulation with unused occurrence slots should
therefore use free slack/annotation variables; imposing a fixed number of
marks per packet creates an artificial congruence not present in the word
problem.

## 3. Trace parity is absent componentwise

Each rail word is cyclic and its complete literal state regenerates after
its period (Theorem 2.1(7) of the unified rail-queue theorem).  If
`partial C` denotes signed `d`-state prefix/suffix boundary,
then

\[
  \partial C=0
  \tag{3.1}
\]

for every component, integrally rather than merely fractionally.  Therefore
the usual parity condition for a balanced directed multigraph is already
satisfied by every selected whole rail component.  Trace serialization can
still fail through disconnectedness or protected-splice incompatibility,
but not through a nonzero boundary parity of the selected cycles.

Consequently the signed-history `2 x 2` obstruction—two integral resource
matchings with boundaries `+2 beta` and `-2 beta` whose midpoint alone is
balanced—cannot occur among **whole** rail-component columns.  It can
reappear if a rounding argument atomizes a rail into open transitions.
Such an atomized argument must either retain the full integer boundary row
or group its atoms into boundary-zero packets before rounding.

## 4. Scalar owner divisibility is absent

Put

\[
 n_0=k-R+d+1.
\]

The pure rail (`a=d,p=1,h_0=1`) is legal at every integer period

\[
  2(d+2)\le L\le n_0.
  \tag{4.1}
\]

For all sufficiently large `k`, this interval contains two consecutive
integers.  A pure rail of period `L` contains exactly `L` owners.  Hence the
greatest common divisor of the available total owner counts is one.

Equivalently, if a modular character is constant, with coefficient `c`, on
all owner coordinates and annihilates all pure-rail columns, then from two
consecutive legal periods

\[
 cL=0,\qquad c(L+1)=0
\]

and therefore `c=0`.  Thus there is no scalar component-count divisibility
barrier.

## 5. Exact surviving lattice gate

The theorem does **not** prove integral rounding.  It proves that the first
possible modular obstruction is narrower than a general full-packet Smith
normal form suggests.

After quotienting by optional lower designations and the identically zero
trace boundary, a surviving congruence must live on rigid data, principally

\[
 \boxed{
   \text{nonconstant owner incidence}
   \;\oplus\;
   \text{phase/socket action}
   \;\oplus\;
   \text{nonoptional typed common-cap resources}.}
 \tag{5.1}
\]

The next necessary steps are therefore:

1. characterize the integer span of the pure/mixed rail owner-cycle
   incidence vectors, not just their scalar lengths;
2. compute the permutation subgroup generated by protected two-phase socket
   trades and exhibit an odd generator when required; and
3. include common-cap coordinates only after deciding which of them are
   physical compulsory resources and which are optional compiler
   designations.

There is one further owner-side narrowing, extending the scalar and
first-order results already proved in
`MATH_THEOREM_NONMAXIMAL_PURE_RAIL_LATTICE_BREAKERS_20260807.md`.  Put
`q=d+1` and
`c=R-q`.  A pure rail of period `L` is the `R`-uniform template

\[
 F_L=\left\{C\cup\{x_i,x_{i+1},\ldots,x_{i+q-1}\}:
              i\in\mathbb Z/L\mathbb Z\right\},
 \tag{5.2}
\]

where `|C|=c`.  Take two consecutive legal periods `L,L+1`.  For
`0<=j<=R`, let `g_j` be the greatest common divisor of all degrees of
`j`-sets in copies of either template.  Then

\[
                         g_j=1\qquad(0\le j\le R).
 \tag{5.3}
\]

Indeed, `g_0=gcd(L,L+1)=1`, and the same two templates have core-vertex
degrees `L,L+1`, so `g_1=1`.  For `j>=2`, choose two toggle labels at
cyclic distance `q-1`; exactly one `q`-window contains both.  Add
`min(c,j-2)` core labels and, if necessary, further toggle labels from the
interior of that unique window until the chosen set has size `j`.  Its
degree in `F_L` is exactly one, proving `g_j=1`.

Thus the usual subset-degree divisibility conditions for an
`{F_L,F_(L+1)}`-design are all trivial.  This is not yet a proof that the
full owner-copy lattice is saturated at the present growing parameters,
but it rules out every standard scalar/vertex/higher-subset divisibility
barrier.  Any surviving owner torsion must be a genuinely nonstandard
occurrence or labelled-copy invariant.

Even if the lattice in (5.1) is saturated, affine-semigroup holes and
resource-disjoint absorption remain possible.  The present theorem removes
the explicit lower-palette parity barrier; it does not remove those later
integral obstructions.

## 6. Two normalization corrections

The bounded topology defect should be counted **in excess of one**.  Thus a
statement with defect `C` should require at most `C+1` Euler components;
the zero-defect case means one component, not zero components.

Also, if

\[
 h=(\Lambda-dW)_+,
\]

then at `k=17`, `R=9`, `W=24310`, `Lambda=65535`, and `d=3`, so `h=0`.
The signed number `Lambda-dW=-7395` is not a number of deleted targets.
The available short-window slack is instead

\[
 dW+\binom{d+1}{2}-\Lambda=7401.
\]

Any later full-matrix statement must distinguish these quantities.
