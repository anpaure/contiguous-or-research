# Exact direct-span CNF for five deletions plus sixteen appends

## Verdict

The formula generated by

```text
scratch/delete_append_span_cnf.cpp
```

is satisfiable if and only if one can delete exactly `d` entries from the
fixed nonzero prefix, append exactly `q` nonzero entries, and obtain a word
covering every nonempty mask by contiguous OR.

Unlike the earlier endpoint-recency formula, it carries no state and creates
no target-occurrence variables at the 465 original endpoints.  Prefix-only
witnesses are represented directly by deletion-conditioned original spans.
Every witness touching the append block is represented by its appended right
endpoint and the exact last-occurrence recurrence.

For the production instance `k=11,d=5,q=16`, with the optional rank-five and
rank-six append-endpoint AMOs, the exact inventory is

```text
variables                         184,400
clauses                         2,196,500
literals                        5,081,294
prefix span variables             129,719
append occurrence variables        32,032
robust targets omitted                  45
CNF bytes                       34,335,756
```

The preceding all-endpoint formula has 2,523,666 variables and 43,004,593
clauses.  The direct-span formula is therefore about 13.7 times smaller in
variables and 19.6 times smaller in clauses.

## 1. Exact compressed-span semantics

Let the original prefix be

```text
A_0,A_1,...,A_{p-1}
```

and let `D_i` mean that original position `i` is deleted.  Every interval in
the compressed retained prefix has unique first and last retained original
positions `l<=r`.  Its OR is exactly a nonempty target `T` if and only if:

1. `D_l=D_r=0`;
2. every `i in [l,r]` with `A_i` not contained in `T` is deleted;
3. for every coordinate `b in T`, at least one position `i in [l,r]` with
   `b in A_i subseteq T` is retained.

Deleting internal positions simply compresses them away.  Conversely, the
retained entries between two retained endpoints remain consecutive, so the
three conditions produce a genuine compressed-prefix interval with OR `T`.

For every statically viable span the generator creates a flag `W(T,l,r)`.
The flag implies the two endpoint literals, every contaminant deletion, and
one retained-provider clause for every target coordinate.  Provider clauses
are omitted when an endpoint already supplies the coordinate or when more
than `d` compatible positions supply it: with exactly `d` deletions, the
latter condition is automatic.

The converse implication is deliberately unnecessary.  A target support
clause may choose a span flag only when all of its sufficient conditions are
true.  Given a real completion, one genuine span flag can always be chosen.
Thus the one-way Tseitin use is both sound and complete while avoiding a
large reverse-definition circuit.

## 2. Why the containment-cap pruning is complete

For target rank `s`, let

```text
C_s(n) = min_r { n-C(k,r)+C(s,r) : C(k,r)<=n }.
```

The established simultaneous containment theorem gives a witness of length
at most `C_s(n)` in every universal zero-free word of total length `n`.

If such a witness lies in the retained prefix, its original span contains its
physical retained entries plus at most the `d` globally deleted entries.
Consequently its raw original length is at most

```text
C_s(n)+d.
```

The generator enumerates every span through exactly this raw deadline.  This
use of the cap occurs only in the completeness direction: start from an
already universal completion, choose a capped witness, and map it to a span
or append endpoint.  Formula soundness does not assume the cap; every selected
span is independently forced to be a literal OR witness.

At `k=11,n=476` the deadlines are

```text
rank 1--5: 14
rank 6:    15
rank 7:    21
rank 8:    42
rank 9:    98
rank 10:  224
rank 11:  476.
```

Therefore raw prefix spans need extend only five original positions farther.

## 3. Robust disjoint-witness shortcut

For each target the generator greedily computes the maximum number of
pairwise position-disjoint intervals whose undeleted prefix OR is already
exactly the target.  Greedy selection by increasing right endpoint is optimal
for interval packing.

If this number exceeds `d`, deleting `d` positions cannot touch every chosen
interval.  One interval remains wholly retained, remains physically
consecutive after compression, and retains exactly the target OR.  Such a
target is unconditionally covered and needs neither span flags nor append
flags.

On the production prefix this proves 45 targets robust.  Their rank counts
are

```text
rank 1:   7
rank 2:  16
rank 9:  11
rank 10: 10
rank 11:  1.
```

The shortcut is purely sufficient; failing it does not discard a target.

## 4. Exact deletion-conditioned seam state

Only the state at the prefix/append seam is required.

For a coordinate `b`, `seen[b]` is constant true when the prefix contains
more than `d` occurrences of `b`.  Otherwise it is the OR of `not D_i` over
all occurrences.

For distinct coordinates `a,b`, the strict relation

```text
newer[a,b] <=> last(a)>last(b)
```

is determined by the newest retained original position containing either
coordinate.  If that entry contains `b`, the relation is false (including a
tie containing both); if it contains `a` but not `b`, it is true.  Among the
newest `d+1` such event positions at least one is retained.  Hence all earlier
events can be discarded.  A short deletion-gated Boolean fold over these at
most `d+1` events constructs the exact seam relation.

This replaces the old recency transition at all 465 prefix positions by at
most six relevant events for each ordered coordinate pair.

## 5. Append endpoints include every cross-gap witness

The appended entries use the audited move-to-front recurrence for `seen` and
all ordered `newer` comparisons.  At an appended endpoint `e`, a target `T`
is a suffix OR if and only if

```text
every b in T is seen, and
last_e(b)>last_e(c) for every b in T and c outside T.
```

Every interval touching the appended block ends at one of its `q` entries,
so this captures both append-only and prefix/append cross-gap witnesses.  No
cross-gap interval is represented by a prefix span, and none is omitted.

The append suffixes are left uncapped.  This is exact, not a relaxation:
they are literal suffix-OR conditions.  The containment caps are redundant
for satisfiability and are used only to bound prefix-span enumeration.

## 6. Equivalence theorem

### Soundness

Decode the exactly `d` deletion variables and the `q` nonzero append masks.
For every non-robust target, its support clause selects either:

- a span flag whose implication clauses prove that the corresponding
  compressed-prefix interval has exact OR equal to the target; or
- an append occurrence flag whose recency clauses prove that a suffix ending
  at that append endpoint has exact OR equal to the target.

Every robust target retains an untouched exact prefix witness.  Hence the
decoded completed word is universal.

### Completeness

Start from any universal word in this delete/append neighborhood.  The
deletion counter and append variables take their literal values.  Assign the
seam and append recurrence their actual last-occurrence states.

For each non-robust target, choose a capped witness.  If it is wholly in the
retained prefix, its original raw span is at most `cap+d` and its exact span
flag is present.  If it touches the appended block, select its appended right
endpoint.  This satisfies every target support clause.

The optional same-rank AMOs at an append endpoint are WLOG: suffix ORs at one
endpoint form an inclusion chain, so two distinct targets of equal rank
cannot both occur there.  Selecting one witness for each target satisfies
all AMOs.

Thus formula satisfiability is equivalent to existence of the requested
completion.
