# PBBS nonoverlap phase corners: quotient-edge charging and the sparse-corner residual

Date: 2026-07-25

Method: pure mathematics only. No computation, finite search, solver, or
external input is used.

## 0. Outcome

Let

\[
 B_m=\operatorname {Cat}_m
\]

and let \(I\) be a genuine first zero-winding PBBS return of step-two
parameter \(s\). Write its canonical staircase blocks as

\[
 D_j=\tau^jD_0=P_j1R_j0S_j,\qquad 0\le j\le s,
\]

and \(T_0,\ldots,T_{s-1}\) for the dual staircase blocks. Put

\[
 \Lambda=\delta(D_0)+\delta(D_s)-2m>0.
\]

This note proves a general quotient-edge collision theorem for the complete
two-phase nonoverlap atlas.

For

\[
 a,b\ge0,\qquad a+b\le s-2,
\]

use the transported phase pair

\[
 t=a,\qquad u=s-b-1,
\]

and define

\[
 X_a=\sum_{j<a}|T_j|,
 \qquad
 Y_b=\sum_{j=s-b}^{s-1}|S_j|.
\]

Then the pair is in the nonoverlap branch exactly when

\[
 X_a+Y_b\ge\Lambda,                              \tag{0.1}
\]

and it is in the overlap branch exactly when

\[
 X_a+Y_b\le\Lambda-2(a+b).                       \tag{0.2}
\]

No actual pair lies in the open band between (0.1) and (0.2).
Consequently the nonoverlap pairs form an upper ideal in the triangular
phase lattice. Let \(\mathcal C(I)\) be its coordinate-minimal elements,
called the **nonoverlap corners**.

If \(c=(a,b)\in\mathcal C(I)\), \(r=a+b\), then every available immediate
predecessor is overlap, and hence

\[
 \boxed{
 a>0\Longrightarrow |T_{a-1}|\ge2r-2,\qquad
 b>0\Longrightarrow |S_{s-b}|\ge2r-2.}           \tag{0.3}
\]

These are not formal marks. The first block is the dual terminal sector
of the actual quotient root \(D_{a-1}\), and the second is the primal
terminal sector of the actual quotient root \(D_{s-b}\).

Let \(\mathcal P\) be any pairwise quotient-edge-disjoint family of
nonwrapping zero-winding returns. For \(R\ge1\), put

\[
 \kappa_R(I)=
 \#\{(a,b)\in\mathcal C(I):a+b\ge R\}.
\]

If

\[
 \mathcal T_m(L)
 =\#\{D\in\mathcal D_m:|S(D)|+1\ge L\},
\]

then the corner charges are injective in a two-coloured copy of the
quotient-edge set and give the exact packing inequality

\[
 \boxed{
 \sum_{I\in\mathcal P}\kappa_R(I)
 \le2\mathcal T_m(2R-1).}                         \tag{0.4}
\]

The required marginal tail has a uniform strict estimate:

\[
 \boxed{
 \mathcal T_m(L)
 \le C B_m\left(L^{-1/2}+m^{-1/4}\right)}
 \qquad(1\le L\le2m).                             \tag{0.5}
\]

The \(m^{-1/4}\) term is the contribution in which the terminal suffix
contains more than half the rank; its height cap is essential.

Combining (0.4)--(0.5), fix \(0<\alpha<A<\infty\) and \(0<\rho<1\).
For every such packing with

\[
 \alpha\sqrt m\le s(I)\le A\sqrt m,
\]

\[
 \boxed{
 \sum_{I\in\mathcal P}
 \#\{c\in\mathcal C(I):|c|_1\ge\rho s(I)\}
 \le C_{\alpha,\rho}B_m m^{-1/4}.}                \tag{0.6}
\]

In particular, for every fixed \(\eta>0\), the subfamily satisfying

\[
 \#\{c\in\mathcal C(I):|c|_1\ge\rho s(I)\}
 \ge\eta s(I)
\]

has cardinality

\[
 \boxed{O_{\alpha,\rho,\eta}(B_m m^{-3/4})
 =o(B_m/\sqrt m).}                               \tag{0.7}
\]

Thus a critical-order packing cannot have a positive density of
macroscopic nonoverlap corners.

More sharply, if

\[
 |\mathcal P_m|\ge c\,B_m/\sqrt m
\]

along a subsequence, then

\[
 \boxed{
 \frac{\sum_{I\in\mathcal P_m}|\mathcal C(I)|}
      {\sum_{I\in\mathcal P_m}s(I)}
 \longrightarrow0.}                              \tag{0.8}
\]

After deleting \(o(|\mathcal P_m|)\) intervals, every surviving interval
therefore has \(o(s)\) minimal nonoverlap corners.

Every other nonoverlap phase pair is generated from one of these minimal
corners by the exact tail cocycle. All phases inside its phase interval
retain the same common corridor, and every dominated pair merely
refactors that corridor. Hence the exact residual is:

1. **persistent overlap:** \(\mathcal C(I)=\varnothing\), necessarily
   \(\Lambda\ge2(s-2)\); or
2. **sparse shields:** the entire nonoverlap atlas is generated by
   \(o(s)\) canonical corner corridors.

There is no same-return third-phase projection left in the second case.
A completion must prove a cross-interval collision/incidence theorem for
these sparse canonical corner roots. The special common-edge corner
family audited in
MATH_AUDIT_OCR_CRITICAL_CORNER_QUOTIENT_PACKING_20260725.md is a
two-corner member of this residual, with minimal corners \((t+1,0)\) and
\((0,s-u)\): one is exposed by its exceptional \(T_t\)-block and one by
its exceptional \(S_u\)-block. No
coefficient-one conclusion is claimed here.

## 1. Exact phase-lattice dichotomy

For a pair \(t<u\), the transported tail equation is

\[
 A_{t,u}R_t=R_uC_{t,u},
\]

where

\[
 A_{t,u}=(\overline T_{u-1}0)\cdots(\overline T_t0),
 \qquad
 C_{t,u}=(0S_u)\cdots(0S_{t+1}).
\]

The audited chronology ledger is

\[
 \Delta_{t,u}=B_{t,u}-\Lambda,                   \tag{1.1}
\]

with

\[
 B_{t,u}
 =\sum_{j<t}(|T_j|+1)
  +\sum_{u<j<s}(|S_j|+1),                        \tag{1.2}
\]

and

\[
 S_{t,u}=s-(u-t)-1.                              \tag{1.3}
\]

The free-monoid dichotomy says

\[
 \Delta_{t,u}\ge S_{t,u}
 \quad\hbox{or}\quad
 \Delta_{t,u}\le-S_{t,u}.                        \tag{1.4}
\]

The first alternative is nonoverlap and the second is overlap.

Substitute

\[
 t=a,\qquad u=s-b-1,\qquad r=a+b.
\]

There are \(a+b=r\) outer delimiter bits in (1.2), so

\[
 B_{t,u}=r+X_a+Y_b,\qquad S_{t,u}=r.             \tag{1.5}
\]

The two alternatives in (1.4) become exactly (0.1) and (0.2).

### Lemma 1.1 (monotone corner ideal)

The set

\[
 \mathcal H(I)
 =\{(a,b):a,b\ge0,\ a+b\le s-2,\ X_a+Y_b\ge\Lambda\}
\]

is an upper ideal under the coordinatewise order. Its minimal elements
form an antichain, so no two elements of \(\mathcal C(I)\) have the same
first coordinate or the same second coordinate.

#### Proof

Both \(X_a\) and \(Y_b\) are nondecreasing. Thus (0.1) remains true when
either coordinate is increased. Minimal elements of any upper ideal are
an antichain. Two distinct points with the same first coordinate are
comparable, as are two distinct points with the same second coordinate.
\(\square\)

If \(\mathcal C(I)=\varnothing\), every point is overlap. At any point
with \(a+b=s-2\), (0.2) and the nonnegativity of \(X_a,Y_b\) give

\[
 0\le\Lambda-2(s-2),
\]

and hence

\[
 \boxed{\Lambda\ge2(s-2).}                       \tag{1.6}
\]

This is the persistent-overlap alternative.

## 2. Minimal corners force actual large quotient sectors

Let \(c=(a,b)\in\mathcal C(I)\), and put \(r=a+b\). Since
\((0,0)\) is overlap when \(\Lambda>0\), one has \(r\ge1\).

If \(a>0\), minimality makes \((a-1,b)\) overlap. Hence

\[
 X_{a-1}+Y_b\le\Lambda-2(r-1),                   \tag{2.1}
\]

whereas the corner itself gives

\[
 X_a+Y_b\ge\Lambda.                              \tag{2.2}
\]

Subtracting and using

\[
 X_a-X_{a-1}=|T_{a-1}|
\]

proves the first inequality in (0.3). The second follows identically
from the overlap predecessor \((a,b-1)\) and

\[
 Y_b-Y_{b-1}=|S_{s-b}|.
\]

There is a word-level strengthening. Let \(Z_c\) be the unique
nonoverlap word for phases

\[
 t=a,\qquad u=s-b-1.
\]

Thus

\[
 R_u=A_{t,u}Z_c,\qquad R_t=Z_cC_{t,u},
 \qquad\operatorname {net}(Z_c)=-r.              \tag{2.3}
\]

If \(a>0\), the overlap factorization at the predecessor
\((a-1,b)\), followed by cancellation of \(A_{t,u}\), gives a unique
positive word \(H_L\) such that

\[
 \boxed{\overline T_{a-1}0=Z_cH_L,
 \qquad\operatorname {net}(H_L)=r-1.}            \tag{2.4}
\]

If \(b>0\), the predecessor \((a,b-1)\) similarly gives a unique
positive word \(H_R\) such that

\[
 \boxed{0S_{s-b}=H_RZ_c,
 \qquad\operatorname {net}(H_R)=r-1.}            \tag{2.5}
\]

Indeed, (2.4) follows by comparing

\[
 A_{a-1,u}=A_{a,u}(\overline T_{a-1}0)
\]

with \(R_u=A_{a,u}Z_c\). For (2.5), compare

\[
 C_{a,u+1}=(0S_{u+1})C_{a,u},
 \qquad u+1=s-b,
\]

with \(R_a=Z_cC_{a,u}\). Taking net heights in (2.4)--(2.5) proves the
displayed values. Their length consequences are

\[
 |T_{a-1}|+1=|Z_c|+|H_L|\ge2r-1,
\]

\[
 |S_{s-b}|+1=|H_R|+|Z_c|\ge2r-1,
\]

which are (0.3) with the delimiter restored.

The sector identities attached to actual roots are

\[
 |S(D_j)|+1=|S_j|+1,                             \tag{2.6}
\]

and

\[
 |S(\phi D_j)|+1=|T_j|+1.                        \tag{2.7}
\]

For (2.7), use the exact chronology notation

\[
 M_j=\delta(D_j)-\sum_{i<j}(|S_i|+1).
\]

The dual staircase identity gives

\[
 M_j-M_{j+1}=|T_j|+1,
\]

while the PBBS voltage identity gives

\[
 M_j-M_{j+1}=d(\phi D_j)=|S(\phi D_j)|+1.
\]

Thus both large blocks in (0.3) are canonical sectors on actual quotient
edges.

## 3. A strict tail bound for the first-maximum suffix

For

\[
 d(D)=|S(D)|+1,
\]

write

\[
 b_{m,j}
 =\#\{D\in\mathcal D_m:d(D)=2j+1\}.
\]

The exact first-highest-component formula is

\[
 b_{m,j}
 =\sum_{h\ge1} A_{m-j,h}\,C_h(j),                \tag{3.1}
\]

where \(A_{n,h}\) counts the prefix through the first primitive component
of global height \(h\), and \(C_h(j)\) counts semilength-\(j\) Dyck paths
of height at most \(h\). In particular,

\[
 b_{m,j}\le B_jB_{m-j}.                           \tag{3.2}
\]

We need an extra estimate when \(j>m/2\), because (3.2) alone loses the
height restriction on the very long suffix.

### Lemma 3.1 (long-suffix tail)

There is an absolute \(C\) such that, for every \(m\ge1\) and every
integer \(J\ge0\),

\[
 \boxed{
 \sum_{j\ge J}b_{m,j}
 \le C B_m\left((J+1)^{-1/2}+m^{-1/4}\right).}   \tag{3.3}
\]

#### Proof

For \(J\le j\le m/2\), the standard Catalan estimates and (3.2) give

\[
 \frac{b_{m,j}}{B_m}
 \le
 C\frac{m^{3/2}}
 {(j+1)^{3/2}(m-j+1)^{3/2}}
 \le\frac{C}{(j+1)^{3/2}}.                       \tag{3.4}
\]

Summing proves the \((J+1)^{-1/2}\) term.

It remains to sum \(j>m/2\). Put \(n=m-j<m/2\). In (3.1),
\(A_{n,h}=0\) unless \(h\le n\), and

\[
 \sum_hA_{n,h}\le B_n.
\]

Therefore

\[
 b_{m,m-n}\le B_n C_n(m-n).                     \tag{3.5}
\]

For \(n>\sqrt m\), discard the cap:

\[
 C_n(m-n)\le B_{m-n}.
\]

The Catalan estimates then give

\[
 \sum_{\sqrt m<n<m/2}
 \frac{B_nB_{m-n}}{B_m}
 \le C\sum_{n>\sqrt m}n^{-3/2}
 \le C m^{-1/4}.                                 \tag{3.6}
\]

For \(n\le\sqrt m\), path-graph diagonalization gives

\[
 C_n(m-n)
 \le
 C4^{m-n}(n+1)^{-3}
 \exp\!\left(-c\frac{m}{(n+1)^2}\right).         \tag{3.7}
\]

Indeed,

\[
 C_n(q)=\frac2{n+2}
 \sum_{k=1}^{n+1}
 \sin^2\frac{k\pi}{n+2}
 \left(2\cos\frac{k\pi}{n+2}\right)^{2q},
\]

and pairing the two endpoint modes and summing the resulting Gaussian
tail proves (3.7) when \(q=m-n\ge m/2\).

Using

\[
 B_n\le C4^n(n+1)^{-3/2},
\qquad
 B_m\ge c4^m m^{-3/2},
\]

the normalized contribution in (3.5) is at most

\[
 C m^{3/2}(n+1)^{-9/2}
 \exp\!\left(-c\frac{m}{(n+1)^2}\right).         \tag{3.8}
\]

Group \(n+1\le\sqrt m\) into the dyadic ranges

\[
 2^{-k-1}\sqrt m<n+1\le2^{-k}\sqrt m.
\]

The contribution of the \(k\)-th range is at most

\[
 C m^{-1/4}2^{7k/2}e^{-c4^k}.
\]

The series over \(k\) converges, so the sum of (3.8) is
\(O(m^{-1/4})\). Together with (3.4) and (3.6), this proves (3.3).
\(\square\)

Taking

\[
 J=\left\lceil\frac{L-1}{2}\right\rceil
\]

in Lemma 3.1 proves (0.5), after changing the absolute constant.

## 4. Quotient-edge injection of all macroscopic corners

For a corner \(c=(a,b)\), choose the following two-coloured charge:

\[
 \chi_I(c)=
 \begin{cases}
  (D_{a-1},-),&a>0,\\
  (D_{s-b},+),&a=0.
 \end{cases}                                     \tag{4.1}
\]

There is no corner \((0,0)\), so the second line always has \(b>0\).
By (2.4)--(2.7), if \(a+b\ge R\), then

\[
 \begin{cases}
 d(\phi D_{a-1})\ge2R-1,&a>0,\\
 d(D_{s-b})\ge2R-1,&a=0.
 \end{cases}                                     \tag{4.2}
\]

### Lemma 4.1 (corner-charge injectivity)

On a pairwise quotient-edge-disjoint nonwrapping family, the map

\[
 (I,c)\longmapsto\chi_I(c)
\]

is injective.

#### Proof

Within one interval, corners with \(a>0\) have distinct \(a\)-coordinates
by Lemma 1.1, hence distinct roots \(D_{a-1}\). There is at most one
corner with \(a=0\). The signs in (4.1) separate that charge from all
horizontal charges.

Every charged root belongs to the exact quotient support

\[
 Q(D_0,s)=\{e_{D_0},e_{D_1},\ldots,e_{D_{s+1}}\}.
\]

Nonwrapping makes its roots distinct inside one support, and
quotient-edge-disjointness makes roots from different selected supports
distinct. Thus no two signed charges agree. \(\square\)

The map \(\phi\) is a permutation of \(\mathcal D_m\), so the number of
roots with \(d(\phi D)\ge L\) is exactly \(\mathcal T_m(L)\). Lemma 4.1
and (4.2) now prove (0.4).

For (0.6), take

\[
 R_I=\lceil\rho s(I)\rceil.
\]

Since \(s(I)\ge\alpha\sqrt m\), every charge has sector length at least

\[
 2\rho\alpha\sqrt m-1.
\]

Use this common lower threshold in (0.4), then apply (0.5). This gives

\[
 \sum_I\kappa_{R_I}(I)
 \le C_{\alpha,\rho}B_m m^{-1/4},
\]

which is (0.6). If each selected interval on the left has at least
\(\eta s(I)\ge\eta\alpha\sqrt m\) such corners, division proves (0.7).

## 5. Critical-order packings have a sparse corner atlas

Fix \(0<\rho<1\). Since the corner set is an antichain, the number of
corners with

\[
 a+b<\rho s
\]

is at most \(\lceil\rho s\rceil\): their distinct first coordinates all
lie in an interval of that many integers. Therefore (0.6) gives

\[
 \sum_{I\in\mathcal P_m}|\mathcal C(I)|
 \le
 \rho\sum_{I\in\mathcal P_m}s(I)
 +|\mathcal P_m|
 +C_{\alpha,\rho}B_m m^{-1/4}.                  \tag{5.1}
\]

Assume

\[
 |\mathcal P_m|\ge cB_m/\sqrt m.
\]

Then

\[
 \sum_I s(I)\ge\alpha\sqrt m\,|\mathcal P_m|
 \ge\alpha cB_m.                                 \tag{5.2}
\]

Divide (5.1) by (5.2). The last two normalized terms are respectively
\(O(m^{-1/2})\) and \(O_{\alpha,\rho,c}(m^{-1/4})\). Hence

\[
 \limsup_{m\to\infty}
 \frac{\sum_I|\mathcal C(I)|}{\sum_Is(I)}
 \le\rho.
\]

Now let \(\rho\downarrow0\), proving (0.8).

Because all \(s(I)\) lie between two fixed multiples of \(\sqrt m\),
(0.8) also implies

\[
 \frac1{|\mathcal P_m|}
 \sum_{I\in\mathcal P_m}\frac{|\mathcal C(I)|}{s(I)}
 \longrightarrow0.                              \tag{5.3}
\]

By Markov's inequality, there is a deterministic
\(\varepsilon_m\downarrow0\) such that all but \(o(|\mathcal P_m|)\)
members satisfy

\[
 |\mathcal C(I)|\le\varepsilon_m s(I).           \tag{5.4}
\]

This is the sparse-corner residual with exact quantifiers.

## 6. Why internal third phases do not shrink the residual

For one corner \(c=(a,b)\), put

\[
 t=a,\qquad u=s-b-1.
\]

Its nonoverlap factorization is (2.3). For every \(t\le v\le u\),
the adjacent tail cocycle gives

\[
 \boxed{
 R_v=A_{t,v}Z_cC_{v,u}.}                         \tag{6.1}
\]

For every subpair \(t\le p<q\le u\), its common word is

\[
 \boxed{
 Z_{p,q}=A_{t,p}Z_cC_{q,u}.}                     \tag{6.2}
\]

Thus every phase pair dominating \(c\) in the triangular lattice is a
literal refactorization of the one corner word. Since the nonoverlap
set is an upper ideal, every nonoverlap pair dominates at least one
minimal corner. Equations (6.1)--(6.2) show that the complete
same-return nonoverlap atlas is generated by \(\mathcal C(I)\).

Consequently (5.4) is not merely a statement that few phase pairs were
selected. It says that the whole atlas has only \(o(s)\) irreducible
base corridors. Adding a third, fourth, or any finite collection of
phases inside the same return supplies no independent projection.

The remaining theorem must couple these sparse base corridors between
different, support-disjoint return intervals. Equality of any two
charged quotient roots gives an immediate collision, as in the audited
explicit corner theorem. What is not proved here is that such equality,
or another cross-interval incidence, occurs for almost all sparse-corner
critical returns.

## 7. Proved boundary

Proved:

1. the exact two-dimensional overlap/nonoverlap phase lattice
   (0.1)--(0.2);
2. the minimal-corner shield identities (2.4)--(2.5);
3. the strict first-maximum suffix tail (0.5);
4. the all-corner quotient-edge packing charge (0.4);
5. little-oh for positive-density macroscopic-corner families, (0.7);
6. sparse corner density for every possible critical-order packing,
   (0.8)--(5.4);
7. redundancy of every same-return internal third phase.

Not proved:

1. a packing bound for persistent-overlap returns;
2. a cross-interval collision theorem for the sparse-shield corner roots;
3. the full zero-winding little-oh estimate;
4. coefficient one.

The precise surviving nonoverlap family is therefore not an unrestricted
two- or three-phase corridor. It is a support-disjoint packing of genuine
returns whose complete nonoverlap atlases are generated by \(o(s)\)
canonical quotient-edge corners.
