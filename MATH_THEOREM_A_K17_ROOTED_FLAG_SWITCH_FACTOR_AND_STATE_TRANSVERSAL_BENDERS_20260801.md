# `k=17`: rooted-flag switch factorization and exact state-transversal Benders theorem

Date: 2026-08-01  
Lane: A / palette-preserving rooted switches and residence-first chronology  
Status: unconditional structural theorem; no finite positive witness is claimed

## 0. Result and scope

Write `N_j` for the cyclic-necklace orbits of rank `j` on `Z_17`.
Every rank-eight-rooted age flag is a nested path

\[
             A\subset B\subset Q,
       \qquad |Q|=8,\quad |B|\in\{6,7\},             \tag{0.1}
\]

where `A=C_0`, `B=C_0 union C_1`, and `Q=C_0 union C_1 union C_2`.
The age type is determined by the pair `(|A|,|B|)`.  This gives an exact
factorization of every lower-palette-perfect rooted flag factor into

1. a role choice for the rank-six necklaces;
2. a predecessor-rank colour on every chosen middle necklace;
3. independent aligned-containment perfect matchings from lower necklaces
   to the middle necklaces; and
4. one aligned-containment perfect matching from the middle necklaces to
   all rank-eight roots.

Consequently, after the role and predecessor-rank colour are fixed, the
complete palette-preserving switch space is generated by ordinary even
alternating cycles in bipartite containment multigraphs, together with
local rank-one pivots.  This fixed-role face has a totally unimodular
extended formulation.

The unrestricted role/colour selection is a different layer.  It has an
exact zero-one Benders formulation whose subproblems and separation
oracles are bipartite matching/min-cut.  The natural raw flag-column matrix
is not totally unimodular: a literal type-`C` three-column minor has
determinant `-2`.  Thus neither the raw exact-cover matrix nor a generic
“switches are matching” assertion supplies integrality for the outer
selection.

On top of the palette factor, the first chronology objective--minimizing
zero-out and zero-in roots--has an exact local-support linearization.  The
full 1,430-state owner/root/`H` transversal is then an exact integer Benders
problem: the master is a root/owner incidence matching and the subproblem
is a split-state bipartite matching.  Its obstruction is precisely a Hall
min-cut, not merely a dead root.

This note deliberately omits upper shadows, quotient Hamiltonicity,
voltage, opening, and compiler constraints.

## 1. The nested-path normal form

The nine allowed triples `(c_0,c_1,c_2)` and masses are

\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c}
t&(c_0,c_1,c_2)&(|A|,|B|)&m_t\\ \hline
A&(1,5,2)&(1,6)&139\\
B&(1,6,1)&(1,7)&297\\
C&(2,5,1)&(2,7)&8\\
D&(3,3,2)&(3,6)&20\\
E&(3,4,1)&(3,7)&20\\
F&(4,3,1)&(4,7)&140\\
G&(5,1,2)&(5,6)&127\\
H&(5,2,1)&(5,7)&237\\
I&(6,1,1)&(6,7)&442.
\end{array}                                                     \tag{1.1}
\]

Thus the type is recovered from the two ranks in (0.1).  Put

\[
 |N_2|,|N_3|,|N_4|,|N_5|,|N_6|,|N_7|,|N_8|
 =8,40,140,364,728,1144,1430.                    \tag{1.2}
\]

Exactly 286 rank-six necklaces occur as middle vertices `B`, since

\[
                 139+20+127=286.                    \tag{1.3}
\]

All 1,144 rank-seven necklaces occur as middle vertices.  The other
`728-286=442` rank-six necklaces occur as lower vertices `A`, exactly the
mass of type `I`.  The remaining lower vertices are all necklaces in ranks
two through five.  The 436 types `A,B` have a rank-one `A`; rank one is not
a tight palette row, so these are local singleton choices rather than
distinct global resources.

Let `I_{r,s}` denote the aligned-containment bipartite multigraph from
`N_r` to `N_s`.  Parallel phase incidences are retained.

### Theorem 1.1 (exact factorization)

A rank-eight-rooted flag factor has the prescribed type masses and one
copy of every lower target orbit at ranks two through eight if and only if
the following data exist.

1. A set \(S\subset N_6\) of size 286.  The middle bank is

   \[
                         B_* = S\mathbin{\dot\cup}N_7.          \tag{1.4}
   \]

2. A predecessor-rank map `kappa:B_* -> {1,...,6}` with the exact fibres

   \[
   \begin{array}{c|ccc}
   B\in N_6&\kappa=1&\kappa=3&\kappa=5\\ \hline
   \#&139&20&127
   \end{array},
   \qquad
   \begin{array}{c|rrrrrr}
   B\in N_7&\kappa=1&2&3&4&5&6\\ \hline
   \#&297&8&20&140&237&442.
   \end{array}                                                   \tag{1.5}
   \]

3. For each `r=2,3,4,5`, a perfect aligned-containment matching from every
   member of `N_r` to the fibre `kappa^{-1}(r)`.  For `r=6`, a perfect
   matching from \(N_6-S\) to \(\kappa^{-1}(6)\subset N_7\).

4. For every `B` with `kappa(B)=1`, one aligned singleton contained in
   `B`.  These choices have no global rank-one capacity.

5. A perfect aligned-containment matching from `B_*` to `N_8`.

Composing the two aligned incidences through each `B` gives the literal
nested path (0.1), including its phase.

#### Proof

Given a flag factor, take `B=C_0 union C_1`.  Exact rank-six coverage says
that every rank-six orbit occurs exactly once, either as `B` or as the
type-`I` lower vertex `A`, and never in both roles.  The masses give (1.3),
(1.4), and all fibres in (1.5).  Exact use of every lower orbit makes the
`A-B` incidences matchings; exact use of every rank-eight root makes the
`B-Q` incidences a matching.  The selected phases are part of these
aligned edges.

Conversely, compose the selected `A-B` and `B-Q` aligned containments.
The two ranks determine one of the nine types in (1.1), and (1.5) gives
the required masses.  Every orbit of ranks two through five occurs once
as an `A`; every rank-six orbit occurs once in exactly one of the two
roles; every rank-seven orbit occurs once as a `B`; and every rank-eight
orbit occurs once as a `Q`.  Therefore the resulting flags have exactly
the required lower palette.  \(\square\)

## 2. Exact switch basis on a fixed role/colour face

Fix `(S,kappa)`.  Each matching in Theorem 1.1 is now an ordinary
bipartite perfect matching polytope.  The product of these polytopes is an
integral, totally unimodular extended formulation.

### Theorem 2.1 (complete alternating-cycle basis)

Any two palette-perfect rooted factors with the same `(S,kappa)` differ by
a disjoint collection of:

1. even alternating cycles in the bipartite graph from `N_r` to
   `kappa^{-1}(r)` for the lower-middle matchings (for `r=3,5` this graph
   contains both the rank-six and rank-seven target fibres, so a cycle may
   use both);
2. even alternating cycles in the selected-middle-to-`N_8` containment
   multigraph; and
3. independent replacement of the contained singleton at a
   `kappa=1` middle vertex.

Conversely, every such move preserves every lower target orbit and every
type mass.

#### Proof

The symmetric difference of two perfect matchings in a bipartite
multigraph is a disjoint union of even alternating cycles.  Apply this
separately to each matching in Theorem 1.1.  Rank-one singleton choices
carry no shared capacity and hence change independently.  Composition at
the unchanged middle vertex preserves a literal nested flag.  \(\square\)

This is the promised small switch basis.  It is complete only on a fixed
role/colour face.  Changing whether a rank-six orbit is an `A` or a `B`, or
changing `kappa`, requires a synchronized exchange among several matching
layers; treating those layerwise cycles independently is unsound.

## 3. Exact outer Hall master

For `v in N_6 union N_7`, let `b_v` indicate membership in `B_*`; set
`b_v=1` on `N_7`.  Let `u_{v,r}` indicate `kappa(v)=r`.  The local rows are

\[
 \sum_r u_{v,r}=b_v,\qquad
 \sum_{v\in N_6}b_v=286,                             \tag{3.1}
\]

together with the allowed rank pairs and the fibre totals (1.5).

For a family `X`, write `N(X)` for its aligned-containment neighbourhood
after forgetting parallel phases.  The matching layers in Theorem 1.1
exist if and only if all of the following Hall systems hold:

\[
 \sum_{v\in X}b_v\le |N_8(X)|
       \qquad(X\subseteq N_6\cup N_7),                         \tag{3.2}
\]

\[
 |X|\le\sum_{v\in N(X)}u_{v,r}
       \qquad(X\subseteq N_r, r=2,3,4,5),                    \tag{3.3}
\]

and

\[
 \sum_{a\in X}(1-b_a)
     \le\sum_{v\in N(X)}u_{v,6}
       \qquad(X\subseteq N_6).                                \tag{3.4}
\]

The total cardinalities in (3.1) and (1.5) make these saturating
matchings.  Each violated inequality is returned by a bipartite
maximum-flow/min-cut computation.  Hence (3.1)--(3.4), with integral
`b,u`, are an exact Benders master for the complete lower-palette factor.
Once `b,u` are fixed, literal matching edges and phases are recovered by
the independent matching subproblems.

### Proposition 3.1 (where total unimodularity stops)

The natural raw rooted-flag exact-cover matrix is not totally unimodular,
already in the type-`C` subcatalogue.

#### Proof

Use masks on `Z_17`.  Let

\[
 Q=0x00ff,\quad Q'=0x017f,\quad
 A=0x0003,\quad A'=0x0005,\quad
 B=0x007f,\quad B'=0x00bf.                           \tag{3.5}
\]

All displayed containments below are literal, and `Q,Q'` are distinct
rank-eight necklace representatives.  The three valid type-`C` columns are

\[
 A\subset B'\subset Q,\qquad
 A'\subset B\subset Q,\qquad
 A\subset B\subset Q'.                               \tag{3.6}
\]

The orbits `[A]` and `[A']` are distinct (adjacent versus separated
two-sets), and `[B]` and `[B']` are distinct (seven consecutive points
versus a different gap pattern).  Restrict the incidence matrix to the
rows `Q`, `[A]`, `[B]` and the three columns (3.6).  It is

\[
 \begin{pmatrix}
 1&1&0\\
 1&0&1\\
 0&1&1
 \end{pmatrix},
 \qquad \det=-2.                                      \tag{3.7}
\]

Therefore the matrix is not totally unimodular.  \(\square\)

The determinant does not contradict Theorem 2.1: the latter is an
extended formulation using the two incidence matchings separately.  The
outer role/colour problem simultaneously imposes several transversal
matching conditions, a partition quota, and the complement coupling
`N_6-S`.  It is not an instance of ordinary two-matroid intersection by
mere inspection.  No integrality claim for the outer `(b,u)` relaxation is
made; `b,u` must remain binary unless an additional theorem proves that
particular relaxation integral.

## 4. First objective: exact zero-root minimization

Let `F_q` be the complete menu of literal rooted flags at root `q`, and use
one binary `x_f` per flag.  The lower-palette equations are equivalently
the original exact-cover rows, or the extended formulation of Sections
1--3.  For complete flag options `f,g`, write `f -> g` when at least one of
their nine attachment states has a literal phase-labelled transition to
an attachment state of `g`.

For every root use binary variables `d_q^+,d_q^-`.  The inequalities

\[
 d_q^+\ge x_f-\sum_{g\in\Gamma^+(f)}x_g
       \qquad(f\in F_q),                              \tag{4.1}
\]

\[
 d_q^-\ge x_f-\sum_{g\in\Gamma^-(f)}x_g
       \qquad(f\in F_q)                               \tag{4.2}
\]

and objective

\[
             \min\sum_q(d_q^++d_q^-)                 \tag{4.3}
\]

compute exactly the minimum number of zero-out plus zero-in roots among
all lower-palette-perfect flag factors.

Indeed, exactly one `f in F_q` is selected.  Its right side in (4.1) is
one precisely when no selected compatible target exists, and is at most
zero otherwise; minimization makes `d_q^+` its exact dead-row indicator.
The incoming statement is identical.

This first objective is a root-support relaxation.  It does not yet make
the nine attachment choices owner-bijective, and it does not coordinate a
single incoming and outgoing transition at the same selected attachment.

## 5. Second objective: exact owner/root/`H` transversal

For every selected flag option `f`, materialize its nine aligned owner
attachment states.  Let `z_s` select a state, and let `y_a` select a
literal phase-labelled transition.  Channel states to flags by

\[
                 z_s\le x_{f(s)}.                    \tag{5.1}
\]

The first incidence matching `D` is exactly

\[
 \sum_{s:\operatorname{root}(s)=q}z_s=1,\qquad
 \sum_{s:\operatorname{owner}(s)=o}z_s=1.            \tag{5.2}
\]

The exact cycle-cover rows are

\[
 \sum_{a:\operatorname{tail}(a)=s}y_a=z_s,\qquad
 \sum_{a:\operatorname{head}(a)=s}y_a=z_s.           \tag{5.3}
\]

Equations (5.1)--(5.3), together with the lower-palette factor, are
necessary and sufficient for a 1,430-state root/owner/`H` transversal and
a literal depth-three cycle cover.  The outgoing transition through every
old root supplies one `H` incidence there; the incoming equation and the
owner row in (5.2) supply one `H` incidence at every owner.  Thus `H` is
automatically perfect.

For a fixed selected state transversal `Z`, split its states into tail and
head copies and retain the literal transition arcs.  Then

\[
 \delta(Z)=\max_{X\subseteq Z}
       \bigl(|X|-|N_Z^+(X)|\bigr)                    \tag{5.4}
\]

is its exact transition-matching deficiency.  The best possible full
deficiency after rooted switches is

\[
 \boxed{
 \Delta_*=\min_{\substack{F\text{ lower-palette perfect}\\
                           Z\text{ root/owner transversal of }F}}
             \delta(Z).}                             \tag{5.5}
\]

A full cycle cover exists if and only if `Delta_*=0`.

An exact integer Benders implementation uses (5.2) as the attachment
master and a bipartite matching subproblem.  If an incumbent `Z` returns a
Hall witness \(X\subseteq Z\), the conditional cut

\[
 \sum_{t\in\Gamma^+(X)}z_t
   +|X|\sum_{s\in X}(1-z_s)\ge |X|                  \tag{5.6}
\]

is valid.  When every state in `X` remains selected, it is precisely its
Hall row; changing any tail state deactivates this incumbent-specific
witness.  Repeating maximum-matching/min-cut separation is complete.

For fixed flags, (5.2) alone is a bipartite perfect-matching polytope and
is integral.  The transition subproblem for fixed `Z` is also bipartite and
integral.  Their correlation through (5.6) is the genuine integer gate;
one must not infer integrality of the joint model from the two separate
matching theorems.

### Proposition 6.1 (103-row necessary radius)

Any rooted flag factor which gives every owner an incoming literal
transition and differs from the authenticated factor on a root set `R`
has

\[
                              |R|\ge103.              \tag{6.2}
\]

#### Proof

Take an owner `o` which had zero incoming support in the authenticated
factor.  A new transition into `o` uses an old-root `H` incidence and a
target-state `D` incidence; both corresponding rank-eight roots are
incident with `o`.  If neither rooted partition changed, exactly the same
complete-state transition would have existed before.  Hence at least one
changed root incident with `o` is necessary.  The incidence neighbourhoods
of `R` must therefore cover all 782 such owners.

Even ignoring overlaps, the 102 largest values of `c(q)` sum to 779,
whereas the 103 largest sum to 786.  Thus 102 changed rows cannot cover all
782 owners.  \(\square\)

This is only a necessary radius bound.  It does not assert that 103 rows
admit palette-preserving replacements, nor that covering the owner
projection resolves root liveness or selected-state Hall.

The exact proof-safe order is:

1. solve (4.1)--(4.3), using only palette-perfect factors, and record the
   optimum pair `(zero-out,zero-in)` lexicographically if desired;
2. if both are zero, add (5.1)--(5.3), or use (5.2) with the Hall oracle
   (5.6), and minimize (5.5);
3. only after `Delta_*=0`, impose quotient subtour cuts and nonzero voltage;
4. impose upper shadows, opening, and common compiler in later layers.

To minimize disruption from the authenticated factor, add as a final
lexicographic objective the number of changed matching incidences in the
factorization of Theorem 1.1.  On a fixed `(S,kappa)` face, Theorem 2.1
makes this literally the minimum total alternating-cycle support.  Across
role/colour faces, the exact Benders master remains valid, but no bounded
local switch theorem is assumed.
