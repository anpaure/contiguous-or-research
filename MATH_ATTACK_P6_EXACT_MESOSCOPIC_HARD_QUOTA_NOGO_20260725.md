\[
 \boxed{

## 1. Exact hard quotas and the packet-forcing lemma

At depth \(q\), put

\[
 N_q=\binom{n}{m-q},\qquad
 c_q=\left\lfloor\frac{W}{N_q}\right\rfloor.
\]

A balanced quota is a map

\[
 \beta_q:\binom{[n]}{m-q}\longrightarrow\{c_q,c_q+1\},
 \qquad
 \sum_S\beta_q(S)=W.
 \tag{1.1}
\]

If \(F\) is an exact factor and \(S\) is a target, let
\(\mathcal O_F(S)\) be the set of rows of \(F\) which own \(S\).  An exact
split \(F=K\dot\cup B\) is quota-safe through the controlled depths when

\[
 |\mathcal O_K(S)|\le\beta_q(S)
 \tag{1.2}
\]

for every controlled \((q,S)\).

At depth one,

\[
 \frac{W}{N_1}
 =\frac{\binom{2m+1}{m}}{\binom{2m+1}{m-1}}
 =\frac{m+2}{m}.
 \tag{1.3}
\]

Therefore \(c_1=1\) for \(m\ge3\), and every balanced depth-one quota is
one or two.

### Lemma 1.1 -- disjoint three-owner forcing

Suppose an exact factor \(G\) contains \(r\) pairwise row-disjoint triples

\[
 T_i=\{E_{i,1},E_{i,2},E_{i,3}\},\qquad 1\le i\le r,
 \]

such that the three rows of \(T_i\) own a common depth-one target \(S_i\).
Let \(F\) be another exact factor and put

\[
 d(F,G)=|G\setminus F|=|F\setminus G|.
\]

For a nonempty family \(\mathcal A\) of exact factors, write

\[
 d(F,\mathcal A)=\min_{G\in\mathcal A}d(F,G).
 \tag{1.3a}
\]

For every balanced quota system containing depth one and every quota-safe
split \(F=K\dot\cup B\),

\[
 \boxed{|B|+d(F,G)\ge r.}
 \tag{1.4}
\]

#### Proof

Deleting one row of \(G\) destroys at most one of the disjoint triples.
Consequently at least \(r-d(F,G)\) triples lie wholly in \(F\).  The core
cannot retain all three rows of any such triple, because its common target
has quota at most two.  Thus \(B\) meets every intact triple.  The intact
triples are row-disjoint, so

\[
 |B|\ge r-d(F,G).
\]

This is (1.4).  Extra owners of \(S_i\), if any, only strengthen the
constraint. \(\square\)

Lemma 1.1 is the integral form of the survival-packet obstruction.  It
uses neither fractional covering nor rounding.

## 2. Invariant targets survive every native component signing

### Lemma 2.1 -- invariant-target principle

Let \(S\) be a target with \(\tau S=S\).  If a canonical row \(E\) owns
\(S\), then both \(E\) and \(\tau E\) own \(S\).

#### Proof

Relabelling carries ownership equivariantly.  Thus \(\tau E\) owns
\(\tau S=S\). \(\square\)

Suppose three distinct canonical rows own such an \(S\).  A vertex of
\(\mathfrak Q_{\tau,m}\) selects, in the component containing each row,
either that row or its \(\tau\)-image.  Exactness makes the selected rows
distinct, and Lemma 2.1 says that all three still own \(S\).  Hence every
native cube vertex contains the resulting three-owner packet, independently
of all component signs.

## 3. A self-contained positive-density invariant packet family

Use the audited semilength-four collision seed

\[
 A=11110000,\qquad
 B_0=11101000,\qquad
 DD=11001100,
 \tag{3.1}
\]

whose three canonical MSW rows own the common internal upper colour

\[
 T_0=10111101.
 \tag{3.2}
\]

Canonical MSW prefix suspension has the following exact form.  If
\(P\in\mathcal D_p\), \(V\in\mathcal D_v\), and the seed is inserted
between them, the three roots

\[
 PAV,\qquad PB_0V,\qquad PDDV
 \tag{3.3}
\]

own the target obtained from the upper-core word

\[
 \overline P\,T_0V.
 \tag{3.4}
\]

This follows from the exact concatenation law

\[
 \pi(PQ)=\pi(P)\mathbin\Vert(2p+\pi(Q))
 \tag{3.5}
\]

for the MSW flip permutation.  Thus the prefix has been complemented when
the seed trajectory begins, while the suffix is still fixed.

Take the prefix-free family

\[
 \mathscr P_{\rm inv}
 =\{10\}\cup
 \{P:P\text{ is primitive Dyck and begins }111\}.
 \tag{3.6}
\]

For \(P=10\), the word in (3.4) begins \(01T_0\).  Its second and third
bits are both one.  If \(P\) is primitive and begins \(111\), then
\(\overline P\) begins \(000\), so again its second and third bits agree.
In either case the complementary depth-one target is fixed by
\(\tau=(2\ 3)\).

There are

\[
 \operatorname{Cat}_{p-1}-\operatorname{Cat}_{p-2}
 \tag{3.7}
\]

primitive Dyck words of semilength \(p\ge3\) beginning \(111\).  Indeed,
there are \(\operatorname{Cat}_{p-1}\) primitive words in total, and the
ones beginning \(110\) are in bijection with \(\mathcal D_{p-2}\).

The cylinders in (3.6) are prefix-free, and different suffixes give
different roots.  Therefore the owner triples in (3.3) are pairwise
row-disjoint.  For \(m\ge6\), their number is

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 I_m
 &=\operatorname{Cat}_{m-5}
 +\sum_{p=3}^{m-4}
   \bigl(\operatorname{Cat}_{p-1}-\operatorname{Cat}_{p-2}\bigr)
   \operatorname{Cat}_{m-p-4}\\
 &=\operatorname{Cat}_{m-4}-\operatorname{Cat}_{m-5}.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{3.8}
\]

The second equality is the Catalan convolution after subtracting its
one-step shift.  Since, for every fixed \(a\),

\[
 \frac{\operatorname{Cat}_{m-a}}{\operatorname{Cat}_m}
 =4^{-a}\bigl(1+O_a(m^{-1})\bigr),
 \tag{3.9}
\]

we obtain

\[
 I_m
 =\left(\frac1{256}-\frac1{1024}+O(m^{-1})\right)t_m
 =\left(\frac3{1024}+O(m^{-1})\right)t_m.
 \tag{3.10}
\]

Lemmas 1.1 and 2.1 already prove the elementary cube-wide bound

\[
 |B|+d(F,\mathfrak Q_{\tau,m})\ge I_m.
 \tag{3.11}
\]

This positive constant alone rules out the required exceptional scale.

## 4. The strongest audited native-cube constant

The recursively refined disjoint seed matching has generating function

\[
 M(z)=
 \frac{z^4C(z)}
 {1-(1+z^2)(zC(z)-z^2-2z^4)-2z^6}.
 \tag{4.1}
\]

Its proved asymptotic data are

\[
 M(1/4)=\frac2{139},
 \qquad
 \frac{[z^m]M(z)}{\operatorname{Cat}_m}
 \longrightarrow
 \delta_*:=\frac{275}{19321}.
 \tag{4.2}
\]

This is the exact recursive matching theorem from
`FRACTIONAL_PACKET_GLOBAL_SEED_MATCHING_20260725.md`; its recursion and
disjointness were independently audited in
`FRACTIONAL_PACKET_GLOBAL_SEED_MATCHING_AUDIT_20260725.md`.  It is a proved
input, not an unproved lemma.

After any primitive prefix beginning \(111\), one may place the whole
recursive matching rather than only the immediate seed.  All resulting
targets remain \(\tau\)-invariant because coordinates two and three lie in
the complemented prefix.  The prefix \(10\) may use only the immediate
seed family.  Hence the invariant packet generating function is exactly

\[
 J(z)=z^5C(z)+H(z)M(z),
 \qquad
 H(z)=z(1-z)(C(z)-1)-z^2.
 \tag{4.3}
\]

To compute its density, put \(s=\sqrt{1-4z}\).  Since

\[
 C(z)=2-2s+O(s^2),
 \tag{4.4}
\]

direct substitution gives

\[
 H(z)=\frac18-\frac38s+O(s^2).
 \tag{4.5}
\]

For square-root singularities, a coefficient \(a_1s\) has asymptotic
ratio \(-a_1/2\) to \(\operatorname{Cat}_m\).  Equation (4.2) therefore
says that the linear coefficient of \(M\) is \(-2\delta_*\).  The linear
coefficient of \(HM\) is

\[
 -\frac14\delta_*-\frac38\frac2{139}.
\]

Consequently

\[
 \frac{[z^m]H(z)M(z)}{\operatorname{Cat}_m}
 \longrightarrow
 \frac18\delta_*+\frac3{16}\frac2{139}
 =\frac{173}{38642}.
 \tag{4.6}
\]

The first term of \(J\) has density \(4^{-5}=1/1024\).  Therefore

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \delta_{\rm inv}
 &=\frac1{1024}+\frac{173}{38642}\\
 &=\frac{107897}{19784704}.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{4.7}
\]

Every native cube vertex contains these \(J_m\) pairwise row-disjoint
three-owner packets.  Lemma 1.1, followed by minimization over the nearest
cube vertex, proves (0.1).  This proves all constants and quantifiers in
the native-cube theorem.

Coordinate relabelling preserves exact factors, ownership, disjointness,
balanced quotas, and row distance.  Hence (0.1) holds around every
conjugate cube \(\sigma\mathfrak Q_{\tau,m}\), without requiring the quota
assignment itself to be symmetric.

## 5. Every fixed-position universal two-for-two cube retains a packet matching

We now prove (0.5)--(0.6).  The proof is independent of invariant-target
mixing: it finds canonical owner rows untouched by every trade in the
chosen fixed-position cube.

Fix \(m\ge7\) and \(0\le p\le m-2\).  A negative-support row of a
fixed-\(p\) trade has root

\[
 P1100R\quad\text{or}\quad P1010R,
 \tag{5.1}
\]

where \(P\in\mathcal D_p\).  Thus a root can be removed only if it returns
to height zero at semilength \(p\) and the next four bits form one of the
two displayed markers.

### Cases \(p=0,2\)

For every \(V\in\mathcal D_{m-5}\), use the three suspended roots

\[
 10AV,\qquad 10B_0V,\qquad 10DDV.
 \tag{5.2}
\]

All begin \(1011\).  When \(p=0\), this is neither marker in (5.1).  When
\(p=2\), the first four bits would have to be the Dyck prefix \(P\), but
\(1011\) is not balanced.  Thus none of these rows is in the negative
support of a fixed-\(p\) trade.

There are \(\operatorname{Cat}_{m-5}\) choices of \(V\), and prefix
suspension proves that each triple coowns its common depth-one target.

### Cases \(p=1,3\) and \(p\ge4\)

Let \(V\) range over the primitive Dyck words of semilength \(m-4\), and
use

\[
 AV,\qquad B_0V,\qquad DDV.
 \tag{5.3}
\]

There are exactly \(\operatorname{Cat}_{m-5}\) such words \(V\).

At semilengths one and three, none of the three seeds in (3.1) has returned
to height zero.  This excludes \(p=1,3\).

For \(p=4\), the marker in (5.1) would be the first four bits of \(V\).
Because \(m\ge7\), the primitive word \(V\) has semilength at least three.
It cannot begin \(1100\), which returns after four bits, or \(1010\),
which returns after two bits.

For \(5\le p\le m-2\), the prospective end of \(P\) lies strictly inside
the primitive suffix \(V\):

\[
 1\le p-4\le m-6<m-4.
\]

The suffix has no return there.  Hence the whole root has no Dyck return at
semilength \(p\), and again cannot have form (5.1).

Thus every triple in (5.2) or (5.3) remains literally present at every
vertex of \(\mathfrak C_{p,m}\).  Support-disjoint exactness prevents a
positive trade row from colliding with one of these untouched rows.  The
triples are row-disjoint as \(V\) varies and retain their common seed
targets.  Lemma 1.1 gives (0.5), and its distance form gives (0.6).
\(\square\)

The finite case \((m,p)=(6,4)\) needs a different presentation because the
primitive suffix \(V=1100\) begins with a marker.  The suspended roots
\(10A10,\ 10B_0\,10,\ 10DD10\) repair that boundary case.  It is irrelevant to
the asymptotic theorem, which was stated for \(m\ge7\).

The phrase "fixed-position universal two-for-two cube" is deliberate.  The
source theorem proves support-disjoint exact two-for-two trades and hence a
Boolean cube; except in the separately identified \(p=0\) C8 family, no
cycle-length or global ownership-component classification is needed or
claimed.

## 6. External-repair and trajectory capacity ceilings

Let

\[
 F_0,F_1,\ldots,F_s
\]

be exact factors, with \(F_0\in\mathfrak Q_{\tau,m}\), and let

\[
 r_i=d(F_{i-1},F_i)
 \]

be the number of actual wreath rows replaced at step \(i\).  Row distance
is a metric on equal-size factors, so

\[
 d(F_s,\mathfrak Q_{\tau,m})
 \le d(F_s,F_0)
 \le\sum_{i=1}^s r_i.
 \tag{6.1}
\]

Applying (0.1) to a terminal quota-safe split
\(F_s=K\dot\cup B\) gives

\[
 \boxed{
 |B|+\sum_{i=1}^s r_i\ge J_m.
 }
 \tag{6.2}
\]

This is an exact total-variation ceiling.  More directly, (0.1) gives

\[
 d(F_s,\mathfrak Q_{\tau,m})
 \ge (J_m-|B|)_+.
 \tag{6.3}
\]

Thus a terminal exception \(o(t_m/\sqrt m)\) requires the terminal factor
to lie at distance

\[
 \bigl(\delta_{\rm inv}-o(1)\bigr)t_m.
 \tag{6.4}
\]

Cube-internal switches do not count as an escape from the cube regardless
of how many are made; their terminal distance to the cube is zero.  If each
step in the displayed exact-factor trajectory replaces at most \(L_m\)
rows, then (6.2) proves

\[
 s\ge
 \left\lceil\frac{(J_m-|B|)_+}{L_m}\right\rceil.
 \tag{6.5}
\]

In particular, for \(L_m=\Theta(m)\) and
\(|B|=o(t_m/\sqrt m)\), one needs \(\Omega(t_m/m)\) trajectory steps and
linear total row variation.

Equation (6.2) does not prohibit a positive-density nonnative walk.  It
proves that such a walk is necessary.

## 7. Prefix-local external rebundling also leaves linear packets

There is a useful hybrid strengthening.  Assume \(m\ge7\).  Fix
\(G\in\mathfrak Q_{\tau,m}\), and let

\[
 \phi_G(R)\in\{E(R),\tau E(R)\}
 \]

be the row selected by \(G\) from the component containing the canonical
source root \(R\).

Suppose an exact factor \(F\) retains every \(\phi_G(R)\) outside a union
of nonempty Dyck-prefix cylinders whose maximum prefix semilength is
\(r\le m-5\).  Inside those cylinders arbitrary exact rebundling is
allowed.  Then, for every balanced quota system containing depth one,
every quota-safe split \(F=K\dot\cup B\) satisfies

\[
 \boxed{
 |B|\ge K^{\rm inv}_{m,r}:=
 \sum_{p=\max(3,r+1)}^{m-4}
 \bigl(\operatorname{Cat}_{p-1}-\operatorname{Cat}_{p-2}\bigr)
 \operatorname{Cat}_{m-p-4}.
 }
 \tag{7.1}
\]

Indeed, take primitive prefixes \(P\) beginning \(111\), of semilength
\(p>r\), and the roots

\[
 PAV,\qquad PB_0V,\qquad PDDV.
\]

No shorter nonempty Dyck word is a prefix of primitive \(P\), so all three
selected cube rows remain in \(F\).  Their common suspended target is
\(\tau\)-invariant, and the triples remain disjoint.  Lemma 1.1 proves
(7.1).

The last summand \(p=m-4\) alone yields

\[
 K^{\rm inv}_{m,r}
 \ge \operatorname{Cat}_{m-5}-\operatorname{Cat}_{m-6}
 =\left(\frac3{4096}+O(m^{-1})\right)t_m.
 \tag{7.2}
\]

Thus arbitrary native component signing followed by arbitrary exact
rebundling confined to a union of nonempty Dyck-prefix cylinders of maximum
semilength at most \(m-5\) still cannot produce the required exceptional
scale.

## 8. Quantitative composition into a literal constant-one word

This section records why the forbidden scale \(o(t_m/\sqrt m)\) is exactly
the one needed for the sharp coefficient.  It also verifies that exact
completion is not a hidden final gate.

Let \(1\le H\le m-1\).  Suppose one exact factor has a common split

\[
 F=K\dot\cup B,\qquad b=|B|,
 \]

and balanced full-mass quotas through depth \(H\), with \(K\) quota-safe at
every one of those depths.  Put

\[
 S_{m,H}=\sum_{q=1}^H\frac1{c_q}.
\]

The exact hard-quota charging and literal central erosion construction give
a nonzero contiguous-OR word of length

\[
 \boxed{
 L\le
 W+(2H+1)t_m+2nbS_{m,H}+T_{m,H}.
 }
 \tag{8.1}
\]

Here an entirely literal choice for the two outer tails is

\[
 T_{m,H}=2L_{\rm tail}(m,m-H-1)+1,
 \tag{8.2}
\]

where

\[
 L_{\rm tail}(m,r)=
 2\sum_{a=0}^{\lfloor m/2\rfloor}
 N_m(a)w_m(a)C_m(r-a)+m,
 \tag{8.3}
\]

\[
 N_m(a)=\binom ma-\binom m{a-1},
 \qquad
 w_m(0)=m,
 \qquad
 w_m(a)=m-2a+2\quad(a>0),
 \tag{8.4}
\]

and

\[
 C_m(u)=
 \begin{cases}
 0,&u<0,\\
 \binom mu,&0\le u\le\lfloor m/2\rfloor,\\
 \binom m{\lfloor m/2\rfloor},&u>\lfloor m/2\rfloor.
 \end{cases}
 \tag{8.5}
\]

To verify (8.2), write
\([n]=[2m]\mathbin{\dot\cup}\{\infty\}\).  Take the exact two-tail word
from (8.3) on \([2m]\), concatenate a second copy in which \(\infty\) is
adjoined to every letter, and append the singleton letter
\(\{\infty\}\).  The first copy covers tail targets avoiding \(\infty\),
the second covers those containing \(\infty\) with nonempty old part, and
the singleton covers the remaining target.  This proves the factor
\(2L_{\rm tail}+1\).

For completeness, the charging behind (8.1) is short.  At each depth the
rows of \(B\) contribute total load \(nb\).  Since \(K\le\beta\), the
overload of the full factor above \(\beta\) is at most \(nb\).  Every
missing target costs at least \(c_q\) units of underload, so the number
\(M_q\) of missing lower targets satisfies

\[
 M_q\le\frac{nb}{c_q}.
 \tag{8.6}
\]

The literal central word has base length
\(W+(2H+1)t_m\), and appending the missing lower and complementary upper
targets costs at most \(2\sum_{q\le H}M_q\).  This proves (8.1); (8.2)--
(8.5) are the exact symmetric-chain tail word.

The audited reciprocal-quota estimate is

\[
 S_{m,H}\le\sum_{q=1}^{m-1}\frac1{c_q}
 \le\sqrt{2\pi m}.
 \tag{8.7}
\]

If hard-quota splits with

\[
 b=o_A(t_m/\sqrt m)
 \tag{8.8}
\]

exist separately for every fixed window \(q\le A\sqrt m\), choose an
integer diagonal \(j=j(m)\to\infty\) slowly enough that

\[
 j\le m^{1/7},\qquad
 \frac{b\sqrt m}{t_m}\longrightarrow0,
 \]

and set \(H=\lceil j\sqrt m\rceil\).  Then

\[
 \frac{(2H+1)t_m}{W}=o(1),
 \qquad
 \frac{2nbS_{m,H}}W=o(1).
\]

Moreover \(H/\sqrt m\to\infty\) and \(H=o(m^{2/3})\), so the
moderate-deviation estimate for (8.3) gives \(T_{m,H}=o(W)\).  Hence

\[
 L=W+o(W).
 \tag{8.9}
\]

The established trimmed one-bit lift gives the other parity.  Thus a small
exact hard-quota completion would compose quantitatively into
\(\nu(k)\le(1+o(1))W(k)\).  Theorems (0.1) and (0.5) prove that the current
mesoscopic canonical cubes cannot supply that completion.

All objects in (8.1) are literal: \(F,K,B\) are integral wreath rows, the
central word is emitted from their cyclic interval orders, missing targets
are appended as actual nonzero entries, and the tails are explicit Euler
words.  No labelled common-owner synchronization is invoked.

## 9. Adversarial audit and exact scope

1. **Depth one alone forces the obstruction.**  The proof does not sum
   incompatible quota choices across depths.  Every balanced depth-one
   quota is at most two.

2. **The packet triples need not be complete owner sets.**  Additional
   owners cannot allow the core to retain all three displayed rows.

3. **Every lower bound is integral.**  Pairwise disjoint triples force
   distinct deleted rows.  Fractional packet duality is not needed.

4. **Exact completion is automatic only because the core is cut from one
   exact factor.**  A partial packing in the ambient wreath hypergraph can
   have a regular leave which is not wreath-factorable; no such completion
   is asserted here.

5. **Internal native switching cannot evade (0.1).**  Invariant targets,
   rather than monochromatic component signs, are decisive.  Mixed choices
   \(E\) and \(\tau E\) still coown the same target.

6. **The fixed-\(p\) theorem is separate for each \(p\).**  It does not
   assert that trades at different positions may be switched
   simultaneously, nor that their negative supports are mutually
   disjoint.

7. **The full native-cube theorem is stronger at \(p=0\).**  It controls
   all components of the \((2\ 3)\) interaction cube, not only the C8
   subcube used by the mesoscopic block construction.

8. **The trajectory inequality counts actual row replacement.**  Repeated
   changes can only increase \(\sum_i r_i\); triangle inequality is in the
   safe direction.

9. **The asymptotic constants have been independently audited.**  The
   elementary fixed-\(p\) proof, its case split, the
   \(1/1024\) constant, and the finite \((6,4)\) boundary were separately
   rechecked.  The recursive constant in (0.3) was independently obtained
   from the singular expansion in Section 4.

10. **The literal transfer uses fixed-window quantifiers correctly.**
    The packet rank and little-o constants may depend on fixed \(A\).  One
    first proves every fixed window and only then takes the slow diagonal;
    no growing-\(A\) rounding theorem is assumed.

11. **No global no-go is claimed.**  The results do not exclude exact
    factors at positive-density distance from every conjugate native cube,
    multitransposition adaptive recomputation with linear row turnover, or
    genuinely global non-prefix trades.

12. **No proof of the sharp constant-one conjecture is claimed.**  The
    report supplies the allowed alternative: a rigorous counterinvariant
    that definitively closes the native, one-fixed-position, and
    prefix-local exact mesoscopic architectures stated here.  A successful
    hard-quota construction must make a positive-density nonnative move and
    simultaneously prove that its terminal disjoint survival-packet
    matching is \(o_A(t_m/\sqrt m)\).

## 10. Final verdict

The canonical \(p=0\) mesoscopic wreath packets do change \(\Theta(m)\)
shallow slots, preserve exact middle ownership, and can be bundled at
positive density.  They nevertheless retain a positive-density disjoint
family of hard-quota survival packets.  Therefore their exact exceptional
completion has size \(\Omega(t_m)\).

The obstruction survives:

* every signing of the full native one-transposition interaction cube;
* every blocking or adaptive selection of the original trades at one fixed
  position \(p\);
* every \(o(t_m)\)-row perturbation of those endpoints; and
* arbitrary exact rebundling confined to the prefix-cylinder class in
  Section 7.

Hence an exact mesoscopic hard-quota absorber with exceptional family
\(o(t_m/\sqrt m)\) cannot be built within the native
one-transposition, one-fixed-position two-for-two, or prefix-local
architectures proved here.  Any genuine escape must use positive-density
external, multitransposition, or global recombination, after which the
hard-quota packet matching has to be proved small anew.
