## 1. Why the one hundred rows force a non-seam packet

### Lemma 1.1 (context-extendable rank-ten seam necessity)

Let rank-nine owner pieces be internally resident.  If an internally
missing rank-ten target `U` is represented by a consecutive interval after
concatenating the intact pieces, then some inter-piece seam in that interval
joins two distinct facets of `U`.  That seam has upper colour `U` and must
itself pass the boundary residence automaton.

#### Proof

Every owner in the witnessing interval is a rank-nine subset of `U`.
Across an inter-piece boundary the adjacent owners are distinct, so their
union has rank ten and is contained in `U`; it therefore equals `U`.
Internal adjacencies were already counted in the internal deck.  The final
claim is necessary for the whole chronology to be resident. \(\square\)

Consequently each of the one hundred rows in (0.3) is a literal no-go for
every ordering and orientation of the frozen `6258` pieces.  A compact
three-facet socket bypasses the obstruction by placing two internal copies
of its target into a newly rethreaded resident macro.

## 3. The smallest exact owner-forest projection

This section isolates the socket-installation projection.  The stronger
successor/orientation, all-rank accumulated-union, and compiler rows are
already formalized in
`MATH_THEOREM_THREAD_D_K17_V7_SOCKET_MULTICHOICE_AND_REGENERATIVE_GATE_20260801.md`.
Nothing here weakens or replaces that full prospective master.

Let `\mathscr C` be the current SCD components and `\Omega_c` the allowed
cut/orientation states of component `c`.  Let `\mathcal D` be a finite set of
closure demands, including every declared recursive child.  For each
`U\in\mathcal D`, let `\mathcal P_U` contain **every allowed closure mode in
the stated catalogue**: a socket, an ordinary provider seam, parent-equal
self-service, an authenticated no-op, or a compiled compound option.  An
option belongs to exactly one root-demand part; multi-parent columns require
the covering formulation of the full prospective master and are outside
this projection.  An option `p` records:

1. exact component assignments and allowed guard-state domains;
2. its facet, guard, endpoint, and occurrence-labelled capacity footprint;
3. its additional deleted old edge set `D_p`, measured relative to the
   selected component states, and its added edge multiset `A_p`;
4. its complete local residence and Johnson replay; and
5. its lower, upper, component, and source/common-cap signatures.

Choose binary `x_p`, one component state `z_(c,o)`, and deletion-union
variables `y_e`.  The exact pre-Hamilton socket master has the following
rows.

### Choice and component consistency

\[
 \sum_{p\in\mathcal P_U}x_p=1\quad(U\in\mathcal D),\qquad
 \sum_{o\in\Omega_c}z_{c,o}=1,                              \tag{3.1}
\]

and `x_p<=z_(c,alpha_p(c))` for every exact component assignment required by
`p`.  If a guard permits a domain `Omega_(p,c) subseteq Omega_c` rather than

\[
                 x_p\le\sum_{o\in\Omega_{p,c}}z_{c,o}.         \tag{3.1a}

### Physical capacities

For every unit-capacity facet, guard occurrence, endpoint slot, internal
owner position, or other typed resource `a`,

\[
                    \sum_{p:a\in R(p)}x_p\le1.                \tag{3.2}

Every additional catalogue-specific incompatibility is an exact binary
nogood `x_p+x_q<=1`.

### Union, not sum, of cuts

For each old occurrence `e` deleted in addition to the cuts already encoded

\[
 x_p\le y_e\quad(e\in D_p),\qquad
 y_e\le\sum_{p:e\in D_p}x_p.                                 \tag{3.3}

Thus a shared physical cut is charged once.  Let `n_V(z)` be the number of
old occurrences of immediate-upper colour `V` in the selected component

Exact joint rank-ten survival is

\[
 n_V(z)-\sum_{e:\,\operatorname{up}(e)=V}y_e
       +\sum_p a_{p,V}x_p\ge1\qquad(\text{every rank-ten }V).   \tag{3.4}
\]

Equation (3.4) is the joint child-provider row missing from an individual
socket census.

## 4. Regeneration becomes exact after compiling complete packets

A **zero-child compound option** includes its socket, all named child
providers, and all recursively required sockets, and protects one fixed
surviving provider of every colour outside its declared target set.  This
last common witness bank prevents two individually harmless compounds from
jointly deleting the last providers of a third colour.

### Theorem 4.1 (finite DAG closure)

Let `\mathcal D^*` be closed under a finite acyclic declared-child relation.
If one chooses one authenticated option for every member of `\mathcal D^*`
such that

1. the options satisfy Theorem 3.1 jointly;
2. every declared child also belongs to `\mathcal D^*`; and
3. every colour outside `\mathcal D^*` has a protected provider untouched by
   the complete selection,

then simultaneous installation closes all demands in `\mathcal D^*` and
creates no new immediate-upper demand.

#### Proof

Every member of `\mathcal D^*` receives an added internal provider.  The
third condition preserves every outside colour.  Hence (3.4) holds for all
colours.  Acyclicity is used only to construct finite compound
descriptions; once a finite jointly closed `\mathcal D^*` is supplied it is
redundant.  The physical conclusion is then Theorem 3.1. \(\square\)

For the individual v7 label projection one may assign weight two to
`75749`, weight one to `14309`, and weight one to every other initial row.
The declared child ratio is at most `1/2`.  This is only a scalar
well-foundedness certificate; the facet-`10213` collision shows why the
joint hypotheses of Theorem 4.1 cannot be omitted.

There is also a dimension-uniform form.  In every reachable state, restrict
each demand to complete options satisfying

\[
              \sum_{V\in\partial p}w(V)\le\rho w(U),
              \qquad 0\le\rho<1,\quad1\le w\le K.             \tag{4.1}
\]

Here `partial p` is a multiset and therefore counts child multiplicities.
If one jointly compatible transversal of these options exists after every
history, then the active potential contracts.  For `H` initial unit demands

installations occur.  This is the exact
regenerative implication needed by the all-`k` lane; option availability is
the remaining theorem.

## 5. The published v7 menu is jointly impossible

The frozen catalogue has only two displayed choices per target, one `L=3`
and one `L=4`.  Under the audited coarse literal-key relation (shared facet,
incompatible exact component state, or identical guard segment), there are

- one `K_4` on `{22206,32391,73395,73635}`;

  `20403-82931`, `23351-82931`, `73586-82931`,
  `67451-73586`; and
- the path `69342-73434-73688`.

### Theorem 5.1 (exact menu-broadening floor)

The minimum vertex-cover number of this nogood graph is

\[
                         \boxed{\tau=19}.                       \tag{5.1}
\]

Consequently at least nineteen target menus must gain a new option before
the displayed L3/L4 catalogue can have a joint selector, even before all
interval, lower, child-provider, endpoint, graphic, and common-cap conflicts
are imposed.

#### Proof

The thirteen `K_2` components contribute thirteen.  The `K_4` contributes
three.  The four-edge tree has vertex cover `{82931,73586}` and contains a
matching of size two.  The final three-vertex path contributes one.  These
components are disjoint, so the sum is `13+3+2+1=19`.  If neither endpoint
of a certified nogood pair receives a new option, its old two-by-two
conflict matrix remains complete; hence every broadened target set is a
vertex cover. \(\square\)

The lead local certificate is the pair `8060,15996`: every one of their
four listed option pairs reuses facet owner `7804`, and the two demands also
force incompatible states `6` and `3` of component `22`.

On the stricter all-`L=3` architecture, exact half-open guard-interval and
component semantics give a certified static-conflict subgraph with one
hundred edges and an explicit matching of size thirty-seven.  Therefore at least thirty-seven
of the fixed `L=3` columns must be replaced.  This is again a restricted
menu lower bound, not a no-go for newly generated sockets.

The component-state part of the unrestricted problem has no ordinary Hall
characterization.  Indeed, give every Boolean component two states and,

fixed-state structure is mathematically substantive rather than cosmetic.

## 6. A checkable positive selector theorem

Fix the component states, a common protected-provider bank, and a topology
face on which every compatible option union is graphic-independent.  Form
the conflict graph `G` on the remaining complete options, with the demand
lists as its vertex parts.

### Theorem 6.1 (Haxell-regenerative selector)

If

\[
              \min_{U\in\mathcal D^*}|\mathcal P_U|
                    \ge\max\{1,2\Delta(G)\},                   \tag{6.1}
\]

then one can choose one pairwise compatible complete option per demand.
Together with (3.4) and the fixed provider/topology face, this is a zero-child
socket selector.  If the same inequality and (4.1) hold hereditarily after
every residual history, it gives the dimension-uniform regenerative bound
of Section 4.

#### Proof

Haxell's independent-transversal theorem selects one vertex from every
part of `G`.  The prepared provider and topology hypotheses convert graph
independence into every remaining row of Theorem 3.1.  The regenerative
conclusion is (4.1) telescoped over the successive states. \(\square\)

Before compiling children into packets, let `E_c` be the graph of possible
directed endpoint seams for child colour `c`.  Necessarily

\[
               \nu\left(\bigcup_{c\in Y}E_c\right)\ge |Y|
                    \quad(Y\subseteq\mathcal C).                \tag{6.2}
\]

The graph form of Aharoni--Haxell gives the sufficient condition

\[
               \nu\left(\bigcup_{c\in Y}E_c\right)
                    >2(|Y|-1)\quad(\varnothing\ne Y).           \tag{6.3}
\]

Condition (6.3) supplies only vertex-disjoint endpoint edges.  It does not
supply residence, component-state, lower-colour, graphic, or cap tickets
unless those resources are incorporated into the packet hyperedges.
Ordinary Hall is exact only on a private one-end/matroid face.  General
providers consume a tail and a head and form a three-partite rainbow
matching problem.

Finally, facet ownership itself is not the asymptotic obstruction.  The
Kruskal--Katona/Hall theorem in
`MATH_THEOREM_SMALL_FAMILY_DISJOINT_FACET_SOCKET_ALLOCATION_20260801.md`
assigns `b=h+1` distinct facets to every target whenever

\[
 |\mathcal D|\le
 \binom{r+\lfloor(r+1)/(h+1)\rfloor}{r+1}.             \tag{6.4}
\]

For `h=O(sqrt(r))` this covers every polynomial-size defect family in large
dimension.  Occurrence guards, component states, and joint provider
survival—not abstract facet supply—are the remaining extraction resources.
