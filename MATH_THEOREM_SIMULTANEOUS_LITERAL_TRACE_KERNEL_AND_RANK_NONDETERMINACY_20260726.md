# The simultaneous literal-trace kernel: zero-voltage cycles, large non-gauge directions, and rank non-determinacy

Date: 2026-07-26

Method: pure mathematics only.

## 0. Outcome

Let \(N=2m\), let \(1\le H\le (m+2)/3\), and let
\(\mathcal D_H\) be the safe-path de Bruijn digraph.  Its edges are the
safe owner histories

\[
  \gamma=(X_0,X_1,\ldots,X_H),
\]

and its vertices are their length-\((H-1)\) prefix and suffix histories.
Write \(M\) for its incidence matrix, \(A\) for root-owner incidence, and
\(S_q^\pm\) for the literal lower-meet and upper-join target maps.

This note gives an exact intrinsic description of

\[
 K_H=\ker_{\mathbb Z}A\cap\ker_{\mathbb Z}M\cap\ker_{\mathbb Z}D
       \cap\bigcap_{q\le H,\,\epsilon\in\{-,+\}}
                    \ker_{\mathbb Z}S_q^\epsilon.              \tag{0.1}
\]

The deletion row \(D\) is redundant.  If

\[
 \chi_H= A\oplus\bigoplus_{q\le H}(S_q^-\oplus S_q^+),        \tag{0.2}
\]

then

\[
 \boxed{K_H=\{\delta\in Z_1(\mathcal D_H;\mathbb Z):
                         \chi_H\delta=0\}.}                    \tag{0.3}
\]

Equivalently, \(K_H\) is the lattice of zero-voltage closed walks in the
abelian cover of \(\mathcal D_H\) whose voltage records the complete root
and literal signed trace ledger.  This is the exact algebraic
characterization; it includes arbitrarily close-packed changes and has no
independent-collar hypothesis.

The kernel is not small.  Over \(\mathbb Q\),

\[
 \dim K_H\ge
 W\big((m)_H^2-(m)_{H-1}^2-1-2H\big)+1,                       \tag{0.4}
\]

so in the regime \(H=o(m)\) it occupies a \(1-o(1)\) fraction of the
entire safe-column space.  More importantly, it is not merely a gauge
kernel.  The certified two-rail \(H\)-lift of the octahedral trade is an
element \(\delta_{\rm oct}\in K_H\) with nonzero immediate-edge
derivative.  Pairwise edge-support-disjoint relabelings of this atom give
an integer class \(\Delta\in K_H\) with

\[
             |\operatorname{supp}P\Delta|=\Theta(W),            \tag{0.5}
\]

where \(P\) projects an \(H\)-history to its first oriented Johnson edge.
Thus an extensive signed non-gauge class exists.

This does **not** provide the desired factor trade.  The negative part of
\(\Delta\) need not lie in one coefficient-one factor, and the relabeled
collars can overlap heavily.  Those are positivity and packing conditions,
not linear trace conditions.

There is a second exact limitation on what can be inferred from (0.1):
joining rank is not a function of a kernel vector.  For the same local
octahedral replacement, if \(\tau\in S_3\) is the exterior port
connectivity and \(\pi\) is the central 3-cycle, then

\[
              r_{\rm join}=c(\tau)-c(\tau\pi).                 \tag{0.6}
\]

The right side can be \(+2,0,-2\) for the same local signed edge vector.
Consequently a universal assertion that an element of \(K_H\) has small
or large joining rank is not well posed until a compatible base factor
and its exterior monodromy are included.

The remaining coefficient-one gate is therefore sharper than a kernel
calculation:

> find \((z,\delta)\) with \(z\) a coefficient-one safe factor,
> \(\delta\in K_H\), \(\delta^-\le z\), and exterior monodromy giving
> total joining rank \(\Theta(W/\sqrt m)\).

Nothing in the large dimension of \(K_H\) supplies the sign compatibility
or the monodromy.

## 1. The colored de Bruijn graph

For a safe column \(\gamma=(X_0,\ldots,X_H)\), put

\[
 L_q(\gamma)=\bigcap_{i=0}^qX_i,
 \qquad
 U_q(\gamma)=\bigcup_{i=0}^qX_i.                               \tag{1.1}
\]

The maps in (0.2) attach to \(\gamma\) the formal color

\[
 \chi_H(\gamma)=e_{X_0}\oplus
       \bigoplus_{q=1}^H(e_{L_q(\gamma)}\oplus e_{U_q(\gamma)}).\tag{1.2}
\]

Here every target at every depth is a distinct basis vector.  The
de Bruijn boundary is

\[
 \partial\gamma=\operatorname{suf}\gamma-\operatorname{pre}\gamma,
\]

up to the harmless sign convention used for \(M\).

### Theorem 1.1 (zero-voltage characterization)

The simultaneous literal trace kernel is exactly (0.3).  Equivalently,
form the bidirected graph obtained from \(\mathcal D_H\) by adjoining a
formal reverse \(\bar e\) to every edge \(e\), with

\[
              \chi_H(\bar e)=-\chi_H(e).                        \tag{1.3}
\]

Then \(K_H\) is generated by projections of closed walks having total
voltage zero.

#### Proof

The equation \(M\delta=0\) is precisely \(\partial\delta=0\), and the
other equations in (0.3) are precisely \(\chi_H\delta=0\).  This proves
the first assertion once the redundancy of \(D\), proved in Lemma 1.2
below, is used.

For the closed-walk form, replace every negative occurrence of an edge in
an integer circulation by a positive occurrence of its formal reverse.
The result is a nonnegative circulation in the bidirected graph and hence
decomposes into directed closed walks.  Their aggregate voltage is zero.
If several closed walks have cancelling nonzero voltages, join them by a
path and its formal reverse.  The connector has zero projected chain and
zero net voltage.  Repeating combines the collection into zero-voltage
closed walks without changing its projection.  The reverse construction
is immediate. \(\square\)

This is also the natural abelian-cover formulation.  Lift a memory state
\(v\) to pairs \((v,g)\), where \(g\) lies in the free abelian group on
the colors, and lift \(e:v\to w\) to

\[
                    (v,g)\longrightarrow(w,g+\chi_H(e)).       \tag{1.4}
\]

Closed lifted walks project exactly to the zero-voltage kernel.

### Lemma 1.2 (the deletion rows are redundant)

Let \(B_r\) be point incidence on rank-\(r\) targets.  Columnwise,

\[
       B_{m-1}S_1^- = B_mA-D.                                  \tag{1.5}
\]

Consequently

\[
      A\delta=S_1^-\delta=0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad D\delta=0.\tag{1.6}
\]

#### Proof

If the first transition deletes \(a\), then
\(L_1(\gamma)=X_0\setminus\{a\}\).  Its point indicator is the point
indicator of \(X_0\) minus \(e_a\), which is exactly (1.5). \(\square\)

Thus \(D\) remains a useful semantic row but adds no equation to the
simultaneous literal kernel.

### Corollary 1.3 (dual/cocycle form)

Over \(\mathbb Q\), a linear functional on safe columns annihilates
\(K_H\) if and only if it is a sum of a memory-state coboundary and
potentials depending only on the root and the individual literal trace
targets.

#### Proof

For a finite matrix \(T\), \((\ker T)^\perp=\operatorname{row}T\).  Apply
this to

\[
                    T= M\oplus\chi_H.                           \tag{1.7}
\]

The rows of \(M\) are memory-state coboundaries, and the rows of
\(\chi_H\) are exactly the stated color potentials. \(\square\)

This is the global version of the local grammar coboundary criterion.  It
also shows what a claimed new linear invariant must look like: unless it
is already a state potential or a combination of the recorded target
ledgers, it cannot vanish on all of \(K_H\).

## 2. The kernel has enormous dimension

The safe de Bruijn graph has

\[
 |E(\mathcal D_H)|=W(m)_H^2,
 \qquad
 |V(\mathcal D_H)|=W(m)_{H-1}^2.                               \tag{2.1}
\]

For \(3H\le m+2\) it is strongly connected, so

\[
                       \operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb Q}M
                         =|V|-1.                               \tag{2.2}
\]

There are \(W\) root rows and

\[
 2\sum_{q=1}^H\binom{2m}{m-q}\le2HW                            \tag{2.3}
\]

literal target rows.

### Theorem 2.1 (dimension lower bound)

For \(3H\le m+2\), (0.4) holds.

#### Proof

The codimension of the simultaneous kernel is at most the sum of the
ranks, hence at most

\[
       W(m)_{H-1}^2-1+W+2HW.                                   \tag{2.4}
\]

Subtract (2.4) from \(|E|=W(m)_H^2\). \(\square\)

For \(H=o(m)\), the quotient of the right side of (0.4) by
\(W(m)_H^2\) tends to one.  Therefore the hoped-for universal statement
"every non-gauge homology class is detected by a protected literal
trace" is false already over \(\mathbb Q\): almost all circulation
directions are invisible to these marginals.

This dimension statement alone says nothing about coefficient-one
feasibility.  A generic vector of the lattice has a negative part spread
over far more than the \(W\) columns selected by one factor.

### Proposition 2.2 (representation-theoretic location of invisible modes)

Over \(\mathbb Q\), let \((\ker M)_\lambda\) denote the
\(S_{2m}\)-isotypic component of the circulation space belonging to a
partition \(\lambda\vdash2m\).  If \(\lambda\) has at least three rows,
then

\[
                         (\ker M)_\lambda\subseteq K_H.         \tag{2.5}
\]

#### Proof

Every map \(A,S_q^-,S_q^+\) is \(S_{2m}\)-equivariant, and its codomain is
a permutation module on one subset rank.  Young's rule gives

\[
 \mathbb Q\!\left[\binom{[2m]}r\right]
   \cong\bigoplus_{j=0}^{\min(r,2m-r)}S^{(2m-j,j)}.             \tag{2.6}
\]

Thus only two-row irreducibles occur in every target codomain.  By
Schur's lemma, each target map vanishes on a source isotypic component
with at least three rows.  Intersecting with \(\ker M\) proves (2.5).
\(\square\)

Two-row circulation modes can also lie in \(K_H\); Proposition 2.2 is a
large automatic subspace, not an equality.  Its role is diagnostic: all
literal subset traces, at however many depths, are representation-blind
to every higher-row circulation mode.

## 3. Immediate-edge projection and the inert subkernel

Define

\[
 P:\mathbb Z^{\Gamma_H}\longrightarrow
       \mathbb Z^{\vec E(J(2m,m))}                              \tag{3.1}
\]

by sending a history to its first oriented Johnson edge.  (Using the last
edge gives the same aggregate on \(\ker M\).)

### Lemma 3.1 (zero edge derivative is inert on factors)

Let \(z,z'\) be coefficient-one integral safe factor circulations.  If

\[
                         P(z'-z)=0,                              \tag{3.2}
\]

then the two oriented successor maps, and hence the two factors, are
identical.  In particular their joining rank is zero.

#### Proof

Each owner has exactly one selected outgoing edge in either factor.
Equation (3.2) says the oriented edge multisets agree, so those unique
successors agree owner by owner.  The entire length-\(H\) column rooted at
an owner is then forced by iterating the common successor map. \(\square\)

It is therefore natural to call \(K_H\cap\ker P\) the **inert** or
immediate-edge gauge subkernel.  The next result shows that it is a proper
subkernel.

### Theorem 3.2 (a non-gauge kernel atom)

For \(H=o(m)\), the certified two-type-rail lift of the octahedral
three-edge trade gives

\[
             \delta_{\rm oct}\in K_H,
             \qquad P\delta_{\rm oct}\ne0.                     \tag{3.3}
\]

Its immediate-edge derivative consists of the three deleted and three
inserted central edges.

#### Proof

The central octahedral resolutions have identical lower and upper
depth-one flag multisets.  Give the three ports on either shore identical
rails, using two disjoint coordinate registries for the two shores.  A
q-window crossing the central switch then has lower target

\[
 (C\setminus(Z_0(r)\cup Z_1(s)))+\{x\}
\]

and upper target

\[
 C\cup Q\cup E_0(r)\cup E_1(s),
\]

where \(r+s=q-1\), and where \(C+x\) and \(C\cup Q\) are the corresponding
central lower and upper flags.  Their multisets agree for both
resolutions.  The root, memory, and run ledgers also agree, so the
difference lies in \(K_H\).  The central matching really changes, giving
the second assertion. \(\square\)

### Lemma 3.3 (a matching in a transitive uniform hypergraph)

Let \(\mathcal H\) be a vertex-transitive \(k\)-uniform multihypergraph
on \(v\) vertices.  Then it has a matching of size at least \(v/k^2\).

#### Proof

Every vertex has the same degree \(d\), so the total number of hyperedges
is \(dv/k\), counted with multiplicity.  If a maximal matching has \(s\)
members, its union has \(ks\) vertices, and every hyperedge meets this
union.  At most \(ksd\) hyperedges meet it when incidences are counted.
Thus \(dv/k\le ksd\), giving \(s\ge v/k^2\). \(\square\)

### Theorem 3.4 (extensive signed non-gauge classes)

For \(H=o(m)\), there is an integer \(\Delta\in K_H\) such that

\[
                    |\operatorname{supp}P\Delta|\ge W.         \tag{3.4}
\]

In particular \(K_H\) contains extensive non-gauge classes.

#### Proof

Let \(S=\operatorname{supp}P\delta_{\rm oct}\), so \(|S|=6\).  Let
\(S_{2m}\) act on the oriented Johnson edges and consider the orbit
hypergraph whose hyperedges are the sets \(gS\).  The action is transitive
on oriented Johnson edges, so this is a vertex-transitive 6-uniform
multihypergraph.  There are

\[
                    |\vec E(J(2m,m))|=Wm^2                       \tag{3.5}
\]

vertices.  Lemma 3.3 gives at least \(Wm^2/36\) pairwise disjoint orbit
supports.  Choose \(\lceil W/6\rceil\) of them and corresponding
permutations \(g_i\), and put

\[
                    \Delta=\sum_i g_i\delta_{\rm oct}.          \tag{3.6}
\]

The defining maps of \(K_H\) are equivariant, hence \(\Delta\in K_H\).
The supports of the vectors \(P(g_i\delta_{\rm oct})\) are disjoint, so
no immediate-edge cancellation occurs and (3.4) follows. \(\square\)

Theorem 3.4 is deliberately a lattice statement.  It does not assert that
all negative columns in (3.6) occur in one factor.  Nor are the full
\(H\)-collars of the relabeled atoms disjoint.  It therefore refutes a
linear injectivity no-go, but it does not solve the positive integral
packing problem.

## 4. Joining rank requires exterior monodromy

For the octahedral atom orient the three deleted central edges as
\(u_i\to v_i\).  The new matching is

\[
                         u_i\longrightarrow v_{\pi(i)},         \tag{4.1}
\]

where \(\pi\) is a 3-cycle.  Delete the old central edges from the ambient
factor.  Following the exterior paths defines a permutation \(\tau\) by

\[
                         v_i\leadsto u_{\tau(i)}.                \tag{4.2}
\]

### Theorem 4.1 (exact rank formula)

The old central completion has \(c(\tau)\) components and the new one has
\(c(\tau\pi)\).  Hence (0.6) holds.

#### Proof

Traversing an old central edge followed by its exterior path acts on the
port index by \(\tau\).  Traversing a new central edge first applies
\(\pi\), and the exterior path then applies \(\tau\), giving the product
\(\tau\pi\) up to the immaterial convention of composing on the other
side.  Components are the cycles of the corresponding permutation.
\(\square\)

For \(\tau=1,\pi,\pi^{-1}\), the difference in (0.6) is respectively

\[
                            +2,\quad0,\quad-2.                   \tag{4.3}
\]

Thus the same local kernel vector can merge three exterior components,
preserve their number, or split one into three.  The external completion,
not the trace vector, decides which occurs.

The correct enriched object is consequently not \(\delta\in K_H\) alone,
but a feasible pair

\[
                   (z,\delta),\qquad
       z\in\{0,1\}^{\Gamma_H},\quad \delta^-\le z,             \tag{4.4}
\]

together with the port monodromy induced by \(z\) outside
\(\operatorname{supp}P\delta\).  Cycle count is nonlinear in this data.

## 5. Exact remaining theorem

The simultaneous literal-trace kernel has now been characterized and is
known to contain extensive non-gauge signed classes.  Therefore neither
of the following can close the coefficient-one problem:

* a dimension or representation-theoretic proof that \(K_H\) is small;
* an injectivity theorem for protected meet/join labels on the full
  circulation space.

What remains is the following positivity--topology statement.

> **Feasible extensive kernel-routing theorem (open).**  For
> \(H=\sqrt m\,\omega(m)=o(m)\), construct a coefficient-one integral
> \(H\)-safe factor circulation \(z\) and an integer \(\delta\in K_H\)
> such that
> \[
>    \delta^-\le z,
> \]
> \(z+\delta\) is coefficient-one, and the exterior monodromies of the
> changed port systems give joining rank
> \[
>    \left({2\over\sqrt\pi}+o(1)\right){W\over\sqrt m}.
> \]

The signed lattice supplies abundant formal directions.  The only
unresolved content is placing enough negative support inside one exact
factor while arranging favorable, simultaneously compatible exterior
monodromy.  This is precisely the dense cross-packet routing gate.
