# Every distance-two D5 reset triple completes to a unique Johnson square

**Date:** 2026-08-14
**Status:** unconditional local Johnson-square lemma plus exact finite phase
obstruction.  It replaces arbitrary geometry by a canonical commuting
completion, but does not by itself give an alternating incidence-factor
trade or a fixed resource bank.

## 0. Outcome

Let `T,B,C` be rank-`R` owners with

\[
                  d_J(T,B)=d_J(T,C)=1,qquad d_J(B,C)=2. \tag{0.1}
\]

Then there are a rank-`(R-2)` core `K` and four distinct active labels
`x,u,y,v` such that

\[
 T=K+x+u,qquad B=K+u+y,qquad C=K+x+v.             \tag{0.2}
\]

The fourth owner

\[
                              D=K+y+v               \tag{0.3}
\]

is the unique vertex completing `T-B-D-C-T` using the two commuting exchange
directions `x<->y` and `u<->v`.  (The two other common neighbours give
noncommuting adjacent exchanges sharing an active label.)  The old owner
matching

\[
                             TB,DC                   \tag{0.4}
\]

and the new owner matching

\[
                             BD,CT                   \tag{0.5}
\]

have the same four owners.  The four Johnson edges
form a resource-simple square; after subdivision they form an incidence
8-cycle.  However, `(0.4)--(0.5)` are two matchings of **Johnson edges**,
not the two alternating incidence-edge matchings of that 8-cycle.  An
exterior closure is still required to realize the switch in a degree-two
incidence factor.  The owner 2-switch changes `T`'s neighbour from `B` to
`C` while changing the collateral neighbour of `D` from `C` to `B`.

Thus the 164 D5 reset rows whose terminal signature is `(1,1,2)` already
carry a canonical prescribed-terminal Johnson square.  Their sharp remaining
problem is to balance the collateral `DC -> DB` action, supply an incidence
closure, and then collar/coinstantiate the resulting bank.

## 1. Normal form and uniqueness

### Theorem 1.1

Every triple satisfying `(0.1)` has the normal form `(0.2)`, with all four
active labels distinct.  The owner `(0.3)` is the unique common Johnson
neighbour of `B,C` distinct from `T` whose square is generated by the two
commuting disjoint exchanges `x<->y` and `u<->v`.  The other two common
neighbours give noncommuting adjacent exchanges sharing an active label.

#### Proof

Since `T,B` are adjacent, write

\[
                         B=T-x+y                    \tag{1.1}
\]

with `x in T` and `y notin T`.  Likewise write

\[
                         C=T-u+v.                   \tag{1.2}
\]

If `x=u`, then `B,C` differ only by `y<->v` and are adjacent.  If `y=v`,
they differ only by `x<->u` and are adjacent.  Condition `(0.1)` therefore
forces `x,u,y,v` pairwise distinct.  Put `K=T-{x,u}`.  This gives `(0.2)`.

Now `D=K+y+v` differs from `B` by `u<->v` and from `C` by `x<->y`, so it
completes the square.  Conversely, a completion using the same two exchange
directions must contain both inserted labels `y,v` and neither deleted
label `x,u`, hence equals `D`. \(\square\)

## 2. Exact local palettes and the q2 boundary

The four immediate-lower colours around the square are

\[
 K+u,\quad K+y,\quad K+v,\quad K+x,                \tag{2.1}
\]

all distinct.  Its immediate-upper colours are

\[
 K+x+u+y,\quad K+u+y+v,\quad K+x+y+v,\quad K+x+u+v.\tag{2.2}
\]

also distinct.  Hence the four-edge square is resource-simple internally.
The old matching `(0.4)` and new matching `(0.5)` nevertheless use different
two-element lower and upper subpalettes; this is not a fixed-bank trade.

The four lower colours in `(2.1)` are the subdivision vertices on the
incidence 8-cycle.  Selecting the two opposite Johnson edges in `(0.4)`
selects two disjoint length-two arcs, not an alternating incidence matching.
Consequently lower- and upper-q2 turns depend on an exterior incidence
closure and its four predecessor/successor incidences.  No zero-current or
residence claim follows from the square alone.

The cyclic owner display does show the intrinsic collar issue: every active
coordinate has trace `1100` around `T,B,D,C`, with positive and zero runs
two.  This is already one owner below the D5 lower-owner depth-two target
`(3,3)`, and an external splice can shorten it further.  A D5 substitution
therefore needs a resident dilation plus the full four-terminal
collar/current audit, not merely the internal palette calculation.

## 3. The 48 adjacent-head rows

When `d_J(B,C)=1`, the three owners `T,B,C` form a top-type Johnson
triangle over their rank-`(R-1)` common facet.  They constitute the canonical
triangle owner geometry for a marked C6 quotient candidate.  Subdividing
the triangle does not make the full suppressed edge `TB` one alternating
incidence matching, so exterior factor phases are still required; no
isolated triangle trade follows from geometry alone.

Consequently the exact D5 local atlas has no arbitrary terminal distances:

\[
\boxed{
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
\text{rows}&d_J(B,C)&\text{canonical owner circuit}\\ \hline
48&1&\text{Johnson triangle / incidence C6}\\
164&2&\text{Johnson square / incidence C8}.
\end{array}}
\tag{3.1}
\]

The remaining global problem is to close, collar, and coinstantiate these
212 canonical owner geometries while preserving the complete D5 factor
action.

## 4. Frozen D5 phase obstruction

A distance-two square completion is a suppressed-owner factor trade only if
the old collateral Johnson edge `DC` is present before the switch and the
new collateral edge `DB` is present after it.  The exact H100 replay gives

\[
\begin{array}{c|c|r}
DC\text{ old}&DB\text{ new}&\text{distance-two rows}\\ \hline
0&0&139\\
0&1&12\\
1&0&13.
\end{array}                                        \tag{4.1}
\]

In particular, no distance-two square has both collateral phases, and no
adjacent-head triangle has its third old edge `BC` (all 48 are absent).
None of the 212 canonical owner geometries can therefore replace its D5 row
in isolation.

The distance-two commuting corners are sparse but not disjoint.  Their 164
occurrences use 163 distinct owners (162 once and one twice); 20 occurrences
coincide with a prescribed terminal owner and 50 lie in the touched
12,420-owner D5 bank.  Across all four edges of all 164 squares, the
immediate-lower palette has 656 occurrences on 629 values
(`602 x 1, 27 x 2`), while the immediate-upper palette has 656 occurrences
on 572 values (`490 x 1, 80 x 2, 2 x 3`).  Thus the obstruction is not a
large-multiplicity pile-up, but neither the auxiliary owners nor the square
palettes form a globally resource-injective bank.

The former aggregate count `209` mixed the 164 distance-two fourth corners
with the already prescribed third owners in the 48 triangle rows and is not
an auxiliary-owner statistic; it is deliberately not used here.

This leaves two exact routes:

1. choose a global bank whose collateral signed edges telescope to zero;
2. attach a marked-socket collar which absorbs each collateral action
   internally.
