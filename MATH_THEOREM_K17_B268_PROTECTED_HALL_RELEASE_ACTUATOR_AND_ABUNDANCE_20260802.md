## 1. Table states, supplier presentations, and socket dependencies

Fix one literal parent.  Let `F subseteq J+` be a row-disjoint transfer
matching and `T(F)` its materialized table.  Every transfer moves the same
bottom label from an `LMR` donor to an `LLR` host, so the hard-head census

\[
                              K=16898                         \tag{1.1}
\]

is invariant on this face.  Let

\[
                  G_T=(H_T,U;E_T)                            \tag{1.2}
\]

be the complete mode-labelled `6/9/4` supplier graph.  Put

\[
 r(T)=\nu(G_T),\qquad \delta(T)=K-r(T).                    \tag{1.3}
\]

A **literal protected socket basis** `Pi` means a fixed selected collection
of ticket incidences and occurrence witnesses satisfying the declared
socket-basis matching/capacity equations in one state-expanded parent.
Its dependency closure `D(Pi)` contains every physical row, owner/root
value, flag, predecessor/successor history, occurrence resource, and state
literal on which any selected record depends.

The distinction is essential:

* a changed row outside the `7,213`-row ticket union preserves those row
  bytes;
* an edge of `J+` has one positive marginal witness in each phase; but
* `Pi` survives only if all its dependency literals survive, or if an exact
  replacement matching for its lost witnesses is supplied.

Thus marginal common positivity and protected-row avoidance are necessary
local filters, not by themselves a socket-basis theorem.

## 2. Protected one-swap Hall-release lemma

Choose a selected transfer `f in F` and an unselected transfer `e in J+`.
Put

\[
              F'=F-\{f\}+\{e\},\qquad T'=T(F').             \tag{2.1}
\]

Assume the following conditions.

1. **Parent and table feasibility.**  The edge `e` is disjoint from
   `F-{f}`, its endpoints are disjoint from those of `f`, every row in their
   endpoint union avoids the protected set, every record has the same literal
   parent, and `T'` has the exact target/root/owner partition.

2. **Socket-basis protection.**  Either every dependency literal in `D(Pi)`
   has the same value in `T` and `T'`, or an exact alternating repair in the
   final literal socket presentation supplies a replacement basis `Pi'` as
   in Section 3.  The literal support separation

   \[
       \operatorname{supp}(T\mathbin\triangle T')
                   \cap D(\Pi)=\varnothing                 \tag{2.2}
   \]

   is a convenient sufficient condition for the first alternative when
   `D(Pi)` is complete.  In that case put `Pi'=Pi`.

3. **Carrier matching.**  The final graph \(G_{T'}\) contains a matching
   `M_0` of size `r(T)`.  This condition includes any neutral rerouting
   required because the swap changes four source menus and mode-labelled
   heads.

4. **Released augmenting path.**  There is an `M_0`-exposed active head `z`,
   an incidence `z u_0` created by undoing `f`, and an `M_0`-alternating path

   \[
       z,u_0,h_1,u_1,\ldots,h_t,u_t                         \tag{2.3}
   \]

   in \(G_{T'}\), ending at an `M_0`-exposed supplier `u_t`.  Every edge of
   the carrier and path survives the addition of `e`.  When `t=0`, the
   newly adjacent supplier `u_0` is itself exposed.

5. **One-unit Hall cap.**  The final graph contains a Hall shore `Q'` with

   \[
        |Q'|-|N_{T'}(Q')|=\delta(T)-1.                      \tag{2.4}
   \]

### Theorem 2.1 (exact protected Hall release)

Under conditions 1--5,

\[
 \boxed{
      r(T')=r(T)+1,\qquad \delta(T')=\delta(T)-1.}          \tag{2.5}
\]

and `Pi'` is a literal protected socket basis for the final table.  Under
dependency separation (2.2), `Pi'=Pi` and the witness collection is
unchanged.

#### Proof

Toggle the alternating path (2.3) relative to `M_0`.  It begins and ends at
exposed vertices, so the result is a supplier matching of size `r(T)+1` in
\(G_{T'}\).  Hence `r(T')>=r(T)+1`.

By Hall's deficiency theorem, (2.4) gives

\[
 r(T')\le K-(\delta(T)-1)=r(T)+1.
\]

This proves (2.5).  Under the first branch of condition 2, every literal
selected record and capacity used by `Pi` stays true.  Under the second
branch, Lemma 3.1 supplies the declared replacement `Pi'`.
\(\square\)

The carrier condition cannot be dropped: row-disjoint table changes can
delete matched supplier incidences far from the released head.  The Hall cap
also cannot be dropped if the conclusion is **exactly** `+1`; conditions
1--4 alone prove only `r(T')>=r(T)+1`.

### Exact certificate and Benders form

Irrespective of how the exchange was found, exact gain one is certified by
two objects:

1. a final supplier matching of size `r(T)+1`; and
2. a final Hall shore of excess `delta(T)-1`, or equivalently a vertex cover
   of size `r(T)+1`.

One maximum matching/min-cut returns both.  Let `Q` be a fixed set of
mode-labelled potential occurrences, let `d_a(T)` be head activity, and let
`N_T(Q)` count only suppliers adjacent to active occurrences.  Put

\[
                 h_T(Q)=\sum_{a\in Q}d_a(T)-|N_T(Q)|.
\]

If `Q` is an old maximum shore with `h_T(Q)=delta(T)`, define its exact
signed release

\[
 \Gamma_Q(T,T')=
 \sum_{a\in Q}\bigl(d_a(T)-d_a(T')\bigr)
 +|N_{T'}(Q)-N_T(Q)|-|N_T(Q)-N_{T'}(Q)|.                  \tag{2.6}
\]

Then `Gamma_Q(T,T')=1` gives \(h_{T'}(Q)=\delta(T)-1\), so `Q`
itself is a valid cap (2.4).  Gains must count distinct supplier identities
net of lost last neighbours; record counts are not a substitute.

## 3. Socket preservation and fundamental exchanges

The dependency-separation condition (2.2) is deliberately strong.  There
is a more flexible exact version after fixing one literal occurrence state.
Let

\[
       \mathcal S_T=(\mathcal R,\mathcal O;A_T)              \tag{3.1}
\]

be the bipartite presentation between protected socket obligations
`mathcal R` and occurrence resources `mathcal O`.  A socket basis is a
matching saturating `mathcal R`.

### Lemma 3.1 (socket-basis repair)

Suppose every retained edge of the old socket matching survives in
\(\mathcal S_{T'}\) except for a declared set of exposed obligations.  If those
obligations admit vertex-disjoint alternating paths to exposed resources in
`mathcal S_(T')`, toggling the paths gives a new protected socket basis.

Transpose the fixed presentation and let `B` be a basis of its transversal
matroid on occurrence resources.  For one entering resource `x`, the
ordinary fundamental-circuit form is

\[
       B-b+x\text{ is a base}
       \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
       b\in C_{M_{\rm sock}}(x,B).                          \tag{3.2}
\]

#### Proof

This is the standard augmenting-path theorem for bipartite matching; (3.2)
is its transversal-matroid base-exchange formulation.  \(\square\)

Lemma 3.1 applies only after the literal occurrence resources and their
capacities have been fixed.  If one socket witness simultaneously consumes
several shared histories/cells, the unexpanded object is a colored
hypermatching, not a transversal matroid.  It must be state-expanded or
handled by branch--Benders; no generic socket-matroid claim is made.

## 5. Orthogonal release banks and a min-cut oracle

For an actuator `a`, let its dependency footprint `Phi(a)` include

* its removed and added transfer rows;
* every carrier-matching and augmenting-path vertex/edge;
* its released head and exposed terminal supplier;
* every socket-basis dependency or socket-repair path; and
* every row mode whose change can delete one of those certificate edges.

A family `A` is a **closed orthogonal release bank** relative to one fixed
maximum matching `M` of `G_T` if every actuator `a` has a signed
`M`-alternating system `P_a`.  The system may contain neutral carrier-repair
components but has exactly one augmenting component.  Require

1. the transfer exchanges are jointly feasible;
2. the vertex supports of the `P_a` are pairwise disjoint;
3. the socket dependencies commute; and
4. for every subfamily `S subseteq A`,

   \[
     M_S=M\mathbin\triangle
          \bigtriangleup_{a\in S}P_a
     \quad\text{is a subgraph matching of }G_{T_S}.         \tag{5.0}
   \]

Condition (5.0) is stronger than having a different carrier matching for
each individually valid actuator.  Individual `+1` certificates with
incompatible carriers need not compose.

### Theorem 5.1 (simultaneous release bank)

For every `S subseteq A`, toggling its signed alternating systems gives

\[
                         \nu(G_{T_S})\ge\nu(G_T)+|S|.        \tag{5.1}
\]

If \(G_{T_S}\) also has a Hall shore of excess `delta(T)-|S|`, equality holds.
Every protected socket basis survives under the bank's socket condition.

#### Proof

The vertex-disjoint signed systems can be toggled simultaneously relative to
the common matching `M`.  Neutral components preserve cardinality and the
unique augmenting component of each system contributes one.  Condition
(5.0) places the resulting matching in the jointly materialized graph.  Hall
supplies the matching upper bound when the stated shore exists.  \(\square\)

The all-subfamily condition is load-bearing: pairwise disjoint transfer
rows alone do not stop one changed supplier mode from deleting another
path's incidence.

### Conflict-graph abundance corollary

Join two individually certified actuators when their dependency footprints
conflict.  Assume the conflict relation is complete in the strong sense that
**every independent set is a closed orthogonal bank** satisfying (5.0); mere
pairwise row-disjointness is insufficient.  If this conflict graph has
maximum degree `Delta` and the raw bank satisfies

\[
             |\mathcal A_{\rm raw}|
                \ge(\Delta+1)(\delta(T)-c),                 \tag{5.2}
\]

then greedy independent-set extraction yields `delta(T)-c` mutually
compatible releases and leaves supplier deficiency at most `c`.

For a fixed carrier matching and inert fillers, maximum packing of release
paths is itself a flow/min-cut problem.  Orient the alternating graph toward
exposed suppliers, split vertices to capacity one, connect released heads to
their new suppliers, and connect exposed suppliers to the sink.  Menger's
theorem equates the maximum number of simultaneous releases with the minimum
vertex cut.  Selecting state-dependent fillers and socket histories is an
additional colored matching/hypermatching layer, not generally one TU flow.

## 6. Conditional induction to `B(k)+O(1)`

Let `B(k)` be the monotone-deadline lower bound, and let \(\nu_{\rm word}(k)\)
denote the minimum universal-word length.  For every `k`, suppose there is a
nonempty class \(\mathscr T_k\) of literal table states satisfying all of the
following with constants independent of `k`.

1. Every state has a physical scaffold of length at most `B(k)+C_0`, one
   literal common-state protected socket basis, and all non-supplier terminal
   gates already closed.
2. The class is closed under length-neutral protected Hall-release swaps.
3. There is a constant `c` such that every state \(T\in\mathscr T_k\) with
   `delta(T)>c` has a Theorem 2.1 successor \(T'\in\mathscr T_k\) with

   \[
                         \delta(T')=\delta(T)-1.             \tag{6.1}
   \]

4. Every state with residual deficiency at most `c` admits one **joint**
   physical completion of all residual supplier defects at total cost at
   most `C_1 delta(T)`, without reopening the protected gates.
5. A state with supplier deficiency zero and the protected terminal gates
   closed compiles to a universal word on its stated physical length.

### Theorem 6.1 (uniform release induction)

Under hypotheses 1--4,

\[
 \boxed{
 \nu_{\rm word}(k)
    \le B(k)+C_0+cC_1=B(k)+O(1).}                           \tag{6.2}
\]

#### Proof

Supplier deficiency is a nonnegative integer.  Repeatedly apply (6.1) until
it is at most `c`; at most `delta(T)-c` length-neutral swaps are used.  Class
closure preserves every other terminal gate and the scaffold length.  Apply
the joint residual completion at cost at most `c C_1`, then use the compiler
hypothesis.  All constants are uniform in `k`, giving (6.2).  \(\square\)

The orthogonal-bank condition (5.2) is a static alternative to repeated step
3: one bank of size `delta(T)-c` reaches deficiency at most `c` directly.
Without singleton Hall caps it need not realize every exact intermediate
deficiency.  A static nonrecycling bank needs at least `delta(T)-c` distinct
usable selected undo edges.  That stock bound is not necessary for a fully
adaptive process which can later undo fillers or regenerate table modes.

The new `def85 -> 84` successor shows that one release can preserve strict
socket Pareto order when its compensator is chosen jointly.  The sibling
floor-budget `84 -> 83` branch shows why the more general induction class may
still be defined by an invariant socket basis and declared floors rather
than coordinatewise monotonicity of every count.  Neither proves Theorem
6.1's all-`k` premises.  In particular, finite K17 supplier abundance does
not close literal common state/history, residual outer matching and long-arc
chronology, transported-phase carrier opening, residence, upper shadows, or
the terminal compiler.
