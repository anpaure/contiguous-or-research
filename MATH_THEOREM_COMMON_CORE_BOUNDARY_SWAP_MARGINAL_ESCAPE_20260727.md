# Common-core boundary swaps escape the marginal shield

Date: 2026-07-27

Method: pure mathematics only.

## 0. Outcome

Use the calibrated common-core notation

\[
 n=2m,\qquad M=m+H,\qquad
 N=\binom{2m}{M},\qquad W=\binom{2m}{m},
\]

\[
 d=m-3H+1,\qquad \kappa=M-d=4H-1,
\qquad T=dN=W-o(W).
\tag{0.1}
\]

Two facts sharpen the coordinate-marginal flatness theorem.

1.  The marginal neutrality of the previously audited six-frame library
    is not an invariant of the legal common-core coefficient support.
    Replacing one path at one top by a boundary-adjacent swap changes
    exactly one middle owner.  Its middle derivative is

    \[
                              e_Y-e_X
    \tag{0.2}
    \]

    for Johnson-adjacent \(m\)-sets \(X,Y\).  This is support-minimal,
    preserves exactly one configuration at every top, and has nonzero
    coordinate marginals.

2.  In the full all-core catalogue, every oriented Johnson edge occurs in
    this way.  Hence the lattice generated by legal one-root support
    differences is the complete zero-total lattice

    \[
      \left\{z\in\mathbb Z^{\binom{[2m]}m}:\sum_Xz_X=0\right\}.
    \tag{0.3}
    \]

    There is no nontrivial linear invariant of middle loads beyond total
    mass once all legal root replacements are allowed.

There is also a stronger quantitative flatness statement.  If

\[
 b_i=\sum_{X\ni i}L_X,\qquad
 \delta_i=b_i-\frac T2,
\tag{0.4}
\]

then every legal coefficient satisfies

\[
 \boxed{
 \|\delta\|_1\le2H\kappa N,\qquad
 \|\delta\|_\infty\le2HN,\qquad
 \|\delta\|_2^2\le4H^2\kappa N^2.}
\tag{0.5}
\]

Consequently

\[
 \boxed{
 \sum_{i=1}^{2m}\left(\frac{b_i}{T}-\frac12\right)^2
 =O\!\left(\frac{H^3}{m^2}\right)=o(1).}
\tag{0.6}
\]

Thus a new shield cannot be obtained merely by spreading the old
\(W/4\) marginal bias over many coordinates: the entire normalized
degree-one Fourier mass of a legal coefficient tends to zero.

These results do not prove \(K^*=o(W)\) or \(\min\Psi=o(W)\).  The
boundary swap is available only at a top currently in the appropriate
path state, and its other-rank traces also move.  What they prove is that
the fixed-marginal obstruction has been fully escaped at the support
level; the remaining issue is global routing and sign of these literal
unit transfers.

## 1. Tight-path boundary normal form

Fix a top \(U\).  As in the all-core path normal form, a middle path is
specified by an injective word

\[
                  w=(w_1,\ldots,w_{d+H-1})
\tag{1.1}
\]

and its deleted \(H\)-windows

\[
 J_j(w)=\{w_j,w_{j+1},\ldots,w_{j+H-1}\},
 \qquad 1\le j\le d.
\tag{1.2}
\]

The middle owners are

\[
                             X_j(w)=U\setminus J_j(w).
\tag{1.3}
\]

Every injective word in (1.1) is a literal all-core path option.  For a
fixed core \(Q_U\), the same assertion holds whenever all displayed
letters lie in the available tail \(U\setminus Q_U\).

Let \(w'\) be obtained by interchanging \(w_1\) and \(w_2\).  Then

\[
 J_1(w')=J_1(w),
\tag{1.4}
\]

because the first window contains both letters.  For \(j\ge3\), neither
position \(1\) nor position \(2\) belongs to \(J_j\), so

\[
 J_j(w')=J_j(w)\qquad(j\ge3).
\tag{1.5}
\]

Only the second window changes:

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 J_2(w)&=\{w_2,w_3,\ldots,w_{H+1}\},\\
 J_2(w')&=\{w_1,w_3,\ldots,w_{H+1}\}.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{1.6}
\]

This elementary boundary asymmetry is absent for an interior adjacent
swap, where two windows change and form a marginal-neutral rectangle.

## 2. The support-minimal marginal-changing primitive

### Theorem 2.1 (one-root unit transfer)

The two paths \(w,w'\) above have middle-support difference

\[
 {\bf1}_{B(w')}-{\bf1}_{B(w)}
 =e_{X_2(w')}-e_{X_2(w)}.
\tag{2.1}
\]

The two changed owners are Johnson adjacent.  More precisely,

\[
 X_2(w')=
 \bigl(X_2(w)\setminus\{w_1\}\bigr)\cup\{w_2\}.
\tag{2.2}
\]

Thus the derivative has coordinate marginals

\[
 b_{w_1}(\Delta)=-1,\qquad
 b_{w_2}(\Delta)=+1,
\tag{2.3}
\]

and all other coordinate marginals zero.

Replacing \(w\) by \(w'\) at one top and changing no other top is a legal
transition between two one-configuration-per-top coefficients.
Furthermore, (2.1) has minimum possible support among all nonzero
zero-total integral middle derivatives.

#### Proof

Equations (1.4)--(1.6) show that the deleted-window supports differ only
at \(j=2\).  Complementing inside the fixed top gives (2.1).  Formula
(1.6) gives (2.2), hence (2.3).

Both words are injective and use the same letters, so both are literal
options at the same top (and use the same fixed core whenever the original
word does).  A nonzero integral vector of total sum zero must have at
least one positive and one negative coordinate.  The derivative (2.1)
has exactly those two coordinates, proving support minimality.
\(\square\)

For the quadratic collision energy

\[
                         \Psi(L)=\sum_X\binom{L_X}{2},
\tag{2.4}
\]

the exact change is

\[
 \Psi(L+e_Y-e_X)-\Psi(L)=L_Y-L_X+1.
\tag{2.5}
\]

Hence the boundary swap is a strict quadratic descent whenever
\(L_X\ge L_Y+2\).  For the linear collision excess, it fills one hole
and removes one duplicate whenever \(L_X\ge2\) and \(L_Y=0\).

### Theorem 2.2 (every Johnson edge occurs)

Let \(X,Y\in\binom{[2m]}m\) be Johnson adjacent.  Then there is a rank
\(M\) top \(U\) and two all-core path options at \(U\) whose support
difference is \(e_Y-e_X\).

If a prescribed \(2H\)-core \(Q\) satisfies \(Q\subseteq X\cap Y\),
the two options may use that same core.

#### Proof

Write

\[
 X\setminus Y=\{a\},\qquad Y\setminus X=\{b\}.
\tag{2.6}
\]

Choose an \((H-1)\)-set

\[
 C\subseteq[2m]\setminus(X\cup Y)
\tag{2.7}
\]

and put

\[
                         U=X\cup\{b\}\cup C.
\tag{2.8}
\]

Then \(|U|=M\).  Start the injective word with

\[
                    w_1=a,\quad w_2=b,\quad
                    \{w_3,\ldots,w_{H+1}\}=C
\tag{2.9}
\]

and fill the remaining positions injectively from \(U\).  Equations
(1.6) give

\[
 U\setminus J_2(w)=X,\qquad
 U\setminus J_2(w')=Y.
\tag{2.10}
\]

Theorem 2.1 now applies.  If \(Q\subseteq X\cap Y\), choose the filler
and unused letters so that the displayed word lies in \(U\setminus Q\);
the calibrated inequality \(m>4H\) leaves ample room.
\(\square\)

The Johnson graph is connected.  Its oriented edge differences generate
the complete root lattice \(A_{W-1}\), proving (0.3).

## 3. Other-rank locality

The swap in Theorem 2.1 is not middle-only, but it is vertically local.
For any fixed deletion length \(\ell\), an interval of an injective word
changes only if it contains exactly one of the first two positions.
There are at most two such boundary intervals.  Therefore, after any
fixed nested phase restriction, the two configurations differ on at most
two physical targets at each signed depth.

Across the \(O(H)\) protected signed depths, the complete configuration
derivative has support \(O(H)\).  Thus the primitive changes a coordinate
marginal at the smallest possible middle cost and at only linear vertical
toll.  What remains unproved is a dense compatibility/routing theorem
which applies many such swaps without paying that \(O(H)\) toll
independently at every repaired collision.

## 4. Aggregate marginal \(L^2\)-flatness

We use the equivalent complete-frame description.  At every top \(U\),
a full cyclic frame has \(M\) middle windows and every \(i\in U\) belongs
to exactly \(m\) of them.  The retained path deletes a consecutive block
of \(\kappa\) middle windows.

Let \(c_i^U\) be the number of deleted middle windows containing \(i\),
with \(c_i^U=0\) for \(i\notin U\).  Let \(t_i^U\) be the number of their
complementary \(H\)-windows containing \(i\).  For \(i\in U\),

\[
                         c_i^U=\kappa-t_i^U,
\tag{4.1}
\]

and

\[
                         \sum_{i\in U}t_i^U=H\kappa.
\tag{4.2}
\]

Put

\[
 h_i^U=
 \begin{cases}
 c_i^U-\dfrac{m\kappa}{M},&i\in U,\\[2mm]
 0,&i\notin U.
 \end{cases}
\tag{4.3}
\]

Since \(m=M-H\), equations (4.1)--(4.2) give

\[
 h_i^U=-\left(t_i^U-\frac{H\kappa}{M}\right)
 \quad(i\in U),
\tag{4.4}
\]

\[
 \sum_i h_i^U=0,\qquad
 \|h^U\|_1\le2H\kappa,\qquad
 \|h^U\|_\infty\le2H.
\tag{4.5}
\]

Let

\[
                         a_i=\sum_Uc_i^U.
\tag{4.6}
\]

The number of tops containing \(i\) is \(MN/(2m)\), so

\[
 \sum_{U\ni i}\frac{m\kappa}{M}=\frac{\kappa N}{2}.
\tag{4.7}
\]

The retained coordinate marginal is

\[
 b_i=\frac{MN}{2}-a_i,
\tag{4.8}
\]

while

\[
 \frac T2=\frac{(M-\kappa)N}{2}.
\tag{4.9}
\]

Consequently

\[
 \delta_i=b_i-\frac T2
 =-\sum_Uh_i^U.
\tag{4.10}
\]

Equations (4.5) and (4.10) imply

\[
 \|\delta\|_1\le2H\kappa N.
\tag{4.11}
\]

For a fixed coordinate, only the \(MN/(2m)<N\) tops containing it
contribute, so

\[
 \|\delta\|_\infty\le2HN.
\tag{4.12}
\]

Finally,

\[
 \|\delta\|_2^2\le
 \|\delta\|_1\|\delta\|_\infty
 \le4H^2\kappa N^2,
\tag{4.13}
\]

which is (0.5).  Since \(T\asymp mN\), \(\kappa=O(H)\), and
\(H=(1+o(1))\sqrt{m\log m}\),

\[
 \frac{\|\delta\|_2^2}{T^2}
 =O\left(\frac{H^3}{m^2}\right)
 =O\left(\frac{(\log m)^{3/2}}{\sqrt m}\right)=o(1).
\tag{4.14}
\]

This proves (0.6).

## 5. Exact boundary

The following are now proved.

* The old shield \(L^\sharp\) is not legal because its singleton
  marginals are not flat.
* Legal singleton marginals are flat not only coordinatewise but in
  normalized aggregate \(L^2\).
* The marginal-neutral six-frame library does not generate the full legal
  support: a one-root boundary swap is a support-minimal
  marginal-changing primitive.
* At the middle, all zero-total unit transfers lie in the lattice generated
  by such legal root-factor differences.

Still unproved:

* a state-dependent routing theorem placing the required old path at a
  top carrying a current duplicate and orienting the new path toward a
  current hole;
* simultaneous control of the \(O(H)\) changed signed traces; and
* the assertion that every legal near-flat marginal fibre contains a
  coefficient with \(\Psi=o(W)\).

The precise remaining issue is therefore nonlinear support reachability,
not a surviving linear marginal invariant.
