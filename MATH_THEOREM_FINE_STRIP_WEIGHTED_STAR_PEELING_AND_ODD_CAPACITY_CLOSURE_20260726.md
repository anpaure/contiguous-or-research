### Theorem A (exact weighted star peeling)

For every nonnegative edge weight \(y\), every target \(v\), and the
restriction \(y'\) obtained by deleting all edges through \(v\),

\[
 \boxed{
 \operatorname {Exc}_D(y)
 \le \operatorname {Exc}_D(y')+
       \mathfrak l_m\nu_{y'},}
 \qquad
 \operatorname {Exc}_D(y)=\sum_Cy_C-D\nu_y .
\tag{0.4}
\]

Consequently, if deleting \(t\) target stars leaves an intersecting
support, then

\[
 \boxed{
 \sum_Cy_C\le(D+t\mathfrak l_m)\nu_y.}
\tag{0.5}
\]

If the \(t\) stars cover the support, \(t\mathfrak l_m\) improves to
\((t-1)\mathfrak l_m\). Moreover,

\[
 {\mathfrak l_m\over D}
 =O\!\left({1\over m}+{h\over m^{3/2}}\right)+o(m^{-A})
\tag{0.6}
\]

for every fixed \(A\). Hence (0.5) gives the Gaussian-annulus accuracy

\[
 \sum_Cy_C\le\left(D+o(D/\sqrt m)\right)\nu_y
\tag{0.7}
\]

whenever

\[
 t\left({1\over\sqrt m}+{h\over m}\right)=o(1).
\tag{0.8}
\]

Thus arbitrary non-clique duals of low star-to-intersecting deletion
number are closed.

The second result closes the entire literal odd-set hierarchy supplied by
physical target capacities.

### Theorem B (all target-capacity rows)

For \(U\subseteq V(\mathcal H^\#)\), \(r=|U|\), and integers
\(2\le p\le r\), define

\[
 a_{U,p}(C)=\left\lfloor{|e_C^\#\cap U|\over p}\right\rfloor,
 \qquad b_{U,p}=\left\lfloor{r\over p}\right\rfloor.
\tag{0.9}
\]

Every matching \(M\) obeys the valid capacity inequality

\[
 \sum_{C\in M}a_{U,p}(C)\le b_{U,p}.
\tag{0.10}
\]

Summing over all columns of the selected hypergraph \(\mathcal H^\#\),

\[
 \boxed{
 {\sum_Ca_{U,p}(C)\over D b_{U,p}}
 \le1+6\delta.}
\tag{0.11}
\]

At \(p=2\), the sharper estimate is

\[
 \boxed{
 {\sum_Ca_{U,2}(C)\over D\lfloor r/2\rfloor}
 \le1+\delta.}
\tag{0.12}
\]

For graph edges and odd \(r\), (0.12) is exactly the ordinary
odd-set/blossom row.
For physical strip edges it is its literal target-capacity extension,
including edges which consume several pairs of vertices of \(U\).

The conclusion is not restricted to indicator sets. Let
\(\omega:V(\mathcal H^\#)\to[0,1]\), put

\[
 R_\omega=\sum_v\omega_v,
\]

and, for an integer \(p\) with \(2\le p\le R_\omega\), define

\[
 a_{\omega,p}(C)=
 \left\lfloor{\sum_{v\in e_C^\#}\omega_v\over p}\right\rfloor,
 \qquad
 b_{\omega,p}=\left\lfloor{R_\omega\over p}\right\rfloor.
\tag{0.12a}
\]

Then every matching satisfies

\[
 \sum_{C\in M}a_{\omega,p}(C)\le b_{\omega,p},
\tag{0.12b}
\]

and

\[
 \boxed{
 {\sum_Ca_{\omega,p}(C)\over D b_{\omega,p}}
 \le1+6\delta.}
\tag{0.12c}
\]

Thus arbitrary fractional target measures also give no obstruction at
the Gaussian precision.

Combining Theorem B with the audited Boolean-envelope Hilton--Milner
theorem shows that

\[
 x_C={1\over(1+6\delta)D}
\tag{0.13}
\]

satisfies simultaneously every target-star row, every clique row, and
every indicator or fractional target-capacity row (0.10), (0.12b).
Since

\[
 (1+6\delta)D=D+O(D/m),
\tag{0.14}
\]

these rows cannot obstruct coefficient one.

This does **not** prove that (0.13) belongs to the full matching
polytope. The exact surviving separator must be a heterogeneous
higher-order set-packing facet which is not implied by these rows; in
Farkas form, its left-minus-right functional lies outside the conic
implication cone generated by the corresponding left-minus-right
functionals and nonnegativity. In addition, Theorem A says that any
sequence violating the Gaussian target
\(D+o(D/\sqrt m)\) has, on a fixed-positive-excess subsequence, growing
star-to-intersecting deletion number. This is strictly narrower than the
previous phrase “arbitrary non-clique obstruction.”

## 1. The singleton Boolean-envelope kernel

For a singleton target label \(A=\{v\}\),

\[
 \operatorname {sp}(A)=0,
 \qquad \mathcal B(A)=\{v\}.
\tag{1.1}
\]

The selected localized adaptive kernel says that whenever
\(v\notin e_E^\#\),

\[
 \sum_{T\in e_E^\#}d^\#(v,T)
 \le4\eta_0(K)d^\#(v)+K\Lambda
 \le\mathfrak l_m.
\tag{1.2}
\]

Every strip \(C\) counted by

\[
 v\in e_C^\#,
 \qquad e_C^\#\cap e_E^\#\ne\varnothing
\]

contributes at least one to the left side of (1.2). Therefore

\[
 \boxed{
 |\{C:v\in e_C^\#,
          e_C^\#\cap e_E^\#\ne\varnothing\}|
 \le\mathfrak l_m.}
\tag{1.3}
\]

This is stronger than applying a maximum pair-codegree and summing over
all \(T\in e_E^\#\): it retains the actual Boolean-envelope conditional
kernel.

Under the audited strip hypotheses,

\[
 \eta_0(K)=
 O\!\left({1\over m}+{K\over m^2}\right)+o(m^{-A}),
 \qquad K=O(h\sqrt m),
\tag{1.4}
\]

while \(K\Lambda/D\) is superpolynomially small. Equations
(0.3) and (1.4) prove (0.6).

## 2. Proof of the weighted star-peeling theorem

Fix \(y\ge0\), put

\[
 \nu=\nu_y,
\]

and delete all positive-weight edges through a target \(v\). Let the
remaining weight be \(y'\), put \(b=\nu_{y'}\), and choose a maximum
\(y'\)-weight matching \(N\), so \(y(N)=b\).

For an edge \(C\ni v\), let

\[
 N_C=\{E\in N:e_E^\#\cap e_C^\#\ne\varnothing\}.
\tag{2.1}
\]

Then

\[
 \{C\}\cup(N\setminus N_C)
\]

is a matching. Hence

\[
 y_C+b-y(N_C)\le\nu,
\]

or

\[
 y_C\le\nu-b+y(N_C).
\tag{2.2}
\]

Sum (2.2) over all positive-weight edges through \(v\). There are at
most \(D\) of them. Reversing the second summation and using (1.3) for
each \(E\in N\) gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
 y(\operatorname {Star}(v))
 &\le D(\nu-b)
   +\sum_{E\in N}y_E
     |\{C:v\in e_C^\#,\ e_C^\#\cap e_E^\#\ne\varnothing\}|\\
 &\le D(\nu-b)+\mathfrak l_m b.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2.3}
\]

Since
\[
 \sum_Cy_C=y(\operatorname {Star}(v))+\sum_Cy'_C,
\]
(2.3) is equivalent to (0.4).

Iterate (0.4) along targets \(v_1,\ldots,v_t\). If \(y_j\) is the
restriction avoiding the first \(j\) stars and \(\nu_j=\nu_{y_j}\),
then

\[
 \boxed{
 \sum_Cy_C
 \le D\nu_0+
   \bigl(\sum_Cy_t(C)-D\nu_t\bigr)
   +\mathfrak l_m\sum_{j=1}^t\nu_j.}
\tag{2.4}
\]

Suppose the terminal support is intersecting. If it lies in one target
star, it has at most \(D\) edges, each of weight at most \(\nu_t\), so
its \(D\)-excess is nonpositive. If it is non-star, the weighted physical
Hilton--Milner theorem gives

\[
 \sum_Cy_t(C)
 \le o(D/\sqrt m)\max_Cy_t(C)
 \le o(D/\sqrt m)\nu_t
 \le D\nu_t
\tag{2.5}
\]

for all sufficiently large \(m\). Thus the parenthesized term in (2.4)
is nonpositive. Since \(\nu_j\le\nu_0\), (0.5) follows. If the stars
cover the support, \(\nu_t=0\), so only \(j=1,\ldots,t-1\) can
contribute, proving the sharper assertion. Equations (0.6) and (0.8)
then prove (0.7).

There is also a useful exact contrapositive. If

\[
 \operatorname {Exc}_D(y)
 \ge {cD\over\sqrt m}\nu_y,
\tag{2.6}
\]

then deleting any ordered set of

\[
 t<{cD\over2\mathfrak l_m\sqrt m}
\tag{2.7}
\]

target stars leaves positive \(D\)-excess, and therefore cannot leave an
intersecting support. Thus every forbidden-scale dual has
star-to-intersecting deletion number

\[
 \Omega\!\left(\left(m^{-1/2}+h/m\right)^{-1}\right).
\tag{2.8}
\]

## 3. A non-clique four-witness extension

Pairwise intersection is not intrinsically required by the four-level
Boolean-envelope recursion. It is used only to supply hitting witnesses.
This gives a second arbitrary-support result.

Use the source theorem's parameters

\[
 B={D\over mK^4},\qquad
 s_0=\left\lceil{\log(4mK^4)\over\log(1/\alpha)}\right\rceil,
 \qquad
 \gamma=4\max_{0\le s\le s_0}\eta_s(K),
\tag{3.1}
\]

and put

\[
 \mathfrak r_m=
 KD\gamma^3+{3D\over mK}+3K^4\Lambda
 =o(D/\sqrt m).
\tag{3.2}
\]

Call a family \(\mathcal Q\) *three-contraction Boolean separated* if it
has the following rooted collision tree. A root strip meets every member
of \(\mathcal Q\). At each active branch labelled by a feasible target
set \(A\), where \(1\le|A|\le3\) and \(d^\#(A)>B\), there is a witness
strip meeting every edge in that branch and avoiding
\(\mathcal B(A)\). Partition the branch by one actual collision target
with that witness, and perform this expansion through labels of size
three.

### Proposition 3.1 (weighted separated-tree bound)

For every \(y\ge0\) supported on such a family,

\[
 \boxed{
 \sum_{C\in\mathcal Q}y_C
 \le\mathfrak r_m\max_{C\in\mathcal Q}y_C.}
\tag{3.3}
\]

#### Proof

The root partition has total full-link capacity at most \(KD\). The
selected adaptive kernel gives successively

\[
\begin{aligned}
 S_2&\le\gamma KD+K^2\Lambda,\\
 S_3&\le\gamma^2KD+\gamma K^2\Lambda+K^3\Lambda,\\
 S_4&\le\gamma^3KD+
       \gamma^2K^2\Lambda+\gamma K^3\Lambda+K^4\Lambda.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3.4}
\]

Stopped nodes at depths one, two, and three contribute at most

\[
 B(K+K^2+K^3)\le{3D\over mK}.
\tag{3.5}
\]

Because \(\gamma\le1\), the additive terms in (3.4) total at most
\(3K^4\Lambda\). Every terminal branch weight is at most its full-link
capacity times \(\max_Cy_C\). Equations (3.2)--(3.5) prove (3.3).
No pairwise-intersection assumption occurs once the displayed hitting
witnesses are supplied. \(\square\)

Consequently, if a weighted support is the disjoint union of \(r\) such
families and a remainder, and deleting \(t\) target stars from that
remainder leaves an intersecting support, then

\[
 \sum_Cy_C
 \le(D+t\mathfrak l_m+r\mathfrak r_m)\nu_y.
\tag{3.6}
\]

This closes every bounded-complexity star/nonstar/envelope-separated
dual at the displayed rate. A dual with fixed-positive Gaussian-scale
excess must concentrate on envelope-entangled branches which admit no
sufficiently economical such decomposition.

## 4. Proof of the target-capacity inequalities

Fix \(U\), \(r=|U|\), and \(2\le p\le r\). For a matching \(M\), the
sets \(e_C^\#\cap U\), \(C\in M\), are disjoint. Therefore

\[
 \sum_{C\in M}a_{U,p}(C)
 \le\left\lfloor{\sum_{C\in M}|e_C^\#\cap U|\over p}\right\rfloor
 \le\left\lfloor{r\over p}\right\rfloor,
\]

which proves (0.10).

Put

\[
 A_{U,p}=\sum_Ca_{U,p}(C),
 \qquad b=\lfloor r/p\rfloor\ge1.
\tag{4.1}
\]

Counting target incidences in \(U\),

\[
 A_{U,p}\le {1\over p}\sum_C|e_C^\#\cap U|
 \le{Dr\over p}.
\tag{4.2}
\]

For every integer \(s\ge0\),

\[
 \binom p2\left\lfloor{s\over p}\right\rfloor
 \le\binom s2.
\tag{4.3}
\]

Indeed, partition \(p\lfloor s/p\rfloor\) of the \(s\) objects into
disjoint \(p\)-blocks and count the within-block pairs. Hence

\[
\begin{aligned}
 \binom p2 A_{U,p}
 &\le\sum_C\binom{|e_C^\#\cap U|}{2}\\
 &=\sum_{\{u,v\}\in\binom U2}d^\#(u,v)\\
 &\le\lambda\binom r2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4.4}
\]

Dividing (4.2) and (4.4) by \(Db\) gives

\[
 {A_{U,p}\over Db}
 \le
 \min\left\{
 {r\over pb},
 \delta{r(r-1)\over p(p-1)b}
 \right\}.
\tag{4.5}
\]

Since \(pb\le r<p(b+1)\), the first term is less than
\(1+1/b\), while the second is less than

\[
 2\delta{(b+1)^2\over b}
 \le\delta(2b+6).
\tag{4.6}
\]

If \(\delta=0\), (4.4) gives \(A_{U,p}=0\). Assume henceforth that
\(\delta>0\). If \(b\ge(2\delta)^{-1}\), use the first term to obtain
\(1+2\delta\). If \(b<(2\delta)^{-1}\), use (4.6) to obtain
\(1+6\delta\). This proves (0.11).

For \(p=2\), even \(r\) is settled by (4.2) with ratio at most one.
If \(r=2b+1\), (4.5) becomes

\[
 {A_{U,2}\over Db}
 \le\min\left\{
 1+{1\over2b},\ \delta(2b+1)
 \right\}.
\tag{4.7}
\]

If \(1/(2b)\le\delta\), the first term is at most \(1+\delta\).
Otherwise \(2\delta b<1\), and the second term is less than
\(1+\delta\). This proves (0.12).

For the fractional form, write

\[
 s_C=\sum_{v\in e_C^\#}\omega_v,
 \qquad R=R_\omega,
 \qquad b=\lfloor R/p\rfloor.
\tag{4.8}
\]

Disjointness of matching edges gives \(\sum_{C\in M}s_C\le R\), so
(0.12b) follows exactly as in (0.10). Incidence counting gives

\[
 \sum_Ca_{\omega,p}(C)
 \le {1\over p}\sum_Cs_C
 \le {DR\over p}.
\tag{4.9}
\]

Because \(0\le\omega_v\le1\),

\[
 \sum_{\{u,v\}\subseteq e_C^\#}\omega_u\omega_v
 ={s_C^2-\sum_{v\in e_C^\#}\omega_v^2\over2}
 \ge\max\left\{0,{s_C(s_C-1)\over2}\right\}.
\tag{4.10}
\]

If \(k=\lfloor s_C/p\rfloor=0\), the required lower bound by
\(k\binom p2\) is trivial. If \(k\ge1\), then \(s_C\ge kp\ge2\), and

\[
 {s_C(s_C-1)\over2}
 \ge k\binom p2.
\tag{4.11}
\]

Therefore

\[
\begin{aligned}
 \binom p2\sum_Ca_{\omega,p}(C)
 &\le
 \sum_C\sum_{\{u,v\}\subseteq e_C^\#}\omega_u\omega_v\\
 &=\sum_{\{u,v\}}d^\#(u,v)\omega_u\omega_v\\
 &\le\lambda\sum_{\{u,v\}}\omega_u\omega_v
 \le{\lambda R^2\over2}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4.12}
\]

If \(\delta=0\), (4.12) gives
\(\sum_Ca_{\omega,p}(C)=0\). Otherwise divide (4.9) and (4.12) by
\(Db\). Since
\(pb\le R<p(b+1)\), the same two-case calculation in
(4.5)--(4.6), with \(R^2\) in place of \(r(r-1)\), gives

\[
 {\sum_Ca_{\omega,p}(C)\over Db}\le1+6\delta,
\]

which proves (0.12c).

### Corollary 4.1 (sharpened pair-core theorem)

If a strip subfamily has a pair-core representation, then

\[
 \boxed{\chi_f'\le D+\lambda=D+O(D/m).}
\tag{4.13}
\]

#### Proof

The representation is a loopless multigraph on physical target
vertices. Its maximum degree is at most \(D\), and its maximum edge
multiplicity is at most \(\lambda\). For an odd set of \(r\ge3\)
multigraph vertices, the fractional blossom density is at most

\[
 \min\left\{D{r\over r-1},\,\lambda r\right\}.
\tag{4.14}
\]

The case \(\lambda=0\) is empty and trivial. Assume \(\lambda>0\).
If \(r\le D/\lambda+1\), the second term is at most \(D+\lambda\).
If \(r\ge D/\lambda+1\), the first is at most \(D+\lambda\).
The fractional matching-polytope formula for a loopless multigraph now
gives (4.13). Equivalently, its odd-set rows are dominated on the
physical diagonal by the \(p=2\) rows above. \(\square\)

This improves the earlier \(D+2\lambda\) estimate and is valid for all
weights on every pair-core support.

## 5. Polyhedral consequence and exact remaining facet

Let \(P_{\rm match}\) be the convex hull of incidence vectors of
ordinary strip matchings. The diagonal vector \(z\mathbf1\) belongs to
\(P_{\rm match}\) if and only if

\[
 \chi_f'(\mathcal H^\#)\le1/z.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Indeed, a convex decomposition of \(z\mathbf1\) into matching vectors,
scaled by \(1/z\), is a fractional edge colouring of total weight
\(1/z\). Conversely, normalize a fractional edge colouring of total
weight at most \(1/z\); its matching marginals dominate
\(z\mathbf1\), and the matching polytope is down-monotone.

Take \(z=((1+6\delta)D)^{-1}\). Target-star rows hold because every
target degree is at most \(D\). If \(\mathcal F\) is an intersecting
family, its clique row is

\[
 \sum_{C\in\mathcal F}x_C\le1.
\tag{5.2}
\]

When \(\mathcal F\) is contained in a target star, it has at most \(D\)
members. Otherwise the Boolean-envelope Hilton--Milner theorem gives
\(|\mathcal F|=o(D/\sqrt m)\). Thus (5.2) holds at the diagonal.
Equations (0.11)--(0.12c) and (0.13) give every indicator or fractional
target-capacity row.

Let \(P_{\rm out}\) be the outer relaxation defined by nonnegativity,
all target-star rows, all clique rows, and all rows (0.10) and
(0.12b). Then

\[
 {\mathbf1\over(1+6\delta)D}\in P_{\rm out}.
\tag{5.3}
\]

Therefore either

\[
 \chi_f'(\mathcal H^\#)
 \le(1+6\delta)D=D+O(D/m),
\tag{5.4}
\]

or a separating valid inequality for \(P_{\rm match}\) is not implied by
the rows defining \(P_{\rm out}\). Equivalently, after every inequality
is written as a left-minus-right functional bounded by zero, the
separator lies outside their Farkas conic implication cone (including
nonnegativity). Such a separator is not an ordinary blossom, not a
single target-capacity cut, and not a clique obstruction.

This last dichotomy concerns the stronger diagonal (5.3). A separator of
that diagonal need have excess only \(O(D/m)\), so Theorem A need not
force a growing deletion number. If instead the Gaussian conclusion
\(\chi_f'\le D+o(D/\sqrt m)\) fails, there are a constant \(c>0\), a
subsequence, and duals satisfying (2.6). On that subsequence Theorem A
and Proposition 3.1 force growing star-to-intersecting deletion number
and rule out every economical Boolean-separated decomposition covered by
(3.6).

This is the precise proved/conditional boundary. No claim that the outer
rows are complete is made.

## 6. Adversarial audit

1. **Weights versus cardinalities.** Theorem A uses a maximum-weight
   matching after each deletion. The exchange (2.2) is weighted; no
   conversion to an unweighted support is made.
2. **Why the witness avoids the singleton envelope.** An edge in the
   residual matching contains no \(v\), so (1.2) applies exactly.
3. **Multiplicity in (1.2).** A strip meeting \(E\) in several targets is
   counted several times on the left, which only strengthens (1.3).
4. **Terminal intersecting residual.** The star and non-star cases use
   different estimates and exhaust all intersecting supports.
5. **Odd-set terminology.** Theorem B closes all rows induced by literal
   or fractional target capacity (0.9), (0.12a). It does not assert that
   every facet of a general
   hypergraph matching polytope is an odd-set row.
6. **Diagonal versus arbitrary duals.** Theorem B controls the uniform
   diagonal. Theorem A is the separate all-weights statement. Neither is
   substituted for the other.
7. **Quantitative rate.** The Gaussian compiler needs additive
   \(o(D/\sqrt m)\), not merely \(o(D)\); this is why (0.8) is retained.
