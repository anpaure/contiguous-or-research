# Mathematical attack J: the exact wreath fibre as a Markov semigroup

Date: 2026-07-25

Method: pure mathematics only.  No literature search, web search, finite
search, solver, or computer-assisted enumeration was used.

## 0. Verdict

This route does **not** prove MWB, labelled synchronization, or the
contiguous-OR conjecture.  It gives a sharp algebraic/positive separation.

1. Let \(L_2\) be the integer span of all degree-two Markov edges which
   genuinely occur between exact wreath factors.  For every
   \(m>2H+2\),
   \[
   \boxed{
   (B_{m-1},\ldots,B_{m-H})(L_2)
   =\bigoplus_{q=1}^H\ker_{\mathbb Z}U_{m-q}.}
   \]
   Thus actual exact-factor \(2\)-switch labels already generate every
   simultaneous signed shadow correction with zero point margins in a fixed
   Gaussian window.  There is no additional integer or modular invariant
   after abelianization.  The theorem is not a path theorem: its generators
   can be based at different exact factors, and their signed sum need not be
   nonnegative.

2. The lifted Graver matrix gives an endpoint-monotone connectivity theorem:
   any two exact factors can be joined by at most
   \(\operatorname{Cat}_m\) applicable lifted-Graver moves while every
   observed lower load moves coordinatewise toward its endpoint value and
   never overshoots.  Adding another observed depth preserves every old
   lifted-Graver move.  This is exact connectivity with unrestricted move
   degree, not a bounded Markov basis.

3. Bounded-degree moves have an exponential speed obstruction.  If every
   move has degree at most \(D\), changing the weighted fixed-window
   overload by \(\delta W\) requires
   \[
   \Omega_A\!\left(\frac{\delta W}{D m^{3/2}}\right)
   \]
   steps.  The same estimate holds for labelled mismatch against a fixed
   nested resolution at the same scale.  This is a diameter and
   speed theorem, not nonconnectivity.

4. There is a new explicit Gaussian-window feasibility invariant.  Along
   infinitely many prime dimensions one can prescribe cyclic-invariant,
   point-regular floor/ceiling quotas at all depths such that the stacked
   target lies in the integer column lattice but is \(\Omega(W)\) outside
   the nonnegative real cone already at one depth
   \(q=x\sqrt m+O(1)\).  Every signed realization has negative mass
   \(\Omega(W/n)\).  Hence arbitrary prescribed high-cell sets can be
   impossible even fractionally.  This does not obstruct MWB, where the high
   cells are mobile and chosen jointly with the factor.

5. The full shadow filtration has an exact signed dimension suspension.  A
   top-\(h\)-neutral relation has exactly \(2h\) allowed directed insertion
   gaps, and its first active shadow is multiplied by \(m+1-h\).  However,
   the nonnegative equivariant gap-orbit cone contains no nonzero packing
   suspension.  Iteration preserves the raw signal factorially, while
   packing capacity grows only binomially; at a Gaussian endpoint
   \(h=A\sqrt M\), literal signal-preserving suspension fails after more
   than \((1/2+o(1))h\) dimension steps.

The stable obstruction is therefore positivity and coherent applicability
inside one right-hand-side-one fibre.  Signed lattice generation, abstract
Graver primitivity, and equivariant suspension are all available; none
desuspends automatically to a sequence of small moves through one exact
factor.

## 1. The exact factor fibre

Put

\[
n=2m+1,
\qquad
W=\binom{n}{m},
\qquad
\operatorname{Cat}_m=\frac{W}{n}.
\]

Let \(\Omega_m\) be the set of unoriented cyclic orders on \([n]\), with
rotation and reversal identified.  For \(1\le r\le m\), let

\[
B_r\in\{0,1\}^{\binom{n}{r}\times\Omega_m}
\]

be cyclic \(r\)-interval incidence.  Thus \((B_r)_{S,C}=1\) exactly when
\(S\) is a cyclic \(r\)-interval of \(C\).  Write

\[
A_m=B_m.
\]

The exact-factor fibre is

\[
\mathfrak F_m
=\{x\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{\Omega_m}:A_mx=\mathbf1\}.
\tag{1.1}
\]

It is the right-hand-side-one fibre of the affine semigroup

\[
\mathcal S_m=A_m\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{\Omega_m}.
\]

For a stacked window, the corresponding column semigroup is generated by
the columns \((A_mC,B_{m-1}C,\ldots,B_{m-H}C)\).  The distinction among its
integer lattice, rational cone, and nonnegative integral semigroup is
central below.

**Established input.**  We use the already-proved exact MSW middle-factor
construction, including one canonical size-two component whose four
ordinary readouts have the positional pattern displayed in (2.2).  This is
the only prior exact-factor construction invoked below.

### Lemma 1.1 (right-hand-side-one positivity is squarefree)

Every \(x\in\mathfrak F_m\) is the indicator of an exact middle wreath
factor and

\[
|\operatorname{supp}x|=\operatorname{Cat}_m.
\tag{1.2}
\]

#### Proof

If \(x_C\ge2\), every middle interval of \(C\) has load at least two,
contrary to \(A_mx=\mathbf1\).  If two positive columns share a middle set,
that row again has load at least two.  Thus \(x\) is squarefree and its
columns are pairwise middle-disjoint.  Summing all middle rows gives

\[
n\sum_Cx_C=W,
\]

because every cyclic order has exactly \(n\) middle intervals.  This proves
(1.2).  \(\square\)

A reduced support-feasible trade is

\[
z=\mathbf1_P-\mathbf1_N\in\ker_{\mathbb Z}A_m,
\tag{1.3}
\]

where \(P,N\) are middle-wreath packings.  Its degree is

\[
\deg z=|P|=|N|.
\]

Equality of the two degrees follows by summing all rows of \(A_mz=0\).
The trade is applicable at a factor \(F\) when \(N\subseteq F\); then
\(F-N+P\) is another exact factor.

For two exact factors, cancel their common columns and form the bipartite
ownership overlay: its left vertices are the old wreaths, its right vertices
are the new wreaths, and every changed middle set joins its two unique
owners.  Every vertex has degree \(n\).

### Lemma 1.2 (ordinary fibre Graver components)

The conformal \(A_m\)-kernel subvectors of a squarefree factor difference
are exactly unions of ownership-overlay components.  In particular, such a
difference is in \(\operatorname{Gr}(A_m)\) if and only if its overlay is
connected.

#### Proof

A conformal subvector selects some old and some new vertices.  In the row of
one ownership edge, the selected old and new coefficients must agree.
Selection is therefore constant on every connected component.  Conversely,
the signed vertex indicator of a component covers the same middle-edge set
on its two sides and lies in \(\ker A_m\).  \(\square\)

This gives exact connectivity using arbitrary component trades, but their
degree can be as large as an entire factor.

## 2. Degree-two exact edges saturate the signed Gaussian window

Let \(\Gamma_2\) be the set of oriented squarefree degree-two moves which
occur as edges between two exact factors, after their common wreaths are
cancelled, and put

\[
L_2=\langle\Gamma_2\rangle_{\mathbb Z}
\subseteq\ker_{\mathbb Z}A_m.
\tag{2.1}
\]

For rank \(r\), let \(U_r\) be point-versus-\(r\)-set incidence.

### Lemma 2.1 (a universal genuine degree-two edge)

Fix distinct labels \(\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\delta\).  Let \(E,O\) be
ordered lists partitioning the remaining labels, with

\[
|E|=m-1,
\qquad
|O|=m-2.
\]

In ordinary cyclic readout put

\[
\begin{aligned}
C&=(\delta,\gamma,E,\beta,\alpha,O),\\
D&=(\beta,\delta,E,\alpha,\gamma,O),\\
C'&=(\delta,\beta,E,\gamma,\alpha,O),\\
D'&=(\gamma,\delta,E,\alpha,\beta,O),
\end{aligned}
\tag{2.2}
\]

and

\[
g(E,O)=e_{C'}+e_{D'}-e_C-e_D.
\tag{2.3}
\]

Then \(g(E,O)\in\Gamma_2\).  If

\[
\partial K=e_{K\cup\{\gamma\}}-e_{K\cup\{\beta\}},
\]

and \(\operatorname{pre}_j,\operatorname{suf}_j\) denote the underlying
sets of the first and last \(j\) entries of an ordered list, then, for
\(2\le r\le m-1\),

\[
\boxed{
B_rg(E,O)
=\partial\operatorname{suf}_{r-1}O
+\partial\operatorname{suf}_{r-1}E
-\partial\operatorname{pre}_{r-1}E
-\partial\operatorname{pre}_{r-1}O.}
\tag{2.4}
\]

Also \(B_1g=B_mg=0\).

#### Proof

The four exceptional positions form two adjacent pairs.  A cyclic
\(r\)-window with \(r<m\) cannot meet both pairs.  Windows meeting neither
pair, or both entries of one pair, cancel.  The four windows meeting exactly
one exceptional entry have respectively the four prefix/suffix cores in
(2.4), with the displayed signs.  The same cut table at rank \(m\) gives
\(B_mg=0\), and singletons cancel at rank one.

The cut table also shows that \(\{C,D\}\) and \(\{C',D'\}\) are middle
packings.  To see exact-factor realizability, start with one canonical
size-two component in the known exact MSW factor.  Its four ordinary-readout
orders have exactly the positional pattern (2.2).  A ground-label
permutation sends its exceptional quadruple and every ordered \(E/O\) slot
to the prescribed data.  It carries both exact endpoint factors with it,
so their cancelled difference is precisely \(g(E,O)\).  Hence
\(g(E,O)\in\Gamma_2\).  \(\square\)

### Lemma 2.2 (integral rectangle lattice)

For \(2\le r\le n-2\), \(\ker_{\mathbb Z}U_r\) is generated by

\[
e_{Kac}-e_{Kbc}-e_{Kad}+e_{Kbd},
\qquad |K|=r-2,
\tag{2.5}
\]

where \(a,b,c,d\notin K\) are distinct.

#### Proof

Let \(\mathscr R_r\) be the lattice generated by (2.5).  Clearly
\(\mathscr R_r\subseteq\ker_{\mathbb Z}U_r\).  Work over an arbitrary
field.  If a function \(f\) on \(r\)-sets annihilates all rectangles, then
for fixed \(a,b\),

\[
f(T+a)-f(T+b)
\]

is independent of the \((r-1)\)-set \(T\) avoiding \(a,b\), by connectivity
of the corresponding Johnson graph.  Denote it by \(d_{a,b}\).  The cocycle
identity gives \(d_{a,b}+d_{b,c}=d_{a,c}\), so

\[
f(S)=c_0+\sum_{i\in S}a_i.
\]

This annihilator has dimension \(n\) over every field, including
characteristics dividing \(r\).  Hence the rectangle span has rank
\(\binom nr-n\) over every field.  The primitive lattice
\(\ker_{\mathbb Z}U_r\) has the same rank.  A nontrivial finite index would
lower the rectangle rank modulo some prime, a contradiction.  Thus the two
lattices agree.  \(\square\)

### Theorem 2.3 (abelianized degree-two window saturation)

If \(m>2H+2\), then

\[
\boxed{
(B_{m-1},\ldots,B_{m-H})(L_2)
=\bigoplus_{q=1}^H\ker_{\mathbb Z}U_{m-q}.}
\tag{2.6}
\]

#### Proof

First let \(q\ge2\).  Write

\[
O=(P,a,b,K),
\qquad |P|=q-2,
\qquad |K|=m-q-2,
\]

and let \(O'=(P,b,a,K)\).  Put

\[
h_q=g(E,O)-g(E,O')\in L_2.
\]

An adjacent swap changes an unordered prefix only at length \(q-1\), and
an unordered suffix only at length \(m-q-1\).  Formula (2.4) therefore gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
B_{m-q}h_q&=\partial(K+b)-\partial(K+a),\\
B_qh_q&=-\partial(P+a)+\partial(P+b),
\end{aligned}
\tag{2.7}
\]

and \(B_rh_q=0\) at every other \(2\le r\le m-1\).

For \(q=1\), take \(E=(a,b,K)\), \(|K|=m-3\), let
\(E'=(b,a,K)\), and put \(h_1=g(E,O)-g(E',O)\).  Then

\[
\begin{aligned}
B_{m-1}h_1&=\partial(K+b)-\partial(K+a),\\
B_2h_1&=-\partial a+\partial b,
\end{aligned}
\tag{2.8}
\]

with all other lower ranks zero.

The high effect in (2.7) or (2.8) is the rectangle

\[
e_{Kb\gamma}-e_{Kb\beta}-e_{Ka\gamma}+e_{Ka\beta}.
\]

Relabeling realizes every rectangle at rank \(m-q\).  Since
\(m>2H+2\), the collateral rank \(q\), or rank two when \(q=1\), lies below
the entire central window.  Lemma 2.2 therefore gives independent
surjectivity onto every direct summand in (2.6).

Conversely, if \(z\in L_2\subseteq\ker A_m\), then summing all middle rows
gives \(\sum_Cz_C=0\).  Every coordinate belongs to exactly \(r\) cyclic
\(r\)-intervals of one order, so

\[
U_rB_rz=r\left(\sum_Cz_C\right)\mathbf1=0.
\]

Thus the image is contained in the right side of (2.6).  \(\square\)

The theorem uses universal edges only for surjectivity.  It does not assert
that every degree-two exact-factor edge has form (2.3).

### Corollary 2.4 (perfect signed balancing by actual edge labels)

Fix \(H\) with \(m>2H+2\), and let \(F\) be any exact factor.  At every
depth \(q\le H\), there is a floor/ceiling-balanced load vector

\[
b_q\in\{c_q,c_q+1\}^{\binom{n}{m-q}}
\]

with the correct point margins, and there is one signed vector
\(z\in L_2\) such that simultaneously

\[
B_{m-q}(\mathbf1_F+z)=b_q
\qquad(1\le q\le H).
\tag{2.9}
\]

The vector \(\mathbf1_F+z\) need not be nonnegative.

#### Proof

Fix rank \(r=m-q\), put \(N=\binom nr\), and write

\[
W=cN+s.
\]

The desired high family must have \(s\) edges and constant point degree

\[
d=\frac{rs}{n}
=\frac{rW}{n}-c\binom{n-1}{r-1},
\tag{2.10}
\]

which is integral because \(W/n=\operatorname{Cat}_m\).

Among all simple \(s\)-edge \(r\)-uniform families, minimize the sum of
squared point degrees.  If \(\deg(a)\ge\deg(b)+2\), some edge containing
\(a\) and not \(b\) can be switched from \(a\) to \(b\) without creating
a duplicate.  Otherwise the switch map would inject every \(a\)-not-\(b\)
edge into a \(b\)-not-\(a\) edge, implying
\(\deg(a)\le\deg(b)\).  The available switch strictly lowers the squared
degree sum, a contradiction.  Thus all degrees differ by at most one, and
the integral average (2.10) forces them all to equal \(d\).

Let \(\mathcal H_q\) be this regular family and set

\[
b_q=c_q\mathbf1+\mathbf1_{\mathcal H_q}.
\]

It has total \(W\) and the same point margins as every wreath-factor shadow.
Therefore

\[
b_q-B_{m-q}\mathbf1_F\in\ker_{\mathbb Z}U_{m-q}.
\]

Apply Theorem 2.3 to the tuple of these discrepancies.  \(\square\)

There is an exact multicover interpretation.  If

\[
z=g_1+\cdots+g_t
\]

is a word in oriented elements of \(\Gamma_2\), choose exact endpoint
factors \(F_j^-,F_j^+\) with difference \(g_j\).  Then

\[
X^-:=\sum_{j=1}^t\mathbf1_{F_j^-},
\qquad
X^+:=\sum_{j=1}^t\mathbf1_{F_j^+}
\]

are decomposable exact \(t\)-fold covers and

\[
X^+-X^-=z.
\tag{2.11}
\]

Thus every signed window correction becomes a genuine nonnegative move after
suspension to a sufficiently large decomposable multicover.  What is missing
is desuspension to the right-hand-side-one fibre, or a coherent ordering in
which each degree-two edge is applicable to the current single factor.

## 3. Lifted-Graver connectivity and depth filtration

For \(H\ge1\), stack the observed lower maps as

\[
\mathcal B_H=(B_{m-1},\ldots,B_{m-H})
\]

and introduce

\[
\mathcal M_H=
\begin{pmatrix}
A_m&0\\
\mathcal B_H&-I
\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{3.1}
\]

The lifted exact fibre consists of

\[
(x,y),
\qquad
A_mx=\mathbf1,
\qquad
y=\mathcal B_Hx,
\qquad
x,y\ge0.
\]

For integer vectors, \(u\sqsubseteq v\) means that they lie in the same
closed orthant and \(|u_i|\le|v_i|\) coordinatewise.  The Graver basis is
the set of nonzero conformally indecomposable kernel vectors.

### Theorem 3.1 (endpoint-monotone Markov connectivity)

Let \(F,F'\) be exact factors, with indicators \(x,x'\) and lower loads
\(y=\mathcal B_Hx\), \(y'=\mathcal B_Hx'\).  There are

\[
s\le|F\setminus F'|\le\operatorname{Cat}_m
\]

projected lifted-Graver moves \(p_1,\ldots,p_s\) such that

\[
(x'-x,y'-y)
=\sum_{i=1}^s(p_i,\mathcal B_Hp_i),
\tag{3.2}
\]

and every partial sum

\[
x_t=x+\sum_{i=1}^tp_i
\]

is an exact factor.  Moreover, for every observed target coordinate,
\(\mathcal B_Hx_t\) lies between its values at \(F\) and \(F'\).  Thus no
lower load overshoots its endpoint interval.

#### Proof

Conformally decompose the lifted kernel vector

\[
d=(x'-x,y'-y)
\]

into Graver elements.  Such a decomposition exists by induction on
\(\|d\|_1\): if \(d\) is not Graver, split it into two nonzero conformal
kernel vectors and continue.

Because \(x'-x\in\{-1,0,1\}^{\Omega_m}\), every projected summand is
squarefree, and the projected supports of distinct summands are disjoint.
Every partial sum lies coordinatewise between \(x\) and \(x'\), hence is
nonnegative.  Its middle equation remains \(A_mx_t=\mathbf1\), so Lemma 1.1
makes it an exact factor.  The same conformality puts every lower-load
partial sum between \(y\) and \(y'\).

Every nonzero projected kernel vector has both signs, because summing the
rows of \(A_mp=0\) gives \(n\sum_Cp_C=0\).  Its negative support contains a
distinct member of \(F\setminus F'\), proving the bound on \(s\).  \(\square\)

### Proposition 3.2 (component-packet characterization)

Let \(p\) be an applicable squarefree factor move with ownership components
\(p_1,\ldots,p_t\), and put

\[
\delta_i=\mathcal B_Hp_i,
\qquad
\delta=\sum_i\delta_i.
\]

Then \((p,\delta)\in\operatorname{Gr}(\mathcal M_H)\) if and only if there
is no nonempty proper \(I\subset[t]\) for which

\[
\sum_{i\in I}\delta_i\sqsubseteq\delta.
\tag{3.3}
\]

#### Proof

By Lemma 1.2, a conformal middle-kernel submove of \(p\) is exactly a union
of ownership components.  Such a union is conformal in the lifted auxiliary
coordinates exactly when (3.3) holds; its complement is then conformal as
well.  \(\square\)

In particular, if \(\delta=0\), lifted Graver packets are exactly the
inclusion-minimal nonempty zero-sum unions of ownership-component effects.

### Theorem 3.3 (depth-filtration suspension)

Assume \(H+1\le m-1\).  Let

\[
\mathcal G_H^\pi
=\{p:(p,\mathcal B_Hp)\in\operatorname{Gr}(\mathcal M_H)\}.
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{\mathcal G_H^\pi\subseteq\mathcal G_{H+1}^\pi.}
\tag{3.4}
\]

#### Proof

Suppose \((p,\mathcal B_{H+1}p)\) had a nontrivial conformal kernel
decomposition.  Delete the last auxiliary block.  The two projected pieces
remain conformal kernel vectors for \(\mathcal M_H\).  Neither can project
to zero: a kernel vector with zero wreath part has every auxiliary part
zero.  This contradicts the Graver minimality of
\((p,\mathcal B_Hp)\).  \(\square\)

Adding observed depths can create new, larger cancellation packets, but it
never destroys an old lifted-Graver move.

### Corollary 3.4 (convex Graver augmentation)

Let

\[
\Phi(y)=\sum_jw_j\phi_j(y_j),
\qquad w_j>0,
\]

where every \(\phi_j\) is discretely convex.  If an exact factor \(F'\) has
smaller \(\Phi\) than \(F\), one applicable projected move
\(p\in\mathcal G_H^\pi\) satisfies

\[
\Phi(\mathcal B_H(x+p))-\Phi(\mathcal B_Hx)
\le
-\frac{\Phi(y)-\Phi(y')}{|F\setminus F'|}.
\tag{3.5}
\]

#### Proof

Use the conformal decomposition in Theorem 3.1.  For scalar conformal
increments \(a_i\), discrete convexity gives

\[
\phi\!\left(t+\sum_i a_i\right)-\phi(t)
\ge
\sum_i\bigl(\phi(t+a_i)-\phi(t)\bigr).
\]

Sum over coordinates.  The sum of the individual move changes is at most
the negative endpoint change, so one of at most \(|F\setminus F'|\) moves
satisfies (3.5).  \(\square\)

Taking

\[
\phi_q(t)=\frac{(t-c_q)(t-c_q-1)}{2c_q}
\]

recovers exact lifted-Graver descent for the weighted collision energy.
Local minima under the full lifted Graver basis are global minima.  No
degree bound follows.

## 4. A bounded-degree speed and diameter obstruction

At depth \(q\), write

\[
W=c_qN_q+r_q,
\qquad
N_q=\binom{n}{m-q}.
\]

For an integer load \(\mu\) of total \(W\), put

\[
D_q^-(\mu)=\sum_S(c_q-\mu(S))_+,
\qquad
D_q^+(\mu)=\sum_S(\mu(S)-c_q-1)_+,
\]

and

\[
O_q(\mu)=\max(D_q^-(\mu),D_q^+(\mu)).
\tag{4.1}
\]

This is the exact minimum floor/ceiling overload.  For
\(H_A=\lceil A\sqrt m\rceil\), define

\[
\mathcal O_A(F)=
\sum_{q=1}^{H_A}\frac{O_q(B_{m-q}\mathbf1_F)}{c_q},
\qquad
S_A(m)=\sum_{q=1}^{H_A}\frac1{c_q}.
\tag{4.2}
\]

Since \(c_q\ge1\),

\[
S_A(m)\le H_A=O_A(\sqrt m).
\tag{4.3}
\]

### Lemma 4.1 (one-move Lipschitz bound)

If \(z\) is a support-feasible trade of degree \(k\) applicable at the
exact factor \(F\), then

\[
\|B_{m-q}z\|_1\le2nk
\tag{4.4}
\]

at every depth, and

\[
|\mathcal O_A(F+z)-\mathcal O_A(F)|
\le nkS_A(m).
\tag{4.5}
\]

#### Proof

Each wreath column contains exactly \(n\) cyclic intervals of each fixed
rank.  The positive and negative sides of \(z\) therefore contribute total
\(nk\) each, proving (4.4).

Equivalently,

\[
O_q(\mu)=\frac12
\min_{b\text{ floor/ceiling balanced}}\|\mu-b\|_1.
\]

Indeed, the two vectors have equal total, so half their \(\ell^1\)-distance
is their total positive excess.  Placing the prescribed \(r_q\) high quotas
on positive-excess coordinates whenever possible leaves exactly
\(\max(D_q^-,D_q^+)\).

Distance to a fixed set is one-Lipschitz, so \(O_q\) changes by at most
\(\frac12\|B_{m-q}z\|_1\).  Combine this with (4.4) and sum with weights
\(1/c_q\).  \(\square\)

### Theorem 4.2 (bounded-degree rebalancing speed limit)

Let

\[
F_0,F_1,\ldots,F_T
\]

be a path of exact factors in which every move has degree at most \(D\).
Then

\[
\boxed{
T\ge
\frac{|\mathcal O_A(F_T)-\mathcal O_A(F_0)|}
     {nD S_A(m)}.}
\tag{4.6}
\]

In particular, reducing weighted overload by \(\delta W\) requires

\[
\boxed{
T=\Omega_A\!\left(\frac{\delta W}{D m^{3/2}}\right).}
\tag{4.7}
\]

If \(D=e^{o(m)}\) and \(T=e^{o(m)}\), the total possible change is \(o(W)\).

#### Proof

Telescope (4.5), use (4.3), and recall \(n=2m+1\).  Also
\(W=\exp(\Theta(m))\), so a subexponential product \(DT\), multiplied by a
polynomial in \(m\), is \(o(W)\).  \(\square\)

There is a labelled analogue.  Fix one nested resolution \(P\), and put

\[
E_A(F,P)=\sum_{q=1}^{H_A}\frac{e_q(F,P)}{c_q}.
\]

A degree-\(k\) trade changes ownership only on the \(nk\) middle masks
covered by its negative side.  Therefore

\[
|E_A(F+z,P)-E_A(F,P)|\le nkS_A(m),
\tag{4.8}
\]

and the analogue of (4.6) has denominator \(nDS_A(m)\).  This estimate is
for one fixed common \(P\); reoptimizing \(P\) after every move is a
different problem.

### Proposition 4.3 (degree-truncated fibre diameter)

Let \(\mathfrak G_D\) be the exact-factor graph using all applicable moves
of degree at most \(D\).  If a component contains both \(F\) and \(\tau F\)
for a coordinate transposition \(\tau\), then their graph distance is at
least

\[
\boxed{
\frac{\operatorname{Cat}_m}{D}.}
\tag{4.9}
\]

#### Proof

For \(m\ge2\), \(F\cap\tau F=\varnothing\).  Indeed, every cyclic order
shares a middle interval with its transposed image: among the \(n\) middle
intervals the two transposed labels have total incidence \(2m=n-1\), so
some interval contains both or neither.  If \(C,\tau C\in F\), this common
interval contradicts exactness.  A single transposition cannot stabilize an
unoriented odd cyclic order, because a nontrivial rotation has no fixed label
and a reflection has cycle type \(1\,2^m\).

Every one of the \(\operatorname{Cat}_m\) original wreaths must therefore
be removed at least once along the path, while one degree-\(D\) step removes
at most \(D\).  \(\square\)

These results do not rule out bounded-degree connectivity.  They show that
bounded or slowly growing moves cannot provide a short bulk-balancing path.

## 5. A Gaussian lattice-outside-cone invariant

The preceding saturation theorem is signed.  The next result shows that the
positive cone can fail by a macroscopic amount even for balanced,
point-regular quotas inside a fixed Gaussian window.

### Theorem 5.1 (Gaussian prescribed-quota cone separation)

Fix \(A>0\).  Choose \(x\in(0,A)\) such that

\[
R=e^{x^2}\notin\mathbb Z,
\qquad
c=\lfloor R\rfloor,
\qquad
\rho=R-c\in(0,1).
\tag{5.1}
\]

Along the infinite subsequence for which \(n=2m+1\) is prime, put

\[
q=\lfloor x\sqrt m\rfloor,
\qquad
r=m-q,
\qquad
N=\binom nr,
\qquad
H=\lceil A\sqrt m\rceil.
\]

For all sufficiently large \(m\) there are cyclic-invariant
floor/ceiling-balanced quota vectors \(b_s\) at every depth \(s\le H\),
with the correct totals and point margins, such that:

1. the target
   \[
   (\mathbf1,b_1,\ldots,b_H)
   \]
   lies in the integer column lattice of
   \((B_m,B_{m-1},\ldots,B_{m-H})\);
2. for some \(\eta_x>0\), every fractional exact factor
   \(y\ge0\), \(B_my=\mathbf1\), satisfies
   \[
   \boxed{\|B_ry-b_q\|_1\ge\eta_xW;}
   \tag{5.2}
   \]
3. every signed integral realization of the stacked target satisfies
   \[
   \boxed{\|y^-\|_1\ge\eta_x\frac Wn.}
   \tag{5.3}
   \]

Thus this target lies in the integer lattice but a macroscopic distance
outside the nonnegative real cone.

#### Proof: the cyclic test family

Fix a cyclic order of the ground set and let

\[
a=\lceil\log_2n\rceil+K,
\tag{5.4}
\]

where the constant \(K\) will be chosen in terms of \(\rho\).  Let
\(\mathcal U\) be the family of rank-\(r\) sets containing at least one
cyclic block of \(a\) consecutive ground points.

For a uniformly random rank-\(r\) set and a fixed \(t\)-set, uniformly for
\(t\le2a\),

\[
p_t:=\frac{(r)_t}{(n)_t}
=2^{-t}\exp O\!\left(\frac{tq+t^2}{m}\right)
=(1+o(1))2^{-t}.
\tag{5.5}
\]

Let \(Z\) count the contained cyclic \(a\)-blocks.  Then

\[
\mathbb EZ=np_a=\Theta_K(1).
\]

Pairs of overlapping blocks contribute \(O(\mathbb EZ)\) to
\(\mathbb EZ^2\): a displacement \(j<a\) gives a union of size \(a+j\),
and \(n\sum_{j<a}p_{a+j}=O(np_a)\).  Disjoint pairs contribute
\(O((\mathbb EZ)^2)\).  Paley--Zygmund and the union bound therefore give a
constant \(\kappa_K>0\) such that

\[
\kappa_KN\le|\mathcal U|\le2^{1-K}N
\tag{5.6}
\]

for all sufficiently large \(m\).  Choose \(K\) so large that
\(2^{1-K}<\rho/2\).

#### Proof: the containment cut

Let \(\partial_q^+\mathcal U\) be the family of middle sets containing a
member of \(\mathcal U\).  Every \(R\in\mathcal U\) has

\[
\binom{m+q+1}{q}
\]

middle supersets.  Every member of \(\partial_q^+\mathcal U\) contains an
\(a\)-block and hence contains at least

\[
\binom{m-a}{q}
\]

members of \(\mathcal U\).  Double counting gives

\[
|\partial_q^+\mathcal U|
\le\Lambda_m|\mathcal U|,
\qquad
\Lambda_m=
\frac{\binom{m+q+1}{q}}{\binom{m-a}{q}}.
\tag{5.7}
\]

Since \(a=O(\log m)\) and \(q=x\sqrt m+O(1)\),

\[
\log\Lambda_m=x^2+o(1),
\qquad
\Lambda_m\longrightarrow R<c+1.
\tag{5.8}
\]

Also

\[
\frac WN\longrightarrow R,
\]

so if \(W=cN+d\), then \(d/N\to\rho\).  Since \(n\) is prime, every
nonempty proper subset orbit under cyclic translation has size \(n\).
Moreover \(n\mid W,N,d\), and \(\mathcal U\) is a union of such orbits.
By (5.6), \(|\mathcal U|<d\), so extend \(\mathcal U\) by whole cyclic
orbits to a family \(\mathcal H_q\) of size \(d\).  Put

\[
b_q=c\mathbf1+\mathbf1_{\mathcal H_q}.
\tag{5.9}
\]

Equations (5.6)--(5.8) give a constant \(\eta_x>0\) with

\[
b_q(\mathcal U)
=(c+1)|\mathcal U|
\ge|\partial_q^+\mathcal U|+\eta_xW.
\tag{5.10}
\]

For a cyclic-order column \(C\), let \(u_C\) count its rank-\(r\) intervals
in \(\mathcal U\), and let \(v_C\) count its middle intervals in
\(\partial_q^+\mathcal U\).  Every selected rank-\(r\) interval has exactly
\(q+1\) cyclic middle extensions, while a middle interval contains at most
\(q+1\) selected cyclic rank-\(r\) subintervals.  Hence

\[
u_C\le v_C.
\tag{5.11}
\]

If \(y\ge0\) and \(B_my=\mathbf1\), then

\[
(B_ry)(\mathcal U)
=\sum_Cy_Cu_C
\le\sum_Cy_Cv_C
=|\partial_q^+\mathcal U|.
\]

Together with (5.10), this proves (5.2).

#### Proof: lattice membership and negative mass

At every other depth choose an arbitrary cyclic-invariant high family of the
required remainder size.  Such a choice exists by taking whole cyclic
orbits.  Cyclic invariance makes every quota point-regular, with exactly the
wreath point margins.  Theorem 2.3, applied exactly as in the proof of
Corollary 2.4, therefore gives an integral signed
wreath vector realizing all \(b_s\) simultaneously.  This proves lattice
membership.

For any signed realization \(y\), the central and lower equations give

\[
\begin{aligned}
b_q(\mathcal U)-|\partial_q^+\mathcal U|
&=\sum_Cy_C(u_C-v_C)\\
&\le n\|y^-\|_1,
\end{aligned}
\]

because \(u_C-v_C\le0\) and \(0\le v_C-u_C\le n\).  Equation (5.10)
proves (5.3).  \(\square\)

An explicit form of the feasibility invariant is

\[
\boxed{
|\mathcal H_q\cap\mathcal U|
\le|\partial_q^+\mathcal U|-c|\mathcal U|}
\tag{5.12}
\]

for the high-cell family of every perfectly balanced fractional or integral
factor load.  The constructed \(\mathcal H_q\) violates (5.12) by
\(\Omega(W)\).

If a signed realization is written as an exact factor plus \(T\) kernel
moves whose negative degrees are at most \(D\), then

\[
T\ge\eta_x\frac{W}{nD}.
\tag{5.13}
\]

This last statement concerns a formal signed correction.  An actual Markov
path remains nonnegative and can never reach the prescribed target at all.

The target is lattice-outside-cone, not a semigroup hole: by definition a
semigroup hole must already belong to the rational cone.  The construction
does not obstruct mobile high cells, jointly chosen quotas, or a common
nested labelled resolution.

## 6. Exact signed dimension suspension and its positive obstruction

This section changes dimension.  Write \(B_r^{(m)}\) for cyclic
\(r\)-interval incidence on \(2m+1\) old labels, at every rank; ranks above
the middle are identified with complementary lower ranks when used below.
Choose oriented
representatives, add fresh labels \(x,y\), and index the old cyclic gaps by
\(a\in\mathbb Z_{2m+1}\).  Let \(E_{a,d}C\) insert \(x\) in gap \(a\) and
\(y\) in gap \(a+d\), and define

\[
P_d^{(m)}e_C
=\sum_{a\in\mathbb Z_{2m+1}}e_{E_{a,d}C}.
\tag{6.1}
\]

For \(1\le h\le m-2\), put

\[
\mathcal K_{m,h}
=\bigcap_{j=0}^{h-1}\ker B_{m-j}^{(m)}.
\tag{6.2}
\]

Thus \(\mathcal K_{m,h}\) consists of relations neutral on the top \(h\)
shadow ranks.

### Lemma 6.1 (exact gap-sector count)

Put \(n=2m+1\) and

\[
\chi_d(\ell)
=(\ell-d)_++(\ell-(n-d))_+.
\tag{6.3}
\]

For a new rank-\(r\) target \(T\),

\[
(B_r^{(m+1)}P_d^{(m)}e_C)_T
=
\begin{cases}
\chi_d(n-r+1)\,
1_{\{T\text{ is an old }r\text{-interval}\}},
&|T\cap\{x,y\}|=0,\\
\bigl(r-\chi_d(r-1)\bigr)\,
1_{\{T\setminus\{x,y\}
\text{ is an old }(r-1)\text{-interval}\}},
&|T\cap\{x,y\}|=1,\\
\chi_d(r-1)\,
1_{\{T\setminus\{x,y\}
\text{ is an old }(r-2)\text{-interval}\}},
&|T\cap\{x,y\}|=2.
\end{cases}
\tag{6.4}
\]

#### Proof

Fix the old interval underlying \(T\).  As the insertion phase \(a\)
varies, the permitted gaps form one consecutive cyclic gap interval.  The
number of directed gap pairs at separation \(d\) with both endpoints in an
interval of \(\ell\) gaps is exactly

\[
(\ell-d)_++(\ell-(n-d))_+.
\]

This gives the no-new and two-new sectors.  The one-new count is the total
\(r\) possible boundary phases minus the phases in which both new labels
occur, giving the middle line of (6.4).  \(\square\)

### Theorem 6.2 (all-window associated-graded suspension)

Suppose

\[
z\in\mathcal K_{m,h},
\qquad
g=B_{m-h}^{(m)}z\ne0.
\]

Then

\[
P_d^{(m)}z\in\mathcal K_{m+1,h}
\]

if and only if

\[
\boxed{m+1-h\le d\le m+h.}
\tag{6.5}
\]

For every allowed \(d\),

\[
\boxed{
B_{m+1-h}^{(m+1)}P_d^{(m)}z
=(m+1-h)(\iota_x+\iota_y)g.}
\tag{6.6}
\]

Consequently \(P_d^{(m)}\) induces an injective integer map

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal K_{m,h}/\mathcal K_{m,h+1}
\hookrightarrow
\mathcal K_{m+1,h}/\mathcal K_{m+1,h+1}.}
\tag{6.7}
\]

There are exactly \(2h\) directed allowed gaps, or \(h\) dihedral gap
types.

#### Proof

The last new row required to vanish is \(r=m-h+2\).  In (6.4), its no-new
and one-new sectors use old rows already annihilated by \(z\).  Its two-new
sector is

\[
\chi_d(m-h+1)\,\iota_{xy}g.
\]

The sectors are disjoint and \(g\ne0\), so neutrality is equivalent to

\[
\chi_d(m-h+1)=0.
\]

Both summands in (6.3) are nonnegative; the equality is precisely

\[
m-h+1\le d,
\qquad
m-h+1\le n-d,
\]

which is (6.5).  All higher new rows use only old neutral rows.

At the first active new row \(r=m-h+1\), the zero-new old row vanishes and
\(\chi_d(m-h)=0\).  Only the two one-new sectors survive, each with
coefficient \(m-h+1\), proving (6.6).  Their supports are disjoint, so the
map on the quotient is injective.  \(\square\)

This is an integer-lattice theorem.  It does not assert support feasibility.

### Theorem 6.3 (the equivariant positive gap cone misses packings)

Consider the nonnegative cone generated by the dihedral gap-orbit operators

\[
P_d^{(m)}+P_{n-d}^{(m)}.
\]

If a nonzero combination preserves the top-\(h\) class in Theorem 6.2, only
the central dihedral types

\[
d=m+1-h,m+2-h,\ldots,m
\tag{6.8}
\]

can occur.  Nevertheless, no such nonzero combination maps even one old
wreath to a new middle packing.

#### Proof

In the last neutral row, all leakage coefficients
\(\chi_d(m-h+1)\) are nonnegative and occupy the same two-new sector.
Therefore a nonnegative combination can have no cancellation there; every
supported gap must satisfy (6.5), which reduces modulo reflection to (6.8).

Let \(a_d=\min(d,n-d)\).  For an allowed gap,

\[
m+1-h\le a_d\le m.
\]

For every old middle interval \(S\), Lemma 6.1 at new middle rank gives

\[
(B_{m+1}^{(m+1)}P_d^{(m)}e_C)_{S\cup\{x\}}
=m+1-\chi_d(m)
=a_d+1
\ge m+2-h.
\tag{6.9}
\]

Thus a single new middle target is repeated within the image of one parent.
No nonzero nonnegative orbit combination is a packing.  \(\square\)

The associated-graded lattice suspends, but this natural positive equivariant
cone meets the partial-factor semigroup only at zero.

### Theorem 6.4 (iterated signal-capacity obstruction)

Fix \(h\), start at \(m_0\ge h+1\), and at each dimension \(j\) choose an
allowed gap

\[
j+1-h\le d_j\le j+h.
\]

For \(M>m_0\), put

\[
z_M=P_{d_{M-1}}^{(M-1)}\cdots P_{d_{m_0}}^{(m_0)}z.
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
B_{M-h}^{(M)}z_M
=\frac{(M-h)!}{(m_0-h)!}
J_{M-1}\cdots J_{m_0}
B_{m_0-h}^{(m_0)}z,}
\tag{6.10}
\]

where \(J_j=\iota_{x_j}+\iota_{y_j}\).  On the other hand, every middle
packing \(F\) in dimension \(M\) satisfies

\[
\boxed{
(B_{M-q}^{(M)}\mathbf1_F)_T
\le
L_{M,q}:=\left\lfloor
\frac1{q+1}\binom{M+q+1}{q}
\right\rfloor}
\tag{6.11}
\]

for every depth-\(q\) target \(T\).  Hence literal support-feasible
suspension of any nonzero integer first-active coefficient requires

\[
\boxed{
\frac{(M-h)!}{(m_0-h)!}\le L_{M,h}.}
\tag{6.12}
\]

If

\[
h=A\sqrt M+O(1),
\qquad
m_0=M-t,
\]

then, for every fixed \(A,\varepsilon>0\), (6.12) fails whenever

\[
\boxed{
\left(\frac12+\varepsilon\right)h\le t\le M-h-1}
\tag{6.13}
\]

and \(M\) is sufficiently large.

#### Proof

Iterate (6.6).  The scalar multiplier is

\[
\prod_{j=m_0}^{M-1}(j+1-h)
=\frac{(M-h)!}{(m_0-h)!},
\]

which proves (6.10).

For (6.11), one occurrence of a rank-\((M-q)\) target has exactly \(q+1\)
middle extensions in its wreath.  Occurrences in distinct rows of a middle
packing use disjoint middle extensions.  The total number of middle
supersets of the target is \(\binom{M+q+1}{q}\).  This proves (6.11), and
the difference of two packings has coordinate magnitude at most the same
bound.  Equation (6.12) follows from any initial nonzero integer
coefficient.

For the Gaussian estimate first put

\[
t_0=\left\lceil\left(\frac12+\varepsilon\right)h\right\rceil.
\]

Since \(t_0=O(\sqrt M)\),

\[
\log\frac{(M-h)!}{(M-t_0-h)!}
=t_0\log M+O_A(1).
\]

Meanwhile

\[
\log L_{M,h}
\le h\log\frac{e(M+h+1)}h
=\frac12h\log M+O_A(h).
\]

The excess \(\varepsilon h\log M\) dominates.  Thus (6.12) fails at
\(t_0\); the factorial ratio is increasing in \(t\), so it fails for every
larger admissible \(t\), proving (6.13).  \(\square\)

The obstruction concerns literal preservation of the amplified
associated-graded signal.  It does not rule out a phase-selected lift which
disperses or renormalizes the signal.

## 7. Exact remaining lemmas

The proved theorems leave two sharply separated positive gates.

### 7.1 Coherent small-move augmentation -- UNPROVED

The most direct Markov-basis replacement lemma is:

> **\(\mathrm{BDLM}_A\).**  For every fixed \(A>0\), there are
> \(D_m=e^{o(m)}\) and \(C_A<\infty\) such that every exact factor \(F\)
> satisfying
> \[
> \mathcal O_A(F)>C_AH_A\operatorname{Cat}_m
> \]
> admits an applicable support-feasible trade \(z\) with
> \(\deg z\le D_m\) and
> \[
> \mathcal O_A(F+z)<\mathcal O_A(F).
> \]

If \(\mathrm{BDLM}_A\) holds, strict descent in the finite factor fibre ends
at a factor with

\[
\mathcal O_A(F)\le C_AH_A\operatorname{Cat}_m=o(W),
\]

which proves fixed-window MWB.  Theorem 4.2 warns that such a descent can
require exponentially many moves; that does not affect its existential
conclusion.

Theorem 2.3 proves the abelianized analogue with degree two and *zero* final
overload.  What it does not prove is that the degree-two labels can be based
and ordered along one nonnegative path.  The Gaussian cone obstruction in
Theorem 5.1 shows that such a coherent theorem must choose the high cells
adaptively; it is false for arbitrary prescribed balanced quotas.

### 7.2 Positive phase-selected suspension -- UNPROVED

For dimension recursion, the exact remaining lemma is to replace the full
gap-orbit sum by one phase per changed wreath, chosen jointly across owners,
so that:

1. the lifted positive and negative sides are middle packings;
2. all required neutral shadow rows agree;
3. the first active row remains nonzero but is dispersed enough to obey
   (6.11); and
4. the negative side has one common exact-factor completion.

Theorems 6.2--6.4 prove that no equivariant nonnegative orbit sum or literal
signal-preserving iteration can supply this.  They do not rule out a
non-equivariant, owner-mixing, renormalized phase section.

Neither unproved lemma supplies a common balanced nested labelled
resolution.  Even after unlabelled MWB, the stronger labelled lane still
requires a common-flow retraction or a direct ownerwise construction.

## 8. Adversarial audit

The strongest positive and negative claims were checked independently.  The
following qualifications are essential.

1. **Actual degree-two edges.**  Every universal move (2.3) is a relabelled
   genuine size-two MSW factor edge.  The proof uses this family only for
   surjectivity.  It does not classify all degree-two edges.

2. **Abelianization is not connectivity.**  The isolated rectangle
   \(h_q=g-g'\) is generally not support-feasible; its same-sign wreaths may
   collide.  The two genuine generators can live at different factor
   vertices.  Equation (2.6) is an equality of global edge-label lattices,
   not of the reachable component from a fixed factor.

3. **Collateral ranks.**  The swap isolating rank \(m-q\) has exactly one
   collateral rank \(q\), or rank two for \(q=1\).  The condition
   \(m>2H+2\) puts every collateral action below the whole central window and
   prevents rank collisions.

4. **Signed balanced correction.**  Corollary 2.4 chooses quota families
   independently at different ranks.  The resulting vector may be negative
   and need not encode a nested owner flow.  Multicover suspension (2.11)
   does not desuspend to one factor.

5. **Lifted Graver scope.**  Squarefreeness and ownership-component packets
   are asserted for moves conformal to differences of exact-factor vertices.
   Arbitrary abstract Graver elements need not be squarefree or applicable.
   Theorem 3.1 gives no sub-factor degree bound.

6. **Bounded-degree obstruction.**  Theorem 4.2 is a speed lower bound
   conditional on an actual overload change.  It does not exhibit a bad
   factor, prove disconnectedness, or preclude an exponentially long path.

7. **Prescribed versus mobile quotas.**  Theorem 5.1 defeats one adversarial
   cyclic-invariant choice of high cells, even fractionally and even with
   unbounded moves.  MWB minimizes over the high cells jointly with the
   factor, so this is not a counterexample to MWB.  The prime-dimension
   subsequence is used to make every nontrivial cyclic orbit have size
   exactly \(n\).

8. **Cone terminology.**  The Gaussian target is in the integer lattice but
   outside the rational cone.  It is therefore not a semigroup hole.  The
   negative-mass lower bound concerns signed representations.

9. **Suspension scope.**  Theorem 6.2 is an associated-graded signed-lattice
   injection.  Theorem 6.3 concerns the explicitly defined nonnegative
   dihedral gap-orbit cone, not arbitrary non-equivariant insertions.

10. **Gaussian suspension range.**  In Theorem 6.4, \(h\) is fixed during
    the iteration and selected at the endpoint scale
    \(h=A\sqrt M+O(1)\).  The logarithmic estimate is proved first at
    \(t_0=\lceil(1/2+\varepsilon)h\rceil\) and extended to larger
    \(t\) by monotonicity; the suspension is defined only while
    \(t\le M-h-1\).

11. **No conjectural conclusion.**  No theorem here bounds the mobile global
    optimum \(\min_F\mathcal O_A(F)\), constructs a common nested labelled
    resolution, or proves the contiguous-OR conjecture.

Subject to these qualifications, every displayed theorem and estimate is
unconditional.  The only statements marked UNPROVED are
\(\mathrm{BDLM}_A\) and the positive phase-selected suspension lemma.
