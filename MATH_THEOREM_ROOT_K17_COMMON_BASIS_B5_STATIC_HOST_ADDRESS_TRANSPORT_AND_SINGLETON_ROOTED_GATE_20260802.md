# K17 common-basis `B_5` host, literal-address obstruction, and the singleton rooted gate

**Date:** 2026-08-02  
**Status:** exact finite theorem on the compressed-normal K17 face, plus an
unconditional pointwise-address obstruction.  One static target-footprint-

## 0. Outcome

There are three distinct statements, and this note does not conflate them.

1. The authenticated private-H K17 fixture has **zero** saturated `B_5 C10`
   suffix sockets on its fixed 18,646 hard suffixes.  Bottom relocation alone
   therefore cannot supply the actuator.
2. Rethreading the two representing matchings inside the **same**
   compressed-normal common-basis face does supply one complete 22-atom old
   module.  All 5,244 rows named as private-ticket shorts or endpoint hosts
   remain unchanged, and the complete lower/upper/tail/head resource footprint
   avoids every target in those rows.
3. Literal pointwise preservation of the co-middle address is impossible for
   every nonidentity fixed-suffix bottom relay.  Hence the remaining protected
   theorem must use an explicit internal address transport, not raw pointwise
   equality of the address name.

The final constructed module has one structural rooted opener.  Its target
edge is `(q,b)=(16,2)` and its complete untyped third-coordinate menu is the
singleton `{6}`.  Thus the protected rooted-Hall question on this host reduces
to one all-or-nothing occurrence-column test at `z=6`; there is no alternative
third coordinate on the fixed host.

## 4. Literal pointwise address is impossible

Fix one consecutive suffix

\[
                         H\subset U,qquad U-H=\{h\}.          \tag{4.1}
\]

For a legal bottom `L subset H`, the other middle of its Boolean interval is

\[
                              \chi(L)=L+h.          \tag{4.2}
\]

### Theorem 4.1 (pointwise co-middle obstruction)

The map `L -> chi(L)` is injective on the legal bottoms of a fixed suffix.
Consequently every nonidentity fixed-suffix bottom move changes the literal
co-middle address at every moved slot.  A saturated `C10` changes all five
addresses and its rooted opener `C6` changes all three.

#### Proof

Because `h` is outside `H` and `L subset H`,

\[
                              \chi(L)\cap H=L.       \tag{4.3}
\]

Thus `chi(L)=chi(L')` implies `L=L'`.  Every slot moved by a nonidentity
bottom matching has `L != L'`, so its address changes.  The primitive `C10`
and `C6` permutations move every point of their five- and three-slot shores.
\(\square\)

On the concrete module, indexing atoms by their common physical upper/root
gives the exact pointwise ledger

\[
\begin{array}{c|rrrr}
 &L&T&H&U\\ \hline
\text{changed among the eight moving roots}&8&8&0&0.
\end{array}                                             \tag{4.4}
\]

Therefore the strongest literal reading of “pointwise address preservation”
is refuted, independently of Hall expansion or common-basis feasibility.

### Corollary 4.2 (necessary replacement)

A valid protected mode theorem must do one of the following.

1. Declare the moving lower/tail names module-internal and prove an explicit
   interface transport under which every exterior address/history/reset/
   residence/source/compiler consumer is equivariant; or
2. prove that every exported column factors through data which omit the
   literal lower/tail name.

Four-row deck equality is not a substitute for either statement.  Raw
pointwise equality can remain required for the fixed head/upper suffix
columns, since those are unchanged in (4.4).

There is already a concrete warning in the owner data.  Let

\[
 \omega_\epsilon(v)=\operatorname{owner}_\epsilon(v)-\operatorname{root}(v)
                                                               \tag{4.5}
\]

be the one-coordinate owner increment in s7 phase `epsilon`.  On the five
`C10` slots, the phase pairs are

```text
(14,14), (9,9), (5,10), (15,14), (9,12),
```

and on the three opener slots they are

```text
(14,14), (4,4), (14,14).
```

Neither cycle is constant.  Hence this module is not clone-transparent if
the literal owner-increment pair is retained as a slot subcolumn.  This is a
conditional obstruction: the present artifacts do not identify (4.5) with
the complete protected signature, and an explicit coordinate transport may
act on it.

## 5. Rooted `C6`: Hall collapses to one protected test

The designated old opener atoms lie on physical rows

```text

```

\[
                         (q,b)=(16,2),\qquad z=6.    \tag{5.1}
\]

### Theorem 5.1 (exact structural menu)

On the constructed host, the complete untyped structural rooted menu through
the named target edge is exactly

\[
                             A^{\rm untyped}_{16,2}=\{6\}.     \tag{5.2}
\]

The three rows and every one of their four resources avoid the target set of
`P`.  Thus the one-target **uncoloured** Hall condition holds and has no
alternative representative.

This is not protected rooted Hall.  Define `A^cap_o` to contain a structural
third coordinate `z` only when there exist phase-specific occurrence
witnesses `w_0,w_1` using that same `z` such that:

1. the corrected endpoint aperture `o subset s` or `o subset t` holds;
2. the three slot signatures are clone-compatible in each phase; and
3. the complete exported columns agree under the declared low/tail transport,
   including state, reset, propagated history, residence, upper/source, and
   compiler data.

For the present host, protected rooted Hall is therefore exactly the single
unproved clause

\[
                                  6\in A^{\rm cap}_{16,2}.     \tag{5.3}
\]

There is no fallback `z` on this host.

For several opener targets, ordinary Hall on `o~z` is sufficient only after
the complete opener footprints have been proved private apart from the named
`z` resource.  Without that privacy, the exact formulation is a literal set
packing with one row for every shared occurrence resource; ordinary Hall is
then only necessary.

\[
  1748\quad\longrightarrow\quad 520\text{ in phase 0},

quantifiers in (5.3) allow different witnesses `w_0,w_1` in the two phases,
provided they live on the same structural host and use the same `z=6`.
