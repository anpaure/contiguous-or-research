# Recursive phase-synchronized `Q_8` associators retain the bottom-wire system

Date: 2026-07-26

Method: pure mathematics only.

## 0. Verdict

The phase-synchronized crossed `Q_8` associator is an exact local
frame-changing gate, but recursive composition of this gate does **not**
escape bottom-wire imprimitivity.  The obstruction is statewise and is
unaffected by owner-dependent choices.

For every bottom `Q_4` leaf put

\[
 H=\langle e_1+e_3,e_2+e_4\rangle
 \quad\hbox{and}\quad
 \mathcal W_4=\{\{1,3\},\{2,4\}\}.
\tag{0.1}
\]

For a network with `b` bottom leaves put

\[
 K_b=H^{\oplus b},
 \qquad
 \mathcal W_b=
 \{\{(a,1),(a,3)\},\{(a,2),(a,4)\}:1\le a\le b\}.
\tag{0.2}
\]

The weight-two words of \(K_b\) are exactly the incidence vectors of the
two-point blocks in \(\mathcal W_b\).  Every legal phase-synchronized `Q_8`
gate normalizes this set, and therefore so does every tensor, serial,
recursive, componentwise, or owner-dependent composition of such gates.
Thus, at every owner \(x\), its accumulated frame label \(\Gamma_x\) satisfies

\[
                         \Gamma_x\mathcal W_b=\mathcal W_b.
\tag{0.3}
\]

This is stronger than a common-component obstruction: the shore may vary
arbitrarily with `x`, and (0.3) still holds pointwise.

Consequently the requested outer cut condition fails.  Take `C` to be
the union of any half of the blocks of \(\mathcal W_b\).  It has coordinate
density `1/2`, while every physical paired direction of every accumulated
frame is contained in one side of `C`.  Hence every interval, of every
length `q`, has exactly zero cut-crossing directions, not `Omega(q)`.

The valid context-array decoder is compatible with this invariant rather
than an escape from it.  Its base kernel is the same `H`, all of its
coordinate corrections normalize \(\mathcal W_b\), and parallel recursion
retains the direct-sum kernel \(K_b\).  Its augmented trace injectivity can
therefore coexist with (0.3).

So there is no `Q_8+` network with the requested properties in the
recursive category generated by the named associator.  An escape needs a
new local gate whose relative coordinate permutation does not normalize
\(K_b\), equivalently one which splits a bottom wire.  Proposition 3.2 of
the source theorem proves that no phase-only correction of the present
`Q_4` seed can do this while retaining isometric cycles.

## 1. The phase kernel remembers the bottom wires exactly

Let

\[
 H=\langle1010,0101\rangle
   =\{0000,1010,0101,1111\}\le\mathbb F_2^4.
\tag{1.1}
\]

The common-phase `Q_4` direction field is constant on the cosets of `H`.
Define two coordinates to be phase-equivalent when their translations on
`Q_4/H` agree.  Then

\[
 i\sim j
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 e_i+e_j\in H
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \{i,j\}\in\mathcal W_4.
\tag{1.2}
\]

Indeed the nonzero words of `H` have supports

\[
                         13,\quad24,\quad1234,
\tag{1.3}
\]

so its weight-two words are precisely `13` and `24`.

The direct-sum version is equally rigid.

### Lemma 1.1 (weight-two reconstruction)

For \(K_b=H^{\oplus b}\), the weight-two words of \(K_b\) are precisely

\[
                         e_i+e_j
       \qquad(\{i,j\}\in\mathcal W_b).
\tag{1.4}
\]

Consequently a coordinate permutation \(g\) normalizes \(K_b\) if and only
if it permutes the blocks of \(\mathcal W_b\).  In particular

\[
 N_{S_{4b}}(K_b)
 =\operatorname {Aut}(\mathcal W_b)
 \cong S_2\wr S_{2b}.
\tag{1.5}
\]

#### Proof

Inside one summand the assertion follows from (1.3).  A word using two
nonzero summands has weight at least four, since every nonzero word of
`H` has weight at least two.  This proves (1.4).

If \(gK_b=K_b\), then \(g\) preserves Hamming weight and hence permutes the
weight-two supports (1.4).  Conversely, a permutation of the two-point
blocks, with arbitrary swaps inside them, permutes the generators in
(1.4) and therefore normalizes their span \(K_b\).  This proves (1.5).
\(\square\)

Thus the bottom-wire partition is not auxiliary metadata.  It is
canonically recoverable from the phase kernel itself.

## 2. One crossed `Q_8` gate normalizes the kernel

Write a `Q_8` state as \((u,v)\in Q_4^L\times Q_4^R\).  The corrected gate
has relative direction involution

\[
 \Xi(Li)=R(Si),\qquad \Xi(Ri)=L(Si).
\tag{2.1}
\]

The classification in Proposition 3.2 of
`MATH_THEOREM_PHASE_SYNCHRONIZED_CROSSED_Q8_ASSOCIATOR_20260726.md` says
that the forced partner has isometric `C_16` components at every owner if
and only if

\[
 S\in S_2\wr S_2=\operatorname {Aut}(\mathcal W_4).
\tag{2.2}
\]

Therefore \(SH=H\).  Moreover \(\Xi\) sends a complete left wire to a
complete right wire:

\[
                  \Xi(LW)=R(SW)\qquad(W\in\mathcal W_4),
\tag{2.3}
\]

and similarly with left and right interchanged.  Hence

\[
 \Xi(H_L\oplus H_R)=H_L\oplus H_R,
 \qquad
 \Xi\mathcal W_8=\mathcal W_8.
\tag{2.4}
\]

There is also a state-level version.  If
\(k=(k_L,k_R)\in H_L\oplus H_R\),
then `k_L,k_R` have even weight, so translating `(u,v)` by `k` does not
change the parity branch of either crossed factor.  The parent direction
field is constant on `H`-cosets.  Inspection of the two formulas

\[
 P_0(u,v)=
 \begin{cases}(G(u),v),&\epsilon=0,\\(u,G(v)),&\epsilon=1,
 \end{cases}
\tag{2.5}
\]

and

\[
 P_1(u,v)=
 \begin{cases}(u,v+e_{S\delta(u)}),&\epsilon=0,\\
 (u+e_{S\delta(v)},v),&\epsilon=1
 \end{cases}
\tag{2.6}
\]

therefore gives

\[
 \delta_{P_j}(z+k)=\delta_{P_j}(z)
 \qquad(j=0,1).                                    \tag{2.7}
\]

So both the relative frame and the actual outgoing direction field retain
the direct-sum phase kernel.

## 3. Recursive and owner-dependent closure

We make the closure statement independent of how exact owner components
are selected.

Call a frame-labelled construction `Q_8`-generated if every elementary
relative frame label is one of the following:

1. a legal crossed `Q_8` involution (2.1) on two child blocks;
2. a coordinate relabelling which carries every already constructed
   child phase kernel to itself;
3. a disjoint/direct-sum placement of earlier labels; or
4. a product of earlier labels, with the chosen product allowed to depend
   arbitrarily on the current owner, context, interaction component, or
   phase.

This includes the usual parallel recursion, serial switch networks, and
componentwise shore selections.  It is deliberately more permissive than
the presently proved exact-factor compilers.

### Theorem 3.1 (statewise bottom-wire invariant)

For every `Q_8`-generated construction on `b` bottom leaves and every
state/context `omega`, its accumulated relative frame label satisfies

\[
                         \Gamma_\omega\in N_{S_{4b}}(K_b).
\tag{3.1}
\]

Equivalently, \(\Gamma_\omega\mathcal W_b=\mathcal W_b\) for every
\(\omega\).

#### Proof

A legal elementary `Q_8` involution lies in the normalizer by (2.4).
A permitted relabelling lies there by definition.  Direct sums of
normalizers normalize the direct-sum kernel.  Products remain in a
normalizer because it is a group.  These statements hold separately for
each value of `omega`; no regularity of the owner-dependent selector is
used.  Lemma 1.1 gives the equivalent wire formulation. \(\square\)

This theorem distinguishes two notions which can otherwise be conflated.
The induced action on the set of wires may be transitive, highly mixing,
or even expanding.  Nevertheless every individual label preserves the
*same set of two-point physical wires*.  Quotient mixing is not a new
physical pair frame.

### Corollary 3.2 (exact positive-density zero cut)

Let \(C\) be the union of any \(b\) blocks of
\(\mathcal W_b\).  Then

\[
                         |C|=2b={1\over2}(4b).
\tag{3.2}
\]

For every state/context \(\omega\), every pair in the transported frame
\(\Gamma_\omega\mathcal W_b\) is contained either in \(C\) or in its
complement.
Thus the number of physical frame directions crossing
\(C\mid C^c\) is zero.  The same is true in every consecutive
\(q\)-window.

#### Proof

By Theorem 3.1 the transported frame is exactly \(\mathcal W_b\).  The set
\(C\) is a union of its blocks. \(\square\)

The conclusion is stronger than the negation of the desired condition:
for this half-density cut the crossing count is zero for every `q`, not
merely `o(q)`.

Here “crossing direction” has the Hall-relevant meaning: a two-coordinate
direction of the paired/Johnson physical frame has one endpoint on each
side of the coordinate cut.  Relative-permutation arcs between *wire
labels* may cross a quotient cut, but those arcs are controller labels,
not physical deletion/addition directions.  Counting them would not alter
the fixed-frame target ledger.

There is a second, literal way to state the same conclusion after the
paired-order lift.  Write the physical coordinates above coarse direction
\(i\) as

\[
                         P_i=\{a_i,b_i\}.
\tag{3.3}
\]

Every coarse frame label \(\Gamma_\omega\) induces
\(P_i\mapsto P_{\Gamma_\omega i}\), so the unordered physical matching

\[
                         \mathcal P=\{P_i:i\in[4b]\}
\tag{3.4}
\]

is fixed statewise.  If \(C_{\rm phys}\) is the union of any half of the
pairs \(P_i\), no completed paired direction straddles
\(C_{\rm phys}\mid C_{\rm phys}^c\).  Thus the no-go holds both for the
coarse bottom-wire cut and for the literal physical perfect-matching cut.

## 4. Exact ownership and isometry do not remove the invariant

The obstruction is not caused by a failure of the local factor axioms.
For one `Q_8` gate:

* `P_0` is a neighbour permutation and its components are isometric
  `C_16`'s;
* the phase-synchronized `P_1` is also a neighbour permutation with
  isometric `C_16` components; and
* the same-owner relation is exactly
  \(\delta_{P_1}(z)=\Xi\delta_{P_0}(z)\).

The parity-alternating product of an isometric `C_(2h)` factor is an exact
isometric `C_(4h)` factor, since two parent steps advance both children
once and the first `2h` child directions form a permutation.  Hence the
standard recursive rows have an exact owner partition and isometric
cycles at every scale.

If two exact factors are switched only along complete interaction
components and the switch is known to retain indegree and outdegree one,
the resulting owner partition is again exact.  But every shore label in
such a switch satisfies (3.1), so arbitrary component choices still obey
the statewise invariant.  Exact ownership can choose among elements of
the normalizer; it cannot turn their product into a non-normalizer.

Thus the three requested properties have the following status in the
named recursive category:

\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
\text{exact owner partition}&\text{available},\\
\text{isometric cycles}&\text{available},\\
\Omega(q)\text{ physical directions across every positive-density cut}
  &\text{impossible by Corollary 3.2}.
\end{array}
\tag{4.1}
\]

## 5. Compatibility audit for the valid context-array decoder

The valid recursive context array starts on `Q_4` with

\[
 K_{\rm dec}=\langle1111,0101\rangle.
\tag{5.1}
\]

But

\[
 \langle1111,0101\rangle
 =\langle1010,0101\rangle=H,                       \tag{5.2}
\]

so the decoder kernel is exactly the phase kernel used above.  Its base
coordinate permutation

\[
                         S_{\rm dec}=(1\ 4\ 3\ 2)
\tag{5.3}
\]

interchanges the two wires `13` and `24`, and hence normalizes `H`.
For its base direction array

\[
                         d_{4,p}(x)=\delta(S_{\rm dec}p+x)
\tag{5.4}
\]

one consequently has

\[
 d_{4,p}(x+k)=d_{4,p}(x),\qquad
 d_{4,p+k}(x)=d_{4,p}(x)\qquad(k\in H),            \tag{5.5}
\]

where the second equality uses \(S_{\rm dec}H=H\).

At a recursive split the selected child depends on the parity of the
current state.  Every word of \(K_b\) has even weight in every bottom leaf,
so this branch is unchanged by \(K_b\).  Induction gives the same direct-sum
kernel for every row and column direction field of the valid array.

The later controlled-`Q_8` decoder is equally consistent with this
ledger.  Its erasure mate permutation is a legal crossed permutation,
so it normalizes \(K_b\); the bottom-block puncture argument and the
round-robin visit order recover the missing bits from exterior data.
That recovery proves augmented-code injectivity.  It never asserts that
the physical wire system has changed.

There are two important scope boundaries.

1. The phase-synchronized pair in the source theorem is only an outgoing
   same-owner certificate; no incoming relation is proved.  It cannot be
   inserted into a bidirectional recursive decoder merely by iteration.
2. The independently valid context-array recursion proves its column
   bijections from the branchwise formulas for the whole array.  Replacing
   a bottom leaf by a conjugate with a different kernel is not covered by
   that proof.

Therefore the decoder neither invalidates Theorem 3.1 nor supplies an
unproved route around it.

### Proposition 5.1 (common-decoder conjugacy criterion)

Let \(g\in S_4\), and conjugate the base direction field by

\[
                 \delta^g(y)=g\,\delta(g^{-1}y).
\tag{5.6}
\]

The direction fibres of \(\delta^g\) are cosets of \(gH\).  Hence this
conjugate has the same phase quotient and the same coset-based bottom
decoder as \(\delta\) if and only if \(gH=H\), equivalently
\(g\in\operatorname {Aut}(\mathcal W_4)\).

#### Proof

The fibres of \(\delta\) are the four cosets of \(H\).  Applying \(g\)
to vertices carries them to the four cosets of \(gH\).  Equality of the
phase partitions is therefore equivalent to \(gH=H\).  Lemma 1.1 with
one leaf identifies this normalizer with the wire automorphism group.
\(\square\)

Thus arbitrary coordinate-conjugate copies of the `Q_8` gate can certainly
have different kernels, but they are not simultaneous inputs to the
*valid existing* context-array decoder.  Combining them would require a
new shore-labelled decoder and a new exact component coupling; it is
exactly the escape gate left open here, not a recursive consequence of
the current certificate.

## 6. Fixed-frame Hall consequence

In the coordinate-disjoint paired lift, (3.4) leaves one common
perfect-matching frame.  For completeness, the exact lower-rank type
ledger for a matching of `m` pairs is

\[
 V_f={m!\over f!^2(m-2f)!}\,2^{m-2f},
\qquad
 T_{f,q}={m!\over f!(f+q)!(m-2f-q)!}\,2^{m-2f-q}.
\tag{6.1}
\]

Here \(V_f\) is the number of middle owners of native pair type \(f\), and
\(T_{f,q}\) is the number of rank-\((m-q)\) targets in the corresponding
channel.  Any factor internal to this frame misses at least

\[
                         D_{m,q}=\sum_f(T_{f,q}-V_f)_+.
\tag{6.2}
\]

For \(q=A\sqrt m+o(\sqrt m)\), the standard local central-limit evaluation
is

\[
 {D_{m,q}\over\binom{2m}{m}}
 \longrightarrow
 e^{-A^2}\Phi(A/2)-\Phi(-3A/2)>0.                  \tag{6.3}
\]

Complementation gives the same upper obstruction.  Thus the statewise
wire invariant does not merely block a proposed expansion proof; in the
usual paired literalization it restores the known positive Gaussian
pair-type deficit.

## 7. Exact escape boundary

For this route, a genuinely new generator must satisfy

\[
                         gK_b\ne K_b.
\tag{7.1}
\]

By Lemma 1.1 this is equivalent to sending at least one bottom wire to a
two-set which is not a bottom wire.  There are only three ways to obtain
such a generator.

1. Use a phase correction \(S\) outside \(S_2\wr S_2\).  Proposition 3.2 of
   the `Q_8` theorem rules this out for the present phase-only gate: some
   owner then has a short phase orbit and repeats a direction before all
   eight directions are used, so the cycle is not isometric.
2. Overlay conjugate bottom factors having different phase kernels.  This
   may generate a larger group, but exact owner components and one common
   context-array decoder must then be proved anew; neither follows from
   the named associator.
3. Introduce an exterior-moving `Q_8+` atom whose physical transition
   itself splits a bottom wire and whose incoming/outgoing decoder is
   certified directly.

Hence recursive use of the existing phase-synchronized `Q_8` associator
is closed.  The next constructive gate is precisely a different-kernel,
wire-splitting local factor, not a deeper network of the same gate.
