# Early-scale port hierarchies: a full-profile PCap theorem and two exact obstructions

Date: 2026-07-26

Method: pure mathematics only. No computation, finite search, solver, or
web input is used.

## 0. Decision

Let \(p=2m+1\) be an odd prime.  The proposed early-scale strategy chooses
\(s\) minimally with

\[
                         d_s:=\operatorname {Cat}_s\ge p.       \tag{0.1}
\]

Then

\[
                         1\le \theta_s:=d_s/p
             <4-\frac6{s+1}<4,                                \tag{0.2}
\]

so four abstract bins of capacity \(p\) could hold one size-\(s\)
Catalan fibre.  This numerical observation is correct.

It does not give a recursive port construction.  Two exact obstructions
close the naive hierarchy.

1. **Coordinate-component moves have only two target cells.**  Every
   component switch generated by the flaw-preserving swaps

   \[
                         s_i=(2i\ 2i+1)                         \tag{0.3}
   \]

   preserves target-orbit mass under the Dyck automorphism group.  The
   persistent singleton target lies in an orbit of size at most two.
   Thus the entire \(s_i\)-menu is a two-bin router, not a four-bin
   router.  At the first crossing (0.1), it cannot even make both bins
   cap-safe unless

   \[
                         \theta_s\le
                         \frac{8s-4}{5s-1}
                         =\frac85+O(s^{-1}).                    \tag{0.4}
   \]

   The Catalan overshoot is not uniformly in this range.

2. **A closed port factor regenerates its own full-hole fibre.**  In any
   size-\(t\) \(D_t\)-port factor, every rooted path starts at \(P\) and
   ends at its complement.  Consequently its full-path intersection is
   exactly the exterior set \(O\), independently of every internal
   reconfiguration.  Hence all \(\operatorname {Cat}_t\) rows again form
   one common full-hole fibre.  Splitting a strict descendant at scale
   \(s<t\) does not split this later fibre.  A size-\(t\) fibre can only
   be altered from a size-\(t+1\) parent which changes one boundary of
   the nested size-\(t\) window.

Therefore a one-time early split cannot prevent hereditary plateaux from
reappearing.  A surviving construction must supply a coherent parent
action at every relevant scale, using port factors outside the coordinate
automorphism orbit, and must control the full affected-window profiles.

Section 4 gives an exact theorem-quality sufficient PCap statement for
such a hierarchy.  Sections 5--7 show why the present early-scale data do
not satisfy it.  No coefficient-one result is claimed.

## 1. Port factors and the full-hole target

Let \(J\) have size \(2t\), let \(O\) be a fixed exterior set, and let
\(D_t\subseteq\binom Jt\) be the inherited Dyck ports.  A rooted
\(D_t\)-port factor is a partition of the middle-levels inclusion graph
into paths

\[
 P=X_0\subset Y_0\supset X_1\subset\cdots
   \subset Y_{t-1}\supset X_t=J\setminus P,
 \qquad P\in D_t.                                      \tag{1.1}
\]

After installation in the outer context, its state path is

\[
                         O\cup X_0,\ldots,O\cup X_t.    \tag{1.2}
\]

### Theorem 1.1 (full-hole regeneration)

For every row of every rooted port factor (1.1),

\[
                         \bigcap_{a=0}^{t}(O\cup X_a)=O. \tag{1.3}
\]

Consequently the \(\operatorname {Cat}_t\) full-hole occurrences of the
local factor have one common target \(O\).  This remains true after any
sequence of substitutions strictly inside the hole which leaves its two
ports fixed.

#### Proof

Every coordinate of \(O\) is present in every state.  If \(x\in J\),
then exactly one of the complementary endpoints \(X_0=P\) and
\(X_t=J\setminus P\) omits \(x\).  Hence no coordinate of \(J\) belongs
to the intersection.  This proves (1.3), independently of the internal
states. \(\square\)

### Corollary 1.2 (no hereditary immunity from an early split)

Suppose a size-\(s\) child window is split into several targets by a legal
size-\(s+1\) parent substitution.  Embed that construction inside a
larger rooted size-\(t\) hole, \(t>s\), without changing the two outer
ports of the size-\(t\) hole.  Its full size-\(t\) window still has target
\(O\) on every one of its \(\operatorname {Cat}_t\) rows.

Thus a later hereditary fibre is not the union of the target bins created
at scale \(s\); the larger complementary endpoints erase every internal
coordinate label.  To split the size-\(t\) window one must act one level
above it, in a size-\(t+1\) parent. \(\square\)

This is the exact obstruction to the phrase “split before the fibre grows.”
The fibre at a larger scale is a new full-port fibre and is regenerated by
the port condition itself.

## 2. Exact coordinate-component packets

Let \(F_t\) be the canonical rooted MSW factor on
\(J=[2t]\), and let

\[
                         s_i=(2i\ 2i+1),qquad1\le i<t. \tag{2.1}
\]

Each \(s_i\) preserves every Chung--Feller layer and the Dyck port family.
Hence the ownership overlay of \(F_t\) and \(s_iF_t\), using both
\(X\)-state and adjacent-union \(Y\)-tokens, decomposes into independently
switchable rooted components.

Let \(\kappa_a\) be the owner permutation induced by \(s_i\) on token
phase \(a\), and reindex the conjugate rows by their inherited ports.  Put

\[
                         f_a=\kappa_a\kappa_0^{-1}.       \tag{2.2}
\]

### Proposition 2.1 (phase-owner orbit normal form)

The common root blocks on the two shores of the ownership overlay are
exactly the orbits of

\[
                         \mathfrak H_i=langle f_a:
                         a\text{ an }X\text{ or }Y
                         \text{ token phase}\rangle.     \tag{2.3}
\]

#### Proof

The phase-zero token joins the two rows having the same inherited root;
contract these matching edges.  At phase \(a\), formula (2.2) gives an
edge between roots \(P\) and \(f_aP\).  All overlay edges arise this
way.  Connectedness after contraction is therefore orbit equivalence
under (2.3). \(\square\)

For \(s_1=(2\ 3)\), the orbit blocks are known exactly.  For
\(0\le j\le t-2\) and \(R\in D_{t-j-2}\), put

\[
 \mathcal A_j=
 \{1u0:u\in D_{j+1}\}
 \mathbin{\dot\cup}
 \{10\,1v0:v\in D_j\},                                \tag{2.4}
\]

\[
                         \mathcal K_{j,R}=\{AR:A\in\mathcal A_j\}.
                                                                  \tag{2.5}
\]

Then these are exactly the component root blocks, with

\[
 |\mathcal K_{j,R}|=k_j=\operatorname {Cat}_j+operatorname {Cat}_{j+1},
 \qquad
 \#\{R\}=\operatorname {Cat}_{t-j-2}.                 \tag{2.6}
\]

Every complete shore choice is integral, exact, and port-valid.  The
bounded-\(j\) components have shore size \(o(p)\) at the logarithmic
scale and carry

\[
                         \left(\frac38+o(1)\right)
                         \operatorname {Cat}_t          \tag{2.7}
\]

roots.  This is a genuine positive-density first split.

## 3. The marked action collapses to one target pair

Now use a local parent of semilength \(t+1\) to inspect its distinguished
size-\(t\) child window.  Canonically the roots \(10v\),
\(v\in D_t\), give

\[
                         d=\operatorname {Cat}_t        \tag{3.1}
\]

occurrences at local target \(2\).  The adjacent boundary class at target
\(3\) has

\[
                         e=\operatorname {Cat}_{t-1}.   \tag{3.2}
\]

For a component \((j,R)\) in the parent factor, the two root arms have
canonical targets \(2\) and \(z_j=2j+4\).  The coordinate swap pairs
exactly \(\operatorname {Cat}_j\) roots between those arms.  Switching
the component has the marked ledger

\[
                         \boxed{
                         \operatorname {Cat}_j(e_3-e_2).} \tag{3.3}
\]

The apparent intermediate target \(z_j\) is removed and refilled with
equal multiplicity.  Thus different values of \(j\) do not create
different marked output bins.

This is a special case of a general orbit invariant.

Let

\[
 \Gamma_{t+1}=\langle(2\ 3),(4\ 5),\ldots,(2t\ 2t+1)\rangle. \tag{3.4}
\]

### Theorem 3.1 (two-cell coordinate-orbit obstruction)

Along every sequence in which a current rooted factor is compared with a
\(g\)-conjugate, \(g\in\Gamma_{t+1}\), and complete ownership components
are switched, the total load on every \(\Gamma_{t+1}\)-orbit of targets
is invariant.

In particular \(\{2,3\}\) is one target orbit, and the distinguished
subprofile always satisfies

\[
                         \mu(2)+\mu(3)=d+e.             \tag{3.5}
\]

Hence

\[
 \max\{\mu(2),\mu(3)\}\ge\frac{d+e}{2},              \tag{3.6}
\]

and its cap-\(p\) overload obeys

\[
 \boxed{
 (\mu(2)-p)_++(\mu(3)-p)_+
 \ge(d+e-2p)_+.}                                      \tag{3.7}
\]

#### Proof

For an equivariant row contribution \(v(C;T)\) and a target orbit \(O\),

\[
 \sum_{T\in O}v(gC;T)
 =\sum_{T\in O}v(C;g^{-1}T)
 =\sum_{T\in O}v(C;T).                                \tag{3.8}
\]

A component switch replaces a packet of rows by their \(g\)-images, so
(3.8) proves orbit-mass invariance.  Equations (3.5)--(3.6) follow from
the canonical boundary Catalan counts.  Finally

\[
                         (x-p)_++(y-p)_+
                         \ge(x+y-2p)_+
\]

gives (3.7). \(\square\)

Using

\[
 \frac e d=\frac{t+1}{2(2t-1)},                       \tag{3.9}
\]

zero overload in (3.7) is possible only if

\[
 \theta_t:=\frac d p
 \le\frac{2}{1+e/d}
 =\frac{8t-4}{5t-1}.                                  \tag{3.10}
\]

At the early crossing (0.1), \(1\le\theta_s<4\), but there is no
uniform reason for (3.10).  If \(\theta_s>8/5+o(1)\), the complete
coordinate-component menu is already incapable of producing cap-safe
marked loads.

At the fatal scale \(d\ge4p\), (3.7) is at least \(2p+e\).  Thus six
purported \(5/8\)--\(3/8\) stages do not produce six independent binary
splits.  They remain inside the one two-cell orbit.

The obstruction is specific to coordinate-conjugate components.  A rooted
path factor outside \(\Gamma_{t+1}\) can change these orbit totals; the
known finite \(D_4\)-port factor with a pair-2-to-pair-3 first-insertion
effect demonstrates that no invariant of the entire anchored fibre is
being asserted.

## 4. A theorem-quality full-profile sufficient statement

The correct recursive object is a product-compatible atlas of **full
affected-window profiles**, not a tree of marked target labels.

Fix a depth window \(1\le q\le H\) in the global
\(p=2m+1\)-coordinate factor.  Let

\[
                         \mathcal V_q=\binom{[p]}{m-q},
 \qquad N_q=|\mathcal V_q|,
 \qquad \Delta_q=W-N_q.                               \tag{4.1}
\]

Group every collection of recursive substitutions coupled by some
serviced window into one joint atom \(A\).  Let \(\mathscr L_A\) be a
finite library of jointly legal assignments of rooted port factors inside
that atom.  Require **product compatibility**: every choice of one member
from each \(\mathscr L_A\) is one integral exact global middle factor.

Let \(\beta_q\) be the histogram of unaffected windows, and for
\(G\in\mathscr L_A\) let

\[
                         u_{A,G,q}\in\mathbb Z_{ge0}^{\mathcal V_q}
                                                                  \tag{4.2}
\]

be the histogram of every depth-\(q\) window assigned to \(A\), including
both crossing collars and every row affected at more than one recursive
level.  Then every integral library choice has

\[
                         \mu_q=\beta_q+sum_Au_{A,G_A,q}. \tag{4.3}
\]

Choose one probability distribution \(\pi_A\) on each library, common to
all depths, and put

\[
 \bar\mu_q=\beta_q+
       \sum_A\mathbb E_{G\sim\pi_A}u_{A,G,q},          \tag{4.4}
\]

\[
 V_q(S)=\sum_A\operatorname {Var}_{G\sim\pi_A}
                              u_{A,G,q}(S).             \tag{4.5}
\]

### Theorem 4.1 (coherent full-profile PCap criterion)

Suppose, for some \(\varepsilon=\varepsilon_m\to0\), the same
distributions satisfy

\[
 \sum_{q\le H}\bigl(K_p(\bar\mu_q)-\Delta_q\bigr)_+
 \le\varepsilon W,                                    \tag{4.6}
\]

and

\[
 \sum_{q\le H}\sum_{S\in\mathcal V_q}\sqrt{V_q(S)}
 \le\varepsilon W.                                    \tag{4.7}
\]

Then some integral, port-valid library choice gives one exact seed \(F_*\)
with

\[
                         \boxed{
                         \operatorname {PCap}_H(F_*)
                         \le\frac32\varepsilon W.}     \tag{4.8}
\]

The mean condition (4.6) is equivalently the fractional-weight inequality

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\max_{0\le\gamma_q\le1}\Bigg\{&
 \sum_{q\le H}
 \left[
  \langle\gamma_q,\beta_q-p\mathbf1\rangle
  -\Delta_q\|\gamma_q\|_\infty
 \right]\\
 &+\sum_A\min_{G\in\mathscr L_A}
       \sum_{q\le H}\langle\gamma_q,u_{A,G,q}\rangle
\Bigg\}\le\varepsilon W.                             \tag{4.9}
\end{aligned}}
\]

#### Proof

For a nonnegative real vector \(x\),

\[
 (K_p(x)-\Delta)_+
 =\max_{0\le\gamma\le1}
 \left[
  \langle\gamma,x-p\mathbf1\rangle
  -\Delta\|\gamma\|_\infty
 \right].                                             \tag{4.10}
\]

Apply (4.10) at every depth and use finite-dimensional minimax.  For a
fixed family \((\gamma_q)\), one atom must choose one common member at
all depths, giving the final minimum in (4.9).  Thus (4.6) and (4.9) are
equivalent.

Select the atom states independently.  Let
\(Y_q=\mu_q-\bar\mu_q\).  Both loads have total mass \(W\), so

\[
 K_p(\bar\mu_q+Y_q)-K_p(\bar\mu_q)
 \le\sum_SY_q(S)_+=\frac12\|Y_q\|_1.                  \tag{4.11}
\]

Subtracting \(\Delta_q\) and taking a positive part preserves this
one-sided bound.  Moreover

\[
                         \mathbb E|Y_q(S)|\le\sqrt{V_q(S)}.
\]

Equations (4.6)--(4.7) show that the expected PCap is at most
\((3/2)\varepsilon W\); one integral outcome is no worse.  Product
compatibility makes that outcome one exact factor. \(\square\)

Theorem 4.1 is only a seed theorem.  A coefficient-one row-power route
still needs one common exact-middle legal exponent map whose actual hole
count exceeds this PCap floor by \(o(W)\), or else a direct cap-one/hole
version of (4.6)--(4.9).

## 5. Why four marked bins do not imply Theorem 4.1

The following three failures are logically independent.

### 5.1 The \(s_i\)-menu does not supply four bins

Theorem 3.1 shows that every marked piece stays in the orbit
\(\{2,3\}\).  Distinct values of \(j\), distinct ownership components,
and distinct even-pair transpositions do not enlarge the orbit containing
target \(2\); the Dyck automorphism group is the direct product of its
two-point coordinate orbits.  Thus the premise of four independent local
destinations is false for this menu.

### 5.2 Component partitions are not hereditary

Even the exact component sizes (2.6) hold for the canonical comparison
\(F_t\) versus \(s_1F_t\), not after a partial component switch.  Distinct
coordinate component partitions are not laminar.  Already on the five
roots of \(D_3\), the \(s_1=(2\ 3)\) blocks are

\[
                         \{T_3,T_4\},\qquad
                         \{T_1,T_2,T_5\},              \tag{5.1}
\]

whereas the \(s_2=(4\ 5)\) blocks are

\[
                         \{T_4,T_5\},\qquad
                         \{T_1,T_2,T_3\}.              \tag{5.2}
\]

All four intersections between the two partitions are nonempty.  Hence no
nontrivial second-generation component is contained in one first-generation
target packet.  Recomputing the overlay preserves exactness and ports, but
it supplies no hereditary \(3/8\)--\(5/8\) law.

### 5.3 Full-profile collateral is uncontrolled

A component of shore size \(k\le p\) contributes at most \(k\) copies of
one fixed target in one shore, because one cyclic row has distinct intervals
at a fixed depth.  This useful pointwise fact does not prove (4.6).

A window meeting substitutions at two recursive levels depends jointly on
both choices.  After overlap closure, its joint atom may contain a
macroscopic fraction of the Catalan fibre.  The positive crossing-collar
targets can refill every marked drain.  Neither the port ledgers nor the
component-size bound gives a sign for the fractional tests in (4.9), and a
different choice may minimize different depths.  Thus marked packet size
\(\le p\) is not a full-profile PCap theorem.

## 6. The hereditary PCap obstruction survives early-only rewiring

Let \(r\) be minimal with

\[
                         d=\operatorname {Cat}_r\ge4p.  \tag{6.1}
\]

Then \(r=\Theta(\log p)\) and \(4p\le d<16p\).  Suppose an early-scale
hierarchy changes only strict descendants of each aligned size-\(r\) hole
and leaves that hole's two ports fixed.  By Theorem 1.1, every such hole
still supplies \(d\) full-hole occurrences with one common target.

For every boundary-served depth \(r\le q\le m/2\), one rooted occurrence
belongs to at most two of these context groups.  Hence the usual incidence
count gives

\[
                         K_q(F)\ge
                         H_{m,r}\left(\frac d2-p\right), \tag{6.2}
\]

where

\[
                         H_{m,r}=\frac12
                         \binom{2(m-r)}{m-r}.            \tag{6.3}
\]

Consequently, for

\[
                         Q=\left\lfloor\frac{p^{1/4}}4\right\rfloor,
                                                                  \tag{6.4}
\]

\[
 \operatorname {PCap}_Q(F)
 \ge\sum_{q=r}^{Q}
 \left[
  H_{m,r}\left(\frac d2-p\right)-(W-N_q)
 \right]_+.                                            \tag{6.5}
\]

Writing \(\theta=d/p\), the duplicate-budget subtraction is negligible
uniformly on this range, and the established Catalan asymptotics give

\[
 \boxed{
 \operatorname {PCap}_Q(F)
 \ge
 \left(\frac{\theta-2}{32\theta\sqrt\pi}+o(1)\right)
 \frac{Wp^{1/4}}{r^{3/2}}.}                            \tag{6.6}
\]

The right side is much larger than \(W\).  Thus early-only internal
rewiring fails even the necessary PCap condition.  A viable hierarchy
must contain genuine size-\(r+1\) parent actions which change a boundary
of every fatal size-\(r\) window; it cannot rely on an earlier split
persisting automatically.

## 7. Precise surviving route

The early-scale observation still has one useful consequence: the number
of capacity bins needed at the first crossing is bounded by four.  What is
missing is not an asymptotically large local alphabet but a hereditary
port packet outside the coordinate orbit.

A successful theorem must provide, for every relevant parent scale:

1. rooted complement-path factors with the same ports but target action
   crossing the \(\Gamma_t\)-orbits;
2. a product-compatible or explicitly joint multiscale installation;
3. full affected-window profiles, including both collars;
4. the coherent fractional inequalities (4.9) for one choice at all
   depths and an integral rounding bound such as (4.7); and
5. after PCap, either a common legal phase realization or direct hole
   control.

The finite \(D_4\)-port factor which transfers one first-insertion unit
between two coordinate-pair classes proves that the first item is not
topologically impossible in the whole anchored fibre.  It supplies no
recursive suspension or multidepth profile theorem.

Therefore the naive early-scale \(s_i\) hierarchy is refuted.  The exact
open replacement is a **hereditary orbit-crossing port atlas** satisfying
Theorem 4.1.  That statement is strictly stronger than a four-bin count
and remains unproved.
