## 1. Full components and the exact bank formula

### Lemma 1.1 (closed-router components)

Assume different packets have disjoint root sets and the final packet is
nontrivial.

* A closed clean `C_6` conveyor has one full component of size three.
* A closed folded-`C_6` transposition packet has one component of size two.
* A closed clean `C_8` conveyor has one component of size four.
* A reciprocal two-path `C_8` has one component of size two.
* A clean closed `C_(2 ell)` conveyor has one component of size `ell`.

#### Proof

Every token outside a packet keeps its old owner, so no full-overlay edge
leaves its root set.  In a clean conveyor, a nonempty transported segment
supplies owner edges `P--tau(P)` for the cyclic tail permutation `tau`;
these connect its complete `ell`-root orbit.  The folded `C_6` supplies the
one edge joining the two roots of its transposition.  A reciprocal `C_8`
exchanges nonempty middle segments between its two roots and therefore
also supplies cross-owner edges.  Common root-port tokens supply the
diagonal shore edges in every case. \(\square\)

A lone clean cycle with monodromy `tau!=1` is not an anchored factor and
cannot be inserted into (0.1).  Formal copies in independent recursive
holes act on different port coordinates and do not close it.  All formulas
below concern literal internal or physically serial zero-monodromy packets.

### Corollary 1.2 (exact uniform-bank identity)

For a root-disjoint bank with common component size `b`, changed-root
fraction `alpha`, and average rooted distance `d_bar`,

\[
                  \boxed{\Xi=b\alpha\bar d.}          \tag{1.1}
\]

For `M` packets this is

\[
                  \Xi={b\over B}\sum_{P\ {\rm changed}}d(P),
 \qquad R=Mb.                                         \tag{1.2}
\]

If packet layers use different root matchings, their full components are
the components of the union of those matchings.  The exact formula remains

\[
                         B\Xi=\sum_Kb_KD_K,            \tag{1.3}
\]

and one may not sum layerwise `Xi` after the union has merged components.

## 2. Phase-corridor and phase-area estimates

Suppose a closed internal conveyor first cuts at phase `t_0`, last cuts at
phase `t_1`, and rejoins every old suffix after the final cut.  Put

\[
                         E=t_1-t_0,\qquad L=E+1.       \tag{2.1}
\]

### Lemma 2.1 (corridor upper bound)

For every affected root,

\[
                         d(P)\le E(E+1)=L(L-1).       \tag{2.2}
\]

At any proper cyclic interval length, if `e_q(P)` is half the row-profile
`L^1` distance, then

\[
                         e_q(P)\le4E.                 \tag{2.3}
\]

#### Proof

The old and new rows agree before the first cut and after the final
zero-monodromy reconnection.  Thus their deletion orders differ inside one
`L`-position block and their insertion orders inside one `L`-position
block.  Agreement outside, together with the common root and complement,
shows that each pair of blocks contains the same coordinate set.  Two
permutations of `L` symbols have Kendall distance at most `binom(L,2)`;
summing the two halves gives (2.2).  A cyclic window can change only if a
boundary cut lies at one of the `2E` internal block cuts.  Each cut can be
the left or right boundary of one window, proving (2.3). \(\square\)

There is also a chronology-sensitive lower bound.

### Lemma 2.2 (phase-area lower bound)

Let `X_t^F(P),X_t^G(P)` be the two rank-`s` path states.  Then

\[
 \boxed{d(P)\ge\sum_{t=1}^{s-1}
             d_J\bigl(X_t^F(P),X_t^G(P)\bigr).}       \tag{2.4}
\]

In a clean conveyor whose transported root label is nontrivial throughout
`E` intermediate phases,

\[
                              d(P)\ge E.              \tag{2.5}
\]

#### Proof

For two permutations of one set, the sum over `t` of the symmetric
difference between their first-`t` sets is half their Spearman footrule.
One adjacent transposition changes that footrule by at most two, so Kendall
distance is at least the sum of the one-sided first-set differences.
Apply this separately to deletion and insertion orders.  Their sum at
phase `t` is exactly the displayed Johnson distance.  Distinct exact-factor
owners have distinct phase states, giving (2.5). \(\square\)

For `M` root-disjoint `b`-root packets in a common corridor,

\[
 {Mb^2E\over B}\le\Xi\le {Mb^2E(E+1)\over B}          \tag{2.6}
\]

whenever every changed row remains nontrivially transported throughout
the `E` intermediate phases.  The upper bound remains valid without that
extra hypothesis.

## 3. Clean and folded short cycles

For closed disjoint packets, (1.1) gives exactly

\[
 \boxed{
 \Xi_{C_6}^{\rm clean}=3\alpha\bar d,\qquad
 \Xi_{C_6}^{\rm fold}=2\alpha\bar d,\qquad
 \Xi_{C_8}^{\rm clean}=4\alpha\bar d.}               \tag{3.1}
\]

An internally closed clean `C_6` uses three transported stages.  If they
are consecutive, `E=2`, so

\[
                         2\le d(P)\le6,\qquad
 {18M\over B}\le\Xi\le {54M\over B}.                 \tag{3.2}
\]

A folded `C_6` must be closed by a second transposition or an equivalent
global monodromy correction.  Its endpoint type alone does not determine
the phase span; for a closed packet of exposure `E`,

\[
             \Xi={2\over B}\sum_Pd(P),\qquad
             d(P)\le E(E+1).                         \tag{3.3}
\]

A clean `C_8` requires four transported stages.  For consecutive stages,
`E=3`, hence

\[
                         3\le d(P)\le12,\qquad
 {48M\over B}\le\Xi\le {192M\over B}.                \tag{3.4}
\]

All these bounded-width estimates are `O(M/B)`.  Thus a dense
`M=Theta(B)` bounded-component bank has `Xi=O(1)`.

### 3.1 Exact block-separable serial ledger

Suppose `q` **closed** packet layers use the same `b`-root blocks, each
layer returns the endpoint monodromy to the identity, and their changed
coordinate-position blocks are disjoint.  Let `d_j(P)` be the rooted
distance inside layer `j`.  Inversions from disjoint position blocks do
not interact, so

\[
 \boxed{
 d_{\rm final}(P)=\sum_{j=1}^q d_j(P),\qquad
 \Xi_{\rm final}={b\over B}\sum_{P\ {\rm changed}}
                         \sum_{j=1}^q d_j(P).}        \tag{3.5}
\]

For `M` identical root-disjoint layers with per-root distance `d_0`, this
is

\[
                         \Xi_{\rm final}={qMb^2d_0\over B}.         \tag{3.6}
\]

For overlapping position blocks, rooted triangle inequality gives the
same expression only as an upper bound; cancellations must be evaluated
on the final words.  An open clean cycle is not one of the closed layers
covered by (3.5).  Its finite-order closure must first be treated as one
physical corridor, as in Lemmas 2.1--2.2.

## 4. The exact reciprocal-`C_8` bank

In one elementary octahedral rectangle the two rooted words each undergo
one adjacent swap in the deletion half and one in the insertion half.
Thus

\[
                         d(A)=d(B)=2,\qquad
 b_KD_K=2(2+2)=8.                                    \tag{4.1}
\]

For example the root `123` changes as

\[
 (2,3,1\mid6,4,5)\longmapsto(3,2,1\mid6,5,4),        \tag{4.2}
\]

and the root `124` has the same distance.  Hence `M` disjoint elementary
rectangles have

\[
                              \boxed{\Xi={8M\over B}.}              \tag{4.3}
\]

The canonical first-two-cut rectangles are exactly

\[
             1100R\longleftrightarrow1010R,\qquad R\in D_{s-2}.     \tag{4.4}
\]

Their root graph is a disjoint matching of `C_(s-2)` edges.  Substitution
in (4.3) proves (0.3).  The active-root and certified first-boundary
transfer fractions are exactly

\[
 {2C_{s-2}\over C_s}\longrightarrow{1\over8},
 \qquad
 {C_{s-2}\over C_s}\longrightarrow{1\over16}.        \tag{4.5}
\]

Therefore one layer is variance-safe but scalar-insufficient.  For `u`
disjoint fixed literal slots, the layer matchings are different and share
some roots.  Their commuting Boolean components give the exact identities

\[
 \Xi_u={4\over C_s}\sum_{h=1}^u{u\choose h}h2^hC_{s-2h}
 \longrightarrow {u\over2}\left({9\over8}\right)^{u-1},
 \qquad G_u^{\rm tag}=uC_{s-2}\sim {u\over16}B.       \tag{4.6}
\]

Here `G_u^tag` is a raw tagged transfer, not certified physical cap
descent.  The full components have at most `2^u` roots.  These assertions
and their simultaneous `X/Y` ownership proof are in
`MATH_THEOREM_DISJOINT_SHIFTED_RECIPROCAL_C8_LAYER_CUBES_20260726.md`.

The scalar inequality `G_u>=delta_theta B` asks exactly for

\[
 u\ge
 \left\lceil\delta_\theta{C_s\over C_{s-2}}\right\rceil.
                                                               \tag{4.7}
\]

For fixed \(\theta\), this is asymptotically
\(u\ge\lceil16\delta_\theta+o(1)\rceil\), and the required value lies
between four and seven for all sufficiently large \(s\).  The finite-\(s\)
ceiling must not be replaced by \(\lceil16\delta_\theta\rceil\).

This is the first explicit alternating-cycle family for which simultaneous
exactness, the sparse-edit constant, and the raw MWB supply constant are
compatible.  It nevertheless fails the physical PCap gate.  Every
complete layer column has zero sum on every orbit of the group
\(\Gamma\) generated by its middle-pair transpositions.  At
\(p=C_s/\theta\),
\(4\le\theta<16\), either target \(2\) is fixed or the first slot makes
\(\{2,3\}\) one isolated orbit.  Therefore

\[
 K_p\big|_{\Gamma\cdot2}\ge
 \min\{C_s-p,\ C_s+C_{s-1}-2p\}
 =\Omega(C_s).                                      \tag{4.8}
\]

This invariant includes crossing collars and mixed-window interactions.
Thus the tagged count cannot be promoted to physical descent within this
coordinate-layer class.

## 5. The correctly normalized MWB action inequality

At one protected depth, let `g_q(P)` be the number of row-start
occurrences at root `P` whose physical target actually moves, and let
their total useful cap efficiency be included in `G_q`.  The exact parent
ledger requires

\[
                         \boxed{G_q\ge\delta_\theta B.}             \tag{5.1}
\]

Equivalently, for changed-root density `alpha`, average useful moved-start
count `g`, and efficiency `gamma`,

\[
                         \boxed{\alpha g\gamma\ge\delta_\theta.}   \tag{5.2}
\]

There is no extra factor `n`: demand and supply acquire the same weighted
depth multiplier.

One adjacent transposition changes at most two positional windows at a
fixed depth.  Therefore

\[
        G_q\le\sum_Pg_q(P)\le2\sum_Pd(P).             \tag{5.3}
\]

For uniform full-component size `b`, equations (0.1) and (5.3) give the
necessary action/moment inequality

\[
                         \boxed{\Xi\ge {b\delta_\theta\over2}.}     \tag{5.4}
\]

This is constant for bounded `b`, so it is compatible with
`Xi=o(sqrt(s))`.  It becomes an obstruction only when

\[
                              b\not=o(\sqrt s).        \tag{5.5}
\]

For the requested router types, the exact scalar consequences are

\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c}
\text{closed router}&b&\Xi\text{ identity}&
       \Xi\text{ forced by }G_q\ge\delta_\theta B\\ \hline
\text{clean }C_6&3&3\alpha\bar d&\Xi\ge3\delta_\theta/2\\
\text{folded }C_6&2&2\alpha\bar d&\Xi\ge\delta_\theta\\
\text{clean }C_8&4&4\alpha\bar d&\Xi\ge2\delta_\theta\\
\text{clean }C_{2\ell}&\ell&\ell\alpha\bar d&
                                      \Xi\ge\ell\delta_\theta/2.
\end{array}                                             \tag{5.6}
\]

If a particular consumer insists on repairing one fixed first-boundary
cell, then each row can remove at most one unit and positive root density
is necessary.  That is a stronger specialization.  The all-start MWB
ledger is (5.1)--(5.2), and it must not be replaced by an unaudited
`Omega(W_s)` endpoint-motion demand.

## 6. A clean `C_(2 ell)` conveyor and serial repetition

One clean cycle has `ell` selected incidences and `ell`-cycle monodromy.
An internally closed lap uses `ell` transported stages on the same roots.
It has one component of size `ell` and `ell^2` formal selected incidences.

For a bank of `M` disjoint closed conveyors,

\[
                         \Xi={\ell\over B}
                            \sum_{P\ {\rm changed}}d(P).            \tag{6.1}
\]

If the `ell` stages are consecutive, their exposure is `E=ell-1`, so

\[
 {M\ell^2(\ell-1)\over B}
       \le\Xi
       \le {M\ell^3(\ell-1)\over B}.                 \tag{6.2}
\]

Now specialize to the ballot-forced extraction (0.6), giving it every
unproved geometric advantage: assume all `N_s` certificates can be made
strand-admissible, root-disjoint, closed clean conveyors with the same
favourable carrier sign.  If certificates overlap or their layer
matchings merge, the full components are larger and the edit bound below
only becomes less favourable.  By (2.3), one lap
changes at most `4 ell(ell-1)` positional starts in one `ell`-root
component at a fixed depth.  Even crediting every changed start with unit
useful gain, (5.1) forces

\[
              4\ell(\ell-1)\ge(\delta_\theta-o(1))s.               \tag{6.3}
\]

Combining (6.2), (0.6), and (6.3) yields

\[
                         \Xi\ge({\delta_\theta\over4}-o(1))\ell
                              =\Omega(\sqrt s).        \tag{6.4}
\]

Thus the **one-pass long-cycle repair is excluded** by the strict
little-`o` threshold.

Serial repetition on the same root orbit has different accounting.  Let
`q` productive laps occupy disjoint phase corridors and retain the same
full components.  Phase area and useful occurrence capacity give

\[
 \Xi\ge {N_s\over B}\,q\ell^2(\ell-1),
 \qquad
 G_q\le4N_s q\ell(\ell-1).                           \tag{6.5}
\]

Therefore meeting (5.1) forces

\[
                         \boxed{\Xi\ge
                          ({\delta_\theta\over4}-o(1))\ell.}       \tag{6.6}
\]

Consequently a necessary condition for serial repetition to be compatible
with sparse edit is

\[
                         \boxed{\ell=o(\sqrt s).}      \tag{6.7}
\]

For bounded `ell`, (6.6) is only a constant.  The required number of laps
is of order `s/ell^2`; fitting their `ell` stages uses order `s/ell`
phases, which is not ruled out by the phase count.  What is missing is an
actual switch-stable bank for that many phases with a common carrier sign.

The corridor upper bound gives the complementary sufficient estimate.  At
the minimally required `q=Theta(s/ell^2)` laps,

\[
 \Xi\le {N_s\over B}\,q\ell^3(\ell-1)=O(\ell^2).      \tag{6.8}
\]

Hence the universal bounds certify `Xi=o(sqrt(s))` when
`ell=o(s^(1/4))`.  For
\(s^{1/4}\lesssim\ell=o(\sqrt s)\), neither bound decides the gate: it is
necessary to compute the actual Kendall distances, which may lie anywhere
between the phase-area lower bound and the corridor upper bound.

If serial layers use different root matchings, they may merge the old
components.  Then (6.1) is invalid and the exact union formula (1.3) must
be used.  A giant merged component can restore the `Omega(s)` obstruction.

## 7. Independent extensive-row obstruction

The native invariant-packet theorem imposes a separate condition on any
one-cube escape from the canonical basin:

\[
 {R\over B}\ge\delta_{\rm inv}-o(1),\qquad
 \delta_{\rm inv}={107897\over19784704}.              \tag{7.1}
\]

For a bank with `M` nontrivial components, Cauchy gives

\[
 \Xi\ge {1\over B}\sum_Kb_K^2
       \ge {R^2\over BM}.                             \tag{7.2}
\]

Thus a bank with only `M=Theta(B/s)` components cannot simultaneously
satisfy (7.1) and `Xi=o(sqrt(s))`; it would have `Xi=Omega(s)`.  Serial
repetition on the same roots does not alter this conclusion.  This is a
valid native-row-escape obstruction, but it is logically separate from
the all-start MWB carrier demand (5.1).

The actual reciprocal `C_8` bank avoids it: its active-root density tends
to `1/8>delta_inv`, while (0.3) gives `Xi->1/2`.  It nevertheless remains
inside the already audited rectangle/invariant family and lacks enough
signed carrier supply in one layer.

> bank) whose complete physical column moves mass between distinct
