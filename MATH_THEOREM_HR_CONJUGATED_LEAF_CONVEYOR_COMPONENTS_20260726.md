# Complete components of the root-scale conjugated leaf-rectangle conveyor

Date: 2026-07-26

Method: pure mathematics only.  No computation, search, solver, or web input
is used.

## 0. Verdict

Let

\[
 H_r=\langle(2\ 3),(4\ 5),\ldots,(2r-2\ 2r-1)\rangle
 \cong C_2^{\,r-1},                                  \tag{0.1}
\]

and let \(G_r\) be the graph on the Dyck words \({\cal D}_r\) whose edges
are all \(H_r\)-translates of the certified leaf-rectangle edges

\[
 N(\bullet,R)\longleftrightarrow
 N(\varnothing,N(\varnothing,R)).                   \tag{0.2}
\]

Equivalently, before conjugation the local word replacement is

\[
 1100\,v\longleftrightarrow1010\,v                 \tag{0.3}
\]

inside an arbitrary rooted tree context.

The graph \(G_r\) is connected exactly for

\[
 \boxed{r\le3.}                                     \tag{0.4}
\]

It is disconnected for every \(r\ge4\).  There is a complete invariant.
Write every \(w\in{\cal D}_r\) uniquely as

\[
 w=1\,b_1b_2\cdots b_{r-1}\,0,\qquad |b_i|=2,       \tag{0.5}
\]

and encode

\[
 11=U,\qquad00=D,\qquad10=L_0,\qquad01=L_1.         \tag{0.6}
\]

After forgetting the subscript on \(L\), (0.5) is a Motzkin excursion
\(M(w)\) of length \(r-1\).  Repeatedly flatten every adjacent peak

\[
 UD\longrightarrow LL.                             \tag{0.7}
\]

This rewriting has a unique peakless normal form \(N(w)\).

In \(N(w)\), call a level step at position \(i\) **locked** when

1. it is a maximal level run of length one; and
2. it is preceded by \(D\), or followed by \(U\).

Boundary positions have no exterior \(D\) or \(U\).  Every locked level
persists throughout the component and retains its color
\(\epsilon_i\in\{0,1\}\).

### Main theorem

Two Dyck words \(w,w'\in{\cal D}_r\) lie in the same component of \(G_r\)
if and only if

\[
 \boxed{
 N(w)=N(w')
 \quad\text{and}\quad
 \epsilon_i(w)=\epsilon_i(w')
 \text{ at every locked level of }N(w).}            \tag{0.8}
\]

Thus root-scale \(H_r\)-conjugation joins the old leaf-rotation components,
but it does not produce the full Catalan conveyor.  For \(r=4\), the two
normal forms

\[
 LLL,\qquad ULD                                     \tag{0.9}
\]

already give two components.

The component generating function is explicit.  Let \(c_n\) be the number
of components of \(G_{n+1}\), and put

\[
 A(z)=\frac{z}{1-z},\qquad
 B(z)=1+2z+\frac{z^2}{1-z}
     =\frac{1+z-z^2}{1-z}.                          \tag{0.10}
\]

Let

\[
 {\cal N}(x,u)
 =\sum_{k\ge1}\sum_{p=1}^k
   \frac1k\binom kp\binom{k}{p-1}u^p x^k            \tag{0.11}
\]

be the Narayana generating function, equivalently

\[
 {\cal N}(x,u)
 =\frac{1-x(1+u)
 -\sqrt{1-2x(1+u)+x^2(1-u)^2}}{2x}.                \tag{0.12}
\]

Then

\[
 \boxed{
 \sum_{n\ge0}c_nz^n
 =\frac1{1-z}
 +B(z)\,
 {\cal N}\!\left(z^2B(z)^2,\frac{A(z)}{B(z)}\right).} \tag{0.13}
\]

The first values are

\[
 c_0,c_1,c_2,c_3,c_4,c_5,\ldots
 =1,1,1,2,6,13,\ldots.                              \tag{0.14}
\]

Consequently the exact edge switches generated by this conjugated menu act
as the direct product

\[
 \prod_{K\in\pi_0(G_r)}\operatorname{Sym}(K),       \tag{0.15}
\]

not as \(\operatorname{Sym}({\cal D}_r)\), for \(r\ge4\).

## 1. The \(H_r\)-orbit normal form is a colored Motzkin path

Let \(w=w_1\cdots w_{2r}\in{\cal D}_r\), with one interpreted as an up
step and zero as a down step.  Necessarily

\[
 w_1=1,\qquad w_{2r}=0.                             \tag{1.1}
\]

Group the remaining positions into

\[
 b_i=w_{2i}w_{2i+1}\qquad(1\le i\le r-1).           \tag{1.2}
\]

At the beginning of \(b_i\), the Dyck height is an odd positive integer.
The four block types have net height changes

\[
 11:+2,\qquad00:-2,\qquad10,01:0.                  \tag{1.3}
\]

After subtracting the permanent height one and dividing by two, the
block-height process is a nonnegative walk from zero to zero with steps
\(U,D,L\).  Hence it is a Motzkin excursion.

Conversely, choose a Motzkin excursion and replace every level step
independently by \(10\) or \(01\).  At a level block the incoming Dyck
height is at least one; the \(01\) orientation can lower it only to zero
at its intermediate position.  Thus every coloring gives a Dyck word.
This proves:

### Lemma 1.1 (orbit classification)

The \(H_r\)-orbits on \({\cal D}_r\) are indexed by uncolored Motzkin
excursions of length \(r-1\).  Inside one orbit, \(H_r\) acts transitively
on the \(2^\ell\) colorings of its \(\ell\) level steps.

#### Proof

The generator \((2i\ 2i+1)\) exchanges \(10\) and \(01\) in block \(b_i\)
and fixes \(11,00\).  The preceding paragraph proves that every such
exchange stays inside \({\cal D}_r\). \(\square\)

This is the exact effect of root-scale conjugation.  It may change every
level color simultaneously, but it never changes the uncolored Motzkin
skeleton.

## 2. Exact translated-edge classification

A certified leaf edge replaces an occurrence of \(1100v\) by \(1010v\)
at the beginning of a rooted subtree.  Only the middle two bits of the
displayed four-bit block change.  Let \(k\) be the global position of its
first bit.

### Theorem 2.1 (the two and only two translated moves)

Under the colored-Motzkin encoding, the edges of \(G_r\) are exactly the
following.

1. **Peak flattening.**  At consecutive block positions,
   \[
    UD\longleftrightarrow L_\alpha L_\beta
    \qquad(\alpha,\beta\in\{0,1\}).                 \tag{2.1}
   \]
   All four color pairs occur.
2. **Eligible level flip.**  The color of \(L_i\) may be flipped,
   \[
    L_0\longleftrightarrow L_1,                     \tag{2.2}
   \]
   precisely when
   \[
    i=1\ \text{or}\ M_{i-1}\ne D,
    \qquad
    i=r-1\ \text{or}\ M_{i+1}\ne U.                \tag{2.3}
   \]

#### Proof

Suppose first that \(k=2i\) is even.  The changed middle bits straddle the
two blocks \(b_i,b_{i+1}\).  Before the switch, the local four bits are
\[
 11\,00,
\]
so the two Motzkin steps are \(UD\).  Afterwards they are
\[
 10\,10,
\]
so they are two levels.  Conjugating independently by
\((2i\ 2i+1)\) and \((2i+2\ 2i+3)\) gives all four color pairs in (2.1).
Conversely every occurrence of \(UD\) is the four-bit Dyck subtree
\(1100\), so the certified leaf rectangle applies.

Now let \(k=2i-1\) be odd.  The changed bits are exactly block \(b_i\), and
the switch exchanges \(10\) with \(01\).  The bit immediately before this
block must be one in the unconjugated \(1100/1010\) pattern.  If \(i>1\),
an \(H_r\)-conjugate can arrange this precisely when block \(b_{i-1}\) is
\(U\) or \(L\), but not when it is \(D\).  At the left boundary the fixed
first bit is one.  Similarly, the bit immediately after \(b_i\) must be
zero.  It can be so arranged precisely when the next block is \(D\) or
\(L\), but not \(U\); at the right boundary the final bit is already zero.
This is (2.3).

In both cases the four-bit word \(1100\) is a primitive Dyck word and
therefore is the word of a rooted subtree in the ambient tree context.
Thus every move described above is a conjugate of a certified leaf edge.
Parity exhausts all possible starting positions \(k\), proving that there
are no other translated moves. \(\square\)

The distinction between (2.1) and (2.2) is the whole conveyor problem.
Move (2.1) changes the Motzkin skeleton; Move (2.2) changes only one level
color.

## 3. Peak-flattening normal form

Orient every skeleton-changing edge as

\[
 UD\longrightarrow LL.                             \tag{3.1}
\]

### Lemma 3.1 (termination and confluence)

The rewriting (3.1) terminates and has a unique normal form.  The normal
form is obtained by replacing every adjacent occurrence of \(UD\) by
\(LL\); it has no adjacent peak.

#### Proof

Every rewrite decreases the number of \(U\)-steps by one, so termination
is immediate.  Two occurrences of \(UD\) cannot overlap: after the \(U\)
of one redex comes its \(D\), which cannot be the \(U\) of another.
Disjoint rewrites commute.  Replacing \(UD\) by \(LL\) creates no new
\(UD\) across either boundary, because both new boundary symbols are
\(L\).  Hence every reduction performs the same disjoint set of rewrites
and reaches the same peakless path. \(\square\)

Write this normal form as \(N(M)\).  Since (2.2) does not change the
skeleton, and (2.1) is the reversible form of (3.1), \(N(M)\) is constant
on every component of \(G_r\).

There are already at least two normal forms for every length \(n\ge3\):

\[
 L^n,\qquad U L^{n-2}D.                             \tag{3.2}
\]

This proves disconnection of \(G_r\) for \(r=n+1\ge4\), before colors are
considered.

## 4. Locked colors are the remaining invariant

Fix a peakless Motzkin excursion \(N=N_1\cdots N_n\).  A level position
\(i\) is **singleton** when neither existing neighbor is \(L\).  It is
locked when

\[
 (i>1\text{ and }N_{i-1}=D)
 \quad\text{or}\quad
 (i<n\text{ and }N_{i+1}=U).                       \tag{4.1}
\]

### Lemma 4.1 (locked colors are invariant)

In every skeleton reducing to \(N\), a locked position remains a singleton
level step.  Its \(0/1\) color is invariant under every edge of \(G_r\).

#### Proof

A position which is nonlevel in \(N\) cannot have participated in a
flattening: every flattened position is a level in the normal form.
Likewise, an isolated level of \(N\) can never be one member of an
\(LL\leftrightarrow UD\) move, since its normal form would then have an
adjacent level at that position.  Thus the step and its two nonlevel
neighbors persist throughout the skeleton component.

Move (2.1) cannot touch it.  Move (2.2) can flip it only when it is not
preceded by \(D\) and not followed by \(U\).  Condition (4.1) is exactly
the negation of that eligibility.  Hence its color is fixed. \(\square\)

### Lemma 4.2 (every other color is free)

Within a fixed peak-normal-form class:

1. all colors in a level run of length at least two can be changed
   arbitrarily; and
2. the color of every unlocked singleton level can be flipped.

#### Proof

For two adjacent levels, raise them by the inverse of (2.1) to one
\(UD\), then flatten that peak using any desired pair of colors.  In
particular one may change either color while keeping the other fixed.
Applying this along a level run makes all its colors independent.

An unlocked singleton is not preceded by \(D\) and not followed by \(U\),
so (2.3) applies directly. \(\square\)

### Theorem 4.3 (complete component invariant)

Equation (0.8) is necessary and sufficient.

#### Proof

Necessity follows from Lemmas 3.1 and 4.1.

For sufficiency, reduce both skeletons to their common normal form using
(2.1).  The locked singleton positions are never touched and have equal
colors by hypothesis.  Lemma 4.2 adjusts every remaining color of the
first reduced word to the corresponding color of the second.  Reverse a
chosen reduction of the second word.  This gives a path in \(G_r\) from
\(w\) to \(w'\). \(\square\)

For \(n=0,1,2\), there is only one peak-normal-form class and no locked
color separates it, so \(G_1,G_2,G_3\) are connected.  Together with
(3.2), this proves (0.4).

## 5. Exact component generating function

Theorem 4.3 gives

\[
 c_n=\sum_{N}2^{\lambda(N)},                        \tag{5.1}
\]

where \(N\) ranges over peakless Motzkin excursions of length \(n\) and
\(\lambda(N)\) is its number of locked singleton levels.

Erase all level steps from \(N\).  If no \(U,D\) steps remain, then
\(N=L^n\), which contributes one component for every \(n\ge0\).  This is
the term \(1/(1-z)\) in (0.13).

Otherwise the nonlevel subsequence is a Dyck word \(\sigma\) of semilength
\(k\ge1\).  Insert level runs into its two boundary gaps and its
\(2k-1\) internal gaps.

* In a gap between an adjacent \(U,D\) of \(\sigma\), zero levels are
  forbidden by peaklessness, one level is the unlocked pattern \(ULD\),
  and a run of at least two levels has no locked singleton.  Its gap
  series is
  \[
   z+z^2+z^3+\cdots=A(z).                            \tag{5.2}
  \]
* In every other internal gap, zero levels have weight one, a singleton
  is locked and has two possible invariant colors, and a longer run has
  no invariant color.  The series is
  \[
   1+2z+z^2+z^3+\cdots=B(z).                        \tag{5.3}
  \]
* The two boundary gaps also have series \(B(z)\): a nonempty Dyck word
  starts with \(U\) and ends with \(D\), so a boundary singleton is
  locked.

If \(\sigma\) has \(p\) peaks, it has \(p\) adjacent \(UD\) gaps and
\(2k-1-p\) other internal gaps.  Its contribution is therefore

\[
 B(z)^2z^{2k}A(z)^pB(z)^{2k-1-p}.                  \tag{5.4}
\]

There are

\[
 N(k,p)=\frac1k\binom kp\binom{k}{p-1}              \tag{5.5}
\]

Dyck words of semilength \(k\) with \(p\) peaks.  Summing (5.4) gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
 \sum_{n\ge0}c_nz^n
 &=\frac1{1-z}
 +B(z)^2
   \sum_{k\ge1}\sum_{p=1}^k
   N(k,p)z^{2k}A(z)^pB(z)^{2k-1-p}\\
 &=\frac1{1-z}
 +B(z)\,
 {\cal N}\!\left(z^2B(z)^2,\frac{A(z)}{B(z)}\right),
\end{aligned}                                       \tag{5.6}
\]

which is (0.13).

## 6. Consequence for exact factor switches

Every edge of \(G_r\) is a certified exact port-preserving rectangle after
root-scale conjugation by an element of \(H_r\).  Edge transpositions of a
connected graph generate the full symmetric group on its vertex set.
Applying this separately to the components classified by Theorem 4.3
gives (0.15).

Thus the root-scale \(H_r\) menu is strictly stronger than the original
embedded leaf-rotation menu: it collapses the old tree normal-form
components through the peak-flattening move.  But the peakless Motzkin
normal form and its locked colors survive every conjugated switch.  To
obtain a genuine Catalan conveyor one must add at least one exact
port-preserving component move which changes that normal form, for example
a move crossing the \(ULD\)-plateau separation in (3.2).
