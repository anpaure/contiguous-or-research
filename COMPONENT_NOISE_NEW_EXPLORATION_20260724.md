# Fixed multitransposition heat: exact profile and collapse alternatives

Date: 2026-07-24

## Verdict

This note does not prove MWB.  It proves a new obstruction to two natural
attempts to bypass the one-transposition component-noise term:

1. sum or couple the component cubes belonging to many transpositions; or
2. package a fixed multistep/commutator word into one larger exact switching
   object.

The obstruction has two sharp alternatives.  They are not mutually
exclusive: disconnected coordinate support may preserve a zero profile
floor and still produce a single owner block, as the point stabilizer does
below.

* If the used transpositions generate a nontransitive coordinate group,
  then every lower-rank load has exact invariant orbit totals.  Those totals
  impose an explicit, floor-corrected lower bound on both balanced overload
  and quadratic energy.
* If the used transpositions connect the coordinate set, then the join of
  their exact owner-component partitions is the indiscrete partition.  A
  simultaneous common component cut is therefore trivial, and the layered
  switching object associated with any fixed word has one bundle.  Its only
  guaranteed endpoint choices are whole coordinate relabellings, which
  preserve every floor-corrected energy exactly.

In fact full coordinate connectivity is not needed for collapse.  The
point-stabilizer subgroup \(S_{n-1}\) already annihilates every admissible
coherent load discrepancy, has zero orbit-profile floor, and nevertheless
has one owner block; its component noise restores the entire energy exactly.

Thus subtracting the discrete floor does not rescue a fixed aggregated
component heat.  Within the canonical component-side mechanism, a viable
multistep proof must recompute components after earlier nontrivial choices;
the nonlocality must be genuinely adaptive.  The only other escape would be
a new theorem producing additional perfect matchings inside the union of the
layer supports.

Everything below concerns genuine exact wreath factors.  No signed
relaxation, random occupancy model, search, or external theorem is used.

## 1. Exact owner relations

Put

\[
 n=2m+1,
 \qquad
 \mathcal X=\binom{[n]}m.
\]

Let \(F\) be an exact middle wreath factor.  Denote by

\[
 o_F:\mathcal X\longrightarrow F
\]

the unique owner map: \(o_F(A)\) is the wreath row of \(F\) whose middle
windows contain \(A\).

Fix a coordinate transposition \(\tau\).  Contract the usual bipartite
ownership overlay between \(F\) and \(\tau F\) by identifying the right row
\(\tau P\) with the left row \(P\).  The fixed-middle-window argument for a
transposition shows that this identification is made inside each overlay
component.  Indeed, each transposed coordinate belongs to exactly \(m\) of
the \(n=2m+1\) cyclic middle windows of a row.  If every window contained
exactly one of them, their total incidence would be \(n\), whereas it is
\(2m\).  Some middle window therefore contains both coordinates or neither,
is fixed by the transposition, and joins \(P\) to \(\tau P\) inside the
overlay.  Consequently the induced equivalence relation
\(\mathcal P_\tau\) on the rows of \(F\) is generated by

\[
 \boxed{
 o_F(A)\sim_\tau o_F(\tau A)
 \qquad(A\in\mathcal X).}
 \tag{1.1}
\]

For a set \(T\) of coordinate transpositions, let

\[
 \mathcal P_T:=\bigvee_{\tau\in T}\mathcal P_\tau
 \tag{1.2}
\]

be the join of these row partitions.

Let \(G=\langle T\rangle\le S_n\), and let the \(G\)-orbits on
\(\mathcal X\) be denoted by \(\mathscr O_m(G)\).

### Theorem 1.1 (owner--Schreier quotient theorem)

Construct a bipartite incidence graph \(\mathfrak B_G(F)\) with vertex
classes

\[
 F
 \quad\text{and}\quad
 \mathscr O_m(G),
\]

joining a row \(P\) to an orbit \(\mathcal O\) when \(P\) owns at least one
middle set in \(\mathcal O\).  Then the blocks of \(\mathcal P_T\) are
exactly the sets of row vertices lying in the connected components of
\(\mathfrak B_G(F)\).

In particular, if \(G\) is transitive on \(\mathcal X\), then

\[
 \boxed{\mathcal P_T=\{F\}.}
 \tag{1.3}
\]

Thus the joined owner partition depends only on the generated subgroup
\(G\), not on the chosen transposition generating set.

#### Proof

Form the Schreier graph on \(\mathcal X\) whose edges are

\[
 A\longleftrightarrow\tau A,
 \qquad \tau\in T.
\]

Its connected components are precisely the \(G\)-orbits.  By (1.1), the
owner images of the two endpoints of every Schreier edge lie in one block
of \(\mathcal P_T\).  It follows that all rows owning sets from one fixed
orbit lie in one block.

Conversely, every generator of \(\mathcal P_T\) comes from a Schreier edge,
and rows are also identified whenever they are literally the same row.
Hence a chain in the joined row relation is exactly an alternating chain
through row vertices and orbit vertices in \(\mathfrak B_G(F)\).  This
proves the first assertion.

If \(G\) is transitive, \(\mathfrak B_G(F)\) has one orbit vertex, incident
with every row, and is connected.  This proves (1.3). \(\square\)

### Corollary 1.2 (connected transposition support collapses the join)

Let \(\Gamma_T\) be the graph on the coordinate set \([n]\) whose edges
are the transpositions in \(T\).  If \(\Gamma_T\) is connected, then

\[
 \boxed{\mathcal P_T=\{F\}.}
 \tag{1.4}
\]

#### Proof

Transpositions along the edges of a connected graph generate \(S_n\).
The latter is transitive on the middle layer, so Theorem 1.1 applies.
\(\square\)

This conclusion is stronger than saying that one particular overlay is
connected.  Even if every individual \(\mathcal P_\tau\) has many blocks,
their join is one block as soon as their coordinate edges connect \([n]\).

### Corollary 1.3 (no common transposition cut)

Suppose \(\Gamma_T\) is connected.  If a row set \(U\subseteq F\) is a
union of \(\mathcal P_\tau\)-blocks for every \(\tau\in T\), then

\[
 \boxed{U=\varnothing\quad\text{or}\quad U=F.}
 \tag{1.5}
\]

#### Proof

Such a set is saturated under every \(\mathcal P_\tau\), hence under their
join.  Use (1.4). \(\square\)

Therefore the one-transposition component variances for a connected family
of transpositions are variances on mutually incompatible product cubes.
They cannot be coupled by assigning one common nontrivial collection of row
components.

## 2. Fixed words and commutators collapse after pullback

Let

\[
 w=\tau_L\tau_{L-1}\cdots\tau_1
\]

be a fixed word of coordinate transpositions.  Put

\[
 \sigma_0=1,
 \qquad
 \sigma_i=\tau_i\sigma_{i-1},
 \qquad
 F_i=\sigma_iF.
\]

Build the layered ownership graph with row layers

\[
 F_0,F_1,\ldots,F_L
\]

and all middle-ownership edges between consecutive layers.  Pulling the
\(i\)-th interface back to \(F\) produces the transposition

\[
 \rho_i=\sigma_{i-1}^{-1}\tau_i\sigma_{i-1}.
 \tag{2.1}
\]

The exact layered-bundle theorem says that the endpoint bundle partition,
viewed on the rows of \(F\), is

\[
 \mathcal P_w=\mathcal P_{\rho_1}\vee\cdots\vee
 \mathcal P_{\rho_L}.
 \tag{2.2}
\]

For completeness, (2.2) follows directly as follows.  If \(\Omega\) is a
connected component of the layered graph and

\[
 K_i=\Omega\cap F_i,
\]

then the fixed-window edge at the \(i\)-th transposition gives

\[
 \tau_iK_{i-1}\subseteq K_i.
\]

Applying the same statement backwards gives equality.  All ownership edges
incident with these rows stay in \(\Omega\), so the two slices own the same
middle-set union.  Pulling slice \(i\) back by \(\sigma_i^{-1}\) identifies
the \(i\)-th adjacency relation with \(\mathcal P_{\rho_i}\).  A layered
path is therefore exactly a chain in the join of these pulled-back
relations, proving (2.2).

The following small group observation makes (2.2) decisive.

### Lemma 2.1 (the pulled-back letters generate the same group)

\[
 \boxed{
 \langle\rho_1,\ldots,\rho_L\rangle
 =\langle\tau_1,\ldots,\tau_L\rangle.}
 \tag{2.3}
\]

#### Proof

Induct on \(i\).  Suppose the two generated groups agree through
\(i-1\); call the common group \(H\).  Then \(\sigma_{i-1}\in H\), and

\[
 \rho_i=\sigma_{i-1}^{-1}\tau_i\sigma_{i-1}.
\]

Adjoining \(\rho_i\) to \(H\) therefore gives the same group as adjoining
\(\tau_i\). \(\square\)

### Theorem 2.2 (fixed-word endpoint collapse)

If the coordinate graph of the letters of \(w\) is connected, then the
layered ownership graph has one component.  Hence the canonical endpoint
component-side mechanism offers at most the two whole endpoint sides

\[
 \boxed{F\quad\text{and}\quad \sigma_LF.}
 \tag{2.4}
\]

More generally, if every layer is offered as a colour in the corresponding
multiway layered component construction, the only guaranteed choices are
the whole relabelled factors \(F_i=\sigma_iF\); rows from different layers
cannot be chosen independently by layered components.

#### Proof

By Lemma 2.1, the pulled-back letters generate the same subgroup as the
original letters.  Connected coordinate support makes this subgroup
\(S_n\).  Theorem 1.1 and (2.2) show that \(\mathcal P_w\) has one block.
The endpoint-bundle switching theorem then offers only the two whole sides
of that block, namely (2.4).  The same one-component conclusion applies to
the multiway collection of layer sides. \(\square\)

### Corollary 2.3 (zero floor-corrected drift)

Let \(\mathcal E\) be any coordinate-relabeling-invariant objective on exact
factors.  This includes, for every height \(H\),

\[
 \sum_{q\le H}\frac{O_q(F)}{c_q},
 \qquad
 \sum_{q\le H}\frac{Q_q(F)}{c_q},
\]

where

\[
 Q_q(F)=\sum_S(\mu_q(S)-c_q)(\mu_q(S)-c_q-1).
\]

Under the hypothesis of Theorem 2.2, every guaranteed layered choice has
exactly the same value of \(\mathcal E\) as \(F\).  In particular, subtracting
the discrete floor creates no hidden contraction:

\[
 \boxed{\Delta\mathcal E=0.}
 \tag{2.5}
\]

This does not rule out additional perfect matchings inside the union of the
layer supports.  It rules out the proposed conclusion from the canonical
componentwise side-choice mechanism itself.

## 3. Exact orbit-profile floors when the support is disconnected

We now prove the complementary obstruction.  Fix a subgroup
\(G\le S_n\), generated by a family of allowed coordinate transpositions.
At depth \(q\), put

\[
 r=m-q,
 \qquad
 \mathcal Y_q=\binom{[n]}r,
 \qquad
 N_q=|\mathcal Y_q|,
\]

\[
 W=c_qN_q+\delta_q,
 \qquad 0\le\delta_q<N_q.
\]

For a nonnegative integer load vector of total \(W\), use the exact balanced
overload and floor energy

\[
 O_q(\mu)=
 \frac12\min_{\substack{b(S)\in\{c_q,c_q+1\}\\
                         \sum_Sb(S)=W}}
 \|\mu-b\|_1,
 \tag{3.0}
\]

\[
 Q_q(\mu)=\sum_S(\mu(S)-c_q)(\mu(S)-c_q-1).
 \tag{3.0a}
\]

Let \(\mathscr O_q(G)\) be the \(G\)-orbits on \(\mathcal Y_q\).  For a load
vector \(\mu_q\), define the orbit totals

\[
 T_{q,\mathcal O}=\sum_{S\in\mathcal O}\mu_q(S).
 \tag{3.1}
\]

### Lemma 3.1 (orbit totals are exact dynamic invariants)

Every sequence of exact component switches using only transpositions in
\(G\) preserves every number \(T_{q,\mathcal O}\), simultaneously at all
depths.

#### Proof

Switching one component for a transposition \(\tau\in G\) changes the
depth-\(q\) load by

\[
 \mu'_q-\mu_q=(\tau-I)a
 \tag{3.2}
\]

for the nonnegative histogram \(a\) contributed by the switched side.
Every \(G\)-orbit is \(\tau\)-invariant, so summing (3.2) over one orbit
gives zero.  Iterate. \(\square\)

When the coordinate graph of the allowed transpositions has components
\(V_1,\ldots,V_s\), the group is

\[
 S_{V_1}\times\cdots\times S_{V_s},
\]

and the rank-\(r\) orbits are exactly the feasible intersection profiles

\[
 (|S\cap V_1|,\ldots,|S\cap V_s|).
 \tag{3.3}
\]

The next theorem gives the exact balanced-overload obstruction carried by
these invariants.

### Theorem 3.2 (exact orbit-profile overload floor)

For each orbit put \(M_{\mathcal O}=|\mathcal O|\) and

\[
 y_{\mathcal O}=T_{q,\mathcal O}-c_qM_{\mathcal O}.
\]

Define

\[
 A_{q,G}=\sum_{\mathcal O}(-y_{\mathcal O})_+,
 \qquad
 B_{q,G}=\sum_{\mathcal O}(y_{\mathcal O}-M_{\mathcal O})_+,
\]

and

\[
 \boxed{\Omega_{q,G}:=\max(A_{q,G},B_{q,G}).}
 \tag{3.4}
\]

Then every load vector with the prescribed orbit totals satisfies

\[
 \boxed{O_q\ge\Omega_{q,G}.}
 \tag{3.5}
\]

The right side is the exact minimum of the half-\(\ell_1\) distance after
one forgets all information inside individual orbits.  In particular it is
invariant under every allowed exact component switch.

#### Proof

A balanced quota vector has the form

\[
 b(S)=c_q+\mathbf1_{S\in\mathcal B},
 \qquad |\mathcal B|=\delta_q.
\]

Let

\[
 d_{\mathcal O}=|\mathcal B\cap\mathcal O|.
\]

Then

\[
 0\le d_{\mathcal O}\le M_{\mathcal O},
 \qquad
 \sum_{\mathcal O}d_{\mathcal O}=\delta_q.
\]

Aggregation over orbits and the triangle inequality give

\[
 \frac12\|\mu_q-b\|_1
 \ge
 \frac12\sum_{\mathcal O}|y_{\mathcal O}-d_{\mathcal O}|.
 \tag{3.6}
\]

Also

\[
 \sum_{\mathcal O}y_{\mathcal O}
 =W-c_qN_q=\delta_q.
\]

It remains to minimize the right side of (3.6).  Initially clamp every
\(y_{\mathcal O}\) to the interval \([0,M_{\mathcal O}]\).  This incurs
\(\ell_1\)-cost \(A_{q,G}+B_{q,G}\), and the clamped total exceeds the
required total by \(A_{q,G}-B_{q,G}\).  If this difference is positive,
reduce the clamped coordinates by \(A_{q,G}-B_{q,G}\) units; if it is
negative, increase them by \(B_{q,G}-A_{q,G}\) units.  Capacity is
available because the desired total lies between zero and
\(\sum M_{\mathcal O}\).  All quantities are integral.  The final
\(\ell_1\)-cost is

\[
 A+B+|A-B|=2\max(A,B).
\]

Thus the minimum of the aggregated half-\(\ell_1\) expression is exactly
\(\Omega_{q,G}\).  Since balanced overload is the minimum of
\(\frac12\|\mu_q-b\|_1\), (3.5) follows. \(\square\)

There is a parallel exact statement for the floor-corrected quadratic
energy.

### Theorem 3.3 (exact orbit-profile quadratic floor)

For an orbit \(\mathcal O\), divide

\[
 T_{q,\mathcal O}=a_{\mathcal O}M_{\mathcal O}
 +\rho_{\mathcal O},
 \qquad 0\le\rho_{\mathcal O}<M_{\mathcal O}.
\]

Put

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathfrak B_{q,G}:=\sum_{\mathcal O}\bigl[&
 (M_{\mathcal O}-\rho_{\mathcal O})
 (a_{\mathcal O}-c_q)(a_{\mathcal O}-c_q-1)\\
 &+\rho_{\mathcal O}
 (a_{\mathcal O}+1-c_q)(a_{\mathcal O}-c_q)
 \bigr].
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{3.7}
\]

Then every load vector with the prescribed orbit totals satisfies

\[
 \boxed{Q_q\ge\mathfrak B_{q,G}\ge2\Omega_{q,G}.}
 \tag{3.8}
\]

Moreover,

\[
 \boxed{
 \mathfrak B_{q,G}=0
 \iff
 \Omega_{q,G}=0
 \iff
 c_qM_{\mathcal O}\le T_{q,\mathcal O}
 \le(c_q+1)M_{\mathcal O}
 \ \text{for every }\mathcal O.}
 \tag{3.9}
\]

#### Proof

The integer function

\[
 \phi(x)=(x-c_q)(x-c_q-1)
\]

is discretely convex: its first difference increases by two at every step.
For a fixed orbit total, repeated Robin Hood transfers therefore minimize
\(\sum_{S\in\mathcal O}\phi(\mu_q(S))\) when the orbit loads differ by at
most one.  The resulting value is exactly the summand in (3.7).  Summing
over the orbits proves the first inequality in (3.8).

For every integer load vector, the scalar floor inequality gives

\[
 Q_q\ge2O_q.
\]

Apply this to an orbitwise balanced minimizer of \(Q_q\), and then use
Theorem 3.2, to obtain \(\mathfrak B_{q,G}\ge2\Omega_{q,G}\).

Finally, \(\phi(x)=0\) exactly at \(x=c_q,c_q+1\).  Hence the orbitwise
minimum is zero exactly when its total lies in the interval displayed in
(3.9).  The same interval criterion is visibly equivalent to
\(A_{q,G}=B_{q,G}=0\). \(\square\)

The orbit floor also has an exact projection-and-restitution form.  This is
useful for comparing it with coherent spectral smoothing.

### Theorem 3.4 (orbit projection plus discrete restitution)

Put

\[
 \lambda_q=W/N_q,
 \qquad
 f_q=\mu_q-\lambda_q\mathbf1,
 \qquad
 \beta_q=N_q\{\lambda_q\}(1-\{\lambda_q\}).
\]

Let \(P_G\) be averaging on every \(G\)-orbit of rank-\(r\) targets.  For
an orbit \(\mathcal O\), put

\[
 \lambda_{\mathcal O}=T_{q,\mathcal O}/M_{\mathcal O},
\]

and define

\[
 \Theta_{q,G}
 =\sum_{\mathcal O}M_{\mathcal O}
 \{\lambda_{\mathcal O}\}(1-\{\lambda_{\mathcal O}\}).
 \tag{3.10}
\]

Then

\[
 \boxed{
 \mathfrak B_{q,G}
 =\|P_Gf_q\|_2^2+\Theta_{q,G}-\beta_q.}
 \tag{3.11}
\]

Consequently, whenever \(G\le G'\),

\[
 \boxed{
 \mathfrak B_{q,G}-\mathfrak B_{q,G'}
 =\bigl(\|P_Gf_q\|_2^2-\|P_{G'}f_q\|_2^2\bigr)
  +\Theta_{q,G}-\Theta_{q,G'}.}
 \tag{3.12}
\]

#### Proof

For every integer load vector of total \(W\), the exact floor identity is

\[
 Q_q=\|\mu_q-\lambda_q\mathbf1\|_2^2-\beta_q.
 \tag{3.13}
\]

At the orbitwise balanced minimizer from Theorem 3.3, the loads in
\(\mathcal O\) are \(\lfloor\lambda_{\mathcal O}\rfloor\) and
\(\lceil\lambda_{\mathcal O}\rceil\).  Their squared deviations from their
own mean sum to

\[
 M_{\mathcal O}
 \{\lambda_{\mathcal O}\}(1-\{\lambda_{\mathcal O}\}).
\]

Orthogonal decomposition into the orbit mean and the within-orbit
fluctuation gives

\[
 \sum_{S\in\mathcal O}(\mu_q(S)-\lambda_q)^2
 =M_{\mathcal O}(\lambda_{\mathcal O}-\lambda_q)^2
 +M_{\mathcal O}
  \{\lambda_{\mathcal O}\}(1-\{\lambda_{\mathcal O}\}).
\]

Summing and applying (3.13) proves (3.11).  Subtraction proves (3.12).
\(\square\)

Formula (3.12) records exactly why a real projection estimate alone is not
the full integer argument: the orbit merger also changes the restitution
term \(\Theta\).  No sign may be assigned to the two summands separately,
although their sum is nonnegative by Theorem 4.2 below.

Thus a disconnected-coordinate commutator is not merely constrained by a
hole count.  It carries an exact positive floor *above the global discrete
floor* whenever one profile has too little or too much mass.

## 4. The profile and collapse alternatives

We can now combine the two sides.

### Theorem 4.1 (fixed multitransposition dichotomy)

Fix an exact factor \(F\) and a finite family \(T\) of coordinate
transpositions.

1. If the coordinate graph \(\Gamma_T\) is disconnected, then every exact
   component-switch sequence using only letters of \(T\) obeys, at every
   depth,
   \[
   O_q\ge\Omega_{q,\langle T\rangle},
   \qquad
   Q_q\ge\mathfrak B_{q,\langle T\rangle}.
   \tag{4.1}
   \]
   These are explicit invariants of the initial factor.

   Consequently, for every height \(H\),
   \[
   \sum_{q\le H}\frac{O_q}{c_q}
   \ge
   \sum_{q\le H}\frac{\Omega_{q,\langle T\rangle}}{c_q},
   \qquad
   \sum_{q\le H}\frac{Q_q}{c_q}
   \ge
   \sum_{q\le H}\frac{\mathfrak B_{q,\langle T\rangle}}{c_q}.
   \tag{4.1a}
   \]

2. If \(\Gamma_T\) is connected, then the common owner-component join is
   one block.  There is no nontrivial row subset switchable simultaneously
   in every \(T\)-cube.  For any fixed word using all these letters whose
   support is connected, the canonical layered endpoint heat has one bundle
   and exactly zero drift for every relabelling-invariant floor-corrected
   objective.

#### Proof

Part 1 is Lemma 3.1 together with Theorems 3.2 and 3.3.  Part 2 is
Corollaries 1.2--1.3 and Theorem 2.2. \(\square\)

### Interpretation for commutators

A commutator supported on a fixed set of coordinates lies on the first side
of the dichotomy.  For example, two overlapping transpositions generate
only an \(S_3\) on their three coordinates; at rank \(r\), their orbits fix
the entire outside subset and remember only how many of the three moved
coordinates are present.  The profile floors (3.4) and (3.7) are therefore
very fine.

To remove all such profile invariants, a word must eventually connect the
coordinate graph.  But if the whole fixed word is aggregated before the
component choice, it lies on the second side: all owner bundles have merged,
and only global relabellings remain.

This is the exact reason a successful commutator construction based only on
component-side choices cannot be a fixed endpoint coupling.  It must use
the order

\[
 \boxed{
 \text{switch}\;\longrightarrow\;
 \text{change the exact factor}\;\longrightarrow\;
 \text{recompute the next components}.}
 \tag{4.2}
\]

The certified finite nonlocal Haar circuit has precisely this adaptive
form.  The theorem above explains why simply taking the join of all its
preparatory transposition partitions would erase the useful intermediate
fragmentation.

### Theorem 4.2 (exact spanning-tree profile-release ledger)

Fix an ordering \(e_1,\ldots,e_{n-1}\) of the edges of any spanning tree on
\([n]\).  Put

\[
 G_t=\langle e_1,\ldots,e_t\rangle,
 \qquad 0\le t\le n-1,
\]

where \(G_0\) is trivial.  For the load vector of one fixed exact factor,
define

\[
 \Delta^{O}_{q,t}
 =\Omega_{q,G_{t-1}}-\Omega_{q,G_t},
\]

\[
 \Delta^{Q}_{q,t}
 =\mathfrak B_{q,G_{t-1}}-\mathfrak B_{q,G_t}.
\]

Then every release is nonnegative and

\[
 \boxed{
 O_q(F)=\sum_{t=1}^{n-1}\Delta^{O}_{q,t},
 \qquad
 Q_q(F)=\sum_{t=1}^{n-1}\Delta^{Q}_{q,t}.}
 \tag{4.3}
\]

Consequently, for every height \(H\), some tree edge satisfies

\[
 \boxed{
 \sum_{q\le H}\frac{\Delta^{O}_{q,t}}{c_q}
 \ge
 \frac1{n-1}
 \sum_{q\le H}\frac{O_q(F)}{c_q},}
 \tag{4.4}
\]

and some (possibly different) tree edge satisfies the analogous inequality
with \(\Delta^Q\) and \(Q_q\).

#### Proof

When a subgroup is enlarged, its target orbits can only merge.  The
aggregated half-\(\ell_1\) optimization defining \(\Omega\) then has fewer
constraints, so \(\Omega_{q,G_t}\le\Omega_{q,G_{t-1}}\).  Equivalently,
this follows by applying the triangle inequality after the finer orbit
totals are summed into the coarser ones.

The quantity \(\mathfrak B_{q,G}\) is the minimum of \(Q_q\) subject to the
\(G\)-orbit totals.  Merging orbits again removes constraints, and hence
\(\mathfrak B_{q,G_t}\le\mathfrak B_{q,G_{t-1}}\).

For the trivial group, every target is a singleton orbit.  The definitions
give

\[
 \Omega_{q,G_0}=\max\left\{
 \sum_S(c_q-\mu_q(S))_+,
 \sum_S(\mu_q(S)-c_q-1)_+
 \right\}=O_q(F),
\]

and

\[
 \mathfrak B_{q,G_0}
 =\sum_S(\mu_q(S)-c_q)(\mu_q(S)-c_q-1)=Q_q(F).
\]

The final tree is connected, so \(G_{n-1}=S_n\) and has one target orbit.
Its total is \(W=c_qN_q+\delta_q\), which lies between \(c_qN_q\) and
\((c_q+1)N_q\).  Equations (3.9) give

\[
 \Omega_{q,G_{n-1}}=\mathfrak B_{q,G_{n-1}}=0.
\]

Telescoping proves (4.3).  Sum with weights \(1/c_q\) and use the
pigeonhole principle to obtain (4.4). \(\square\)

At the quadratic level, Theorem 3.4 refines the same telescope into

\[
 \sum_{t=1}^{n-1}
 \bigl(\|P_{G_{t-1}}f_q\|_2^2-
       \|P_{G_t}f_q\|_2^2\bigr)
 =\|f_q\|_2^2,
 \tag{4.5}
\]

\[
 \sum_{t=1}^{n-1}
 (\Theta_{q,G_{t-1}}-\Theta_{q,G_t})
 =-\beta_q.
 \tag{4.6}
\]

Their sum is \(Q_q=\|f_q\|_2^2-\beta_q\).  Individual restitution
increments in (4.6) need not have a sign; the combined releases
\(\Delta^Q_{q,t}\) do.  This is the exact discrete-floor correction to the
spanning-tree coherent-smoothing ledger.

Theorem 4.2 is a positive selection statement at the relaxed profile level:
one coordinate bridge always releases at least a \(1/(n-1)\) share of the
current weighted defect.  It does **not** say that the component cube for
that bridge realizes the release.  The missing exact-factor assertion can
now be phrased sharply: route a fixed fraction of the profile release
through actual recomputed owner components before the successive joins make
the common bundle indiscrete.

## 5. Exact subgroup heat after subtracting the orbit floor

The owner--Schreier theorem also gives a genuine multiway exact heat for
every subgroup \(G\) generated by transpositions.  It yields an exact
necessary-and-sufficient comparison between profile release and harmful
component noise.

Let \(\mathcal K_G(F)\) be the blocks of the joined owner partition from
Theorem 1.1.  For \(K\in\mathcal K_G(F)\), let

\[
 U_K=\bigsqcup_{P\in K}\mathcal W_m(P)
\]

be its owned middle-set union.  Relation (1.1) implies

\[
 \boxed{gU_K=U_K\qquad(g\in G).}
 \tag{5.1}
\]

For each block choose \(g_K\in G\), and put

\[
 F_{\mathbf g}=\bigcup_{K\in\mathcal K_G(F)}g_KK.
 \tag{5.2}
\]

### Lemma 5.1 (multiway block choices stay exact)

Every \(F_{\mathbf g}\) in (5.2) is an exact middle wreath factor.

#### Proof

The rows in \(g_KK\) partition the middle support \(g_KU_K=U_K\).
The sets \(U_K\) over the owner blocks partition the complete middle layer.
Taking one relabelled row partition of each \(U_K\) therefore gives an exact
factor. \(\square\)

At depth \(q\), let \(u_{K,q}\) be the histogram contributed by the rows in
\(K\), and let \(P_G\) denote uniform averaging over \(G\).  Define the
complete-side subgroup variance

\[
 \mathcal V_{q,G}(F)
 =\sum_{K\in\mathcal K_G(F)}
 \left(\|u_{K,q}\|_2^2-\|P_Gu_{K,q}\|_2^2\right).
 \tag{5.3}
\]

Recall \(\Theta_{q,G}\) from (3.10), and put

\[
 \boxed{
 \mathcal C_{q,G}(F)
 =\mathcal V_{q,G}(F)-\Theta_{q,G}.}
 \tag{5.4}
\]

### Theorem 5.2 (exact subgroup profile-release identity)

Choose the \(g_K\)'s independently and uniformly from \(G\).  Then

\[
 \boxed{
 \mathbb E\,Q_q(F_{\mathbf g})
 =\mathfrak B_{q,G}+\mathcal C_{q,G}(F),}
 \tag{5.5}
\]

where

\[
 \boxed{\mathcal C_{q,G}(F)\ge0.}
 \tag{5.6}
\]

Equivalently, the expected fair decrease is

\[
 \boxed{
 Q_q(F)-\mathbb E Q_q(F_{\mathbf g})
 =\underbrace{Q_q(F)-\mathfrak B_{q,G}}_
             {\text{available orbit-profile release}}
  -\underbrace{\mathcal C_{q,G}(F)}_
             {\text{excess subgroup component noise}}.}
 \tag{5.7}
\]

Equality \(\mathcal C_{q,G}=0\) holds precisely when, for every target
\(S\), every multiway child load \(\mu_q(F_{\mathbf g})(S)\) lies in the two
adjacent integers

\[
 \left\{
 \left\lfloor\frac{T_{q,\mathcal O}}{|\mathcal O|}\right\rfloor,
 \left\lceil\frac{T_{q,\mathcal O}}{|\mathcal O|}\right\rceil
 \right\},
 \qquad S\in\mathcal O.
 \tag{5.8}
\]

#### Proof

The random depth-\(q\) histogram is

\[
 \mu'_q=\sum_Kg_Ku_{K,q}.
\]

Its mean is

\[
 \mathbb E\mu'_q
 =\sum_KP_Gu_{K,q}=P_G\mu_q.
 \tag{5.9}
\]

Independence makes all cross-block centered terms vanish, giving

\[
 \mathbb E\|\mu'_q-P_G\mu_q\|_2^2
 =\mathcal V_{q,G}(F).
 \tag{5.10}
\]

Since \(P_G\mu_q-\lambda_q\mathbf1=P_Gf_q\), the floor identity (3.13)
and orthogonal mean--fluctuation decomposition give

\[
 \mathbb E Q_q(F_{\mathbf g})
 =\|P_Gf_q\|_2^2+\mathcal V_{q,G}(F)-\beta_q.
 \tag{5.11}
\]

Substitute (3.11) to obtain (5.5).

For \(S\in\mathcal O\), the integer random variable
\(\mu'_q(S)\) has mean

\[
 (P_G\mu_q)(S)=T_{q,\mathcal O}/|\mathcal O|.
\]

An integer random variable of mean \(a+\theta\), \(0\le\theta<1\), has
variance at least \(\theta(1-\theta)\), with equality exactly when it is
supported on \(\{a,a+1\}\).  Summing this scalar inequality over all
targets gives

\[
 \mathcal V_{q,G}(F)\ge\Theta_{q,G},
\]

which is (5.6).  Every tuple \(\mathbf g\) has positive probability, so
simultaneous scalar equality is equivalent to (5.8) for every child.
\(\square\)

For a height \(H\), put

\[
 \mathcal Q_H(F)=\sum_{q\le H}\frac{Q_q(F)}{c_q},
\]

\[
 \mathfrak B_{H,G}=\sum_{q\le H}\frac{\mathfrak B_{q,G}}{c_q},
 \qquad
 \mathcal C_{H,G}(F)=\sum_{q\le H}\frac{\mathcal C_{q,G}(F)}{c_q}.
\]

Then one exact multiway child satisfies

\[
 \boxed{
 \mathcal Q_H(F_{\mathbf g})
 \le \mathfrak B_{H,G}+\mathcal C_{H,G}(F).}
 \tag{5.12}
\]

Thus the exact subgroup gate is simply

\[
 \boxed{
 \mathcal C_{H,G}(F)
 <\mathcal Q_H(F)-\mathfrak B_{H,G}.}
 \tag{5.13}
\]

It is necessary and sufficient for the uniform independent block-relabeling
heat defined above to have strictly smaller expected energy than \(F\).

### Corollary 5.3 (the full symmetric-group heat has exactly zero drift)

For \(G=S_n\), one has

\[
 \mathfrak B_{q,G}=0,
 \qquad
 \mathcal C_{q,G}(F)=Q_q(F).
 \tag{5.14}
\]

Hence

\[
 \boxed{\mathbb E Q_q(F_{\mathbf g})=Q_q(F).}
 \tag{5.15}
\]

#### Proof

There is one target orbit, so (3.9) gives \(\mathfrak B_{q,G}=0\).
Theorem 1.1 gives one owner block.  Thus every child is a whole relabelling
\(gF\), and its energy equals \(Q_q(F)\).  Equation (5.5) then forces
\(\mathcal C_{q,G}=Q_q(F)\). \(\square\)

The same complete cancellation already occurs for a proper subgroup.

### Theorem 5.4 (point-stabilizer collapse and exact noise restitution)

Fix a coordinate \(x\), and put

\[
 G_x=S_{[n]\setminus\{x\}}.
\]

Then, simultaneously at every depth \(q\),

\[
 \boxed{
 P_{G_x}f_q=0,
 \qquad
 \Omega_{q,G_x}=0,
 \qquad
 \mathfrak B_{q,G_x}=0.}
 \tag{5.16}
\]

Moreover, the joined owner partition \(\mathcal K_{G_x}(F)\) has one block.
Consequently

\[
 \boxed{
 \mathcal C_{q,G_x}(F)=Q_q(F),
 \qquad
 \mathbb E Q_q(F_{\mathbf g})=Q_q(F).}
 \tag{5.17}
\]

Thus averaging over all permutations of only \(n-1\) coordinates gives
perfect coherent smoothing and exactly equal component-noise restitution.

#### Proof

At rank \(r=m-q\), the group \(G_x\) has two target orbits:

\[
 \mathcal O_1=\{S:x\in S\},
 \qquad
 \mathcal O_0=\{S:x\notin S\}.
\]

Every wreath row contains exactly \(r\) cyclic rank-\(r\) intervals through
\(x\).  Since an exact factor has \(W/n\) rows,

\[
 T_{q,\mathcal O_1}=\frac{rW}{n}.
 \tag{5.18}
\]

But

\[
 |\mathcal O_1|=\binom{n-1}{r-1}=\frac r nN_q,
\]

so the mean load on \(\mathcal O_1\) is exactly \(W/N_q=\lambda_q\).
The complementary orbit has the same mean by total mass.  Therefore the
orbit average of \(f_q\) vanishes, proving \(P_{G_x}f_q=0\).  Both orbit
means have fractional part \(\{\lambda_q\}\), whence

\[
 \Theta_{q,G_x}
 =N_q\{\lambda_q\}(1-\{\lambda_q\})=\beta_q.
\]

Equation (3.11) now gives \(\mathfrak B_{q,G_x}=0\).
Equation (3.8) then gives \(\Omega_{q,G_x}=0\).

On the middle layer, \(G_x\) again has two orbits: middle sets containing
\(x\), and those avoiding \(x\).  Every wreath row owns exactly \(m\) sets
of the first kind and \(m+1\) of the second.  Thus every row vertex of
\(\mathfrak B_{G_x}(F)\) is adjacent to both orbit vertices.  The incidence
graph is connected, and Theorem 1.1 gives one owner block.

Every multiway child is therefore one whole relabelling \(gF\).  Its energy
equals \(Q_q(F)\).  Alternatively, substitute one block,
\(\mathfrak B=0\), and (5.5) to obtain (5.17). \(\square\)

Theorem 5.4 is the sharp coherent-smoothing/component-fragmentation
incompatibility.  The point-stabilizer average kills every harmonic degree
allowed by the exact zero total and point margins, yet the exact owner
geometry leaves no independent block choices.  Passing from transpositions
to a large proper subgroup therefore does not weaken the component-noise
gate.

In particular, any fixed word whose letter graph is connected on
\([n]\setminus\{x\}\) and leaves only \(x\) isolated already has one
layered owner bundle, even though its coordinate support graph is not
connected on all of \([n]\).

For \(G=\langle\tau\rangle\), Theorem 5.2 reduces to the familiar
one-transposition component identity, but with the parity/integer
restitution floor already removed.  For larger \(G\), it gives the exact
multiway analogue.  Corollary 5.3 proves that enlarging all the way to
\(S_n\) does not upper-bound the harmful noise: it makes the harmful noise
equal the entire profile release.

## 6. Consequence for the summed component-variance route

For one transposition \(\tau\), let \(\mathcal C_\tau(F)\) be its owner
components and let \(d_{\tau,C,q}\) be the depth-\(q\) component effect.
The aggregate one-step variance is built from

\[
 R_H(F)=
 \sum_\tau\sum_{C\in\mathcal C_\tau(F)}
 \sum_{q\le H}\frac{\|d_{\tau,C,q}\|_2^2}{c_q}.
 \tag{6.1}
\]

Theorem 1.1 proves a structural fact not visible in this scalar sum:

\[
 \boxed{
 \text{the component decompositions indexed by all transpositions have
 no nontrivial common component cut.}}
 \tag{6.2}
\]

Indeed the family of all coordinate transpositions has connected support
graph, so its partition join is one block.  Hence (6.1) is a sum of
variances belonging to
different, incompatible exact-factor cubes.  It is not the variance of one
larger legal product cube.  Replacing those cubes by their canonical joint
layered coupling gives one component and zero floor-corrected drift.

This does not invalidate one-step identities or a genuinely sequential
Markov chain.  It proves that an upper bound for (6.1) cannot be obtained by
the following proposed shortcut:

*take all transposition component coins simultaneously, regard them as one
common component product, and invoke additional cancellation after
subtracting the integer floor.*

There is no such nontrivial common component product in the exact-factor
fibre.

## 7. Exact remaining mathematical target

The new theorem narrows the component-noise lane to an adaptive statement.
It also gives a precise nonadaptive subgroup alternative.  For fixed
Gaussian height \(H=\lceil A\sqrt m\rceil\), define the subgroup release

\[
 \mathcal R_{H,G}(F)
 =\mathcal Q_H(F)-\mathfrak B_{H,G}.
 \tag{7.1}
\]

Here \(\operatorname{Cat}_m=W/n\).

The exact subgroup heat-gap lemma would assert that, for constants
\(\eta_A>0\) and \(C_A<\infty\), every exact factor admits a
transposition-generated subgroup \(G\) with

\[
 \boxed{
 \mathcal R_{H,G}(F)-\mathcal C_{H,G}(F)
 \ge
 \frac{\eta_A}{n}\mathcal Q_H(F)
 -\frac{C_A}{n}H\operatorname{Cat}_m.}
 \tag{SG_A}
\]

By (5.12), \((SG_A)\) gives one exact child with the corresponding energy
decrease.  At a global minimizer its left side is nonpositive, and hence

\[
 \mathcal Q_H(F_*)
 \le \frac{C_A}{\eta_A}H\operatorname{Cat}_m=o(W).
\]

Thus \((SG_A)\) would prove the fixed-window theorem and then MWB by the
already audited diagonalization.  The point-stabilizer theorem shows the
sharp obstruction: it has \(\mathcal R_{H,G_x}=\mathcal Q_H\) but also
\(\mathcal C_{H,G_x}=\mathcal Q_H\), so its heat gap is exactly zero.

If no one-shot subgroup satisfies \((SG_A)\), the remaining route is
genuinely adaptive.  One possible clean formulation is:

> **Adaptive fresh-fragmentation theorem (open).**  For every fixed Gaussian
> window, every high-overload exact factor admits a finite sequence of legal
> component switches such that (i) the components are recomputed after each
> chosen switch, (ii) the cumulative coordinate support releases every
> positive orbit-profile floor before it becomes locked, and (iii) the final
> factor has strictly smaller weighted overload by a quantitatively useful
> amount.

Theorems 3.2--3.3 provide exact bookkeeping for clause (ii).  Theorems
1.1 and 2.2 show why recomputation in clause (i) is indispensable.  What
remains unproved is the positive, state-dependent fragmentation assertion
in clause (iii).

In particular, neither MWB nor the constant-one upper bound is claimed
here.  The proved advance is the exact floor-sensitive no-go theorem:

\[
 \boxed{
 \text{disconnected fixed support }\Rightarrow\text{ invariant profile
 floor};\qquad
 \text{connected fixed support }\Rightarrow\text{ one owner bundle}.}
\]

## 8. Constant and endpoint audit

Three limiting cases independently check all factors in Theorem 5.2.

### 8.1 Trivial subgroup

If \(G=\{1\}\), every target orbit is a singleton and every owner block
may be taken separately.  Then

\[
 \mathfrak B_{q,G}=Q_q(F),
 \qquad
 \Theta_{q,G}=0,
 \qquad
 \mathcal V_{q,G}=0,
 \qquad
 \mathcal C_{q,G}=0.
\]

Equation (5.5) reads \(\mathbb E Q_q(F)=Q_q(F)\).

### 8.2 Full symmetric group

If \(G=S_n\), Theorem 1.1 gives one owner block and one target orbit.
Then

\[
 \mathfrak B_{q,G}=0,
 \qquad
 \Theta_{q,G}=\beta_q,
 \qquad
 \mathcal V_{q,G}=\|f_q\|_2^2,
\]

so

\[
 \mathcal C_{q,G}=\|f_q\|_2^2-\beta_q=Q_q(F).
\]

This checks (5.14) without using the qualitative relabelling argument.

### 8.3 One transposition

Let \(G=\langle\tau\rangle\).  Since

\[
 P_G=(I+\tau)/2,
\]

one component contributes

\[
 \|u_K\|_2^2-\|P_Gu_K\|_2^2
 =\frac14\|u_K-\tau u_K\|_2^2.
 \tag{8.1}
\]

For an unordered moved target pair \(p=\{S,\tau S\}\), put

\[
 d_K=u_K(S)-u_K(\tau S),
 \qquad
 z=\sum_Kd_K=\mu_q(S)-\mu_q(\tau S).
\]

The pair contribution to \(\mathcal V_{q,G}\) is

\[
 \frac12\sum_Kd_K^2.
\]

Its orbit mean is half-integral exactly when \(z\) is odd, and hence its
contribution to \(\Theta_{q,G}\) is

\[
 \frac12\mathbf1_{\{z\text{ odd}\}}.
\]

Therefore the pair contribution to the harmful excess is exactly

\[
 \boxed{
 \frac12\left(
 \sum_Kd_K^2-\mathbf1_{\{z\text{ odd}\}}
 \right).}
 \tag{8.2}
\]

This is the audited parity-corrected one-transposition component excess.
Thus the subgroup theorem has the same normalization as the established
binary heat identity; there is no hidden factor of two or four.
