# Coherent \(H\)-memory routing in the BTK product SCD has an asymptotically full sink set

Date: 2026-07-26

Method: pure mathematics only. No computation, solver, search, or web
input is used.

## 0. Theorem and verdict

Let

\[
 \Omega=A\mathbin{\dot\cup}B,\qquad |A|=|B|=m,
 \qquad W=\binom{2m}{m},
\]

and put the standard BTK symmetric-chain decomposition on each half.
Its rank-\(m\) product diagonals partition
\(\binom{\Omega}{m}\) into atomic Johnson paths.

Set

\[
 H=\left\lfloor\sqrt m\,\omega\right\rfloor,
 \qquad \omega=\log\log m.
\tag{0.1}
\]

A **coherent join** consists of traversing one whole atomic path, taking
one Johnson seam between endpoints, and then traversing the next whole
atomic path from that endpoint.  It is state-valid when its physical
edge-label word obeys two-sided \(H\)-safety.

The following gives a negative answer for the fixed BTK/product-SCD
architecture even after arbitrary histories are replaced by histories
actually generated by the route.

### Theorem 0.1 (coherent sink theorem)

Let \(P\) be a BTK product diagonal of length \(h\).  If

\[
                         5\le h,\qquad 2h+3\le H,
\tag{0.2}
\]

then, after either complete orientation of \(P\) is traversed, there is
no state-valid coherent join to any Johnson-adjacent oriented product
diagonal.

Thus both orientations of \(P\) are sinks in the coherent
\(H\)-memory endpoint-routing graph.

### Theorem 0.2 (sink census and path-cover obstruction)

Let \(\mathcal S_H\) be the set of product diagonals satisfying (0.2).
Then

\[
 |\mathcal S_H|
 =\binom m{\lfloor m/2\rfloor}^{\!2}-o(W/H)
 =\left(\frac{2}{\sqrt\pi}+o(1)\right)\frac{W}{\sqrt m}
 =\left(\frac{2}{\sqrt\pi}+o(1)\right)
     \omega\,\frac WH.
\tag{0.3}
\]

Consequently, any state-valid path cover by whole BTK product diagonals
which omits \(u\) middle owners has at least

\[
 p\ge |\mathcal S_H|-u
\tag{0.4}
\]

components.  In particular, if \(u=o(W/H)\), then

\[
 p\ge
 \left(\frac{2}{\sqrt\pi}+o(1)\right)
 \omega\,\frac WH,
\tag{0.5}
\]

which is not \(o(W/H)\).

This is a genuine generated-history obstruction.  The forbidden queue at
the end of \(P\) is not chosen adversarially: it is the physical label
word of \(P\) itself.

It rules out the corrected stateful fusion gate for the fixed BTK product
SCD.  It does not rule out an SCD whose adjacent endpoint blocks replace
a positive fraction of their active alphabets, nor an architecture which
splits the atomic diagonals before the repeated label is exposed.

## 1. Product diagonals and their exact census

Let \(C,D\) be BTK chains on \(A,B\), with minimum ranks \(a,b\).
Write

\[
 r=\max(a,b),\qquad h=m-2r.
\tag{1.1}
\]

The rank-\(m\) slice of their product is the Johnson path

\[
 P(C,D)=
 \bigl(C_{r+t}\cup D_{m-r-t}:0\le t\le h\bigr).
\tag{1.2}
\]

It has \(h\) edges.  Its low and high endpoints have \(A\)-ranks

\[
 r=\frac{m-h}{2},
 \qquad
 m-r=\frac{m+h}{2}.
\tag{1.3}
\]

Traversing low to high inserts each member of an \(h\)-set
\(I_A(P)\subseteq A\) exactly once and removes each member of an
\(h\)-set \(I_B(P)\subseteq B\) exactly once.  Traversing in the reverse
direction uses the same physical labels in reverse roles.

Every SCD of \(2^{[m]}\) has

\[
 \binom mr-\binom m{r-1}
\tag{1.4}
\]

chains of minimum rank \(r\), and \(\binom mr\) chains of minimum rank at
most \(r\).  Hence the exact number of product diagonals of length
\(h=m-2r\) is

\[
 \boxed{
 P_h=\binom mr^2-\binom m{r-1}^2.}
\tag{1.5}
\]

The product diagonals partition the middle owners.  Notice that (1.5)
counts blocks, while

\[
 \sum_h(h+1)P_h=W
\tag{1.6}
\]

counts their vertices.

## 2. The record-tail interface input

We use the exact BTK endpoint lemma proved in
MATH_OBSTRUCTION_BTK_PRODUCT_SCD_ENDPOINT_RECORD_TAIL_CUT_20260726.md.
It is recalled here in precisely the form needed for coherent routing.

### Lemma 2.1 (BTK adjacent-endpoint overlap)

Let \(X\) be the high endpoint of a product diagonal \(P\) of length
\(h\ge5\), and let \(Y\) be any Johnson-adjacent product-diagonal
endpoint.  If \(Q\) is the unique product diagonal ending at \(Y\), then:

\[
 Y\text{ is a high endpoint},\qquad
 h(Q)\in\{h-2,h,h+2\},
\tag{2.1}
\]

and

\[
 \boxed{|I_A(P)\cap I_A(Q)|\ge h-4>0.}
\tag{2.2}
\]

For low endpoints the complement-reversal version of (2.1)--(2.2)
holds, with a nonempty common physical active-label set.

#### Justification

For a high BTK endpoint, \(I_A(P)\) is the terminal \(h\)-set of strict
ascending record times of its \(A\)-word.  A Johnson move changes that
record set in at most four places; a cross-half move changes the
\(A\)-rank by one and hence changes \(h\) by two.  The terminal-set edit
bound gives (2.2).  Since the \(A\)-rank of \(X\) is
\((m+h)/2\), a neighboring endpoint still has \(A\)-rank above \(m/2\)
when \(h\ge5\), so it is high and (2.1) follows.  BTK is invariant under
reverse-complement together with the half swap, giving the low version.
\(\square\)

The essential feature is not merely that the next block meets a chosen
forbidden set.  It must reuse a physical label which the immediately
preceding block has actually used.

## 3. The coherent repeated-label obstruction

We use the exact two-sided safety criterion from
MATH_LEMMA_TWO_SIDED_H_SAFETY_AND_STATEFUL_FUSION_20260726.md:

> a directed Johnson word is safe through both lower and upper depths
> \(H\) if and only if no physical coordinate occurs on two edges in any
> \(H\)-edge window.

### Proof of Theorem 0.1

Orient \(P\) from low to high, and suppose that a coherent successor
exists.  Its word has the form

\[
 \underbrace{e_1\cdots e_h}_{P}\;
 \underbrace{s}_{\text{seam}}\;
 \underbrace{f_1\cdots f_{h'}}_{Q},
\tag{3.1}
\]

where the seam joins the high endpoint \(X\) of \(P\) to an endpoint
\(Y\) of \(Q\).

By Lemma 2.1, \(Y\) is high,

\[
                         h'\le h+2,
\tag{3.2}
\]

and there is a coordinate

\[
                         c\in I_A(P)\cap I_A(Q).
\tag{3.3}
\]

The coordinate \(c\) occurs on exactly one of the \(P\)-edges and
exactly one of the \(Q\)-edges.  If their positions in the complete word
are \(i<j\), then

\[
 1\le i\le h,\qquad
 h+2\le j\le h+h'+1,
\]

and therefore

\[
                         j-i\le h+h'\le2h+2\le H-1.
\tag{3.4}
\]

The two occurrences lie in one \(H\)-edge window.  This contradicts
two-sided \(H\)-safety.  If the seam itself also uses \(c\), the
violation only occurs sooner.

For the reverse orientation, start at the high endpoint and finish at
the low endpoint.  Apply the low-end version of Lemma 2.1 to the proposed
successor.  It again supplies a common physical active label and the same
bound (3.4).  Hence neither orientation has a coherent successor.
\(\square\)

This proof uses no arbitrary forbidden queue.  It also does not matter
what valid history preceded \(P\): the two offending occurrences are
both inside the displayed coherent join.

## 4. Exact telescoping count of sinks

Let \(L\) be the largest integer having the parity of \(m\) such that

\[
                         5\le L,\qquad 2L+3\le H.
\tag{4.1}
\]

Thus

\[
                         L=\frac H2+O(1).
\tag{4.2}
\]

Put

\[
 R=\left\lfloor\frac m2\right\rfloor,
 \qquad r_L=\frac{m-L}{2}.
\tag{4.3}
\]

As \(h\) runs through the parity class of \(m\), the parameter
\(r=(m-h)/2\) runs through consecutive integers.  Formula (1.5)
therefore telescopes:

\[
 \sum_{\substack{0\le h\le L\\h\equiv m\ (2)}}P_h
 =\binom mR^2-\binom m{r_L-1}^2.
\tag{4.4}
\]

The finitely many terms with \(h<5\) contribute

\[
                         O\left(\frac1m\binom mR^2\right).
\tag{4.5}
\]

Indeed, for fixed \(h\), the exact difference in (1.5) is at most a
constant times \(m^{-1}\binom mR^2\).

It remains to estimate the second term in (4.4).  For
\(d=R-(r_L-1)=L/2+O(1)\), the exact central ratio is a product of \(d\)
factors.  Since \(L=o(m^{2/3})\), its logarithm gives

\[
 \log
 \frac{\binom m{r_L-1}}{\binom mR}
 =-\frac{L^2}{2m}+o\left(\frac{L^2}{m}\right).
\tag{4.6}
\]

After squaring and using \(L=H/2+O(1)\),

\[
 \frac{\binom m{r_L-1}^2}{\binom mR^2}
 =\exp\left[-\frac{L^2}{m}
       +o\left(\frac{L^2}{m}\right)\right]
 =\exp\left[-\frac{\omega^2}{4}+o(\omega^2)\right]
 =o(1).
\tag{4.7}
\]

Equations (4.4)--(4.7) prove

\[
 \binom mR^2-|\mathcal S_H|
 =\binom m{r_L-1}^2
   +\sum_{\substack{0\le h<5\\h\equiv m\ (2)}}P_h
 =o(W/H).
\tag{4.8}
\]

Indeed, after division by \(W/H\), the two error terms are respectively

\[
 O\left(\omega
   \exp[-\omega^2/4+o(\omega^2)]\right)=o(1),
 \qquad
 O(\omega/m)=o(1).
\tag{4.9}
\]

Finally, Stirling's formula gives

\[
 \binom mR
 =\left(\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi m}}+o(m^{-1/2})\right)2^m,
\qquad
 W=\left(\frac1{\sqrt{\pi m}}+o(m^{-1/2})\right)4^m.
\tag{4.10}
\]

Consequently

\[
 \binom mR^2
 =\left(\frac{2}{\sqrt\pi}+o(1)\right)\frac W{\sqrt m}
 =\left(\frac{2}{\sqrt\pi}+o(1)\right)
    \omega\,\frac WH,
\tag{4.11}
\]

proving (0.3).

## 5. The path-cover lower bound

Orient every retained product diagonal according to the directed cover.
By Theorem 0.1, every retained member of \(\mathcal S_H\) has no legal
successor and hence must be the final block of its own directed
component.  Distinct sink blocks terminate distinct components.

Because the product diagonals partition the middle owners, deleting one
block deletes at least one owner.  A cover omitting \(u\) owners can
therefore omit at most \(u\) members of \(\mathcal S_H\).  This gives

\[
                         p\ge|\mathcal S_H|-u,
\]

which is (0.4).  Combining it with (0.3) proves (0.5).

## 6. Exact boundary and surviving escape

The obstruction applies to:

* the standard BTK SCD on both halves;
* whole rank-\(m\) product diagonals as the routing atoms;
* one Johnson seam between successive endpoint atoms; and
* two-sided safety through
  \(H=\lfloor\sqrt m\log\log m\rfloor\).

It is stronger than the arbitrary-history robust-degree countercut:
almost every atomic block generates a queue which kills every coherent
successor.

The proof identifies the exact property a positive replacement must
break.  If \(I(P)\) denotes the physical active alphabet visible from an
endpoint, a successful SCD cannot have

\[
 |I(P)\cap I(Q)|>0
\tag{6.1}
\]

for every adjacent endpoint block \(Q\) whenever
\(h(P)+h(Q)\le H-1\).  It needs one of:

1. endpoint neighbors with genuinely fresh active alphabets;
2. atomic splitting or a collar long enough to let old labels expire;
3. moving between genuinely different SCD frames; or
4. a non-BTK SCD whose endpoint active map is not stable under one
   Johnson move.

Thus ordinary Johnson expansion remains irrelevant for this fixed
architecture: the generated state graph itself has an asymptotically
full set of sinks.
