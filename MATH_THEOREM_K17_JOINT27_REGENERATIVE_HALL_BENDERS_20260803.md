## 4. Parent-local Hall theorem

Fix a node \(\Pi\) and a finite semantic universe of potential hard heads.
For a complete lifted state \(\theta\) of that node, let

\[
 a_h^\Pi(\theta)=1
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 h\text{ is an active head in the exact child table},
 \tag{4.1}
\]

and let \(e_{hu}^\Pi(\theta)\) state that active head \(h\) is incident with
supplier identity \(u\).  For a semantic shore \(Q\), define

\[
 \Gamma_Q^\Pi(\theta)=
 \sum_{h\in Q}(1-a_h^\Pi(\theta))
 +\sum_u\bigvee_{h\in Q}
    \left(a_h^\Pi(\theta)\wedge e_{hu}^\Pi(\theta)\right).
 \tag{4.2}
\]

The OR in (4.2) is essential: several heads or several DNF alternatives
exposing the same supplier count once.

### Theorem 4.1 (exact local Hall cut)

Every complete state of supplier deficiency at most \(d\) satisfies

\[
 \boxed{\Gamma_Q^\Pi(\theta)\ge |Q|-d}
 \tag{4.3}
\]

for every \(Q\).  One maximum matching either meets the target or returns a
violated shore.

#### Proof

The active heads in \(Q\) have cardinality
\[
 |Q|-\sum_{h\in Q}(1-a_h^\Pi).
\]
Their distinct neighborhood has the
cardinality of the second sum in (4.2).  Rearranging Hall's deficiency
inequality gives (4.3). \(\square\)

For the new shore \(Q_{27}\), target deficiency 21 requires

\[
 \boxed{\Gamma_{Q_{27}}^{\Pi_{27}}\ge 23-21=2.}
 \tag{4.4}
\]

At \(T_{27}\), all 23 shore heads are active and their neighborhood is the
single supplier 14851, so the left side is 1.  Thus the exact next obligation
is one **net** new Hall credit.  It is not an obligation to select one more
root action.  For example, retiring the only head that exposes supplier
14851 would add one inactive-head term and lose the one neighbor term, for
net gain zero.

The old fixed-face 50-head shore is no replacement for (4.4).  Evaluated on
\(T_{27}\), it has 28 inactive heads and five neighbors, hence value 33.  It
already satisfies its target-21 row \(33\ge 50-21=29\), while the new 23/1
shore still proves deficiency 22.  A fresh maximum matching is therefore a
logical requirement, not an optimization heuristic.

## 6. Recursive Benders theorem

Let \(\delta(\Pi)\) be the exact supplier deficiency of a fully materialized
node, \(X_\Pi\) its parent-local master choices, \(\mathcal R_\Pi(x)\) the
exact residual completion set, and \(\Phi_\Pi(x,r)\) the canonical
authenticated child.  For a depth bound \(k\), define

\[
 \operatorname{Reach}^{0}_{d}(\Pi)
 \iff \delta(\Pi)\le d,
 \tag{6.1}
\]

\[
 \operatorname{Reach}^{k+1}_{d}(\Pi)
 \iff
 \delta(\Pi)\le d
 \;\lor\;
 \exists x\in X_\Pi\ \exists r\in\mathcal R_\Pi(x):
 \operatorname{Reach}^{k}_{d}(\Phi_\Pi(x,r)).
 \tag{6.2}
\]

### Theorem 6.1 (sound recursive exclusion)

A projected no-good on \(x\) at remaining depth \(k+1\) is sound exactly when
the certified subproblem proves

\[
 \forall r\in\mathcal R_\Pi(x),\qquad
 \neg\operatorname{Reach}^{k}_{d}(\Phi_\Pi(x,r)).
 \tag{6.3}
\]

Failure of one chosen completion supplies only a lifted no-good for that
\((x,r)\), unless its literal activation/incidence functions have been exposed
in a universally valid parent-local cut.

#### Proof

Equation (6.2) is an existential disjunction over all exact successors.  A
no-good for \(x\) is valid precisely when every disjunct below \(x\) is false,
which is (6.3). \(\square\)

For a finite canonical state graph, unbounded reachability is the least fixed
point of (6.2).  A complete negative proof needs exhaustive successor
coverage or a certified closed non-target region.  A controller that insists
on strict deficiency descent terminates after finitely many accepted steps,
but failure of that controller proves only the restricted monotone policy:
supplier deficiency is not a general potential for all valid paths.

## 7. What transports across a parent change

The following schemas may be reinstantiated:

- the abstract Hall inequality (4.3);
- universal common-basis, matching, row-capacity, and protected-row rules;
- a semantic head set accompanied by an authenticated row/head map.

The following instantiated objects do not transport by default:

- mode, occurrence, role, root-action, SCC, ticket, or supplier-edge IDs;
- head-activity and supplier-incidence DNFs;
- distinct-supplier ORs, fixed/dynamic splits, constants, or coefficients;
- allowed-edge/SCC screens;
- a parent-local no-good, DRAT conclusion, matching, or Hall neighborhood;
- a singleton action's materialization or claimed supplier credit.

An old shore may be recompiled only after authenticating its semantic head
map and rebuilding every activity/incidence predicate over all new
parent-local columns.  Exact transport of an instantiated cut would require
equality of all those circuits after relabelling; unchanged row footprints or
unchanged protected bytes do not establish that equality.

This is a SHA-indexed branch--Benders tree with local relinearization, not one
monotone global incidence model.
