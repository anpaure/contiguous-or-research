# The first primitive multi-kink Apéry extreme occurs at period five and is positive

**Date:** 2026-08-05  
**Method:** pure mathematics; exact cyclic-block cone classification and
Fourier reserve; no computation, search, or solver  
**Status:** unconditional.  The cone of cyclically superadditive Apéry
increments is generated by one-kink rays and proper-period lifts through
residue period four.  At period five it has a genuinely primitive new
extreme ray, the mechanical word `01011`.  Its complete Rayleigh phase
functional is nevertheless strictly positive throughout the normalized
period range `P<=zeta`.  Thus a one-kink cone decomposition is false, but
the smallest missing extreme is not a formal counterclock.

## 1. Cyclic block characterization

Let

\[
 0=s_0\le s_1\le\cdots\le s_g=P
\]

be a shift table, and put

\[
 x_i=s_i-s_{i-1}\qquad(1\le i\le g).
\tag{1.1}
\]

Indices on the increments are cyclic modulo `g`.  For a starting index
`a` and a length `j`, write

\[
 B_a(j)=x_{a+1}+\cdots+x_{a+j}.
\tag{1.2}
\]

### Lemma 1.1 (universal minimum rotation)

The table `s` is internally cyclically superadditive if and only if

\[
 \boxed{B_0(j)\le B_a(j)
 \quad(0\le a<g,\ 1\le j<g).}
\tag{1.3}
\]

Thus every prefix block is a minimum-weight cyclic block of its length.

#### Proof

If `a+j<=g`, then

\[
 B_a(j)=s_{a+j}-s_a\ge s_j=B_0(j)
\]

is ordinary superadditivity.  If `a+j>g`, the carry inequality gives

\[
 B_a(j)=P+s_{a+j-g}-s_a\ge s_j=B_0(j).
\]

The same two identities read backward prove the converse. \(\square\)

In particular, the length-one inequality says that `x_1` is a global
minimum, while the length-`g-1` inequality says that `x_g` is a global
maximum.

The one-kink tables

\[
 s_r=\eta(r-h)_+
\]

have increment rays

\[
 v_h=(\underbrace{0,\ldots,0}_{h},
       \underbrace{1,\ldots,1}_{g-h}),
 \qquad0\le h<g.
\tag{1.4}
\]

Their nonnegative cone is exactly the cone of nonnegative nondecreasing
increment sequences.

## 2. Exact classification through period four

### Theorem 2.1

For `g<=3`, every admissible increment vector is nondecreasing and hence
is a nonnegative combination of the one-kink rays (1.4).

For `g=4`, every admissible vector is a sum of a nondecreasing vector and
a nonnegative multiple of

\[
 p=(0,1,0,1).
\tag{2.1}
\]

The ray `p` is the period-two lift and therefore collapses by a proper
critical residue.  Consequently there is no genuinely primitive new
extreme ray before period five.

#### Proof

For `g=2`, (1.3) says `x_1<=x_2`.  For `g=3`, the length-one and
length-two conditions say respectively that `x_1` is minimum and `x_3`
is maximum, so

\[
 x_1\le x_2\le x_3.
\]

For `g=4`, the length-one and length-three conditions say exactly

\[
 x_1\le x_2,x_3,x_4,
 \qquad
 x_4\ge x_1,x_2,x_3.
\tag{2.2}
\]

These imply every length-two inequality, so they characterize the cone.
Subtract the constant vector `x_1(1,1,1,1)` and write the result as

\[
 y=(0,y_2,y_3,y_4),
 \qquad y_4\ge\max(y_2,y_3).
\]

If `y_2<=y_3`, this vector is nondecreasing.  If `y_2>y_3`, put
`t=y_2-y_3`.  Then

\[
 y-tp=(0,y_3,y_3,y_4-y_2+y_3)
\]

is nonnegative and nondecreasing.  This proves the decomposition.

For `p`, the shift prefixes are `(0,0,1,1,2)`.  At residue two,

\[
 s_2=1={2P\over4},
\]

so the residue defect vanishes and the fundamental-period theorem
collapses the clock to period two. \(\square\)

## 3. The first primitive new extreme

Put

\[
 \boxed{x^*=(0,1,0,1,1).}
\tag{3.1}
\]

Its prefix shifts are

\[
 (s_0,s_1,s_2,s_3,s_4,s_5)=(0,0,1,1,2,3).
\tag{3.2}
\]

### Theorem 3.1

The vector `x^*` is an extreme ray of the period-five cyclic-block cone.
Its Apéry table is primitive.  It is not in the cone generated by
period-five one-kink rays and proper-period lifts.

#### Proof

The cyclic block sums of lengths one through four are:

\[
\begin{array}{c|ccccc}
j& B_0(j)&B_1(j)&B_2(j)&B_3(j)&B_4(j)\\ \hline
1&0&1&0&1&1\\
2&1&1&1&2&1\\
3&1&2&2&2&2\\
4&2&3&2&3&2.
\end{array}
\tag{3.3}
\]

The first entry is minimal in every row, so Lemma 1.1 proves
admissibility.

Suppose `x^*=y+z` with `y,z` admissible.  Every inequality active at
`x^*` must be active separately at `y` and `z`.  Nonnegativity and
`x_1^*=x_3^*=0` give

\[
 y_1=y_3=0,
 \qquad z_1=z_3=0.
\]

The active length-two equalities

\[
 B_0(2)=B_2(2)=B_4(2)
\]

then give

\[
 y_2=y_4=y_5,
 \qquad z_2=z_4=z_5.
\]

Thus both `y` and `z` are scalar multiples of `x^*`; the ray is extreme.

After scaling (3.2) by `eta>0`, its endpoint is `P=3eta`, with maximum
density `lambda=3eta/5`.  For every proper residue,

\[
 0=s_1<{3\eta\over5},
 \quad
 \eta<{6\eta\over5},
 \quad
 \eta<{9\eta\over5},
 \quad
 2\eta<{12\eta\over5}.
\]

Hence every proper defect is positive and the table is primitive.
Finally, five is prime, so a proper-period lift is constant.  The
one-kink cone is nondecreasing, whereas `x_2^*>x_3^*`.  Therefore this
ray belongs to neither class. \(\square\)

The word `01011` is the lower mechanical word

\[
 x_r^*=\left\lfloor{3r\over5}\right\rfloor
       -\left\lfloor{3(r-1)\over5}\right\rfloor.
\tag{3.4}
\]

So the first missing extreme is a genuine balanced rotor, not an
unstructured perturbation.

## 4. Exact phase reduction of the new ray

Fix `eta>0`, put `P=3eta`, and write

\[
 F_P(a)=\sum_{q\ge0}K(qP+a).
\]

The complete phase functional of (3.2) is

\[
\begin{aligned}
 \Phi_5^*(\eta)
 &=2F_P(0)+2F_P(\eta)+F_P(2\eta)\\
 &=C(\eta)+\Psi(\eta),
\end{aligned}
\tag{4.1}
\]

where

\[
 \Psi(\eta)=F_P(0)+F_P(\eta).
\tag{4.2}
\]

Indeed, the three complete residue classes modulo `P=3eta` give

\[
 F_P(0)+F_P(\eta)+F_P(2\eta)=C(\eta).
\]

It remains only to sign the two-phase rotor `Psi`.

## 5. A strict Fourier reserve for the two-phase rotor

Let `H` count the clock points

\[
 qP,qquad qP+\eta\qquad(q\ge0),
\]

and put

\[
 D(x)=H(x)-{2x\over P}.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Equal work cancels the linear term, so

\[
 \Psi(\eta)=\int_0^\infty D(x)q(x)dx,
 \qquad q=-K'.
\tag{5.2}
\]

The discrepancy `D` is `P`-periodic.  On one period its mean is

\[
 \widehat D_0=1-{\eta\over P}={2\over3}.
\tag{5.3}
\]

For `n!=0`, its periodic distributional derivative gives

\[
 \widehat D_n
 ={1+e^{-2\pi i n/3}\over2\pi i n},
\tag{5.4}
\]

up to the immaterial common sign fixed by Fourier convention.  Therefore

\[
 |1+e^{-2\pi i n/3}|=
 \begin{cases}
 2,&3\mid n,\\
 1,&3\nmid n,
 \end{cases}
\]

and

\[
 \sum_{n\ge1}{|1+e^{-2\pi i n/3}|\over n^3}
 ={28\over27}\zeta(3).
\tag{5.5}
\]

The all-period transform theorem gives

\[
 \left|\int_0^\infty q(x)e^{2\pi i n x/P}dx\right|
 <{B P^2\over4\pi^2n^2},
 \qquad B={5324\over875}.
\tag{5.6}
\]

The Fourier products are absolutely summable.  Pairing positive and
negative frequencies, their total absolute contribution is less than

\[
 {B P^2\over4\pi^3}{28\over27}\zeta(3)
 ={7B\eta^2\over3\pi^3}\zeta(3).
\tag{5.7}
\]

The zeroth mode is `2M/3`.  In the normalized Apéry range `P<=zeta`, the
proved bound `zeta^2<2/3` gives

\[
 \eta^2={P^2\over9}<{2\over27}.
\]

Together with `zeta(3)<5/4`, `pi^3>27`, and `M>11/125`, equation (5.7)
gives

\[
 \left|\text{nonzero modes}\right|
 <{2662\over54675},
\tag{5.8}
\]

while

\[
 {2M\over3}>{22\over375}.
\tag{5.9}
\]

Their exact rational difference is

\[
 {22\over375}-{2662\over54675}
 ={2728\over273375}>0.
\tag{5.10}
\]

Hence

\[
 \boxed{
 \Psi(\eta)>{2728\over273375}>0
 \qquad(3\eta\le\zeta).}
\tag{5.11}
\]

## 6. Positive reserve for the first new extreme

The all-mesh arithmetic-comb theorem gives

\[
 C(\eta)>{377\over108000}.
\]

Combining this with (4.1) and (5.11) proves

\[
 \boxed{
 \Phi_5^*(\eta)
 >{377\over108000}+{2728\over273375}>0
 \qquad(0<3\eta\le\zeta).}
\tag{6.1}
\]

Thus the first primitive multi-kink extreme has a dimension-free strict
reserve throughout the entire allowed period range.

## 7. Exact consequence and remaining boundary

The proposed decomposition of every Apéry increment profile into
one-kink rays and proper-period lifts is false.  Its first failure is the
period-five mechanical extreme `01011`.  Nevertheless that ray is
positive, by an exact decomposition into one arithmetic comb and one
strictly positive two-phase rotor.

This does not classify the cyclic-block cone for arbitrary `g`, and
positivity on profile extreme rays would not by itself sign a nonlinear
phase functional on their convex hull.  The next viable extremal theorem
must therefore control the Rayleigh phase functional under interpolation,
or prove directly that a minimizing profile is mechanical/duty-cycle.
Finite-availability shoulders remain separate.
