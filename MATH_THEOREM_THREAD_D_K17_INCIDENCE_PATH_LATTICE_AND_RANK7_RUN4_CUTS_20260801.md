## 1. The free quotient incidence lattice

Let

\[
 \mathcal L=\binom{[17]}8/Z_{17},\qquad
 \mathcal O=\binom{[17]}9/Z_{17}.                  \tag{1.1}
\]

Every nontrivial `Z_17` subset orbit is free, so both shores have size
`binom(17,8)/17=1430`.  Fix one representative on each shore.  An edge
`e=(L,T,s)` means

\[
                 L\subseteq \rho^s T,              \tag{1.2}
\]

with the unique aligned physical containment represented by that edge.
There are nine edges at every vertex and `12870` in total.  Parallel edges
are retained: they have different alignment voltages.

Orient every edge from `mathcal L` to `mathcal O`, and let `B` be the signed
node-edge incidence matrix.

### Theorem 1.1 (saturated fundamental-cycle basis)

The graph is connected.  If `K` is any spanning tree and `e` is a chord,
let `gamma_e` be the signed alternating vector of the unique cycle in
`K+e`, normalized to have coefficient `+1` on `e`.  Then

\[
 \ker_{\mathbb Z}B
    =\bigoplus_{e\notin K}\mathbb Z\gamma_e,       \tag{1.3}
\]

and its rank is

\[
             12870-2860+1=10011.                  \tag{1.4}
\]

In particular the cycle lattice is saturated.

#### Proof

Signed graph-incidence matrices are totally unimodular.  Delete one row of
`B` and restrict to the tree columns.  The resulting square matrix has
determinant `+-1`.  Eliminating each chord against this unimodular tree
matrix gives its fundamental cycle with integral coefficients.  Every
integer circulation is uniquely reduced chord by chord, proving (1.3), and
the same determinant proves saturation.  Connectedness and the displayed
counts are independently replayed by the audit in Section 8.  `square`

### Proposition 1.2 (formal physical `C6/C8` generators)

The integer cycle lattice of the physical rank-eight/rank-nine incidence
graph is generated by signed `C6` and `C8` cycles.  Their quotient
projections generate the zero-voltage lattice in (2.2).

#### Proof

At every rank-nine vertex `T`, the incident coefficient vector of an
incidence circulation has sum zero.  Choose one facet of `T` as a temporary
base and write that vector integrally as a sum of differences of two
facets.  Replace each difference by the corresponding oriented edge of
`J(17,8)`.  The incidence balance at rank-eight vertices says that the
result is a Johnson circulation.  Lifting a Johnson edge `L->L'` through
the unique rank-nine set `L union L'` recovers the original incidence
chain.  Changing the temporary base changes the Johnson decomposition by
triangles inside the nine-facet top clique, and each such top triangle
lifts to the zero chain.

The Johnson graph is the one-skeleton of the hypersimplex.  Since the
hypersimplex is convex, the boundaries of its two-faces generate the
integer cycle lattice of its one-skeleton.  Those two-faces are triangles
and squares.  A bottom triangle lifts to an alternating `C6`, a square
lifts to an alternating `C8`, and a top-clique triangle vanishes as in the
preceding paragraph.  This proves the physical assertion.  For a connected
regular `Z_17` cover, the image of the physical first homology in quotient
first homology is the kernel of the voltage homomorphism, proving the second
assertion.  `square`

Degenerate quotient projections may be zero, repeated, or shorter signed
walks; all are retained as formal signed generator columns.  Being a
generator is not the same as being an applicable alternating switch in a
fixed factor: applicability and resource conflicts remain separate rows.

## 2. Voltage and an explicit zero-voltage basis

For a signed quotient cycle `x`, define

\[
             \omega(x)=\sum_e s_e x_e\pmod {17}.  \tag{2.1}
\]

This is exactly the sheet displacement of its physical lift.  The audit
finds a fundamental cycle with nonzero voltage, so, because `17` is prime,

\[
 \omega(\ker_{\mathbb Z}B)=\mathbb Z_{17},
 \qquad
 \ker_{\mathbb Z}B/(\ker_{\mathbb Z}B\cap\ker\omega)
       \cong\mathbb Z/17\mathbb Z.                 \tag{2.2}
\]

This also gives an explicit construction of generator columns for K once
the audit's tree and chord vectors are serialized.  (The current audit
emits their statistics, not the columns.)  Choose a fundamental
cycle `gamma_0` with voltage `u!=0`.  For every other fundamental cycle
choose `a_e in {0,...,16}` with

\[
                 a_eu=\omega(\gamma_e)\pmod {17}. \tag{2.3}
\]

Then

\[
  17\gamma_0,\qquad \gamma_e-a_e\gamma_0\quad(e\ne0) \tag{2.4}
\]

is a `Z`-basis of the zero-voltage sublattice: it lies in the kernel and
its change-of-basis determinant has absolute value `17`, the known index.

For a fixed oriented component an alternating packet preserves monodromy
only if its signed delta has voltage zero.  If a packet changes component
topology or reverses a component, monodromy must instead be replayed after
the switch; (2.4) must not be used as a substitute for that topology audit.

There is one implementation sign convention to retain.  This section uses
`L subset rho^s T`, whereas K's incidence map stores
`rho^{s_K}L subset T`.  Thus `s=-s_K (mod 17)`.  Surjectivity and the
zero-voltage kernel are unchanged, but a serialized basis must negate the
stored shifts before using (2.1).

## 3. The native rank-seven bank

At an owner `T`, choose two distinct incident rank-eight facets

\[
                 F_x=T-\{x\},\qquad F_y=T-\{y\}.  \tag{3.1}
\]

Their intersection `T-{x,y}` is the native triple-owner rank-seven colour.
It is independent of the direction in which the two facets are traversed
and of a common physical phase.

### Theorem 3.1 (`36/45` biregularity and Hall slack)

There are `binom(9,2)=36` rooted turns at every owner orbit and
`binom(10,2)=45` rooted turns producing every rank-seven target orbit.
Thus the turn graph has

\[
          1430\cdot36=1144\cdot45=51480           \tag{3.2}
\]

edges and contains a matching saturating all `1144` rank-seven targets.

#### Proof

The first degree is (3.1).  A fixed physical seven-set has ten missing
coordinates, and choosing two gives its `45` rank-nine supersets.  Freeness
divides both sides by `17` without changing these rooted degrees.  For any
set `X` of target orbits, the `45|X|` incident edges meet an owner at most
`36` times, so

\[
                 |N(X)|\ge(45/36)|X|\ge |X|.       \tag{3.3}
\]

Hall proves the claim.  `square`

This theorem is prospective.  A selected degree-two factor chooses only
one of the 36 turns at each owner.  It must still be constrained to cover
all targets.

For incidence variables `x_e`, introduce `ell_{o,e,f}` for each unordered
pair of incidences at owner `o` and impose

\[
 \ell\le x_e,\quad \ell\le x_f,
 \quad \ell\ge x_e+x_f-1.                         \tag{3.4}
\]

The degree-two row makes exactly one such variable true at every owner.
For every rank-seven orbit `Q`, add

\[
 \sum_{(o,e,f):\,[F_e\cap F_f]=Q}\ell_{o,e,f}\ge1. \tag{3.5}
\]

Rows (3.4)--(3.5) are orientation-free and exact for native rank-seven
surjectivity.  They do not assign the 1144 rank-seven age types to those
occurrences.

## 4. Exact run four and the four-cut theorem

Fix an oriented quotient cycle of distinct owners `T_i`, indexed by
`P=Z_1430`, and assume the adjacent rank-eight colours are distinct.  Here
`T_i,alpha_i,beta_i` are kept in one voltage-consistent physical gauge
along the lift; crossing the quotient boundary applies the accumulated
monodromy rather than resetting to canonical representatives.  Put

\[
        \gamma(i)=[T_i\cap T_{i+1}\cap T_{i+2}]
             \in\mathcal R_7,                     \tag{4.1}
\]

where `|mathcal R_7|=1144`.

Let `G_4` be the positions at which a coordinate has a literal exact
four-owner run: it is absent at `T_{i-1}`, present at
`T_i,T_{i+1},T_{i+2},T_{i+3}`, and absent at `T_{i+4}`.  There is at most
one such coordinate at one start, because one coordinate is inserted at a
Johnson transition.  On a valid four-deletion spine this is equivalently

\[
                       \beta_{i-1}=\alpha_{i+3}.   \tag{4.2}
\]

Without the spine hypothesis the literal six-state definition, not merely
(4.2), must be encoded.

The certified nine age types project to four classes:

| class | type vectors | run-four required | rank-seven representative | size |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| `A0` | `(1,5,2,1)` | yes | no | 139 |
| `A1` | `(1,6,1,1)` | yes | yes | 297 |
| `B` | `(2,5,1,1),(3,4,1,1),(4,3,1,1),(5,2,1,1),(6,1,1,1)` | no | yes | 847 |
| `C` | `(3,3,2,1),(5,1,2,1)` | no | no | 147 |

A coarse placement is a partition
`P=A0 dotcup A1 dotcup B dotcup C` with these sizes,
`A0 union A1 subset G_4`, and `gamma` mapping `A1 union B`
bijectively onto `mathcal R_7`.

### Theorem 4.1 (exact fixed-cycle criterion)

A coarse placement exists if and only if

\[
\begin{array}{ll}
({\rm C0})&\gamma(P)=\mathcal R_7,\\
({\rm C1})&|G_4|\ge436,\\
({\rm C2})&|\gamma(G_4)|\ge297,\\
({\rm C3})&|G_4|+|\gamma(P\setminus G_4)|\ge1283.
\end{array}                                        \tag{4.3}
\]

#### Proof

Necessity of C0 is the rank-seven bijection.  The `139+297` positions of
`A0 union A1` prove C1, and the 297 distinct colours represented on `A1`
prove C2.

Put `O=gamma(P-G_4)` and `F=mathcal R_7-O`.  Every colour in `F` must use a
distinct representative in `G_4`; the 139 positions of `A0` are additional.
Thus

\[
 |G_4|\ge |F|+139=1144-|O|+139,                   \tag{4.4}
\]

which is C3.

Conversely assume C0--C3.  Choose a set
`D subset gamma(G_4)` of 297 colours containing all of `F` if `|F|<=297`,
or 297 colours of `F` otherwise.  By C0, `F subset gamma(G_4)`; C2 then
makes this choice possible.  Give each colour
of `D` a distinct occurrence in `G_4` and put those positions in `A1`.
Reserve a distinct `G_4` occurrence for every colour of `F-D`; these will
belong to `B`.  The number of used positions is `max(297,|F|)`.  Conditions
C1 and C3 say that at least 139 further `G_4` positions remain; put them in
`A0`.  Indeed C1 and C3 are exactly the two inequalities whose maximum is
`max(297,|F|)+139<=|G_4|`.  Represent every remaining colour from `O-D` outside `G_4` and put
those representatives in `B`.  Then `|B|=1144-297=847`, and the remaining
147 positions form `C`.  This is the required placement.  `square`

The constructive proof shows that this coarse projection has no hidden
modular obstruction: C0--C3 are the complete integral obstruction.

## 5. Rows and cuts consumable by K

For an ordered prospective cycle, let `g_i` be the literal exact-run-four

\[
 w^G_{iQ}=g_i z_{iQ},\qquad
 w^O_{iQ}=(1-g_i)z_{iQ},                           \tag{5.1}
\]

and OR-support variables

\[
 p^G_Q=\bigvee_i w^G_{iQ},\qquad
 p^O_Q=\bigvee_i w^O_{iQ}.                         \tag{5.2}

The cycle-only rows are

\[
\begin{split}
 &\sum_Q z_{iQ}=1 &&(i\in P),\\
 &\sum_i z_{iQ}\ge1 &&(Q\in\mathcal R_7),\\
 &\sum_i g_i\ge436,\\
 &\sum_Qp^G_Q\ge297,\\
 &\sum_i g_i+\sum_Qp^O_Q\ge1283.                 \tag{5.3}
\end{split}

Standard AND/OR clauses make (5.1)--(5.2) exact.  These are the linear form
of C0--C3.

An even stronger exact coarse master uses class variables
`a^0_i,a^1_i,b_i,c_i`, their four exact totals, the rows

\[
 a^0_i+a^1_i+b_i+c_i=1,\qquad
 a^0_i+a^1_i\le g_i,\qquad
 \sum_{i:\gamma(i)=Q}(a^1_i+b_i)=1.               \tag{5.4}

With the original type variables `x_{it}`, define

\begin{split}
 h_i&=x_{i0}+x_{i1},\\
 s_i&=x_{i1}+x_{i2}+x_{i4}+x_{i5}+x_{i7}+x_{i8}.
\end{split}                                        \tag{5.5}

Then `sum_t x_{it}=1`, `h_i<=g_i`, the nine exact type totals, and

are the literal typed version.  Products in (5.6) require guarded

## 6. What `C(ker_Z B)` means after path expansion

Neither `gamma(i)` nor `g_i` is a linear function of one raw incidence
edge.  Therefore there is no honest raw-edge matrix `C` whose image
`C(ker_Z B)` already contains the rank-seven/run-four problem.

There are two proof-safe replacements.

### 6.1 Fixed physical packets

For a fixed typed carrier, give each alternating packet `Gamma` its exact
signed deltas

\[
 \Delta_7(\Gamma),\qquad \Delta_4(\Gamma),
 \qquad\omega(\Gamma),                             \tag{6.1}
\]

computed from the changed owner turns and every affected four-step halo.
Use packet variables with edge-capacity, resource-conflict, voltage-zero,
rank-seven and run-four rows.  If packets overlap in a four-step halo, their
joint delta must be represented by a compound column; summing isolated
deltas is unsound.

### 6.2 Time-expanded successor graph

Expand a state by the current quotient owner/facet data, the age/type state,
and enough bounded history to decide the next four-step ticket.  Let
`B_H` be this directed state-arc incidence matrix and let `C_H` emit the
additive six-suffix incidence counts, the exact-run-four count and
boundary-pin counts.  Monodromy is imposed modulo `17`: either first replace
the fundamental cycles by a basis of their zero-voltage sublattice as in
(2.4), or append a `17`-slack column to the Smith system.  Treating voltage
as an ordinary integer-zero row would be too strong and gauge-dependent.
Then
`B_H` is again a network matrix.  Given one integral endpoint path `p_0` and
a fundamental-cycle matrix `Gamma_H`, every integral solution of the
endpoint equations has the form

\[
                  p=p_0+\Gamma_H z,\qquad z\in\mathbb Z^r. \tag{6.2}
\]

The exact integral endpoint-flow emission lattice is

\[
                C_Hp_0+(C_H\Gamma_H)\mathbb Z^r.  \tag{6.3}
\]

Thus a target delta `delta` passes the lattice test exactly when it belongs
to the integer column lattice of

\[
                         M=C_H\Gamma_H.             \tag{6.4}
\]

If `UMV=diag(d_1,...,d_s,0,...)` is Smith normal form, this is equivalent
to

\[
 d_j\mid(U\delta)_j\ (j\le s),\qquad
 (U\delta)_j=0\ (j>s).                             \tag{6.5}
\]

Passing (6.5) proves only membership in this additive emission-count
lattice.  Global first-use/OR support is not made additive by bounded
history; it remains in explicit support rows unless first-use state is also
included.  Arc bounds, one-copy owner use, connected support and binary path
existence likewise remain separate.  The bare balance-plus-voltage system
has only the universal index `17` from (2.2).  An arbitrary payload image
`C_H(ker B_H)` need not remain saturated; any additional torsion is exactly
what (6.5) is meant to detect in the restricted history/type matrix or in a
chosen packet subcatalogue.  The coarse theorem itself has no such torsion.

## 9. Sharp remaining gate

The raw incidence lattice and the coarse rank-seven/run-four assignment are
now closed.  The exact remaining gate is narrower:

1. choose a connected nonzero-voltage incidence cycle satisfying the eager
   native rank-seven and rank-ten rows and C0--C3;
2. refine its coarse placement to the certified nine-type Euler word while
   satisfying all six suffix rows and the comparator/boundary pins; and
3. on the resulting bounded-history state graph, pass both the Smith test
   (6.5) and the binary one-copy path constraints.

Failure of (6.5) would be a genuine modular obstruction.  Failure of the
binary path after (6.5) would be a nonlattice Hall/odd-set/topology
obstruction.  Neither has been proved by the present audit.
