## 0. Verdict

The theorem note

MATH_THEOREM_K_COHERENT_EDGE_SUPPLY_FORCED_PORT_HALL_ROUTER_CATALOGUE_20260731.md

is mathematically sound in its final prepared scopes.  The strongest new
conclusion is not another Hall or router criterion.  It is this conditional
construction statement:

> one component-faithful unit-expanded ECO hypertree, owner-aligned for one
> exported leaf-forest matching, dynamically matching-seam compatible, and
> equipped with \(\rho(t)=|E_t|-1\) node-private unit occurrence channels
> per atom, executes in every order.

Here and below the execution object is a **unit-expanded ECO hypertree**,
or the equivalent incidence-tree backbone with the atomic weighted router

The coherent-ECO theorem proves static connected component incidence in

a spanning hypertree.

## 1. Factorwise forced-port theorem

The fixed-cycle residual-Hall theorem extends to a disjoint factor exactly
as stated in Lemma 1.1.  A global upper-colour transversal decomposes into
sets \(I_\alpha\) on the factor components.  Each nonempty
\(I_\alpha\) creates one cyclic gap per chosen upper occurrence, and
componentwise alternation chooses exactly one lower occurrence in every
such gap.  Therefore forced lower ports must occupy distinct gaps and
colours; after their endpoints are deleted, the remaining choices are one
global perfect matching.

If \(I_\alpha=\varnothing\), the component has no selected lower port.  If
it is allowed to remain unmarked, the prepared state must store one of its
two residual alternating cross-edge phases.  If every component must be
marked, one instead requires \(I_\alpha\ne\varnothing\) for all
\(\alpha\).  This distinction is explicit in the theorem note.

## 2. Quantifier and selected-tree audit

For one prepared decoration \(\mathcal D\), retain only coherent merge
labels whose six ports and complete frozen signatures are
\(\mathcal D\)-compatible.  The router theorem then applies to that
restricted ground.  Hence the exact order is

\[
                 \exists\mathcal D\ \forall Y,\Pi,                 \tag{2.1}
\]

not a decoration depending on the cut.  Equivalently, one and the same
selected tree must be component-spanning, gammoid-independent and
forced-port feasible.  Separate Hall and router trees do not recombine.

The every-ordering conclusion is valid only because H0--H5 are quantified
on the full subset cube.  Every prefix of a component tree is graphic
independent, one simultaneous linkage restricts to every prefix, and the
gap/reachability guards are assumed on every permitted transition.
Root-only coherence or singleton-toggle checks would not prove it.

## 3. Owner alignment and matching-seam preservation

On a gap forest, the perfect matching is unique.  Thus forced lower port
edges extend if and only if they already lie in that matching.  The
off-forest zero-flux replacement is also correct: on pairwise disjoint
blocks with \(M(G_t)=C_t\), replace the restriction of \(M\) by the forced
bijection.

Dynamic preservation needs more than endpoint leaf-peelability.  At a
private root \(r_t\), the two bank alternatives must have the same matching
degree \(b_t\), must cover every nonroot bank vertex, and the core must
cover every nonroot core vertex with complementary root degree
\(1-b_t\).  These are exactly the conditions making every union matching
perfect.  Without them,

\[
 \{g_1c_1,g_2c_2\}\longrightarrow
 \{g_1c_2,g_2c_1\}                                  \tag{3.1}
\]

changes the owner of a later port although both endpoint graphs are
leaf-peelable.  The corrected Lemma 5.1 carries the complementary root
status explicitly.

## 4. Pascal/cylinder preservation audit

A fixed-coordinate lift preserves a private collar only under three
literal conditions.

1. The added coordinates are fresh and the occurrence map is injective.
2. The lifted cyclic order and upper transversal are exact copies, so no
   new selected upper point subdivides an old gap.
3. The router has a vertex-injective embedding whose different child images
   remain disjoint.

Under these hypotheses the gap graphs are isomorphic, owner edges and
zero-flux blocks lift, coherence keeps the same external labels, and route
privacy survives.  Local coherence alone proves none of these global
facts.

Separately matched children need not assemble to a matched parent.  The
cyclic order \(u_1,c_1,c_2,u_2\) with upper set
\(\{u_1,u_2\}\) has one gap containing both lower colours and one empty
gap, although each child singleton system is matched.  Therefore the
recursion must export the actual transversal, matching owner map and
boundary gap order, or install a zero-flux boundary tube.

## 5. Joint matching-route obstruction

The missing-rectangle example is exact.  Fix the first task at
\((H_0,R_0)\).  If the second task offers precisely

\[
 (H_0,R_0),\quad(H_0,R_1),\quad(H_1,R_0),            \tag{5.1}
\]

then matching privacy alone chooses \(H_1\), route privacy alone chooses
\(R_1\), but the required joint corner \((H_1,R_1)\) is absent.  Up to
renaming, (5.1) is the inclusion-maximal two-by-two menu with nonempty
marginal projections and no joint witness.

The Cartesian recombination lemma is correct: if each literal catalogue is
the product of its matching and route modes and all remaining physical
guards are product-compatible, separately compatible selectors combine
coordinatewise.  Conditional products must use the same terminal-mode
assignment.  Separate accepting mode assignments do not recombine.

The greedy and local-lemma pressure bounds in the theorem note apply only
to literal pairwise option conflicts after private-tube reduction.  Generic
alternating-cycle matching or laminar routing is global and is outside those
bounds.

## 6. ECO supply and topology audit

The authoritative coherent-ECO theorem proves:

* a standard MMM label is coherent exactly when its interior word \(u\) is
  empty;
* those standard coherent labels are disconnected from \(n=5\) onward;
* every Dyck parent gives the explicit coherent central-zero atom; and
* the coherent ECO component hypergraph has connected two-section in every
  dimension.

At one fixed rotation, the physical-port and both forced-owner collision
graphs are in fact the same path forest.  Its edges are

\[
                   1p100v\longrightarrow1p010v,                    \tag{6.1}
\]

and Catalan convolution gives exactly \({\rm Cat}_{n-2}\) edges.  Thus one
independent set removes all three local collision systems.  This proves
collision-free forced faces, not a complete turn transversal or owner
matching.

The owner formula in Corollary 6.3 required a shore correction.  For the
canonical old matching

\[
 L_aU_{ca},\qquad L_bU_{ab},\qquad L_cU_{bc},        \tag{6.2}
\]

the A-position ports \(L_a,L_b,L_c\), not the U-ports, belong to the upper
transversal.  The B-position owner equations are

\[
\begin{aligned}
 \operatorname{owner}(g(U_{ab}))&=H-e+b,\\
 \operatorname{owner}(g(U_{bc}))&=H-e+c,\\
 \operatorname{owner}(g(U_{ca}))&=H-e+a.
\end{aligned}                                                       \tag{6.3}
\]

The corrected note uses (6.3).

The unit-expanded hypertree execution lemma is valid.  For atom \(t\), put
\(\rho(t)=|E_t|-1\), choose a tree \(R_t\) on its support components, and
require \(\bigcup_tR_t\) to be one tree.  If two support components of a
pending atom had already been connected by earlier atoms, the prior unit
path together with the path in \(R_t\) would create a cycle.  Thus all its
support blocks are distinct; component faithfulness merges them, and
induction works in every order.

The exact topology characterization also passes.  The incidence graph is a
forest exactly when

\[
 \sum_{t:E_t\subseteq A}\rho(t)\le |A|-1
 \qquad(\varnothing\ne A\subseteq V),                \tag{6.4}
\]

and it is a spanning tree after adding
\(\bigcup_tE_t=V\) and \(\sum_t\rho(t)=|V|-1\).  Rooting this incidence
tree gives the one-anchor atom order, and replacing every atom star by any
tree on its component support gives the unit-expanded tree.  The converse
follows from connectedness and the same edge count.  The mixed-rank
independence system is not a matroid: the ternary atom \(123\) and the
binary forest \(\{12,23\}\) give the exchange counterexample.

Connected two-section alone is insufficient.  The two hyperedges
\(\{1,2,3\}\) and \(\{1,2,4\}\) span a connected two-section but are both
needed and create a four-cycle in the incidence graph.  Equivalently their
rank-two unit expansions have four edges on four vertices and cannot form a
tree.  This proves only a scoped obstruction to the unit-expanded hypertree
architecture; a more general
ordered hypergraph braid may escape it.

The raw canonical project-\(m=5\) theorem proves that the distinction is
real.  All 648 legal minimal two-ECO Hamilton sequences have disjoint ports
and forced faces, yet all 324 endpoints realize only 81 of 84 turn colours
on each shore and miss the same complementary period-three triples.  Hence
there is no upper transversal before owner alignment is even defined.  This
closes only the unrepaired canonical minimal-ECO route.

The repaired project-\(m=5\) theorem supplies the exact positive base which
the raw theorem leaves open.  After the synchronized three-\(C_{10}\)
repair, the pre-glue factor has components \(120,132\), one transported
leaf-forest decoration, and a unique perfect matching.  All five
fixed-rotation ECO atoms survive and Hamiltonize; precisely
\[
 110100,\quad110010,\quad101100,\quad101010
\]
are all-six marked and pointwise owner-aligned.  Direct replay proves that
each preserves the accepting decoration.  Thus repair-first ECO integration
is unconditional at the first raw-obstructed dimension, while its uniform
all-\(m\) export remains open.

The compiled repaired atom \(g1=Z(101100)\) also has one explicit private
rank-one route \(s_{g1}\to z_{g1}\).  This authenticates the finite
singleton local-channel row, but supplies neither a recursive router export
nor a rank-two ECO channel.

A three-component ECO atom can lower the component count by two.  It must
export two independently routable unit channels, or an exact grouped
two-source state; one path has rank one and is insufficient.  With
\(\rho(t)\) mutually private channels per atom, deleting \(Y\) kills at
most \(|Y|\) edges of the unit tree and proves router resilience.

For an all-or-none atom, the correct alternative is the weighted identity
\[
 c(B_Y)-1=\sum_{t\ {\rm dead\ under}\ Y}\rho(t).
\]
Pairwise-disjoint private banks with
\[
 t\text{ dead under }Y\Longrightarrow |Y\cap B_t|\ge\rho(t)
\]
therefore suffice.  One private path cannot pay for a rank-two atom.

The common collision path forest gives an exact reduction only for the
literal **port sets**: orient each shared old edge to one incident atom and
each atom loses at most two old edges.  To infer global channel
disjointness, this ownership must additionally extend to pairwise-disjoint
reserved router vertex sets \(B_t\), with every local channel contained in
its \(B_t\).  The collision theorem does not supply those network banks.
Indeed, disjoint residual port sets may still route through one public
bottleneck.  Under an authenticated bank export, the remaining local row is
the ownership-resolved integral max-flow test of value \(\rho(t)\), or the
grouped atomic kill-cost test above.

## 6A. State-expanded path-backbone audit

Theorem 6.9 is a valid strengthening of the bare path-tiling TU theorem.
Once the repaired component tree is explicitly a path, a binary or ternary
atom covers one or two consecutive path edges.  Decorating such a tile by
its exact incoming and outgoing occurrence state simply replaces the path
node \(i\) by state nodes \((i,\sigma)\).  Feasible executions are therefore
directed source--accepting paths in an acyclic multigraph, and the
corresponding unit-flow polytope remains integral.

The proof does not infer composability from matching states alone.  Its
transition hypothesis explicitly carries every cross-cut owner, occurrence,
router, residence, shadow, socket/voltage and compiler obligation, while
completed unit and atomic banks are mutually private.  With this hypothesis,
equality of successive states is an exact induction interface.  Mixed router
semantics are valid: replace unit-separable atoms by their unit trees and
retain atomic atoms as incidence nodes; deletion damage is
\[
 \#\{\text{killed unit channels}\}
   +\sum_{t\ {\rm dead\ atomic}}\rho(t)\le |Y|.
\]
The recurrence
\[
 R_j=\{\tau:\exists\,i\in\{j-2,j-1\},\ \sigma\in R_i,\
                (i,\sigma)\to(j,\tau)\}
\]
is necessary and sufficient, with out-of-range indices omitted.  Pascal
concatenation needs one bridge arc between a reachable left exit and a
co-reachable right entrance; parent-totality is stronger than necessary.
Its width is
\(\max\{w_{\rm left},w_{\rm bridge},w_{\rm right}\}\), and equals the child
maximum only when the bridge fits that width.

The path face is only sufficient.  The \(K_{1,3}\) three-tile matrix has
determinant two and unique fractional cover \(1/2,1/2,1/2\), so a branching
backbone may fail integrally in that exact three-ternary-tile catalogue even
after every unary filter.  Additional tiles or another backbone can escape
it.  Also, the ECO collision path is on atom labels and cannot be identified
with the component path.  Thus the exact missing all-\(m\) statement is a
literal repair-exported component path plus its accepted state transducer,
not bare coherent abundance.

## 6B. Decoration-after-Hamiltonization separation

The central quantifier simplification is exact.  A pairwise-disjoint strict
ECO incidence hypertree Hamiltonizes without a decoration: disjoint toggles
commute, strict phase makes each a component merge, and
\(\sum_t(|S_t|-1)=|\mathcal V|-1\) leaves one cycle.  Owner alignment and
router resilience are unnecessary for that explicit Stage-A family.

For any Stage-A Hamilton cycle \(C_0\) and any terminal Hamilton cycle
\(C_*\), the red/blue symmetric difference has equal red and blue degree at
every vertex and therefore decomposes into alternating circuits.  Successive
toggles remain degree-two factors and reach \(C_*\); intermediate factors
need not be Hamiltonian.  Hence the weakest exact missing central theorem in
the middle-levels-resolvable route is:

\[
 \operatorname{DHC}(m):\quad
 \text{some ML}(2m-1)\text{ Hamilton cycle has a joint alternating SDR}
 \text{ on the binary-trace forest face}.             \tag{6.5}
\]

The trace qualification is necessary: a Catalan decoration on the unique
one-cycle face gives a degree-two lift with one cycle, not a Catalan linear
forest.  This is an equivalence only in the middle-levels-resolvable class;
it does not assert that every abstract CLMT has a Hamilton support.

The repaired \({\rm ML}(7)\) theorem calibrates the distinction.  Separate
turn surjectivity does not imply joint gap Hall, while one incidence-hex
toggle repairs the counterexample.  The exact census
\(31/16/10/6\), based at the repaired positive cycle, counts alternating
hexes, Hamilton outputs, decorable outputs, and Hamilton toggles whose
source/output pair admits at least one common decoration.  The six
multiplicities are \(576,576,144,144,432,540\); all are forest-trace
decorations and every pair has a leaf-peelable common witness.  The
two-multiset plus boundary-mark transparent criterion is required only when
a fixed decoration is preserved through the toggle; it is not a condition
on an arbitrary terminal circuit repair.

Project \(m=5\) supplies the exact two-stage positive base.  The disjoint
standard fixed-rotation atoms \(101010,101100\) turn the canonical
component profile \(36+72+144\) into one 252-cycle.  The three disjoint
\(C_{10}\) circuits then produce the authenticated decorated Hamilton cycle.
Their supports are disjoint from the two ECO atoms, so the finite operations
also commute.  No all-\(m\) commutation or RSB preservation follows.

The repair-first private collar remains a stronger preservation
architecture: it carries one decoration and can carry downstream state
prefixwise.  The post-glue theorem removes that baggage for central
existence but pushes all decoration work into DHC(\(m\)).  Arbitrary circuit
decomposition does not preserve residence, deep shadows, voltage, sockets,
or the compiler.
