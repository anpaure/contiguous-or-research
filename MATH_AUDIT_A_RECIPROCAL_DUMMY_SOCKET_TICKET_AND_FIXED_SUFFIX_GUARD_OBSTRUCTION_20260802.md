# Reciprocal dummy-socket tickets and the fixed-suffix guard obstruction

**Date:** 2026-08-02  
**Lane:** A, bottom-token common base and private socket export  
**Status:** proof audit and exact contraction criterion.  This note proves a
completed-slot actuator and a fixed-suffix impossibility theorem.  It does
not construct a literal moving-suffix collar, a rank-1748 ticket bank, a
common-state cycle cover, residence, upper shadows, topology, a source, a
compiler, or a word.

## 0. Result

There are two different answers, depending on which resource ledger is
meant by “preserve the guards/caps.”

* On the completed-chain ledger, a dummy-bearing Boolean `C4` is the
  support-minimal short-basis exchange.  Two modes of a completed reciprocal
  collar contract to one `U_(1,2)` socket ticket.  Their internal
  predecessor/successor identities and flags need not agree; their exterior
  attachment signature and every globally named resource row must agree.
* On a fixed coefficient-one suffix table, if the protected ledger includes
  the **typed active-interval** head or upper occurrence, no short-basis
  exchange exists at any support size.  For each slot `v`,

  \[
                  H(M_v)+s_v=1,
                  \qquad U(U_v)+s_v=1.               \tag{0.1}
  \]

  This is a free coordinatewise obstruction.  Dummy-free `C6/C8/C10`
  companions cannot cancel it.

Thus the B5 atlas supplies a completed-ledger partition ticket but not the
full `M_sock` ticket demanded by a typed guard/cap interface.  The smallest
formal escape transports the entire short/long suffix packet, rather than
moving only its bottom/dummy status.

## 1. The reciprocal completed-collar criterion

Let `e_0,e_1` be two candidate short slots.  A **reciprocal collar** is a
pair of literal modes `mu_0,mu_1` with the following data.

1. Mode `mu_i` has unary exported short signature `{e_i}` after all
   ticket-internal short statuses have been contracted.
2. Each mode contains a replayed common-state socket record, including its
   short address, predecessor and successor roles and flags, owner/root,
   history, cap, reset and guard incidences.
3. The two modes have the same exterior attachment signature.  Equivalently,
   any mode-dependent boundary port is included inside the ticket rather
   than left for the ambient host to choose.
4. Every globally prescribed additive resource has the same incidence
   vector in `mu_0` and `mu_1`.  A resource may differ only when the whole
   differing footprint is declared ticket-private and is not a global
   capacity/coverage row.

For a ticket `t`, put

\[
                     A_t=\{e_0,e_1\}.                \tag{1.1}
\]

Call a bank of such collars **reciprocally private** when every injective
ticket--slot assignment composes literally with no omitted shared row.  A
strong convenient sufficient condition is disjointness of the unions of all
possible mode footprints.  If two menus overlap, merely forbidding
simultaneous choice of the same exported slot is not enough: one mode may
still consume another menu slot internally.

### Theorem 1.1 (reciprocal contraction)

A reciprocally private collar contracts to one transversal ticket with menu
`A_t`.  A complete-footprint-disjoint bank of binary collars presents the
direct sum of `U_(1,2)` blocks.  More generally, an overlapping menu bank
presents the corresponding transversal matroid only when literal
composition has been checked for every injective mode selection; overlap of
menu names alone does not prove this.

The predecessor/successor identities, internal flags and internal source
cells of `mu_0` and `mu_1` need not be equal.  Equality is required only for
the exterior signature and globally prescribed rows.  Distinct ticket
names without the union-footprint privacy condition are insufficient.

#### Proof

Exactly one mode of each ticket is selected.  Conditions 2--4 make either
choice a completed literal object with the same ambient interface.  The
reciprocal-privacy hypothesis says that modes assigned to distinct slots
compose, so the only remaining interaction between selected ticket
identities is injective use of their exported ground slots.  This is
precisely a bipartite matching of tickets into slots, the defining
representation of a transversal matroid.  For complete-footprint-disjoint
two-element menus it is the direct sum of `U_(1,2)`.
Internal data from the two modes are alternatives and are never
simultaneously selected, so they need not agree.  Union-footprint privacy is
what prevents one mode of another ticket from consuming them.  \(\square\)

This theorem gives a sufficient physical subface.  To call it the complete
socket face requires the reverse statement that every allowed realization
decomposes into the declared collar modes.

## 2. The support-minimal completed-ledger actuator

Fix a real bottom `B` and distinct coordinates `x,y,z` outside `B`.  Let

\[
\begin{array}{c|cc}
       &M&U\\ \hline
 e_0&B+x&B+x+z\\
 e_1&B+y&B+y+z.
\end{array}                                             \tag{2.1}
\]

For one dummy `u`, compare

\[
             P_0=\{u e_0,B e_1\},\qquad
             P_1=\{B e_0,u e_1\}.                    \tag{2.2}
\]

Both modes contain the same named bottom and the same two completed fixed
suffixes.  Their moving co-middle is also identical:

\[
       \chi(B;e_0)=B+(U_{e_0}-M_{e_0})=B+z
                  =\chi(B;e_1).                       \tag{2.3}
\]

Therefore (2.2) is an exact short-basis exchange on the completed-chain plus
co-middle projection.  Conversely, a one-bottom/one-dummy `C4` preserves
that projection exactly when its two `chi` labels agree.  No smaller
matching circuit changes a dummy-matched slot.

If independently replayed endpoint collars for the two modes satisfy
Theorem 1.1, (2.2) becomes a literal `U_(1,2)` ticket.  Equation (2.3) alone
does not supply those collars.

## 3. Why the full fixed-suffix signature is impossible

Suppose each slot `v` has a distinct fixed head `M_v` and upper `U_v`.
Let `s_v` be its short indicator.  A typed active real interval exists at
`v` exactly in long mode, so

\[
       H(M_v)=1-s_v,\qquad U(U_v)=1-s_v.             \tag{3.1}
\]

### Theorem 3.1 (fixed-suffix short--guard obstruction)

Any fixed-suffix replacement preserving the complete typed active-head
palette or the complete typed active-upper palette fixes the short vector
pointwise.  This remains true after adjoining arbitrary overlapping
dummy-free `C6`, `C8` and `C10` circuits.

#### Proof

Subtract (3.1) between the two modes.  Typed-head equality gives
`Delta s_v=0` for every slot; typed-upper equality gives the same conclusion.
A dummy-free circuit has zero short boundary, so adding any number of them
does not change the coordinatewise equations.  \(\square\)

For the `C4` (2.2), with mode 1 minus mode 0,

\[
       \Delta H=[M_{e_0}]-[M_{e_1}],\qquad
       \Delta U=[U_{e_0}]-[U_{e_1}].                 \tag{3.2}
\]

These are the exact uncompensated guard rows.  They are nonzero when the
suffixes are coefficient-one.  The obstruction is free, not parity or
torsion, and is stronger than a B5 aperture/core-flux count.

There is no contradiction with completed-target preservation: a short row
still contains its fixed suffix `M_v<U_v`.  Equation (3.1) concerns the
typed occurrence of `M_v,U_v` as the head/upper of an active real interval.
One must state which ledger the downstream compiler uses.

## 4. Exact B5 and common-state consequences

Replacing one bottom of a hub cycle by a dummy gives completed-ledger
menus of sizes three (`C6`) and four (`C8`): the missing bottom's `chi`
label is the same in every mode.  These menus fit in B5 and yield
partition-matroid blocks only after complete-footprint privacy is proved.
They do not evade Theorem 3.1; their omitted typed head/upper changes with
the short slot.

Nor does completed-ledger equality imply an adjacent literal socket.  Let

\[
 A=(B\subset B+x\subset B+x+z),\qquad
 S=(B+y\subset B+y+z).                               \tag{4.1}
\]

There is no canonical source transition `A -> S`.

* In the short `12` state, the two shared source cells must union to
  `B+y`, but both lie in the preceding upper `B+x+z`, which omits `y`.
* In the short `23` state, the first shared cell must contain `z`; as the
  middle cell of the preceding long window, its upper bound is `B` or
  `B+x` in all four long flags.

Reflection excludes `S -> A`.  Hence two reciprocal candidates cannot be
adjacent in a fixed linear collar.  If both must have internal predecessor
and successor roles, at least five chain roles are necessary:

\[
                         g_0,e_0,g_1,e_1,g_2.          \tag{4.2}
\]

This is a lower bound, not a B5 existence theorem.

## 5. First admissible full-signature escape

Theorem 3.1 rules out every larger circuit which leaves the suffix packet at
each site fixed.  The support-minimal formal escape is the transposition of
one complete short packet `A` and one complete long packet `L`:

\[
\begin{array}{c|cc}
      &p&q\\ \hline
 \text{mode 0}&A&L\\
 \text{mode 1}&L&A.
\end{array}                                             \tag{5.1}
\]

The packet includes suffix, owner/root attachment, typed head/upper,
state/history, cap and guard labels.  Its labelled multiset is identical in
the two modes and the physical short site changes.  Literal legality at the
two new sites, identical exterior ports and the five-role condition (4.2)
remain to be proved.

This escape is necessarily outside the fixed cotransversal face
`M_short=(M/F)^*`: that matroid was defined after each suffix packet was
frozen.  A positive realization therefore belongs to a joint chain/slot
master.  No fixed-suffix B5 aperture or dummy-free companion can replace
this moving-packet step.
