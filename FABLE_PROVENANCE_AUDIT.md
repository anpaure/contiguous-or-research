## 5. Independent check of Lemma G

Write selected middle witnesses as

\[
 I_i=[i+\alpha_i,i+\beta_i],\qquad
 0\le\alpha_1\le\cdots\le\alpha_M\le D,
 \quad \alpha_i\le\beta_i.
\]

Positions before the first witness contribute `alpha_1`.  A hole between
`I_i` and `I_(i+1)` has size

\[
 \max(0,\alpha_{i+1}-\beta_i)
 \le \alpha_{i+1}-\alpha_i.
\]

The tail contributes `D-beta_M`.  Hence the number of positions in no
selected witness is at most

\[
 \alpha_1+\sum_{i<M}(\alpha_{i+1}-\alpha_i)+D-\beta_M
 =D+\alpha_M-\beta_M\le D.
\]

If position `p` lies in selected witnesses indexed by a family `J`, then
every array entry at `p` is coordinatewise below every selected target
`T_i`, `i in J`, because the OR/join of `I_i` is exactly `T_i`.  Thus

\[
                         A_p\le\bigwedge_{i\in J}T_i.
\]

Both parts of Lemma G are therefore correct.  They are modest but potentially
useful localization facts missing from the handoff.

## 6. Independent audit of Theorem O

Fable's Section 10.3 extension is correct, but its consequences were
overstated.

Fix a constant `0<c<2`.  A dangerous plateau has
`lambda_j>ca`, so edge disjointness gives

\[
 m\le{M_a-1\over ca}=O_c(a),\qquad
 \sum_j\delta_j=O(a^2),\qquad
 \delta_j=\lambda_j-ca\ge0.
\]

The dangerous-free-window lemma, first-dangerous assignment, and
localization

\[
 n_j\le\min(L-\lambda_j,\lambda_{j-1}+1+g_{j-1})
\]

never use `c>1`.  After the standard first/boundary terms are absorbed,

\[
 \sum_j\delta_jn_j
 \le\sum_j\delta_j\lambda_{j-1}
   +\sum_{j\ge2}\delta_j\min(g_{j-1},L-\lambda_j)
   +O_c(a^2).
\]

The original proof used a secant inequality on `[ca,2a]`.  It is unnecessary:
for every `j`, `lambda_(j-1)<=2a`, hence

\[
 \sum_j\delta_j\lambda_{j-1}
 \le2a\sum_j\delta_j.
\]

This proves, for every **fixed** `0<c<2`,

\[
 Q\le caM_a+2a\sum_j\delta_j
   +\sum_{j\ge2}\delta_j\min(g_{j-1},L-\lambda_j)
   +O_c(a^2).
\]

The span/congestion and seam estimates are unchanged.  Combining this with
the run-spectrum lower bound in a hypothetical `D=o(a^2)` word gives the mass
law

\[
                  2e(c)+H(c)\ge4-3c-o(1)
\]

for each fixed `0<c<4/3`.

Important scope: this is a fixed-`c` theorem.  Statements at `c->0` mean
first taking `a->infinity` for fixed `c`, then taking `c->0`; they do not
supply an error term uniform for an arbitrary `c(a)->0`.

## 7. The decisive Section 10.4 error

### 7.1 Supply is not plateau count

Let

\[
 \mu_a={1\over a}\sum_j\delta_{x_j},\qquad x_j={\lambda_j\over a}.
\]

Then the normalized plateau-count tail is

\[
 {F(c)\over a}=\mu_a((c,2]),
\]

but the normalized interval supply is

\[
 {\operatorname{supply}(ca)\over a^2}
 =\int_{(c,2]}\left(4-x+{2\over a}\right)d\mu_a(x).
\]

Fable Section 10.4 sets `s(c)=mu([c,2])` and then imposes the supply-law
lower bound on `s(c)`.  This drops the factor `4-x` and is false.

The valid pointwise supply law is

\[
 {\operatorname{supply}(ca)\over a^2}
 \ge {4-3c\over2-c}-o(1).
\]

Since one plateau contributes at most `(4-c)a+O(1)` service starts, the
derived **count** law is only

\[
 {F(c)\over a}
 \ge {4-3c\over(2-c)(4-c)}-o(1).
\]

In particular, as `c->0+` it forces at least `(1/2-o(1))a` plateaux, not
`(2-o(1))a`.  Therefore the count, mean-length, and purported uniqueness
arguments in the last half of Section 10.4 do not follow.

### 7.2 Explicit nonsaturated scalar profile

Take

\[
                         \mu=2\delta_{4/3},\qquad H(c)=0.
\]

Then

\[
 \sigma=\int x\,d\mu={8\over3}<3,
 \qquad
 e(c)=2(4/3-c)quad(0<c<4/3).
\]

Consequently

\[
 2e(c)=4(4/3-c)\ge3(4/3-c)=4-3c.
\]

Its actual normalized supply is

\[
 \int(4-x)d\mu=2(4-4/3)={16\over3},
\]

which comfortably exceeds `(4-3c)/(2-c)`.  The count tail is two, and even
Fable's erroneously stronger count version happens to be satisfied.  The line
capacity also holds: at `x=4/3`,

\[
                         2\le6(2-4/3)=4.
\]

Thus every correctly stated scalar condition is satisfied with `sigma=8/3`.
At the abstract interval level, `2a` plateaux of cost `(4/3)a`, placed
adjacently on distinct eligible lines, use only `(8/3)a^2+O(a)` positions;
the unused `Theta(a^2)` positions may remain in a boundary reservoir invisible
to `H`.  This is a scalar/line-capacity countermodel, not a claimed complete
ordering of `H_a`, exactly the scope needed to refute the alleged scalar
implication.

Therefore Section 10.4 proves neither `sigma->3`, nor `H->0` for all
survivors, nor dense adjacency, nor uniqueness of the `2delta_(3/2)` atom.

## 8. Other consistency corrections

### 8.1 Theorem L is not wholly superseded

The coarse Theorem L gives

\[
 Q\le[3c+(4-c)e_c]a^3+O(a^2),
\]

while first-dangerous service (now extended by Theorem O) gives

\[
 Q\le[3c+2e_c+H_c]a^3+O_c(a^2).
\]

The first-dangerous term is better exactly when

\[
                         H_c<(2-c)e_c.
\]

The correct combined bound is

\[
 Q\le\left[3c+min\{(4-c)e_c,2e_c+H_c\}\right]a^3+O_c(a^2).
\]

Thus Fable's repeated "SUPERSEDED" label contradicts
`FABLE_INTERVAL_SUPPLY_AUDIT.md`.

### 8.2 Theorem N does not imply the excess-mass law by itself

A large service sum can be supplied by many plateaux barely above the
threshold, with arbitrarily small excess mass.  The excess lower bound
`e_c>=(4-3c)/(4-c)-o(1)` uses the multiplicity/cost ledger of Theorem L (or
the first-dangerous mass theorem), not the pointwise supply lower bound alone.

### 8.3 Area is not clustering

Theorem M bounds the measure of a union of clipped service intervals over a
position-threshold rectangle.  A small union area can be spatially dispersed.
No component-count or clustering conclusion follows without an additional
interval geometry argument.

### 8.4 Handoff range/provenance ambiguity

Handoff item 530 says the first-dangerous endpoint conclusion holds "for any
fixed `c<4/3`" while citing a source/audit stated only for `1<c<2`.  Theorem O
now supplies a valid proof for fixed `0<c<2`, so the mathematics can be made
correct, but the current citation does not establish the broader wording.
Until Theorem O is added with its fixed-`c` proof, item 530 should either say
`1<c<4/3` or cite the extension explicitly.

### 8.5 Audit of the late Section 11

**Proposition Q is correct.**  If an `x=t` peak plateau is immediately
followed by an `x=t'` peak plateau, peakness of the first requires `t'<t`,
while peakness of the second requires `t<t'`.  Thus literally adjacent
same-letter peak plateaux are impossible.

**Lemma R is wrong as written at an equality seam.**  Suppose `z` rises into
a seam, falls out of it, and the two seam values satisfy `z_-=z_+=v`.  The
local word is

\[
                         \cdots,u,v,v,w,\cdots,
 \qquad u<v>w.
\]

Then the two seam vertices form an internal maximal component of
`{z>=v}` of two vertices, hence edge cost one.  Fable says equality creates
no new run.  That assertion is false.  The intended argument may be repairable:
a rise-to-fall seam always creates a run of cost at most one (cost zero for a
strict jump maximum, cost one at equality).  Between such reversals, `z` has
at most one fall-to-rise valley, so strict `z`-changing plateau edges total at
most `4a+O(1)`.  But this repaired formulation and its endpoint bookkeeping
were not independently established in Fable's note.

**Theorem P has a hypothesis gap.**  Its statement covers an arbitrary chain
of adjacent directed peak plateaux alternating between two letters.  Its proof
uses

\[
 m_k\le{4a+2\over ca+1}+1,
\]

which assumes every plateau in a block has at least `ca+1` edges/vertices in
the relevant convention.  That is a dangerous/minimum-length hypothesis not
present in the theorem statement.  Short directed plateaux may themselves
provide cheap runs, so a corrected dichotomy is plausible, but it must be
written and its service-interval union proved.  The claim that all but
`o(a^2)` window starts see a cheap boundary also needs the explicit estimate

\[
 \sum_k (|B_k|-L)_+
   \le O(\#\text{blocks})+\text{exceptional gap mass}=o(a^2),
\]

not the per-block phrase "`o`-share," since exceptional positions may be
unevenly concentrated.

Finally, even a repaired pure-two-cycle theorem would eliminate only one
special dense-chain architecture.  Section 7.2 already shows that the scalar
laws do not force density or saturation.  Therefore Section 11.4's new claim
that the sole remaining case is an aperiodic dense pattern plus gaps is still
not an exhaustive reduction.
