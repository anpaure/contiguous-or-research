# IRRC16 history-trade lattice and the exact FRR embedding boundary

Date: 2026-07-29

Status: theorem-level structural advance.  This note does **not** prove
IRRC16.  It proves an exact description of its integral trade lattice,
constructs a minimum-projected-support palette-changing trade, gives a
checkable absorber/Hall criterion, and shows precisely why the present
source-relative `FRR(7,4)` rethread is not yet a sparse correction inside
the legal IRRC16 history graph.

No search or computation is used.

## 1. The constrained circulation lattice

Use the legal memory-three history graph \(\mathcal H_3\) and the notation
of
`THREAD_A_K16_UNRESTRICTED_RAINBOW_BIRESIDENT_FACTOR_NORMAL_FORM_20260729.md`.
Let \(A=A(\mathcal H_3)\), let

\[
 B:\mathbb Z^A\longrightarrow\mathbb Z^{V(\mathcal H_3)}
\]

be the directed node--arc incidence map, and let

\[
 (Cz)_X=
 \sum_{a:\,\operatorname{terminal}(\operatorname{tail}a)=X}z_a
 \qquad\left(X\in\binom{[16]}8\right).
 \tag{1.1}
\]

The integral trade lattice is

\[
 \mathcal K_{16}=\ker_{\mathbb Z}
 \begin{pmatrix}B\\ C\end{pmatrix}.
 \tag{1.2}
\]

If \(y,y'\) are binary IRRC owner-exact circulations before palette rows
are imposed, then \(z=y'-y\in\mathcal K_{16}\).  Conversely, if
\(z\in\mathcal K_{16}\), \(z^-\le y\), and \(y+z\in\{0,1\}^A\), then
\(y+z\) is another owner-exact legal circulation.  Thus (1.2), together
with the box inequalities relative to a base point, is the exact trade
space; there is no extra chronology condition hidden outside \(Bz=0\).

### Proposition 1.1 (signed-history circuit description)

For \(z\in\mathbb Z^A\), form a directed signed multigraph on
\(V(\mathcal H_3)\) by taking

* \(z_a\) forward copies of \(a\), marked `+`, when \(z_a>0\); and
* \(-z_a\) reversed copies of \(a\), marked `-`, when \(z_a<0\).

Then \(z\in\mathcal K_{16}\) if and only if

1. this signed multigraph is Eulerian at every history state; and
2. for every physical owner \(X\), the number of `+` occurrences whose
   original arc tail ends at \(X\) equals the corresponding number of
   `-` occurrences.

The conformally indecomposable elements of \(\mathcal K_{16}\) are exactly
the nonempty signed Eulerian families satisfying item 2 which have no
proper nonempty signed Eulerian subfamily satisfying item 2.

#### Proof

After reversal, Euler balance is precisely \(Bz=0\).  Item 2 is precisely
\(Cz=0\).  A conformal decomposition of \(z\) is the same thing as a
partition, with multiplicity, into proper signed subfamilies satisfying
both balances.  This proves the last assertion as well.  \(\square\)

This is the exact Graver-circuit description.  Nonnegative integral
circulations in \(\ker B\) decompose into directed state cycles, while the
full signed lattice \(\ker_{\mathbb Z}B\) is generated by signed incidence
circuits.  Neither kind of generator generally lies in \(\ker C\): its
physical-owner incidence need not vanish.  The owner rows are therefore a
genuine extra coupling, not an innocuous side quota.

### Proposition 1.2 (the symmetric point has no sparse additive rounding)

Let

\[
 y_a^*=\frac1{25(8)_3^2}\qquad(a\in A)
 \tag{1.3}
\]

be the symmetric IRRC16 fractional circulation.  For every binary vector
\(y\in\{0,1\}^A\), and hence for every binary owner-exact circulation,

\[
 \operatorname{supp}(y-y^*)=A.                         \tag{1.4}
\]

Consequently no sparse real correction in
\(\ker_{\mathbb R}(B;C)\) can be added to \(y^*\) to produce an integral
point.  Any absorber proof must first obtain
a global binary owner-exact chronology (or an integral convex
decomposition argument) and only then use sparse trades to repair palette
deficits.

#### Proof

Every coordinate of \(y^*\) lies strictly between zero and one.  Thus both
\(0-y_a^*\) and \(1-y_a^*\) are nonzero.  This proves (1.4).  \(\square\)

The palette slack also has no unused aggregate reserve.  There are 11,440
colours on either shore, so the total fractional excess of (1.3) is

\[
 11440\left(\frac98-1\right)=1430,                     \tag{1.5}
\]

exactly the total excess \(12870-11440\) of every integral palette-complete
factor.  The \(1/8\) margin must therefore be localized into integral
duplicate witnesses; it cannot pay an additional global loss.

## 2. Exact port-rethread form of binary trades

Let \(F\) be an oriented legal cycle cover on a set of physical owners.
Cut \(r\) directed transitions.  What remains is a collection of directed
paths.  A rethread pairs every terminal port to an initial port by a new
Johnson transition.

For a path \(P\), retain its ordered last three swap labels at the terminal
port and its ordered first three swap labels at the initial port, using all
available labels when the path is shorter.  A proposed seam is called
**history-compatible** when each four-transition window crossing that seam
has pairwise disjoint swap labels.  Explicitly, if the labels are

\[
 \alpha_3,\alpha_2,\alpha_1,c,\beta_1,\beta_2,\beta_3,
\]

this consists of the nine tests

\[
 c\cap\alpha_i=c\cap\beta_i=\varnothing\quad(1\le i\le3),
 \tag{2.0a}
\]

\[
 \alpha_1\cap\beta_1=\alpha_1\cap\beta_2
 =\alpha_2\cap\beta_1=\varnothing.                     \tag{2.0b}
\]

If all retained paths have at least three transitions, this is a condition
only on the displayed two three-label ports and the seam label.

### Theorem 2.1 (binary port-rethread theorem)

Suppose the retained paths have at least three transitions.  A permutation
of their terminal and initial ports produces another binary owner-exact
legal-history circulation on the same owner set (and an IRRC circulation
when that set is all of \(\mathcal V\)) if and only if

1. every proposed seam is a Johnson transition between its two endpoints;
2. every proposed seam is history-compatible; and
3. every resulting projected component has at least eight transitions.

For the signed difference \(z=y'-y\),

\[
 z\in\mathcal K_{16},                                      \tag{2.1}
\]

and its two palette projections are exactly

\[
 \Delta_\lambda(z)=
   \sum_{e\in E_{\rm new}}{\bf1}_{\lambda(e)}
  -\sum_{e\in E_{\rm old}}{\bf1}_{\lambda(e)},
 \qquad
 \Delta_\upsilon(z)=
   \sum_{e\in E_{\rm new}}{\bf1}_{\upsilon(e)}
  -\sum_{e\in E_{\rm old}}{\bf1}_{\upsilon(e)}.          \tag{2.2}
\]

Moreover, if \(|E_{\rm old}|=|E_{\rm new}|=r\), then

\[
 |\operatorname{supp}z|\le 8r.                            \tag{2.3}
\]

Conversely, the difference of any two binary owner-exact legal
circulations is obtained by this construction after their common directed
transitions are split into maximal common paths.  Wholly common directed
cycles are discarded, and every owner incident with no common transition is
retained as a zero-edge singleton piece.  If a resulting piece has fewer
than three transitions, condition 2 is replaced by the literal check of
every four-transition window through the resulting compound seam.

#### Proof

Degree one in and out is restored at every physical owner by a permutation
of the ports.  Internal path windows remain unchanged.  Under the length
hypothesis, every changed four-window crosses a unique seam, so condition 2
is necessary and sufficient for legality there.  Condition 3 deals with
the strict constant-trace convention.  Sliding the memory-three window
around the new projected cycles now gives a binary history circulation;
the owner set is unchanged, proving (2.1).

Only physical seam edges change in the palette ledger.  Retained physical
edges whose history states change occur once with each sign and cancel
after projection, proving (2.2).

A selected state arc projecting transition \(e_i\) remembers precisely
\(e_{i-3},e_{i-2},e_{i-1},e_i\).  On either chronology, only the new or old
seam and the next three transitions can therefore change their history
arcs.  This is at most \(4r\) arcs on each side, proving (2.3).

For the converse, remove the directed transitions common to the two
projected successor maps.  Maximal common directed paths remain, and the
two successor maps give two permutations of their ports.  Since both
history lifts are legal, the stated literal window conditions hold.
\(\square\)

The theorem is an exact way to use \(\mathcal H_3\): a trade is not merely
an alternating physical circuit.  It is an alternating circuit equipped
with its three-transition entrance and exit histories.

## 3. The minimum projected palette-changing trade

The history lattice has genuinely palette-moving elements.  In
particular, there is no local invariant forcing both q1 projections to be
fixed.

Partition \([16]\) into eight ordered coordinate pairs

\[
 P,R,A,B,C,D,E,F.
 \tag{3.1}
\]

Let \(X\) contain the `0` endpoint of every pair.  For a family \(S\) of
pairs, write \(X^S\) for the owner obtained by toggling precisely the pairs
in \(S\).

On the following two disjoint eight-vertex sets take the cycles with the
displayed cyclic swap words:

\[
 \Gamma_1:\quad P,A,B,C,P,A,B,C,                         \tag{3.2}
\]

starting at \(X\), and

\[
 \Gamma_2:\quad P,D,E,F,P,D,E,F,                         \tag{3.3}
\]

starting at \(X^R\).  They are disjoint because \(R\) is constantly zero
on \(\Gamma_1\) and constantly one on \(\Gamma_2\).  Every repeated swap
label in (3.2)--(3.3) is at cyclic distance four, so both cycles are legal.

Delete

\[
 e_1=XX^P,qquad e_2=X^R X^{R,P},                        \tag{3.4}
\]

and add the two \(R\)-rungs

\[
 f_1=XX^R,qquad f_2=X^P X^{R,P}.                        \tag{3.5}
\]

### Theorem 3.1 (minimum-support Q8 rung trade)

The replacement (3.4)--(3.5) turns \(\Gamma_1\cup\Gamma_2\) into one
legal sixteen-cycle on exactly the same owners.  Hence the difference of
the two history lifts is an element \(z_{Q8}\in\mathcal K_{16}\).  Its
projected physical symmetric difference consists of exactly four edges,
and both q1 palette projections are nonzero.  Four is the minimum possible
projected support of a nontrivial trade between simple two-factors.

With compatible orientations, the history support has exactly eight
positive and eight negative state arcs.

#### Proof

After the switch, a cyclic swap word is

\[
 R,F,E,D,P,F,E,D,R,A,B,C,P,A,B,C.                       \tag{3.6}
\]

Every repeated label in (3.6) has cyclic separation at least four.  The
cycle is therefore legal by the cooldown theorem.  Both old and new edge
sets have degree two on the same sixteen owners, so their history
difference lies in \(\mathcal K_{16}\).

Write \(p_0,r_0\in X\) for the selected endpoints of \(P,R\), and
\(p_1,r_1\notin X\) for their mates.  The deleted lower colours are

\[
 X-\{p_0\},\qquad X^R-\{p_0\},                           \tag{3.7}
\]

whereas the added lower colours are

\[
 X-\{r_0\},\qquad X^P-\{r_0\}.                          \tag{3.8}
\]

The four sets in (3.7)--(3.8) are distinct.  Likewise the deleted upper
colours

\[
 X+\{p_1\},\qquad X^R+\{p_1\}                           \tag{3.9}
\]

and added upper colours

\[
 X+\{r_1\},\qquad X^P+\{r_1\}                          \tag{3.10}
\]

are distinct.  Thus both palette projections change.

The symmetric difference of two distinct projected simple two-factors is an even
alternating graph.  Its least possible nonempty component has two old and
two new edges, proving the lower bound four.  The construction attains it.

Orient \(\Gamma_1\) as displayed in (3.2) and \(\Gamma_2\) in the reverse
direction.  These are precisely the orientations inherited by the retained
paths in the new cycle (3.6).  The two seams in either chronology are eight
transitions apart.  For each
seam exactly that seam and the next three selected state arcs change.
Thus there are eight arcs of each sign.  \(\square\)

This theorem establishes minimum support only after **physical
projection**.  It does not assert that sixteen is the minimum support of a
Graver element of the much larger matrix \((B;C)\); purely historical
circuits could in principle be smaller.  What is proved is the relevant
statement for palette repair: no palette-changing physical trade can use
fewer than four projected edges, and the bound is attained under full
cooldown.

### Proposition 3.2 (smallest upper-neutral static circuit)

Let \(\mathcal I_{8,9}\) be the inclusion graph between rank-eight owners
and rank-nine upper colours.  Replace every factor edge \(XY\), with
\(U=X\cup Y\), by its two flags \(XU,YU\).  If two spanning factors have the
same upper-load vector, then the signed difference of their flag multisets
is balanced at both shores of \(\mathcal I_{8,9}\), and hence decomposes
into alternating simple circuits.

The graph \(\mathcal I_{8,9}\) has no four-cycle.  Consequently every
nonzero upper-load-neutral flag circuit has length at least six, using at
least three deleted and three inserted flags.  Six is attained: for a
rank-seven core \(K\) and distinct \(a,b,c\notin K\),

\[
 K+a\;-\;K+a+b\;-\;K+b\;-\;K+b+c\;-\;K+c
 \;-\;K+a+c\;-\;K+a
 \tag{3.11}
\]

is such a circuit.  The lower-neutral statement is the complementary
rank-\((7,8)\) version.

#### Proof

At an owner, both factors contribute degree two.  At an upper colour \(U\),
the flag degree is twice its physical edge load, which is equal by
hypothesis.  After cancelling common flags, positive and negative degrees
therefore agree at every bipartition vertex.  Alternately following a
positive and a negative flag decomposes the signed graph into alternating
circuits.

Two distinct rank-eight owners have at most one common rank-nine superset,
namely their union when it has rank nine.  Thus a simple four-cycle is
impossible.  The displayed six-cycle proves sharpness.  \(\square\)

This is the exact static primitive behind (4.10): its general solutions are
alternating-circuit packets.  It is not yet a history absorber.  At every
upper vertex the changed flags must still pair to simple physical edges,
and the resulting circuit packet must pass the port conditions of Theorem
2.1.  In particular, a decomposition into six-cycles need not give a legal
sequence of intermediate factors.

## 4. A rigorous absorber/Hall criterion

Let \(y\) be any binary owner-exact legal circulation and let
\(w_c\) be its load at a lower or upper q1 colour \(c\).  Let
\(\mathscr G\) be a family of pairwise owner-disjoint, history-compatible
port trades, each individually applicable to \(y\).  Assume their signed
history supports are pairwise disjoint, so all subfamilies remain binary and
commute.  For \(g\in\mathscr G\), let
\(L(g)\) and \(G(g)\) be its multisets of lost and gained q1 colours on the
union of the two palette shores, and put

\[
 \Delta_g(c)=
 \operatorname{mult}_{G(g)}(c)-\operatorname{mult}_{L(g)}(c),
\quad
 d_g(c)=(-\Delta_g(c))_+,\quad
 q_g(c)=(\Delta_g(c))_+ .
\]

### Theorem 4.1 (commuting-trade capacity criterion)

For \(I\subseteq\mathscr G\), applying exactly the trades in \(I\) retains
both q1 palettes if and only if

\[
 \sum_{g\in I}d_g(c)
 \le w_c-1+\sum_{g\in I}q_g(c)
 \qquad\text{for every colour }c.                       \tag{4.1}
\]

In particular, let \(\mathcal M=\{c:w_c=0\}\).  Suppose:

1. for every colour \(c\), the whole bank obeys the global reserve bound
   \[
   \sum_{g\in\mathscr G_0}d_g(c)\le (w_c-1)_+;
   \]
   equivalently its negative units inject into distinct baseline duplicate
   occurrences while leaving one baseline occurrence untouched; and
2. the bipartite graph joining \(c\in\mathcal M\) to trades
   \(g\in\mathscr G_0\) that gain \(c\), meaning \(q_g(c)>0\), satisfies
   \[
   |N(S)|\ge |S|\qquad(S\subseteq\mathcal M).             \tag{4.2}
   \]

Then there is an integral commuting trade family which covers every
initially missing colour and retains every initially present colour.

#### Proof

Because the owner and history collars are disjoint, the trades commute and
Theorem 2.1 preserves the owner and cooldown rows.  The final load of
colour \(c\) is exactly

\[
 w_c+\sum_{g\in I}\Delta_g(c),
\]

which proves (4.1).  Under item 1, every subfamily respects the loss
capacity.  Hall's theorem applied to (4.2) assigns a distinct trade to each
missing colour.  Apply their union; duplicated gains only help.  \(\square\)

For the Q8 rung trade, each trade has two lost and two gained colours on
each shore.  The theorem therefore gives a literal smallest-absorber route.
It also identifies what the symmetric load \(9/8\) does **not** provide:
fractional surplus does not itself produce an integral bank of commuting
trades with reserve tokens and the Hall property (4.2).

### Theorem 4.2 (exact \(8+1\) rainbow-path rounding)

Suppose a set \(P\) contains exactly one provider of every lower and every
upper q1 colour and partitions the middle owners into 1430 directed paths,
each having eight edges and internally satisfying cooldown four.  Form the
bipartite connector graph \(G_P\) from path terminals to path initials,
retaining every Johnson connector.  Let \(Z\subseteq E(G_P)\) be the
connectors which fail at least one of the nine port inequalities
(2.0a)--(2.0b).

If \(G_P\) has a fractional perfect matching \(w\) with

\[
 w(Z)<1,                                                \tag{4.3}
\]

then it has an integral perfect matching avoiding \(Z\), and adjoining
those 1430 connectors to \(P\) gives an IRRC16 factor with both q1 palettes.

#### Proof

The arithmetic is forced:

\[
 11440=8\cdot1430,\qquad 12870=9\cdot1430.
\]

The bipartite perfect-matching polytope is integral, so decompose \(w\) as
a convex combination of integral perfect matchings \(M_i\).  Then

\[
 \sum_i\theta_i|M_i\cap Z|=w(Z)<1.
\]

Since each cardinality is a nonnegative integer, some \(M_i\) avoids \(Z\).
It completes degree two at every path endpoint.  Every cycle in the result
contains at least one eight-edge path and therefore has length at least
nine.  Internal windows are legal by hypothesis and crossing windows are
legal by the nine port tests.  Finally, every palette witness lies in
\(P\), so connector colours cannot destroy coverage.  \(\square\)

Once \(P\) is fixed, this is a genuine network-flow rounding theorem.
Differences of connector perfect matchings decompose into alternating
circuits of their bipartite graph; a four-cycle is their smallest possible
nonzero trade and is automatically palette-safe because the protected
witness bank \(P\) is untouched.  The missing step is constructing the
rainbow path skeleton \(P\), not rounding its connector layer.

## 5. Comparison with the source-relative FRR circulation

Equations (4.10)--(4.11) of
`MATH_THEOREM_K16_BIRESIDENT_COMPLEMENT_DOUBLE_AND_TURN_SELECTOR_20260729.md`
say, for the rank-seven facet rail on \([15]\),

\[
 \sum_{U\in H}
 \left({\bf1}_{U-a_U}+{\bf1}_{U-b_U}
       -{\bf1}_{U-a_U^0}-{\bf1}_{U-b_U^0}\right)=0        \tag{5.1}
\]

and impose the corresponding lower-colour restoration inequalities.
After adjoining the sixteenth coordinate \(z\), (5.1) is exactly the
**projected physical-owner degree equation** for the `z`-containing rail.
The lower rows become the K16 lower colours containing \(z\), and the
perfect selector row supplies exactly the K16 upper colours containing
\(z\).  Thus there is no marginal mismatch between FRR and IRRC16.

There is, however, an exact chronology obstruction.  The independent audit
`THREAD_A_FRR_IRRC16_SPARSE_HISTORY_LIFT_AUDIT_20260729.md` also records
that the certified number `60` is only the minimum positive-collar **cut**
size: no replacement selector or rethread of that size has been proved.

### Theorem 5.1 (FRR embedding criterion and present failure)

Let \(B_0,B_1\) be two rank-seven factors on \([15]\) differing by a
direction-coherent FRR cut-and-rethread on \(r\) physical edges, and lift
them to the \(z\)-containing rail of \(J(16,8)\).

1. If both \(B_0\) and \(B_1\) have **two-sided** cooldown four, then their
   history lifts exist in \(\mathcal H_3\), their signed difference belongs
   to \(\mathcal K_{16}\), equations (4.10)--(4.11) are exactly its owner
   and containing-\(z\) palette rows, and
   \[
   |\operatorname{supp}(y_1-y_0)|\le8r.                 \tag{5.2}
   \]
2. A hypothetical rethread at the certified 60-orbit cut scale has
   \(r=60\cdot15=900\), so a strengthened legal-to-legal rethread would
   give a correction supported on at most
   \[
   8r=7200                                                \tag{5.3}
   \]
   selected history arcs.
3. The actual saved facet source \(B_0\) has 1,425 positive runs of length
   three.
   Hence its swap word repeats a coordinate at transition distance three,
   and its sliding history contains illegal four-windows.  It is not a
   circulation on \(\mathcal H_3\).  Moreover `FRR(7,4)` asks only for
   positive residence, not two-sided cooldown.  Therefore the current
   60-orbit FRR proposal cannot, as stated, be represented as a sparse signed
   correction in \(\mathcal K_{16}\).

#### Proof

Adjoining \(z\) changes no old-coordinate swap label.  Under two-sided
cooldown, Theorem 2.1 applies verbatim and gives (5.2); the marginal
identifications follow from

\[
 \lambda((X+z)(Y+z))=(X\cap Y)+z,
 \qquad
 \upsilon((X+z)(Y+z))=(X\cup Y)+z.
\]

Equation (5.3) is (5.2) with \(r=900\).  A run of length three is bounded
by two changes of the same coordinate at distance three, contradicting
the defining disjointness of a legal history arc.  Thus the negative old
arcs required by a source-relative signed correction do not even belong
to \(A(\mathcal H_3)\).  Positive residence alone also leaves the
complementary short-gap windows uncontrolled.  \(\square\)

There are two further audited frozen-sign facts.  The unchanged opposite
rank-eight rail has `2,010` short zero-gaps, so a repair confined to the
facet rail cannot make the advertised two-rail source an IRRC16 point.
The facet derivative itself has `915` short zero-gaps in addition to its
1,425 short positive runs; the 60-orbit calculation optimized only the
positive collars.  These are defect censuses, not separate lower bounds on
the number of cuts.

The obstruction is sharp in scope.  It does not say that an FRR endpoint
cannot be part of a K16 construction.  It says that to use the **IRRC16
history lattice**, one must either strengthen FRR to a two-sided
legal-to-legal rethread, or enlarge the state space to admit defective
source windows and prove a separate repair-to-legal theorem.

## 6. Exact remaining lemma after the trade audit

The Q8 rung trade rules out a palette invariant and supplies the smallest
possible projected absorber.  What remains unproved is not the existence
of local palette motion.  It is the following global reservoir statement.

> **History-reservoir rounding lemma `HRR16`.**  There is a binary
> owner-exact circulation on \(\mathcal H_3\), together with a commuting
> bank of legal port trades, such that the bank has enough integral reserve
> copies for every lost colour and its gain incidence satisfies the Hall
> condition (4.2) for all lower and upper q1 deficits.

Theorem 4.1 shows that `HRR16` implies IRRC16.  The symmetric circulation
with load \(9/8\) proves the fractional analogue of the palette margin, but
does not prove either part of `HRR16`: it gives neither a binary
owner-perfect legal chronology nor an integral reserve-token/Hall bank.

Thus the first explicit integral obstruction is a **simultaneous owner and
reserve localization problem**.  There is no parity, palette-motion, or
minimum-trade obstruction: the four-edge Q8 rung circuit disposes of those.
The current FRR 60-orbit certificate supplies only a positive-collar cut
set.  Even a future solution of its projected degree and one-palette ledger
would start from outside the legal history graph, so it cannot serve as the
missing reservoir without a two-sided chronology upgrade.
