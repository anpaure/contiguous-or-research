## 2. Exact packet materialization

Let `P` be a declared state-relative menu of alternating `C6`, star-`C8`,
and octahedral-`C8` circuits.  A circuit `p` has old incidence set `D_p` and
new incidence set `A_p`.  For an action-disjoint terminal selector `lambda`,

\[
 y_e=y^0_e-\sum_{p:e\in D_p}\lambda_p
             +\sum_{p:e\in A_p}\lambda_p.          \tag{2.1}
\]

If supports overlap, use binary deletion/insertion OR channels and rebuild
the final `y`; signed singleton deltas may not be summed.  Enforce the exact
lower and owner degrees, protected incidences, and all guards on `y`.

For a strict ordered packet, construct stage states `y^i` and require every
circuit to be alternating at its actual tail.  Every claimed prefix must
pass all hard rows.  A terminal-union formulation makes no prefix claim.

At every ordinary root `L`, rebuild its selected owner-pair channel

\[
 p_{L,\{T,H\}}\longleftrightarrow
 y_{L,T}\wedge y_{L,H}.                              \tag{2.2}
\]

All q1, upper, primary-provider, and chronology calculations below use
(2.2), never a sum of base-local pair deltas.

## 3. Exact primary root-support master

Let a primary diamond `e=(L,U,T,H)` be available only when the rebuilt pair
`p_(L,{T,H})` is true and `U=T union H`.  Let `x_e` select primary diamonds.
For the fixed all-`19448` palette impose

\[
 \sum_{e:U(e)=U}x_e=1,                              \tag{3.1}
\]

\[
 \sum_{e:L(e)=L}x_e\le1,\qquad
 \sum_{e:T\in\{T(e),H(e)\}}x_e\le b_T,             \tag{3.2}
\]

\[
                         x_e\le p_e(y),             \tag{3.3}
\]

together with the protected primary rows and the required primary-forest
rank inequalities.  Because the declared circuits avoid `M,D`, their
boundary capacity ledger `b_T` is fixed.  A menu which changes the `D` bank
must instead expose those boundary incidences as master variables.

Put

\[
 A(x)=\mathcal R_8^\circ-U(x),\qquad
 c_x(T)=b_T-\deg_x(T),                               \tag{3.4}
\]

and, for an owner shore `Y`,

\[
 w_Y(L)=\bigl(2-|N(L)\setminus Y|\bigr)_+,
\quad
 \Psi_x(Y)=\sum_{L\in A(x)}w_Y(L)-\sum_{T\in Y}c_x(T). \tag{3.5}
\]

For fixed palette size let `D=2|A(x)|`.  The residual completion value is

\[
                  f(x)=D-d(x),\qquad
                  d(x)=\max_Y\Psi_x(Y).             \tag{3.6}
\]

Equivalently, with

\[
 \alpha_Y(e)=\mathbf1_{T(e)\in Y}+\mathbf1_{H(e)\in Y}-w_Y(L(e)),
\quad
 \kappa_Y=\sum_Lw_Y(L)-\sum_{T\in Y}b_T,            \tag{3.7}
\]

the exact primary Benders rows are

\[
 \boxed{\theta\ge\kappa_Y+\sum_e\alpha_Y(e)x_e
        \quad\hbox{for every owner shore }Y.}       \tag{3.8}
\]

At an integral or fractional master point satisfying (3.1)--(3.4), one
minimum cut in the residual root-owner network returns a maximally violated
row (3.8).  Thus `theta=0` is an exact residual-completion gate.  Packet
selection remains an integral Benders master; polynomial separation does
not imply that the whole guarded selector is a polynomial min-cost flow.

### 3.1 Full-q1 lollipop complement lemma

There is a stronger simplification on the present carrier face.  Delete the
exceptional `M,D` roots from a connected normalized augmented factor and
contract every ordinary root.  The result is two spanning owner paths with
`24308` ordinary occurrence edges.  Choose any one *actual factor occurrence*
for each of the `19448` rank-ten colours.  This primary set is root-injective
and is a linear forest because it is a subgraph of those two paths.

For either licensed opening, its internal seam joins the two ordinary paths
into the `24310`-owner Hamilton path.  Removing the `19448` selected primary
occurrences leaves `4861` path occurrences and therefore residual flow

\[
                         2\cdot4861=9722.            \tag{3.9}
\]

After contracting the primary forest, this is a path on `4862` quotient
vertices.  Adding the closing seam yields `4862` occurrences, flow `9724`,
and the quotient cycle.  Thus the literal complement saturates every
residual owner capacity, so

\[
             \Psi_x(Y)\le0\quad\hbox{for all }Y,
             \qquad \Psi_x(\varnothing)=0.          \tag{3.10}
\]

Consequently every one-per-colour re-marking inside a connected full-q1
carrier has exact residual deficiency zero.  There is no positive shore and
no residual-flow improvement above full; the empty shore is always tight.
Other tight shores may depend on the chosen primary marking, so the factor
alone does not define a canonical DM lattice.

For the present packet selector this means that the primary residual gate is
automatically preserved once the endpoint is independently proved connected
and full q1 in both openings: simply reselect one actual occurrence per
colour and use its complement.  It does **not** make the primary forest
connected, and it ceases to be automatic after source/compiler/socket rows
forbid occurrences.  Those later correlations must return to (3.1)--(3.8).

## 4. Tight shores, DM closure, and the takeover cut

Fix a primary state `x` and let `d=d(x)`.  Its tight provider shores are

\[
                    \mathcal T(x)=\{Y:\Psi_x(Y)=d\}. \tag{4.1}
\]

For a fixed legal signed primary exchange `z`, define

\[
 \delta_z(Y)=\Psi_{x+z}(Y)-\Psi_x(Y)
             =\sum_ez_e\alpha_Y(e).                 \tag{4.2}
\]

Then

\[
 \max_{Y\in\mathcal T(x)}\delta_z(Y)\le0           \tag{4.3}
\]

is exactly the statement that no *current* maximum shore worsens.  Replacing
the right side by `-g` says that every current tight shore is crossed by
margin `g`.  This is necessary for an improvement of `g`, but not sufficient:
an initially slack shore may take over.  The exact acceptance row is

\[
 \boxed{\max_Y\bigl(\Psi_x(Y)+\delta_z(Y)\bigr)
                    \le d-g.}                       \tag{4.4}
\]

It is one fresh min-cut.  In particular, at residual deficiency zero there
is no strict residual-flow gain; the correct packet condition is existence
of a rebuilt `x'` satisfying (3.1)--(3.8) with `theta=0`.

Equivalently, put `nu_0` for the old maximum flow, define old cut slack and
the fully materialized capacity current by

\[
 s(C)=C_0(C)-\nu_0\ge0,\qquad
 \chi_P(C)=C_P(C)-C_0(C).                            \tag{4.4a}
\]

When old and new networks have the same demand ledger,

\[
 \boxed{\nu_P\ge\nu_0+g
 \iff \chi_P(C)\ge g-s(C)\quad\hbox{for every cut }C.} \tag{4.4b}
\]

Thus a gain-one move must add one unit on every tight cut, lose nothing on
every slack-one cut, and may lose at most `s-1` on a slack-`s` cut.  Merely
meeting every current tight shore is sufficient only for an addition-only
change, where all other cut currents are nonnegative.  Circuit exchanges
delete as well as insert providers, so (4.4b), not the tight subset alone,
is the exact takeover criterion.

The Dulmage--Mendelsohn form is obtained from a maximum residual flow.
Contract the strongly connected components of its residual graph.  The
minimum cuts are exactly the appropriate closed ideals of this condensation
containing the source component and excluding the sink component.  Hence a
candidate can be separated over *all* current tight shores without listing
them.

An especially simple exact implementation compares the old and candidate
residual networks on a common arc universe.  If `C_0(S),C_1(S)` are their
nonnegative integral cut capacities and `B` exceeds every possible `C_1`, a
minimum cut with capacity

\[
                         B C_0(S)+C_1(S)             \tag{4.5}
\]

first minimizes `C_0` and, among all old minimum cuts, returns the least new
capacity.  This is precisely the worst current tight shore.  A separate
ordinary min-cut in network one is the takeover test (4.4).  Formula (4.5)
is the proof-safe max-closure/DM oracle; testing one displayed shore is not.

When the primary selector itself is allowed to change, (4.5) is a pricing
and diagnostic oracle only.  The exact test is the joint `x'` master with
lazy rows (3.8), followed by a fresh integral residual replay.

## 6. Literal upper and `Phi` separator

For a decoded integral factor candidate:

1. verify degrees, guards, protected rows, and (5.1);
2. reconstruct its tail, both licensed openings, and their unique opening
   bijections;
3. replay both rank-ten decks and the complete rank-11--17 interval-OR deck;
4. test (1.4), and test (1.5) when upper closure is hard;
5. rebuild (2.2), solve/separate (3.1)--(3.8), and require `theta=0`;
6. accept only after all literal replays pass.

For a finite one-hot whole-factor column bank these predicates are exact
column coefficients.  For a multi-packet selector, residence and arbitrary-
width upper changes are not sums of singleton coefficients.  A failing
integral endpoint yields a semantic no-good, or a stronger witness-provider
disjunction when the decoder can prove it.  Every normalized cut stores its
type, opening frame, target/shore identity, candidate factor hash, and exact
literal vector; duplicate vectors are merged only after provenance is kept.

### Theorem 6.1 (sound fail-closed loop)

The following loop is exact for a finite declared packet universe and its
declared terminal/prefix semantics.

1. Solve the structural packet/primary master.
2. Materialize every selected physical incidence and pair channel.
3. Separate graphic rank by (5.2).
4. Separate primary deficiency by (3.8).
5. Decode the two openings and separate q1, upper closure, and (1.4).
6. If every gate passes, accept; otherwise add the normalized rejecting cut
   and repeat.

Every emitted row is necessary for the declared acceptance predicate, so no
accepted state is removed.  There are finitely many integral packet states;
therefore exact no-goods alone prove termination, while stronger cuts only
accelerate it.  An UNSAT proof certifies no accepted state only in the frozen
packet universe and semantics.

## 7. One-packet and neutral-pivot decisions

A one-packet improvement from `F` is a current-state circuit whose literal
endpoint satisfies all hard carrier gates, (1.4), `theta=0`, and

\[
            \Phi_{\sigma(o)}(F')<\Phi_o(F)
            \quad\hbox{for at least one }o,          \tag{7.1}
\]

with no increase in any coordinate.  If none exists in a *complete frozen
singleton catalogue*, this is a scoped singleton no-go, not a global no-go.

The smallest useful neutral pivot `p` minimizes

\[
 (|\operatorname{rootSupp}(p)|,|D_p|+|A_p|)          \tag{7.2}
\]

subject to `C(F_p)=C(F)`, `theta(F_p)=0`, all carrier gates at `F_p`, and
the existence of a regenerated strict quench `q` for which both `F_p` and
`F_{p,q}` satisfy (5.1), the primary takeover test (4.4), upper closure, and
(1.4).  If the complete lifted fan has no accepting sink, its source-
reachable state set is the exact Farkas cut; a sampled miss is not.

## 9. Scope

This theorem couples carrier circuit selection, literal two-opening
residence/all-upper costs, primary root-support residual completion, and
global augmented topology.  It does not construct or certify a source
antecedent, lower/compiler incidence, terminal opening beyond the encoded
`M/D` aperture, residence zero, ranks11--17 completion, regeneration from
every future state, or a universal word.
