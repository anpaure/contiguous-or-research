# Lane J: an all-rank parity-kernel obstruction to the universal circuit atlas

Date: 2026-07-25

Method: pure mathematics only. No web search, finite search, solver, or
computer experiment is used.

## 0. Result and scope

Let \(n=2m+1\) with \(m\ge 4\) even, let \(\Omega_m\) be the unoriented
cyclic orders on \([n]\), and let \(B_r\) be cyclic \(r\)-interval
incidence.  Write

\[
 W=\binom{n}{m},\qquad B=\operatorname{Cat}_m=\frac Wn.
\]

This note proves the following exact no-go for the known universal
two-for-two wreath circuits.

1. Cyclic orders have a well-defined sign
   \(\epsilon(C)\in\{\pm1\}\), and the integral vector
   \(\epsilon=(\epsilon(C))_{C\in\Omega_m}\) satisfies
   \[
        B_r\epsilon=0\qquad(0\le r\le n).
   \]
   Thus sign is invisible not only in a fixed Gaussian window, but to the
   entire tower of contiguous-interval load maps.

2. Every known universal two-for-two circuit preserves the exact number
   \(N_-(F)\) of negative cyclic orders in an exact factor.  Nevertheless,
   there are genuine exact factors \(G\) and \(\pi G\), with \(\pi\) a
   coordinate transposition, such that
   \[
      |N_-(\pi G)-N_-(G)|\ge \operatorname{Cat}_{m-3},
   \]
   while every coordinate-symmetric floor-corrected multidepth energy has
   exactly the same value at the two factors.

3. More strongly, a nonzero multiple of \(\epsilon\) is the difference of
   two integral multicovers, each explicitly decomposed into genuine exact
   wreath factors.  Hence the lattice generated by the universal circuits
   misses a factor-decomposable direction which is neutral at every rank.

Consequently the known universal circuits cannot be a Markov basis, cannot
give a whole-fibre normal form, and cannot acquire either property by
adding more lower-rank energies or changing their positive weights.  This
is a method no-go, not a counterexample to MWB or to the contiguous-OR
conjecture.  In particular, it does **not** prove that some universal-circuit
component has a high floor-energy minimum.

## 1. Cyclic-order sign

Fix a reference unoriented cyclic order \(C_0\).  If \(C=\sigma C_0\), put

\[
                \epsilon(C)=\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma).
\tag{1.1}
\]

### Lemma 1.1 (well-definedness)

For even \(m\), (1.1) is independent of the choice of \(\sigma\).

#### Proof

The stabilizer of an unoriented cyclic order is the dihedral group
\(D_n\).  An \(n\)-cycle has sign \((-1)^{n-1}=1\).  A reflection of an
odd \(n=2m+1\) cycle has cycle type \(1\,2^m\), and therefore sign
\((-1)^m=1\).  Hence \(D_n\subseteq A_n\), and two permutations carrying
\(C_0\) to the same unoriented cyclic order have the same sign. \(\square\)

Put

\[
 \Omega_m^+=\{C:\epsilon(C)=1\},\qquad
 \Omega_m^-=\{C:\epsilon(C)=-1\}.
\tag{1.2}
\]

Any odd coordinate permutation interchanges the two classes, so they have
equal cardinality.

## 2. The sign vector is neutral at every rank

### Theorem 2.1 (simultaneous all-rank kernel)

For every \(0\le r\le n\),

\[
                         \boxed{B_r\epsilon=0.}
\tag{2.1}
\]

#### Proof

First take \(1\le r\le n-1\) and fix an \(r\)-set \(S\).  At least one of
\(S\) and \(S^c\) contains two coordinates.  Choose a transposition
\(\tau\) within that part.  Then \(\tau S=S\).

Let

\[
 \Omega(S)=\{C\in\Omega_m:S\text{ is a cyclic }r\text{-interval of }C\}.
\]

The map \(C\mapsto\tau C\) is a bijection of \(\Omega(S)\), and

\[
                    \epsilon(\tau C)=-\epsilon(C).
\tag{2.2}
\]

It has no fixed point.  Indeed a fixed cyclic order would make the single
transposition \(\tau\) an element of its dihedral stabilizer.  A nontrivial
rotation is not a transposition, while a reflection has \(m\ge2\)
transpositions in its cycle decomposition.  Thus (2.2) pairs the summands
in

\[
 (B_r\epsilon)(S)=\sum_{C\in\Omega(S)}\epsilon(C)
\]

with opposite signs, proving zero.

For \(r=0,n\), the unique target is an interval of every cyclic order, and
the sum is zero because \(|\Omega_m^+|=|\Omega_m^-|\). \(\square\)

### Corollary 2.2 (energy cannot see the parity direction)

Let \(\mathcal R\subseteq\{0,\ldots,n\}\) be arbitrary and let
\(\Phi\) be any function of the load tuple
\((B_rx)_{r\in\mathcal R}\).  Whenever both arguments belong to its
domain,

\[
       \Phi\bigl((B_r(x+t\epsilon))_r\bigr)
       =\Phi\bigl((B_rx)_r\bigr)
\tag{2.3}
\]

for every integer or real \(t\).  In particular, this holds for every
weighted sum of floor-corrected collision energies and for every weighted
overload objective.

This is a lattice statement.  It does not assert that \(x+t\epsilon\) is
the indicator of an exact factor.

## 3. The universal two-for-two census

For distinct \(\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\delta\), ordered complementary lists
\(|E|=m-1\), \(|O|=m-2\), put

\[
\begin{aligned}
C&=(\delta,\gamma,E,\beta,\alpha,O),&
D&=(\beta,\delta,E,\alpha,\gamma,O),\\
C'&=(\delta,\beta,E,\gamma,\alpha,O),&
D'&=(\gamma,\delta,E,\alpha,\beta,O).
\end{aligned}
\tag{3.1}
\]

The vector \(e_{C'}+e_{D'}-e_C-e_D\) is the known universal genuine
two-for-two exact-factor circuit.  Let \(L_{\mathrm{univ}}\) be the integer
span of all its coordinate relabellings.  Define

\[
 \chi(x)=\sum_{C\in\Omega_m^-}x_C,
 \qquad N_-(F)=\chi(\mathbf1_F).
\tag{3.2}
\]

### Lemma 3.1 (exact census conservation)

Every universal circuit \(z\) satisfies \(\chi(z)=0\).  Hence \(N_-\) is
constant on every connected component of the universal-circuit graph.

#### Proof

Let \(\rho=(\beta\ \gamma)\).  Then \(C'=\rho C\) and \(D'=\rho D\), so
both signs are reversed.  Relative to the displayed readout of \(C\), the
position word of \(D\) is

\[
 (m+2,1,3,4,\ldots,m+1,m+3,2,m+4,\ldots,2m+1).
\tag{3.3}
\]

Its inversion number is

\[
                    (m+1)+(m-1)+1=2m+1,
\]

which is odd.  Thus \(C,D\) have opposite signs.  The old pair contains
one order of each sign, and applying \(\rho\) shows the same for the new
pair.  Relabelling the configuration preserves this conclusion. \(\square\)

### Corollary 3.2 (an explicit all-rank-neutral quotient class)

One has

\[
 \epsilon\in\bigcap_{r=0}^n\ker_{\mathbb Z}B_r,
 \qquad
 \epsilon\notin L_{\mathrm{univ}}.
\tag{3.4}
\]

#### Proof

The first assertion is Theorem 2.1.  Every element of
\(L_{\mathrm{univ}}\) is killed by \(\chi\), whereas

\[
       \chi(\epsilon)=-|\Omega_m^-|\ne0.
\]

Therefore \(\epsilon\notin L_{\mathrm{univ}}\). \(\square\)

Thus universal-circuit lattice generation fails already inside the kernel
of the *entire* rank tower.  Separate lower-rank saturation results cannot
repair this missing direction.

## 4. Genuine equal-energy exact factors separated by the census

We use the established MSW component hierarchy for the canonical exact
factor \(F_m\) and \(\tau_0=(2\ 3)\).  Its \(j=1\) stratum consists of

\[
                    L=\operatorname{Cat}_{m-3}
\tag{4.1}
\]

independently switchable components, each containing three wreaths on each
side.  If one side of such a component contains \(a\) negative orders, its
transposed side contains \(3-a\); changing the component alters the census
by the nonzero odd number \(3-2a\).

### Theorem 4.1 (macroscopically separated equal-energy factors)

There is a genuine exact factor \(G\) such that

\[
 \left|B-2N_-(G)\right|\ge \operatorname{Cat}_{m-3}.
\tag{4.2}
\]

Consequently, for every coordinate transposition \(\pi\), the genuine
exact factors \(G\) and \(\pi G\) satisfy

\[
 \boxed{
 |N_-(\pi G)-N_-(G)|\ge\operatorname{Cat}_{m-3}
 =\left(\frac1{64}+o(1)\right)B.}
\tag{4.3}
\]

At the same time, for every depth \(q\),

\[
 B_{m-q}\mathbf1_{\pi G}=\pi B_{m-q}\mathbf1_G.
\tag{4.4}
\]

Hence every coordinate-symmetric floor-corrected energy or overload has
exactly the same value at \(G\) and \(\pi G\), simultaneously for all
depth windows and all choices of weights.

#### Proof

In each of the \(L\) independent three-wreath components, choose first the
side with the smaller negative census, keeping all other component choices
fixed.  This gives an exact cube vertex \(G_-\).  Choose instead the larger
census side in every one of these components, giving another exact vertex
\(G_+\).  Their census difference is

\[
 N_-(G_+)-N_-(G_-)
 =\sum_{K=1}^{L}|3-2a_K|\ge L.
\tag{4.5}
\]

At least one of the two integers \(N_-(G_-),N_-(G_+)\) is at distance at
least \(L/2\) from \(B/2\).  Choose that factor as \(G\).  This proves
(4.2).

The odd permutation \(\pi\) reverses the sign of every cyclic order, so

\[
                 N_-(\pi G)=B-N_-(G).
\]

Equations (4.2)--(4.3) follow.  Equation (4.4) is equivariance of cyclic
interval incidence.  A coordinate permutation merely permutes the target
coordinates of every load vector, so every symmetric floor energy and
overload is unchanged.  Finally, the Catalan adjacent-ratio formula gives
\(\operatorname{Cat}_{m-3}/\operatorname{Cat}_m\to4^{-3}=1/64\).
\(\square\)

### Corollary 4.2 (connectivity and monotonicity obstructions)

The factors \(G\) and \(\pi G\) lie in different universal-circuit
components despite having identical multidepth floor energy.  Moreover:

1. any path between them using trades of degree at most \(D\) has at least
   \(\operatorname{Cat}_{m-3}/D\) steps, because a degree-\(k\) trade changes
   \(N_-\) by at most \(k\);
2. no path from one to the other can be strictly decreasing for any one
   coordinate-symmetric floor energy, since its endpoint values are equal;
3. no nonincreasing path can contain a strict step.

Items 2--3 apply to arbitrary trades, while disconnection applies to the
known universal circuits.

## 5. A factor-decomposable neutral direction

The lattice vector in Corollary 3.2 is not merely a formal signed
relation.  A nonzero multiple of it is a difference of two sums of actual
exact factors.

Let \(A_n\) act on exact factors and define

\[
 X_G=\sum_{\sigma\in A_n}\mathbf1_{\sigma G},
 \qquad
 X_{\pi G}=\sum_{\sigma\in A_n}\mathbf1_{\sigma\pi G}.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Each side is, by construction, an integral multicover decomposed into
\(|A_n|\) genuine exact factors.

### Theorem 5.1 (exact orbit-sum identity)

Put \(d=N_+(G)-N_-(G)=B-2N_-(G)\).  Then

\[
                  \boxed{X_G-X_{\pi G}=2n\,d\,\epsilon.}
\tag{5.2}
\]

In particular, \(d\ne0\), the two factor-decomposable multicovers are
distinct, and

\[
             B_rX_G=B_rX_{\pi G}\qquad(0\le r\le n).
\tag{5.3}
\]

#### Proof

The stabilizer of any unoriented cyclic order is \(D_n\), of order \(2n\),
and Lemma 1.1 gives \(D_n\subseteq A_n\).  The group \(A_n\) is transitive
on each of \(\Omega_m^+\) and \(\Omega_m^-\).  For fixed same-sign cyclic
orders \(E,C\), exactly \(2n\) elements of \(A_n\) carry \(E\) to \(C\).
Consequently

\[
 X_G
 =2n\left(
 N_+(G)\mathbf1_{\Omega_m^+}
 +N_-(G)\mathbf1_{\Omega_m^-}
 \right).
\tag{5.4}
\]

Because \(\pi\) is odd, its factor has the two census numbers exchanged.
Subtracting the analogue of (5.4) for \(\pi G\) gives (5.2).  Equation
(5.3) follows from Theorem 2.1. \(\square\)

This is the precise semigroup-level obstruction: after passing to a common
factor multiplicity, two explicit sums of literal exact factors have the
same loads at every rank, but their difference is outside the universal
circuit lattice.

## 6. Consequence for Lane J and the smallest replacement lemma

The preceding theorems definitively close the following proposed route:

> generate the exact-factor fibre, or a unique load-energy normal form in
> it, using only the known universal two-for-two circuits, with the choice
> justified by any collection of contiguous-interval load energies.

The obstruction has two independent halves.  The census is exactly
conserved by those circuits, while the whole rank tower is exactly blind to
the corresponding parity direction.  Adding more depths cannot turn the
census into an energy gradient.

What is **not** proved is equally important.  The result does not exclude
the possibility that every attained census class separately contains a
factor of energy \(O(H\operatorname{Cat}_m)\), nor does it exhibit a high
local minimum under every genuine trade of bounded degree.  Thus it is not
a counterexample to a class-by-class augmentation theorem.

The smallest honest replacement for the universal-circuit route is the
following support-feasible escape statement.

### Census-changing escape lemma \(\mathrm{CCE}_A\) — UNPROVED

For every fixed \(A>0\), there are \(D_A(m)=e^{o(m)}\) and \(C_A<\infty\)
such that, whenever an exact factor \(F\) has floor-corrected window energy

\[
 \mathcal Q_A(F)>C_AH_A\operatorname{Cat}_m,
 \qquad H_A=\lceil A\sqrt m\rceil,
\]

and no applicable universal two-for-two circuit decreases
\(\mathcal Q_A\), there is an applicable genuine trade \(z\) of degree at
most \(D_A(m)\) with

\[
       \chi(z)\ne0,
       \qquad
       \mathcal Q_A(F+z)<\mathcal Q_A(F).
\tag{6.1}
\]

Degree-three MSW component trades prove that census-changing exact circuits
exist at special factors.  They do not prove their applicability, or
energy descent, at an arbitrary universal-local minimum.  That is exactly
the additional positive geometry which the parity-kernel obstruction
forces any successful bounded/slowly-growing Markov augmentation argument
to supply.

## 7. Adversarial audit

1. **Parity is restricted to even \(m\).**  When \(m\) is odd, reflections
   of an odd cyclic order are odd permutations, so (1.1) is not defined on
   unoriented cyclic orders.  The theorem is an infinite-subsequence
   obstruction, which is sufficient to refute a purported all-\(m\)
   universal-circuit Markov theorem.

2. **Universal circuits are not all degree-two circuits.**  Census
   conservation is proved only for the relabelled family (3.1).  No claim
   is made that every genuine degree-two wreath trade preserves \(N_-\).

3. **Equal energy is not low energy.**  Theorem 4.1 gives genuine factors
   with exactly equal symmetric floor energies; it does not bound their
   common value by \(O(HB)\) or below.

4. **The orbit sums are multicovers.**  Each side of (5.2) is explicitly a
   sum of actual exact factors, but it is not one squarefree exact factor.
   Theorem 4.1, not Theorem 5.1, supplies the single-factor separation.

5. **No conjecture counterexample.**  A disconnected move atlas may have a
   low-energy factor in every component.  The report refutes whole-fibre
   connectivity and incidence-only normal forms for the known universal
   atlas, not MWB and not \(\nu(k)\le(1+o(1))W(k)\).

6. **Why all-rank neutrality is exact.**  The transposition used in the
   proof of Theorem 2.1 is chosen after fixing the target \(S\), merely to
   pair the columns incident with that row.  It is not asserted that one
   transposition pairs all rows simultaneously.  Rowwise cancellation is
   exactly what the matrix identity \(B_r\epsilon=0\) requires.
