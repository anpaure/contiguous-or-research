# De Bruijn fibre lifting, root rigidity, and the first \(H=2\) obstruction

Date: 2026-07-26

Method: pure mathematics only.

## 0. Outcome

Let

\[
                 \Omega=\binom{[2m]}m,\qquad W=|\Omega|.
\]

The attempted induction from the exact \(H=1\) Latin resolution has a
rigid failure:

> Once an integral prefix section is fixed, its de Bruijn extension is not
> a fibrewise choice. If the prefix section is generated by the permutation
> \(P\), suffix balance forces the next state to be \(P^{H+1}X\).

Thus the \((m-H)^2\) individually legal continuations of an \(H\)-arc
collapse to one continuation after the global root and circulation rows
are imposed. A color of an \(H=1\) Latin resolution lifts to \(H=2\) if
and only if

\[
                         d_J(X,P^2X)=2\quad(X\in\Omega).          \tag{0.1}
\]

The \(H=1\) Latin theorem alone gives no control of (0.1).

There are nevertheless two positive results.

1. At the **arc-state** level, all \(H=2\) safety constraints can be
   resolved integrally: at every owner, pair every incoming Johnson arc
   with a safe outgoing arc. These local pairings decompose the full
   directed Johnson arc set into \(2\)-safe arc cycles.
2. Fixing a coordinate pairing gives a non-equivariant \(2\)-safe cyclic
   root section on all but

   \[
   E_m=\begin{cases}
   \binom m{m/2},&m\text{ even},\\[2mm]
   2m\binom{m-1}{(m-1)/2},&m\text{ odd}
   \end{cases}                                                   \tag{0.2}
   \]

   roots. Hence \(E_m/W=O(m2^{-m})\), and the number of component resets
   is also \(o(W)\).

Neither statement solves the root-transversal quota problem. The first
leaves a whole-cycle set-partitioning matrix; the second gives no bound on
the collision energy of its signed targets. Global coordinate relabeling
cannot repair that energy, because it only permutes target coordinates.

There is also a genuine integral obstruction beyond equivariance. For
\(m=H=2\), every safe section would obey \(P^2X=X^c\). Complementation on
the six roots is a product of three transpositions, but a permutation
square has an even number of transpositions. Thus the symmetric fractional
circulation is nonempty while the integral root-transversal polytope is
empty.

For asymptotic near-balance, the surviving gate is quantitative: find a
\(2\)-safe root permutation, or an \(o(W)\)-boundary path section, whose
signed collision energies differ by \(o(W)\) from their convex minima.
Exact floor/ceiling quotas are neither needed nor appropriate.

## 1. Integral chronology and fibre rigidity

An integral \(H\)-section selects one safe arc

\[
                         (X_0,X_1,\ldots,X_H)                     \tag{1.1}
\]

at every root \(X_0\in\Omega\), with equality between its selected prefix
and suffix \(H\)-state multisets. Equivalently,

\[
                         Rx=\mathbf1_\Omega,\qquad B_Hx=0.        \tag{1.2}
\]

### Theorem 1.1 (chronology rigidity)

Every integral solution of (1.2) is generated by one permutation
\(P:\Omega\to\Omega\): its selected arc at \(X\) is

\[
                         (X,PX,P^2X,\ldots,P^HX).                  \tag{1.3}
\]

Conversely, (1.3) is an integral \(H\)-section exactly when \(P\) is a
Johnson cycle cover and every \(H\)-step window is a Johnson geodesic.

#### Proof

The first two entries of the selected arc at \(X\) define a Johnson
successor \(P(X)\). The root row gives one outgoing successor at every
root. Prefix--suffix balance says that the suffix beginning at \(P(X)\)
is the selected prefix rooted at \(P(X)\). Its next state is therefore
\(P^2(X)\). Iteration gives (1.3). The same balance gives one predecessor
at every root, so \(P\) is a permutation. Safety of the selected arcs is
precisely local geodesicity of its powers. The converse follows by reading
the cyclic windows of \(P\). \(\square\)

### Corollary 1.2 (a fixed prefix has at most one lift)

Fix an integral \(H\)-section generated by \(P\). An integral
\((H+1)\)-section having exactly those selected length-\(H\) prefixes
exists if and only if

\[
                         d_J(X,P^{H+1}X)=H+1
                         \quad(X\in\Omega).                       \tag{1.4}
\]

If it exists, it is unique.

Thus the \((m-H)^2\) safe outgoing extensions of an individual prefix are
not independent choices. Suffix balance selects the one whose last state
is \(P^{H+1}X\). A Latin resolution at depth \(H\) cannot be refined color
by color unless its colors were already \((H+1)\)-safe. A successful
induction must recolor or trade whole prefix sections before extending
them.

## 2. The exact \(H=2\) compatibility condition

Let \(D=m^2\) be the directed Johnson degree, and let

\[
                         \{P_c:c\in[D]\}                         \tag{2.1}
\]

be an \(H=1\) Latin resolution. Every directed Johnson arc occurs in one
\(P_c\), and every \(P_c\) is a root permutation.

At an owner \(Y\), write an incoming move as

\[
            X\longrightarrow Y:\quad a\mapsto b,
            \qquad a\notin Y,\quad b\in Y,                       \tag{2.2}
\]

and an outgoing move as

\[
            Y\longrightarrow Z:\quad c\mapsto d,
            \qquad c\in Y,\quad d\notin Y.                       \tag{2.3}
\]

The two-step path is safe exactly when

\[
                              c\ne b,\qquad d\ne a.               \tag{2.4}
\]

For each color, its incoming and outgoing arcs at \(Y\) are paired by
equality of color. Hence a colorwise lift of (2.1) exists exactly when all
these \(D\) local pairs obey (2.4) at every \(Y\).

This is not a consequence of proper edge coloring. Locally, the
equality-of-color pairing may be any bijection between the \(D\) incoming
and \(D\) outgoing moves. It can pair every move with its reverse, making
all \(D\) pairs unsafe, or it can be completely safe. For the latter,
choose fixed-point-free permutations

\[
                         \sigma_Y:Y\to Y,\qquad
                         \tau_Y:Y^c\to Y^c                         \tag{2.5}
\]

and pair \((a,b)\) with

\[
                         (c,d)=(\sigma_Y(b),\tau_Y(a)).            \tag{2.6}
\]

### Theorem 2.1 (local \(H=2\) de Bruijn resolution)

For every \(m\ge2\), the full directed Johnson arc set admits an integral
pairing of incoming and outgoing arcs at every root such that every paired
two-step path is safe. Consequently the arc states decompose into directed
cycles all of whose consecutive two-step windows are geodesic.

This does **not** give a root-transversal factor. Let \(\mathcal C\) be the
resulting arc-state cycles and put

\[
                         M_{X,C}=\#\{\text{arcs of }C
                         \text{ whose tail is }X\}.                \tag{2.7}
\]

Selecting whole cycles to make one root section is exactly

\[
                         My=\mathbf1_\Omega,\qquad
                         y\in\{0,1\}^{\mathcal C}.                 \tag{2.8}
\]

Thus local de Bruijn pairing leaves a set-partitioning problem, not a
network matrix. If some cycle has \(M_{X,C}\ge2\), that cycle cannot occur
in any root-transversal color. Even when \(M\) is zero--one, its columns
need not possess a resolution. This is the precise root obstruction left
after all local safety constraints have been solved.

## 3. Quantitative obstruction to repairing one \(H=1\) color

For a Johnson permutation \(P\), define

\[
                         \mathcal U(P)
  =\{X:d_J(X,P^2X)<2\},\qquad U(P)=|\mathcal U(P)|.                 \tag{3.1}
\]

### Theorem 3.1 (edit lower bound)

If \(P'\) is a \(2\)-safe Johnson permutation and

\[
                         C=\{X:P'(X)\ne P(X)\},                    \tag{3.2}
\]

then

\[
                         \mathcal U(P)\subseteq C\cup P^{-1}C,
                         \qquad |C|\ge {U(P)\over2}.               \tag{3.3}
\]

#### Proof

If \(X\notin C\) and \(P(X)\notin C\), then

\[
                         P'(X)=P(X),\qquad P'^2(X)=P^2(X).         \tag{3.4}
\]

Such an \(X\) remains unsafe under \(P'\), which is impossible. Therefore
every unsafe \(X\) lies in \(C\cup P^{-1}C\), a set of size at most
\(2|C|\). \(\square\)

This is an asymptotic obstruction to literal colorwise induction. A Latin
color with \(U(P)=\Omega(W)\) cannot be made liftable by an \(o(W)\)-size
trade. For example, a Johnson perfect matching, oriented in both
directions, gives an involution \(P\) with \(P^2=1\) and \(U(P)=W\).

Such a matching exists elementarily. Separate the middle sets according as
they contain coordinate \(1\). After deleting \(1\) from the first shore,
adjacency is the \(m\)-regular inclusion graph between the
\((m-1)\)-sets and the \(m\)-sets of \([2m]\setminus\{1\}\), so Hall gives
a perfect matching. Its involutive directed factor can be included as one
color of an \(H=1\) bipartite Latin resolution, since deleting it leaves a
regular bipartite graph. Hence the exact \(H=1\) theorem cannot assert that
each color is even approximately liftable.

There is a corresponding stability fact for target histograms.

### Lemma 3.2 (target stability under sparse successor edits)

If permutations \(P,P'\) differ on \(k\) roots, then their signed
depth-\(q\) occurrence histograms, wherever both \(q\)-step traces are
defined, differ in \(\ell^1\) by at most

\[
                              2qk.                                 \tag{3.5}
\]

#### Proof

The \(q\)-step word from \(X\) is unchanged unless

\[
                         X\in\bigcup_{j=0}^{q-1}P^{-j}C,           \tag{3.6}
\]

a set of size at most \(qk\). Changing one occurrence changes a histogram
in \(\ell^1\) by at most two. \(\square\)

Consequently the sharp absorption target is: find an \(H=1\) color with
\(U(P)=o(W)\) and near-balanced traces on its safe starts, then repair it
using \(o(W)\) successor edits. Lemma 3.2 shows that such a repair preserves
near-balance. The Latin theorem alone supplies none of these correlated
properties.

## 4. A non-equivariant \(H=2\) near-section

Fix a perfect pairing

\[
                         \Pi=\{\{u_i,v_i\}:1\le i\le m\}           \tag{4.1}
\]

of the \(2m\) coordinates. For \(X\in\Omega\), let \(h(X)\) be the number
of pairs of \(\Pi\) on which \(X\) contains exactly one endpoint. Once the
empty pairs, full pairs, and the set of \(h\) split pairs are fixed, the
owners form a cube \(Q_h\), with an edge flipping the chosen endpoint of
one split pair.

The number of roots with exactly \(h\) split pairs is

\[
 A_h=\binom mh2^h
     \binom{m-h}{(m-h)/2},                              \tag{4.2}
\]

when \(h\equiv m\pmod2\), and zero otherwise. Every cube \(Q_h\) with
\(h\ge2\) has a cyclic Gray Hamilton cycle. Consecutive edges of a simple
cube cycle flip different coordinate pairs, including at the cyclic seam.
Therefore each such cycle is \(2\)-safe in the Johnson graph.

### Theorem 4.1 (pair-cube near-section)

The union of cyclic Gray cycles over all pair-cube components with
\(h\ge2\) is an integral, non-equivariant, \(2\)-safe cyclic root section
on \(W-E_m\) roots, where \(E_m\) is (0.2). Moreover

\[
                         {E_m\over W}=O(m2^{-m}).                   \tag{4.3}
\]

The number of cycles is at most \(3^m=o(W)\).

#### Proof

The parity relation \(h\equiv m\pmod2\) shows that the omitted roots have
\(h=0\) for even \(m\) and \(h=1\) for odd \(m\). Formula (4.2) gives
(0.2). Central-binomial estimates give (4.3). Different pair-type
components are disjoint, and a cyclic Gray cycle is \(2\)-safe as observed
above. Finally, a component is specified by declaring every coordinate
pair empty, full, or split, subject to one size equation. Hence there are
at most \(3^m\) components, while \(W\asymp4^m/\sqrt m\). \(\square\)

This proves that neither non-equivariance nor root integrality causes a
positive-density \(H=2\) leave. It suffices for a relaxed compiler which
may pay \(O(1)\) per omitted root and per component. It is not a
target-coverage theorem.

## 5. Exact pair-frame target capacities

The failure to prove target balance in Theorem 4.1 is not a scalar target
capacity failure. Let \(T\) have rank \(m-q\), and let \(e_\Pi(T)\) be the
number of coordinate pairs disjoint from \(T\). Writing the numbers of
empty, split, and full pairs as \(e,s,f\) gives

\[
                         e-f=q,\qquad e\ge q.                       \tag{5.1}
\]

The number of ordered \(q\)-step pair-flip paths whose lower intersection
is \(T\) equals

\[
                         2^q(e_\Pi(T))_{\underline q}.             \tag{5.2}
\]

Indeed, choose and order \(q\) empty pairs and choose the initial endpoint
in each. Similarly, for a rank-\((m+q)\) upper target \(U\), if
\(f_\Pi(U)\) is its number of full coordinate pairs, then \(f_\Pi(U)\ge q\)
and the number of ordered pair-flip paths with union \(U\) is

\[
                         2^q(f_\Pi(U))_{\underline q}.             \tag{5.3}
\]

Thus every signed target at \(q=1,2\) is physically admissible in one fixed
pair frame. But the capacities range from \(2^qq!\) to order \(m^q\).
Choosing one Gray cycle per cube component does not itself resolve this
nonuniform occurrence census.

There is also no benefit from globally relabeling one completed factor.
For a section \(P\), let \(n_{q,T}^\pm(P)\) be its occurrence multiplicity
at signed target \(T\). A coordinate permutation merely permutes the
entries of \(n_q^\pm(P)\). Hence every symmetric orbit of one factor has
the same hole count and collision energy. Mixing must occur between
components or frames before the root section is frozen.

## 6. The correct asymptotic balance functional

Suppose a partial section supplies \(V\) signed depth-\(q\) occurrences on
a target layer of size

\[
                         N_q=\binom{2m}{m-q}.                       \tag{6.1}
\]

For a histogram \(n=(n_T)\), put

\[
                         \Phi(n)=\sum_T\binom{n_T}{2}.             \tag{6.2}
\]

Write \(V=aN_q+r\), \(0\le r<N_q\). Discrete convexity gives

\[
                         \Phi_{\min}(V,N_q)
  =N_q\binom a2+ra.                                      \tag{6.3}
\]

### Lemma 6.1 (energy criterion for near-balance)

If

\[
                         \Phi(n)-\Phi_{\min}(V,N_q)=o(W),          \tag{6.4}
\]

then \(n\) can be changed into a floor/ceiling histogram of mass \(V\) by
moving \(o(W)\) unit occurrences. In particular its \(\ell^1\) distance
from such a histogram is \(o(W)\).

#### Proof

If two bins have loads \(x\ge y+2\), moving one unit from the first to the
second reduces \(\Phi\) by

\[
                         (x-1)-y\ge1.                              \tag{6.5}
\]

Repeat until all loads differ by at most one. The number of moves is at
most the initial energy excess, and each move changes \(\ell^1\) by two.
\(\square\)

For a \(2\)-safe section, (6.4) is needed simultaneously for

\[
                         n_1^-,\ n_1^+,\ n_2^-,\ n_2^+.            \tag{6.6}
\]

The pair-cube near-section proves the root and safety parts with an
exponentially small leave, but no estimate of these four energy excesses
has been proved. Conversely, a target-balanced \(H=1\) Latin color with
\(U(P)=\Omega(W)\) is separated by Theorem 3.1 from every \(2\)-safe
section at \(o(W)\) edit distance. Root safety and target energy must
therefore be optimized jointly.

## 7. A finite integral obstruction at \(H=2\)

### Theorem 7.1 (the \(m=2\) square-root obstruction)

For \(m=H=2\), the symmetric fractional overlap circulation has no integral
root-transversal section.

#### Proof

A safe two-step path starting at a two-subset \(X\subset[4]\) deletes both
members of \(X\) and inserts both members of \(X^c\). Theorem 1.1 therefore
forces

\[
                              P^2(X)=X^c.                           \tag{7.1}
\]

Complementation on the six two-subsets is a product of three
transpositions. But squaring a permutation produces transpositions in
pairs: each transposition in a square comes from a four-cycle, and the
square of a four-cycle is a product of two transpositions. Hence no
permutation square can equal a product of three transpositions. Equation
(7.1) is impossible. \(\square\)

This is an actual integrality obstruction, not a failure of equivariant
choice. It is finite and does not imply a positive-density obstruction as
\(m\to\infty\); Theorem 4.1 points in the opposite direction.

## 8. Precise surviving lift gate

The direct induction from an arbitrary \(H=1\) Latin resolution is closed:
prefix/suffix fibres cannot be chosen independently, and a color with
\(U(P)=\Omega(W)\) needs \(\Omega(W)\) edits before it can lift.

For any safe local arc pairing from Theorem 2.1, let
\(A_q^\pm(C,T)\) count the signed depth-\(q\) occurrences of target \(T\)
on the arc-state cycle \(C\). The exact asymptotic selection problem is

\[
\begin{aligned}
 My&\le\mathbf1_\Omega,\\
 \mathbf1^\top My&=W-o(W),\\
 y&\in\{0,1\}^{\mathcal C},\\
 \Phi(A_q^\pm y)-\Phi_{\min}(\mathbf1^\top My,N_q)&=o(W)
       \qquad(q=1,2).
\end{aligned}                                                     \tag{8.1}
\]

Replacing the first two rows by \(My=\mathbf1_\Omega\) asks for an exact
root-transversal cycle cover. Thus (8.1), rather than a scalar Euler
circulation, is the precise near-balanced integral lift.

For \(H=2\), any of the following would give the required asymptotic
section.

1. **Compatible Latin color.** Construct a Johnson permutation \(P\) with
   \(U(P)=0\) and all four energy excesses in (6.4) equal to \(o(W)\).
2. **Sparse-boundary lift.** Construct an \(H=1\) permutation with
   \(U(P)=o(W)\), balanced signed traces on all safe starts, and an
   \(o(W)\)-edit absorber. Theorem 3.1 and Lemma 3.2 give the exact repair
   and stability scales.
3. **Mixed-frame cube resolution.** Start from Theorem 4.1 and change pair
   frames on whole cube components so as to reduce the four convex energies
   by \(o(W)\), while spending only \(o(W)\) roots and component seams.

The local pairing theorem proves that safe continuations exist everywhere;
the pair-cube theorem proves that root integrality has no asymptotic scalar
deficit. What remains is a joint whole-component/root-color discrepancy
problem. Global group averaging cannot solve it, because it preserves the
target energy of every individual factor.
