\[
                 P_{\rho,p}=\rho(Q_p^0\cup Q_p^1).       \tag{0.4}

Thus common-cap containment is not the obstruction.

The length conclusion has three distinct scopes.

1. If seven old host letters `X_h` are already present, refining each as
   `X_h -> X_h,B_h^epsilon` costs exactly seven positions.  Both endpoint
   states have the same length, so repeated C8 phase switches do not
   *accumulate* further length; they carry one fixed seven-boundary bank.
2. In the authenticated sharp blocks none of the seven `X_h` occurs.
   Literal insertion of the two-cell blocks therefore costs fourteen

3. The smallest zero-length local payment would replace two adjacent old
   letters `(U,V)` by `(X_h,B_h^epsilon)`.  Necessarily `U union V=X_h`.
   Exact replay for every `5<=d<=64`, both phases and every relabelled block,
   finds **zero** such adjacent pairs for all seven hosts.  Hence the native
   local two-cell replacement is impossible.  A nonlocal seven-contraction
   payment remains a separate occurrence-labelled contraction-Hall problem.

There is also a decisive flat-owner obstruction.  Before host planting the
four blocks have `32d+92` central owner occurrences but only `8d+24`
distinct owners, an occurrence surplus of

\[
                              24d+68.                     \tag{0.5}
\]

Seven local host supports, even accompanied by seven separate local

at least

\[
                         24d+68-14(d+2)=10d+40            \tag{0.6}
\]

duplicate-surplus units survive untouched.  Thus the seven-host bank cannot
make the literal four-block relation a simple owner factor, even under the
most generous local zero-length payment.  This strengthens the local
adjacent-union no-go.  The exact largest common `d`-overlap already fails as

Independently, inserting one internal binary host
into an existing exact rank-`r` Johnson source creates `d` forced windows of

the asymmetric host `X_S` itself has rank `r`; every internal rank-`r`
owner window containing the two-cell block `(X_S,B_S^epsilon)` must equal

when `d>=2`.  Therefore `X_S` must be clipped at a global source endpoint,
paid by a zero-length rethread, or handled by a genuinely nonflat compiler.

The seven-ray menu is consequently **cap-feasible and length-stable after
birth**, but it cannot physicalize the literal four-block macro.  A positive
construction must first replace the repeated central bank by a genuinely

compiler interface become relevant.

 (4,4,5,6,4,d+3,4)                                     \tag{1.3}

             (P,G_d,G_{d+1},G_{0,d+1},E,S,R).           \tag{1.4}

 (r-d+1,r-d+1,r-d+2,r-d+3,r-d+1,r,r-d+1),              \tag{1.5}

so every cap has rank at most `r` for `d>=5`.  At a phase-aligned host

       (\rho_bX_h,\rho_bB_h^\epsilon),\qquad
       (P_{h,0},P_{h,1})=(\rho_bX_h,\rho_bX_h).          \tag{1.6}

## 2. Exact length ledger

For `s` already-present old hosts, binary refinement changes length by `s`.
Thus one global seven-slot bank has

\[
        4N\longmapsto4N+7=36d+99,                       \tag{2.1}
\]

while one independent seven-slot bank in each of the four blocks has

\[
        4N\longmapsto4N+28=36d+120.                     \tag{2.2}
\]

Both relation states have the same charge.  Contracting and resplitting the
same bank prevents temporal accumulation, but does not erase the initial
`+7` (or `+28`).

If the hosts are absent, planting `(X_h,B_h^epsilon)` by insertion costs two
positions per slot.  To obtain zero net length one must either:

* replace two old positions by the two host positions locally; or
* contract the same number of old boundaries elsewhere, with a simultaneous
  protected-target matching and owner/deadline certificate.

The first option has the following exact necessary condition.  If an old
adjacent pair `(U,V)` is replaced by `(X,B)` and intervals which contained
the complete old pair are to retain their OR by the canonical block map,
then

\[
                              U\cup V=X.                 \tag{2.3}
\]

No adjacent pair of either sharp phase satisfies (2.3) for any menu host.
Coordinate relabelling preserves unions, so this remains true in all four
blocks (1.1).  This proves the scoped local no-go.

The second option is not a scalar consequence of the four-block deck
identity.  Contracting a boundary can merge physical cells and destroy
partial-endpoint occurrences even when all complete-block intervals
transport.  It is safe exactly when the protected target-to-coarsened-cell
equality graph satisfies Hall, together with the physical owner/deadline
rows.  No such seven-boundary certificate is present in the current menu.

### 2.1 The local-support owner lower bound

The authoritative physical audit gives

\[
 \#\text{owner occurrences}=32d+92,
 \qquad \#\text{distinct owners}=8d+24.                 \tag{2.4}
\]

If a source edit is supported on one adjacent pair, only owner windows whose
start lies in an interval of length `d+2` can change.  A seven-host
zero-length payment has at most fourteen such supports: seven host supports
and seven contraction supports.  Therefore at most `14(d+2)` original owner
occurrences can change.  Every untouched occurrence retains its old owner
value, so eliminating all multiplicity would require touching at least the
surplus in (2.4).  But

\[
                 14(d+2)<24d+68\qquad(d\ge1).           \tag{2.5}
\]

This proves (0.6).  The argument permits overlaps, arbitrary orientations,
and the best possible new values at every touched window.  It is a support
lower bound, not a Hall heuristic.

The same obstruction can be phrased as a minimum-support statement: any
adjacent-support physicalization must use at least

\[
                     \left\lceil\frac{24d+68}{d+2}\right\rceil \tag{2.6}
\]

support intervals merely to touch enough duplicate occurrences.  The seven
hosts and seven payment contractions supply only fourteen.

There is no rescue by uniform private block tags.  The signed residual
transport has the connected `K_(2,2)` surplus graph with shores `{0,1}` and
`{2,3}` and edges `02,03,12,13`.
If block `b` receives a uniform private tag `tau_b`, equality along its four
edges forces

\[
                       \tau_0=\tau_1=\tau_2=\tau_3.       \tag{2.7}
\]

The tags therefore cannot distinguish the four repeated copies.  A useful
tag must be occurrence-local, and then it is counted by the support bound
(2.5).

### 2.2 Position-dependent edge-shared tag rails

There is a more refined escape than uniform tags.  Put one tag on each edge
of the surplus `K_(2,2)`,

\[
                         t_{02},t_{03},t_{12},t_{13}.     \tag{2.8}
\]

For one matched shallow occurrence key, exact equality permits precisely two
one-tag patterns:

\[
\begin{array}{c|cccc}
 &0&1&2&3\\ \hline
\text{parallel}&t_{02}&t_{13}&t_{02}&t_{13}\\
\text{crossed}&t_{03}&t_{12}&t_{12}&t_{03}.
\end{array}                                               \tag{2.9}
\]

Thus each protected residual interval of width at most `d` must be
monochromatic—parallel or crossed.  If it saw both orientations, then in
each block it would see both incident tags, while its matched mate has a
different two-tag set.

The raw authoritative relation is only **column-marginal exact** at these
widths.  Direct replay gives

\[
 \Sigma_{\le d}(0,2)^-=\Sigma_{\le d}(0,2)^+,
 \qquad
 \Sigma_{\le d}(1,3)^-=\Sigma_{\le d}(1,3)^+,          \tag{2.10}
\]

but both row equalities for `(0,1)` and `(2,3)` fail.  The independent
referee theorem
`MATH_THEOREM_THREAD_D_C8_FOURBLOCK_SEVEN_HOST_REFEREE_20260801.md`
checks all ten commuting-involution source relations and gives the finite
classification row-only `2`, column-only `2`, both `0`, neither `6`, for
both unguarded and guarded blocks through `d=8`.  Therefore a raw
row-and-column two-anchor tag construction is already missing one shallow
marginal in the authoritative column-only relation.  The seven planted rays
would have to repair this row residue simultaneously; it cannot be discarded
as a cap choice.

The positionwise common cap remains immediate.  If `R_b(p)` is the
state-independent rail-tag set placed at address `(b,p)`, use

\[
       P'_{b,p}=\rho_b(Q_p^0\cup Q_p^1)\cup R_b(p).      \tag{2.11}
\]

Both C8 states lie in this cap, and adding/replacing coordinate labels at an
existing source address costs no source positions.  This is a genuine
zero-length cap row.  It is not yet an exact-rank owner row: for fresh tags,

\[
 |T'_{b,i}|=|T_{b,i}|+
   \left|\bigcup_{p=i}^{i+d}R_b(p)\right|                \tag{2.12}
\]

unless an old-coordinate deletion rail removes the same number of owner
coordinates.  Such a deletion rail is an additional common-cap/source
equation, not supplied by the seven hosts.

There is also a sharp window obstruction to the proposed stagger.  Let an
owner window `W=[i,i+d]` contain at least one parallel and one crossed key,
so that its four block signatures become the four distinct incident-edge
pairs

\[
 \{t_{02},t_{03}\},\{t_{12},t_{13}\},
 \{t_{02},t_{12}\},\{t_{03},t_{13}\}.                  \tag{2.13}
\]

If every subinterval of width at most `d` is protected, opposite
orientations inside `W` must be distance exactly `d`; hence they occur at
the two endpoints.  Indeed any closer pair lies in a protected interval of
length at most `d`.  Now shift to `[i+1,i+d+1]`.  To be mixed safely it too
would need opposite endpoint orientations.  Keeping the same orientation
puts the new left endpoint within distance `d-1` of the old right endpoint;
reversing it puts the new left endpoint adjacent to the old left endpoint.
Either creates a mixed protected interval of width at most `d`.

Therefore, for aligned occurrence keys,

\[
 \boxed{\text{four-way distinguished owner starts form an independent set}}
                                                               \tag{2.14}
\]

for one edge-tag layer.  A pure parallel or pure crossed owner window has
only two signatures and leaves multiplicity two.  Hence one aligned rail
cannot distinguish a consecutive run of common owner addresses.

This last inference is deliberately aligned-scope.  The four Klein paths
traverse the common owner set in different orders, so the four occurrences
of one owner need not have the same start address.  An occurrence-permuted
rail could try to stagger different copies at different positions; (2.13)
does not exclude it.  Such a construction must give an explicit
owner-occurrence matching, not only a relative-position rail.  It must also
solve the exact-rank deletion equation (2.12).  The support lower bound
(2.5), by contrast, does not require aligned owner orders and still closes
the stated seven-host plus seven-local-contraction class.

## 3. Exact owner equations for a zero-length local replacement

Let an old source contain the adjacent pair

\[
                         (Q_p,Q_{p+1})=(U,V)             \tag{3.1}
\]

and replace it without changing source length by `(X,B)`, with `B subseteq
X`.  Define the two exterior depth-`d` unions

\[
 L_p=\bigcup_{j=p-d}^{p-1}Q_j,
 \qquad R_p=\bigcup_{j=p+2}^{p+d+1}Q_j.                 \tag{3.2}
\]

Every length-`d+1` owner window outside `[p-d,p+1]` is unchanged.  The two
endpoint rows become

\[
       L_p\cup U\longmapsto L_p\cup X,
       \qquad V\cup R_p\longmapsto B\cup R_p.           \tag{3.3}
\]

Each of the `d` intervening windows contains both positions and changes by

\[
       C_i\cup U\cup V\longmapsto C_i\cup X,
               \qquad p-d+1\le i\le p,                 \tag{3.4}
\]

where `C_i` is the union of its other `d-1` letters.  Consequently the
original owner row is preserved exactly iff

\[
 \boxed{
 U\cup V=X,qquad L_p\cup U=L_p\cup X,qquad
 V\cup R_p=B\cup R_p.}                                  \tag{3.5}
\]

Under `U,V subseteq X`, the last two conditions say

\[
             X\setminus U\subseteq L_p,qquad
             V\mathbin\triangle B\subseteq R_p.         \tag{3.6}
\]

Equations (3.5), plus Johnson distinctness and the common caps (1.6), are
the smallest literal zero-length host test.  The current source fails its
first equation before the other rows are reached.

## 4. Flat insertion obstruction and the rank-`r` host

There is a construction-independent stutter obstruction.  If an internal
flat source contains consecutive letters `(X,B)` with `B subseteq X`, then at
the owner transition where `B` enters while `X` remains, the new owner is a
subset of the old owner.  Equal rank forces equality, not a Johnson edge.
For the reversed order `(B,X)`, use the transition where `B` exits while
`X` remains.  Hence **no** menu pair `(X_h,B_h^epsilon)` can occur internally
in a flat simple owner path, including the neutral pair `(X_R,X_R)`.  It must
be endpoint-clipped, embedded in a nonflat schedule, or combined with a
simultaneous rethread that changes the surrounding transition.

For completeness, suppose an old source letter `Q_p=X` is refined internally
as `(X,B)`.  The `d` new strict middle windows are

\[
        C_s=\bigcup_{q=p-d+s}^{p+s-1}Q_q,qquad1\le s\le d. \tag{4.1}
\]

Putting `a=p-d+s`, one has

\[
                   C_s\subseteq T_{a-1}\cap T_a,        \tag{4.2}
\]

where `T` is the old rank-`r` Johnson owner path.  Hence

\[
                             |C_s|\le r-1.               \tag{4.3}
\]

This is independent of `B`; an isolated internal split cannot preserve a
flat owner row.

There is an obstruction which does not presuppose an old owner row.  The
menu host

\[
                   X_S=K\cup\{a_1,a_3\}\cup F[1,d+1]    \tag{4.4}
\]

has rank exactly `r`.  If `(X_S,B_S^epsilon)` is internal to a flat
rank-`r` source, each of the `d` windows containing both positions contains
`X_S`; it must therefore equal `X_S`.  For `d>=2` the owner chronology has
repeated vertices.  Thus a resident simple path can use this pair only at a
globally clipped endpoint, in a zero-length replacement satisfying (3.5),
or in an explicitly nonflat schedule.

## 5. Exact surviving gate

The four-block relation and seven-ray menu combine to prove:

* a phase-independent cap at every old and planted address;
* ranks at most `r` for the seven planted host caps (the free old-address
  unions are source caps and need not have owner rank);
* equal source length in the two C8 endpoint states; and
* a fixed seven-boundary state which does not grow under repeated phase
  changes, provided the same slots are reused.

They do not prove, and in the literal four-block owner bank cannot prove:

* zero length relative to the unplanted relation;
* a legal internal flat owner row (the untouched surplus is at least
  `10d+40` under the entire seven-host/seven-contraction local class);
* contraction safety for seven compensating old boundaries; or
* common-cap Hall after such contraction.

The weakest next theorem is therefore two-stage.  First construct a global
quotient weave which removes the central owner/lower multiplicities; the
literal Klein four-block concatenation and every fourteen-support local
variant are closed.  On that new owner factor, prove a **seven-credit
zero-length rethread**: choose seven two-cell replacements satisfying (3.5),
or seven nonlocal contractions whose coarsened-cell equality graph saturates
every protected row, while placing `X_S` at a clipped/nonflat socket.  Without
the first theorem the macro is not physical; without the second, the honest
compiler charge is a reusable fixed `+7`, not zero.

### 5.2 Explicit strongest common rail and its exact failure

For completeness, here is the literal positive row behind that scope
reconciliation.  It uses the two menu caps

\[
 X_L=K\cup\{z,a_1,a_3,f_1\},\qquad
 X_R=K\cup\{z,a_1,a_3,f_d\}.                         \tag{5.1}
\]

Put

\[
 D=K\cup\{z,a_1,a_3\}\cup F[0,d+1],\qquad |D|=r+2. \tag{5.2}
\]

For `d>=4`, order the filler labels by

\[
 (v_0,\ldots,v_{d+1})=
 (f_2,f_{d+1},f_1,f_3,\ldots,f_{d-2},f_d,f_{d-1},f_0) \tag{5.3}
\]

and define

\[
 T_j=D\setminus\{v_j,v_{j+1}\}\qquad(0\le j\le d).  \tag{5.4}
\]

At the two small depths the omitted-pair rows are

\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
d&(D-T_0,\ldots,D-T_d)\\ \hline
2&(f_2f_3,f_1f_2,f_0f_1),\\
3&(f_2f_4,f_1f_2,f_1f_3,f_0f_1).
\end{array}                                             \tag{5.5}
\]

The owners in (5.4)--(5.5) are distinct rank-`r` Johnson rows.  A literal
common source inverse is

\[
 A_0=X_L,\qquad A_{2d}=X_R,                             \tag{5.6}
\]

and, for the order (5.3),

\[
\begin{aligned}
 A_p&=D\setminus\{v_0,\ldots,v_{p+1}\}&& (1\le p\le d),\\
 A_p&=D\setminus\{v_{p-d},\ldots,v_{d+1}\}&& (d\le p\le2d-1).
\end{aligned}                                           \tag{5.7}
\]

The two formulas agree at `p=d`, where `A_d=K+za_1a_3`, and direct union
gives

\[
             \bigcup_{p=j}^{j+d}A_p=T_j
                        \qquad(0\le j\le d).           \tag{5.8}
\]

Every displayed source letter is nonempty.  Each omitted coordinate occurs
on an interval of owner indices, so every positive coordinate run is either
of length at least `d+1` or boundary clipped.  The same row joins both
folded phases.  Thus owner rank, Johnson adjacency, internal residence,
nonzero source reconstruction, and a phase-common cap are all genuinely
soluble for these two planted hosts.

The failure is the literal boundary lower palette.  Write the first and
last omitted pairs of an arbitrary common rail as

\[
       D-T_0=\{f_{d+1},x\},\qquad
       D-T_d=\{f_0,y\}.                                 \tag{5.9}
\]

The two boundary lower colours in one phase are

\[
 D\setminus\{a_1,f_{d+1},x\},\qquad
 D\setminus\{a_3,f_0,y\},                              \tag{5.10}
\]

and in the other phase they are

\[
 D\setminus\{a_3,f_{d+1},x\},\qquad
 D\setminus\{a_1,f_0,y\}.                              \tag{5.11}
\]

Equality of the two multisets forces the cross pairing

\[
                       x=f_0,\qquad y=f_{d+1}.           \tag{5.12}
\]

Then `D-T_0=D-T_d={f_0,f_(d+1)}`, so `T_0=T_d`.  Hence no
**simple common rail** can simultaneously retain the exact two-phase lower
boundary palette.  For the explicit row (5.3), the two directed boundary
differences have size two each.

There is an exact six-edge owner-level companion circuit which cancels this
signed boundary current.  In omitted-pair notation its two phase rows are

\[
\begin{array}{c|cc}
 &\text{left}&\text{right}\\ \hline
-&ah-ax-bx-hx&yt-ay-by-bt,\\
+&bh-bx-ax-hx&yt-by-ay-at.
\end{array}                                             \tag{5.13}
\]

Its complete lower and upper counters agree between phases, but the left
`h`-run and right `t`-run both have internal length two.  More generally,
every separated lower-neutral left halo from `ah` to `hx` has an internal
positive `h`-run, and likewise on the right.  Depth-`d` residence therefore
requires at least `d+2` edges on each side: at least `2d+4` in total.  A
direct subdivision of the two old seams consequently adds at least `2d+2`
positions.  This is a growing charge, not a zero-length realization of the
seven-host menu.

Equations (5.6)--(5.13) sharpen the verdict.  The menu does contain the
correct typed caps and admits a resident common source row, but the direct
simple-common completion is palette-impossible, while the smallest
palette-neutral completion has growing residence charge.  A zero-length
positive result must therefore be a noncommon or nonlocal equal-length
rethread with an occurrence-labelled contraction certificate; it cannot be
obtained by placing the seven local split rows into the literal four-block
word.
