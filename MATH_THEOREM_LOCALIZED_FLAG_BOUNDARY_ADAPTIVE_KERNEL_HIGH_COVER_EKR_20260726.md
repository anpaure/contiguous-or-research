\tag{0.1}

 D=D_1,\qquad
 K=\max_C|e_C^\#|.
\tag{0.2}

Here \(e_C^\#\subseteq{\cal T}_H(C)\) is obtained by the independent

\[
 K\le C_0h\sqrt m.
\tag{0.3}

\[
 I(A)=\bigcap_{S\in A}S,\qquad
 U(A)=\bigcup_{S\in A}S,\qquad
 \operatorname {sp}(A)=|U(A)\setminus I(A)|,
\tag{0.4}

and

\[
 {\cal B}(A)=
 \{T:m-H\le|T|\le m+H,\ I(A)\subseteq T\subseteq U(A)\}.
\tag{0.5}

Then

\[
 |{\cal B}(A)|\le2^{\operatorname {sp}(A)}.
\tag{0.6}

The decisive new estimate is an off-envelope conditional kernel.  For
each fixed \(s\), every link with \(\operatorname {sp}(A)=s\) sees a

\[
 O_s\!\left({1\over m}+{|P|\over m^2}\right)
+\text{an active-disjoint superpolynomial tail}.
\tag{0.7}
\]

The estimate is a **sum of conditional link degrees**, not an ordinary

### Theorem A (weighted physical Hilton--Milner theorem)

If \({\cal F}\) is a pairwise-intersecting family of selected physical
port edges and \(y_C\ge0\), then either \({\cal F}\) is contained in one
target star, or

\[
 \boxed{
 \sum_{C\in{\cal F}}y_C
 \le o(D/\sqrt m)\,\max_{C\in{\cal F}}y_C.}
\tag{0.11}
\]

In particular, every non-star intersecting physical strip-port family
satisfies

\[
 |{\cal F}|=o(D/\sqrt m).
\tag{0.12}
\]

Since a weighted matching on an intersecting support consists of one
edge,

\[
 \nu_y({\cal F})=\max_Cy_C,
\tag{0.13}
\]

so (0.11) is exactly the requested weighted clique inequality.

The proof is local.  Fix \(E\in{\cal F}\).  The ambient family of strips
meeting \(E\) is initially contained in at most \(K\) target stars and
has the crude size at most \(KD\).  At each subsequent node, replace its
literal target label \(A\) by its boundary envelope \({\cal B}(A)\).
If that envelope is not a cover, choose a witness edge avoiding it.
Three conditional contractions reduce \(KD\) to

\[
 KD\,O_s\!\left({1\over m}+{h\over m^{3/2}}\right)^3
o(D/\sqrt m)
=o(D/\sqrt m).
\tag{0.14}
\]

Large-span nodes are already negligible by the audited all-order physical
codegree profile.  Thus the result covers, in particular, every family
with target-cover number at least

\[
 q_*={1\over\widehat\rho_m\sqrt m},
\tag{0.15}
\]

and in fact all non-star families after combining it with the existing
low-cover estimate.

This closes the physical high-cover **EKR/clique** gate for the
strengthened exact-regular port outcome.  It does not prove the full
fractional edge-colouring inequality for arbitrary non-clique weighted
supports.

## 1. Physical strips and the inputs retained

A raw strip has a core \(K_C\), an excluded core \(L_C\), both of size
\(m-h\), and a cyclic order on the remaining \(2h\) active points.  At
rank \(m+a\), \(|a|\le H\), its targets are

\[
 K_C\cup I_z(t,h+a),\qquad t\in{\mathbb Z}_{2h}.
\tag{1.1}
\]

The raw degree of a rank-\((m+a)\) target is

\[
 D_a={(m+a)!(m-a)!\over2(m-h)!^2}.
\tag{1.2}
\]

At every nonmiddle target of signed depth \(q\), independently choose a
uniform \(D\)-subset of its \(D_q\) raw incident strips.  Thus

\[
 \theta_q={D\over D_q}={N_q\over N_1},
\tag{1.3}
\]

and exact nonmiddle degree \(D\) is automatic.  Middle incidences are
retained.

We use two already audited consequences of this same experiment.

First, some outcome has the width (0.3).  Second, for every nonempty
co-occurring target set \(A\),

\[
 d^\#(A)
 \le
 2D\alpha^{\operatorname {sp}(A)}+\Lambda,
\qquad
 \alpha={h+H\over m-H},
\tag{1.4}
\]

where

\[
 d^\#(A)=|\{C:A\subseteq e_C^\#\}|,
\qquad
 \Lambda=C_1(\log M+hH),
\tag{1.5}
\]

and \(M\) is the number of raw physical strips.  Equation (1.4) is the
span half of the all-order selected-codegree theorem.  The new
conditional events proved below can be imposed simultaneously with
(0.3) and (1.4).

## 2. The exact raw off-envelope kernel

For an integer \(s\ge0\), put

\[
 M_s=m-H-s,\qquad
 R=h+H,\qquad
 g_s=h-H-s.
\tag{2.1}
\]

Assume \(g_s\ge1\), \(R<M_s\), and

\[
 3H+s<h.
\tag{2.1a}
\]

For \(K'\ge1\), define

\[
\begin{aligned}
 \eta_s(K')={}&
 2^{s+1}\sum_{r=1}^{R}
 { (r+1)(r+s+1)\over\binom{M_s}{r}}\\
 &+{2K'\over M_s^2}
 +2hK'\left({R\over M_s}\right)^{2g_s}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2.2}
\]

For fixed \(s\), \(h=o(m)\), and \(K'=O(h\sqrt m)\),

\[
 \eta_s(K')
 =
 O_s\!\left({1\over m}+{K'\over m^2}\right)
+2hK'\left({h+H\over m-H-s}\right)^{2(h-H-s)}.
\tag{2.3}
\]

The last term is superpolynomially small under (0.1).

### Lemma 2.1 (raw conditional flag-boundary kernel)

Let \(A\) be a nonempty feasible target set with
\(\operatorname {sp}(A)=s\).  Let \(P\) be at most \(K'\) targets
belonging to one raw physical strip \(E\), and assume

\[
 P\cap{\cal B}(A)=\varnothing.
\tag{2.4}
\]

Then

\[
 \boxed{
 \sum_{T\in P}{d_0(A\cup\{T\})\over d_0(A)}
 \le\eta_s(K'),}
\tag{2.5}
\]

where

\[
 d_0(A)=|\{C:A\subseteq{\cal T}_H(C)\}|.
\tag{2.6}
\]

#### Proof

Write

\[
 I=I(A),\qquad U=U(A),\qquad Q=U\setminus I,
\qquad |Q|=s.
\tag{2.7}
\]

The group

\[
 G=\operatorname {Sym}(I)\times
   \operatorname {Sym}([2m]\setminus U)
\tag{2.8}
\]

fixes \(Q\) pointwise and fixes every member of \(A\).  For a target
\(T\notin{\cal B}(A)\), set

\[
 a=|I\setminus T|,\qquad
 b=|T\setminus U|,\qquad
 P_0=T\cap Q.
\tag{2.9}
\]

At least one of \(a,b\) is positive.  Its \(G\)-orbit has size

\[
 \binom{|I|}{a}\binom{2m-|U|}{b}.
\tag{2.10}
\]

The family of raw strips containing \(A\) is \(G\)-invariant.  Double
count pairs \((C,T')\) with

\[
 A\subseteq{\cal T}_H(C),\qquad
 T'\in G\cdot T,\qquad T'\in{\cal T}_H(C).
\]

Every member of \(G\cdot T\) has the same \(A\)-conditional degree, so

\[
 {d_0(A,T)\over d_0(A)}
 =
 {\frac1{d_0(A)}
   \sum_{C:A\subseteq{\cal T}_H(C)}
       |(G\cdot T)\cap{\cal T}_H(C)|
  \over
  \binom{|I|}{a}\binom{2m-|U|}{b}}.
\tag{2.11}
\]

Choose \(S\in A\).  Relative to \(S\), every target in the orbit has
set-difference parameters

\[
 i=a+p,\qquad j=b+q,\qquad 0\le p,q\le s.
\tag{2.12}
\]

The cyclic pair lemma bounds the numerator inside (2.11) as follows:

* in a comparable case it is at most the rank gap plus one;
* if both differences are positive and the active intervals overlap, it
  is at most \(2\);
* in the active-disjoint case it is at most \(2h\).

Also

\[
 |I|,\ 2m-|U|\ge M_s.
\tag{2.13}
\]

We now sum over the actual packet \(P\).

Suppose first \(a=0,b=r\ge1\), and fix \(P_0\subseteq Q\).  Put

\[
 R_0=I\cup P_0.
\tag{2.14}
\]

Every relevant target of \(E\) is a rank-fixed superset of \(R_0\)
obtained by adding \(r\) points outside \(U\).  Since

\[
 |R_0|\ge|I|\ge m-H-s>m-h=|K_E|,
\tag{2.15}
\]

\(R_0\setminus K_E\) is nonempty.  In the active cycle of \(E\), a
target interval must contain this fixed nonempty set with at most \(r\)
slack positions.  More explicitly,

\[
 r=|T|-|I|-|P_0|
 \le(m+H)-(m-H-s)=2H+s.
\]

The band ranks and (2.1a) therefore give

\[
 r\le2H+s<h-H.
\tag{2.15a}
\]

Equivalently, the complementary interval has length greater than the
slack.  Decomposing the complement of the fixed set into cyclic runs
therefore shows that a prescribed-length cyclic interval can contain it
in at most \(r+1\) placements.  Hence there are at most \(r+1\) such
targets in \(P\).  Moreover, relative to the fixed \(S\in A\), the
opposite difference is at most \(s<h-H\).  Thus this one-sided case
cannot be active-disjoint, and its cyclic numerator is at most
\(r+s+1\).  The complementary case is identical.
There are at most \(2^s\) choices of \(P_0\), while (2.12) bounds the
per-target numerator by \(r+s+1\).  This gives

\[
 2^s{(r+1)(r+s+1)\over\binom{M_s}{r}}.
\tag{2.16}
\]

Complementation gives the same bound when \(b=0,a=r\).  Summing (2.16)
over \(1\le r\le R\) gives the first line of (2.2).

If \(a,b\ge1\) and the active intervals overlap, (2.11)--(2.13) give at
most \(2/M_s^2\) per target.  There are at most \(K'\) targets, giving
the second term of (2.2).

Finally suppose the active intervals are disjoint.  Then (2.12) and the
cyclic geometry give

\[
 a,b\ge h-H-s=g_s.
\tag{2.17}
\]

Because two band targets of one strip share its core,

\[
 a,b\le h+H=R.
\tag{2.18}
\]

Thus

\[
 \binom{M_s}{a}\ge(M_s/R)^a,\qquad
 \binom{M_s}{b}\ge(M_s/R)^b.
\tag{2.19}
\]

Equations (2.11), (2.17)--(2.19) give at most

\[
 2h(R/M_s)^{2g_s}
\]

per target.  Multiplication by \(K'\) proves (2.5).  The estimate
(2.3) follows because the binomial sum is dominated by its
\(r=1\) term when \(R=o(M_s)\). \(\square\)

## 3. Simultaneous selected conditional kernels

Let \(X_{C,T}\) be the indicator that the incidence of target \(T\) with
raw strip \(C\) is selected.  For a fixed feasible target set \(A\), put

\[
 \pi(A)=\prod_{\substack{T\in A\\|T|\ne m}}\theta_{||T|-m|},
\qquad
 \mu(A)=\pi(A)d_0(A)=\mathbb E d^\#(A).
\tag{3.1}
\]

For \(T\notin A\),

\[
 \mathbb E d^\#(A,T)
 =\pi(A)\theta_{||T|-m|}d_0(A,T)
 \le\pi(A)d_0(A,T),
\tag{3.2}
\]

where the factor \(\theta\) is interpreted as \(1\) at the middle rank.

### Lemma 3.1 (large minimum bounded-span links)

For every fixed \(s_*\) and every feasible \(A\) with

\[
 1\le|A|\le4,\qquad\operatorname {sp}(A)\le s_*,
\tag{3.3}
\]

one has

\[
 d_0(A)\ge
 { (m-H-s_*)!^2\over8h(m-h)!^2}.
\tag{3.4}
\]

#### Proof

Choose a raw strip \(C_0\) containing \(A\), and use the group \(G\) in
(2.8).  Its orbit stays inside the link of \(A\).  The stabilizer of
\(C_0\) in \(G\) has size at most \(4h(m-h)!^2\): after arbitrary
permutations inside the two \((m-h)\)-cores, its action on the active
cycle is one of at most \(4h\) dihedral symmetries.  Hence

\[
 |G\cdot C_0|
 \ge
 {|I(A)|!\,(2m-|U(A)|)!\over
       4h(m-h)!^2}.
\tag{3.5}
\]

Both factorials in the numerator are at least
\((m-H-s_*)!\).  The harmless extra factor \(2\) in (3.4) covers either
catalogue convention for unoriented cycles. \(\square\)

The right side of (3.4) is

\[
 \exp[\Omega((h-H-s_*)\log(m/h))].
\tag{3.6}
\]

At the audited Gaussian scales, multiplication by the bounded number of
retention factors in (3.1) still dominates \(\log M+hH\).  More
explicitly, the only concentration hypothesis used below is

\[
 \theta_*^4
 { (m-H-s_*)!^2\over8h(m-h)!^2}
 \gg \log M+hH,
\qquad
 \theta_*=\min_{1\le q\le H}\theta_q.
\tag{3.7}
\]

### Theorem 3.2 (selected localized adaptive kernel)

Fix an integer \(s_*\).  Under (3.7), the independent exact targetwise
port outcome can be chosen simultaneously with (0.3), (1.4), and the
following assertion.

For every feasible \(A\) with

\[
 1\le|A|\le3,\qquad
 s=\operatorname {sp}(A)\le s_*,
\tag{3.8}
\]

and every physical strip \(E\) satisfying

\[
 e_E^\#\cap{\cal B}(A)=\varnothing,
\tag{3.9}
\]

one has

\[
 \boxed{
 \sum_{T\in e_E^\#}d^\#(A\cup\{T\})
 \le4\eta_s(K)d^\#(A)+K\Lambda.}
\tag{3.10}
\]

#### Proof

For every fixed \(A\) in (3.3), the variables

\[
 Z_C(A)=\prod_{T\in A}X_{C,T}
\tag{3.11}
\]

over candidate strips \(C\) are negatively associated.  Indeed,
fixed-size sampling in one target star is negatively associated,
different target stars are independent, and the products in (3.11) are
increasing functions of disjoint coordinate blocks for distinct
strips.  Chernoff's lower tail, Lemma 3.1, and (3.7) therefore give,
simultaneously,

\[
 d^\#(A)\ge\frac12\mu(A).
\tag{3.12}
\]

The identical upper-tail argument, now for each feasible pair
\((A,T)\) with \(|A|\le3\) and \(T\notin A\), gives

\[
 d^\#(A,T)\le2\mathbb E d^\#(A,T)+\Lambda.
\tag{3.13}
\]

There are at most

\[
 4M\sum_{j=1}^{4}\binom{2h(2H+1)}j
\tag{3.14}
\]

feasible labelled target sets to check.  Its logarithm is
\(O(\log M+\log(hH))\), so the constant in \(\Lambda\) may be chosen
to make the union bound summable.  The same experiment's width,
all-order profile, pair, and off-edge-star failures have already
summable probabilities; intersecting the good events preserves positive
probability.

Apply (3.13) to the realized packet

\[
 P=e_E^\#.
\]

No conditional distribution of \(P\) is invoked: inequalities
(3.12)--(3.13) were fixed simultaneously for every feasible labelled
target set before the realized subset \(e_E^\#\) was inserted.
It has size at most \(K\), lies in one physical strip, and is disjoint
from \({\cal B}(A)\).  Equations (3.2), Lemma 2.1, and (3.12) give

\[
\begin{aligned}
 \sum_{T\in P}d^\#(A,T)
 &\le
 2\pi(A)\sum_{T\in P}d_0(A,T)+K\Lambda\\
 &\le2\eta_s(K)\mu(A)+K\Lambda\\
 &\le4\eta_s(K)d^\#(A)+K\Lambda.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3.15}
\]

This is (3.10). \(\square\)

The use of the sum in (3.10) is deliberate.  An ordinary off-edge-star
bound controls a union of links and cannot be iterated after different
branches choose different witnesses.  Equation (3.10) controls the sum
of the full child-link capacities, which is exactly what the adaptive
tree requires.

## 4. The four-level localized boundary tree

Let \({\cal F}\) be pairwise intersecting.  Its target-cover number is

\[
 \tau({\cal F})
 =\min\{|X|:X\cap e_C^\#\ne\varnothing
               \text{ for every }C\in{\cal F}\}.
\tag{4.1}
\]

Put

\[
 B={D\over mK^4},
\qquad
 s_0=
 \left\lceil
 {\log(4mK^4)\over\log(1/\alpha)}
 \right\rceil,
\tag{4.2}
\]

and assume

\[
 \Lambda\le B/2.
\tag{4.3}
\]

If \(d^\#(A)>B\), then (1.4) implies

\[
 \operatorname {sp}(A)\le s_0.
\tag{4.4}
\]

Indeed,

\[
 {B\over2}<2D\alpha^{\operatorname {sp}(A)},
\]

so

\[
 \alpha^{\operatorname {sp}(A)}
 >{1\over4mK^4}.
\]

Define

\[
 \gamma=4\max_{0\le s\le s_0}\eta_s(K).
\tag{4.5}
\]

### Theorem 4.1 (high-cover weighted containment)

Suppose

\[
 \tau({\cal F})>2^{s_0}.
\tag{4.6}
\]

For every nonnegative weight vector \(y\) supported on \({\cal F}\),

\[
\boxed{
 \sum_{C\in{\cal F}}y_C
 \le
 \left[
 KD\gamma^3+{3D\over mK}+3K^4\Lambda
 \right]\max_{C\in{\cal F}}y_C,}
\tag{4.7}
\]

provided \(\gamma\le1\).

#### Proof

Fix \(E_\varnothing\in{\cal F}\).  Every member of \({\cal F}\) meets
\(E_\varnothing\).  Partition \({\cal F}\) by choosing one literal
collision target in that intersection.  The resulting depth-one nodes
are singleton target labels \(A=\{T\}\), and their total full-link
capacity is at most

\[
 S_1\le|e_{E_\varnothing}^\#|D\le KD.
\tag{4.8}
\]

At a node labelled by \(A\), stop if

\[
 d^\#(A)\le B.
\tag{4.9}
\]

Otherwise (4.4) gives \(\operatorname {sp}(A)\le s_0\), and (0.6)
gives

\[
 |{\cal B}(A)|\le2^{s_0}<\tau({\cal F}).
\tag{4.10}
\]

Consequently \({\cal B}(A)\) is not a target cover of \({\cal F}\).
Choose

\[
 E_A\in{\cal F},\qquad
 e_{E_A}^\#\cap{\cal B}(A)=\varnothing.
\tag{4.11}
\]

Every edge in the current branch intersects \(E_A\), because the
original family is pairwise intersecting.  Partition the branch by one
chosen target \(T\) in this intersection and pass to the child label

\[
 A\cup\{T\}.
\tag{4.12}
\]

Run this expansion at active nodes of depths one, two, and three, and
terminate every node at depth four.  Since labels at active nodes have
size at most three, Theorem 3.2 applies.  If \(S_r\) denotes the sum of
full-link capacities of active descendants before stopping, then

\[
\begin{aligned}
 S_2&\le\gamma KD+K^2\Lambda,\\
 S_3&\le\gamma^2KD+\gamma K^2\Lambda+K^3\Lambda,\\
 S_4&\le\gamma^3KD+
       \gamma^2K^2\Lambda+\gamma K^3\Lambda+K^4\Lambda.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4.13}
\]

Here the number of nodes at depth \(r\) is at most \(K^r\).

Stopped nodes occur only at depths one, two, and three.  Their total
full-link capacity is at most

\[
 B(K+K^2+K^3)\le{3D\over mK}.
\tag{4.14}
\]

The terminal branch classes partition \({\cal F}\).  If

\[
 \mu=\max_{C\in{\cal F}}y_C,
\tag{4.15}
\]

then a class labelled by \(A\) has weight at most

\[
 \mu\,d^\#(A).
\tag{4.16}
\]

Sum (4.16) over all stopped and depth-four nodes.  Equations
(4.13)--(4.14), together with \(\gamma\le1\), give (4.7). \(\square\)

This proves the requested localized containment statement: the initial
ambient restriction to strips meeting one \(E\) costs \(KD\), Boolean
boundary compression prevents rectangle inflation, and three genuinely
new off-envelope collisions supply the cubic kernel gain.

## 5. The \(o(D/\sqrt m)\) ledger

Assume (0.1), (0.3), and (0.8).  Because

\[
 \alpha={h+H\over m-H}=(1+o(1)){h\over m},
\qquad
 K=O(h\sqrt m),
\tag{5.1}
\]

the integer \(s_0\) in (4.2) is bounded.  Uniformly when
\(h=o(m^{7/8})\), one may take

\[
 s_0\le64
\tag{5.2}
\]

for all sufficiently large \(m\).  Hence Theorem 3.2 is needed only for
one fixed finite family of bounded-span events.

For \(0\le s\le64\), (2.3) gives uniformly

\[
 \eta_s(K)
 =
 O\!\left({1\over m}+{h\over m^{3/2}}\right)
+o(m^{-A})
\tag{5.3}
\]

for every fixed \(A\).  Therefore

\[
\begin{aligned}
 K\gamma^3
 &=
 O\!\left[
 h\sqrt m
 \left({1\over m}+{h\over m^{3/2}}\right)^3
 \right]\\
 &=
 O\!\left((h/m)^4\right)+o(m^{-1/2})
 =o(m^{-1/2}).
\end{aligned}
\tag{5.4}
\]

The last equality is exactly where \(h=o(m^{7/8})\) is used.
Furthermore,

\[
 {1\over mK}=o(m^{-1/2}),
\tag{5.5}
\]

and

\[
 K^4\Lambda=o(D/\sqrt m),
\tag{5.6}
\]

because \(K,\Lambda\) are polynomial/subexponential on the audited
scales, while

\[
 \log D=(2h+o(h))\log m.
\tag{5.7}
\]

Equations (4.7) and (5.4)--(5.6) prove

\[
 \tau({\cal F})>2^{s_0}
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \sum_{C\in{\cal F}}y_C
 =o(D/\sqrt m)\max_Cy_C.
\tag{5.8}
\]

For reference, at the default scale,

\[
 K=m^{7/6+o(1)},\qquad
 \eta_s(K)=m^{-5/6+o(1)},\qquad
 K\eta_s(K)^3=m^{-4/3+o(1)},
\tag{5.9}
\]

## 7. Why the rectangle obstruction disappears, rather than being ignored

For a crossing interval grid, the many interior targets lie between the
common intersection and union of its boundary flags.  Hence they lie in
the Boolean envelope (0.5).  Ordinary cardinality spread incorrectly
charges each interior target as a new independent condition.  The present
tree instead asks for a witness strip disjoint from the whole envelope.
Every collision with that witness leaves the existing interval grid and
therefore pays at least one genuine external orbit coordinate in
Lemma 2.1.

This observation has two exact consequences.

1. The deterministic \(r\times r\) grid responsible for
   \(\kappa=1+o(1)\) in ordinary spread is a local link cluster, not a
   high-cover weighted obstruction.
2. A sequence of overlapping grids cannot be recycled at zero cost in
   the adaptive tree: whenever its bounded-span link remains large, its
   envelope has constant size and is avoided by the next witness; whenever
   its span becomes large, (1.4) stops it at cost at most \(B\).

Thus the proof is flag-boundary compressed but still uses literal target
collisions.  No quotient target or fictitious rectangle atom is inserted.
