# Native MSW inverse pairs are not a q1 Markov basis

## Status

The inverse-pair move on two canonical MSW wreath rows is exact on the two
central shores and has the four-term immediate-upper current

\[
 [\overline{Y^-+a}]+[\overline{Y^++d}]
 -[\overline{Y^-+d}]-[\overline{Y^++a}].
\tag{0.1}
\]

Across arbitrary coordinate conjugates, currents of this form generate the
entire coordinate-balanced lattice.  This note proves that the quantifier
cannot be changed to “inside one fixed canonical MSW factor”.

For every `m>=4`, even the **entire possible catalogue of inverse
separated-double-swap pairs in one fixed MSW factor** has rational span of
dimension strictly smaller than the coordinate-balanced current space.
Its orthogonal complement therefore contains exponentially many additional
directions beyond total and coordinate marginals.

For the proved all-parameter block-replacement family

\[
                 u1100v\longleftrightarrow u1010v,
\tag{0.2}
\]

the conclusion is stronger: more than a positive fraction of all q1
targets occur in none of its four-term currents.  Evaluation at any such
target is an explicit extra invariant of that proved move family.

Consequently the uniform fractional q1 solution plus the native inverse
pairs does not yield an integral circulation or Markov-basis theorem.
Longer functional cycles, overlapping serial moves, or a different exact
factor are mathematically necessary for arbitrary defect routing.

No computation or search is used.

## 1. The balanced current space

Put

\[
 N=2m+1,
 \qquad k=m-1,
 \qquad L=\binom Nk,
 \qquad C_m={1\over m+1}\binom{2m}{m}.
\tag{1.1}
\]

Let `V=Q^(binom([N],k))`, and define the coordinate-incidence map

\[
 A:V\longrightarrow\mathbb Q^N,
 \qquad
 (Az)_i=\sum_{S\ni i}z_S.
\tag{1.2}
\]

Every lower form of (0.1) is a square

\[
 [Z+r+a]+[Z+s+d]-[Z+r+d]-[Z+s+a]
\tag{1.3}
\]

and hence belongs to `ker A`.  Complementation identifies the same space
with the immediate-upper currents.

### Lemma 1.1 (exact dimension)

For `1<=k<=N-1`, the map `A` has rank `N`.  Therefore

\[
                    \dim_{\mathbb Q}\ker A=L-N.
\tag{1.4}
\]

#### Proof

Suppose `alpha in Q^N` gives a row relation, so

\[
                         \sum_{i\in S}\alpha_i=0
\tag{1.5}
\]

for every `k`-set `S`.  Comparing two `k`-sets which differ by exchanging
`i` and `j` gives `alpha_i=alpha_j`.  All coordinates of `alpha` are equal,
and (1.5) then gives `k alpha_i=0`.  Thus the rows are independent.
\(\square\)

The annihilator generated by the coordinate marginals is `im A^T`, of
dimension `N`.  The total-sum functional is already in this space, since
the sum of all coordinate marginals is `k` times the total.

## 2. A universal count on inverse-pair atoms

The canonical MSW factor has exactly `C_m` shortest wreath rows.  A row has
exactly `N` cyclic starts for a separated-double-swap atom.  The removed
triple determines its added triple, so these atoms form a partial functional
digraph.  An inverse-pair move is a directed `2`-cycle in this graph.

### Lemma 2.1 (catalogue bound)

The number `I_m` of distinct inverse-pair moves in one fixed MSW factor
satisfies

\[
                         I_m\le\left\lfloor{NC_m\over2}\right\rfloor.
\tag{2.1}
\]

#### Proof

There are `NC_m` row-start atoms.  In a functional digraph, distinct
directed `2`-cycles are vertex-disjoint.  Every inverse pair consumes two
atoms.  \(\square\)

This bound includes every inverse separated-double-swap pair, not merely
the currently classified `1100/1010` subfamily.

The exact relation between the target count and the Catalan count is

\[
                 L={mN\over m+2}C_m.
\tag{2.2}
\]

### Theorem 2.2 (fixed-factor span deficiency)

Let `S_m` be the rational span of all inverse-pair q1 currents available
inside one fixed canonical MSW factor.  For every `m>=4`,

\[
 \dim S_m
 \le {NC_m\over2}
 < L-N
 =\dim\ker A.
\tag{2.3}
\]

More precisely,

\[
 \dim S_m^\perp-\dim\operatorname{im}A^T
 \ge
 N\left({C_m(m-2)\over2(m+2)}-1\right)>0.
\tag{2.4}
\]

Thus there are non-coordinate linear invariants for the complete native
inverse-pair catalogue.

#### Proof

One current contributes at most one dimension, so Lemma 2.1 gives the first
inequality in (2.3).  Using (2.2),

\[
\begin{aligned}
 L-N-{NC_m\over2}
 &=N\left({mC_m\over m+2}-{C_m\over2}-1\right)\\
 &=N\left({C_m(m-2)\over2(m+2)}-1\right).
\end{aligned}
\tag{2.5}
\]

At `m=4` the parenthesis is `14/6-1>0`; it then increases with `m`.
This proves (2.3).  Since

\[
                 \dim S_m^\perp=L-\dim S_m
\]

and the coordinate-potential space `im A^T` has dimension `N`, (2.4)
follows.  \(\square\)

Asymptotically, `NC_m/2=(1/2+o(1))L`.  Hence the failure is not a
codimension-one parity wrinkle: at least `(1/2-o(1))L` independent dual
directions remain after the coordinate marginals are removed.

For `m=3`, the universal count (2.1) alone is inconclusive.  This finite
dimension is already outside the asymptotic use of the theorem and does not
affect the all-dimensional no-go.

## 3. The proved block family has untouched targets

The explicit canonical block-replacement supply has

\[
                         J_m=(2m-1)C_{m-2}
\tag{3.1}
\]

inverse pairs.  Each current has support on at most four rank-`k` targets.
Therefore the union of all target supports has order at most `4J_m`.

The Catalan recurrence gives

\[
 {C_m\over C_{m-2}}
 ={4(2m-1)(2m-3)\over m(m+1)}.
\tag{3.2}
\]

### Theorem 3.1 (literal untouched-target invariant)

For every `m>=3`,

\[
                         4J_m<L.
\tag{3.3}
\]

Consequently there is a rank-`(m-1)` target `S_*` which appears in none of
the currents from the proved family (0.2).  The coefficient functional

\[
                             z\longmapsto z_{S_*}
\tag{3.4}
\]

is invariant under every integer or rational combination of those moves.
It is not a linear combination of the coordinate marginals.

#### Proof

Equations (2.2), (3.1), and (3.2) reduce (3.3) to

\[
       (m+1)(m+2)<(2m-3)(2m+1),
\tag{3.5}
\]

or `3m^2-7m-5>0`, which holds for `m>=3`.  Since each current has four
terms, at least `L-4J_m` targets are untouched, proving (3.4).

It remains to exclude a coordinate-potential representation of the unit
functional at `S_*`.  If numbers `alpha_i` satisfied

\[
        \sum_{i\in S}\alpha_i=1_{S=S_*}
\tag{3.6}
\]

for every `k`-set `S`, compare pairs of sets different from `S_*` which
exchange any prescribed coordinates `i,j`; because `2<=k<=N-2`, an
auxiliary common `(k-1)`-set can be chosen avoiding `S_*` in both
comparisons.  This forces all `alpha_i` equal.  Then the left side of
(3.6) is constant on all `k`-sets, a contradiction.  Thus (3.4) is an
extra invariant.  \(\square\)

In fact

\[
 {4J_m\over L}
 ={(m+1)(m+2)\over(2m-3)(2m+1)}
 ={1\over4}+O(m^{-1}),
\tag{3.7}
\]

so at least `(3/4-o(1))L` target coordinates are untouched by the proved
family.

## 4. Why coordinate conjugation does not repair the quantifier

Every abstract square (1.3) is realized by the inverse-pair move in **some
coordinate conjugate** of the canonical MSW factor.  It follows correctly
that the union of the conjugate catalogues generates `ker_Z A`.

But a coordinate conjugate replaces the complete factor.  Two generators
realized in two different conjugates are not automatically serial moves of
one incumbent factor, and their old row pairs need not coexist.  Theorems
2.2--3.1 prove that this is a load-bearing distinction:

\[
 \boxed{
 \text{orbitwise abstract generation}
 \ \not\Rightarrow\ 
 \text{fixed-factor Markov generation}.}
\tag{4.1}
\]

The same distinction prevents using the exact uniform fractional q1 point
as an integrality proof.  Fractional feasibility determines the correct
coordinate marginals, but rounding within one factor requires a path in
the fixed-factor move graph.  Native inverse pairs leave a large quotient
of that balanced lattice immobile.

## 5. Consequence for the immediate-upper programme

The result rules out only one proposed actuator atlas.  It does not show
that a q1-exact or `O(1)`-defect owner factor is impossible.  The shortest
remaining algebraic possibilities are:

1. use longer directed cycles in the complete separated-swap functional
   graph, whose currents are not sums of disjoint inverse-pair generators;
2. prove a regenerating sequence in which a move creates new inverse pairs,
   so the union of the time-dependent spans is larger than the time-zero
   span;
3. change to a noncanonical exact wreath factor with a genuinely spanning
   rectangle atlas; or
4. solve the fixed-first-factor three-resource coloured matching problem
   directly, without local Markov generation.

Any positive proof must additionally retain last-provider safety.  Even a
full signed lattice theorem would not by itself produce a positive path
through exact factors.
