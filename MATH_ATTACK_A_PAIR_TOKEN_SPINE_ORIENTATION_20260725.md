# Pair-omission token spines: sharp run count and the exact orientation loss

Date: 2026-07-25

Method: pure mathematics only.  No search, solver, or finite computation is
used.

## 0. Outcome

Put

\[
 n=2m+1,\qquad W=\binom{n}{m},\qquad
 A_m=\binom{2m-1}{m-1},\qquad
 N_q=\binom{n}{m-q}\quad(q\ge0).
\]

There are three positive conclusions.

First, the local exact factors in the first-avoided-pair construction can
be relabelled so that its selected starts form only

\[
 \boxed{J=O(W/m)}
\]

cyclic runs.  This removes the two logarithms in the previous worst-case
bound.  The proof counts actual selected/unselected boundaries after a
random relabelling; no random factor theorem is assumed.

Second, at one corridor transition a source run is a path whose edges are
the current interval occurrences and whose vertices are their two physical
endpoint children.  Orienting every edge of a run uniformly left or
uniformly right keeps one contiguous run and covers every carrier vertex
except one endpoint.  Thus the one-step tree/endpoint correction is
\(O(J)=O(W/m)\).  Iterating uniform choices gives one literal,
row-coherent Pascal routing, and the sum of its *incremental losses relative
to the carriers generated by the preceding choices* is

\[
 O(HJ)=O(HW/m)=o(W),\qquad H=o(m).
\]

After division by the exact Gaussian capacities, the combined cumulative
endpoint loss over *every* signed window is in fact
\(O(J\sqrt m)=O(W/\sqrt m)=o(W)\).

This does not yet bound loss relative to the full depth-\(q\) corridors.
The missing boundary strips accumulated from previous choices can total
\(\Theta(H^2J)\), which need not be \(o(W)\) at Gaussian depth.  Thus the
token spine closes the one-step run/tree correction, but a common collar or
multidepth transport theorem is still required.

More exactly, a row-monotone orientation of a source path deletes one
arbitrarily prescribed carrier occurrence and creates no within-row
duplicate.  Consequently the least residual upper-capacity excess at one
transition is the deficiency of one explicit bipartite deletion matching;
Theorem 3.1B gives its exact cut formula.  This isolates cross-row target
load as the only possible transition loss after the token spine has made
the number of source paths \(O(W/m)\).

Such a collar is available at the literal-support level.  Spending one
additional length-\(H\) prefix per run, for total extra cost
\(HJ=o(W)\), exposes every lower and upper interval in every selected
owner's full Pascal corridor.  This removes the accumulated boundary-strip
loss without selecting an abstract nested matching.  What remains is the
global support/Hall problem inside the union of those full physical
corridors.

There is also a sharp conditional first-transition theorem.  If one local
exact factor has \(M\) missing first-shadow interval targets, relabelled
copies can be used in all phases so that the complete first-upper Hall loss
is \(O(M+W/m)\).  Together with the phase-one lower bound below, this
characterizes the optimized first transition up to an absolute constant.

It does not close bulk cyclic support.  In phase 1 all rows are complete
cycles, so every two-parent orientation has exactly the same available
upper support.  If \(h_1(F_1)\) is the number of missing local
rank-\((m-2)\) interval targets, then the first-upper Hall deficiency is
exactly

\[
 \boxed{
 \delta_{1,+}=\frac{W}{2m+1}+h_1(F_1).}
\]

Hence an \(O(W/m)\) first-transition theorem is equivalent already in
phase 1 to the unproved near-rainbow condition
\(h_1(F_1)=O(W/m)\).  Endpoint orientations cannot supply this condition.

## 1. Setup

Partition \(2m\) coordinates into ordered disjoint pairs
\(P_1,\ldots,P_m\), leaving one coordinate unpaired, and put

\[
 Q_j=[n]\setminus P_j,\qquad L=|Q_j|=2m-1.
\]

For every \(j\), take an exact central wreath factor \(F_j\) on \(Q_j\).
In one of its cyclic rows \(\pi\), write

\[
 S_i=I_\pi(i,m-1),\qquad X_i=I_\pi(i,m).
\]

The first-avoided-pair spine selects start \(i\) precisely when

\[
 S_i\cap P_h\ne\varnothing\quad(h<j).
\tag{1.1}
\]

Every rank-\((m-1)\) set is selected exactly once and the selected middle
owners are distinct.  Let \(J_j\) be the total number of selected cyclic
runs in phase \(j\), and \(J=\sum_jJ_j\).

## 2. A sharp \(O(W/m)\) run theorem

We first record the sampling inequality used twice below.

### Lemma 2.1 (negative association for disjoint pair meetings)

Let \(R\) be a uniform \(k\)-subset of an \(N\)-set and let
\(C_1,\ldots,C_s\) be disjoint two-sets.  Then

\[
 \Pr(R\cap C_a\ne\varnothing\text{ for every }a)
 \le p_{N,k}^{s},
 \qquad
 p_{N,k}=1-\frac{(N-k)(N-k-1)}{N(N-1)}.
\tag{2.1}
\]

#### Proof

Let \(D\) be the union of some already exposed pairs and put
\(X=|R\cap D|\).  Conditional on \(X=x\), the probability \(f(x)\) that
all pairs in \(D\) are met is nondecreasing in \(x\): couple a uniform
\(x\)-subset of \(D\) to a uniform \((x+1)\)-subset by adding one random
unused point.  For a new pair disjoint from \(D\), its conditional meeting
probability \(g(x)\) is nonincreasing in \(x\), because only \(k-x\)
points remain to be chosen outside \(D\).

Conditional on \(X\), the choices inside and outside \(D\) are independent
uniform subsets of their prescribed sizes.  Hence the joint conditional
probability is \(f(X)g(X)\).
For oppositely monotone functions of one random variable,
\(\mathbb E[f(X)g(X)]\le\mathbb Ef(X)\,\mathbb Eg(X)\).  Thus meeting all
previous pairs is negatively correlated with meeting the next pair.
Iterating this inequality, and using the displayed exact marginal meeting
probability, proves (2.1). \(\square\)

### Theorem 2.2 (sharp first-avoided run extraction)

The exact local factors may be chosen, merely by independently relabelling
arbitrary exact factors on the fixed sets \(Q_j\), so that

\[
 \boxed{J=O(A_m/m)=O(W/m).}
\tag{2.3}
\]

Consequently, uniformly for \(H=o(m)\), their literal initialization cost
is

\[
 (2H+1)J=o(W).
\tag{2.4}
\]

#### Proof

For phase \(j\), apply a uniform random coordinate permutation of \(Q_j\)
to an arbitrary exact factor.  Write \(x_i\in\{0,1\}\) for the selection
word on one cyclic row.  Its cyclic run count satisfies

\[
 J(x)\le \frac1L\sum_i x_i+\sum_i(1-x_{i-1})x_i.
\tag{2.5}
\]

For a nonfull word the second term is exactly its run count; for the full
word the first term equals one.  Summing the first terms over all rows and
phases gives

\[
 \frac1L\sum_jN_j=\frac{N_1}{2m-1}=O(W/m),
\tag{2.6}
\]

because every rank-\((m-1)\) target is selected once in total.

It remains to count the directed boundaries in the second term of (2.5).
Under the random relabelling, a fixed row edge
\(S_{i-1}\to S_i\) is uniform among ordered adjacent Johnson pairs.
Conditional on \(S_i\), its entering element
\(u=S_i\setminus S_{i-1}\) is uniform in \(S_i\).

Put \(r=j-1\).  If \(x_i=1\) but \(x_{i-1}=0\), then for some earlier
pair \(P_h\), the unique point of \(S_i\cap P_h\) is \(u\).  Conditional
on \(S_i\) meeting all \(r\) earlier pairs, a union bound therefore gives

\[
 \Pr(x_{i-1}=0\mid x_i=1)\le\frac r{m-1}.
\tag{2.7}
\]

For a uniform \((m-1)\)-subset of the \(L=2m-1\) coordinates,
Lemma 2.1 gives

\[
 \Pr(x_i=1)
 \le
 \left(1-\frac{m(m-1)}{(2m-1)(2m-2)}\right)^r
 \le \rho^r
\tag{2.8}
\]

for an absolute \(\rho<1\) and all sufficiently large \(m\).
Every phase factor has \(A_m\) directed row edges.  Hence the expected
total second term in (2.5), through all phases, is at most

\[
 \frac{A_m}{m-1}\sum_{r\ge0}r\rho^r=O(A_m/m)=O(W/m).
\tag{2.9}
\]

Together with (2.6), this proves \(\mathbb EJ=O(W/m)\).  Some deterministic
choice of the independent relabellings attains this bound.  Finally
\(A_m=\Theta(W)\), so (2.4) follows. \(\square\)

## 3. Exact row-coherent orientation at one transition

The next lemma separates endpoint ordering from bulk support.

Let a transition carrier be a loopless multigraph \(G\).  An edge is one
current interval occurrence and its two endpoints are its two legal
same-row endpoint children.  Suppose the edge occurrences are partitioned
into source-row paths and cycles.

### Lemma 3.1 (row-monotone orientations attain the unrestricted support)

There is an edge orientation maximizing the number of covered vertices
such that on every source path the orientation word has the form

\[
 0\cdots01\cdots1,
\tag{3.1}
\]

and on every source cycle it is constant.  If the nonisolated connected
components of \(G\) are \(C\), the exact maximum covered support is

\[
 \sum_C\min\{e(C),v(C)\}.
\tag{3.2}
\]

Equivalently, the exact edge-to-distinct-vertex Hall deficiency is

\[
 \delta(G)=\sum_C(e(C)-v(C))_+.
\tag{3.3}
\]

If \(T(G)\) is the number of tree components and
\(V(G)\) is the actual carrier support, then

\[
 \boxed{\delta(G)=|E(G)|-|V(G)|+T(G).}
\tag{3.4}
\]

If the carrier is partitioned into \(p\) source paths and any number of
source cycles, then \(T(G)\le p\).

#### Proof

In a connected component with \(e=v-1\), root a spanning tree and orient
each edge towards its child; this covers \(v-1=e\) vertices.  If
\(e\ge v\), take a spanning unicyclic subgraph, orient its cycle
cyclically and its attached trees away from the cycle, and cover every
vertex.  This proves (3.2), and (3.3) is the equivalent matching
deficiency.  Summing \(e-v\) over components and observing that a tree is
the only component contributing \(-1\) proves (3.4).

It remains to impose the row form.  On a source path, if two consecutive
edge bits contain a descent \(10\), change the second bit to \(1\).  The
common vertex remains covered by the first edge, and the second edge now
covers its other endpoint, so the global covered set cannot shrink.
Repeatedly removing descents gives (3.1).  Replacing all orientations on a
source cycle by either constant cyclic orientation covers every vertex of
that source cycle and likewise cannot shrink the global union.  Applying
these operations row by row preserves optimality.  Finally a tree component
contains no source cycle and contains at least one source path; distinct
tree components contain distinct source paths.  Hence \(T(G)\le p\).
\(\square\)

The preceding uncrossing has an exact occurrence-level form which is useful
when actual target labels coincide between different rows.

### Lemma 3.1A (exact path cycle-excess identity)

Let a source path have vertices

\[
 v_0,v_1,\ldots ,v_\ell
\]

and edges \(e_i=v_{i-1}v_i\), \(1\le i\le\ell\).  Write \(b_i=0\)
when \(e_i\) is sent to \(v_{i-1}\), and \(b_i=1\) when it is sent to
\(v_i\).  Before identifying equal target labels from different rows, the
number of duplicated vertex occurrences is

\[
 C(b)=|\{i:b_i b_{i+1}=10\}|,
\tag{3.4a}
\]

and the number of omitted vertex occurrences is

\[
 Z(b)=|\{i:b_i b_{i+1}=01\}|
       +\mathbf1_{\{b_1=1\}}+\mathbf1_{\{b_\ell=0\}}.
\tag{3.4b}
\]

They satisfy \(Z(b)=C(b)+1\).  On a source cycle the corresponding two
counts are equal.  In particular a word \(0\cdots01\cdots1\) omits exactly
one arbitrarily prescribed path vertex and has \(C(b)=0\), while a constant
cycle orientation covers every cycle occurrence once.

#### Proof

An internal vertex \(v_i\) has load

\[
 \mathbf1_{\{b_i=1\}}+\mathbf1_{\{b_{i+1}=0\}}.
\]

This load is respectively \(1,0,2,1\) on the adjacent bit patterns
\(00,01,10,11\), proving (3.4a)--(3.4b), including the two endpoint
terms.  Since the number of ascents minus the number of descents equals
\(b_\ell-b_1\), one obtains \(Z-C=1\).  On a cycle ascents and descents
are equinumerous.  To omit \(v_j\), use all ones when \(j=0\), all zeros
when \(j=\ell\), and zeros through \(e_j\) followed by ones from
\(e_{j+1}\) when \(0<j<\ell\). \(\square\)

The next statement is the exact upper-capacity Hall theorem furnished by
this identity.  A *carrier occurrence* remembers its source row even when
two occurrences have the same actual target label.

### Theorem 3.1B (exact row-coherent deletion Hall theorem)

Let \(\mathscr P\) be the source paths at one transition, let the remaining
source components be cycles, and let \(V\) be the set of actual target
labels.  For \(v\in V\), let \(d(v)\) be the number of carrier occurrences
labelled \(v\), including path and cycle occurrences.  Prescribe integral
upper capacities \(u(v)\ge0\), and put

\[
 a(v)=(d(v)-u(v))_+.
\]

Form the bipartite deletion graph \(D\) between \(\mathscr P\) and \(V\),
joining a path to \(v\) precisely when that path has a carrier occurrence
labelled \(v\).  Then the minimum, over row-monotone path orientations and
constant cycle orientations, of the total residual capacity excess is

\[
 \boxed{
 \min\sum_{v\in V}(\lambda(v)-u(v))_+
 =
 \max_{U\subseteq V}
 \left(a(U)-|N_D(U)|\right)_+.}
\tag{3.4c}
\]

Here \(\lambda(v)\) is the oriented load and
\(a(U)=\sum_{v\in U}a(v)\).  In particular all capacities are met by a
row-coherent orientation with zero within-row duplicate excess if and only
if

\[
 a(U)\le |N_D(U)|\qquad(U\subseteq V).
\tag{3.4d}
\]

#### Proof

By Lemma 3.1A, choosing a monotone orientation on a path is exactly choosing
one of its carrier occurrences to omit; every such choice is realizable.
A constant cycle orientation omits none.  If \(r(v)\) path occurrences
labelled \(v\) are omitted, then

\[
 (\lambda(v)-u(v))_+=(a(v)-r(v))_+.
\]

Replace each target \(v\) by \(a(v)\) identical demand clones, all adjacent
to the paths in \(N_D(v)\).  A matching of a clone to a path records one
useful deletion, and conversely every useful deletion gives such a matching.
Unmatched paths may omit any one of their occurrences; this can only lower
loads further.  Therefore the minimum residual excess is the number of
unmatched demand clones in a maximum matching.

The deficiency form of Hall's theorem says that this number is

\[
 \max_{\mathcal U}(|\mathcal U|-|N(\mathcal U)|)_+,
\]

where \(\mathcal U\) ranges over clone families.  If a clone family contains
one clone of \(v\), adjoining all other clones of \(v\) changes no
neighbourhood and cannot decrease its deficiency.  Hence a maximizing
family is the union of all clones over some \(U\subseteq V\), which gives
(3.4c).  Lemma 3.1A realizes the resulting deletion choices with the stated
row orientations. \(\square\)

There is an even simpler simultaneous construction which never fragments
the physical runs.

### Theorem 3.2 (straight token-spine routing)

At each downward or upward corridor transition, orient every occurrence in
one current source run uniformly to the same endpoint.  Then:

1. every owner's choices form a literal nested Pascal path;
2. every source run remains one consecutive source run at the next rank;
3. if \(\mathcal C_q\) is the union of all two-parent carrier targets at
   that transition and \(\mathcal S_q\) is the support actually selected,
   then
   \[
    |\mathcal C_q\setminus\mathcal S_q|\le J;
   \tag{3.5}
   \]
4. for both signed sides through depth \(H\), the sum of the incremental
   carrier losses \(|\mathcal C_q\setminus\mathcal S_q|\) is at most
   \(2HJ\);
5. at every transition its within-row cycle excess is zero, and its entire
   row-ordering discrepancy consists of one omitted occurrence per source
   path.  Thus Theorem 3.1B, with at most \(J\) path vertices on its left,
   is the exact remaining upper-capacity test at that transition.

With the factors from Theorem 2.2, uniformly for \(H=o(m)\), this is

\[
 \boxed{2HJ=O(HW/m)=o(W).}
\tag{3.6}
\]

In particular this applies to the tail-killing Gaussian window
\(H=\Theta(\sqrt{m\log m})\).

More generally, arbitrary weights \(0\le w_q\le1\) give the bound
\(J\sum_qw_q\).  For the actual Gaussian capacity weights, put

\[
 N_q^\pm=\binom n{m\pm q},\qquad
 c_q^\pm=\left\lfloor\frac W{N_q^\pm}\right\rfloor\ge1.
\]

Then, uniformly for every window contained in the Boolean lattice, the
combined signed weighted incremental carrier loss is at most

\[
 \boxed{
 J\sum_{q=1}^H\left(\frac1{c_q^-}+\frac1{c_q^+}\right)
 =O(J\sqrt m)=O(W/\sqrt m)=o(W).}
\tag{3.6a}
\]

#### Proof

One source path with edges
\(e_a,\ldots,e_b\) has consecutive carrier vertices
\(v_{a-1},v_a,\ldots,v_b\).  Uniform left orientation covers
\(v_{a-1},\ldots,v_{b-1}\), while uniform right orientation covers
\(v_a,\ldots,v_b\).  Thus it misses at most one vertex of its complete
carrier.  A source cycle misses none.  Taking unions over the \(J\) source
runs proves (3.5), even when different rows identify some actual targets.

The selected vertices in either uniform orientation are again consecutive
vertices of the same physical row.  Induction therefore preserves the
same \(J\) runs at every depth.  Each owner has chosen one legal endpoint
at every step, so its cumulative choices are a literal monotone Pascal
path.  Lemma 3.1A gives the zero cycle-excess assertion.  Summing (3.5)
over the two signs and \(H\) depths proves the quantitative assertions.
For (3.6a), if \(x=W/N_q^\pm\ge1\), then
\(\lfloor x\rfloor\ge x/2\), and hence

\[
 \frac1{c_q^\pm}\le\frac{2N_q^\pm}{W}.
\]

The sum of the rank sizes in the displayed window is at most \(2^n\),
while \(2^n/W=\Theta(\sqrt m)\).  Theorem 2.2 now gives (3.6a).
\(\square\)

If \(\mathcal R_q\) denotes the entire Boolean rank and

\[
 M_q=|\mathcal R_q\setminus\mathcal C_q|
\tag{3.7}
\]

is its carrier hole after all preceding routing choices, then (3.5) gives
the exact useful estimate

\[
 |\mathcal R_q\setminus\mathcal S_q|\le M_q+J.
\tag{3.8}
\]

Thus the tree term in the exact one-step Hall identity of Lemma 3.1 and the
incremental straight-routing correction of Theorem 3.2 are both
\(O(W/m)\).  The term \(M_q\) is not merely a factor-level bulk hole: it is
routing-dependent and can contain endpoint strips lost at earlier depths.
For one linear run with \(\ell\) owners, the full depth-\(q\) corridor has
\(\ell+q\) possible start occurrences, whereas a straight route selects
\(\ell\) and its immediate carrier has only \(\ell+1\).  Thus the other
\(q-1\) starts have migrated into \(M_q\).  Summing this worst-case strip
mass gives \(J\sum_{q\le H}(q-1)=\Theta(H^2J)\).

The one-step Hall and simultaneous support assertions should also not be
conflated.  A support-maximizing Hall orientation may split a run once;
the straight construction preserves the runs but does not by itself give
one injective, capacity-balanced nested matching through every depth.

The apparent \(\Theta(H^2J)\) boundary-strip accumulation can nevertheless
be removed at the literal-word level by spending one additional
length-\(H\) collar per run.  This is affordable precisely because of
Theorem 2.2.

### Theorem 3.3 (an expanded collar exposes every Pascal corridor)

Fix \(0\le H\le m-1\).  Let \(I=[a,b]\) be one selected linear run in a cyclic row \(\pi\), with
\(\ell=b-a+1\), and put

\[
 E_s=I_\pi(s,m-H).
\tag{3.9}
\]

Emit the block

\[
 \boxed{E_{a-H},E_{a-H+1},\ldots,E_{b+2H}.}
\tag{3.10}
\]

It has exactly \(\ell+3H\) nonzero letters.  For every selected owner

\[
 X_i=I_\pi(i,m),\qquad a\le i\le b,
\]

every \(0\le q\le H\), every cyclic interval
\(L\in\binom{X_i}{m-q}\), and every cyclic interval
\(U\in\binom{Q_j}{m+q}\) containing \(X_i\), both \(L\) and \(U\)
occur as contiguous ORs wholly inside (3.10).

Consequently all selected runs together admit one literal word of length

\[
 \boxed{N_1+3HJ+O(1)=W+o(W)}
\tag{3.11}
\]

for every \(H=o(m)\), and that word exposes the complete two-sided Pascal
corridor of every selected owner through depth \(H\).

#### Proof

For consecutive base masks one has the exact identity

\[
 \bigvee_{u=0}^{t-1}E_{s+u}
 =I_\pi(s,m-H+t-1),
\qquad 1\le t\le2H+1.
\tag{3.12}
\]

Every length-\((m-q)\) interval contained in \(X_i\) has the form

\[
 I_\pi(s,m-q),\qquad i\le s\le i+q.
\tag{3.13}
\]

Take \(t=H-q+1\) in (3.12).  Its witness begins no earlier than
\(E_a\), and its last index is

\[
 s+H-q\le i+H\le b+H.
\]

Every length-\((m+q)\) cyclic interval containing \(X_i\) has the form

\[
 I_\pi(s,m+q),\qquad i-q\le s\le i.
\tag{3.14}
\]

Take \(t=H+q+1\).  Its witness starts at
\(s\ge i-q\ge a-H\), while its last index satisfies

\[
 s+H+q\le i+H+q\le b+2H.
\]

Thus every displayed witness lies inside (3.10).  Its length is

\[
 (b+2H)-(a-H)+1=\ell+3H.
\]

Concatenate these blocks over the \(J\) runs.  Their selected-owner counts
sum to \(N_1\), and Theorem 2.2 gives
\(3HJ=O(HW/m)=o(W)\) for \(H=o(m)\).  All letters have size
\(m-H\ge1\), so the nonzero-letter condition holds. \(\square\)

### Theorem 3.4 (conditional sharp first-upper theorem)

Let \(F\) be one exact local factor on a set of size \(L=2m-1\), and put

\[
 B=\binom{L}{m+1},\qquad
 M=B-|\mathcal I_{m+1}(F)|.
\tag{3.15}
\]

For every phase \(j\), take a relabelled copy \(F_j\) of \(F\) on
\(Q_j\).  The relabellings can be chosen so that the complete first-upper
two-parent graph of the first-avoided spine has the exact integral Hall
loss

\[
 \boxed{\delta_+=O(M+W/m).}
\tag{3.16}
\]

In particular, \(M=O(W/m)\) implies an \(O(W/m)\) first-upper Hall loss.

#### Proof

For a rank-\((m+1)\) target, let its category be its first avoided pair.
Let \(E_j\) be the number of selected phase-\(j\) owners and let \(Z_j\)
be the number of rank-\((m+1)\) targets of category \(j\).  The already
proved category coupling gives

\[
 \Delta_{\rm cat}:=\sum_j(E_j-Z_j)_+=O(W/m).
\tag{3.17}
\]

For completeness, choose a uniform rank-\((m-1)\) set \(S\), add a
uniform two-set from its complement, and call the resulting uniform
rank-\((m+1)\) set \(U\).  The category can change only if the added
two-set meets the first pair avoided by \(S\), an event of probability

\[
 1-\frac{\binom m2}{\binom{m+2}2}
 =\frac{2(2m+1)}{(m+1)(m+2)}.
\]

The total-variation coupling inequality gives (3.17).

Relabel the copies \(F_j\) independently and uniformly.  Every fixed
target in \(\binom{Q_j}{m+1}\) is absent from the full interval support
with probability \(M/B\).  Since the category families are disjoint,

\[
 \mathbb E(\hbox{category targets missing from the full factors})
 \le \frac MB\sum_jZ_j=O(M),
\tag{3.18}
\]

because \(\sum_jZ_j\le W\) and \(W/B=O(1)\).

It remains to count a target occurrence which exists in \(F_j\) but is
not incident with a selected phase-\(j\) owner.  Write such a fixed upper
occurrence as

\[
 U=C\cup\{\ell,r,t\},\qquad |C|=m-2,
\tag{3.19}
\]

so that its two adjacent lower selection tests are

\[
 S_L=C\cup\{\ell\},\qquad S_R=C\cup\{r\}.
\tag{3.20}
\]

Conditional on the uniform set \(U\), the ordered triple
\((\ell,r,t)\) is uniform among the ordered triples of distinct elements
of \(U\).

Let \(r_0=j-1\).  A uniform \((m+1)\)-subset of \(Q_j\) meets one fixed
earlier pair with probability

\[
 p_m=1-\frac{(m-2)(m-3)}{(2m-1)(2m-2)}\le\rho<1
\tag{3.21}
\]

for an absolute \(\rho\) and all sufficiently large \(m\) (for example,
\(\rho=9/10\)).  The events of meeting disjoint pairs obey

\[
 \Pr(U\text{ meets all }r_0\text{ earlier pairs})\le p_m^{r_0}.
\tag{3.22}
\]

This is Lemma 2.1 with \(N=2m-1\) and \(k=m+1\).

Conditional on \(U\), the set \(S_L=U\setminus\{r,t\}\) can miss a fixed
earlier pair met by \(U\) only if all its points in \(U\) lie among
\(\{r,t\}\).  Whether the intersection has size one or two, this has
probability at most \(2/(m+1)\).  A union bound and (3.22) give

\[
 \Pr(U\text{ has category }j, S_L\text{ is not selected})
 \le \frac{2r_0}{m+1}p_m^{r_0}.
\tag{3.23}
\]

If neither adjacent owner is selected then in particular \(S_L\) is not
selected.  There are exactly \(A_m\) upper occurrences in each local
factor, so (3.23) yields

\[
 \mathbb E(\hbox{selection-bad upper occurrences})
 \le\frac{2A_m}{m+1}\sum_{r_0\ge0}r_0\rho^{r_0}
 =O(W/m).
\tag{3.24}
\]

A category target absent from the two-parent carrier either is absent from
the full factor or all of its occurrences are selection-bad.  Thus
(3.18)--(3.24) bound the expected total carrier-hole count by
\(O(M+W/m)\).  The same relabellings have expected total run count
\(O(W/m)\) by Theorem 2.2.  Hence one deterministic choice makes the sum
of the carrier-hole count and run count \(O(M+W/m)\).

Finally decompose the first-upper graph by categories.  If its
phase-\(j\) carrier support has size \(V_j\) and it has \(T_j\) tree
components, Lemma 3.1 gives

\[
 \delta_j=E_j-V_j+T_j
 \le(E_j-Z_j)_+ +(Z_j-V_j)+T_j.
\tag{3.25}
\]

Summing (3.25), using (3.17), the carrier-hole estimate, and
\(\sum_jT_j\le J\), proves (3.16).  Every step is an integral matching of
actual owner-edges to actual incident row targets. \(\square\)

## 4. Phase 1 makes the remaining bulk term exact

Let \(F_1\) be the phase-one exact factor on
\(Q_1=[n]\setminus P_1\).  For an integer \(r\), let

\[
 \mathcal I_r(F_1)
 =\{I_\pi(i,r):\pi\in F_1,\ i\in\mathbb Z_L\}
\tag{4.1}
\]

be its actual cyclic interval support.  Put

\[
 B_q=\binom{L}{m-q-1}=\binom{L}{m+q},\qquad
 h_q(F_1)=B_q-|\mathcal I_{m-q-1}(F_1)|.
\tag{4.2}
\]

### Theorem 4.1 (phase-one orientation invariance)

For every row-coherent ownerwise upper corridor orientation of the fixed
phase-one rows, its rank-\((m+q)\) support is contained in
\(\mathcal I_{m+q}(F_1)\).  Conversely, one constant endpoint choice in
every row simultaneously attains this whole support for every \(q\).
Furthermore,

\[
 |\mathcal I_{m+q}(F_1)|
 =|\mathcal I_{m-q-1}(F_1)|=B_q-h_q(F_1).
\tag{4.3}
\]

No later phase can fill one of these phase-one holes.

#### Proof

Every corridor interval through a phase-one owner is an interval in the
same cyclic order of \(F_1\), so its upper target belongs to
\(\mathcal I_{m+q}(F_1)\).  Phase 1 selects every start of every row.
Choosing the same endpoint at every step merely shifts all cyclic starts,
and therefore realizes every member of \(\mathcal I_{m+q}(F_1)\)
simultaneously for every \(q\).

Complementation inside the cyclic universe \(Q_1\) maps a length
\(m+q\) interval to a cyclic interval of length
\(L-(m+q)=m-q-1\), proving (4.3).  Finally every upper flag from a later
phase contains its selected lower set, which meets \(P_1\); every
phase-one target avoids \(P_1\).  Hence later phases cannot fill a missing
phase-one target. \(\square\)

At \(q=1\), the two-parent carrier graph is a union of complete source-row
cycles, so it has no tree component.  It has \(A_m\) edges and
\(B_1-h_1(F_1)\) vertices.  Lemma 3.1 therefore gives:

### Corollary 4.2 (exact first-transition loss)

\[
\boxed{
 \begin{aligned}
 \delta_{1,+}
 &=A_m-(B_1-h_1(F_1))\\
 &=\frac{2A_m}{m+1}+h_1(F_1)\\
 &=\frac{W}{2m+1}+h_1(F_1).
 \end{aligned}}
\tag{4.4}
\]

In particular,

\[
 \delta_{1,+}=O(W/m)
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 h_1(F_1)=O(W/m).
\tag{4.5}
\]

The arithmetic term in (4.4) is the previously proved phase-one baseline.
The second term is an immutable bulk-support hole and may not be discarded
from an orientation theorem.

Combining Corollary 4.2 with Theorem 3.4 gives a sharp characterization for
the fixed-base architecture.  Let \(D^*(F)\) be the least first-upper Hall
loss over all relabellings of copies of one base factor \(F\), all choices
of the global priority order, and all row-coherent two-parent orientations,
while retaining the exact first-avoided assignment for the chosen order.
With \(M=h_1(F)\),

\[
 \boxed{
  \frac{W}{2m+1}+M
  \le D^*(F)
  \le C\left(M+\frac Wm\right)
 }
\tag{4.6}
\]

for an absolute \(C\).  Therefore, for every \(H=o(m)\),

\[
 \boxed{
 D^*(F)=o(W/H)
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 h_1(F)=o(W/H).}
\tag{4.7}
\]

Thus the first-upper row-coherent orientation gate is asymptotically
equivalent, in this fixed-base pair-omission architecture, to the local
first-shadow support gate.  The token spine adds no further asymptotic
loss, but it also cannot repair a failed local shadow.

This equivalence does not include a hybrid central rematching which assigns
some set to a non-first avoided pair.  Such a token switch changes the
phase-one owner family itself and lies outside the fixed-spine orientation
problem treated by Theorem 4.1.

There is, however, an exact obstruction to the most symmetric proposed
hybrid.

### Proposition 4.3 (no flat all-pair common-base token atlas)

For each pair block \(P_a\), suppose there is a bijection

\[
 f_a:\binom{Q_a}{m-1}\longrightarrow\binom{Q_a}{m},
 \qquad S\subset f_a(S).
\tag{4.8}
\]

For distinct \(a,b\), let \(\theta_{ab}\) interchange the two pair blocks
\(P_a,P_b\) pointwise and fix every other coordinate.  If \(m\ge4\), the
maps cannot satisfy

\[
 f_b(S)=\theta_{ab}f_a(S)
\tag{4.9}
\]

for every \(S\) avoiding both \(P_a\) and \(P_b\).

#### Proof

Let \(S\) avoid three distinct pair blocks \(P_a,P_b,P_c\), and put
\(Y=f_a(S)\).  Direct transport from \(a\) to \(c\), and transport through
\(b\), give

\[
 \theta_{ac}Y=f_c(S)=\theta_{bc}\theta_{ab}Y.
\]

On the three pair blocks,
\(\theta_{ac}\theta_{bc}\theta_{ab}=\theta_{bc}\).  Hence

\[
 Y=\theta_{bc}Y.
\tag{4.10}
\]

Because \(Y=S\cup\{x\}\) and \(S\) is fixed by \(\theta_{bc}\), the
single added point \(x\) lies outside \(P_b\cup P_c\).

Suppose first that \(m=2r\).  Take \(S\) to be the unpaired coordinate
together with \(r-1\) complete pair blocks.  Then \(|S|=m-1\), and at
least three pair blocks are empty.  Fix one empty block \(P_a\).  Varying
\(b,c\) among the other empty blocks in (4.10) excludes every point of
those blocks as the added point; membership in \(Q_a\) excludes \(P_a\).
But every point outside \(S\) lies in one of these empty blocks, a
contradiction.

Now let \(m=2r+1\).  Take \(X\) to be the unpaired coordinate together
with \(r\) complete pair blocks.  Thus \(|X|=m\), and at least three pair
blocks are empty.  Fix an empty \(P_a\).  For each \(u\in X\), put
\(S_u=X\setminus\{u\}\).  The same triple argument excludes every point
outside \(S_u\) except \(u\), so

\[
 f_a(S_u)=X
\]

for every \(u\in X\), contradicting injectivity of \(f_a\). \(\square\)

Thus a path-independent Yang--Baxter token system cannot be used to move
each lower set freely among all of its avoided pairs.  Proposition 4.3 does
not exclude a single acyclic adjacent-swap chain or a non-equivariant
hybrid; those require separate collision control.

## 5. Exact constant-one boundary

For the factors supplied by Theorem 2.2, use the expanded blocks of
Theorem 3.3.  Let \(\widehat{\mathcal C}_q^-\) and
\(\widehat{\mathcal C}_q^+\) be the unions of all rank-\((m-q)\) and
rank-\((m+q)\) targets, respectively, lying in the complete physical
Pascal corridor of at least one selected owner.  Put

\[
 \widehat M_q^-=\binom{n}{m-q}-|\widehat{\mathcal C}_q^-|,
 \qquad
 \widehat M_q^+=\binom{n}{m+q}-|\widehat{\mathcal C}_q^+|.
\tag{5.1}
\]

The expanded-collar word has length

\[
 N_1+3HJ+O(1)=W+o(W)
\tag{5.2}
\]

and misses at most

\[
 \boxed{
  \frac{2W}{m+2}
  +\sum_{q=1}^H(\widehat M_q^-+\widehat M_q^+)}
\tag{5.3}
\]

targets in the middle and signed shallow ranks.  The first term is
\(W-N_1\), the number of middle targets not owned by the selected rows.
Consequently the proved sufficient condition

\[
 \boxed{
 \sum_{q=1}^H(\widehat M_q^-+\widehat M_q^+)=o(W)}
\tag{5.4}
\]

implies a literal length-\(W+o(W)\) shallow-band word by appending each
remaining missing target once.  Unlike the straight-routing carrier holes
in (3.7), the quantities in (5.1) are routing-independent full-corridor
support holes.  The extra collar has absorbed the accumulated endpoint
strips into the literal base word.

This shallow-band condition has the exact global consequence needed for
constant one.

### Proposition 5.1 (tail-complete literal criterion)

Fix \(\alpha>1/\sqrt2\) and take

\[
 H=\left\lceil\alpha\sqrt{m\log m}\right\rceil.
\]

If (5.4) holds for the factors and selected runs above, then

\[
 \boxed{\nu(2m+1)\le W+o(W).}
\]

#### Proof

It remains only to pay for ranks outside the displayed band.  Write
\(U_q=\binom{2m+1}{m+q}\).  For \(q\ge1\),

\[
 \frac{U_q}{W}
 =\prod_{i=1}^q\frac{m+2-i}{m+i}
 \le \exp\left(-\frac{q(q-1)}{m+q}\right).
\tag{5.4a}
\]

Indeed the \(i\)-th factor is
\(1-2(i-1)/(m+i)\), and \(\log(1-x)\le-x\).

Moreover

\[
 \frac{U_{q+1}}{U_q}=\frac{m+1-q}{m+q+1},
\]

so the upper tail from \(H\) onward is at most

\[
 O\left(\frac mH U_H\right)
 =Wm^{1/2-\alpha^2+o(1)}=o(W).
\tag{5.4b}
\]

Complementation gives the same bound for the lower tail.  The expanded
collar costs \(N_1+3HJ+O(1)=W+o(W)\), (5.3)--(5.4) allow all shallow
holes to be appended in \(o(W)\) letters, and (5.4b) allows every deep
nonempty target to be appended in another \(o(W)\) letters.  Each appended
target is itself a nonzero letter and hence a one-letter contiguous OR
witness.
\(\square\)

If (5.4) is obtained for every sufficiently large \(m\), the same conclusion
holds in even dimension.  Indeed a universal word \((A_i)_{i=1}^L\) on
\([2m+1]\) gives the word

\[
 A_1,\ldots,A_L,\ \{x\},\
 A_1\cup\{x\},\ldots,A_L\cup\{x\}
\]

on one additional coordinate, of length \(2L+1\).  The first copy covers
targets avoiding \(x\), the singleton covers \(\{x\}\), and the last copy
covers every \(T\cup\{x\}\) with \(T\ne\varnothing\).  Since

\[
 \binom{2m+2}{m+1}=2\binom{2m+1}{m},
\]

the odd-dimensional conditional conclusion implies
\(\nu(k)=(1+o(1))W(k)\) along all dimensions.

Theorem 4.1 shows that (5.4) necessarily contains

\[
 \sum_{q=1}^Hh_q(F_1)=o(W)
\tag{5.5}
\]

on the upper side.  Even its first estimate
\(h_1(F_1)=O(W/m)\), needed for an \(O(W/m)\) first-transition loss, is
not supplied by the known existence theorem for exact middle wreath
factors.

Thus the pair-omission token spine gives the following positive,
quantitative boundary:

* the spine may be chosen with \(O(W/m)\) physical runs;
* the tree correction and incremental carrier-endpoint loss at each
  transition are \(O(W/m)\);
* one additional length-\(H\) collar per run exposes the entire physical
  Pascal corridor at total cost \(o(W)\);
* the fixed-base first-upper gate is characterized, up to an absolute
  constant, by (4.6).

The minimal unproved lemma for this particular literal construction is the
full-corridor support bound (5.4); phase-one local interval holes are
immutable constituents of it by (4.4).  Condition (5.4) is asserted as a
sufficient condition for these fixed blocks, not as a necessary condition
for every possible recoding.  If one insists on an exact common-owner
balanced resolution, the expanded word does not supply that matching: the
simultaneous nested Hall/cycle-excess theorem remains additional and
unproved.

## 6. Adversarial audit

1. The random argument changes only coordinate labels of already existing
   exact factors.  Every relabelled object remains an integral exact
   factor, and expectation is used only to select a deterministic one.
2. Formula (2.5) handles full cyclic selection words through its mass term;
   no boundary-to-run identity is applied to a full word.
3. Lemma 2.1 is used with both \(k=m-1\) and \(k=m+1\); its proof is valid
   for arbitrary fixed-size sampling.
4. Theorem 3.2 does not assert that its carrier is the full depth corridor.
   Its exact bound is \(M_q+J\), where \(M_q\) includes earlier routing
   losses.
5. Cross-row target collisions do not invalidate (3.5): every internal
   carrier vertex occurrence is selected within its own row, so only row
   endpoint occurrences can disappear from the union.
6. Theorem 3.1B counts carrier *vertex occurrences*, not multigraph degree.
   Its cloned Hall proof permits parallel labels across rows.  It is an
   upper-capacity theorem for one transition: an internal omitted vertex
   can split the next source row, so independently optimal deletions cannot
   be iterated while keeping \(J=O(W/m)\) without an additional common-flow
   argument.
7. The straight choices are common-owner compatible.  Each owner makes one
   legal endpoint move at every depth, and uniformity along a row preserves
   the same physical run instead of splicing unrelated row labels.
8. The expanded collar is deliberately longer than the standard canonical
   collar by exactly \(H\) letters per run.  The inequalities in
   (3.13)--(3.14) check both extreme starts and show that no full-corridor
   target is inferred without a contiguous witness inside its own block.
9. Equation (4.4) is a lower and upper equality.  Full phase-one cycles
   make the tree correction zero, and a constant row orientation realizes
   every available interval target.
10. Proposition 5.1 uses a fixed \(\alpha>1/\sqrt2\), so the approach to
    the sharp Gaussian threshold is one-sided; no ambiguous
    \((1/\sqrt2+o(1))\)-notation is used.
11. The report proves neither (5.4) nor constant one.  It proves the sharp
   run count, one-step orientation bounds, the full-corridor collar, and
   the exact fixed-base first-transition characterization.  Full-corridor
   global support and exact nested Hall matching remain unproved.
12. An independent proof audit verified the path cycle-excess identity,
    the cloned-Hall deficiency formula (3.4c), and every index and OR
    witness in the expanded collar.  Its substantive warning is exactly
    item 6: independently optimal internal omissions may proliferate runs,
    so (3.4c) is not silently iterated as a common flow.
