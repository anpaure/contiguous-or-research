## 1. Bilateral source support is forced

Fix an endpoint-disjoint triple \(I=\{e,f,g\}\), where \(e\) is one of the
468 authenticated H sources.  Let \(o_e=(k,t_e^0,t_e^1)\) be the source
option in a complete triple occurrence packing.  Put

\[
 S_\phi(o_e)=\{y\in\{f,g\}:h_y\in F(t_e^\phi)\}.
\tag{1.1}
\]

Unary emptiness gives

\[
 S_0(o_e)\cup S_1(o_e)\ne\varnothing.
\tag{1.2}
\]

Suppose the union in (1.2) were the singleton \(\{f\}\).  The option uses
neither \(h_g\) nor \(d_g\): the former by assumption and the latter because
\(d_g\) is absent from the triple provider bank.  Restoring \(d_g\) and
removing \(h_g\) therefore leaves the same source option in the directed
pair child \((e,f)\).  Completeness of the H-source rescue-prefix ledger
places this orientation in the 94-prefix branch.  The exact extension replay
closes every such three-mode chain.  The same argument applies with \(f,g\)
interchanged.  Hence every feasible H-source triple obeys

\[
 \boxed{S_0(o_e)\cup S_1(o_e)=\{f,g\}.}
\tag{1.3}
\]

This argument needs a source option in the pair child, not a feasible
two-role packing.  Thus it does not assume the pair has an occurrence edge.

## 2. The nine source-aperture joins

For \(S\subseteq\{f,g\}\), define the exact source aperture relation

\[
 E_{e,S}^{\phi}(k;f,g)=
 \{t\in {\cal T}_e^\phi(k;I):
   \{y\in\{f,g\}:h_y\in F(t)\}=S\}.
\tag{2.1}
\]

All relations are triple-local: all three donors have already been deleted,
all three hosts inserted, and every physical atom identity, footprint, flag,
private-row rule and phase reservation retained.

There are exactly

\[
 4^2-2\cdot2^2+1=9
\tag{2.2}
\]

ordered pairs \((S_0,S_1)\) of subsets of \(\{f,g\}\) whose union is
\(\{f,g\}\).  Explicitly they are

\[
 (\varnothing,fg),(fg,\varnothing),(f,g),(g,f),
 (f,fg),(g,fg),(fg,f),(fg,g),(fg,fg).
\tag{2.3}
\]

Define the bilateral source-option relation

\[
 {\cal B}_e(f,g)=
 \bigcup_{(S_0,S_1)\text{ in }(2.3)}
 \bigcup_k
 E_{e,S_0}^{0}(k;f,g)
 \bowtie_{\rm flag}
 E_{e,S_1}^{1}(k;f,g),
\tag{2.4}
\]

where the join uses one source key and requires the union of the two
row-to-flag maps to be a function.

### Theorem 2.1 (bilateral aperture projection)

Every occurrence-valid H-source triple lies in

\[
 \boxed{
 \pi_{e,f,g}\{(e,f,g,b):b\in{\cal B}_e(f,g)\}.
 }
\tag{2.5}
\]

Consequently \({\cal B}_e(f,g)=\varnothing\) is an exact occurrence no-go
for that anchored helper pair.  The projection in (2.5) is generated by the
nine fixed-arity joins, so the search need not form the blind helper square.

#### Proof

Equation (1.3) places the two phase tuples of the source option in exactly
one of the nine terms of (2.4).  Conversely, (2.4) asserts only that the
source has an internally valid bilateral option; helper occurrence remains
to be checked below.  Therefore emptiness is a no-go, while nonemptiness is
only a candidate generator.  \(\square\)

## 3. Reduction of the 2,565 support matrices

In one phase, assign each installed host row to one of the three roles or to
``unused``.  The two-provider bound removes the three assignments sending
all hosts to one role, leaving 61 phase supports.  In two phases there are
\(61^2\) raw supports.

There are 46 phase supports in which \(h_f\) is not assigned to source
\(e\): there are \(4\cdot3\cdot4=48\) assignments before removing the
all-to-\(f\) and all-to-\(g\) violations.  There are likewise 46 avoiding
\(h_g\to e\), and 34 avoiding both.  Inclusion--exclusion and (1.3) give

\[
 \boxed{61^2-2\cdot46^2+34^2=645.}
\tag{3.1}
\]

Thus the exact 94-prefix no-go reduces the global H-source host-incidence
partition from 2,565 to 645 bilateral matrices.  Of these, 357 use both
helper hosts in the same source phase at least once, and 288 are split-only:

\[
 2\cdot3\cdot61-3^2=357,
 \qquad 645-357=288.
\tag{3.2}
\]

If a triple contains two H roles, applying (1.3) to both leaves 84 of the
\(61^2\) matrices.  Indeed the third host must go to the two H roles in
opposite phases.  For either orientation, each remaining required host has
seven two-phase assignments; excluding the two all-to-one-role violations
and restoring their intersection gives

\[
 2(7^2-4-4+1)=84.
\tag{3.3}
\]

If all three roles are H, only 8 matrices remain.  Each host is assigned in
the two phases to its two other roles, and choosing which of those roles
receives it in phase 0 gives \(2^3=8\).

The nine source joins are preferable to iterating the 645 matrices: a
bilateral source record already binds the physical helper identities, after
which the remaining host assignments are enforced by helper-option
compatibility.

## 4. Exact helper-option min--max

For the fixed triple \(I\), let \({\cal O}_f(I)\) and \({\cal O}_g(I)\) be
the complete same-key, two-phase option menus built in the same triple
provider bank.  For two options \(u,v\), let

\[
 C(u,v)=1
\tag{4.1}
\]

exactly when their phase footprints are disjoint in each phase and their
combined row-to-flag map is a function.  Put \(c(u,v)=1-C(u,v)\).

Define, with the minimum over an empty set equal to \(+\infty\),

\[
 \boxed{
 \Lambda(I)=
 \min_{b\in{\cal B}_e(f,g),\ u\in{\cal O}_f(I),\ v\in{\cal O}_g(I)}
 \max\{c(b,u),c(b,v),c(u,v)\}.
 }
\tag{4.2}
\]

### Theorem 4.1 (exact three-role min--max)

\[
 \boxed{I\text{ has a complete six-ticket occurrence packing}
        \iff \Lambda(I)=0.}
\tag{4.3}
\]

#### Proof

All remaining global constraints are pairwise: phase-local row capacity and
global equality of every repeated physical flag.  A packing therefore gives
three pairwise-compatible options and value zero in (4.2).  Conversely,
value zero gives one internally valid option for every role and all three
pairwise compatibilities.  Their same-phase footprints are jointly disjoint,
and a pairwise-consistent union of functions is a function, so the six
tickets form the required packing.  \(\square\)

For one bilateral source option \(b\), filter

\[
 F_b=\{u\in{\cal O}_f(I):C(b,u)=1\},\qquad
 G_b=\{v\in{\cal O}_g(I):C(b,v)=1\}.
\tag{4.4}
\]

Then (4.3) is equivalently

\[
 \exists b\in{\cal B}_e(f,g):
 E_{fg}\cap(F_b\times G_b)\ne\varnothing,
\tag{4.5}
\]

where \(E_{fg}=\{(u,v):C(u,v)=1\}\).  This is a triangle test in the
three-partite option graph, not a claim that any two-role prefix is feasible.

## 5. Sharp resource-cover obstruction

The complement of \(E_{fg}\) is an exact union of resource rectangles.
For every phase and row, one rectangle consists of helper options both using
that row in that phase.  For every row and two unequal flags, another
rectangle consists of options assigning those two flags to that row.  Hence

\[
 ({\cal O}_f\times{\cal O}_g)\setminus E_{fg}
 =\bigcup_{\rho\in{\cal R}} A_\rho\times B_\rho,
\tag{5.1}
\]

with \({\cal R}\) the finite set of literal capacity and flag-conflict
labels.

For fixed \(b\), the exact obstruction is

\[
 \boxed{
 F_b\times G_b\subseteq
 \bigcup_{\rho\in{\cal R}} A_\rho\times B_\rho.
 }
\tag{5.2}
\]

Equation (5.2) is necessary and sufficient for that source option to have no
helper completion.  It is a compact audit certificate when the rectangles
are stored as option bitsets.  It is sharper than a marginal empty-menu or
pair-prefix test: it covers the complete surviving Cartesian product in the
literal triple child.

## 6. S47 and downstream boundary

The authenticated unary zero for the 47 S modes extends the external-host
necessity lemma and the 2,565-matrix natural join to an S anchor.  It does
not, by itself, imply bilateral support.  A no-go for a complete two-role
packing also does not rule out a pair-child source option whose partner is
rescued only by the third mode.  Therefore the reduction from 2,565 to 645,
and from 15 nonempty source phase-support pairs to the nine bilateral pairs,
is asserted here only for H sources covered by the exact 94-prefix extension
no-go.

the global flag map, then undergo simultaneous three-transfer materialization,
protected-row replay and a fresh complete supplier matching.  Nothing here
proves deficiency 20, chronology, residence, upper coverage, compilation or
a K17 word.  Phase 1 remains transported-owner evidence.
