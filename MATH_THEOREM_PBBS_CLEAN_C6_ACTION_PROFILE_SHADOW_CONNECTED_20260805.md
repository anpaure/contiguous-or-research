# The action-profile shadow of deficit-three PBBS C6 templates is connected

**Date:** 2026-08-05  
**Method:** peak-pruning algebra and the Young graph; no search  
**Status:** unconditional at the action-profile/template level.  Every
clean C6 carried by the standard three-unmatched-zero PBBS template projects
to a sibling triple of partitions, and the graph generated by all abstract
deficit-three sibling templates is connected.  This does not assert that
every template satisfies PBBS direction, max-height selection, or the
common-deletion q2 condition.

## 1. Peak profiles around three unmatched zeros

Let a cyclic binary word of length `2m+1` and weight `m-1` have its three
forward-unmatched zeros displayed as

\[
 0_{a_0}D_0\,0_{a_1}D_1\,0_{a_2}D_2,
\tag{1.1}
\]

where every `D_i` is a Dyck word.  Let `h_i` be the height of `D_i`, with
the empty word assigned height zero.  For a Dyck word `D`, write
`beta(D)` for its parallel peak-pruning profile, padded by zeros, and put

\[
                         b=\beta(D_0)+\beta(D_1)+\beta(D_2).
\tag{1.2}
\]

The sum of the entries of `b` is `m-1`.

Flip `a_i` from zero to one and call the resulting rank-`m` cyclic state
`Z_i`.  The two unmatched zeros consumed by this flip are `a_i` and
`a_(i+1)`, so the remaining zero `a_(i-1)` is the root.  Its normalized
Dyck word is

\[
 D_{i-1}\,(1D_i0)\,D_{i+1}.
\tag{1.3}
\]

Peak profiles add under concatenation.  Wrapping a height-`h` Dyck word in
one outer pair leaves all old pruning layers unchanged and contributes one
last peak at layer `h+1`.  Hence

\[
 \beta(1D_i0)=\beta(D_i)+e_{h_i+1}.
\tag{1.4}
\]

Therefore the three action profiles are exactly

\[
             \boxed{\lambda_i=b+e_{h_i+1}\quad(i=0,1,2).}
\tag{1.5}
\]

In particular, all three are children of the same partition `b` in the
Young graph.  Repeated heights give repeated action profiles; distinct
heights give distinct profiles.

## 2. Every sibling adjacency has a deficit-three template

Let `b` be any partition of `m-1`, written as a nonincreasing profile

\[
                         b_1\ge b_2\ge\cdots\ge0.
\]

Its multiplicity of columns of height `h` is

\[
                         c_h=b_h-b_{h+1}.
\tag{2.1}
\]

The child `b+e_j` is a partition precisely when `j=1` or `c_(j-1)>0`.
Suppose two distinct children `b+e_i` and `b+e_j` are prescribed.

Represent `b` as a concatenation of mountains: take `c_h` copies of
`1^h0^h` for every `h>=1`.  If `i>1`, place one height-`i-1` mountain in
`D_0`; if `i=1`, take `D_0` empty.  Do the same for `j` in `D_1`.  Put all
remaining mountains, in any order, in `D_2`.  Equation (2.1) and the
addability condition guarantee that the required mountains exist.

The three profiles in (1.5) then contain both prescribed children.
Consequently every edge of the down-up Young graph on partitions of `m`
occurs in the two-section of an abstract deficit-three C6 profile triple.

## 3. Connectedness and diameter

Let `Y_m` be the graph on partitions of `m` in which two partitions are
adjacent when they have a common parent of size `m-1`.  Then `Y_m` is
connected, with diameter at most `m-1` from the one-row partition `(m)`.

Indeed, if `lambda!=(m)`, remove a corner from any nonfirst row and add a
corner to the first row.  The old and new partitions share the intermediate
partition of size `m-1`, and the number of boxes outside the first row drops
by one.  Iteration reaches `(m)` in at most `m-lambda_1` steps.

Combining this observation with Section 2 gives

\[
 \boxed{\text{the abstract deficit-three C6 action-profile shadow is
 connected}.}
\tag{3.1}
\]

Thus no invariant depending only on the soliton partition can split the
full abstract **template** atlas into many blocks.

## 4. Exact remaining angle obstruction

Equation (3.1) is deliberately not a PBBS connector-existence theorem.
For a profile template to become a selected q2-neutral clean C6, one still
must realize simultaneously:

1. the three directed PBBS old shores;
2. the max-height one-occurrence choices on all six required q1 shores;
3. one common companion deletion `d`; and
4. physical compatibility with the other chosen switches.

Moreover a fixed action profile can contain many rooted shape cycles and
many physical angle components.  The explicit three-component connector
is maximally rigid in this respect: all its rotations remain on one fixed
triple of physical components even though its three action profiles are
distinct.

The global `O(1)`-block theorem therefore cannot be obstructed at the
partition level.  Its unresolved content is an **angle-lifting and
selection theorem**: lift enough sibling moves from `Y_m` to common-pivot,
selected, physically compatible PBBS connectors.
