# Subadditive prices as anchored-window measures and superadditive clocks

**Date:** 2026-08-04  
**Status:** unconditional pure-mathematical reduction.  The universal
Rayleigh configuration inequality is not proved here.

Put

\[
 A={\sqrt\pi\over2}
\]

and let the signed tail of socket measure minus job measure be

\[
 K(x)=
 \begin{cases}
 1-e^{-(A-x)^2}-e^{-(A+x)^2},&0\le x\le A,\\
 -e^{-(A+x)^2},&x>A.
 \end{cases}                                           \tag{0.1}
\]

The remaining continuum configuration-dual question is whether

\[
 \int f\,d\mu-\int f\,d\nu\ge0                         \tag{0.2}
\]

for every nonnegative nondecreasing subadditive covering price `f`, with
`f(0)=0` and finite integrals.  This note gives two exact equivalent forms
of that question.  The second form converts every admissible price into a
single nondecreasing superadditive clock.

There is one support qualification.  Physical sockets have capacities only
in `(0,A)`.  For an arbitrary nonnegative nondecreasing subadditive `f`, put

\[
 \widehat f_A(x)=\inf\left\{
   \sum_{i=1}^nf(y_i):
   n\ge1,\ 0<y_i<A,\ \sum_i y_i\ge x
 \right\}.                                           \tag{0.3}
\]

Then

\[
 \widehat f_A(x)=f(x)\quad(0<x<A),
 \qquad \widehat f_A(x)\ge f(x)\quad(x>0).           \tag{0.4}
\]

Indeed, one socket attains equality below `A`, while subadditivity and
monotonicity give

\[
             \sum_i f(y_i)\ge f\!\left(\sum_i y_i\right)\ge f(x).
\]

Thus the universal physical covering-price inequality is equivalent to
the apparently stronger statement for **all** monotone subadditive `f`:
closing `f` by (0.3) leaves its socket integral unchanged and can only
increase its job integral.  Endpoint values at `A` are Gaussian-null.  This
justifies the full subadditive test cone used below; an individual test
function need not itself be generated by its restriction to `(0,A)`.

## 1. Anchored sliding-window measures

Let `f` be nondecreasing, right-continuous on `(0,infinity)`, and satisfy
`f(0)=0`.  We allow the physically important initial jump
`f(0+)>0`.  Let `rho=df` be its Stieltjes measure, with

\[
 \rho(\{0\})=f(0+)
\]

and, away from zero,

\[
 \rho((a,b])=f(b)-f(a)\qquad(0<a<b).                   \tag{1.1}
\]

### Theorem 1.1 (anchored-window characterization)

The function `f` is subadditive if and only if

\[
 \boxed{
 \rho((x,x+t])\le \rho([0,t])
 \qquad(x>0,\ t>0).
 }                                                       \tag{1.2}
\]

Moreover,

\[
 \boxed{
 \int f\,d\mu-\int f\,d\nu=\int_{[0,\infty)}K(x)\,d\rho(x).
 }                                                       \tag{1.3}
\]

#### Proof

For `x>0`, subadditivity is exactly

\[
 f(x+t)-f(x)\le f(t)-f(0),
\]

which is (1.2), because the right side is
`rho([0,t])=f(t)`.  The case `x=0` is tautological.  Conversely (1.2)
recovers the displayed subadditivity inequality for every `x>0`, and the
zero case again adds nothing.

For (1.3), write

\[
 f(z)=\int_{[0,\infty)}{\bf1}_{x<z}\,d\rho(x)
\]

at every continuity point of `f`.  Both Gaussian measures are absolutely
continuous, so their integrals do not see the countable exceptional set.
Tonelli, first for the positive and negative parts and then by truncation,
gives

\[
 \int f\,d(\mu-\nu)
 =\int\bigl(\mu((x,\infty))-\nu((x,\infty))\bigr)\,d\rho(x).
\]

The parenthesized tail is exactly (0.1).  This proves (1.3). \(\square\)

Thus the entire continuum dual is the following one-dimensional
measure-cone assertion:

> every nonnegative measure whose mass in a translated interval never
> exceeds the mass in the equally long initial interval has nonnegative
> pairing with `K`.

All concave prices correspond to measures `rho` with decreasing density.
Ceiling prices correspond to equally weighted arithmetic combs.  The
finite covering price `(1,2,2,2,3)` corresponds to the non-arithmetic jump
pattern `(1,1,0,0,1)` and shows that those two familiar subclasses do not
generate the full anchored-window cone.

## 2. Superadditive inverse clocks

For a nondecreasing unbounded price `f`, define its capacity clock by

\[
 a_f(u)=\sup\{x\ge0:f(x)\le u\},\qquad u\ge0.           \tag{2.1}
\]

Endpoint choices in (2.1) are irrelevant to the Gaussian integrals.  A
bounded price is handled by allowing `a_f(u)=infinity` and setting
`K(infinity)=0`.

### Theorem 2.1 (inverse-clock equivalence)

If `f` is nonnegative, nondecreasing and subadditive, then `a_f` is
nondecreasing and superadditive:

\[
 \boxed{a_f(u+v)\ge a_f(u)+a_f(v).}                    \tag{2.2}
\]

Conversely, let `a:[0,infinity)->[0,infinity]` be nondecreasing,
superadditive, and not identically infinite, and define the generalized
inverse

\[
 f_a(x)=\inf\{u\ge0:a(u)\ge x\}.                       \tag{2.3}
\]

Then `f_a` is nonnegative, nondecreasing and subadditive (after the
irrelevant right-continuous endpoint convention).  Finally,

\[
 \boxed{
 \int f\,d\mu-\int f\,d\nu
 =\int_0^\infty K(a_f(u))\,du.
 }                                                       \tag{2.4}
\]

Consequently, the full continuum configuration inequality is equivalent
to

\[
 \boxed{
 \int_0^\infty K(a(u))\,du\ge0
 }
                                                               \tag{2.5}
\]

for every nondecreasing superadditive clock `a` for which the integral is
defined.

#### Proof

Take `x<a_f(u)` and `y<a_f(v)` at continuity values.  Then
`f(x)<=u` and `f(y)<=v`, so

\[
 f(x+y)\le f(x)+f(y)\le u+v.
\]

Letting `x` and `y` increase to the two suprema proves (2.2).

Conversely, if `s>f_a(x)` and `t>f_a(y)`, choose them outside the countable
endpoint set so that `a(s)>=x` and `a(t)>=y`.  Superadditivity gives
`a(s+t)>=x+y`, hence

\[
 f_a(x+y)\le s+t.
\]

Let `s` decrease to `f_a(x)` and `t` decrease to `f_a(y)`.  This proves
subadditivity; monotonicity is immediate from (2.3).

For (2.4), the layer-cake formula gives

\[
 \int f\,d(\mu-\nu)
 =\int_0^\infty
   \bigl(\mu\{x:f(x)>u\}-\nu\{x:f(x)>u\}\bigr)\,du.
\]

Up to endpoints of zero Gaussian measure, the upper level set is
`(a_f(u),infinity)`.  Its signed mass is `K(a_f(u))`.  This proves (2.4).
The two generalized-inverse constructions recover each other outside
irrelevant flat/jump endpoints, proving the equivalence (2.5). \(\square\)

### Discrete integer form

If `f` is integer-valued, let `a_n` be its successive jump positions.  Then

\[
 0\le a_0\le a_1\le\cdots,
 \qquad a_{m+n}\ge a_m+a_n,                            \tag{2.6}
\]

and

\[
 \int f\,d\mu-\int f\,d\nu=\sum_{n\ge0}K(a_n).        \tag{2.7}
\]

Arithmetic clocks give the ceiling rays already proved positive.  Mixed
denominations give genuinely non-arithmetic superadditive jump trains.
Thus (2.7), rather than conic generation by ceilings, is the exact discrete
extremal problem.

## 3. A rigorous residue lower envelope

The clock form has a useful exact reduction, although it does not by itself
prove (2.5).

Fix `h>0` such that

\[
 T:=a(h)\ge A.                                         \tag{3.1}
\]

For `0<=x<infinity`, put

\[
 G_T(x)=K(x)-\sum_{q\ge1}e^{-(A+x+qT)^2}.              \tag{3.2}
\]

When `0<=x<=A`, this is equivalently

\[
 G_T(x)=1-e^{-(A-x)^2}
        -\sum_{q\ge0}e^{-(A+x+qT)^2}.                  \tag{3.3}
\]

### Theorem 3.1 (residue envelope)

Every admissible clock satisfies

\[
 \boxed{
 \int_0^\infty K(a(u))\,du
 \ge \int_0^h G_T(a(r))\,dr.
 }                                                       \tag{3.4}
\]

Its residue profile also satisfies

\[
 a(r)+a(h-r)\le T\qquad(0\le r\le h).                 \tag{3.5}
\]

#### Proof

For integers `q>=0`, superadditivity gives

\[
 a(qh+r)\ge qT+a(r).                                   \tag{3.6}
\]

For `q>=1`, the right side is at least `A`.  On `[A,infinity)`,

\[
 K(x)=-e^{-(A+x)^2}
\]

is increasing.  Therefore

\[
 K(a(qh+r))\ge-e^{-(A+qT+a(r))^2}.                    \tag{3.7}
\]

Split the integral into intervals `[qh,(q+1)h)`, use (3.7), and apply
Tonelli to the absolutely convergent Gaussian tail.  The `q=0` term is
`K(a(r))`; the remaining terms give (3.2), proving (3.4).

Equation (3.5) is simply superadditivity applied to `r+(h-r)=h`.
\(\square\)

This reduces the infinite tail of an arbitrary clock to one compact
residue interval.  A proof of

\[
 \int_0^hG_T(a(r))\,dr\ge0                             \tag{3.8}
\]

under the full monotonicity and superadditivity constraints would close
the continuum configuration gate.

## 4. Why pairwise residue constraints are insufficient

It is tempting to replace the full clock constraints by (3.5) and seek a
pointwise inequality

\[
 G_T(x)+G_T(y)\ge0\qquad(x+y\le T).                    \tag{4.1}
\]

That inequality is false at the exact Gaussian constant.

### Theorem 4.1 (critical endpoint obstruction)

At `T=A`,

\[
 \boxed{
 G_A(0)+G_A(A)
 =-4\sum_{m\ge1}e^{-4\pi m^2}<0.
 }                                                       \tag{4.2}
\]

#### Proof

Directly from (3.2)--(3.3),

\[
 G_A(0)+G_A(A)
 =1-2\sum_{n\ge1}e^{-n^2A^2}.                         \tag{4.3}
\]

Jacobi's theta transformation and `A^2=pi/4` give

\[
 \sum_{n\in\mathbb Z}e^{-n^2A^2}
 =2\sum_{m\in\mathbb Z}e^{-4\pi m^2}.                \tag{4.4}
\]

Since the left side of (4.4) is
`1+2 sum_(n>=1)e^(-n^2 A^2)`, substituting into (4.3) gives (4.2).
\(\square\)

Thus neither the pair constraint (3.5) nor a pointwise translated-interval
packing proves the universal inequality.  The missing argument must use
the global distributional restrictions imposed by a single monotone
superadditive clock (equivalently, all anchored-window inequalities
simultaneously).

## 5. Exact remaining analytic statement

The continuum configuration obstruction is now isolated as either of the
equivalent assertions:

1. **Anchored-window form.**  Prove `int K d rho>=0` for every nonnegative
   measure satisfying (1.2).
2. **Clock form.**  Prove `int K(a(u))du>=0` for every nondecreasing
   superadditive `a`.

All ceiling combs pass strictly, but the finite covering cone contains
non-arithmetic jump trains.  The residue envelope (3.4) reduces every such
train to a compact profile; Theorem 4.1 proves that any successful compact
argument must retain more than the single pair constraint (3.5).

This is a strict reduction of the open continuum dual, not a proof of the
additive-constant OR-word conjecture.
