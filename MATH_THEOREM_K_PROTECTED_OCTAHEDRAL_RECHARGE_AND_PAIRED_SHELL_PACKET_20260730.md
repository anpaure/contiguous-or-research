 \{d,p+q\}\longmapsto\{p,d+q\},\qquad

Consequently two placements of the same atom give the rechargeable charges

\[

## 1. Exact octahedral cell

Let the full child ground set have size (2r+1), and let the middle owners
be the rank-((r+1)) sets.  Fix

\[
 R\in\binom{[2r+1]}{r-1},\qquad
 L=\{0,1,2,3\}\subseteq[2r+1]\setminus R.             \tag{1.1}
\]

For distinct (i,j\in L), write

\[
 L\setminus\{i,j\}=\{u,v\},
\qquad
 q_{ij}=\{R+i+u,\ R+i+v\}.                            \tag{1.2}
\]

This is a Johnson edge.  Its lower and upper colours are

\[
 c^-(q_{ij})=R+i,
 \qquad
 c^+(q_{ij})=R+(L\setminus\{j\}).                    \tag{1.3}
\]

### Theorem 1.1 (full octahedral kernel)

For an integral signed change (w=(w_{ij})_{i\ne j}), the following are
equivalent.

1. Every lower-colour multiplicity, upper-colour multiplicity, and degree
   of each of the six owners (R+i+j) is preserved.
2. One has

   \[
    w_{ij}=-w_{ji},\qquad
    \sum_{j\ne i}w_{ij}=0\quad(i\in L).               \tag{1.4}
   \]

Thus the local integer kernel is the circulation lattice of (K_4).  It is
generated by oriented triangles.  A minimum-support nonzero squarefree
change is

\[
 q_{ab}+q_{bc}+q_{ca}
 \;\longleftrightarrow\;
 q_{ba}+q_{cb}+q_{ac}.                                 \tag{1.5}
\]

Its old/new overlay is an alternating (C_6).

#### Proof

Lower-colour preservation gives the row equations

\[
 \sum_{j\ne i}w_{ij}=0.                               \tag{1.6}
\]

At owner (R+i+j), degree preservation says

\[
 \sum_{\ell\notin\{i,j\}}(w_{i\ell}+w_{j\ell})=0.   \tag{1.7}
\]

Subtracting (1.7) from the two relevant row equations yields
(w_{ij}+w_{ji}=0).  Conversely, skew-symmetry and the row equations imply
every owner equation.  They also imply each upper-colour equation because

\[
 \sum_{i\ne j}w_{ij}=-\sum_{i\ne j}w_{ji}=0.          \tag{1.8}
\]

This is precisely the integral circulation lattice of (K_4).  Every
nonzero circulation contains a directed cycle, so a squarefree nonzero
change needs at least three positive and three negative directed entries.
An oriented triangle attains that bound.  QED.

### Corollary 1.2 (classification relative to (z))

The triangle atoms have exactly four types.

1. If (z\in R), all six edges are `AA`.
2. If (z\notin R\cup L), all six edges are `BB`.
3. If (z\in L) but is the fourth label outside the active triangle, all
   six edges are cross edges.
4. If (z) is an active triangle label, each shore contains exactly one
   `AA`, one cross, and one `BB` edge.

The first two types preserve the pointwise internal degree on their shore.
The fourth type changes the endpoint/cross assignment and is the new
primitive.

## 2. Pure `AA` tail rotation

Remove (z) from the core in case 1 of Corollary 1.2.  This gives a
rank-((r-2)) old core and four old labels.  Suppose the three old matching
edges lie in three distinct path components of the `AA` forest (P).

Orient old edge (i) from (u_i) to (v_i), and let its deletion leave an
(u_i)-fragment of size (alpha_i) and a (v_i)-fragment of size
(\beta_i).  The opposite octahedral matching can be indexed so that it
joins (v_i) to (u_{i+1}), cyclically.  Hence

\[
 \ell_i=\alpha_i+\beta_i,
 \qquad
 \ell_i'=\beta_i+\alpha_{i+1}.                        \tag{2.1}
\]

### Theorem 2.1 (literal three-tail exchange)

Under the distinct-component hypothesis, every new edge is absent, the
switched `AA` graph is again a spanning nonisolated linear forest, and it
has exactly the same owner degrees and lower/upper (q=1) colour
multiplicities as (P).  Its component lengths are given by (2.1).

#### Proof

Each new edge joins endpoints belonging to two of the three old edges.  If
it were already selected, those two old edges would lie in the same path
component.  Thus all new edges are absent.  Deleting the old edges creates
six path fragments.  The new edges are a matching on these six fragments,
so they form three paths and no cycle.  Formula (2.1) reads their sizes.
The degree and colour claims are Theorem 1.1.  QED.

In particular, choosing

\[
 (\alpha_1,\alpha_2,\alpha_3)=(t,t+1,t)               \tag{2.2}
\]

gives

\[
 (\ell_1,\ell_2,\ell_3)
 \longmapsto
 (\ell_1+1,\ell_2-1,\ell_3).                          \tag{2.3}
\]

If (ell_1<h<\ell_2), this lowers

\[
 D_h(P)=\sum_{C\in\operatorname{comp}(P)}(h-|C|)^+    \tag{2.4}
\]

by exactly one.  The endpoint/internal sets of (P) are unchanged, so a
fixed endpoint service, cross assignment, and `BB` turn section remain
literal valid data.

The same theorem applies to three distinct nontrivial path components of
the `BB` graph (Q).  It cannot repair an isolated (Q)-owner: pure `BB`
circuits preserve the pointwise `BB` degree, and an isolate supplies no old
edge to cut.  The authenticated (Q)-forests have (4,9,22) singleton
components at (k=11,13,15), so pure `AA`/`BB` atoms alone leave at least
(16,36,88) units of the (h=5) split zero-gap curvature.

## 3. The split-active mixed atom

Let

\[
 L=\{z,a,b,c\},\qquad
 A_x=R+z+x,qquad B_{xy}=R+x+y.                       \tag{3.1}
\]

One orientation of (1.5) is the literal exchange

\[
 \boxed{
 \{A_bA_c,\ A_aB_{ac},\ B_{ab}B_{bc}\}
 \longrightarrow
 \{A_aA_c,\ A_bB_{bc},\ B_{ab}B_{ac}\}.}            \tag{3.2}
\]

It moves the (A)-endpoint from (A_a) to (A_b) and the cross load from
(B_{ac}) to (B_{bc}).

### Theorem 3.1 (mixed (C_6) exactness and minimality)

Exchange (3.2) preserves every middle-owner degree and exactly the same
three lower and three upper (q=1) colours.  It is support-minimal among
alternating exact-factor circuits which change the pointwise cross load of
a (B)-owner in a lower-(q=1)-exact factor: no alternating (C_4) can do
so.

#### Proof

The old lower colours are

\[
 R+z,\quad R+a,\quad R+b,                             \tag{3.3}
\]

and the new lower colours are the same three.  The old upper colours are

\[
 R+z+b+c,\quad R+z+a+c,\quad R+a+b+c,                 \tag{3.4}
\]

again equal to the new three.  Each of the six owners is incident with one
old and one new cell edge.  This proves exactness.

For minimality, inspect the number of (A)-owners on an alternating
four-cycle.  With zero or four there is no cross edge.  With two, either all
four edges are cross—so every (B)-owner has one old and one new cross
edge—or one matching is `AA`+`BB` and the other consists of two cross
edges.  The latter changes the number of lower colours containing (z), so
cannot preserve the lower deck.

With one (A)-owner, both matchings consist of one cross and one `BB` edge.
Both cross edges incident with that (A=z+T) have lower colour (T).
Lower-multiset equality therefore forces the two `BB` intersections to be
equal.  The two cross-adjacent (B)-owners are distinct supersets of (T),
so their intersection is (T); the common `BB` intersection must also be
(T).  The old matching would then contain two edges of lower colour (T),
contradicting lower-(q=1) exactness.  With three (A)-owners, the unique
(B)-owner has one old and one new cross edge, so its pointwise cross load
does not change.  Thus support four is impossible, while (3.2) has support
six.  QED.

## 4. Exact paired path action

Assume the old endpoint path through (A_a) is distinct from the `AA` path

\[

\[
 \{\bar d,\bar p+\bar q\}

## 5. Protected composition with three compiler shells

The curvature action is bounded-local.  All-depth witnesses and compiler
pins are not.  The following theorem is the strongest correct composition
statement.

Let (t) owner-support-disjoint octahedral atoms be realized by one
orientation-compatible head permutation.  Let (C) be the set of their
(s=3t) deleted physical transitions.  Put

\[
 h=d+2,\qquad D=d+1.                                   \tag{5.1}
\]

For each compiler depth (q\in\{d-1,d,d+1\}), let (R_q) be the actual
changed source-word/envelope halo: all source positions whose (q)-window
or promoted envelope meets a changed transition, enlarged by one unchanged
envelope column at each boundary.  Let (D_q) be the set of old compiler
targets whose selected pins lie in (R_q), and let (K_q^+) be the final
free physical positions after recomputing the full envelopes.

### Theorem 5.1 (protected octahedral composition)

Assume all of the following.

1. Every inserted edge is absent, squarefree, and Johnson; the final `AA`
   and `BB` shore graphs have the declared forest types.
2. The cuts are (D)-separated on their source components.  Every old
   positive or zero event of length below (h) has its closed event span
   hit, and every inserted seam passes the exact (D)-collar event test.
3. Every required lower target at every depth and every proper
   arbitrary-upper target has either
   * a named old witness whose full edge span avoids (C), or
   * a named final service path whose complete retained/new support is
     fixed.
4. For each (q\in\{d-1,d,d+1\}), the physical core-envelope graph has a
   matching

   \[
    D_q\longrightarrow K_q^+.                         \tag{5.2}
   \]

   All immutable pins outside (R_q) remain fixed.
5. Final monodromy, the safe opening, boundary cells, and terminal suffix
   pins are retained or explicitly replaced.

Then the atom product is a literal (q=1)-exact factor, is (h)-biresident,
retains or replaces every declared all-depth witness, and carries all three
compiler packages (P_{d-1},P_d,P_{d+1}).  The exact crude halo bounds are

\[
 |R_q|,|D_q|\le s(q+2),                                \tag{5.3}
\]

and hence

\[
 \sum_{q=d-1}^{d+1}|D_q|
 \le s(3d+6)=9t(d+2).                                  \tag{5.4}
\]

Disjoint physical halo clusters may be matched independently.  Overlapping
halos must be treated as one Hall instance.

#### Proof

Theorem 1.1 gives degree and (q=1) exactness atom by atom; disjoint support
lets the changes compose.  A named witness avoiding (C) lies wholly in a
retained path fragment, so it remains contiguous, possibly reversed, and
has the same intersection/union flag.  Named final service paths cover the
remaining rows by assumption.

At depth (q), one deleted transition affects at most the (q) crossing
starts and the two promoted boundary-envelope positions.  Taking unions
gives (5.3), and summing at (d-1,d,d+1) gives (5.4).  Delete the pins in
each actual halo and apply the literal compiler Hall matching (5.2); pins
outside the halo are unchanged.  The separated event-span and collar tests
give (h)-biresidence.  The last assumption supplies the global orientation,
opening, and terminal data which local degree equations do not determine.
QED.

### Proposition 5.2 (all-depth nonlocality is intrinsic)

No list of independent bounded atom signatures can by itself certify every
deep witness.  For (q>D), a witness can traverse seams from two atoms.  If
their side bits are (x,y\in\{0,1\}), the witness indicator can be (xy).
There are no constants (a,b,c) such that

\[
 xy=a+bx+cy\quad\text{on }\{0,1\}^2.                  \tag{5.5}
\]

Thus cross-atom deep-witness state contains genuine interaction terms.
Arbitrary-upper accepting paths may also be arbitrarily long.  The global
witness section in Theorem 5.1(3) cannot be deleted from an all-(r)
theorem.

## 6. The exact hereditary implication

Define the global integer curvature potential

\[
 \Psi_h(F)=\sum_x\bigl(\phi^-_{h,x}(F)+\phi^+_{h,x}(F)\bigr).    \tag{6.1}
\]

Call a packet **globally descending** if its fully replayed charge satisfies

\[
 \Delta\Psi_h\le-1.                                    \tag{6.2}
\]

This is stronger than having negative distinguished-coordinate charge.

### Corollary 6.1 (protected octahedral recharge implication)

Suppose that, at every admissible intermediate state with
(Psi_h>0), there exists a globally descending octahedral packet satisfying
Theorem 5.1 and returning an admissible state of the same class.  Then after
at most (Psi_h(F_0)) packets one obtains zero curvature while preserving
the all-depth witness section and all three compiler packages.

#### Proof

(Psi_h) is a nonnegative integer.  Every packet lowers it by at least one,
and the hereditary hypothesis supplies the next packet until it is zero.
All other data persist by Theorem 5.1.  QED.

The existence premise in Corollary 6.1 is the remaining
**protected-octahedral-recharge gate**, abbreviated `POR(r,h,d)`.  It is not
proved here.  Requiring only a split-coordinate normal form would be false:
the authenticated mixed core (2977) has

\[
 \Delta(\phi^-_{5,14},\phi^+_{5,14})=(-1,-1),qquad
 \Delta(\Phi^-_5,\Phi^+_5)=(-1,+1),                   \tag{6.3}
\]

so its total global potential does not fall.
