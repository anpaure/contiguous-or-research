# Independent audit: boundary--core rigidity

## Verdict

The side-capacity lemma, rank-excess stability, and boundary--core rigidity
theorem in the attached note are sound.  The main genuinely new statement is:
at exact rank-count length `b_r(k)`, every literal rank-`r` entry is distinct,
all such entries lie in the two boundary blocks, and the one intervening core
alone covers the complete lower ideal.

This is a useful all-dimensional equality normal form, but it does not prove
existence at `B(k)` and does not change any exact-value bound.

## Proof audit

1. In a segment of length `n=q+t`, witnesses of `q` distinct equal-rank
   targets have increasing left and right endpoints, hence the `i`th witness
   lies in `[i,i+t]`.  Every interval of length at least `t+1` contains one of
   them.  The resulting bound by `F(n,t)` is valid.

2. The superadditivity identity

       F(a+b,u+v)-F(a,u)-F(b,v)=u(b-v)+va >= 0

   is valid in every application because `0<=u<=a` and `0<=v<=b`.

3. After deleting entries above rank `r`, every target of rank at most `r`
   has a witness wholly within one surviving segment.  Assigning the middle
   targets among those segments and applying superadditivity gives exactly
   the rank-excess bound `h<=c`.

4. At equality, delete all literal rank-`r` entries.  If `h` occurrences
   realize only `z<h` distinct masks, the surviving segments have total
   slack `d-h+z<d`, contradicting minimality of `d`.  Hence the occurrences
   are distinct.

5. If the lower-entry positions form `s` nonempty segments, their positive
   slacks `t_j` sum to `d`, so `t_j<=u=d-s+1`.  Convexity gives

       sum C(t_j+1,2) <= C(u+1,2)+s-1.

   Since at least `s-1` deleted rank-`r` positions separate the segments,
   `uh>=s-1`.  The lower ideal would fit at slack `u`; minimality forces
   `u>=d`, hence `s=1`.  This establishes the claimed single core.

No hidden fixed-row, Johnson, or connectivity hypothesis is used.

## Numerical and inherited-consequence audit

Direct exact recomputation confirms:

- `k=11,r=6`: `M=462,d=3,sigma=369`, `b_5(11)=464`, so there is at most
  one literal six-set, and it is an endpoint.
- In the one-six-set branch, deleting it leaves an exact 464-position
  rank-five equality word; its rank-five boundary blocks total at most
  `464-b_4(11)=133` positions.
- The non-singleton six-set witnesses have at most three physical union
  components (`q+c <= 465`).
- In every odd dimension, `d_-<=d_+<=d_-+1`, so an equality at the
  upper-middle rank permits at most one literal upper-middle entry, at an
  endpoint; if `d_+=d_-`, none occurs.
- `k=14,r=7`: `M=3432,d=2,sigma=392`; the two boundary bounds are 429 and
  `floor(392/2)=196`, so the stated 196 bound is correct.

The count of literal active-rank entries was already available from the
rank-truncation theorem in handoff Section 191.  For `k=11`, the exact normal
form "no rank-six entry, or the sole rank-six entry is the first entry after
symmetry" was already established in handoff Items 619--623.  What is new is
the clean all-dimensional proof that all active-rank entries are distinct
boundary entries and that their deletion leaves one lower-ideal core.

## Scope correction

The later sentence that upper masks "must be generated by hulls of those
components and the two boundary blocks" is not a consequence of the theorem.
An upper-rank target can have a short witness occupying a hole of the lower
band, or a longer witness containing only part of a chosen middle-witness
component.  The proved conclusion concerns diagonal entry ranks and the
lower-ideal core, not a complete classification of upper-rank witnesses.

Likewise, when there are no literal rank-`r` entries, the boundary--core form
is formally the whole word as the core.  Thus for the main `k=11` Case I it
does not by itself shrink the factor/search space beyond the already known
rank ceiling.

## Big-picture value

This is a strong necessary equality theorem and is worth adding to the
handoff.  It supports recursive/onion formulations and global SAT cuts, and
shows that active-rank singleton mass cannot be dispersed through an optimal
word.  It does not address the decisive sufficiency issues--joint endpoint
ordering, pin survival, or upper-shadow coverage--so it is structural progress
rather than a resolution of the all-`k` conjecture.
