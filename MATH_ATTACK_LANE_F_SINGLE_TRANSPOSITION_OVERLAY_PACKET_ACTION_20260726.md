# Lane F: one-transposition MSW overlay packets and plateau action

Date: 2026-07-26

Method: pure mathematics only.

## 0. Outcome

Let \(F_m\) be the canonical anchored \(D_m\)-port MSW factor on local
coordinates \([2m]\), with \(\infty\) fixed, and let

\[
                       s_i=(2i\ \ 2i+1).                 \tag{0.1}
\]

Every \(s_i\) preserves the Chung--Feller layers and the Dyck port set.
The ownership overlay of \(F_m\) and \(s_iF_m\) therefore decomposes into
independently switchable anchored packets.

There are three exact conclusions.

1. If \(\kappa_t\) is the phase-owner permutation induced by \(s_i\),
   and

   \[
                         f_t=\kappa_t\kappa_0^{-1},       \tag{0.2}
   \]

   then the overlay components are exactly the orbits of the permutation
   group generated by all \(f_t\).  This includes the interleaved
   \(X\)- and \(Y\)-token phases.

2. For the favorable choice \(s_1=(2\ 3)\), the complete components are
   known.  They are indexed by

   \[
       0\le j\le m-2,\qquad R\in D_{m-j-2},              \tag{0.3}
   \]

   and have exactly

   \[
                         k_j=\operatorname {Cat}_j+
                              \operatorname {Cat}_{j+1}  \tag{0.4}
   \]

   rows on each shore.  At the calibrated scale
   \(r\asymp\log p\), all but the top three component layers in the
   parent factor \(F_{r+1}\) have packet size at most \(p\).  These small
   packets control a \((21/32+o(1))\)-fraction of the distinguished
   \(\operatorname {Cat}_r\) child plateau.

3. A component switch has the exact full target action

   \[
       d_{q,K}(T)=u_{q,K}(s_iT)-u_{q,K}(T),              \tag{0.5}
   \]

   where \(u_{q,K}\) is the canonical target histogram of its row packet.
   Hence every \(s_i\)-target-orbit total is invariant.  For \(s_1\), the
   distinguished child action of component \((j,R)\) is exactly

   \[
                \operatorname {Cat}_j(e_{S_3}-e_{S_2}). \tag{0.6}
   \]

Thus the component choices do split the old Catalan plateau into many
independently selectable chunks; they do not merely move it wholesale.
But all chunks remain in the two-point orbit \(\{S_2,S_3\}\).  Consequently

\[
 \boxed{
   K_p\big|_{\{S_2,S_3\}}
      \ge(\operatorname {Cat}_r-2p)_+.}                  \tag{0.7}
\]

At the working cutoff \(\operatorname {Cat}_r\ge4p\), this is at least
\(2p\).  A single-transposition overlay is therefore a genuine two-bin
splitter, but not a plateau disperser at coefficient-one accuracy.

## 1. Phase-owner orbit theorem

It is useful to state the component construction without relying on a
particular MSW recursion formula.  List the canonical local token layers in
path order as

\[
       \Theta=(X_0,Y_0,X_1,Y_1,\ldots,Y_{m-1},X_m).      \tag{1.1}
\]

For a root \(P\in D_m\), let \(T_t(P)\) be its token in phase \(t\).
Here a \(Y\)-token denotes the corresponding \(\infty\)-containing
odd-graph vertex, equivalently its adjacent-union colour.

Because \(s_i\) preserves each Chung--Feller token layer, there is a
permutation \(\kappa_t\in\operatorname {Sym}(D_m)\) satisfying

\[
                         s_iT_t(P)=T_t(\kappa_tP).        \tag{1.2}
\]

Both coordinate transposition and complementation are involutions, so the
terminal and initial port permutations agree.  Reindex \(s_iF_m\) by its
fixed Dyck ports.  Its token at phase \(t\) on the row rooted at \(P\) is

\[
 s_iT_t(\kappa_0^{-1}P)
   =T_t(\kappa_t\kappa_0^{-1}P)=T_t(f_tP),           \tag{1.3}
\]

where \(f_t\) is (0.2).  In particular \(f_0=f_{2m}=1\).

### Theorem 1.1 (component-orbit characterization)

The root blocks carried by the connected ownership components of
\(F_m\cup s_iF_m\) are exactly the orbits of

\[
                         \mathfrak H_i=\langle f_t:t\in\Theta\rangle
                         \le\operatorname {Sym}(D_m).   \tag{1.4}
\]

Consequently the number of rows on either shore of a component is its
\(\mathfrak H_i\)-orbit size.

#### Proof

At phase zero, the token \(T_0(P)\) joins the canonical row rooted at
\(P\) to the conjugate row rooted at the same \(P\).  Contract these
phase-zero matching edges.  By (1.3), the token \(T_t(f_tP)\) joins the
canonical row \(f_tP\) to the conjugate row \(P\); after contraction this
is the undirected generator edge

\[
                              P\;--\;f_tP.             \tag{1.5}
\]

All overlay edges arise from one token phase, so the contracted connected
components are precisely the orbits generated by (1.5).  Undoing the
phase-zero contractions gives the same root blocks on the two shores.
\(\square\)

This theorem is the exact role of the phase owner permutations.  Looking
only at the \(X\)-phases gives a subgraph and may miss mergers caused by
the \(Y\)-tokens; the full set \(\Theta\) gives the ownership components
needed for an exact odd-factor switch.  Thus the phrase ``preserves every
Chung--Feller layer'' must be read for the full interleaved token ledger.
If only the \(X\)-state layers have been named, one must also introduce the
analogous owner permutations for the \(Y\)-token layers; omitting them can
only refine, and need not equal, the true component partition.

## 2. Exact full target action of one component

Let \(K\subseteq F_m\) be the canonical shore of one ownership component.
For a fixed depth \(q\), write

\[
 u_{q,K}(T)=\sum_{C\in K}v_q(C;T),                      \tag{2.1}
\]

where \(v_q(C;T)\) is the number of rooted depth-\(q\) occurrences of
target \(T\) on row \(C\).

Switching the component replaces \(K\) by \(s_iK\).  Coordinate
equivariance gives

\[
 v_q(s_iC;T)=v_q(C;s_iT).                               \tag{2.2}
\]

### Theorem 2.1 (component target vector)

The complete signed depth-\(q\) action of \(K\) is

\[
 \boxed{
 d_{q,K}(T)=u_{q,K}(s_iT)-u_{q,K}(T).}                  \tag{2.3}
\]

In particular,

\[
 d_{q,K}(s_iT)=-d_{q,K}(T),\qquad
 d_{q,K}(T)=0\quad(s_iT=T),                             \tag{2.4}
\]

and for every two-point orbit

\[
          u_{q,K}(T)+u_{q,K}(s_iT)                      \tag{2.5}
\]

is unchanged by the switch.

#### Proof

Sum (2.2) over \(C\in K\) and subtract the old packet (2.1).  Applying
the involution \(s_i\) once more gives (2.4), and summing the two entries
gives (2.5). \(\square\)

This is the requested full action.  It includes every cyclic arm and every
boundary-crossing window; it is not only the distinguished nested child
window.  It also gives the general norm bounds

\[
 \|d_{q,K}\|_1
   \le2\sum_Tu_{q,K}(T),qquad
 \|d_{q,K}\|_\infty
   \le |K|(2m+1),                                      \tag{2.6}
\]

with the second deliberately crude.  More importantly, (2.4) says that a
target fixed by \(s_i\) is completely invisible to every component choice.

## 3. The complete favorable hierarchy for \(s_1=(2\ 3)\)

For \(s_1\), the canonical component hierarchy is exact.  Define

\[
 \mathcal A_j=
 \{1u0:u\in D_{j+1}\}
 \ \dot\cup\
 \{10\,1v0:v\in D_j\}.                                \tag{3.1}
\]

For \(0\le j\le m-2\) and \(R\in D_{m-j-2}\), put

\[
                         \mathcal C_{j,R}
                            =\{AR:A\in\mathcal A_j\}.  \tag{3.2}
\]

### Theorem 3.1 (exact component hierarchy)

The sets \(\mathcal C_{j,R}\) are exactly the canonical root blocks of
the ownership overlay \(F_m\cup s_1F_m\).  For each \(j\), there are

\[
                         \operatorname {Cat}_{m-j-2}    \tag{3.3}
\]

components, each with

\[
                         k_j=\operatorname {Cat}_j+
                              \operatorname {Cat}_{j+1} \tag{3.4}
\]

rows on either shore.

The size identity

\[
 \sum_{j=0}^{m-2}\operatorname {Cat}_{m-j-2}k_j
      =\operatorname {Cat}_m                              \tag{3.5}
\]

checks that no roots are omitted.

#### Proof

This is the suffix-local component theorem for the MSW comparison by
\((2\ 3)\), proved in `MSW_BOUNDARY_CONNECTIVITY.md` and
`MSW_COMPONENT_HIERARCHY_REDUCTION.md` and recorded in
`MSW_TRANSPOSITION_COMPONENTS.md`, Section 5.  In phase-owner language,
the orbit of a root is obtained by retaining the final Dyck suffix \(R\)
and moving within the initial atom \(\mathcal A_j\).  Boundary connectivity
of the MSW path factor makes the action transitive on \(\mathcal A_j\);
suffix locality prevents an edge from changing \(R\).  Hence these are
exactly the orbits in Theorem 1.1.  Their sizes and counts follow from
(3.1)--(3.2), and (3.5) is the Catalan convolution identity. \(\square\)

For a general \(s_i\), Theorem 1.1 remains an exact component
characterization.  The closed formula (3.2)--(3.4) is special to the
boundary transposition \(s_1\); no unsupported all-\(i\) equality is used
below.  Since the construction needs one favorable transposition, \(s_1\)
is sufficient.

## 4. Packet sizes at the calibrated scale

Let \(r\) be minimal with

\[
                         \operatorname {Cat}_r\ge4p.    \tag{4.1}
\]

Then \(r=\Theta(\log p)\) and

\[
                    4p\le\operatorname {Cat}_r<16p.     \tag{4.2}
\]

To act on the aligned \(\operatorname {Cat}_r\) child fibre, use the
parent factor \(F_{r+1}\).  Its \(s_1\)-components have

\[
 0\le j\le r-1,qquad
 \#\mathcal C_j=\operatorname {Cat}_{r-j-1},qquad
 |\mathcal C_j|=k_j.                                  \tag{4.3}
\]

The component sizes increase with \(j\).  For every \(j\le r-4\),

\[
 k_j\le k_{r-4}
   =\operatorname {Cat}_{r-4}+\operatorname {Cat}_{r-3}
   =\left(\frac5{256}+o(1)\right)\operatorname {Cat}_r
   <p                                                       \tag{4.4}
\]

for all sufficiently large \(r\), by (4.2).  Thus only the top three
layers \(j=r-3,r-2,r-1\) are discarded in a uniform \(\le p\) packet
guarantee.

The largest retained distinguished chunk is even smaller:

\[
             \operatorname {Cat}_{r-4}
             =\left(\frac1{256}+o(1)\right)
                 \operatorname {Cat}_r
             <\left(\frac1{16}+o(1)\right)p.          \tag{4.5}
\]

The discarded distinguished mass is

\[
 \operatorname {Cat}_{r-1}
 +\operatorname {Cat}_{r-2}
 +2\operatorname {Cat}_{r-3}
 =\left(\frac{11}{32}+o(1)\right)\operatorname {Cat}_r. \tag{4.6}
\]

Hence components of size at most \(p\) control

\[
 \boxed{
    \left(\frac{21}{32}+o(1)\right)\operatorname {Cat}_r} \tag{4.7}
\]

distinguished plateau occurrences.  This is a positive-density small-packet
reservoir; a maximum-component bound over the entire overlay would hide it.

For reference, the absolute largest component has

\[
 k_{r-1}=\operatorname {Cat}_{r-1}+\operatorname {Cat}_r
        =\left(\frac54+o(1)\right)\operatorname {Cat}_r
        <(20+o(1))p.                                  \tag{4.8}
\]

Thus even the full overlay has \(O(p)\), not Catalan-uncontrolled, packets;
the retained menu has the required \(\le p\) bound.

## 5. Distinguished child action component by component

Inside a size-\((r+1)\) parent, the aligned right-child fibre consists of
the roots

\[
                         10v,\qquad v\in D_r,            \tag{5.1}
\]

and has size \(\operatorname {Cat}_r\).  Its depth-\(r\) child window is
the noninitial phase interval.  In the canonical factor every such row has
local target

\[
                              S_2=O\cup\{2\},            \tag{5.2}
\]

where \(O\) is the fixed exterior set of the ambient context.

In component \(\mathcal C_{j,R}\), the roots belonging to (5.1) are
exactly

\[
                       10\,1v0\,R,\qquad v\in D_j.       \tag{5.3}
\]

There are \(\operatorname {Cat}_j\) of them.  Applying \(s_1\) moves
their target to

\[
                              S_3=O\cup\{3\}.            \tag{5.4}
\]

Therefore:

### Theorem 5.1 (exact distinguished component vector)

Switching component \((j,R)\) has distinguished child action

\[
 \boxed{
       d^{\rm child}_{j,R}
         =\operatorname {Cat}_j(e_{S_3}-e_{S_2}).}       \tag{5.5}
\]

Summing its negative masses over every component gives

\[
 \sum_{j=0}^{r-1}\operatorname {Cat}_{r-j-1}
                         \operatorname {Cat}_j
 =\operatorname {Cat}_r,                                \tag{5.6}
\]

exactly the whole plateau.

#### Proof

The second family in (3.1) is precisely (5.3).  In the deletion--insertion
normal form, every root beginning with `10` first deletes coordinate one
and inserts coordinate two.  Its noninitial full-child window therefore
has intersection \(\{2\}\), with \(O\) adjoined.  Coordinate relabelling
by \((2\ 3)\) carries this target to (5.4).  Equation (2.3) gives the
signed vector, and Catalan convolution gives (5.6). \(\square\)

This proves genuine fragmentation: different components may independently
move chunks of sizes \(\operatorname {Cat}_j\).  In particular the small
packet menu of Section 4 moves more than half of the old plateau in chunks
of size at most \((1/16+o(1))p\).

## 6. Why this is still relocation on the target scale

Let \(I\) be any chosen component set.  On the distinguished child
occurrences, put

\[
 a_I=\sum_{(j,R)\in I}\operatorname {Cat}_j.             \tag{6.1}
\]

Then Theorem 5.1 gives exactly

\[
 \mu_I^{\rm child}(S_2)=\operatorname {Cat}_r-a_I,
 \qquad
 \mu_I^{\rm child}(S_3)=a_I.                            \tag{6.2}
\]

No third target receives any distinguished mass.  Hence

\[
\begin{aligned}
 & (\mu_I^{\rm child}(S_2)-p)_+
  +(\mu_I^{\rm child}(S_3)-p)_+\\
 &\hspace{35mm}\ge
       (\operatorname {Cat}_r-2p)_+.                    \tag{6.3}
\end{aligned}

This lower bound is independent of the packet sizes and of how accurately
the subset sum \(a_I\) balances the two bins.  Under (4.1), it is at least
\(2p\).

More generally, Theorem 2.1 gives the same obstruction on every target
orbit of \(s_i\): a fixed target cannot change at all, and a two-point
orbit can only redistribute its conserved total between two bins.  Thus:

* **owner scale:** the plateau is genuinely split among many independent
  components;
* **target scale:** the operation is only a two-orbit relocation.

The first fact is useful for packetization, but the second is fatal for
removing a \(\operatorname {Cat}_r\ge4p\) cap plateau.  To reach cap
\(p\), one needs a library whose generated coordinate action exposes at
least

\[
                         \left\lceil
                           \operatorname {Cat}_r/p
                         \right\rceil\ge4              \tag{6.4}
\]

effective target bins, together with components small enough to balance
them.  One transposition supplies only two.

## 7. Scope

The phase-owner theorem applies to every flaw-preserving \(s_i\).  The
explicit hierarchy, calibrated packet bounds, and componentwise Catalan
action in Sections 3--6 use the fully proved \((2\ 3)\) component theorem.
No general-\(i\) hierarchy is assumed.

The full vector (2.3) is the exact answer for all lower and upper targets.
The distinguished formula (5.5) is only one projection of it.  Other
cyclic arms can add collateral elsewhere, but they cannot violate the
two-point-orbit census (2.5), and nonnegative collateral cannot remove the
cap floor (6.3) carried by the distinguished plateau itself.

Therefore the sharper overlay route succeeds at producing many packets
of size at most \(p\), but fails as a one-transposition dispersion theorem.
The next viable object is a multi-conjugate component library whose target
orbits have size at least four (and preferably grow), not a connectivity
proof for the one-transposition face.
