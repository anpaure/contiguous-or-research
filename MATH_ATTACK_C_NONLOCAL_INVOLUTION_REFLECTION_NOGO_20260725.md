Put

B=\operatorname {Cat}_m=\frac Wn,
\qquad H=\lceil L\sqrt m\rceil,

\[
r_q=m-q,\qquad N_q=\binom n{r_q},\qquad
c_q=\left\lfloor\frac W{N_q}\right\rfloor,

\[
\mathcal Q_H(F)
=\sum_{q=1}^H\frac1{c_q}
 \sum_{S\in\binom{[n]}{r_q}}
 (\mu_q^F(S)-c_q)(\mu_q^F(S)-c_q-1).
\tag{0.1}
\]

Let \(\mathcal I_m\) be the conjugacy class of fixed-point involutions,
that is, permutations of cycle type \(1\,2^m\).  The main theorem is the
following exact prescribed-permutation obstruction.

\boxed{\gamma_\sigma(F_{m,\sigma})=0.}
\tag{0.6}

There is also a nontrivial version, so the obstruction is not merely a

Thus even after singleton stabilizers are excluded, no prescribed

simultaneous involution comparison, cannot be a prescribed universal bypass:

\forall L>0\ \exists m_0(L)\ \forall m\ge m_0(L)\
\forall\sigma\in\mathcal I_m\ \exists F_{m,\sigma}

are coupled into one coordinate group.

> **Theorem B (exact compilation of a transposition-generated group
> packet).**  Let \(E\) be any set of coordinate transpositions,
> \(\Gamma=\langle E\rangle\), and let \(F\) be any exact factor.  Form the

Consequently a nonlocal group-Haar phase does not remove multistep cost; it
only packages it.  In particular, the AB8 menu lock applies unchanged to
every group generated by its menu, and the AB8 escape-depth lower bound

## 1. The MSW reflection identity

Let \(\mathcal D_m\) denote the Dyck words of semilength \(m\), and put

\[
\mu(w)=\overline{\operatorname {rev}(w)}.
\tag{1.1}
\]

The map \(\mu\) is an involution of \(\mathcal D_m\).  For
\(w\in\mathcal D_m\), let

\[
\rho(w)=(\rho_1(w),\ldots,\rho_{2m}(w))
\]

be the MSW flip-position permutation.  The omitted-label cyclic word of
the corresponding wreath is

\[
\widehat\rho(w)=(\rho(w),n).
\tag{1.2}
\]

We use the two exact MSW recurrences

\[
\rho(PQ)=\rho(P)\mathbin\Vert(|P|+\rho(Q))
\tag{1.3}
\]

for Dyck words \(P,Q\), and, for a primitive word \(1u0\) of length
\(2a\),

\[
\rho(1u0)=
\bigl(2a,\ 2a-\rho(\mu(u)),\ 1\bigr).
\tag{1.4}
\]

Subtraction and addition in these formulas are entrywise.

For a permutation word on \([2a]\), define

\[
s_a(i)=2a+1-i
\tag{1.5}
\]

entrywise.

### Lemma 1.1 (exact flip-list reflection)

For every \(a\ge1\) and every \(w\in\mathcal D_a\),

\[
\boxed{
s_a(\rho(w))=\operatorname {rev}(\rho(\mu(w))).}
\tag{1.6}
\]

#### Proof

We induct on \(a\), simultaneously allowing a decomposition into primitive
Dyck components.

First suppose \(w=1u0\) is primitive.  Formula (1.4) gives

\[
\rho(w)=
(2a,\ 2a-\rho(\mu(u)),\ 1),
\]

whereas \(\mu(w)=1\mu(u)0\), so

\[
\rho(\mu(w))=
(2a,\ 2a-\rho(u),\ 1).
\]

Therefore

\[
s_a(\rho(w))
=(1,\ 1+\rho(\mu(u)),\ 2a),
\tag{1.7}
\]

while

\[
\operatorname {rev}(\rho(\mu(w)))
=(1,\ 2a-\operatorname {rev}(\rho(u)),\ 2a).
\tag{1.8}
\]

Apply the inductive identity to the Dyck word \(\mu(u)\), whose length is
\(2a-2\).  Since \(\mu^2(u)=u\), it says

\[
(2a-1)-\rho(\mu(u))=\operatorname {rev}(\rho(u)).
\]

Rearranging gives

\[
1+\rho(\mu(u))
=2a-\operatorname {rev}(\rho(u)),
\]

so (1.7) and (1.8) agree.  The case \(a=1\), where
\(\rho(10)=(2,1)\), is immediate.

For a general word, write its primitive decomposition as

\[
w=P_1P_2\cdots P_t.
\]

Then

\[
\mu(w)=\mu(P_t)\cdots\mu(P_2)\mu(P_1).
\]

By (1.3), \(\rho(w)\) is the concatenation of the locally shifted lists
\(\rho(P_i)\).  Global reflection \(s_a\) reverses the order of their
coordinate intervals.  On a block of length \(d\), starting after \(b\)
coordinates, it sends the entry \(b+x\) to

\[
2a+1-b-x
=(2a-b-d)+(d+1-x),
\]

which is exactly the new block offset followed by the local reflection.
The primitive case therefore gives (1.6) block by block.  This completes
the induction. \(\square\)

Define the coordinate involution

\[
\sigma_0(i)=n-i\quad(1\le i\le2m),
\qquad \sigma_0(n)=n.
\tag{1.9}
\]

It has cycle type \(1\,2^m\).  Lemma 1.1 and (1.2) give

\[
\sigma_0\widehat\rho(w)
=\bigl(\operatorname {rev}(\rho(\mu(w))),n\bigr).
\tag{1.10}
\]

The right side is the reversal, with the origin again placed after \(n\),
of the cyclic word \(\widehat\rho(\mu(w))\).  Wreaths are unoriented cyclic
orders, so (1.10) proves

\[
\boxed{\sigma_0 C_w=C_{\mu(w)}.}
\tag{1.11}
\]

Since \(\mu\) permutes \(\mathcal D_m\), the canonical MSW factor

\[
F_m^{\rm MSW}=\{C_w:w\in\mathcal D_m\}
\]

satisfies

\[
\boxed{\sigma_0F_m^{\rm MSW}=F_m^{\rm MSW}.}
\tag{1.12}
\]

This is setwise invariance of the literal rows, not an equality of a signed
or fractional shadow profile.

## 2. Proof of Theorem A

The canonical MSW factor is a corner of its full intrinsic
\((2\ 3)\)-component cell.  The audited private-pair floor theorem for that
cell states that every corner \(F\) satisfies

\[
\mathcal Q_H(F)
\ge
2W\left(
\frac{4^H}{2048K_LnH^4}-1
\right).
\tag{2.1}
\]

This is the exact integer floor polynomial (0.1): the constants in (2.1)
already include \(c_H=\lfloor W/N_H\rfloor\), the adjacent-integer floor,
and the Catalan private-pair estimate.  Applying (2.1) to
\(F_m^{\rm MSW}\) and using (1.12) proves Theorem A for \(\sigma_0\).

Every \(\sigma\in\mathcal I_m\) is conjugate to \(\sigma_0\).  Choose
\(h\in S_n\) with

\[
\sigma=h\sigma_0h^{-1}
\]

and put

\[
F_{m,\sigma}=hF_m^{\rm MSW}.
\tag{2.2}
\]

Coordinate relabelling preserves exact factorhood and \(\mathcal Q_H\).
Moreover,

\[
\sigma F_{m,\sigma}
=h\sigma_0F_m^{\rm MSW}
=hF_m^{\rm MSW}
=F_{m,\sigma}.
\]

This proves (0.2)--(0.4).

Since \(B=W/n\), division of (0.3) by \(HB\) gives

\[
\frac{\mathcal Q_H(F_{m,\sigma})}{HB}
\ge
\frac{4^H}{1024K_LH^5}-\frac{2n}{H},
\tag{2.3}
\]

whose right side tends to infinity for \(H=\lceil L\sqrt m\rceil\).
This proves (0.5).

Finally, \(F_{m,\sigma}=\sigma F_{m,\sigma}\), so cancellation in the
binary comparison leaves no old or new row.  In the intrinsic owner graph,
the owner of \(\sigma T\) is \(\sigma\) times the owner of \(T\); hence
each owner component is itself \(\sigma\)-invariant and has only one
physical phase.  Both nonlocal cells are singletons.  Their best exact gain
is zero, proving (0.6). \(\square\)

### 2.1 A row-Lipschitz lemma for the exact floor

We first record the exact estimate used for the nontrivial construction.

### Lemma 2.1 (exact row-distance oscillation)

Suppose exact factors \(F,G\) satisfy

\[
|F\setminus G|=|G\setminus F|\le a.
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
|\mathcal Q_H(G)-\mathcal Q_H(F)|
\le 2anH(2B+a+3).}
\tag{2.4}
\]

#### Proof

At depth \(q\), write

\[
\delta(S)=\mu_q^G(S)-\mu_q^F(S).
\]

Every row contributes exactly \(n\) cyclic rank-\(r_q\) intervals.  Hence

\[
\sum_S|\delta(S)|\le2an,
\qquad |\delta(S)|\le a.
\tag{2.5}
\]

For \(h_c(x)=(x-c)(x-c-1)\), direct expansion gives

\[
h_c(x+d)-h_c(x)=2d(x-c)+d(d-1).
\tag{2.6}
\]

Here \(0\le x\le B\) and \(c=c_q\ge1\).  Therefore

\[
\frac{|h_c(x+d)-h_c(x)|}{c}
\le
2|d|\left(\frac Bc+1\right)
+\frac{|d|(|d|+1)}c
\le(2B+a+3)|d|.
\tag{2.7}
\]

Sum (2.7), use (2.5), and then sum over \(q\le H\). \(\square\)

### 2.2 Proof of Theorem A\(^+\)

First take \(\sigma=\sigma_0\), put

\[
r=m-2,\qquad R=1^r0^r\in\mathcal D_r,
\qquad \tau=(2\ \ 3),
\tag{2.8}
\]

and note that \(\mu(R)=R\).  The exact contextual MSW component theorem at
the first boundary supplies a genuine two-old/two-new \(\tau\)-component
\(K\), whose old rows are indexed by

\[
1100R,\qquad1010R.
\tag{2.9}
\]

Put

\[
\tau'=\sigma_0\tau\sigma_0=(2m-2\ \ 2m-1).
\tag{2.10}
\]

Since \(1100\) and \(1010\) are fixed by \(\mu\), equation (1.11) maps
\(K\) to the genuine terminal-boundary \(\tau'\)-component
\(K'=\sigma_0K\), whose old rows are indexed by

\[
R1100,\qquad R1010.
\tag{2.11}
\]

For \(m\ge5\), one has \(r\ge3\).  Both words in (2.11) begin with
\(111\), whereas the two words in (2.9) begin with \(110\) and \(101\),
respectively.  Thus the old-row sides \(K,K'\) are disjoint.  Since the
rows of the canonical exact factor \(F_m^{\rm MSW}\) have pairwise disjoint
middle ownership,
their middle roots \(U_K,U_{K'}\) are disjoint as well.

\[
K'=\sigma_0K,
\qquad U_K\cap U_{K'}=\varnothing.
\tag{2.12}
\]

Let \(F_0=F_m^{\rm MSW}\), and switch precisely \(K\) in its
\(\tau\)-cell:

\[
\widetilde F=F_0^K.
\tag{2.13}
\]

This is a literal exact factor and differs from \(F_0\) by two old and two
new rows.  Equations (1.12) and (2.10)--(2.11) give

\[
\sigma_0\widetilde F=F_0^{K'}.
\tag{2.14}
\]

Indeed, \(\sigma_0(\tau K)=\tau'K'\); no commutation assumption is needed.
The two factors in (2.13)--(2.14) are distinct.  After their common rows are
cancelled, their comparison overlay has exactly two connected components:
on the root of \(K\), the sides are \(\tau K,K\); on the root of \(K'\),
the sides are \(K',\tau'K'\).  The involution \(\sigma_0\)
interchanges these two components.  Hence they form one component orbit,
so the bundled involution-Haar cell has one active packet.  The equivalent
intrinsic owner-group packet is the union of their two roots.  Its two
phases are exactly \(\widetilde F\) and \(\sigma_0\widetilde F\), whose
energies agree by coordinate invariance.

Apply Lemma 2.1 with \(a=2\), and then apply (2.1) to \(F_0\).  This gives

\[
\mathcal Q_H(\widetilde F)
\ge
2W\left(\frac{4^H}{2048K_LnH^4}-1\right)
-8nBH-20nH,
\]

which is (0.6b).

The unbundled comparison cube independently chooses the two sides on the
roots of \(K,K'\), so it has four states.  Any two of those states differ
by at most four removed and four inserted rows.  Lemma 2.1 with \(a=4\)
therefore bounds every possible gain between two cube states by

\[
8nH(2B+7)=16nBH+56nH.
\]

On dividing (0.6b) by \(HB\), the exponential term in (2.3) still
dominates the additional \(8n+20n/B\).  Thus (0.6c) follows.

Finally conjugate the entire construction by an \(h\in S_n\) satisfying
\(\sigma=h\sigma_0h^{-1}\).  Exactness, row distance, comparison
components, and \(\mathcal Q_H\) are all preserved.  This proves Theorem
A\(^+\). \(\square\)

## 3. Exact compilation of group packets

Let \(E\) and \(\Gamma=\langle E\rangle\) be as in Theorem B.  For a
\(\Gamma\)-owner component \(K\), let

\[
U_K=\bigcup_{C\in K}\mathcal W_m(C)
\tag{3.1}
\]

be its middle root.  By the owner-component definition,

\[
gU_K=U_K\qquad(g\in\Gamma).
\tag{3.2}
\]

Choose a shortest word

\[
g_K=e_{K,\ell_K}\cdots e_{K,2}e_{K,1},
\qquad e_{K,i}\in E,
\qquad \ell_K=\ell_E(g_K).
\tag{3.3}
\]

Hold every other root fixed.  Suppose the current rows over \(U_K\) are
\(P\).  They factor \(U_K\), while the residual rows factor its complement.
For \(e\in E\), (3.2) says that both \(U_K\) and its complement are
\(e\)-invariant.  Therefore no freshly recomputed \(e\)-ownership component
crosses the cut \(U_K/U_K^c\).  Switching all components contained in
\(U_K\), and no others, is **one** legal complete component cut in the AB8
sense: one cut may choose an arbitrary subset of the freshly recomputed
components of a single transposition cell.  Rows common to \(P\) and
\(eP\) are already fixed and require no component choice.  The cut replaces
\(P\) by \(eP\) and leaves every other root unchanged.

Apply this operation successively for
\(e_{K,1},\ldots,e_{K,\ell_K}\).  It replaces \(K\) by \(g_KK\) in
exactly \(\ell_K\) ordinary cuts.  Then process the distinct roots one at a
time.  Their disjointness and common \(\Gamma\)-invariance ensure that all
previously processed roots remain fixed.  The total number of cuts is at
most

\[
\sum_K\ell_K=D(\mathbf g),
\]

and the final factor is \(\bigcup_Kg_KK\).  This proves Theorem B. \(\square\)

If an AB8 cage menu \(\mathcal M_s\) contains \(E\), every compiled stage
above is a menu-supported freshly recomputed component cut.  The exact AB8
menu-minimizer theorem therefore gives

\[
\mathfrak F_L(F_{\mathbf g})
\ge\mathfrak F_L(G_{m,s}).
\tag{3.4}
\]

If arbitrary colours are allowed and a compiled child has
\(\mathfrak F_L(F_{\mathbf g})\le CW\), the exact AB8 escape-depth theorem
gives

\[
D(\mathbf g)
\ge
2(H-s)-10-
\log_2\!\left(
K_L nH^4\left(C+1+\frac1{2n}\right)
\right).
\tag{3.5}
\]

Thus a macro phase of small displayed depth may hide, but cannot erase, the
literal transposition-cut cost.

## 4. What happens when the giant-shield colours are coupled

Let \(E\) be any set of transpositions and let \(G_E\) be the simple graph
on \([n]\) having edge set \(E\).  If its connected components are
\(C_1,\ldots,C_r\), then the elementary edge-transposition theorem gives

\[
\boxed{
\langle E\rangle
=S_{C_1}\times\cdots\times S_{C_r}.}
\tag{4.1}
\]

Indeed, the edge transpositions of a spanning tree generate the full
symmetric group on its vertices, and different graph components have
disjoint supports.

Consequently all \(|E|-(n-r)\) edges outside a spanning forest add no new
coordinate-group freedom.  If \(E\) is the active-shield family forced by
the growing-harmonic C theorem, then

\[
|E|
\ge
\left(\frac{3\gamma L}{4}+o_{L,\gamma}(1)\right)
\frac{m^{3/2}}{\log(108m)},
\tag{4.2}
\]

so all but at most \(n-1=O(m)\) of these shield colours are redundant as
coordinate-group generators.

If \(G_E\) is connected, (4.1) gives \(\Gamma=S_n\).  This group is
transitive on the middle layer.  For any two rows \(C,D\in F\), choose a
middle set owned by each and a permutation carrying the first set to the
second.  The definition of the \(\Gamma\)-owner graph then directly joins
\(C\) and \(D\).  Hence the owner graph has one component.  Its only phases
are the global images \(gF\), and

\[
\mathcal Q_H(gF)=\mathcal Q_H(F)
\qquad(g\in S_n).
\tag{4.3}
\]

If \(G_E\) is disconnected, its orbits on middle sets are exactly the
intersection-count strata

\[
\left\{T\in\binom{[n]}m:
|T\cap C_i|=a_i\ (1\le i\le r)
\right\},
\qquad \sum_i a_i=m.
\tag{4.4}
\]

Every owner root is a union of these strata.  Thus the alternative to the
one-packet collapse is an exact orbit-root conservation law, not free
independent use of the superlinear shield list.

This gives the sharp group-coupling boundary.  A productive nonlocal bypass
must use an adaptively selected permutation not fixing the current factor,
must retain at least two genuinely active owner packets, and must prove that
their common multidepth residual is smaller than the coherent displacement.
Neither giant component size nor the number of shield colours supplies that
residual inequality.  The adaptive statement

\[
\forall F\text{ with }\mathcal Q_H(F)\gg HB\quad
\exists\sigma\in\mathcal I_m:\ \gamma_\sigma(F)>0
\tag{4.5}
\]

remains open.  No constant-one theorem is claimed.

## 5. Self-audit and exact boundary

1. **Reflection recurrence.**  Formula (1.4) is the primitive specialization
   of the exact MSW recursion
   \[
   \rho(1u0v)=
   (d,\ d-\rho(\mu(u)),\ 1,\ d+\rho(v)),
   \qquad d=|u|+2.
   \]
   The proof of Lemma 1.1 uses \(\mu(u)\), not plain reversal of \(u\).
   The inner reflection has size \(2a-2\), which is why its entrywise
   complement is \((2a-1)-x\); this audits the two constants in (1.7)--(1.8).

2. **Unoriented-row convention.**  The MSW list \(\widehat\rho(w)\) is the
   omitted-label encoding of the wreath.  Its rank windows use the step-two
   cyclic order, but rotation and reversal of the omitted-label list induce
   the same unoriented wreath.  Hence (1.10) really is equality of literal
   wreath rows, not merely equality of their middle incidence columns.

3. **Singleton owner cell.**  If \(C\in F_{m,\sigma}\) owns a middle set
   \(T\), then \(\sigma C\) owns \(\sigma T\).  The owner-graph edge for
   \(T\) directly joins \(C\) to \(\sigma C\).  Thus every owner component
   \(K\) satisfies \(\sigma K=K\), so every nominal phase selects the same
   physical row set.  No hidden nontrivial packet survives cancellation.

4. **Nontrivial reflected packet.**  Mirror reversal sends a contextual
   index \((P,R)\) to \((\mu(R),\mu(P))\), because it reverses the three
   Dyck blocks and fixes both four-letter gadgets.  Thus the first-boundary
   colour \((2\ 3)\) is carried to the terminal-boundary colour
   \((2m-2\ 2m-1)\).  No commutation is used.  The explicit mountain word
   \(R=1^{m-2}0^{m-2}\) makes the two old row pairs disjoint for \(m\ge5\),
   which in the exact factor makes their middle roots disjoint.  These facts
   audit (2.10)--(2.14) and exclude an additional reduced component.

5. **Raw-cube oscillation.**  Lemma 2.1 uses only the exact facts that a
   row has \(n\) intervals at every retained rank, a target load is at most
   \(B\), and \(c_q\ge1\).  The four raw corners differ by at most four
   rows on either side, giving \(8nH(2B+7)=16nBH+56nH\) with no asymptotic
   suppression inserted into the proof.

6. **Cut count in Theorem B.**  The count is one cut per letter per root,
   not one cut per connected component.  At that stage all components inside
   the chosen root are selected as one arbitrary subset of the current
   transposition cell.  Therefore the proved bound is exactly
   \(\sum_K\ell_E(g_K)\).  Different roots carrying the same next letter
   could be processed together, so this is an upper bound and never an
   undercount.

7. **Floor and asymptotic.**  The energy in (0.3) is the exact unhalved
   polynomial (0.1).  No fractional variance is substituted.  Equation
   (2.3) follows exactly from \(B=W/n\), and its first term is
   \(\exp((L\log4+o_L(1))\sqrt m)\) divided by a fixed polynomial, so it
   dominates \(2n/H=O_L(\sqrt m)\).

8. **Quantifier boundary.**  Theorems A and A\(^+\) establish
   \(\forall\sigma\ \exists F\) high exact traps.  They therefore refute a
   universal theorem for any fixed prescribed \(\sigma\), as well as a
   uniform statement with order \(\forall\sigma\ \forall F\).  They do not
   disprove an adaptive selector with order
   \(\forall F\ \exists\sigma\).  Theorem B likewise compiles
   group-owner phases but does not prohibit an oracle-scale comparison
   circuit outside the transposition-generated owner family.  These two
   adaptive possibilities are the precise unclosed boundary.
