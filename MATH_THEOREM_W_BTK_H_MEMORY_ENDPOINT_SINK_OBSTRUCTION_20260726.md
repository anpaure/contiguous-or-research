# The natural BTK product-endpoint state is an exact \(H\)-memory sink

Date: 2026-07-26

Method: pure mathematics only. No computation, finite search, solver, or
web input is used.

## 0. The exact outcome

Let \(A\mathbin{\dot\cup}B\) be a partition of the \(2m\) physical
coordinates into two \(m\)-sets. Put the standard
de Bruijn--Tengbergen--Kruyswijk/Greene--Kleitman symmetric-chain
decomposition on each half and partition the middle layer into the complete
rank-\(m\) product-diagonal paths.

Let \(P\) be one such path, of length

\[
                             h=m-2r.
\tag{0.1}
\]

If

\[
                             h\ge3,\qquad H\ge7,
\tag{0.2}
\]

then the natural two-sided \(H\)-memory state obtained after traversing
\(P\), in either orientation, admits no continuation consisting of one
Johnson endpoint seam followed immediately by another complete product
path. Thus both orientations of \(P\) are sinks in the exact state-expanded
endpoint graph.

This is not a small-degree phenomenon. A concrete such endpoint has at
least

\[
                    2r(m-r)=\frac{m^2-h^2}{2}
\tag{0.3}
\]

Johnson-adjacent product endpoints. More generally every BTK product atom
at level \(r\) has at least \(r^2=(m-h)^2/4\) raw cross-half endpoint
neighbours in the outward direction. The stateful degree nevertheless
equals zero.

Now take

\[
 H=\lfloor\sqrt m\,\omega\rfloor,
 \qquad \omega\longrightarrow\infty,
 \qquad H=o(m).
\tag{0.4}
\]

Of the

\[
 c_m^2,\qquad c_m=\binom m{\lfloor m/2\rfloor},
\tag{0.5}
\]

product atoms, all but \(O(c_m^2/m)=o(W/H)\), where
\(W=\binom{2m}{m}\), satisfy (0.2). Consequently every state-valid cover
formed from complete atoms and direct endpoint seams has

\[
 p\ge c_m^2-O(c_m^2/m)
  =\left(\frac2{\sqrt\pi}+o(1)\right)\frac W{\sqrt m}.
\tag{0.6}
\]

Allowing an owner leave \(u=o(W/H)\) changes the right side by at most
\(\lfloor u/4\rfloor\); see Corollary 6.1.

This is a factor \(\Theta(\omega)\) larger than the required
\(o(W/H)\) component scale. Within the fixed-colour complete-atom
catalogue, pairing a selected route with a forced complement/reversal copy
does not help because both physical orientations are sinks. Allowing the
next atom itself to come from the complementary SCD colour is a mixed-colour
operation and lies outside this statement.

The scope is exact. This closes the proposed route for one fixed BTK
product colour, complete unsplit atoms, and immediate endpoint seams. It
does not rule out a genuinely mixed-SCD-colour transition, splitting an
atom, or inserting an exterior-moving cooldown/recoding connector. Those
operations change the active-word catalogue used below.

## 1. Exact memory convention

Write a Johnson transition as

\[
                        X_t\longrightarrow X_{t+1}=X_t-a_t+b_t.
\tag{1.1}
\]

A route has correct lower and upper ranks through depth \(H\) if and only
if no physical coordinate occurs in two distinct edge supports inside an
\(H\)-edge window. Equivalently, immediately before edge \(t\), retain the
labels used by edges

\[
                         t-H+1,\ldots,t-1.
\tag{1.2}
\]

The new support \(\{a_t,b_t\}\) must be disjoint from that age-labelled
queue. In directed form, an insertion at edge \(i\) followed by a removal
of the same coordinate at edge \(j>i\) is illegal exactly when

\[
                              j-i+1\le H.
\tag{1.3}
\]

This is why an endpoint graph without the full recent history is
insufficient. The theorem below uses the history actually generated by
the preceding product atom, not an adversarial formal queue.

For comparison, if the live queue has \(s\le H-1\) edges, its recently
inserted labels are \(s\) distinct members of the current owner and its
recently removed labels are \(s\) distinct members of the complement.
Therefore the unrestricted Johnson automaton has the exact outdegree and
indegree

\[
                              (m-s)^2.
\tag{1.4}
\]

At a full queue this is \((m-H+1)^2\). Thus finite memory does not reduce
the ambient state graph appreciably. The collapse below is entirely due
to requiring the successor to be a complete endpoint atom of the same
fixed SCD colour.

## 2. BTK chains and record-time active sets

Encode a half-set by a word \(w=w_1\cdots w_m\in\{0,1\}^m\). Greedily
pair each \(1\) with the most recent still-unpaired \(0\). After deleting
paired positions, the remaining word is

\[
                              1^p0^{s-p}.
\tag{2.1}
\]

Fixing the paired coordinates and replacing (2.1) successively by

\[
                 0^s,\ 10^{s-1},\ \ldots,\ 1^s
\tag{2.2}
\]

gives the standard BTK symmetric chain.

For a word \(w\), put

\[
 D_w(t)=\sum_{i=1}^t(2w_i-1),\qquad D_w(0)=0,
\tag{2.3}
\]

and let

\[
 \mathcal R(w)=
 \{t:D_w(t)>\max_{0\le u<t}D_w(u)\}
\tag{2.4}
\]

be its strict ascending record times.

### Lemma 2.1 (record-tail formula)

The unpaired \(1\)-positions of \(w\) are exactly \(\mathcal R(w)\).
If \(|w|=k\ge m/2\) and \(h=2k-m\), then the coordinates inserted
between the rank-\((m-k)\) and rank-\(k\) members of the BTK chain are
the last \(h\) elements of \(\mathcal R(w)\).

#### Proof

After cancellation in a prefix, its unmatched word has form \(1^a0^b\).
A newly read \(1\) remains unpaired precisely when \(b=0\), equivalently
when the new height is a strict record. Thus the unpaired ones are the
record times.

Let \(M=\max_tD_w(t)=|\mathcal R(w)|\). The minimum rank of the chain
containing \(w\) is \(k-M\). Its rank-\((m-k)\) member uses

\[
                 (m-k)-(k-M)=M-h
\]

of the unpaired positions, while \(w\) uses the first \(M\). Their
difference is therefore the last \(h\) record positions. \(\square\)

Write this terminal record set as \(I_h(w)\).

## 3. One Johnson move changes only four record times

### Lemma 3.1 (record stability)

If equal-weight words \(w,w'\) differ by one Johnson exchange, then

\[
                   |\mathcal R(w)\mathbin\triangle
                     \mathcal R(w')|\le4.
\tag{3.1}
\]

If they differ in one bit, then

\[
                   |\mathcal R(w)\mathbin\triangle
                     \mathcal R(w')|\le2.
\tag{3.2}
\]

#### Proof

Suppose first that a \(0\) at \(u\) and a \(1\) at \(v>u\) are
exchanged. On \([u,v)\), the height walk is translated upward by two;
outside this interval it is unchanged. Relative to the maximum before
\(u\), this can create record times only at the first local visits to the
two newly exposed record levels. At \(v\) the walks meet. Their maxima
accumulated before \(v\) differ by at most two, so the common suffix can
lose record times only at its first two record levels. This gives (3.1).
The opposite exchange follows by interchanging \(w,w'\).

A one-bit change translates one entire suffix by two. Local record times
inside the suffix are unchanged after the first two newly exposed or
suppressed levels, giving (3.2). \(\square\)

### Lemma 3.2 (terminal-set edit bound)

Let \(E,E'\) be finite subsets of a linearly ordered set with
\(|E\triangle E'|\le d\). If \(T_s(E)\) and \(T_t(E')\) are their
last \(s\) and last \(t\) elements, then

\[
                 |T_s(E)\setminus T_t(E')|
                    \le d+(s-t)_+.
\tag{3.3}
\]

#### Proof

Passing from the last \(s\) elements of \(E\) to its last \(t\) elements
discards at most \((s-t)_+\). Each subsequent insertion or deletion in
the ground set can displace at most one member of the current terminal
\(t\)-set. There are at most \(d\) such edits. \(\square\)

### Lemma 3.3 (ordered active-tail stability)

Let \(X,Y\) be Johnson-adjacent high endpoints of BTK product paths of
lengths \(h,h'\). Put

\[
                         S=I_h(X_A),\qquad T=I_{h'}(Y_A).
\tag{3.4}
\]

Then \(h'\in\{h-2,h,h+2\}\), and the following sharper ordered
statements hold.

1. If the seam exchanges two \(A\)-coordinates, then \(h'=h\) and
   \(|S\setminus T|=|T\setminus S|\le2\).
2. If it exchanges two \(B\)-coordinates, then \(S=T\).
3. If it moves a coordinate from \(B\) to \(A\), then
   \(h'=h+2\), \(S\subseteq T\), and \(|T\setminus S|=2\).
4. If it moves a coordinate from \(A\) to \(B\), then
   \(h'=h-2\), \(T\subseteq S\), and \(|S\setminus T|=2\).

In particular, if \(h\ge3\), then \(S\cap T\ne\varnothing\). If
\(z=\max(S\cap T)\), its insertion occurs among the last three edges of
the first atom and its removal among the first three edges of the second.

#### Proof

The \(A\)-rank changes by \(-1,0\), or \(+1\), so the displayed list of
\(h'\) follows.

For an \(A\)-internal exchange, suppose first that the height walk is
lifted by two on an interval \([u,v)\). Every old record inside the
interval remains a record, and at most two new record levels appear
there. After \(v\), the lifted walk has accumulated a maximum at most two
larger, so at most two old suffix records disappear and no new suffix
record appears. Thus the new record times added before \(v\) and the old
ones deleted after \(v\) each number at most two. Passing to equal-sized
terminal \(h\)-sets can replace at most two elements: the additions all
precede the deletions, so they cannot cause further loss among the
surviving later terminal elements. Reversing the exchange gives the same
bound. This proves item 1.

For a \(B\)-internal exchange the \(A\)-word is unchanged, proving item
2. If one \(A\)-bit is changed from \(0\) to \(1\), the height walk is
raised on a suffix. Every old record remains and at most two records are
added. Since the requested terminal-set size rises from \(h\) to
\(h+2\), its old terminal \(h\)-set is contained in the new terminal
\((h+2)\)-set. This proves item 3. Lowering a suffix gives item 4.

It remains to check that \(Y\) is a high endpoint. The \(A\)-rank of
\(X\) is \((m+h)/2\), so that of \(Y\) is at least

\[
                       \frac{m+h}{2}-1>\frac m2
\]

when \(h\ge3\). Every low product endpoint has \(A\)-rank at most
\(m/2\), so \(Y\) is high.

The order of BTK chain increments is increasing coordinate order. Hence
the largest common element has at most two elements of \(S\) above it
and at most two elements of \(T\) above it, proving the last assertion.
\(\square\)

## 4. The natural-state sink theorem

### Theorem 4.1 (high-end sink)

Let \(P\) have length \(h\) satisfying (0.2), and traverse it from its
low endpoint to its high endpoint \(X\). There is no two-sided
\(H\)-safe concatenation

\[
       P^{\rightarrow},\quad X\longrightarrow Y,\quad
       Q^{\leftarrow},
\tag{4.1}
\]

where \(X\to Y\) is a Johnson edge, \(Y\) is an endpoint of another
complete BTK product path \(Q\), and \(Q^{\leftarrow}\) is traversed
from high to low.

#### Proof

The forward traversal of \(P\) inserts every coordinate in
\(S=I_h(X_A)\). By Lemma 3.3, the next high endpoint \(Y\) has active
set \(T=I_{h'}(Y_A)\) and

\[
                     z=\max(S\cap T).
\tag{4.2}
\]

Both high endpoints contain \(z\), so the seam does not toggle it. The
backward traversal of \(Q\) removes it. Lemma 3.3 gives the following
exact upper bounds on the inclusive insertion--removal span:

\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
\text{seam type}&\text{span}\le\\ \hline
A\text{-internal}&7\\
B\text{-internal}&3\\
B\to A&5\\
A\to B&5.
\end{array}
\tag{4.3}
\]

For example, in the worst case the insertion is the third-last edge of
\(P\), then comes the seam, and the removal is the third edge of \(Q\).
Thus the span is at most seven, which is at most \(H\).

This is an insertion--removal residence forbidden by (1.3). \(\square\)

### Theorem 4.2 (low-end sink)

The same conclusion holds when \(P\) is traversed from high to low and
the seam leaves its low endpoint.

#### Proof

At a low endpoint the \(B\)-part is the high half-word. The reverse
traversal of \(P\) inserts its \(B\)-active record tail. An adjacent low
endpoint differs in its \(B\)-word by the same three possible one-edge
changes, and the next low-to-high atom removes its \(B\)-active tail.
Lemma 3.3 applied to the \(B\)-words gives the same ordered overlap and
the same constant-seven span bound. \(\square\)

The two theorems use the complete active word of the atom preceding the
seam. They are therefore a reachable-state obstruction, not the weaker
claim that some artificially chosen forbidden set kills the endpoint.

## 5. Large raw degree does not help

Take the explicit BTK chain whose bottom and top words are

\[
                     (01)^r0^h,\qquad (01)^r1^h,
\tag{5.1}
\]

in each half, and form their product diagonal. At its high endpoint,
exchanging a present and absent coordinate wholly inside \(A\) leaves the
\(B\)-bottom fixed, hence lands at another product high endpoint. This
gives \(r(m-r)\) neighbours. The analogous exchanges in \(B\) give
another \(r(m-r)\). Therefore (0.3) holds.

There is also an outward cross-half lower bound valid for every BTK atom.
At least one half chain has minimum \(r\). Moving one of the \(r\)
present \(B\)-coordinates to one of the \(r\) absent \(A\)-coordinates
moves both half ranks outward. The BTK top/bottom record description
shows that the changed boundary half becomes a top/bottom of minimum
\(r-1\); the other half automatically has minimum at most \(r-1\).
Thus all \(r^2\) such moves land at level-\((r-1)\) high endpoints.

Theorems 4.1--4.2 reject all of these neighbours after the natural atomic
history. Hence neither ambient degree, cross-half degree, nor the average
\(\Theta(m^{3/2})\) endpoint census implies state-conditioned degree.

### Proposition 5.1 (bounded carrier edit is a general coherent no-go)

For one half of an arbitrary fixed SCD, let \(A_h(X)\) be the active
alphabet of the complete product segment ending at a high endpoint \(X\)
of length \(h\). Suppose that every adjacent high product endpoint \(Y\)
satisfies

\[
 h(Y)\in\{h-2,h,h+2\},\qquad
 |A_h(X)\mathbin\triangle A_{h(Y)}(Y)|\le D_m.
\tag{5.2}
\]

If

\[
                         D_m<h,\qquad 2h+3\le H,
\tag{5.3}
\]

then the natural state after the complete low-to-high atom has no direct
complete-atom continuation.  The analogous condition on the other half
gives the low-end statement.

#### Proof

Since \(h(Y)\ge h-2\), (5.2)--(5.3) force the two active alphabets to
intersect.  A common coordinate is inserted on the first atom and removed
on the reversed second atom.  Its inclusive span is at most
\(h+1+h(Y)\le2h+3\), so the automaton rejects it. \(\square\)

Consequently, a single SCD with \(D_m=o(\sqrt m)\) cannot support the
proposed route on its Gaussian-length core when
\(H/\sqrt m\to\infty\): all but an \(o(1)\) fraction of its product atoms
have \(D_m<h=o(H)\).  A successful fixed SCD must instead have an
active-alphabet avalanche--a one-Johnson-edge endpoint move replacing
\(\Theta(\sqrt m)\) carrier labels on a positive-density family.

## 6. Almost every atom is a sink

The number of product paths of length at least \(L\) is exactly

\[
 P_{\ge L}
   =\binom m{\lfloor(m-L)/2\rfloor}^{\!2}.
\tag{6.1}
\]

Indeed, a path has length at least \(L\) precisely when both half chains
have minimum rank at most \(\lfloor(m-L)/2\rfloor\), and an SCD has
\(\binom mt\) chains with minimum at most \(t\).

Atoms of length below three number

\[
 c_m^2-\binom m{\lfloor(m-3)/2\rfloor}^{\!2}
                  =O(c_m^2/m)=o(W/H).
\tag{6.2}
\]

Finally Stirling's formula gives

\[
 c_m^2
  =\left(\frac2{\sqrt\pi}+o(1)\right)\frac W{\sqrt m}.
\tag{6.3}
\]

Equations (6.2)--(6.3) prove that the sink core

\[
 \mathcal C=\{P:h(P)\ge3\}
\tag{6.4}
\]

has exact size

\[
 |\mathcal C|=\binom m{\lfloor(m-3)/2\rfloor}^{\!2}
              =c_m^2-O(c_m^2/m)=c_m^2-o(W/H)
\tag{6.5}
\]

whenever \(H=\sqrt m\,\omega\), \(\omega\to\infty\), and \(H=o(m)\).

### Corollary 6.1 (component obstruction)

Every two-sided \(H\)-safe path/cycle cover whose indivisible retained
pieces are complete fixed-colour BTK product atoms, whose inter-piece
links are single endpoint Johnson seams, and which omits \(u\) middle
owners has at least

\[
 p\ge |\mathcal C|-\lfloor u/4\rfloor
    =c_m^2-O(c_m^2/m)-\lfloor u/4\rfloor
\tag{6.6}
\]

components.

#### Proof

In either orientation, a retained member of \(\mathcal C\) cannot have a
successor atom. Hence it must be the last atom of a directed path
component and cannot lie in a cycle. A component contains at most one
retained member of \(\mathcal C\). Every omitted member of \(\mathcal C\)
has at least four owners, and the complete atoms partition the middle
owners, so at most \(\lfloor u/4\rfloor\) such atoms are omitted. This
proves (6.6). \(\square\)

Since \((W/H)=(1+o(1))W/(\sqrt m\,\omega)\), (6.6) exceeds the desired
component scale by \(\Theta(\omega)\).

## 7. Complement and implication scope

The obstruction is orientation-symmetric: the high and low endpoint
proofs separately make both orientations sinks. Therefore selecting
direct seams in complement/reversal pairs cannot repair a fixed-colour
route. There are no fixed simple-path or fixed seam exceptions for
\(m\ge2\); self-complementary cycles are irrelevant because a sink-core
atom cannot lie in any cycle.

One must not enlarge this conclusion beyond its proved catalogue. Raw set
complementation does not permute the chains of any single SCD when
\(m\ge2\): the chain containing \(\varnothing\) and \([m]\) would have
to contain both \(\{x\}\) and \([m]\setminus\{x\}\), contradicting
nestedness. A two-colour atlas using an SCD and its complement is a
strictly larger state graph. The record-tail argument above controls
same-colour transitions, not arbitrary cross-colour seams.

Thus the proposed random-active-set argument cannot prove the requested
fixed-product-SCD endpoint theorem: the actual active sets are maximally
correlated with the queue naturally produced by the preceding atom. A
viable successor must establish at least one of the following new facts:

1. a mixed-colour endpoint Hall theorem whose cross-colour active words
   escape the record-tail cut;
2. an owner-disjoint atom splitting which flushes the record tail before
   the next endpoint decision; or
3. a literal exterior-moving/cooldown connector, with its full
   \(H\)-collar and target-column cost audited.

No constant-one conclusion follows from the obstruction. What is closed
is the complete-atom, direct-endpoint, fixed-BTK-colour realization of the
proposed \(H\)-memory forest.

## 8. The mixed-orbit comparison and the remaining joint factorization gate

The sink theorem does not say that the queue entropy is intrinsically too
large.  In fact the complete labelled coordinate orbit of any SCD has an
exact endpoint-mixing law.

Fix a half-endpoint \(T\) of rank \(k=m-r\), put \(h=m-2r\), and let
\(\mathscr D\) be any SCD of the \(m\)-cube.  Label every conjugate
\(\pi\mathscr D\) by its permutation \(\pi\).  Among the chain
occurrences rooted at \(T\), every ordered active \(h\)-set in \(T\)
has the same multiplicity.  Indeed a central chain segment partitions the
half-ground-set into parts of sizes \(h,r,r\).  Once its ordered active
word is mapped to a prescribed ordered \(h\)-set, the two \(r\)-parts can
be mapped in exactly \((r!)^2\) ways.  Summing over the
\(\binom mr\) central segments gives multiplicity

\[
                            \binom mr(r!)^2.
\tag{8.1}
\]

Consequently, for a two-half high endpoint and relevant live forbidden
supports \(I,R\) of sizes \(f,g\), the conservative fraction of active
alphabet pairs avoiding the whole queue is exactly

\[
 p_{f,g}
  =\frac{\binom{k-f}h\binom{k-g}h}{\binom kh^2}.
\tag{8.2}
\]

More is true.  Let \(T'\) denote the corresponding \(k\)-coordinate
active domain in the other half.  Join a history-support pair
\((I,R)\in\binom Tf\times\binom{T'}g\) to an active pair
\((U,V)\in\binom Th\times\binom{T'}h\) when
\(I\cap U=R\cap V=\varnothing\).  This bipartite graph is biregular.
Assume \(f+h\le k\) and \(g+h\le k\), so that it is nonempty.  Then for
every history family \(\mathcal F\),

\[
 \frac{|\Gamma(\mathcal F)|}{\binom kh^2}
 \ge
 \frac{|\mathcal F|}{\binom kf\binom kg},
\tag{8.3}
\]

and every empty rectangle \(\mathcal F\times\mathcal V\) obeys

\[
 \frac{|\mathcal F|}{\binom kf\binom kg}
 +\frac{|\mathcal V|}{\binom kh^2}\le1.
\tag{8.4}
\]

This proof is exact edge counting: the left and right degrees are

\[
 \binom{k-f}h\binom{k-g}h,
 \qquad
 \binom{k-h}f\binom{k-h}g,
\]

and total edge counts agree.  It remains valid for an adaptively selected
**set** of queue supports.  Demands with repeated support types require the
separate asymptotic-flatness hypothesis stated below.  Adding complemented
SCD colours doubles the labelled option multiplicity, exchanges the two
signed supports, and makes the option stack complement closed.

This is a one-step conservative support projection.  It forgets FIFO ages
and does not by itself prove Hall expansion for propagated full memory
states.  To lift it, one must also fix the age-slot/region pattern on each
left orbit, the relevant base-chain type on each right orbit, and impose
dagger closure on the selected routes and seams.

At \(H=\sqrt m\,\omega\), \(h=O(\sqrt m)\), and \(f+g\le2H+O(1)\),

\[
                      p_{f,g}\ge\exp[-O(\omega)].
\tag{8.5}
\]

Hence the proposed hypergeometric survival scale is correct in the full
ungrouped orbit, while Theorem 4.1 shows it is maximally false after one
fixed BTK colour has been selected and its natural history revealed.

### 8.1 Bare fixed-radius grouping is exactly integral

Owner overlap in the full orbit is not, by itself, an obstruction. Fix one
radius \(h=m-2r\) and let

\[
 \mathcal V_t=\{S\mathbin{\dot\cup}T:
      |S|=r+t,\ |T|=m-r-t\},\qquad0\le t\le h.
\tag{8.6}
\]

Join consecutive layers by every cross-half monotone Johnson edge. The
complete coordinate orbit contains every source-to-sink path in this
layered network. Both endpoint layers have

\[
                            N_r=\binom mr^2
\tag{8.7}
\]

vertices. Put \(v_t=|\mathcal V_t|\) and give every phase-\(t\) vertex
load \(N_r/v_t\le1\), distributed equally over its outgoing arcs. The
successive binomial ratio is exactly the backward/forward degree ratio,
so this is a conserved fractional flow of value \(N_r\). Vertex splitting
and integral max flow therefore give exactly \(N_r\) pairwise
owner-disjoint complete paths. Thus the bare fixed-radius full-orbit
grouping problem has an exact integral solution.

The complete path hypergraph also has favourable exact pair codegrees.
Writing \((u)_v=u(u-1)\cdots(u-v+1)\), the degree of a phase-\(t\)
owner is

\[
 D_t=\bigl((r+t)_t(r+h-t)_{h-t}\bigr)^2.
\tag{8.8}
\]

For compatible owners at phases \(s<t\), with \(d=t-s\), the codegree is

\[
 \lambda_{s,t}
 =\bigl((r+s)_s\,d!\,(r+h-t)_{h-t}\bigr)^2,
\tag{8.9}
\]

and it is zero for incompatible owners. Consequently

\[
 {\lambda_{s,t}\over D_s}
  ={1\over\binom{r+h-s}{d}^{2}},\qquad
 {\lambda_{s,t}\over D_t}
  ={1\over\binom{r+t}{d}^{2}}.
\tag{8.10}
\]

In particular the codegree-to-degree ratio at either endpoint is at most

\[
                         \binom{r+1}{d}^{-2}\le(r+1)^{-2}
                         =O(m^{-2})
\tag{8.11}
\]

in the Gaussian band. Hence growing path size and raw owner collisions are
not the live obstruction.  Since \(h<m\), no such path contains two
complementary owners; after quotienting owners and paths by complement,
the relative codegree is at most \(2/(r+1)^2\).

Complement and ownership alone are also compatible at negligible cost.
Fix a cross-pairing of the coordinates and partition owners by their
empty/full/split pair-status skeleton. Each skeleton cell is a
split-orientation cube. Pair every cell with its complemented skeleton and
put any cube SCD on one member and the complemented SCD on its mate. This
gives owner-disjoint complement-paired product paths. Only the all-split
self-complementary cell needs to be left, and it has \(2^m=o(W/H)\)
owners. This construction does not retain the independent active-alphabet
mixing of (8.3).

### 8.2 The exact cross-history coordinate cut

Let \(\mathcal M\) be any owner-disjoint family of length-\(h\) paths,
and let \(\ell_0,\ell_h\) be its two endpoint leaves. For every
\(A\)-coordinate \(z\), let \(n_z\) be the number of selected paths
which insert \(z\). Endpoint incidence gives the exact quota

\[
 \left|n_z-\frac hmN_r\right|\le\ell_0+\ell_h.
\tag{8.12}
\]

The analogous formula holds for every removed \(B\)-coordinate. Indeed
\(n_z\) is the number of used high endpoints containing \(z\) minus the
number of used low endpoints containing it; the corresponding complete
endpoint populations differ by exactly \((h/m)N_r\).

This cut is sharp. If one common \(A\)-coordinate is forbidden on every
selected path, the exact maximum packing size is

\[
                         \left(1-\frac hm\right)N_r.
\tag{8.13}
\]

Split the layered network according to whether the coordinate is present
and apply the same integral flow separately in the two signatures; the
smaller endpoint population in each signature is \((r/m)N_r\). For
\(h=C\sqrt m\) and \(H=\sqrt m\,\omega\), the forced leave
\((h/m)N_r\) exceeds \(N_r/H\) by the factor \(C\omega\).

Thus separate history-option Hall expansion is insufficient even after
bare owner grouping has been solved: it may allow many history fibres to
avoid the same physical direction. A positive theorem must recouple the
history-dependent path commodities while enforcing (8.12) simultaneously
for all coordinates and both signs.

The normalized Hall theorem (8.3) still does not build a physical cover.
Different orbit occurrences are chains from different SCD colours and
can reuse the same intermediate and middle owners.  If \(x_C\) records a
selected labelled chain occurrence, grouping them into one SCD requires

\[
                         \sum_{C\ni Z}x_C=1
          \qquad\text{for every half-cube set }Z,
\tag{8.14}
\]

with \(x_C\in\{0,1\}\).  A direct grouping of product occurrences has
the weaker analogous rows
\(\sum_{P\ni X}x_P=1\) for every middle owner \(X\), together with the
chronological compatibility equations.  The uniform fractional point is
feasible before history filtering--indeed one may select one whole orbit
colour--but (8.3) supplies neither integrality nor even joint fractional
feasibility after owner, seam, chronology, and simultaneous-shelf rows are
intersected.

Equation (8.14) is only a sufficient child-SCD formulation and is stronger
than necessary for middle-owner paths.  It also cannot be made literally
complement invariant inside one child SCD.  The direct middle-owner rows,
as realized by the near-factor above, are the authoritative ownership
interface.

Thus the exact surviving positive lemma is a **complement-closed coherent
stateful route-factorization theorem**.  It must simultaneously provide:

1. owner-disjoint product-path coverage with owner leave \(o(W/H)\);
2. literal typed seams and valid chronological propagation;
3. asymptotic flatness in every
   endpoint/minimum-rank/base-chain/full-age-pattern fibre, aggregated
   across many physical roots;
4. one common choice across all shelves and depths;
5. dagger-equivariant routes, histories, seams, and owner assignment;
6. physical normalization turning the labelled-orbit Hall deficiencies
   into \(o(W/H)\) components; and
7. every coordinate-transport quota (8.12), and its multicoordinate
   monotone-coupling refinements, to additive \(o(N_r/H)\).

Flatness cannot be imposed separately at one physical root.  If a fixed
root has

\[
                         M=\binom kf\binom kg
\]

possible history supports, an integral factor supplies total demand one.
A pointwise bound \(d_\xi\le(1+\varepsilon)/M\) would force
\(\varepsilon\ge M-1\) at its unique nonzero coordinate.  Hence the
flatness in item 3 must arise only after typed aggregation over many roots.

The first joint sublemma is already fractional and has an exact dual.  Let
\(\bar V\) be the complement-owner pairs, \(\mathcal O\) the
history-legal complement-path orbits, and \(\Xi\) the full age-refined
history bins.  Given desired bin masses \(d_\xi\), one needs

\[
 \sum_{O\ni v}x_O=1\quad(v\in\bar V),\qquad
 \sum_{O:\xi(O)=\xi}x_O=d_\xi\quad(\xi\in\Xi),\qquad x_O\ge0.
\tag{8.15}
\]

By Farkas, (8.15) fails exactly when there are weights \(y_v,z_\xi\)
such that

\[
 \sum_{v\in O}y_v+z_{\xi(O)}\ge0
          \quad\text{for every }O\in\mathcal O,
\tag{8.16}
\]

but

\[
 \sum_{v\in\bar V}y_v+
 \sum_{\xi\in\Xi}d_\xi z_\xi<0.
\tag{8.17}
\]

The unfiltered path factor excludes the projection with \(z=0\), and
root-level Kneser Hall excludes the projection with \(y=0\).  Neither
excludes genuinely mixed certificates \((y,z)\), which are the precise
missing Hall inequalities.  If history filtering could be shown to retain
an \(e^{-O(\omega)}=m^{-o(1)}\) fraction of the degree at every
**interior owner-pair**, then (8.11) would leave relative codegree
\(m^{-2+o(1)}\), the natural input to an integral matching/absorption step
after deterministic odd-set and lattice cuts are checked.

Under that joint lemma, the corresponding orbitwise versions of
(8.3)--(8.4) would give the required shelf-by-shelf Hall matchings,
route-level dagger pairing would transfer the lower estimates to the upper
sign, and a
total Hall deficiency \(o(W/H)\) yields a state-valid cover with
\(o(W/H)\) components.

No such stateful route-factorization theorem is presently proved.
Bare owner/complement grouping has the positive constructions above;
history-decorated fractional feasibility is now the first unproved joint
gate.  Separate biregular projections do not imply it: small cyclic chain
catalogues can have a \(\mathbb Z/3\mathbb Z\) lattice obstruction, and

entire singleton repair is \(2^z=o(W/H)\); this parity cut is subcritical.

* exact fixed-colour obstruction, including the natural generated queue;
