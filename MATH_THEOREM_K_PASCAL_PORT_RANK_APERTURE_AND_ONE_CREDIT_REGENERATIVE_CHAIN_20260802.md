# Pascal closing-port rank aperture and a one-credit regenerative chain

**Date:** 2026-08-02  
**Status:** unconditional obstruction and exact occurrence-level regenerative
construction.  The obstruction applies to every strict one-copy immediate-
lower table, independently of degrees or chronology.  The positive
construction is a literal protected-port interface with one rank-defect-one
predecessor occurrence.  Embedding that interface into a complete canonical
owner/target table, and repaying its one immediate-lower occurrence deficit,
remain separate tasks.  No upper-shadow, residence, exterior-window, or
common-cap/compiler assertion is made.

## 0. Result

The private Pascal rectangle has a rank obstruction which is invisible in
the stationary pull clock.

Let two closing ports have the same ordered rail `w`, let

\[
 R=\bigcup w,
 \qquad Q_\epsilon=R\cup c_\epsilon,
 \tag{0.1}
\]

where `c_epsilon` is the leading block of the selected predecessor token,
and let their owners be distinct rank-`r` sets `T_0,T_1`.  If the two ports
cross, then

\[
                 Q_0\cup Q_1\subseteq T_0\cap T_1.
\tag{0.2}
\]

Consequently two rank-`(r-1)` predecessor supports must coincide.  Thus a
strict immediate-lower-exact table, in which those supports occur once,
contains **no nondegenerate compatible closing-port pair at all**.  This is
not a small-degree failure: arbitrarily many central ports cannot help.

More quantitatively, if

\[
 e_\epsilon=(r-1)-|Q_\epsilon|\ge 0,
\tag{0.3}
\]

then every compatible pair obeys the sharp aperture inequality

\[
                 |Q_0\mathbin\triangle Q_1|
                    \le e_0+e_1.
\tag{0.4}
\]

The minimal distinct-support escape has defects `(1,0)` and nested
supports.  It is sufficient for an arbitrarily long anchored sequence of
Pascal splices.  One rank-defect-one predecessor token can be passed from
parent to parent; it is not consumed.  The exact sequential port-diversity
condition is a directed Hamilton-path condition in a small anchored port
graph.

An explicit family realizes equality in (0.4).  For `r>=d+2`, it gives up
to `r-1` successive compatible ports in `B_(2r-1)`, with pairwise-distinct
rank-`r` owners and pairwise-distinct central immediate-lower supports.  Its
only noncentral support is the reusable aperture token.  Adding one fresh
coordinate to one rail block and to every owner suspends the interface from
rank `r` to rank `r+1`, preserving the aperture and every splice equation.
This is an exact regenerative **port-state** recurrence, not yet a complete
Pascal-sector induction.

## 1. Port supports

Use the notation of
`MATH_THEOREM_K_FREEZE_AFTER_RECTANGLE_PRIVATE_PASCAL_SPLICE_20260802.md`.
A depth-`d` closing port is a selected assignment

\[
                       p\longrightarrow g.
\tag{1.1}
\]

Write

\[
 h_p=(c,A_1,\ldots,A_{d-1}),
 \qquad
 h_g=(A_1,\ldots,A_{d-1},D),
\tag{1.2}
\]

and set

\[
 w=(A_1,\ldots,A_{d-1}),qquad
 R=\bigcup_{t=1}^{d-1}A_t,qquad
 Q(p)=R\cup c.
\tag{1.3}
\]

Thus `Q(p)` is the full union support of the predecessor head occurrence.
If `T_g` is the owner of role `g`, put

\[
 U_g=R\cup D,qquad P_g=T_g\setminus U_g.
\tag{1.4}
\]

The literal port condition is

\[
                  P_g\subseteq c\subseteq T_g.
\tag{1.5}
\]

All occurrences remain labelled.  Equality of two set-valued supports does
not identify their physical head tokens.

## 2. The rank-aperture obstruction

### Theorem 2.1 (common-intersection law)

Let `(p_0,g_0)` and `(p_1,g_1)` be two occurrence-distinct closing ports
with the same rail `w`.  If they satisfy both crossed port assignments,
then

\[
       Q(p_0)\cup Q(p_1)\subseteq T_{g_0}\cap T_{g_1}.
\tag{2.1}
\]

#### Proof

Every block of the common rail lies in each closing head union, hence

\[
                         R\subseteq T_{g_0}\cap T_{g_1}.
\tag{2.2}
\]

The old assignment to `g_epsilon` gives
`c_epsilon subseteq T_(g_epsilon)`.  The crossed assignment gives
`c_epsilon subseteq T_(g_(1-epsilon))`.  Therefore both leading blocks lie
in both owners.  Unite this fact with (2.2). \(\square\)

### Corollary 2.2 (strict central one-copy no-go)

Assume `T_(g_0),T_(g_1)` are distinct rank-`r` owners and

\[
                    |Q(p_0)|=|Q(p_1)|=r-1.
\tag{2.3}
\]

Then compatibility forces

\[
 Q(p_0)=Q(p_1)=T_{g_0}\cap T_{g_1}.
\tag{2.4}
\]

In particular, if rank-`(r-1)` predecessor supports are one-copy labels,
no two occurrence-distinct closing ports can start a nondegenerate
freeze-after-rectangle splice.

#### Proof

Distinct rank-`r` owners have intersection of rank at most `r-1`.
Theorem 2.1 places two rank-`(r-1)` sets, and their union, inside that
intersection.  All three sets must therefore be equal.  One-copy exactness
forbids the repeated support. \(\square\)

This obstruction applies to a bank of any size: every candidate crossed
pair fails separately unless it repeats an immediate-lower support.  A
correction bank made only of strict central one-copy ports cannot repair the
problem.

### Theorem 2.3 (quantitative aperture inequality)

Suppose the two owners are distinct of rank `r`, and
`|Q(p_epsilon)|<=r-1`.  Define `e_epsilon` by (0.3).  Every compatible pair
satisfies

\[
              |Q(p_0)\mathbin\triangle Q(p_1)|
                     \le e_0+e_1.
\tag{2.5}
\]

If the supports are distinct, at least one `e_epsilon` is positive.  The
smallest possible escape is a nested pair of ranks `r-2,r-1`.

#### Proof

By Theorem 2.1,

\[
                         |Q(p_0)\cup Q(p_1)|\le r-1.
\]

Use

\[
 |Q_0\triangle Q_1|
   =2|Q_0\cup Q_1|-|Q_0|-|Q_1|
\]

and substitute `|Q_epsilon|=r-1-e_epsilon`.  This gives (2.5).
If both defects vanish, Corollary 2.2 gives equality of the supports.
For distinct supports the least possible total defect is one, realized by
a nested pair differing in one point. \(\square\)

### Consequence for a saturated table

In a table with exactly as many head occurrences as central q1 targets,
complete one-copy q1 coverage forces every head support to be central and
distinct.  Corollary 2.2 therefore rules out the splice absolutely.  A
rank-deficient aperture requires at least one additional occurrence credit,
or else one missing central q1 target.  This counting debt is separate from
the port equations.

## 3. The exact anchored recurrence

Fix one predecessor occurrence `p_*` with rail `w` and leading block
`c_*`.  Let closing roles `g_0,...,g_s` have the same initial rail `w` and
caps

\[
                         I_j=[P_j,T_j].
\tag{3.1}
\]

For `j>=1`, let `p_j` be the original predecessor of `g_j`, with leading
block `c_j`.  Assume an initial private-rooted table exports
`p_* -> g_0`, and occurrence-disjoint child tables export
`p_j -> g_j`.

Define the **anchored port digraph** on the closing roles by

\[
       g_i\longrightarrow g_j
       \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
       c_j\in I_i\ \hbox{ and }\ c_*\in I_j.
\tag{3.2}
\]

All vertices in (3.2) are understood to have the common literal rail `w`.

### Theorem 3.1 (anchored Hamilton-path criterion)

For a prescribed order

\[
                         g_0,g_{j_1},\ldots,g_{j_s},
\tag{3.3}
\]

the children can be merged sequentially by freeze-after-rectangle splices,
always retaining `p_*` as the predecessor of the surviving closing role,
if and only if (3.3) is a directed path in the graph (3.2).

Consequently all `s+1` tables admit such an anchored regenerative merge if
and only if (3.2) has a directed Hamilton path starting at `g_0`.

#### Proof

Suppose the current exported port is `p_* -> g_i`, and the next child port
is `p_j -> g_j`.  The two crossed assignments are literal exactly when

\[
                         c_j\in I_i,qquad c_*\in I_j,
\tag{3.4}
\]

because the rails already agree.  These are precisely the arc conditions
in (3.2).  Cross the assignments, freeze `g_i` at predecessor `p_j`, and
leave `g_j` unfrozen.  The new exported port is `p_* -> g_j`; the anchor
occurrence has survived unchanged.  Induction proves sufficiency.

Conversely, every anchored sequential merge from `g_i` to `g_j` must use
the two crossed assignments in (3.4), hence follows an arc of (3.2).
Visiting every child once gives the claimed Hamilton path. \(\square\)

This criterion is exact for the anchored scheme.  It is stronger than a
degree count and weaker than requiring a complete port box.

## 4. A sharp one-aperture family

Let

\[
                         r\ge d+2,qquad d\ge2,
\tag{4.1}
\]

and choose a set `R` of size `r-3`, partitioned into `d-1` nonempty
ordered blocks

\[
                         w=(A_1,\ldots,A_{d-1}).
\tag{4.2}
\]

Choose distinct points

\[
                 b,a_0,a_1,\ldots,a_{s+1}\notin R,
                 \qquad 1\le s\le r-1.
\tag{4.3}
\]

The upper bound on `s` makes the displayed coordinates fit inside a
`(2r-1)`-element ground set.

Define one anchor predecessor and `s` ordinary predecessors by

\[
 c_* =\{b\},\qquad h_{p_*}=(c_*,w),                  \tag{4.4}
\]

\[
 c_j=\{b,a_j\},\qquad h_{p_j}=(c_j,w)
       \quad(1\le j\le s).                            \tag{4.5}
\]

For `0<=j<=s`, define the closing role by

\[
 h_{g_j}=(w,D_j),\qquad D_j=\{a_j,a_{j+1}\},          \tag{4.6}
\]

\[
 T_j=R\cup\{b,a_j,a_{j+1}\}.                         \tag{4.7}
\]

Its head union and lower cap are

\[
 U_j=R\cup\{a_j,a_{j+1}\},qquad P_j=\{b\}.         \tag{4.8}
\]

Select `p_* -> g_0` initially, and select `p_j -> g_j` in child `j`.

### Theorem 4.1 (one-credit regenerative chain)

The records (4.4)--(4.8) have the following properties.

1. Every selected port is literal.
2. For every `1<=j<=s`, the current anchored port
   `p_* -> g_(j-1)` crosses the child port `p_j -> g_j`.
3. After the crossing, freezing `g_(j-1)` and retaining `g_j` exports
   `p_* -> g_j`.  Hence the same aperture token performs all `s` merges.
4. The owners `T_0,...,T_s` are pairwise distinct rank-`r` sets.
5. The central supports

   \[
       U_j=R\cup\{a_j,a_{j+1}\},qquad
       Q_j=R\cup\{b,a_j\}\quad(1\le j\le s)
   \tag{4.9}
   \]

   are pairwise distinct, including across the two displayed families.
6. The anchor support

   \[
                         Q_*=R\cup\{b\}               \tag{4.10}
   \]

   has rank `r-2`.  It is the sole noncentral support in the displayed
   interface, and each pair `(Q_*,Q_j)` attains equality in (2.5).

#### Proof

Every predecessor ends in `w`, and every closing role begins in `w`.
Equations (4.7)--(4.8) show that each cap is `[ {b},T_j ]`.
The selected leading blocks `c_*` and `c_j` lie in their corresponding
caps, proving item 1.

For the `j`-th crossing,

\[
 c_j=\{b,a_j\}\subseteq
       R\cup\{b,a_{j-1},a_j\}=T_{j-1},
 \qquad
 c_*\subseteq T_j.                                    \tag{4.11}
\]

Both contain the lower cap `{b}`.  Thus both crossed assignments are
literal.  The freeze-after-rectangle theorem gives item 3, and induction
reuses the same occurrence `p_*`.

Distinct consecutive pairs of the pairwise-distinct `a_j` give distinct
owners and distinct `U_j`.  The `Q_j` are distinct because their `a_j` are
distinct.  Every `Q_j` contains `b`, whereas no `U_j` does, so the two
families are disjoint.  Finally `|R|=r-3` gives ranks `r-2` and `r-1` in
(4.9)--(4.10), and

\[
                         Q_j=Q_*\cup\{a_j\},
\]

which proves sharpness of (2.5). \(\square\)

### Payload scope

Theorem 4.1 proves a collision-free literal interface on all displayed
owners and central q1 supports.  The shared proper suffixes of the portal
heads must be quarantined unmarked, or assigned through an independent
protected occurrence theorem.  In a saturated `W`-role central table,
the one deficient support `Q_*` leaves one central q1 occurrence unpaid.
Thus the theorem identifies the exact bounded correction ledger; it does
not silently claim that the missing central target has been restored.

## 5. Suspension of the port state

Let `z` be a fresh coordinate.  Replace

\[
 A_1\mapsto A_1\cup\{z\},qquad
 T_j\mapsto T_j\cup\{z\}                              \tag{5.1}
\]

for every role and leave `c_*`, `c_j`, and all terminal blocks unchanged.
Call the resulting rail union `R'=R union {z}`.

### Proposition 5.1 (rank suspension)

The suspended records satisfy Theorem 4.1 with owner rank `r+1`.
Every central support in (4.9) gains `z`, the aperture support (4.10) gains
`z`, and their respective defects remain zero and one.  All cap lower
bounds remain `{b}`, all port crossings remain literal, and the anchor
token remains reusable.

#### Proof

The new closing head union is `U_j union {z}`, so

\[
 (T_j\cup\{z\})\setminus(U_j\cup\{z\})=\{b\}.
\]

The ordered rails still agree.  Adding `z` to both upper cap endpoints and
to every rail support preserves every inclusion in (4.11).  All relevant
ranks increase by one, so the defect relative to the new central rank is
unchanged. \(\square\)

For a literal same-parity lift the ambient ground set receives two fresh
coordinates.  Proposition 5.1 uses one in the protected port state; the
placement of the other coordinate and completion of the two Pascal sectors
remain part of the missing global construction.

## 6. Relation to stationary pull clocks and flip gaps

The stationary pull clock records rank and age types, not `Q(p)`, the
literal rail, or the containing owner intersection.  It cannot detect
Theorem 2.1 or supply the unit aperture.

Likewise, a long-run Middle Levels cycle controls the distance from each
coordinate insertion to its next deletion.  That is sufficient for the
stated residence row after a chronology exists.  It neither repeats an
ordered rail nor changes the containment (2.1).  In particular, cutting
two strict central one-copy ports from one or two long-run cycles still
meets Corollary 2.2.  Flip-gap quality cannot overcome the aperture no-go.

Theorem 4.1 deliberately uses one noncentral predecessor support.  Whether
it can be inserted while preserving a prescribed long-run chronology is a
separate protected rethread problem.

## 7. Exact remaining recursive interface

The strict central closing-port lemma is false.  The weakest proof-safe
replacement isolated here is:

> **Unit-aperture Pascal interface.**  Export one protected predecessor
> occurrence of central defect one, a common literal rail, and a family of
> closing roles whose anchored port digraph (3.2) has the required directed
> Hamilton path.  Repay the one missing central q1 occurrence without
> altering that protected path.  Preserve the state under the rank
> suspension (5.1) and the full two-sector Pascal embedding.

Once this interface is supplied, the freeze-after-rectangle theorem gives
one private-rooted Hamilton rotor and zero lower Hall charge after every
splice.  The unit aperture is reused rather than accumulated.
