# Rectangle rank selectors for calibrated top packets

Date: 2026-07-25

Pure mathematics only. No computation, search, solver, web input, or
probabilistic matching black box is used.

## 0. Verdict

Let a top \(U\) have size

\[
M=m+H,
\]

where \(H\sim\sqrt{m\log m}\) is calibrated as in
`MATH_ATTACK_CALIBRATED_TOP_PACKET_HYPERGRAPH_20260725.md`. A cyclic order
of \(U\) supplies one promotion packet and, at every length
\(1\le s<M\), the incidence vector of all cyclic \(s\)-intervals.

The proposed four-order rectangle is an exact rank selector. Take two
disjoint adjacent label swaps whose cuts have cyclic separation \(r\) and
\(M-r\). Replacing the diagonal pair

\[
\pi_{00}+\pi_{11}
\quad\text{by}\quad
\pi_{10}+\pi_{01}
\tag{0.1}
\]

has zero signed interval incidence at every length except

\[
\boxed{r\quad\text{and}\quad M-r.}
\tag{0.2}
\]

At length \(r\), its image is one elementary four-target square

\[
e_{Kac}-e_{Kbc}-e_{Kad}+e_{Kbd},
\qquad |K|=r-2,
\tag{0.3}
\]

up to overall sign. The length-\((M-r)\) image is its complementary copy.

This gives genuine vertical decoupling in the calibrated band.

* Taking \(r=m-q\) changes only lower depth \(q\) inside the band; the
  complementary exceptional length is \(H+q\ll m-H\).
* Taking \(r=m+q\), for \(q\le H-2\), changes only upper depth \(q\);
  the complementary length is \(H-q\ll m-H\).
* Every such trade preserves the complete middle-owner incidence vector,
  not merely its cardinality.

The two final upper ranks are exceptional. Length \(M-1\) interval families
are invariant under adjacent swaps, and length \(M\) is the top tag. Their
total size is

\[
N_{H-1}+N_H=O(W/m)=o(W),
\tag{0.4}
\]

so they may be repaired literally.

The exact rank-\(r\) lattice generated by all relabeled rectangles is

\[
\boxed{
\Lambda_r=\ker_{\mathbb Z} A_r,
}
\tag{0.5}
\]

where \(A_r\) is the point-versus-rank-\(r\) incidence matrix. Hence the
only target-incidence invariants are the point marginals; total mass zero
follows from them. There is no further parity or Smith-lattice obstruction.
Because both orientations of every rectangle are present abstractly, the
real cone generated by signed rectangles is the whole linear space
\(\ker_{\mathbb R}A_r\).

There are, however, two positivity obstructions which the signed lattice
does not see.

1. A rectangle preserves the total packet multiplicity \(k_U\) at every
   individual top. Consequently it preserves the coordinate-degree vector
   \[
   d_i=\sum_{U\ni i}k_U.
   \]
   At rank \(r\), every attainable interval load has point marginals
   \(r d_i\).
2. Two arbitrary selected packets at a doubled top do not automatically
   form one of the two diagonals in (0.1). The feasible rectangle cone at a
   current nonnegative packet vector may even be \(\{0\}\), although the
   unrestricted signed cone is the full margin kernel.

Thus sequential rectangles solve the multirank problem **exactly at the
signed-lattice level**: ranks can be corrected one at a time without
disturbing the middle or previously corrected band ranks. They do not yet
give a bounded, nonnegative absorber sequence. The exact remaining lemma is
a support-feasible Markov/absorption theorem for doubled tops. No
incidence-lattice obstruction refutes it.

## 1. Interval-incidence notation

Fix a top \(U\), \(|U|=M\). Let \(\Omega(U)\) be the directed cyclic orders
of \(U\), modulo rotation. For \(1\le s<M\), define

\[
B_s^U:\mathbb Z^{\Omega(U)}
\longrightarrow\mathbb Z^{\binom Us}
\tag{1.1}
\]

by

\[
(B_s^Ux)_S
=\sum_{\pi\in\Omega(U)}x_\pi
\mathbf1\{S\text{ is a cyclic }s\text{-interval of }\pi\}.
\tag{1.2}
\]

Take a representative order

\[
\pi=(1,2,\ldots,M).
\]

For \(2\le r\le M-2\), put

\[
\tau=(1\ 2),\qquad
\sigma=(r+1\ r+2).
\tag{1.3}
\]

The swaps are disjoint and adjacent in \(\pi\). Define

\[
\pi_{00}=\pi,\quad
\pi_{10}=\tau\pi,\quad
\pi_{01}=\sigma\pi,\quad
\pi_{11}=\tau\sigma\pi,
\tag{1.4}
\]

and orient the rectangle replacement (0.1) as

\[
z_r=e_{\pi_{10}}+e_{\pi_{01}}
-e_{\pi_{00}}-e_{\pi_{11}}.
\tag{1.5}
\]

It has two positive and two negative packet coefficients, total coefficient
zero, and is supported inside one top.

## 2. The exact selector identity

### Theorem 2.1 (four-order rank selector)

For every \(1\le s<M\),

\[
B_s^Uz_r=0
\qquad\text{unless }s\in\{r,M-r\}.
\tag{2.1}
\]

Let

\[
K=\{3,4,\ldots,r\}.
\tag{2.2}
\]

At the first exceptional rank, up to reversing all signs,

\[
B_r^Uz_r
=e_{K\cup\{2,r+1\}}
-e_{K\cup\{1,r+1\}}
-e_{K\cup\{2,r+2\}}
+e_{K\cup\{1,r+2\}}.
\tag{2.3}
\]

At length \(M-r\), the image is the coordinate-complement of (2.3), with
the corresponding orientation sign.

#### Proof

Let \(P=\{1,2\}\) and \(Q=\{r+1,r+2\}\). If a set \(S\) contains zero or
two members of \(P\), the two terms related by \(\tau\) cancel in (1.5).
The same holds for \(Q\) and \(\sigma\). Thus a nonzero coefficient is
possible only if \(S\) contains exactly one member of each adjacent pair.

If such an \(S\) is a cyclic interval in one of the four orders, its two
ends must cut the pairs \(P,Q\). The two arcs between those cuts have
lengths exactly \(r\) and \(M-r\). This proves (2.1). At length \(r\), the
four choices of one endpoint from each pair give precisely the four sets in
(2.3). Complementing cyclic intervals gives the second exceptional image.
\(\square\)

The theorem is occurrence-exact. It uses all cyclic starts and does not
assume \(\gcd(M,r)=1\).

## 3. Isolation of the calibrated lower and upper rows

The controlled packet band consists of interval lengths

\[
m-H,m-H+1,\ldots,m+H=M.
\tag{3.1}
\]

### Corollary 3.1 (lower selector)

For \(1\le q\le H\), set

\[
r=m-q.
\tag{3.2}
\]

Then the rectangle changes the lower depth-\(q\) row and no other row in
the controlled band. Indeed,

\[
M-r=H+q\le2H<m-H
\tag{3.3}
\]

for all sufficiently large \(m\).

### Corollary 3.2 (upper selector)

For \(1\le q\le H-2\), set

\[
r=m+q.
\tag{3.4}
\]

Then the rectangle changes the upper depth-\(q\) row and no other row in
the controlled band, because

\[
M-r=H-q<m-H.
\tag{3.5}
\]

Both kinds of selector preserve the middle row \(s=m\) exactly. The case
\(q=H-1\) would require \(r=M-1\), whose interval family is invariant under
all adjacent swaps; \(q=H\) is the top itself. Since

\[
\frac{N_{H-1}}{N_H}=\frac{m+H}{m-H+1}=1+o(1)
\]

and \(N_H=(1+o(1))W/m\), the two unsupported upper ranks contain only
\(o(W)\) masks in total.

## 4. The exact rank lattice

Let \(V\) be an \(n\)-point set, and let

\[
A_r:\mathbb Z^{\binom Vr}\longrightarrow\mathbb Z^V,
\qquad
(A_rf)_i=\sum_{S\ni i}f_S
\tag{4.1}
\]

be the point-incidence map. For \(|K|=r-2\) and distinct
\(a,b,c,d\notin K\), define the elementary square

\[
g(K;a,b;c,d)
=e_{Kac}-e_{Kbc}-e_{Kad}+e_{Kbd}.
\tag{4.2}
\]

Every vector (4.2) lies in \(\ker_{\mathbb Z}A_r\).

### Theorem 4.1 (integral square-lattice theorem)

For \(2\le r\le n-2\),

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb Z}
\{g(K;a,b;c,d)\}
=\ker_{\mathbb Z}A_r.
}
\tag{4.3}
\]

Consequently the square lattice is saturated, and reduction modulo any
integer, including two, creates no additional invariant beyond the point
margins.

#### Proof

Let \(R_r\) be the integer span of the squares. In the quotient
\(G=\mathbb Z^{\binom Vr}/R_r\), the relation (4.2) says that for fixed
distinct \(a,b\), the difference

\[
[K\cup\{a,c\}]-[K\cup\{b,c\}]
\]

is unchanged when the auxiliary \((r-2)\)-core and the final coordinate are
moved by one Johnson edge avoiding \(a,b\). Connectivity of the relevant
Johnson graphs therefore makes this difference depend only on \(a,b\).
The resulting differences obey the cocycle identity, so there are elements
\(p_i\in G\) and one constant \(c\in G\) such that

\[
[S]=c+\sum_{i\in S}p_i
\qquad(S\in\tbinom Vr).
\tag{4.4}
\]

This is an integral normal form, obtained only by adding square relations.
Under \(A_r\), the differences \([K\cup\{i\}]-[K\cup\{j\}]\) map to
\(e_i-e_j\), while one fixed \(r\)-set maps to a vector of coordinate sum
\(r\). Hence the quotient maps isomorphically onto

\[
\operatorname{im}_{\mathbb Z}A_r
=\{y\in\mathbb Z^V:\sum_i y_i\equiv0\pmod r\},
\tag{4.5}
\]

with no torsion kernel. Therefore the kernel of \(A_r\) is exactly \(R_r\),
proving (4.3). \(\square\)

For the calibrated packet problem, every square with \(r+2\le M\) embeds
in some top \(U\) containing \(K\cup\{a,b,c,d\}\). Theorem 2.1 lifts that
square to a packet rectangle without changing any other controlled rank.
Therefore the global image lattice of all top rectangles at rank \(r\) is
exactly \(\ker_{\mathbb Z}A_r\) throughout the supported band.

### Corollary 4.2 (real cone)

Both orientations of every rectangle are available abstractly. Hence the
nonnegative cone generated by the signed move set \(\{g,-g\}\) is

\[
\operatorname{cone}_{\mathbb R}\{g,-g\}
=\ker_{\mathbb R}A_r.
\tag{4.6}
\]

This cone is a linear space. It is not the state-dependent cone of
nonnegative packet selections considered in Section 6.

## 5. Top-count and point-margin invariants

Let \(x_{U,\pi}\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}\) be packet multiplicities and put

\[
k_U=\sum_{\pi\in\Omega(U)}x_{U,\pi}.
\tag{5.1}
\]

A rectangle inside \(U\) preserves \(k_U\) exactly. Define the induced
coordinate degrees

\[
d_i=\sum_{U\ni i}k_U.
\tag{5.2}
\]

Let \(\mu_r\) be the global rank-\(r\) interval multiplicity vector of the
selected packets.

### Proposition 5.1 (frozen point margins)

For every \(i\in[2m]\),

\[
\boxed{
(A_r\mu_r)_i=r d_i.
}
\tag{5.3}
\]

Consequently rectangles at rank \(r\) can move \(\mu_r\) only inside its
affine lattice

\[
\mu_r+\ker_{\mathbb Z}A_r.
\tag{5.4}
\]

#### Proof

In one cyclic order on \(U\), every point of \(U\) belongs to exactly \(r\)
of the \(M\) cyclic \(r\)-intervals, while a point outside \(U\) belongs to
none. Sum over selected packet multiplicities. \(\square\)

Thus any proposed exactly uniform or floor/ceiling target at rank \(r\) must
first have the same point marginals \(r d_i\). The square-lattice theorem
then says that this necessary condition is also the only **signed integral**
condition.

### 5.1 Parity from \(0/2\) top multiplicities

If every \(k_U\in\{0,2\}\), then:

1. the total number of packets \(\sum_Uk_U\) is even, so pure \(0/2\)
   multiplicities are impossible when \(N_H\) is odd;
2. every coordinate degree \(d_i\) is even; and
3. the rank-\(r\) point margins in (5.3) are divisible by \(2r\).

These are invariants of the chosen top multiplicity vector, not extra
invariants of the rectangle image lattice. Allowing one top of multiplicity
one handles an odd total. More generally bounded multiplicities remove the
parity restriction while preserving the exact point-margin law (5.3).

Even the parity restriction is asymptotically cheap. If \(N_H\) is even,
choose uniformly a half-family \(\mathcal A\) of the tops and put

\[
k_U=2\mathbf1_{\mathcal A}(U).
\tag{5.5}
\]

For each coordinate, its expected degree equals the degree obtained from
one packet at every top, and its variance is at most \(N_H\). Therefore

\[
\mathbb E\sum_i|d_i-\mathbb E d_i|
\le2m\sqrt{N_H}=o(W).
\tag{5.6}
\]

So some \(0/2\) allocation has only \(o(W)\) aggregate coordinate-degree
imbalance. This direct averaging observation does not choose the cyclic
orders or prove flag coverage.

More decisively, it cannot be used with the two full diagonal packets of a
rectangle. Those orders differ by two adjacent swaps, and one adjacent swap
changes at most two middle windows. Hence the two diagonal full packets
share at least \(M-4\) of their \(M\) owners and their union has size at most

\[
M+4.
\tag{5.7}
\]

Putting such a pair on half the tops covers at most

\[
\frac{M+4}{2}N_H
=\left(\frac12+o(1)\right)W
\tag{5.8}
\]

distinct owners. Thus direct \(0/2\) deployment is fatal despite its benign
point marginals. Full-packet rectangles can only be tangent directions, or
must be replaced by restricted-segment absorbers after an owner
near-transversal has already been chosen.

The latter repair is now exact. The theorem note
`MATH_ATTACK_RESTRICTED_SEGMENT_RECTANGLE_ABSORBER_20260725.md` proves that
two complementary promotion segments with \(O(Q)\) phase overlap reproduce
the full rectangle incidence at every length \(m-Q,\ldots,m+Q\), while
using only \(M+O(Q)\) states and retaining \(M-O(1)\) distinct middle owners
per top. This removes the owner-collapse obstruction locally; the remaining
issue is positive multi-top scheduling of the available segment diagonals.

### 5.2 Order-parity invariant

There is also a finer order-level parity when \(M\) is odd. A cyclic
rotation of \(M\) symbols is then an even permutation, so permutation parity
is well defined on directed cyclic orders modulo rotation. The two orders
in either diagonal of a rectangle have the same parity. A rectangle moves
two selected orders from one parity class to the other, so the number of
selected odd orders at a fixed top is invariant modulo two.

When \(M\) is even, cyclic rotation itself is odd, so this parity does not
descend to cyclic orders modulo rotation. In either case it is an
order-selection invariant, not a target-incidence invariant at rank \(r\).

## 6. Signed decoupling versus feasible absorbers

At the signed level the rectangle mechanism completely decouples the
supported band ranks.

### Theorem 6.1 (signed sequential correction)

Fix any finite collection of desired integral corrections \(f_r\), one at
each supported band rank, satisfying

\[
A_rf_r=0.
\tag{6.1}
\]

There is a signed integral packet vector \(z\), expressed as a sum of
rank-selecting top rectangles, whose image at every chosen rank is exactly
\(f_r\), whose middle image is zero, and whose image at every other
controlled band rank is zero.

#### Proof

By Theorem 4.1, express each \(f_r\) as an integral sum of elementary
squares. Embed every square in a containing top and lift it with Theorem
2.1. Corollaries 3.1--3.2 say that its complementary exceptional row lies
outside the controlled band. Summing first within each rank and then over
ranks gives the required \(z\). \(\square\)

This theorem proves that there is no lattice accumulation, parity loss, or
cross-rank interference in a sequential correction. The obstruction is
positivity.

For a current packet vector \(x\ge0\), a rectangle orientation is feasible
only when both orders on its negative diagonal have positive multiplicity.
Define the state-dependent feasible cone

\[
\mathcal C_r(x)
=\operatorname{cone}\{B_rz:
z\text{ is a rank-}r\text{ rectangle feasible at }x\}.
\tag{6.2}
\]

In general,

\[
\mathcal C_r(x)\subsetneq\ker_{\mathbb R}A_r.
\tag{6.3}
\]

Indeed, if every top has at most one selected packet, no rectangle is
feasible and \(\mathcal C_r(x)=\{0\}\). Even when a top contains two
packets, a move exists only if that pair is one of the diagonals
\(\{\pi_{00},\pi_{11}\}\) or \(\{\pi_{10},\pi_{01}\}\) for two appropriate
disjoint adjacent swaps. A generic pair need not satisfy this condition.

After one move the new pair is the opposite diagonal, so the move is
reversible. It need not be a usable diagonal for the next desired separation
or rank. Thus the existence of many doubled tops supplies potential support,
not a Markov-connectivity theorem.

## 7. The exact absorber gate

The strongest algebraic conclusion is now precise.

* Lower depths \(1\le q\le H\) and upper depths
  \(1\le q\le H-2\) can be corrected independently by signed integral
  rectangles.
* The middle incidence vector and all already corrected band ranks remain
  exactly fixed.
* The only incidence invariant is the point-margin vector forced by the top
  multiplicities.
* The final two upper ranks and missing top masks cost only \(o(W)\) to
  append.

What remains is the following positivity statement.

> **Rectangle absorber connectivity \((\mathrm{RAC}_Q)\).** Choose bounded
> top multiplicities \(k_U\), with total \(N_H\), whose coordinate degrees
> have aggregate imbalance \(o(W)\). Choose an initial nonnegative packet
> vector respecting those multiplicities. For every lower and upper depth
> \(q\le Q\), successively use only support-feasible rank-selecting rectangle
> moves so that:
>
> 1. packet multiplicities remain bounded and nonnegative;
> 2. the middle owner incidence remains unchanged;
> 3. every previously treated band rank remains unchanged; and
> 4. the total residual flag holes over the treated ranks are \(o(W)\).

The natural target is \(Q=H-2\), leaving the two unsupported upper boundary
ranks for literal repair. Because rectangle moves do not change the number
of packets or promotion paths, an arbitrarily long absorber sequence has no
direct word-length toll; only its final hole count matters.

Theorem 6.1 proves \((\mathrm{RAC}_Q)\) after deleting the words
"nonnegative" and "support-feasible." It does not prove the stated gate.
Conversely, neither point-margin parity nor the interval-incidence lattice
refutes it. The unresolved content is expansion/connectivity of the graph of
bounded packet pairs under separated adjacent-swap rectangles.

## 8. Audit ledger

### Proved

1. The four-order rectangle is an exact selector for lengths \(r,M-r\).
2. Lower depth \(q\) and upper depth \(q\le H-2\) are isolated from every
   other controlled band rank.
3. Every selector preserves the full middle-owner incidence vector.
4. The rank-\(r\) image lattice is exactly \(\ker_{\mathbb Z}A_r\).
5. The real signed cone is exactly \(\ker_{\mathbb R}A_r\).
6. Point marginals \(r d_i\) are the only incidence-level invariants.
7. Pure \(0/2\) allocations have the stated total and coordinate parities;
   there is no extra rank-incidence parity.
8. Some \(0/2\) top allocation has only \(o(W)\) aggregate coordinate-degree
   imbalance.
9. Arbitrary zero-margin corrections at all supported ranks can be lifted
   simultaneously and integrally without cross-rank interference.
10. The final two upper boundary ranks have only \(o(W)\) masks.

### Open

1. Support-feasible connectivity of doubled packet pairs under rectangles.
2. A bounded-multiplicity sequence realizing the signed corrections without
   negative packet coefficients.
3. Aggregate \(o(W)\) residual flag holes through \(Q=H-2\).
4. Therefore \((\mathrm{RAC}_{H-2})\) and coefficient one by this
   vertical-decoupling route.

The rectangle proposal succeeds completely as an exact signed rank
selector and lattice generator. Its remaining difficulty is no longer
vertical coupling: it is positivity inside the bounded packet-pair fibre.
