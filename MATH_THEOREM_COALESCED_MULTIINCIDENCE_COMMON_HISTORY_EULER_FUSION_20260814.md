# Jointly feasible common-history incidences coalesce before the separated-port test

**Date:** 2026-08-14  
**Status:** unconditional extension of the separated multi-port Euler
theorem. It permits arbitrarily many component-graph incidences to reuse one
port when they admit one jointly feasible literal history, even when their
forced signatures differ. The occurrence-level successor-swap argument and
the multiway union/intersection criterion were independently audited on
2026-08-14.

## 1. Setup

Let \(G=(V,E)\) be connected. For each \(v\in V\), let \(T^v\) be a
cyclic simple resident Johnson trace of length \(L_v\ge d+1\), with one fixed
global traversal orientation. Write \(P^v_j\) and \(F^v_j\) for its maximal
and forced depth-\(d\) antecedent letters.

For every incidence \((v,e)\), choose a cyclic start \(b_{v,e}\). For every
edge \(e=uv\), choose one literal length-\(d\) word

\[
 H^e=(H^e_0,\ldots,H^e_{d-1})
\]

satisfying at both endpoints

\[
 F^v_{b_{v,e}+j}\subseteq H^e_j
 \subseteq P^v_{b_{v,e}+j}
 \qquad(0\le j<d).
\]

At one component \(v\), partition the incident edges into **port classes**.
All edges in one class \(C\) use the same start \(b_{v,C}\), and they must
request one common literal word

\[
 K^{v,C}=H^e\qquad(e\in C).
\]

Thus the same word is common across each component-graph edge and across all
incidences coalesced at one physical port. Assume the length-\(d\) blocks of
distinct port classes are disjoint and separated cyclically by at least one
unaltered source position. No bound is placed on the number of incidences in
one class.

Equality of edge histories is transitive. Accordingly, take the equivalence
relation on \(E\) generated by “two edges lie in one port class at some
component.” Every edge in one generated **history class** must use one common
word. A port class is its incidence set at a particular component. This
transitive consistency is automatic in the earlier identical-history
formulation but must be stated for jointly chosen histories.

## 2. Exact multiway criterion

Fix one generated history class and a proposed word
\(K=(K_0,\ldots,K_{d-1})\). Let \({\cal I}\) be the collection of all
oriented endpoint ports of its edges. At offset \(j\), put

\[
 U_j=\bigcup_{i\in{\cal I}}F^{i}_j,
 \qquad
 M_j=\bigcap_{i\in{\cal I}}P^{i}_j.
\]

### Lemma 2.1 (multiway common-history criterion)

One literal word \(K\) is legal at every endpoint in \({\cal I}\) if and
only if

\[
 U_j\subseteq M_j\qquad(0\le j<d).
\]

When this holds, the canonical minimal choice is \(K_j=U_j\).

#### Proof

Any common letter contains every forced set, hence contains their union, and
lies in every maximal set, hence lies in their intersection. This proves
necessity. Conversely, if \(U_j\subseteq M_j\), then
\(F^i_j\subseteq U_j\subseteq P^i_j\) for every endpoint \(i\), so
\(K_j=U_j\) is jointly legal. \(\square\)

This criterion is genuinely multiway. Pairwise compatibility of endpoints
does not in general imply that one word works for the entire class.

## 3. Coalesced-port theorem

### Theorem 3.1

Under the setup above, all component source circuits serialize into one
cyclic source word, with zero added positions, preserving the complete
occurrence-labelled literal subword deck through width \(d+1\).

In particular, the complete owner ledger and every strict-lower compiler cell
supported on a source word of width at most \(d\) transport exactly.

#### Proof

On one component, replace the maximal antecedent word once for every port
class by its assigned word \(K^{v,C}\). Joint feasibility says that this one
word contains the component's forced sets and lies in its maximal sets. Every
incidence in the class is satisfied by the same replacement on the same
positions, so it introduces no additional alteration. The distinct altered
blocks are separated. The simultaneous multi-block freedom theorem therefore
proves that the resulting word is still an antecedent of precisely \(T^v\).

View every resulting component word as an occurrence-labelled closed walk in
the order-\(d\) de Bruijn multigraph. For every edge \(e=uv\), the two walks
share the literal vertex \(H^e\). Several edges may share one history vertex
and may even reuse the same incoming occurrence on the accumulated component;
this does not identify or delete a de Bruijn edge occurrence. The union of the
component walks is balanced and weakly connected because \(G\) is connected.
Hence it has an Euler circuit.

Equivalently, process a spanning tree of \(G\). At an edge joining a new child
cycle to the accumulated cycle at \(H^e\), swap successors after incoming
occurrence edges ending at \(H^e\). If a later edge reuses the same incoming
occurrence, swap its **current** successor with the successor of an incoming
occurrence in the genuinely new child cycle. Both successors still leave the
same history vertex, so each swap is legal and merges two distinct permutation
cycles.

Algebraically, if \(p\) is the reused incoming occurrence and
\(q_1,\ldots,q_t\) come from successive child cycles, the swaps

\[
 (p,q_1),\ (p,q_2),\ \ldots,\ (p,q_t)
\]

transpose successor values. At every stage \(p\) remains one labelled
occurrence with one successor; no occurrence is copied, identified, deleted,
or traversed twice.

Every labelled order-\(d\) de Bruijn edge retains its literal
\((d+1)\)-letter context. An Euler circuit uses every such labelled edge once,
preserving the width-\(d+1\) deck; shorter words are its literal suffixes.
\(\square\)

## 4. Exact MSW formulation

For an oriented tight MSW row \(w\) at start \(a\), record the full port
datum

\[
 \Pi_w(a)=\bigl((F_j(w,a),P_j(w,a))\bigr)_{0\le j<d}.
\]

An edge option selects oriented starts at its two endpoints satisfying the
two-way instance of Lemma 2.1. Multiple selected options may coalesce at one
row-start even when their forced signatures differ, provided the endpoint
ports of the entire class satisfy the multiway test

\[
 \bigcup_i F^{(i)}_j\subseteq\bigcap_i P^{(i)}_j
 \qquad(0\le j<d).
\]

Only distinct physical starts must have cyclic distance at least \(d+1\).
Thus the authoritative proof-safe global gate is:

1. choose one compatible parent incidence for every nonroot component;
2. choose one global orientation per component;
3. partition same-start incidences into jointly feasible common-history
   classes by Lemma 2.1; and
4. require distinct used starts at a component to have cyclic distance at
   least \(d+1\).

The earlier equality-only forced-signature CSP is a sufficient submodel, not
the exact gate.

## 5. Finite MSW fibre census

For every parent row and inverse highest-valley packet type, the H100 audit
asked whether **all** children in that fibre could use one parent start and
one jointly feasible word. The parent orientation was fixed globally; every
child orientation and start was free. Counts are identical in the two parent
orientations.

| \((m,d)\) | packet fibres | equality-only failures | recovered by multiway \(H\) | remaining multiway failures |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| \((7,2)\) | 852 | 399 | 114 | 285 |
| \((10,3)\) | 32,974 | 9,522 | 3,820 | 5,702 |

The recovery/failure counts by packet type are:

| \((m,d)\) | recovered `01/001/011/0011` | remaining `01/001/011/0011` |
|---:|---:|---:|
| \((7,2)\) | `31/40/40/3` | `217/29/29/10` |
| \((10,3)\) | `1320/1245/1245/10` | `4014/844/844/0` |

For example, at \((10,3)\) the parent `11111111100100000000` and its two
inverse `001` children have no universal equality-only forced signature, but
they admit a common parent start and a joint word whose three letter sizes are
\(3,2,2\).

The relaxation is substantial but does not justify one universal class per
packet. At \((7,2)\), parent `11111100100000` and its two inverse `01`
children remain jointly infeasible at every parent start.

The census is finite evidence about packet-wide coalescence, not a global
selector or an all-parameter theorem.

## 6. Scope

The theorem says nothing about existence of a coalesced incidence assignment
in an infinite MSW family. It transports no source interval longer than
\(d+1\), no proper-upper current, and no final opening cap. Those remain
separate gates.
