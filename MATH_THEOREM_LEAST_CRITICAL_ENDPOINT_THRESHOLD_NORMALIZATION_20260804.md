# Least-critical endpoint branches reduce to the threshold face

**Date:** 2026-08-04  
**Status:** unconditional pure-mathematical normalization.  It sharpens the
endpoint-saturation theorem for every finite Bellman grid.  It does not
prove Bellman positivity or `nu(k) <= B(k) + O(1)`.

Put

\[
 A={\sqrt\pi\over2}.
\]

For `n=1`, endpoint saturation gives `c_1'=A` immediately, so every claim
below is trivial.  Hence assume `n>=2`.

Let

\[
 (c_0,c_1,\ldots,c_n),\qquad c_0=0,
\]

be a nonnegative internally superadditive table in first-crossing form:

\[
 c_j<A\quad(1\le j<n),\qquad c_n\ge A.
\tag{0.1}
\]

Its Bellman clock and functional are

\[
 V_0=0,
 \qquad
 V_m=\max_{1\le j\le\min(m,n)}(c_j+V_{m-j}),
 \qquad
 \Phi(c)=\sum_{m\ge0}K(V_m).
\tag{0.2}
\]

Define the best lower-denomination partition value at capacity `n` by

\[
 P_n=\max\left\{
       \sum_i c_{j_i}:1\le j_i<n,\ \sum_i j_i=n
                    \right\}.
\tag{0.3}
\]

The endpoint-saturation theorem replaces `c_n` by

\[
                         c_n'=\max\{A,P_n\}          \tag{0.4}
\]

without increasing the Bellman functional.

## Theorem 1 (least-critical endpoint normalization)

After endpoint saturation, suppose the endpoint size `n` is the least
maximum-density denomination:

\[
 {c_n'\over n}>{c_j\over j}
 \qquad(1\le j<n).                                 \tag{1.1}
\]

Then

\[
                              \boxed{c_n'=A.}        \tag{1.2}
\]

Equivalently, every saturated table with `c_n'>A` has a lower denomination
of density at least `c_n'/n`.  Hence it belongs to a lower critical-size
branch; it is not a genuinely endpoint-efficient case.

### Proof

Suppose `c_n'>A`.  Equation (0.4) then gives `c_n'=P_n`.  Choose a
partition attaining (0.3):

\[
 n=j_1+\cdots+j_t,
 \qquad
 c_n'=c_{j_1}+\cdots+c_{j_t}.
\]

Dividing by `n` writes the endpoint density as the weighted average

\[
 {c_n'\over n}
 =\sum_{i=1}^t{j_i\over n}{c_{j_i}\over j_i}.
\tag{1.3}
\]

At least one summand density is therefore at least the weighted average:

\[
 {c_{j_i}\over j_i}\ge {c_n'\over n}
\]

for some `j_i<n`, contradicting (1.1).  Thus `c_n'` cannot exceed `A`,
while (0.4) gives `c_n'>=A`.  This proves (1.2). \(\square\)

## Corollary 2 (branch assignment by the least maximizer)

Assign every saturated table to the least index

\[
 h=\min\operatorname*{argmax}_{1\le j\le n}{c_j\over j}.
\tag{2.1}
\]

Then the branch `h=n` lies entirely on the threshold face

\[
                              c_n=A.                 \tag{2.2}
\]

If saturation gives `c_n=P_n>A`, the endpoint generator is Bellman-inert
and the attaining partition in (0.3) contains a lower generator of density
at least `c_n/n`; the table is assigned to a branch `h<n` instead.
Indeed, every occurrence of a size-`n` generator in a Bellman partition may
be replaced by the fixed attaining lower-denomination partition of capacity
`n` and equal value, so the endpoint does not change any Bellman maximum.

In particular, for the exact five-slot Apéry reduction, the genuinely
size-five-efficient branch may be restricted to

\[
                              c_5=A.                 \tag{2.3}
\]

All sixteen residue-path forms and all availability-filtered head terms
remain necessary on that threshold face; this theorem changes only the
endpoint parameter.

## Scope

This is a branch normalization, not a deletion of an inert endpoint while
preserving first-crossing form.  A saturated inert endpoint may be needed
to display the first threshold even though the same Bellman clock is
generated by lower denominations.  The theorem only says that such a table
must be analyzed in a lower maximum-density branch.
