# The Klein-four component and the cell-invisibility of dense `D_4` fringe packets

Date: 2026-07-26

Method: pure mathematics only.

## 0. Outcome

Let

\[
 \mathcal B_s=
 \{1u0:u\in\mathcal D_s\}
 \mathbin{\dot\cup}
 \{10\,1v0:v\in\mathcal D_{s-1}\}
 \mathbin{\dot\cup}
 \{1v0\,10:v\in\mathcal D_{s-1}\}
 \mathbin{\dot\cup}
 \{10\,1w0\,10:w\in\mathcal D_{s-2}\}.
\tag{0.1}
\]

The four family sizes are

\[
                         (C_s,C_{s-1},C_{s-1},C_{s-2}).
\tag{0.2}
\]

The canonical left and right endpoint coordinate swaps generate a
Klein-four group \(V_4\).  Their four-factor ownership overlay has all of
\(\mathcal B_s\) in one component.  Consequently a legal coordinate shore
choice only permutes (0.2); one Boolean cell always retains load \(C_s>p\).
The abstract two-partition discrepancy theorem is therefore inapplicable to
this canonical overlay even when

\[
              C_s+2C_{s-1}+C_{s-2}\le4p.
\tag{0.3}
\]

The non-coordinate fourteen-row \(D_4\) factor does create many independent
exact packets.  In fact, inside the variable fillings \(u,v,w\), first-
fringe packets cover all but

\[
                         R_s=a_s+2a_{s-1}+a_{s-2}=o(M_s)
\tag{0.4}
\]

roots of \(\mathcal B_s\), where \(a_t\) counts size-\(t\) trees with no
size-four fringe subtree and

\[
                         M_s=C_s+2C_{s-1}+C_{s-2}.
\tag{0.5}
\]

Every packet has fourteen roots and either the canonical or non-coordinate
\(D_4\) shore may be selected independently.  Thus exact packet coverage
and legality hold asymptotically.

They do **not** fragment the Boolean-cell load.  Each such packet is a
strict port-preserving substitution inside one of the fillings \(u,v,w\).
The protected two-endpoint window contains both complementary ports of the
substitution, so its target is independent of the chosen \(D_4\) factor.
Consequently every factor generated by all these fringe choices has exactly
the same four-cell vector (0.2).

This gives a new indivisibility statement:

\[
 \boxed{
 \text{dense strict-interior packet freedom is cell-diagonal; it moves no
 two-endpoint preload at all.}}
\tag{0.6}
\]

To escape, a non-coordinate packet must be **boundary-active**: its changed
slab must meet an endpoint of the protected window, so that it changes the
exported entrance or exit data rather than a strictly internal filling.
No present theorem partitions the full \(M_s\) occurrence set into such
boundary-active packets.  The four-state \(D_4/H_4\) menu at one marked port
is therefore only a seed, not a fragmentation theorem.

## 1. The canonical Klein-four component

Let \(L\) and \(R\) be the two endpoint coordinate transpositions.  They
commute, and

\[
                         V_4=\langle L,R\rangle.
\tag{1.1}
\]

The largest one-ended ownership component for \(L\) joins

\[
 \{1u0:u\in\mathcal D_s\}
 \quad\hbox{to}\quad
 \{10\,1v0:v\in\mathcal D_{s-1}\}.
\tag{1.2}
\]

Its right-end mirror joins the first family in (1.2) to

\[
                         \{1v0\,10:v\in\mathcal D_{s-1}\}.
\tag{1.3}
\]

Applying both relations joins (1.3) to

\[
                         \{10\,1w0\,10:w\in\mathcal D_{s-2}\}.
\tag{1.4}
\]

Both endpoint relations preserve every interior return cut.  Hence the
four families (0.1), characterized by having no fixed interior return cut,
form one component and no root with such a cut enters it.

For the distinguished two-endpoint target, the four families occupy the
four Boolean cells with multiplicities (0.2).  A component choice selects
one complete shore.  The four shores are coordinate images under
\(1,L,R,LR\), so they only permute the four cells.

### Theorem 1.1 (canonical statewise no-go)

Every factor obtained by choosing shores of the canonical \(V_4\) overlay
has maximum Boolean-cell load at least \(C_s\).  Therefore it violates cap
\(p\) whenever \(C_s>p\), independently of (0.3), of discrepancy, and of
the exterior carrier common to the four cells.

#### Proof

The component containing \(\mathcal B_s\) is indivisible.  On each of its
four shores the multiset of cell loads is (0.2).  Its largest entry is
\(C_s\).  Other components add nonnegative load and cannot reduce that
entry.  A common exterior carrier injects the four cells into physical
targets without changing their multiplicities. \(\square\)

Thus the partitions suggested by separate left and right overlays are not
two independently signable partitions of the same occurrences.  Their join
has already fused the relevant blocks into one ownership component.

## 2. Exact dense `D_4` fringe packet coverage

Let \(a_t\) count ordered binary trees of size \(t\) containing no fringe
subtree of size four, and put

\[
                         A(z)=\sum_{t\ge0}a_tz^t.
\tag{2.1}
\]

A tree is empty or is formed from two avoiding children, except that all
fourteen trees of total size four must be removed.  Therefore

\[
                         A(z)=1+zA(z)^2-14z^4.
\tag{2.2}
\]

At \(z=1/4\), the discriminant of the quadratic solution is positive:

\[
                         1-4z+56z^5={7\over128}>0.
\tag{2.3}
\]

The coefficients are nonnegative, so Pringsheim's theorem gives radius of
convergence strictly larger than \(1/4\).  Hence

\[
                         {a_t\over C_t}=o(1)
\tag{2.4}
\]

exponentially.

For a nonavoiding filling, choose its first size-four fringe subtree in
preorder.  Replacing that subtree by any of the fourteen size-four trees
does not change its root position and does not change which size-four fringe
root is first.  Thus the nonavoiding fillings split into blocks of exactly
fourteen.

Apply this separately to the fillings \(u,v,v,w\) in (0.1).  The resulting
packet numbers are

\[
 {C_s-a_s\over14},\qquad
 2{C_{s-1}-a_{s-1}\over14},\qquad
 {C_{s-2}-a_{s-2}\over14}.
\tag{2.5}
\]

Their root supports are disjoint and their union misses exactly \(R_s\) in
(0.4).

### Theorem 2.1 (independent exact legality)

In every block in (2.5), replace the canonical fourteen rooted paths by
either the canonical \(D_4\) factor or any complete non-coordinate
\(D_4\)-port factor with the same ports.  Every collection of such block
choices is one exact port-rooted factor on the growing hole.

#### Proof

Fix one outer one-hole context.  The two local \(D_4\) factors have the
same fourteen root/complement port pairs, enumerate every local \(X\)-state
once, and enumerate every adjacent-union \(Y\)-colour once.  The rooted
context functor therefore gives identical complete ownership ledgers on the
two block shores.

Distinct first-fringe blocks have disjoint root sets.  Replacing either
complete shore in each block partitions the same union of \(X\)- and
\(Y\)-tokens, and the outer root attached to every row remains fixed.
Therefore all block choices compose and preserve exactness and all growing-
hole ports. \(\square\)

This proves genuine asymptotically complete packet coverage and exact
legality.  It does not yet say that the packets move the protected target.

## 3. A target-cell invisibility invariant

Consider a packet in (2.5).  Its size-four hole lies wholly inside the
variable filling \(u,v\), or \(w\); the optional left and right endpoint
atoms displayed explicitly in (0.1) lie outside it.  In a row rooted at a
local port \(P\), both local boundary states

\[
                              P,qquad J\setminus P
\tag{3.1}
\]

occur in the protected two-endpoint window.

### Lemma 3.1 (strict-subhole invisibility)

The intersection target of the protected window is independent of every
port-valid factor chosen in the size-four subhole.

#### Proof

All local states outside the subhole are common.  Inside it, the protected
window contains both states in (3.1).  Its local intersection is therefore
contained in

\[
                         P\cap(J\setminus P)=\varnothing.
\tag{3.2}
\]

The common exterior intersection is unchanged, so the physical target is
the same for every local factor choice. \(\square\)

### Theorem 3.2 (fringe-cell indivisibility)

For every factor generated by all independent fringe choices of Theorem
2.1, the four distinguished Boolean-cell loads on \(\mathcal B_s\) remain

\[
                         (C_s,C_{s-1},C_{s-1},C_{s-2})
\tag{3.3}
\]

up to a common permutation coming from an optional global endpoint
coordinate relabelling.  In particular the maximum remains \(C_s\).

#### Proof

The outer word form in (0.1) determines which endpoint atoms are present
and hence which Boolean cell contains the distinguished occurrence.  Every
first-fringe packet lies inside the displayed variable filling, so Lemma
3.1 says its factor state cannot change that target.  Summing over every
packet and over the exceptional roots proves (3.3).  A global endpoint
coordinate relabelling only renames the cells. \(\square\)

This is stronger than saying that the fringe packets fail to split the
canonical ownership component.  They do supply many independent exact
factor coordinates, and so can alter the ownership overlay and many shorter
or crossing shadows.  What they cannot alter is the particular
two-endpoint cell statistic whose preload is \(M_s\).

## 4. Boundary-active packets are the only escape

Call a packet **boundary-active** for the protected window if its changed
slab meets at least one endpoint state of that window.  Lemma 3.1 proves the
converse statement:

\[
 \boxed{
 \text{every port-preserving packet strictly inside the window is
 cell-diagonal.}}
\tag{4.1}
\]

Thus neither replacing the canonical \(D_4\) factor by the new pair-transfer
factor nor adjoining all its \(H_4\)-conjugates inside the dense fringe
atlas redistributes any of (3.3).  Their nonzero first-insertion and higher
interval profiles can matter only when the packet is installed across a
relevant boundary or is tested by a window which does not contain both
local ports.

For \(\theta\le4/(M_s/C_s)\), a successful four-cell theorem must therefore
provide boundary-active independently assignable blocks covering enough of
the principal \(C_s\) family to lower its cell from \(C_s\) to the actual
residual capacity.  The abstract discrepancy theorem becomes applicable
only after those blocks, not the interior fringe blocks, have been proved.

For \(\theta>4/(M_s/C_s)\), at least

\[
                         \left\lceil{M_s\over p}\right\rceil
\tag{4.2}
\]

physical cells are necessary.  A boundary-active multistate library must
both exceed four cells and fragment the occurrence mass.  The four-state
fixed-port \(D_4/H_4\) table proves only that one marked row has a larger
menu; it supplies no partition of \(\mathcal B_s\) into independently
assignable boundary blocks.

## 5. Decision

The non-coordinate \(D_4\) fringe construction passes two audits and fails
the decisive third:

1. **coverage:** it covers \(M_s-o(M_s)\) roots in exact fourteen-row
   blocks;
2. **legality:** every selection of block shores is a literal exact
   port-rooted factor;
3. **protected action:** every covered block is strictly internal and hence
   fixes its Boolean target cell.

Therefore the dense fringe library does not fragment the canonical
two-endpoint preload in the sense needed for cap descent.  The remaining
problem is no longer abstract discrepancy.  It is the construction of a
boundary-active non-coordinate packet atlas, with complete carrier and
background ledgers, which covers a positive enough fraction of every large
root family and has four states below the preload threshold or at least
\(\lceil M_s/p\rceil\) states above it.
