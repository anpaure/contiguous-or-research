# Combining the two unconditional \(q=1\) cycles: the exact exchange graph, complete pentagon lattice, and legal-descent gate

Date: 2026-07-26

Method: pure mathematics only.  No computation, search, solver, or web
input is used.

## 0. Outcome

Work first on the odd ground

\[
             \Omega=[2m+1],\qquad m\ge3,
\]

where the two unconditional one-sided inputs live on the same owner set
\({\cal M}=\binom\Omega m\):

* a GMM tight two-level enumeration contracts to a Hamilton Johnson cycle
  \(C^-\) which covers every lower colour in
  \({\cal L}=\binom\Omega{m-1}\); and
* a middle-levels Hamilton cycle projects to a Hamilton Johnson cycle
  \(C^+\) whose upper colours are exactly
  \({\cal U}=\binom\Omega{m+1}\), once each.

Terminology matters here.  The strict lower-rainbow GMM saturating core has
only \(N_-\) owners.  Its full-owner tight-enumeration completion has
\(W\) edges and therefore cannot be lower-injective because
\(W>N_-\); it is lower-complete with exactly \(E\) units of surplus.
The full-owner form is the one which can be compared directly with the
middle-levels upper-rainbow cycle.  The shorter strict-rainbow core requires
owner-changing exchanges in addition to the trades studied below.

Put

\[
 W=|{\cal M}|=|{\cal U}|,qquad
 N_-=|{\cal L}|={m\over m+2}W,qquad
 E=W-N_-={2W\over m+2}.                              \tag{0.1}
\]

The following conclusions are exact.

1. The symmetric difference of the two cycles decomposes into balanced
   red--blue alternating circuits.  Flipping any subfamily preserves every
   middle-owner degree, giving an exact Boolean cube of spanning
   \(2\)-factors.  Lower coverage and Hamilton connectivity are additional
   constraints on that cube, not automatic consequences of alternation.

2. If \(x\) is the current factor, with lower and upper load vectors
   \(l=B_-x\), \(u=B_+x\), then a circuit subfamily \(S\) is lower-legal
   precisely when
   \[
       l(R)+\sum_{j\in S}(B_-z_j)(R)\ge1
             \qquad(R\in{\cal L}).                    \tag{0.2}
   \]
   This is the exact exchange graph.  Its upper objective is the literal
   repeat excess
   \[
                   R^+(x)=\sum_{U\in{\cal U}}(u(U)-1)_+.       \tag{0.3}
   \]

3. For fixed owners and a fixed exact lower-load vector, every trade
   satisfies the point invariant
   \[
                   P_{m+1}B_+z=0.                    \tag{0.4}
   \]
   More strongly, this is the **complete integral linear invariant**:
   \[
    \boxed{
      B_+\bigl(\ker_{\mathbb Z}B_0\cap
                    \ker_{\mathbb Z}B_-\bigr)
       =\ker_{\mathbb Z}P_{m+1}.}                    \tag{0.5}
   \]
   The inclusion is the diamond valuation.  Surjectivity is generated by
   the six-coordinate complemented-pentagon trades.  Dually, the original
   pentagon tables give
   \[
      B_-\bigl(\ker_{\mathbb Z}B_0\cap
                    \ker_{\mathbb Z}B_+\bigr)
       =\ker_{\mathbb Z}P_{m-1}.                     \tag{0.5a}
   \]

4. Consequently, fixing the GMM lower load \(l\), an upper load \(u'\)
   is reachable at the **integer incidence-lattice level** if and only if
   it has the forced point margins
   \[
                   P_{m+1}u'
          =2P_m\mathbf1_{\cal M}-P_{m-1}l.            \tag{0.6}
   \]
   This does not assert that the corresponding signed trade is conformal
   to a simple Hamilton cycle.

5. Write the full-owner lower load as
   \[
                         l=\mathbf1_{\cal L}+d,
          \qquad d\ge0,\qquad \sum_Rd(R)=E.           \tag{0.7}
   \]
   Exact upper rainbowness is possible with this fixed lower load only if
   the lower surplus is a regular one-design:
   \[
    \boxed{
       P_{m-1}d=\delta\mathbf1_\Omega,qquad
       \delta={(m-1)E\over2m+1}.}                    \tag{0.8}
   \]
   Conversely, (0.8) is sufficient at the incidence-lattice level by
   (0.5).  For every fixed-lower-load state,
   \[
    \boxed{
      R^+(x)\ge
       {\|P_{m-1}d-\delta\mathbf1\|_1\over2(m+1)}.}   \tag{0.9}
   \]

6. Four-coordinate lower-neutral alternating switches have zero upper
   action.  Four-coordinate rectangles which move lower surplus do have
   upper action and can rebalance (0.8), but they need two available lower
   surplus tokens and may split a Hamilton cycle.  The six-coordinate
   pentagon is the first lower-neutral upper-moving primitive.

Thus the linear/integral trade lattice is completely understood: there is
no hidden character beyond point margins.  The missing result is nonlinear
and physical.  One must find a sequence or bounded batches of **eligible**
rectangle and pentagon switches satisfying (0.2), keeping only
\(o(W/H)\) components, and driving (0.3) to \(o(W)\).  A distance-two
repeat/hole separation gives an exact local minimum for the pentagon
library, so existence of the two endpoint cycles alone does not prove
descent or connectivity.

## 1. The three incidence maps

Let \({\cal E}\) be the edges of \(J(2m+1,m)\).  For
\(e=\{X,Y\}\), define

\[
                         L(e)=X\cap Y,qquad U(e)=X\cup Y.      \tag{1.1}
\]

On the free edge module define

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 B_0e&=e_X+e_Y,\\
 B_-e&=e_{L(e)},\\
 B_+e&=e_{U(e)}.
 \end{aligned}                                                \tag{1.2}
\]

For a Hamilton cycle indicator \(x\),

\[
                         B_0x=2\mathbf1_{\cal M}.              \tag{1.3}
\]

The GMM tight enumeration gives a state satisfying

\[
                         B_-x\ge\mathbf1_{\cal L},             \tag{1.4}
\]

while the middle-levels upper projection gives a state satisfying

\[
                         B_+x=\mathbf1_{\cal U}.               \tag{1.5}
\]

These are separate integral states of the same physical edge space.

## 2. Exact alternating-circuit exchange graph

Colour the edges of \(C^-\setminus C^+\) red and those of
\(C^+\setminus C^-\) blue.  At every owner, red degree equals blue
degree, because both original graphs have degree two.  Therefore the
red--blue symmetric difference decomposes into edge-disjoint alternating
circuits

\[
                         \Gamma_1,\ldots,\Gamma_s.              \tag{2.1}
\]

The circuits may repeat vertices, but at every visited owner each circuit
has equal red and blue degree.  Put

\[
 z_j=\mathbf1_{E_{\rm blue}(\Gamma_j)}
       -\mathbf1_{E_{\rm red}(\Gamma_j)}.                       \tag{2.2}
\]

Then \(B_0z_j=0\).  For \(t\in\{0,1\}^s\), define

\[
                         x(t)=x^-+\sum_jt_jz_j.                 \tag{2.3}
\]

### Theorem 2.1 (alternating-circuit cube)

Every \(x(t)\) is the indicator of a simple spanning \(2\)-factor, and
\(x(0)=x^-\), \(x(\mathbf1)=x^+\).  Its lower-covered states are exactly
the binary points satisfying (0.2).  Requiring one Hamilton cycle further
restricts to those points for which the \(2\)-factor in (2.3) is
connected.

#### Proof

On one alternating circuit, every removed red incidence at an owner is
replaced by one blue incidence.  Edge-disjointness permits any subfamily
to be flipped, preserving degree two at every owner.  Red edges are
removed before their blue replacements are added, so the result remains
simple.  Equation (0.2) is just the coordinatewise statement
\(B_-x(t)\ge\mathbf1\).  A finite simple two-regular graph is Hamilton
exactly when it is connected. \(\square\)

This theorem proves unconstrained \(2\)-factor connectivity between the
two one-sided cycles.  It does not prove connectivity of the lower-covered
induced subgraph of the cube.  A circuit which removes the sole occurrence
of a lower colour cannot be flipped until another selected circuit restores
that colour.  Cyclic dependencies between such circuits force a batch
move and can isolate a state under single-circuit exchanges.

For an arbitrary signed trade \(z\), the exact objective change is

\[
 \Delta_zR^+
  =\sum_U\left[(u(U)+(B_+z)(U)-1)_+-(u(U)-1)_+\right].         \tag{2.4}
\]

If \(B_+z\in\{-1,0,1\}^{\cal U}\), with positive support \(A\) and
negative support \(D\), this becomes

\[
 \boxed{
 \Delta_zR^+
  =|\{U\in A:u(U)\ge1\}|
   -|\{U\in D:u(U)\ge2\}|.}                                  \tag{2.5}
\]

## 3. Diamond valuation and point margins

For \(k\)-sets, let \(P_k\) be point-versus-set incidence.  On every
Johnson edge,

\[
 \boxed{
       P_{m-1}B_-+P_{m+1}B_+=P_mB_0.}                          \tag{3.1}
\]

Indeed, for every point \(v\),

\[
 \mathbf1_{v\in X\cap Y}+\mathbf1_{v\in X\cup Y}
   =\mathbf1_{v\in X}+\mathbf1_{v\in Y}.                     \tag{3.2}
\]

Thus a fixed-owner, exact-lower-load trade lies in the upper point kernel,
proving the inclusion ``\(\subseteq\)'' in (0.5).

For a full-owner lower-covered factor, write (0.7).  Since every cycle has
\(W\) edges, \(\sum_Rd(R)=W-N_-=E\).  If \(u=B_+x\), subtract the
fully upper-rainbow target from (3.1).  Symmetry and total incidence give

\[
 \boxed{
       P_{m+1}(u-\mathbf1_{\cal U})
        =\delta\mathbf1_\Omega-P_{m-1}d,\qquad
       \delta={(m-1)E\over2m+1}.}                              \tag{3.3}
\]

This proves necessity of (0.8).  Moreover, because
\(\sum_Uu(U)=|{\cal U}|=W\),

\[
                         \|u-\mathbf1\|_1=2R^+(x).             \tag{3.4}
\]

Every column of \(P_{m+1}\) has \(m+1\) ones, so (3.3)--(3.4) prove
(0.9).

Notice the scale.  Since \(E=O(W/m)\), the right side of (0.9) can force
exact failure, but its universal maximum is only \(O(W/m)\).  Point
margins therefore do not by themselves obstruct the coefficient-one
target \(R^+=o(W)\) for a full tight enumeration.  They are potentially
linear for the shorter fixed-owner lower-rainbow core, where the owner
point counts need not be balanced.

## 4. The integer point kernel is generated by local squares

We need one elementary lattice lemma.  Let \({\cal K}=\binom{[n]}k\),
where \(2\le k\le n-2\), and let \(P=P_k\).  For an
\((k-2)\)-set \(A\) and distinct \(a,b,c,d\notin A\), put

\[
 q(A;a,b,c,d)
 =e_{Aab}+e_{Acd}-e_{Aac}-e_{Abd}.                            \tag{4.1}
\]

### Lemma 4.1 (square generation)

The vectors in (4.1) generate \(\ker_{\mathbb Z}P\).

#### Proof

Work modulo the lattice generated by (4.1).  For distinct points \(i,j\)
and a \((k-1)\)-set \(A\) avoiding them, put

\[
                         d_{ij}(A)=e_{A+i}-e_{A+j}.             \tag{4.2}
\]

If \(A=C+a\) and \(A'=C+b\) are adjacent in
\(J(n-2,k-1)\), then

\[
 d_{ij}(A)-d_{ij}(A')
 =e_{Cai}-e_{Caj}-e_{Cbi}+e_{Cbj},                            \tag{4.3}
\]

which is a signed square (4.1).  The graph \(J(n-2,k-1)\) is connected,
so the class of (4.2) is independent of \(A\); call it \(d_{ij}\).

Adjacent \(k\)-sets differ by one exchange, so every difference
\(e_S-e_T\) is a sum of the \(d_{ij}\)'s.  Fixing one base set \(B\),
the quotient module is therefore generated by \(e_B\) and \(n-1\)
differences \(d_{i i_0}\).  Their images under \(P\) are

\[
                 \mathbf1_B,qquad e_i-e_{i_0}.                \tag{4.4}
\]

These are integrally independent: the total coordinate sum first forces
the coefficient of \(\mathbf1_B\) to be zero, and the remaining point
differences are independent.  Hence the quotient maps injectively under
\(P\), proving that its kernel is zero. \(\square\)

## 5. Pentagon trades lift every square

The complemented six-coordinate pentagon gives a fixed-owner,
lower-neutral trade whose upper action is the difference of two perfect
matchings of a six-set whose union is one alternating six-cycle.  In pair-
complement notation it has form

\[
                    \sum_{e\in M_1}e_{K+(D\setminus e)}
                    -\sum_{e\in M_2}e_{K+(D\setminus e)},      \tag{5.1}
\]

where \(|K|=m-3\), \(|D|=6\), and \(M_1,M_2\) are disjoint perfect
matchings of \(D\).  Every ordered alternating six-cycle is a coordinate
relabeling of the explicit pentagon table, so every vector (5.1) has an
integral lift in

\[
                         {\cal T}:=\ker_{\mathbb Z}B_0
                                  \cap\ker_{\mathbb Z}B_-.     \tag{5.2}
\]

### Theorem 5.1 (complete upper image of the protected trade lattice)

Equation (0.5) holds.

#### Proof

Only surjectivity remains.  By Lemma 4.1, it suffices to lift an upper
square

\[
                 e_{Aab}+e_{Acd}-e_{Aac}-e_{Abd},              \tag{5.3}
\]

where now \(|A|=m-1\).  Choose two distinct points \(e,f\in A\), put

\[
                  K=A\setminus\{e,f\},qquad
                  D=\{a,b,c,d,e,f\}.                           \tag{5.4}
\]

On \(D\), consider the perfect matchings

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 M_1&=\{ab,cd,ef\},\\
 M_2&=\{ac,bd,ef\},\\
 M_3&=\{ad,be,cf\}.
 \end{aligned}                                                \tag{5.5}
\]

Both \(M_1\cup M_3\) and \(M_3\cup M_2\) are alternating six-cycles.
Hence \(M_1-M_3\) and \(M_3-M_2\) lift to two pentagon trades.  Their
sum has upper action \(M_1-M_2\).  The common \(ef\) target cancels, and
taking pair complements inside \(D\) leaves exactly (5.3).  Thus every
square generator lifts.  Lemma 4.1 completes the proof. \(\square\)

### Corollary 5.2 (complete linear-invariant classification)

Fix an owner-degree vector and an exact lower-load vector.  Two integer
upper-load vectors occur in the same projected trade-lattice coset if and
only if they have the same point margins.  In particular, (0.8) is
sufficient for converting the current upper load to \(\mathbf1_{\cal U}\)
at the integer incidence-lattice level.

The dual statement (0.5a) follows from the same proof using the original,
uncomplemented rooted-pentagon tables: their upper ledger is fixed and
their lower action is the difference of two perfect matchings on six
labels.  Hence, starting from the middle-levels upper-rainbow cycle, point
margins are likewise the only integral linear invariants to repairing the
lower load while protecting the exact upper ledger.

The words “integer incidence-lattice” are load-bearing.  The lift supplied
by Theorem 5.1 is a signed sum of pentagon tables.  Its negative edges need
not all occur in the current cycle, its positive edges may already occur,
and intermediate graphs need not be connected or even Boolean.

### Corollary 5.3 (strict GMM lower-rainbow core)

Let \(V\subseteq{\cal M}\), \(|V|=N_-\), be the owner set of the strict
GMM lower-rainbow saturating core.  Fixing \(V\) and its exact lower load
\(\mathbf1_{\cal L}\), a \(0/1\) upper load \(b\) of size \(N_-\) is
reachable at the incidence-lattice level if and only if

\[
          P_{m+1}b
            =2P_m\mathbf1_V-P_{m-1}\mathbf1_{\cal L}.          \tag{5.6}
\]

Thus the point-margin condition is not only necessary but the complete
linear obstruction for the fixed strict-rainbow core.  If no such \(b\)
exists, no lower-neutral alternating trade can make the core upper-
injective.  If such a \(b\) exists, pentagon sums solve the signed lattice
equations, but Boolean and cycle legality remain unproved.

## 6. Lower-load motion: the rectangle primitive

Let \(|K|=m-2\) and take four labels \(a,b,c,d\) outside \(K\).  The
middle owners

\[
 K+ab,\quad K+ac,\quad K+cd,\quad K+bd                         \tag{6.1}
\]

form a Johnson four-cycle.  Replacing the opposite edges

\[
 (K+ab)(K+ac),\quad(K+cd)(K+bd)                                \tag{6.2}
\]

by the other opposite pair gives the signed colour changes

\[
 \Delta_-=
       e_{K+c}+e_{K+b}-e_{K+a}-e_{K+d},                       \tag{6.3}
\]

\[
 \Delta_+=
       e_{K+acd}+e_{K+abd}-e_{K+abc}-e_{K+bcd}.                \tag{6.4}
\]

This is an alternating-circuit switch and preserves every middle degree.
It preserves lower coverage whenever the two negative lower colours have
load at least two.  It then moves two units of the surplus vector \(d\),
and (3.1) says exactly how their point discrepancy is transferred to the
upper shore.

If one additionally requires \(\Delta_-=0\), every four-coordinate trade
has \(\Delta_+=0\): the local signed edge matrix has zero row sums and,
by middle neutrality, is skew-symmetric, so its column sums vanish.  Thus
four-coordinate switches cannot replace the pentagon inside the protected
lower-load fibre.

A rectangle which is legal as a degree switch need not preserve one cycle.
Removing its two selected edges cuts a Hamilton cycle into two paths; the
new port pairing either reconnects those paths crosswise into one cycle or
closes them separately into two cycles.  The cyclic order of the four ports
decides which case occurs.  This is the first nonlinear obstruction absent
from the lattice theorem.

## 7. Pentagon descent and its exact local lock

For an eligible pentagon, write its upper action as

\[
                         \Delta_+=\mathbf1_A-\mathbf1_D,       \tag{7.1}
\]

where \(|A|=|D|=3\).  Formula (2.5) gives

\[
 \Delta R^+
 =|\{U\in A:u(U)\ge1\}|
  -|\{U\in D:u(U)\ge2\}|.                                  \tag{7.2}
\]

Thus it is a strict descent precisely when the repeated negative targets
outnumber the already occupied positive targets.

All six targets in an embedded pentagon have Johnson diameter at most two.
Let

\[
       {\cal R}=\{U:u(U)\ge2\},\qquad
       {\cal H}=\{U:u(U)=0\}.                                  \tag{7.3}
\]

If

\[
                         d_J({\cal R},{\cal H})\ge3,            \tag{7.4}
\]

then neither orientation of any eligible pentagon decreases \(R^+\).
Indeed, a pentagon containing one repeated negative target contains no
positive hole, so all three additions cost one while at most three
removals gain one; without a repeated negative target there is no gain at
all.

This proves a genuine nonlinear local-minimum obstruction despite the
complete lattice generation in Theorem 5.1.  Larger-diameter compound
pentagon chains may cross the lock, but their simultaneous Boolean and
Hamilton eligibility is a new theorem.

## 8. Exact combined target

The two unconditional cycles reduce the desired full-owner two-sided
\(q=1\) cycle to the following integral system:

\[
 \boxed{
 \begin{aligned}
 B_0x&=2\mathbf1_{\cal M},\\
 B_+x&=\mathbf1_{\cal U},\\
 B_-x&=\mathbf1_{\cal L}+d,qquad d\ge0,\\
 P_{m-1}d&=\delta\mathbf1_\Omega,\\
 x&\in\{0,1\}^{\cal E},\\
 \operatorname{supp}(x)&\text{ is connected.}
 \end{aligned}}                                               \tag{8.1}
\]

Here \(\sum_Rd(R)=E\), and the fourth line follows from the first three
but displays the exact design constraint which a lower-covered upper-
rainbow cycle must satisfy.

At the relaxed lattice level, Theorem 5.1 closes the common-linear-
invariant question.  At the factor level, the unresolved requirements are:

1. route the lower surplus to a near-regular design using legal rectangles;
2. realize the required point-kernel correction by eligible pentagons or
   larger compound trades;
3. keep the number of temporary/final components \(o(W/H)\); and
4. defeat the distance-two local lock.

Therefore the correct verdict is neither connectivity nor a global
invariant no-go.  The exchange lattice is connected modulo the explicit
point margins, but legal Hamilton-cycle connectivity and monotone descent
remain open.  Any future positive proof can focus exclusively on
eligibility, component control, and nonlocal transport; no further linear
colour invariant can exist.
