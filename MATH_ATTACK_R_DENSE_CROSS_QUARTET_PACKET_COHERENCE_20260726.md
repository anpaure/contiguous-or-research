## 0. Outcome

Let \(m\) be even and sufficiently large, and fix a Gaussian height

\[
H=\lfloor A_0\sqrt m\rfloor
\]

with \(A_0>0\) fixed. Choose a power of two \(r\) with

\[
2H\le r=o(m).                                           \tag{0.1}
\]

All conclusions below hold uniformly for such \(r\). For the one-round
linear-margin specialization, choose the first power of two at least
\(2H\); then

\[
2H\le r<4H.                                             \tag{0.1a}
\]

There are two actual, coordinate-unrelated, owner-disjoint packet
near-factors \(F^0,F^1\) on one common owner set

\[
S=W-L,\qquad W=\binom{2m}{m},\qquad
L=e^{-\Omega(m)}W=o(W/H),                              \tag{0.2}
\]

with all of the following properties.

1. Every selected object is a physical \(Q_r\) packet carrying a closed
   certified isometric \(C_{2r}\)-factor. There is no packet seam, port, or
   collar term. The finest owner-overlay components between \(F^0\) and
   \(F^1\) are unions of complete old and new cycles. We group them into
   full \(C_4\)-macroblocks, which remain independently switchable.

2. Apart from one possible local carrier axis, every active packet axis
   joins two different quartets of one fixed quartet decomposition. Thus
   every selected packet has at least \(r-1\) physically cross-quartet
   axes. Every depth-\(q\) window with \(2\le q<r\) contains a crossing
   axis, and at \(q=1\) the crossing fraction is at least \(1-1/r\).

3. On a changed owner bank of exact pre-leave size

   \[
   B_m^{\rm raw}
   =576\binom{2m-12}{m-5}
   =\left(\frac9{64}+o(1)\right)W,                    \tag{0.3}
   \]

   the two endpoints use internal versus cross-quartet perfect matchings.
   The actual changed mass after the common packet leave is

   \[
   B_m=\left(\frac9{64}+o(1)\right)W.                 \tag{0.4}
   \]

4. At every depth \(1\le q\le H\), the endpoint-energy difference is
   zero separately on the lower and upper target spaces. Hence, with
   those spaces placed in their orthogonal direct sum,

   \[
   \Delta_q=0,                                       \tag{0.5}
   \]

   while its owner-component effects satisfy

   \[
   \boxed{
   V_q=2\frac qr B_m,\qquad
   A_q\ge3\frac qr B_m,\qquad
   \Gamma_q=A_q-V_q\ge\frac qr B_m.}                 \tag{0.6}
   \]

   Consequently

   \[
   \Gamma_q
   \ge\left(\frac{9q}{64r}+o(1)\right)W
   >|\Delta_q|.                                      \tag{0.7}
   \]

5. For arbitrary fixed nonnegative signed-layer weights
   \(w_{q,-},w_{q,+}\), the same component choice is used at every depth,
   and

   \[
   \Gamma_{\le H}
   \ge
   \frac{B_m}{2r}
   \sum_{q=1}^{H}q\bigl(w_{q,-}+w_{q,+}\bigr),
   \qquad
   \Delta_{\le H}=0.                                 \tag{0.8}
   \]

   Hence the exact common-bias component theorem gives one integral
   packet near-factor \(F_x\) satisfying

   \[
   Q(F_x)
   \le Q(F^0)-\frac{\Gamma_{\le H}}4
   =Q(F^1)-\frac{\Gamma_{\le H}}4.                   \tag{0.9}
   \]

This is a genuine positive answer to the two-factor coherence question.
It also disproves the proposed universal inequality
\(\Gamma\le|\Delta|\) for fixed local packet pairs when the two shores
are allowed to transport mass between profile cells. It does not
contradict the immutable-profile theorem in Section 9.

The result does not prove \(HCRT\), \(CPM\), or coefficient one. The
strict coherence is generated by one differing carrier slot per changed
packet. When \(H/r\to0\), its one-step depth-\(q\) margin is only
\((q/r)B_m=o(W)\). The dense common cross-axis preload passes the new Hall
necessary condition, but it does not route the floor defect. A complete
hierarchy must either iterate \(\Omega(r/q)\) such coherent carrier
stages while retaining the common all-depth sign, or compare endpoints
which differ on that many dispersed slots without allowing their direct
overlay to expand into giant product components.

The report also independently audits the new full-internal-quartet Hall
cut. Its exponent \(e^{-A_0^2/11}\), positive-density profile window, and
crossing-axis toll are correct. Two precision corrections are recorded in
Section 8.

## 1. Floor energy and the component quantities

Let the controlled target index be

\[
a=(q,\epsilon),\qquad 1\le q\le H,\quad
\epsilon\in\{-,+\}.
\]

The two packet near-factors cover the same \(S\) owners. Put

\[
N_a=\binom{2m}{m+\epsilon q},\qquad
c_a=\left\lfloor\frac{S}{N_a}\right\rfloor.          \tag{1.1}
\]

For a load vector \(\mu\), define the weighted integral floor energy

\[
Q(\mu)
=\sum_a w_a\sum_T
(\mu_a(T)-c_a)(\mu_a(T)-c_a-1),\qquad w_a\ge0.       \tag{1.2}
\]

Before multiplication by \(w_a\), every summand is a nonnegative even
integer. For arbitrary real weights the resulting weighted summand need
not be integral, but remains nonnegative. The fixed floor subtraction
does not affect comparisons between factors of the same owner mass.

Let \(\mathcal K\) be any chosen partition into independently switchable
trade blocks, each a union of actual owner-overlay components of
\(F^0,F^1\); their owner sets are disjoint. In this construction the
chosen blocks are the full \(C_4\)-macroblocks.
For \(K\in\mathcal K\), let \(\mu_K^i\) be the complete physical
target-load contribution of the \(i\)-shore and put

\[
\delta_K=\mu_K^1-\mu_K^0.                            \tag{1.3}
\]

In the weighted direct-sum target space define

\[
\begin{aligned}
D&=\sum_K\delta_K,\\
A&=\|D\|_w^2,\\
V&=\sum_K\|\delta_K\|_w^2,\\
\Gamma&=A-V,\\
\Delta&=Q(F^1)-Q(F^0).
\end{aligned}                                       \tag{1.4}
\]

Independent selection of the \(F^1\)-shore with common probability \(p\)
gives the exact identity

\[
\mathbb E_pQ(F_X)
=(1-p)Q(F^0)+pQ(F^1)-p(1-p)\Gamma.                  \tag{1.5}
\]

Indeed, with independent Bernoulli variables \(X_K\),
\(\mu_X=\mu^0+\sum_KX_K\delta_K\), and

\[
\mathbb E\left\|\sum_KX_K\delta_K\right\|_w^2
=p^2\|D\|_w^2+p(1-p)\sum_K\|\delta_K\|_w^2.
\]

All linear terms in the floor polynomial interpolate affinely between
the endpoints. Subtracting the affine interpolation of the quadratic
terms gives exactly \(-p(1-p)(A-V)\), proving (1.5).

Thus \(\Gamma>|\Delta|\) produces an integral child below both endpoints.
When \(\Delta=0\), \(p=1/2\) and conditional expectation give

\[
Q(F_x)\le Q(F^0)-\Gamma/4.                           \tag{1.6}
\]

All statements below concern literal packet-cycle histograms. No orbit
projection or formal shadow vector is used.

## 2. A dense crossing-axis reservoir resolution

Reserve three disjoint coordinate sets

\[
Z,\ U,\ V,\qquad |Z|=|U|=|V|=4.                    \tag{2.1}
\]

The set \(Z\) will be a frozen anchor. The quartets \(U,V\) carry the
coherent local transport. Partition the remaining \(2m-12\) coordinates
into quartets.

### Lemma 2.1 (cross-quartet perfect frame)

There is a perfect matching \(M^\times\) of the remaining coordinates
such that every matching edge meets two different residual quartets.

#### Proof

Pair residual quartets and match their four coordinates bijectively. If
an odd number of quartets remains, use three of them and put two matching
edges between each consecutive pair in a three-cycle. Every coordinate is
used once and no edge is internal to one quartet. \(\square\)

Write

\[
2M=2m-12,\qquad M=m-6.                               \tag{2.2}
\]

For a residual owner, call an edge of \(M^\times\) split when exactly one
of its endpoints is selected. For an integer \(d=o(M)\), every owner with
at least \(d\) split edges belongs to a canonical \(Q_d\) packet: select
the first \(d\) split edges in a fixed order, vary their orientations,
and freeze every other pair status and orientation.

The selected split set is constant on the resulting packet. Therefore
these packets are disjoint and partition every owner with at least \(d\)
split edges.

### Lemma 2.2 (exponentially small residual leave)

Uniformly for residual ranks \(M+t\) with \(|t|\le12\) and
\(d=o(M)\), the fraction of residual owners with fewer than \(d\) split
edges is \(e^{-\Omega(M)}\).

#### Proof

Before rank conditioning, the number of split edges is
\(\operatorname{Bin}(M,1/2)\). More explicitly, the number of all
pair-status assignments with exactly \(s\) split edges is

\[
2^M\binom Ms,
\]

while the total number is \(4^M\). Hence the unconditioned bad proportion
is

\[
2^{-M}\sum_{s<d}\binom Ms=e^{-\Omega(M)}.            \tag{2.3}
\]

Indeed, for \(d=o(M)\),

\[
\sum_{s<d}\binom Ms
\le d\left(\frac{eM}{d}\right)^d=e^{o(M)},
\]

so (2.3) is \(\exp(-(\log 2-o(1))M)\).

The fixed-rank layer \(\binom{[2M]}{M+t}\), for bounded \(t\), has size
\(4^M/\operatorname{poly}(M)\). Conditioning therefore costs only a
polynomial factor, which is absorbed by the exponential bound.
\(\square\)

In every unchanged owner fibre use \(d=r\). In the special local bank use
\(d=r-1\) and adjoin one local carrier direction on \(U\cup V\). Thus
every selected object is a physical \(Q_r\).

Every background packet has \(r\) crossing axes. Every special packet has
at least \(r-1\), and has \(r\) when its local carrier is itself
cross-quartet. Install identically synchronized affine copies of the
certified recursive isometric \(C_{2r}\)-factor. Its two-sided labelled
shadow maps are injective through depth \(r/2\). This is why (0.1), rather
than an arbitrary common-order Hamming factor, is used.

If the one local carrier is internal, the two occurrences of that
direction in the word \(\pi\pi\) are the only noncrossing direction
occurrences. Therefore the exact crossing-window fraction is

\[
\phi_q=
\begin{cases}
1-1/r,&q=1,\\
1,&2\le q<r.
\end{cases}                                         \tag{2.4}
\]

This is stronger than the weighted crossing-axis count required by the
Hall theorem audited in Section 8.

Applying Lemma 2.2 in the finitely many \(Z,U,V\) local fibres gives one
common owner leave satisfying (0.2).

## 3. The local \(k=3\) alternating \(C_4\) bank

Partition the six two-subsets of \(U\) into their three complementary
pairs, and do the same for \(V\). Match the three pair classes. Let

\[
\mathcal R\subseteq\binom U2\times\binom V2          \tag{3.1}
\]

be the union of the resulting three copies of \(K_{2,2}\). Thus
\(|\mathcal R|=12\), and every two-subset on either side has degree two.

For \((I,T)\in\mathcal R\), write

\[
U=I\mathbin{\dot\cup}\{\alpha_0,\alpha_1\},\qquad
V=T\mathbin{\dot\cup}\{\beta_0,\beta_1\}.            \tag{3.2}
\]

Define four local middle owners

\[
p_j=(I+\alpha_j,T)\in\binom U3\times\binom V2,
\qquad
n_j=(I,T+\beta_j)\in\binom U2\times\binom V3.        \tag{3.3}
\]

Use the internal matching edges

\[
p_0p_1,\qquad n_0n_1,                               \tag{3.4}
\]

and the cross matching edges

\[
p_0n_0,\qquad p_1n_1.                               \tag{3.5}
\]

### Lemma 3.1 (exact twelve-\(C_4\) resolution)

As \((I,T)\) ranges over \(\mathcal R\), the quadruples in (3.3)
partition

\[
\left(\binom U3\times\binom V2\right)
\mathbin{\dot\cup}
\left(\binom U2\times\binom V3\right).               \tag{3.6}
\]

The internal edges (3.4) form a perfect matching, the cross edges (3.5)
form another perfect matching, and their union consists of twelve
alternating \(C_4\)'s.

#### Proof

Fix \(T\). Its two neighbors \(I\) in \(\mathcal R\) form one
complementary pair of two-subsets of \(U\). The two corresponding edges
\(p_0p_1\) are precisely one one-factor of \(J(U,3)\), so they use every
three-subset of \(U\) once. The reciprocal statement with \(I\) fixed
proves the same for the \(n\)-vertices.

Each cross edge deletes the unique coordinate in
\((I+\alpha_j)\setminus I\) and inserts the unique coordinate in
\((T+\beta_j)\setminus T\). It is therefore a literal Johnson edge.
The four displayed edges form one alternating four-cycle. Different
\((I,T)\)'s use disjoint vertices, proving exhaustion.
\(\square\)

Tensor every edge with one common residual \(Q_{r-1}\). Each edge becomes
a physical \(Q_r\) packet, each alternating \(C_4\) has owner mass \(2R\),
where

\[
R=2^r,                                               \tag{3.7}
\]

and the full \(k=3\) sector has owner mass \(24R\).

## 4. Port closure and the exact local target Gram

Two-color the four vertices of every local \(C_4\). Use that color to
orient the abstract carrier bit in each of its four \(Q_r\) packets.
This is only a packetwise bit translation. Install one common certified
isometric factor on the resulting abstract \(Q_r\)'s.

At every shared physical owner, the internal and cross shores now have
the same abstract state. A noncarrier transition is therefore literally
the same physical transition on both shores. A carrier transition follows
the old internal matching or the new cross matching.

All selected cycles are closed. More precisely, one independently
switchable trade block is the full \(C_4\)-macroblock: its old shore is
the union of the two complete internal \(Q_r\)-packet factors and its new
shore is the union of the two complete cross \(Q_r\)-packet factors. The
finest owner-overlay components are indexed by the abstract
\(C_{2r}\)-cycles inside this block. Grouping them is legal because their
two shores are independently switchable and, by trace injectivity, their
target derivatives are orthogonal. Choosing either grouped shore for
each macroblock therefore gives an exact packet-cycle near-factor. No
cycle is cut, and there is no external port equation or seam occurrence.

For \(1\le q\le H\le r/2\), one named direction occurs twice in every
length-\(2r\)
cycle word and lies in exactly \(q/r\) of its cyclic \(q\)-windows. Put

\[
h_q=\frac{qR}{r}.                                    \tag{4.1}
\]

Two-sided trace injectivity makes the residual traces associated with the
\(h_q\) carrier-containing abstract starts pairwise distinct.

For a local component \(C=(I,T)\), let
\(d_C^\pm\) denote cross-shore incidence minus internal-shore incidence
for one fixed residual trace.

### Lemma 4.1 (one-sign local derivative)

For the \(k=3\) bank,

\[
d_C^-=0.                                             \tag{4.2}
\]

The internal upper targets are

\[
U\cup T,\qquad I\cup V,                              \tag{4.3}
\]

while the two cross upper targets have local profile \((3,3)\):

\[
(I+\alpha_j)\cup(T+\beta_j),\qquad j=0,1.            \tag{4.4}
\]

Consequently

\[
\|d_C^+\|_2^2=4.                                    \tag{4.5}
\]

#### Proof

Both internal lower edges and both cross lower edges have intersection
\(I\cup T\), with multiplicity two on each shore. This proves (4.2).

The union of the two \(p\)-vertices is \(U\cup T\), and the union of the
two \(n\)-vertices is \(I\cup V\). A cross edge has one added coordinate
in each quartet, giving (4.4). The profiles \((4,2),(2,4),(3,3)\) are
disjoint, so the four signed entries are distinct and (4.5) follows.
\(\square\)

### Lemma 4.2 (positive local cross-Gram)

Over all twelve local components,

\[
\sum_C\|d_C^+\|_2^2=48,\qquad
\left\|\sum_Cd_C^+\right\|_2^2\ge72.                \tag{4.6}
\]

Equivalently the local coherence is at least

\[
\gamma_{\rm loc}^+\ge24.                            \tag{4.7}
\]

#### Proof

The first identity follows from (4.5). For each fixed
\(T\in\binom V2\), its two neighbors \(I\) in \(\mathcal R\) give two
different components with the same negative target \(U\cup T\). This
gives six same-sign component pairs. For each fixed
\(I\in\binom U2\), the same argument gives six pairs sharing
\(I\cup V\).

Each shared coefficient contributes \(2\) to
\(\|\sum_Cd_C\|^2-\sum_C\|d_C\|^2\), for a total of at least
\(2(6+6)=24\). Cross targets can only create additional same-sign
overlaps because their local profile is disjoint from (4.3). This proves
(4.6)--(4.7).
\(\square\)

Repeating the local incidence vector over the \(h_q\) orthogonal
residual traces gives

\[
V_{q,k=3}=48h_q,\qquad
A_{q,k=3}\ge72h_q,\qquad
\Gamma_{q,k=3}^+\ge24h_q,                           \tag{4.8}
\]

and the lower derivative is zero.

Complementation on \(U\cup V\) gives the \(k=2\) sector. There

\[
\Gamma_{q,k=2}^-\ge24h_q,\qquad d_C^+=0,             \tag{4.9}
\]

with the same values of \(A,V\).

## 5. The frozen-anchor cancellation

Freeze the four coordinates of \(Z=\{1,2,3,4\}\) in every packet.
Partition its six rank-two subsets into

\[
\mathcal S_Z=\{12,13,14\},
\qquad
\mathcal T_Z=\{23,24,34\}.                           \tag{5.1}
\]

The first family is a three-edge star in \(K_4\); the second is the
complementary triangle.

On an anchor fibre \(C\in\mathcal S_Z\), take

\[
(F^0,F^1)=(\text{internal},\text{cross})             \tag{5.2}
\]

in both the \(k=2\) and \(k=3\) sectors. On
\(C\in\mathcal T_Z\), reverse the two shores.

Use the dense residual resolution of Section 2 with exactly the same
rules in all six anchor fibres. On every other local \(Z,U,V\) fibre,
install an identical common packet factor in \(F^0,F^1\), again with
anchor-uniform rules.

Every lower and upper trace retains its exact frozen anchor subset \(C\).
Therefore target spaces belonging to different anchor fibres are
disjoint. They are also isomorphic under coordinate permutations of
\(Z\).

Fix one signed depth. Let \(E_{\rm cr}\) be the complete floor energy in
one fibre when the cross bank is selected, including its inner products
with the common background in that fibre. Define \(E_{\rm in}\)
analogously. Anchor uniformity makes

\[
E_{\rm cr}-E_{\rm in}=D_q^\epsilon                 \tag{5.3}
\]

independent of the chosen rank-two anchor subset. The three star fibres
contribute \(3D_q^\epsilon\) to
\(Q(F^1)-Q(F^0)\), while the three triangle fibres contribute
\(-3D_q^\epsilon\). Hence

\[
\boxed{\Delta_q^\epsilon=0}                         \tag{5.4}
\]

separately for every depth and sign. This proves (0.5) and the second
part of (0.8).

Reversing both shores negates every \(\delta_K\) but leaves
\(\|\sum\delta_K\|^2\), \(\sum\|\delta_K\|^2\), and \(\Gamma\)
unchanged. Distinct anchor fibres are target-orthogonal. Thus all six
fibres add positively in (0.6).

## 6. Owner density and global coherence

For a fixed rank-two anchor subset, the \(k=2\) local owner sector has
48 states on \(U\cup V\) and residual rank \(m-5\). The \(k=3\)
sector has 48 states and residual rank \(m-7\). Since

\[
\binom{2m-12}{m-7}=\binom{2m-12}{m-5},              \tag{6.1}
\]

the pre-leave changed owner mass is

\[
\begin{aligned}
B_m^{\rm raw}
&=6(48+48)\binom{2m-12}{m-5}\\
&=576\binom{2m-12}{m-5}.                            \tag{6.2}
\end{aligned}
\]

For every fixed twelve-coordinate pattern, the central conditional
probability is \(2^{-12}+o(1)\). Therefore

\[
\frac{B_m^{\rm raw}}W\longrightarrow
\frac{576}{4096}=\frac9{64}.                         \tag{6.3}
\]

Lemma 2.2 removes only \(e^{-\Omega(m)}W\), proving (0.4).

Every alternating \(C_4\) macroblock has owner mass \(2R\) and, at its
active sign, component-variance contribution \(4h_q\). There are
\(B_m/(2R)\) such macroblocks. Trace injectivity makes the raw cycle
components inside one macroblock orthogonal, so

\[
V_q=4h_q\frac{B_m}{2R}
=2\frac qrB_m.                                      \tag{6.4}
\]

Each \(24R\)-owner \(k\)-sector has coherence at least \(24h_q\),
which equals \(q/r\) times its owner mass. Summing the two signs and all
anchor fibres gives

\[
\Gamma_q\ge\frac qrB_m.                              \tag{6.5}
\]

There is no hidden adverse cross-context term in this lower bound. In
the \(k=3\) sector the positive upper tags have local profile \((3,3)\),
whereas the negative tags have profiles \((4,2)\) and \((2,4)\). In the
\(k=2\) sector the complementary lower profiles are likewise disjoint.
Coincidences across residual packets can therefore occur only between
coefficients of the same sign and can only increase \(A_q\); distinct
anchor fibres are target-orthogonal.

Equations (6.4)--(6.5) give

\[
A_q=V_q+\Gamma_q\ge3\frac qrB_m,                     \tag{6.6}
\]

proving (0.6)--(0.7).

The \(k=2\) and \(k=3\) sectors have equal mass even after the leave:
residual complementation interchanges ranks \(m-5\) and \(m-7\), preserves
the set of split matching edges, and hence preserves the canonical
first-\((r-1)\)-split eligibility rule. Thus each sector has changed mass
exactly \(B_m/2\). Therefore, with possibly different lower and upper
weights,

\[
\Gamma_{\le H}
\ge\frac{B_m}{2r}
\sum_{q=1}^{H}q(w_{q,-}+w_{q,+}),                   \tag{6.7}
\]

which is (0.8). Since \(\Delta_{\le H}=0\), (1.6) proves (0.9).

Under the short-scale specialization (0.1a), the top protected depth gives

\[
\Gamma_H\ge\frac{H}{r}B_m
\ge\left(\frac9{256}+o(1)\right)W.                  \tag{6.8}
\]

Thus the construction has a genuinely linear Gaussian-scale margin.

## 7. The factors are not coordinate relabelings

The background can be chosen with the following explicit intrinsic
features.

1. \(Z\) is exactly the set of coordinates which never occur as a
   transition direction. Every coordinate outside \(Z\) occurs in some
   selected packet direction.
2. All background and residual-packet rules are uniform under
   permutations of \(Z\), and no residual direction depends on the
   anchor subset.
3. The only transition support depending on the rank-two anchor tag is
   carried by \(U\cup V\).

To realize the first item, note that every coordinate of \(U\cup V\)
occurs in an explicit local carrier edge. For any residual matching edge
choose a common-background local fibre whose residual rank \(M+t\) has
the parity of \(r\), make that edge and exactly \(r-1\) other edges split,
and make every other edge nonsplit. Of the remaining \(M-r\) edges,
declare exactly \((M+t-r)/2\) full and the rest empty. Both parities of
\(t\) occur among the common-background fibres. Since \(r=o(m)\) and
\(t\) is bounded, this is possible for all sufficiently large \(m\), and
the chosen edge lies among the first \(r\) split edges because there are
exactly \(r\) of them. Thus every residual edge is selected in at least
one canonical packet, whereas \(Z\) is frozen in every packet.

The first property makes \(Z\) invariant under every coordinate
permutation carrying one factor to the other. The anchor-dependent
eight-coordinate support recovers \(U\cup V\). In the \(k=2\) sector,
the anchor-dependent bank contains every mixed local three-subset, so the
exceptional common-background three-subsets form

\[
\binom U3\mathbin{\dot\cup}\binom V3.                \tag{7.1}
\]

The two components of their two-coordinate-overlap graph recover the
unordered bipartition \(\{U,V\}\). Thus internal directions (lying within
one part) and cross directions (meeting both parts) are intrinsic.

In \(F^0\), the anchor tags selecting the internal shore form one of the
families in (5.1), while in \(F^1\) they form the other. A permutation of
\(Z\) preserves the vertex-degree sequence of a family of two-subsets.
The star has degree sequence

\[
(3,1,1,1),
\]

while the triangle has degree sequence

\[
(2,2,2,0).
\]

No permutation of \(Z\) sends one to the other. Hence no coordinate
permutation sends \(F^0\) to \(F^1\).

The conclusion here is exactly the one required in this lane: the factors
are not related by a permutation of the \(2m\) coordinates. No stronger
classification modulo the central-layer complement is used.

## 8. Independent audit of the full internal-quartet Hall cut

Let the ground set be partitioned into \(c=m/2\) quartets, and let
\(F(S)\) be the number of full quartets contained in \(S\).

### Proposition 8.1 (correct invariant)

Every path using only block-internal Johnson moves preserves the local
cardinality of every quartet. Consequently, if \(T\) is the lower
intersection of a depth-\(q\) internal path with middle source \(X\),

\[
F(T)=F(X).                                            \tag{8.1}
\]

#### Proof

An internal Johnson move deletes and inserts inside the same quartet, so
its local rank is invariant. A rank-four quartet admits no nontrivial
internal move and remains full throughout the path. A quartet of local
rank at most three can never become full in the path or in its
intersection. This proves (8.1).
\(\square\)

This replaces one over-specific sentence in the source proof: an active
quartet has intersection size \(j-1\) only when its axis is actually used
by the window. The rank-preservation proof covers unused axes and even
architectures with several internal axes in one quartet.

Put

\[
h(z)=(1+z)^4-z^4=1+4z+6z^2+4z^3.                   \tag{8.2}
\]

For the target and source profile classes

\[
\mathcal T_{q,k}
=\{T:|T|=m-q,\ F(T)=k\},\qquad
\mathcal X_k=\{X:|X|=m,\ F(X)=k\},                  \tag{8.3}
\]

the exact counts are

\[
\begin{aligned}
|\mathcal T_{q,k}|
&=\binom{c}{k}[z^{m-q-4k}]h(z)^{c-k},\\
|\mathcal X_k|
&=\binom{c}{k}[z^{m-4k}]h(z)^{c-k}.
\end{aligned}                                       \tag{8.4}
\]

Let \(J\) have probabilities

\[
\Pr(J=j)=\frac{\binom4j}{15},\qquad0\le j\le3.
\]

Then

\[
\mathbb EJ=\frac{28}{15},\qquad
\operatorname{Var}J=\frac{176}{225}.                \tag{8.5}
\]

Take

\[
q=A_0\sqrt m+O(1),\qquad
k=\frac m{32}+y\sqrt m+O(1).                        \tag{8.6}
\]

Writing \(d=c-k\), direct substitution gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
d&=\frac{15m}{32}-y\sqrt m+O(1),\\
\operatorname{Var}(J)d&=\frac{11m}{30}+O(\sqrt m),\\
s_T-\mathbb EJ\,d
&=\left(-A_0-\frac{32y}{15}\right)\sqrt m+O(1),\\
s_X-\mathbb EJ\,d
&=-\frac{32y}{15}\sqrt m+O(1).
\end{aligned}                                       \tag{8.7}
\]

The span-one local central limit theorem yields

\[
\log\frac{|\mathcal X_k|}{|\mathcal T_{q,k}|}
=\frac{15A_0^2+64A_0y}{11}+o(1).                   \tag{8.8}
\]

At \(y=-A_0/4\), this is

\[
-\frac{A_0^2}{11}+o(1).                             \tag{8.9}
\]

Uniformly for

\[
\left|y+\frac{A_0}{4}\right|\le\frac{A_0}{128},
\]

the ratio is at most

\[
\exp\left(-\frac{A_0^2}{22}+o(1)\right)<1.          \tag{8.10}
\]

Under the target layer conditioning, the number of full quartets has

\[
\mathbb EF
=\frac m{32}-\frac{A_0}{8}\sqrt m+O(1),\qquad
\operatorname{Var}F
=\frac{11m}{512}+O(\sqrt m).                        \tag{8.11}
\]

For an independent quartet at density \(1/2\), the full indicator has
variance \(15/256\), the quartet size has variance \(1\), and their
covariance is \(1/8\). Conditioning on total size therefore leaves
variance \(15/256-(1/8)^2=11/256\) per quartet, which gives the second
constant in (8.11); the first follows by expanding
\((m/2)(1/2-A_0/(2\sqrt m))^4\).

Thus the window in (8.10) is a fixed standard-deviation displacement of
fixed positive width. It contains \(c_{A_0}N_q\) targets for some
\(c_{A_0}>0\). Since

\[
\frac{N_q}{W}\longrightarrow e^{-A_0^2},            \tag{8.12}
\]

the profile Hall deficits add to \((\delta_{A_0}-o(1))W\) for an explicit
\(\delta_{A_0}>0\). The exponent \(e^{-A_0^2/11}\) and every displayed
constant are therefore correct. Upper targets use the complementary
empty-quartet invariant.

One completely explicit admissible choice is obtained as follows. Put

\[
\sigma_F=\sqrt{11/512},\qquad
c_{A_0}^\star=
\Phi\!\left(-\frac{15A_0}{128\sigma_F}\right)
-\Phi\!\left(-\frac{17A_0}{128\sigma_F}\right)>0,
\]

where \(\Phi\) is the standard normal distribution function. The
conditional local limit theorem makes the target mass of (8.10) at least
\(c_{A_0}^\star N_q/2\) for all sufficiently large \(m\). Thus, for
example,

\[
\delta_{A_0}
=\frac18\,c_{A_0}^\star e^{-A_0^2}
 \left(1-e^{-A_0^2/22}\right)                       \tag{8.12a}
\]

is a valid fixed positive constant for all sufficiently large \(m\). The
displayed factor \(1/8\) deliberately absorbs all three asymptotic
half-losses.

### Proposition 8.2 (precise crossing-window toll)

For a packet near-factor, let \(B_\times\) be the aggregate number of
depth-\(q\) windows containing a cross-quartet axis, and let
\(M_{q,\mathrm{hard}}^-\) be the number of holes remaining in the lower
hard-profile window used above. Then

\[
B_\times+M_{q,\mathrm{hard}}^-
\ge(\delta_{A_0}-o(1))W.                            \tag{8.13}
\]

If the displayed near-factor has owner/start leave \(L\), let
\(\widehat F\) be any completion using at most those \(L\) omitted starts.
Then the corresponding post-completion form is

\[
B_\times+L+M_{q,\mathrm{hard}}^-(\widehat F)
\ge(\delta_{A_0}-o(1))W.                            \tag{8.13a}
\]

For an owner-disjoint \(Q_r\)-packet decomposition into isometric
\(C_{2r}\)-factors, if packet \(P\) has \(s(P)\) crossing axes and
\(q<r\), then

\[
B_\times
\le\frac qr\sum_Ps(P)|P|.                           \tag{8.14}
\]

Consequently, when both \(L=o(W)\) and
\(M_{q,\mathrm{hard}}^-(\widehat F)=o(W)\),

\[
\frac1W\sum_Ps(P)|P|
=\Omega_{A_0}(r/q).                                 \tag{8.15}
\]

#### Proof

Internal starts in profile \(k\) can hit at most
\(|\mathcal X_k|\) targets in \(\mathcal T_{q,k}\). Every cross start
adds capacity for at most one further target. Every target still not hit
is charged to \(M_{q,\mathrm{hard}}^-\). Summing the positive profile
deficits proves (8.13). A completion can contribute at most \(L\) further
starts, which proves (8.13a). In particular, the theorem does not force
many crossing windows in a construction which simply accepts a linear
number of holes.

In a word \(\pi\pi\), one marked axis has two antipodal occurrences.
Exactly \(2q\) cyclic starts have a length-\(q\) window containing one of
them, a fraction \(q/r\). The union bound over \(s(P)\) marked axes gives
(8.14), and (8.15) follows.
\(\square\)

The axis count is necessary but not sufficient. If the \(2s\) marked
direction occurrences have cyclic gaps \(g_1,\ldots,g_{2s}\), summing to
\(2r\), then the exact fraction of starts seeing a marked direction is

\[
\phi_q=\frac1{2r}\sum_{i=1}^{2s}\min(g_i,q).         \tag{8.16}
\]

Thus marked axes must also be dispersed. In the dense reservoir
construction of Section 2, all but at most one axis are crossing, and
(2.4) verifies the dispersion requirement exactly.

## 9. The profile-floor obstruction and why it does not apply here

There is a correct restricted version of the proposed covariance no-go.
It explains why cross-profile transport is essential.

Within each signed-depth layer, let the target coordinates be partitioned
into immutable profile cells \(A_\alpha\), so that the layer weight is a
single constant \(w_\alpha\ge0\) on each cell. Suppose every component
shore has the same occurrence mass in every \(A_\alpha\). Let

\[
c_\alpha=\left\lfloor
\frac{M_\alpha}{|A_\alpha|}
\right\rfloor,
\]

where \(M_\alpha\) is the fixed total mass in that cell, including common
background load.

Because \(M_\alpha\) is fixed between shores, replacing the global
layer floor \(c_a\) by \(c_\alpha\) changes the energy of every factor by
the same cellwise constant. It therefore changes neither \(\Delta\) nor
\(\Gamma\).

### Theorem 9.1 (profile-floor stationarity)

If \(F^0\) is profile-floor-balanced, meaning that its loads in every
\(A_\alpha\) are \(c_\alpha\) or \(c_\alpha+1\), then for every
port-closed additive comparison factor

\[
\boxed{\Gamma\le\Delta=|\Delta|.}                   \tag{9.1}
\]

#### Proof

Work in one profile cell, suppress its constant weight, and sum at the
end. Write

\[
\mu^0=c+b,\qquad b_T\in\{0,1\},\qquad
D=\sum_K\delta_K.
\]

Profile-mass preservation gives \(\sum_TD_T=0\). Expanding the floor
energy gives

\[
\Delta-\Gamma=2\sum_Tb_TD_T+\sum_{K,T}\delta_K(T)^2. \tag{9.2}
\]

Put \(x_-=\max\{-x,0\}\). The right side has the exact decomposition

\[
\begin{aligned}
\Delta-\Gamma
={}&
\left(\sum_{K,T}\delta_K(T)^2
      -\sum_{K,T}|\delta_K(T)|\right)\\
&+\left(\sum_{K,T}|\delta_K(T)|
      -\sum_T|D_T|\right)\\
&+2\left(
 \sum_{b_T=1}(D_T)_+
 +\sum_{b_T=0}(D_T)_-
\right).
\end{aligned}                                       \tag{9.3}
\]

The first line is nonnegative because every \(\delta_K(T)\) is integral.
The second is the triangle inequality, and the third is nonnegative.
Multiplying by \(w_\alpha\ge0\) preserves the inequality. Thus
\(\Gamma\le\Delta\). Since a floor-balanced integral vector minimizes the
square energy at its fixed mass, \(\Delta\ge0\).
\(\square\)

For a deficient cell with \(c_\alpha=0\), arbitrary endpoints satisfy the
useful weaker bound

\[
\boxed{
(\Gamma_\alpha-|\Delta_\alpha|)_+
\le2\min\{\widehat Q_\alpha(F^0),
           \widehat Q_\alpha(F^1)\}.}               \tag{9.4}
\]

Here this is a cellwise statement and

\[
\widehat Q_\alpha(F)
=w_\alpha\sum_{T\in A_\alpha}\mu(T)(\mu(T)-1)       \tag{9.4a}
\]

is the profile-floor energy. If the global layer floor in (1.2) differs
from \(c_\alpha\), changing between the two energies adds a constant
depending only on \(M_\alpha,|A_\alpha|,c_a,c_\alpha\), not on the factor;
endpoint differences are therefore unchanged. Equation (9.4) is not a
claim obtained by summing absolute values across cells.

Indeed,

\[
\Gamma_\alpha
\le w_\alpha\sum_T\left[
\mu^0(T)(\mu^0(T)-1)
+\mu^1(T)(\mu^1(T)-1)\right]
=\widehat Q_\alpha(F^0)+\widehat Q_\alpha(F^1),     \tag{9.5}
\]

while
\(\Delta_\alpha=\widehat Q_\alpha(F^1)-
\widehat Q_\alpha(F^0)\). Subtracting the absolute difference gives
(9.4).

For completeness, (9.5) is an exact component inequality. Adjoin the
common background as a dummy block \(K=0\), with
\(a_0(T)=b_0(T)\) equal to its load. For every genuine block put
\(a_K(T)=\mu_K^0(T)\) and \(b_K(T)=\mu_K^1(T)\). Then

\[
\begin{aligned}
\widehat Q_\alpha(F^0)+\widehat Q_\alpha(F^1)-\Gamma_\alpha
=w_\alpha\Bigg[
&\sum_{K,T}\bigl(a_K(T)(a_K(T)-1)
                 +b_K(T)(b_K(T)-1)\bigr)\\
&+2\sum_{K<L,T}
 \bigl(a_K(T)b_L(T)+b_K(T)a_L(T)\bigr)
\Bigg]\ge0.
\end{aligned}                                       \tag{9.6}
\]

The full internal quartet atlas preserves the full-quartet profiles from
Section 8. At a profile-floor endpoint it is therefore subject to
(9.1), in addition to the Hall deficit.

The present pair deliberately leaves that hypothesis: its cross matching
moves occurrences between the \(U,V\) occupancy profiles
\((4,2),(2,4),(3,3)\) on the upper side and their complements on the lower
side. Its endpoints also carry the collision energy which the strict
coherence removes. There is no contradiction with (9.1).

## 10. Exact surviving HCRT boundary

The following are proved.

1. Two coordinate-unrelated physical packet near-factors with common
   exponentially small leave have \(\Gamma>|\Delta|\) simultaneously
   across any fixed collection of depths and signs.
2. Their overlay switches select complete old/new cycles, so integrality,
   port closure, and additive target ownership are literal.
3. The pair acts on \(9/64+o(1)\) owner density and has a linear margin at
   every Gaussian depth with \(r=\Theta_{A_0}(\sqrt m)\).
4. A dense, dispersed cross-quartet frame can be preloaded into every
   packet without changing the coherence calculation.
5. The complete block-internal quartet atlas has the audited linear Hall
   deficit and therefore cannot replace cross-quartet transport.
6. A universal local-pair covariance inequality is false when shores may
   cross profile cells; for immutable-profile pairs the profile-floor
   form (9.1) is valid.

What remains unproved is the hierarchical circulation.

* For the coefficient-one packet route one needs \(H/r\to0\) so that
  linearizing the many \(C_{2r}\) cycles costs \(o(W)\).
* In that regime the one-step margin of the present differing carrier is
  \(O((q/r)W)=o(W)\) at a Gaussian depth.
* In the naive independent Cartesian tensor, accumulating
  \(s=\Omega(r/q)\) dispersed differing carriers directly in two endpoints
  expands their owner overlay by a factor exponential in \(s\). This does
  not rule out a genuinely recoupled multi-axis packet. Comparing
  successive one-carrier stages keeps the present components bounded but
  leaves a common all-depth sequence of directed choices to prove.
* Dense common crossing axes satisfy the Hall capacity toll but supply no
  floor anticorrelation by themselves.

Thus the new theorem closes the existence of a genuine positive-coherence
packet pair. One sufficient unresolved scheduling statement is:

> schedule \(\Omega(r/q)\) bounded cross-profile coherence stages on a
> common owner near-tiling, with dispersed physical crossing directions,
> so that one common all-depth component history preserves the accumulated
> negative floor covariance and ends with \(Q=o(W)\).

This is a concrete route to \(HCRT\), not a proof that every successful
route must take this form. No coefficient-one conclusion is claimed here.
