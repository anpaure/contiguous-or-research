# Separated double swaps generate the full alternating row-order group

## Status

The native inverse-pair packet acts on each of its two cyclic coordinate
orders by two separated adjacent transpositions.  This note determines the
abstract permutation group generated by those row operations: it is the
entire alternating group.

Thus the involution at one fixed slot is not a row-order obstruction once
the active slot can move.  The exact unresolved issue is physical: an
inverse-pair substitution requires a compatible companion row at every
step, and the current factor is not known to supply the companions needed
by the group word below.

## 1. The positional generators

Put

\[
                         N=2m+1,\qquad m\ge2.         \tag{1.1}
\]

Index the positions of a cyclic coordinate order by `Z/NZ`, and let

\[
                         s_i=(i\ i+1)                 \tag{1.2}
\]

be the adjacent transposition at edge `i`.  The two exchanges in one
separated-double swap occur at antipodal-near edges `i` and `i+m`, so its
positional action is

\[
                         g_i=s_i s_{i+m}.             \tag{1.3}
\]

The two transpositions in (1.3) are disjoint.  In the notation of
`MATH_THEOREM_MSW_INVERSE_PAIR_Q1_BIRAIL_CURRENT_20260806.md`, the
substitution

\[
 (a,b,X,c,d,Y)\longmapsto(b,a,X,d,c,Y)               \tag{1.4}
\]

is exactly one `g_i`, after a cyclic shift of the positions.  The companion
row undergoes the same positional generator.

## 2. Exact generation theorem

### Theorem 2.1

For every odd `N=2m+1>=5`,

\[
                 \langle g_i:i\in\mathbb Z/N\mathbb Z\rangle=A_N.
                                                               \tag{2.1}
\]

#### Proof

Every `g_i` is a product of two transpositions, so the generated group is
contained in `A_N`.

Modulo `N`,

\[
                         i+2m=i-1.                    \tag{2.2}
\]

Therefore

\[
\begin{aligned}
 g_i g_{i+m}
   &=(s_i s_{i+m})(s_{i+m}s_{i+2m})\\
   &=s_i s_{i-1}.                                    \tag{2.3}
\end{aligned}
\]

The right-hand side is an adjacent three-cycle on the positions
`i-1,i,i+1` (with one of the two orientations, according to the convention
for composition).  Adjacent three-cycles generate `A_N`: for example the
cycles on consecutive triples generate every three-cycle by conjugation
along the cycle graph, and the three-cycles generate the alternating
group.  Hence the left-hand side of (2.1) contains `A_N`, proving equality.
\(\square\)

### Corollary 2.2 (two slots already give a local three-cycle)

If a row can undergo legal separated-double swaps at slots `i` and `i+m`
successively, its net positional action is the adjacent three-cycle

\[
                         s_i s_{i-1}.                 \tag{2.4}
\]

In particular, moving the active slot eliminates the fixed-slot two-cycle
rigidity and permits arbitrary even reorderings of the exchange schedule.

## 3. Exact physical qualification

Theorem 2.1 is a group statement about one coordinate order.  A literal
inverse-pair move acts diagonally on two rows satisfying the companion
pattern

\[
 (a,b,X,c,d,Y),\qquad(b,d,X,a,c,Y).                  \tag{3.1}
\]

After the first move, the second row required to realize `g_(i+m)` need
not occur in the current factor.  Nor does (2.1) preserve or price the
all-width two-rail current, topology, or compiler tickets.

Accordingly the remaining theorem can be stated sharply:

> **Moving-companion lemma.**  In one exact factor, every two-step word
> `g_i g_(i+m)` needed for a generating set of `A_N` admits compatible
> companion rows, either directly or after zero-current four-row
> associators, with aligned all-width rails.

Theorem 2.1 proves that this local moving-companion lemma would give full
row-order reachability within the unavoidable parity sector.  It does not
prove the lemma itself.
