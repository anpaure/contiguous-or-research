# Complementary clustered payloads have an exact coherent endpoint coupling

**Status (2026-08-21).**  Every statement below is proved.  Conditional on
the tight-cycle factors used by the two-block product route, complementary
payload ranks can use the same cyclic orders.  Pairing their clustered atoms
with aligned counter origins makes their two dominant endpoint shadows
labelled-disjoint.  Across all odd upper offsets in the DCC band, this gives

\[
 (1/2-o(1))W_b
\]

distinct upper targets, while the holes inside the corresponding balanced
split profiles total only `O(W_b/sqrt b)=o(W_b)`.

This is a positive coherent order-level theorem, not merely a phase or
fractional statement.  It covers only the single balanced split profile at
each odd upper offset.  The other split profiles, the even offsets, and the
full simultaneous order-bank transport remain open.

## 1. Complementary factor banks

Let `b` tend to infinity through odd primes, put

\[
 W_b={2b\choose b},
\]

and let

\[
 H/\sqrt b\longrightarrow\infty,
 \qquad H=o(b^{2/3}).                                  \tag{1.1}
\]

Fix an odd integer `q` with `1<=q<=H` and put

\[
 r={b-q\over2},\qquad s={b+q\over2}=b-r.             \tag{1.2}
\]

Conditionally take a tight-cycle factor `F_r` of the rank-`r` subsets of a
`b`-set.  Its cyclic orders have disjoint rank-`r` interval decks which
partition that layer.  Complementing every interval on the same cyclic
order shows that those very orders also form a tight-cycle factor `F_s` of
the rank-`s=b-r` layer.  In particular

\[
 |F_r|=|F_s|={1\over b}{b\choose r}.                 \tag{1.3}
\]

Use labelled copies of this same order bank on disjoint `b`-sets `A,B`.
For every ordered pair `(alpha,beta) in F_r times F_r`, form both product
atoms

\[
 (\alpha,\beta;\tau_r),\qquad
 (\alpha,\beta;\tau_s),                              \tag{1.4}
\]

where `tau_t=A^tB^(b-t)`, and align their stream-counter origins.  In the
first atom, `alpha` is read at rank `r` and `beta` at the complementary rank
`s`; in the second their local payload ranks are `s` and `r`.  Thus (1.4)
uses the same labelled cyclic-order coordinates in both payloads.

## 2. Exact endpoint-shadow theorem

At upper offset `q`, both endpoint sources in (1.4) land in the balanced
split profile

\[
 \mathcal P_q=
 \{Y:|Y\cap A|=|Y\cap B|=s\},
 \qquad
 |\mathcal P_q|={b\choose r}^2.                       \tag{2.1}
\]

The payload `r` source uses the phases in which all next `q` steps have type
`A`; the payload `s` source uses the phases in which all next `q` steps have
type `B`.  Each source has exactly

\[
 h_q=r-q+1={b-3q+2\over2}                             \tag{2.2}
\]

such phases.

### Theorem 2.1 (coherent complementary endpoint coverage)

All balanced-profile endpoint targets produced by the atoms (1.4) are
distinct, and their exact number is

\[
 \boxed{
 \left(1-{3q-2\over b}\right){b\choose r}^2.}        \tag{2.3}
\]

Equivalently, the exact hole count inside `P_q` is

\[
 {3q-2\over b}{b\choose r}^2.                        \tag{2.4}
\]

#### Proof

Every contributing phase contains `b` counter pairs.  Hence one common
order pair contributes `2h_qb=(b-3q+2)b` endpoint occurrences.  There are
`|F_r|^2=binom(b,r)^2/b^2` order pairs, giving the count in (2.3).

It remains to prove that the count is labelled-simple.  A target in
`P_q` has an `s`-interval on `alpha` and an `s`-interval on `beta`.  Since
the rank-`s` decks of the common factor bank partition the whole layer,
these two local interval sets uniquely determine their order coordinates
`alpha,beta` and their endpoints.  Thus a collision between different order
pairs is impossible.

Within one order pair, each payload atom is internally simple.  A collision
between the payload-`r` and payload-`s` sources would have the same two local
interval endpoints and hence the same counter-sum diagonal.  But the
clustered upper phase map

\[
 \phi_p^{(q)}(t)=t+|\{0,\ldots,t-1\}\cap
                    \{p+1,\ldots,p+q\}|
\]

is strictly increasing in the payload `t` on every fixed phase diagonal.
The two distinct payload ranks therefore cannot produce the same target.
This proves simplicity and hence (2.3)--(2.4).  \(\square\)

The same proof gives a quantitative sufficient condition away from the
balanced diagonal.

### Theorem 2.2 (common-order endpoint criterion)

Fix `1<=q<=H` and suppose both payload ranks `s-q,s` lie in
`[H+2,b-H-2]`.  Let `F_s` and `F_(s-q)` be tight-cycle factors on
one `b`-set, viewed as sets of oriented cyclic orders up to rotation, and
put

\[
 f_+=|F_s|={1\over b}{b\choose s},\qquad
 f_-=|F_{s-q}|={1\over b}{b\choose s-q}.             \tag{2.5}
\]

After any allowed coherent conjugations, let

\[
 \mathcal C=F_s\cap F_{s-q},\qquad K=|\mathcal C|.   \tag{2.6}
\]

Use the complementary factors generated by these same orders on the other
local ranks.  For every `(alpha,beta) in C times C`, pair the endpoint atoms
of payloads `s` and `s-q` with aligned origins.  Their selected rank-`(b+q)`
endpoint targets are all distinct, and their exact number is

\[
 \boxed{K^2b(b-3q+2).}                               \tag{2.7}
\]

Relative to the whole split profile, their coverage fraction is

\[
 \boxed{
 \left(1-{3q-2\over b}\right){K^2\over f_+f_-}.}    \tag{2.8}
\]

#### Proof

The two endpoint phase counts are `b-s-q+1` and `s-2q+1`, whose sum is
`b-3q+2`; each phase contains `b` counter pairs.  This proves the occurrence
count in (2.7).

For a common order pair, an upper target has an `s`-interval on `alpha` and
a `(b+q-s)=b-(s-q)` interval on `beta`.  The first is owned uniquely by the
rank-`s` factor, while the second is owned uniquely by the complement of the
rank-`(s-q)` factor.  Because both orders lie in `C`, the same coordinates
identify both endpoint payloads.  Different common order pairs therefore
cannot collide, and the upper phase injection excludes collisions between
the two payload ranks within one pair.  Thus every occurrence in (2.7) is
distinct.  Finally the profile size is

\[
 {b\choose s}{b\choose s-q}=b^2f_+f_-,
\]

which gives (2.8).  \(\square\)

Put

\[
 P_{q,s}={b\choose s}{b\choose s-q}.
\]

Consequently, on any family `R` of offset/profile pairs for which the
factor orientations, conjugations, and origins are chosen once coherently,
literal common-order overlap is sufficient for endpoint complementarity
whenever

\[
 \sum_{(q,s)\in R}P_{q,s}
 \left(1-{K_{q,s}^2\over f_sf_{s-q}}+{3q\over b}\right)=o(W_b). \tag{2.9}
\]

Here `f_t=binom(b,t)/b` and
`K_(q,s)=|F_s cap F_(s-q)|` for the one globally chosen coherent family of
factor banks.

For offsets `q<=Q=o(sqrt b)`, the phase-loss part of (2.9), summed over all
profiles, is `O(Q^2W_b/b)=o(W_b)`.  Thus one sufficient remaining
low-offset endpoint problem is to obtain weighted common-order overlap
`K_(q,s)^2=(1-o(1))f_sf_(s-q)` on all but `o(W_b)` profile mass.  The
criterion is sufficient, not necessary: more general interval-coordinate
isomorphisms could couple two nonidentical cyclic orders.

The balanced theorem is physically compatible with the product route.
Distinct odd `q` use disjoint complementary payload pairs `{r,b-r}`, so no atom bank is
reused.  The common origin is an allowed rotation of one local cyclic order,
and the already proved linear connector serializes all these atoms.  Indeed,
the number of atoms is at most
`2 sum_(q odd)P_q/b^2<=W_b/b^2`; `O(b)` connector length therefore costs
`O(W_b/b)`, and charging all `O(H)` band incidences at every seam costs
`O(HW_b/b)=o(W_b)`.

## 3. Aggregate linear-scale coverage

Let `Q_H` be the odd integers in `[1,H]` and put

\[
 P_q={b\choose (b-q)/2}^2.
\]

### Theorem 3.1

The balanced profiles over all odd upper offsets satisfy

\[
 \sum_{q\in Q_H}P_q=(1/2+o(1))W_b,                  \tag{3.1}
\]

while their total endpoint hole count is

\[
 \sum_{q\in Q_H}{3q-2\over b}P_q
   =O\left({W_b\over\sqrt b}\right)=o(W_b).          \tag{3.2}
\]

Consequently the construction of Section 2 covers

\[
 (1/2-o(1))W_b                                      \tag{3.3}
\]

distinct upper targets coherently.

#### Proof

Uniformly for `q<=H=o(b^(2/3))`, the central local limit estimate is

\[
 {P_q\over W_b}
 ={2+o(1)\over\sqrt{\pi b}}e^{-q^2/b}.              \tag{3.4}
\]

Since `H/sqrt(b)->infinity`, the odd-mesh Riemann sum gives

\[
 {2\over\sqrt{\pi b}}
 \sum_{\substack{q>=1\\q\ \mathrm{odd}}}e^{-q^2/b}
 \longrightarrow {1\over2},                        \tag{3.5}
\]

and the omitted tail beyond `H` is `o(1)`.  This proves (3.1).

The same local estimate, with a uniform Gaussian upper bound, gives

\[
 \sum_{\substack{q>=1\\q\ \mathrm{odd}}}
 qP_q
 =O(W_b\sqrt b).
\]

Multiplication by `3/b` proves (3.2).  Subtracting (3.2) from (3.1) proves
(3.3).  \(\square\)

### Corollary 3.2 (the coherent half may use band-constant origins)

In every atom used for offset `q`, retain only origins whose next `H`
type steps are all `A` or all `B`.  The two complementary endpoint
sources then have `r-H+1` phases each, so their exact balanced-profile
coverage becomes

\[
 \left(1-{q+2H-2\over b}\right)P_q.                \tag{3.6}
\]

These targets remain labelled-distinct, and

\[
 \sum_{\substack{1\le q\le H\\q\ {\rm odd}}}
 {q+2H-2\over b}P_q
 =O\!\left({W_b\over\sqrt b}+{H\over b}W_b\right)
 =o(W_b).                                           \tag{3.7}
\]

Consequently the physical coherent coverage in (3.3) can be chosen so that
every retained origin chain in every contributing atom has a constant side
type throughout the entire band.

#### Proof

Restricting the phases in Theorem 2.1 changes the per-order-pair occurrence
count from `(b-3q+2)b` to
`2(r-H+1)b=(b-q-2H+2)b`.  The uniqueness argument is unchanged, proving
(3.6).  Equations (3.1) and the estimate
`sum_q qP_q=O(W_b sqrt b)` from the proof of Theorem 3.1 give (3.7).
\(\square\)

## 4. Exact remaining endpoint gate

Complementation supplies perfect cross-rank order compatibility only on the
balanced diagonal `s+(s-q)=b`.  Away from that diagonal, the rank-`s` and
rank-`(s-q)` factors need not share any cyclic orders.  A sufficient general
extension would be a common-order coupling whose incompatible weighted
profile mass is `o(W_b)`; the clustered phase injection would then make all
compatible columns upper-simple.  Rankwise Baranyai--Katona factors alone do
not provide such a coupling.

Pointwise, endpoint sources lose a `Theta(q/b)` phase fraction in a general
profile.  The later pooled calculation in
`MATH_THEOREM_CONSTANT_ORIGIN_ENDPOINT_PROFILE_CAPACITY_20260821.md`
shows that this pointwise observation is not a scalar obstruction: even the
smaller family of origins that stay in one type run throughout the whole
band has aggregate split-profile deficit `o(W_b)`.  Thus the interior
phase types may be discarded from the scalar real-claim ledger.  What
remains open here is the labelled physical endpoint coupling away from the
balanced diagonal; Theorem 3.1 supplies its first coherent
`(1/2-o(1))W_b` portion.
