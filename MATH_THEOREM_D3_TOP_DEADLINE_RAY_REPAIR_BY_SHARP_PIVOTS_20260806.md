# The top deadline band of the `D_3` suffix current is repaired by a fixed sharp-pivot bank

**Date:** 2026-08-06  
**Method:** exact nested-current indexing plus the sharp pivot packet  
**Status:** unconditional local ray realization and conditional common-host
repair/protected-factor planting.  The twenty-two generic suffix-current
channels in the noncoalesced part of the top deadline band are literal rays
of twenty-two depth-`d` sharp-pivot packets; the coalesced maximal rank needs
at most nine further packets.  Each packet costs one source position when it
is installed at its displayed monotone cut and then preserves that host's
complete old OR deck.  Resource-disjoint owner/upper-`q1` incidence paths
embed in one two-factor for all sufficiently large dimensions.  This note
does **not** prove that all cuts coexist in one literal source chronology,
that their clipped residence flags are globally completed, or that one
global compiler matching survives.  It also does not repair the deeper
payload band or construct the global upper-decorated connected host.

## 1. Parameters and the top band

Use the odd suspended pentagon parameters

\[
       k=2m+1,\qquad p=m-2,\qquad 1\le d\le p-1.       \tag{1.1}
\]

The canonically suspended whole-factor transition has the eight nested
families in Theorem 3.1 of
`MATH_THEOREM_D3_WHOLE_FACTOR_SUFFIX_CURRENT_AND_22_RAY_BATTERY_GATE_20260806.md`.
At a generic proper length `ell`, a `q`-label boundary term is decorated by
a bank profile of length

\[
                              t=\ell-q,\qquad q\in\{1,2\}.     \tag{1.2}
\]

The top deadline band is

\[
                         m-d\le\ell\le m-1.             \tag{1.3}
\]

The eight-rail formula is noncoalesced only on

\[
                         m-d\le\ell\le m-2.             \tag{1.4}
\]

At `ell=m-1` the opposite boundary intervals coalesce and the separate
eight-rail formula is replaced by the maximal-profile formula.  For fixed
`q`, put

\[
                   t_0=m-d-q.                         \tag{1.5}
\]

As `ell` runs through the noncoalesced range (1.4), the corresponding
profiles have lengths

\[
                   t_0,t_0+1,\ldots,t_0+d-2.          \tag{1.6}
\]

In the generic interior, the positive mass of the lost side is twenty-two:
three channels each for the four `q=1` families and respectively
`2,3,3,2` channels for the four `q=2` families.

For `d>=2`, append one arbitrary fresh one-coordinate extension to each
chain in (1.6).  This creates a `d`-term nested ray suitable for a depth-`d`
sharp pivot.  Its last term is a buffer and is not asserted to be a term of
the coalesced maximal current.  When `d=1`, the noncoalesced range is empty
and only the maximal-rank construction below is used.

## 2. One channel is one sharp pivot ray

Assume first that `d>=2`.  Let

\[
                         Z_0\subset Z_1\subset\cdots\subset Z_{d-1}
                                                               \tag{2.1}
\]

extend one of the decorated profile chains in (1.6), so that its first
`d-1` terms are prescribed by the current and

\[
                         |Z_i|=t_0+i.                 \tag{2.2}
\]

Let `N` be the fixed positive endpoint set in one unit decomposition of the
corresponding base current; thus `|N|=q`, `N` is disjoint from every profile,
and the required noncoalesced lost targets are

\[
                         S_i=Z_i\cup N,\qquad0\le i<d-1.      \tag{2.3}
\]

The last member `S_(d-1)=Z_(d-1) union N` is a harmless terminal buffer
target rather than a prescribed generic-current term.

Equation (1.5) gives

\[
                         |S_0|=t_0+q=m-d.             \tag{2.4}
\]

Choose one additional coordinate `rho_d` and an ordered `d`-tuple
`L=(lambda_1,...,lambda_d)`, all outside `S_0` and the prescribed
differences `Z_i minus Z_(i-1)`.  In the sharp pivot construction take

\[
 Q=X=S_0,qquad
 \rho_i=\text{the unique member of }Z_i\setminus Z_{i-1} (1\le i<d),
                                                               \tag{2.5}
\]

with the chosen `rho_d` as the last member of the `R` bank.

### Theorem 2.1 (literal channel realization)

The depth-`d` sharp pivot packet with data (2.5) has among its new source
cells exactly the complete extended ray

\[
                         S_0,S_1,\ldots,S_{d-1}.       \tag{2.6}
\]

It has a simple rank-`m` Johnson owner geodesic, pairwise-distinct lower and
upper immediate colours, and preserves every old contiguous-union
occurrence in every exterior context.  If `3d<=m+1`, its standard collar
gives exact length-`d+1` runs for the active labels.  The collar's far-left
and far-right flags remain clipped and must be extended or protected by the
ambient host.

#### Proof

The sharp-pivot aperture is met with equality by (2.4):

\[
                              |X|=m-d.                 \tag{2.7}
\]

The right new ray of that packet is

\[
 X,\quad X\cup\{\rho_1\},\quad\ldots,\quad
 X\cup\{\rho_1,\ldots,\rho_{d-1}\}.                 \tag{2.8}
\]

Substitution of (2.5) makes (2.8) equal to (2.6).  The owner, `q1`, collar,
and deck-transparency assertions are Theorems 2--4 of
`MATH_THEOREM_SHARP_PIVOT_APERTURE_AND_RESIDENT_GEODESIC_PACKET_20260801.md`.
The unused left ray and the final coordinate `rho_d` create additional
legal cells and owner resources; they do not delete any old value.  \(\square\)

The construction is asymmetric on purpose.  No signed reverse port is
needed: a final upper-bound word only has to restore the targets on the lost
side.  Surplus values and the unused second ray are harmless.

## 3. Twenty-two packets repair the generic top band

Use the unit decompositions of `A,...,F` displayed in (3.1) of
`MATH_THEOREM_D3_28_PORT_LOWER_AND_RANK_SATURATED_DUAL_ABSORBER_20260806.md`.
For every positive channel of `-Delta`, apply Theorem 2.1 to its profile
chain in (1.6), with the one-step buffer just described.

### Theorem 3.1 (top-band repair)

For `d>=2`, twenty-two sharp-pivot packets supply every lower target
occurrence lost by the suspended whole-factor transition in all
noncoalesced top-band ranks

\[
                         m-d,\ldots,m-2.              \tag{3.1}
\]

At the maximal rank `m-1`, where profiles coalesce and the `G` cap appears,
do not continue the generic indexing.  Instead, for each positive occurrence
of the negative maximal-profile current `-Delta_(m-1)` choose an arbitrary nested chain of `d`
subsets ending at that target and beginning in rank `m-d`; the sharp-pivot
construction realizes it.  The exact positive mass is nine, so at most nine
further pivots supply every maximal-rank loss.  (For `d=1`, these are the
only packets.)  Hence at most

\[
                              H_0=31                  \tag{3.2}
\]

depth-`d` local pivot packets realize the complete top-deadline loss bank.

For each packet separately, every old interval value survives insertion at
its displayed monotone cut.  If the packets are co-instantiated at distinct
literal cuts of one source chronology, successive monotone insertions
preserve the old deck.  Under the sharp-pivot theorem's target-release and
common-cap hypotheses, each packet's selected ray has a zero-deficiency
local compiler assignment.  Neither the common source host nor one global
compiler matching is asserted here.

#### Proof

At every noncoalesced generic length, the eight profile supports are
disjoint and their positive masses sum to twenty-two.  Each positive unit
persists along one nested chain as `ell` varies.  Append one buffer step and
apply Theorem 2.1; the packet supplies all `d-1` prescribed terms.  This
proves (3.1).

At the maximal length, Theorem 4.1 of the suffix-current note gives positive
mass nine for `Delta_(m-1)`.  Its total coefficient is zero, so
`-Delta_(m-1)` also has positive mass nine.  For every corresponding lost
target occurrence `S` of rank `m-1`, delete
coordinates one at a time to choose a chain from an `(m-d)`-subset to `S`;
the ground has ample fresh coordinates for the unused opposite ray.  Thus
one further packet supplies `S`, and no more than nine are needed, proving
(3.2).  The final deck and compiler sentences are exactly the conditional
composition statements in the theorem: local transparency is unconditional,
whereas common-host placement, clipped residence completion, target release,
and one common cap are additional hypotheses.  \(\square\)

The bound `31` is deliberately proof-safe rather than optimized.  The exact
28-port signed decomposition shows that fewer channels suffice after bottom
and top cancellation, but a numerical improvement is irrelevant to an
additive-constant theorem.

## 4. Conditional owner/upper-`q1` planting

One collared packet contributes `6d` protected incidences.  Lift its
rank-`m` owner path through the distinct rank-`(m+1)` union colours (the
balanced middle-levels shore on `[2m+1]`).  If the at most `H_0=31` packets
above are selected with pairwise-disjoint owner and union-colour incidence
vertices, their union is 2-bounded and has at most

\[
                              186d                   \tag{4.1}
\]

protected incidences.

### Corollary 4.1

For all sufficiently large `m`, every such resource-disjoint packet bank
extends to a spanning owner/upper-`q1` two-factor, because

\[
                              186d\le m-1.             \tag{4.2}
\]


#### Proof

The optimal deadline has `d=Theta(sqrt(m))`, so (4.2) holds eventually.
Apply the small protected middle-levels factor theorem with parameter
`m+1`.  \(\square\)

This corollary embeds the abstract owner/union-colour incidence paths.  It
does not by itself prove that the paths are simultaneous sharp-pivot cuts in
one source antecedent, complete the clipped residence flags, or provide the
global compiler matching.

Selecting the prescribed packets resource-disjointly, completing the upper
palette, joining components, and installing the deep payload atlas remain
separate.  In particular Theorem 3.1 provides a constant local witness bank
for the top-`d` lower-current row; its common-host realization is still a
premise.  It does not turn the full 28-port linear battery into a flat source
block, which is impossible by
`MATH_OBSTRUCTION_BATTERY_EQUIPPED_CROWN_FLAT_OWNER_HOST_20260806.md`.

## 5. Exact gain and remaining gate

The source-flat obstruction to the raw long battery is not an obstruction
to the top deadline band.  Buffering every rail by its rank-`(m-d)` base is
exactly the sharp aperture, and the existing pivot packet supplies the
required flat resident owner path.

After this theorem the suffix transition has three distinct residual rows:

1. the top-`d` strict-lower loss has a fixed bank of literal local pivot
   witnesses, conditionally co-instantiable as one compiler repair;
2. the polynomial upper loss may be assigned protected geodesic backups by
   the rank-stratified backup theorem; and
3. the lower ranks below `m-d` still belong to the global relative payload
   atlas and are not repaired locally.

Thus the remaining D3-to-`O(1)` bridge is no longer current arithmetic at
the compiler boundary.  It is the same genuinely global deep-payload and
host-coinstantiation problem already isolated in the PBBS route.
