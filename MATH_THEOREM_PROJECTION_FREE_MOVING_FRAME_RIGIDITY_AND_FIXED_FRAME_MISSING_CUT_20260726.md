# Projection-free moving frames: component rigidity and the fixed-frame missing cut

Date: 2026-07-26

Method: pure mathematics only.

## 0. Outcome

There is an exact obstruction to the proposed projection-free repair
inside the natural moving-perfect-matching/status-cell model.

Let \({\cal A}\) be a catalogue of perfect matchings of the \(2m\)
physical coordinates.  Every matching partitions the middle layer into
its empty/split/full status cubes.  Suppose different matching
partitions are recoupled only on complete common-owner components, so
that owner partition and whole-cycle factorhood remain exact.

Let

\[
                         G_{\cal A}
 =\left([2m],\bigcup_{M\in{\cal A}}M\right)           \tag{0.1}
\]

be the coordinate union graph.  Then:

1. common-owner components are exactly the sets with fixed occupancy in
   every connected component of \(G_{\cal A}\);
2. if \(G_{\cal A}\) is connected, the entire middle layer is one
   common-owner component, so exact recoupling permits only one global
   matching \(M_\ast\);
3. the catalogue may nevertheless be maximally projection-free: taking
   all perfect matchings makes \(G_{\cal A}=K_{2m}\);
4. after the exact selector chooses \(M_\ast\), every realized packet
   axis is an edge of \(M_\ast\), so the number of full \(M_\ast\)-pairs
   is preserved by every lower window; and
5. at \(q=A\sqrt m+O(1)\), \(A>0\), this invariant gives a literal target
   family of missing-shadow deficiency \(\Omega_A(W)\).

The linear diverse compiler does not affect the obstruction.  One can
take

\[
 {m\over128}<R\le {m\over64},\qquad
 d=4\left\lceil {5\over2}\log_2m\right\rceil ,
\]

discard only \(e^{-\Omega(m)}W\) low-split owners, and retain exact
complement symmetry, \(C_{2R}\)-cycles, parent-cell rainbows, and all
depth-\(H=o(m)\) trace recovery.  The lower missing-shadow count is still
\(\Omega_A(W)\), and complementation gives the upper cut.

Thus overlapping or moving matching frames face the exact dichotomy

\[
\boxed{
\begin{array}{c}
\text{frame union with a macroscopic component union}\\
\Longrightarrow\text{a common coordinate projection survives},\\[1mm]
\text{connected projection-free frame union}\\
\Longrightarrow\text{one common-owner component}\\
\Longrightarrow\text{one globally realized matching}\\
\Longrightarrow\text{a fixed-frame literal Hall cut}.
\end{array}}                                                   \tag{0.2}
\]

This is a no-go for complete status-cell recoupling, not for every
conceivable exterior-moving packet.  A positive construction must use
owner components whose physical exchanges are not subordinate to one
perfect matching selected on a whole overlap component.

The objective throughout is the missing-shadow hinge

\[
                         {\mathfrak H}
 =\sum_{q,\varepsilon,T}(1-L_q^\varepsilon(T))_+,
\]

not CPCR.

## 1. Status cells and exact recoupling

For a perfect matching \(M\) and middle owner
\(X\in\binom{[2m]}m\), define its status cell

\[
 C_M(X)
 =\left\{Y\in\tbinom{[2m]}m:
          |Y\cap e|=|X\cap e|\text{ for every }e\in M\right\}.
                                                               \tag{1.1}
\]

If \(s_M(X)\) pairs are split, then \(C_M(X)\cong Q_{s_M(X)}\).
The cells of one \(M\) partition the middle layer exactly.

Let \(\sim_{\cal A}\) be the equivalence relation generated by

\[
                         X\sim_MY
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad C_M(X)=C_M(Y),
 \qquad M\in{\cal A}.                                  \tag{1.2}
\]

An exact complete-cell recoupling may choose a different matching on
different \(\sim_{\cal A}\)-classes, but not on a proper subset of one
class: cells from different frames overlap there.

## 2. Common-owner component theorem

Let \(B_1,\ldots,B_c\) be the connected components of \(G_{\cal A}\).

### Theorem 2.1 (exclusion-component classification)

Two middle owners \(X,Y\) satisfy \(X\sim_{\cal A}Y\) if and only if

\[
                         |X\cap B_i|=|Y\cap B_i|
 \qquad(1\le i\le c).                                  \tag{2.1}
\]

### Proof

An orientation flip inside an \(M\)-cell moves one occupied coordinate
along one edge of \(M\), hence along one edge of \(G_{\cal A}\).  It
preserves every occupancy in (2.1), proving necessity.

Conversely, the simple exclusion graph on the \(k\)-subsets of a
connected graph is connected.  To see this, fix a spanning tree and a
canonical \(k\)-set; move particles along the tree, starting at leaves,
until the canonical set is reached.  Every elementary move uses an edge
belonging to some matching in \({\cal A}\), hence lies inside one status
cell.  Apply this independently in all \(B_i\). \(\square\)

### Corollary 2.2 (connected rigidity)

If \(G_{\cal A}\) is connected, then

\[
                         \binom{[2m]}m
\]

is one common-owner component.  Therefore every exact componentwise
matching selector is global.

In particular, the catalogue of all perfect matchings has complete union
graph \(K_{2m}\), crosses every nontrivial coordinate cut at catalogue
level, and still permits only one globally selected matching.

## 3. Linear compiler installation survives

Fix the globally selected matching \(M_\ast\).  Its status cells partition
the middle layer.  Under the unconditioned Boolean-cube law, the number
of split pairs is exactly

\[
                         S\sim\operatorname{Bin}(m,1/2).        \tag{3.1}
\]

Choose the largest admissible \(R=8\cdot2^r\le m/64\).  Then

\[
                         {m\over128}<R\le {m\over64},
 \qquad16R\le {m\over4}.                              \tag{3.2}
\]

Hoeffding gives

\[
                         \Pr(S<16R)\le e^{-m/8}.                 \tag{3.3}
\]

Conditioning on total rank \(m\) costs only \(O(\sqrt m)\), so the
low-split middle-owner mass is \(e^{-\Omega(m)}W\).

On every retained cell, divide \(16R\) split axes into \(R\) groups of
sixteen, apply the exact complement-equivariant \(Q_{16}\) matching in
each group, and install the diverse-order compiler in every resulting
\(Q_R\)-packet.  Exactly as in the patched linear theorem, this gives:

* \(C_{2R}\)-cycles with \(2R=\Theta(m)\);
* \(o(W/H)\) cycles and exponential owner leave;
* geodesic windows and literal trace recovery through every \(q\le H\);
* exact packet recovery and parent-cell rainbows; and
* \(P(X^c)=P(X)^c\), hence fused signs.

The logarithmic bookkeeping blocks of size
\(d=(10+o(1))\log_2m\) may be aligned with groups of matching edges.  They
remain above the all-depth full-block threshold.  None of these changes
the fixed physical frame \(M_\ast\).

## 4. The preserved literal profile

For a set \(Z\subseteq[2m]\), put

\[
                         F_M(Z)=\#\{e\in M:e\subseteq Z\}.       \tag{4.1}
\]

### Lemma 4.1 (full-pair invariance)

If every packet axis is an edge of \(M\), then for every lower window
with middle start \(X\) and intersection target \(T\),

\[
                         F_M(T)=F_M(X).                         \tag{4.2}
\]

### Proof

A full \(M\)-pair is frozen in its status cell, so both endpoints occur
in every vertex of the window and hence in \(T\).  An empty pair remains
empty.  A split pair contributes either one endpoint to \(T\), if its
direction is not completed, or no endpoint, if it is completed.  It
never becomes full.  Thus the full pairs of \(T\) are exactly those of
\(X\). \(\square\)

The assertion is independent of the cycle order, compiler conjugate,
packet tiling, and correlations between different cells.

## 5. Exact source and target profile counts

For \(0\le f\le m/2\), let

\[
\begin{aligned}
 S_f&=\#\{X\in\tbinom{[2m]}m:F_M(X)=f\},\\
 T_{q,f}&=\#\{T\in\tbinom{[2m]}{m-q}:F_M(T)=f\}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Choosing full, split, and empty pairs gives exactly

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
 S_f&={m!\over f!\,f!\,(m-2f)!}\,2^{m-2f},\\
 T_{q,f}
 &={m!\over f!\,(f+q)!\,(m-q-2f)!}\,2^{m-q-2f}.
\end{aligned}}                                                   \tag{5.1}
\]

Consequently

\[
\boxed{
 {T_{q,f}\over S_f}
 =2^{-q}{(m-2f)_q\over(f+1)^{\overline q}},}           \tag{5.2}
\]

where \((x)_q=x(x-1)\cdots(x-q+1)\) and
\((x+1)^{\overline q}=(x+1)\cdots(x+q)\).

By Lemma 4.1, every legal configuration covers at most \(S_f\) distinct
lower targets in profile \(f\).  Therefore the exact fixed-frame Hall
deficit is

\[
                         \Delta_{m,q}(M)
 =\sum_f(T_{q,f}-S_f)_+.                              \tag{5.3}
\]

It is a missing-shadow deficit; no multiplicity above one occurs in
(5.3).

## 6. Gaussian evaluation

Put

\[
                         q=A\sqrt m+O(1),\qquad A>0.             \tag{6.1}
\]

The full-pair count of a uniform rank-\((m-q)\) target has mean

\[
 \mu_q={ (m-q)(m-q-1)\over2(2m-1)}
       ={m\over4}-{q\over2}+O_A(1)                    \tag{6.2}
\]

and variance \(\Theta_A(m)\).  Its normalized law has a nondegenerate
Gaussian limit, by the local central limit theorem applied to the
coefficient array \((1+2xz+x^2)^m\).

Write

\[
                         f={m\over4}-{A\over2}\sqrt m+z\sqrt m.
                                                               \tag{6.3}
\]

Uniformly for bounded \(z\), expansion of (5.2) gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
 \log {T_{q,f}\over S_f}
 &=q\log{m-2f\over2f}
   -{q^2\over2(m-2f)}-{q^2\over2f}+o(1)\\
 &=A^2-8Az+o(1).                                     \tag{6.4}
\end{aligned}
\]

Choose the target band

\[
 {\cal I}_A
 =\left\{f:
   \left|f-\left({m\over4}-{A\over2}\sqrt m\right)\right|
          \le {A\over16}\sqrt m\right\}.             \tag{6.5}
\]

Equation (6.4) gives, uniformly on this band,

\[
                         {T_{q,f}\over S_f}
 \ge e^{A^2/2+o(1)}.                                  \tag{6.6}
\]

The local central limit theorem gives a constant \(c_A>0\) with

\[
                         \sum_{f\in{\cal I}_A}T_{q,f}
 \ge(c_A-o(1))N_q.                                    \tag{6.7}
\]

Combining (5.3), (6.6), and (6.7) proves:

### Theorem 6.1 (fixed-frame missing-shadow cut)

For every legal compiler and packet state subordinate to \(M\),

\[
\boxed{
 {\mathfrak H}_q^-
 \ge(c_A-o(1))(1-e^{-A^2/2})N_q
 =\Omega_A(W).}                                      \tag{6.8}
\]

For the fixed realized frame \(M\), the indicator of the lower targets
with \(F_M(T)\in{\cal I}_A\) is a literal Hall witness of order \(W\).
When the outer option menu still contains several possible global
matchings, this *profile-band* witness depends on the selected matching.
Nevertheless (6.8) is uniform in the global option, so the all-ones
weight on the whole target layer is a common separator for the convex
hull of the **literal support indicators**: every option support has size
at most \(N_q-\Omega_A(W)\). It does not separate the raw occurrence
vectors, whose symmetric average has coordinate load \(W/N_q>1\).
Collapsing repeats before taking the convex hull is therefore essential.

Under complementation, full lower pairs become empty upper pairs.
Therefore the same fused state has the identical upper obstruction.

## 7. The projection-free dichotomy

The catalogue \({\cal A}\) can have excellent geometric expansion.  For
example, all perfect matchings give every coordinate edge.  That
fractional catalogue fact does not imply that a realized exact factor
uses moving frames on different owner regions.

Within complete status-cell recoupling, the two decisive cases are:

1. If \(G_{\cal A}\) has more than one macroscopic coordinate component,
   the associated occupancy vector is a statewise positive-density
   projection invariant.
2. If \(G_{\cal A}\) is connected, the selector is global by Corollary
   2.2, and Theorem 6.1 applies to its selected matching.

The intermediate situation of one \(2m-o(m)\) coordinate component plus
an \(o(m)\) fringe is not needed for the connected no-go and is not
claimed to be covered by the first case without an additional fringe
conditioning argument.

Hence no connected moving-perfect-matching atlas can simultaneously have:

* exact owner partition by complete status cells;
* owner-dependent frame selection;
* statewise projection-free packet directions; and
* \(o(W)\) missing shadows at Gaussian depth.

This conclusion concerns the exact \(L^1\) missing objective.  It neither
uses nor claims CPCR.  A possible escape must use packets whose exterior
also moves while frames change, so that their owner overlap is not the
simple exclusion component of a union of perfect matchings.
