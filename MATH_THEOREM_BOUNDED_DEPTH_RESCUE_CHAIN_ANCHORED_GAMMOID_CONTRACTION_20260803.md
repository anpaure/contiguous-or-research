### Lemma 1.1 (iterated host aperture)

Fix an already materialized endpoint-disjoint mode set \(C\), a role \(x\),
a phase \(\phi\), and one further admissible fixed-bank-safe transfer \(t\)
whose endpoints are disjoint from \(C\).  Adding \(t\) deletes all long

\[
 {\cal T}_x^\phi(k;C+t)
 =\{u\in{\cal T}_x^\phi(k;C):d_t\notin\operatorname{row}(u)\}
   \;\dot\cup\;
   \{u\in{\cal T}_x^\phi(k;C+t):h_t\in\operatorname{row}(u)\}. \tag{1.3}
\]

In particular, if \({\cal T}_x^\phi(k;C)=\varnothing\), every tuple created
at key \(k\) by \(t\) uses \(h_t\).

#### Proof

Deleting a physical row can only remove tuples.  Every tuple not inherited
after that deletion must use a newly available long state, and the only new
long states are at \(h_t\).  The two displayed classes are disjoint because
\(h_t\) was not a long-state row before installing \(t\). \(\square\)

## 2. Terminally closed bounded-size rescue modules

Fix a parent completed supplier graph \(\widehat G^0=(L,R;E^0)\), a parent
maximum matching \(M\) of size \(r\), and one fully materialized and fully
replayed child \(\widehat G^+=(L,R;E^+)\).  Fix

\[
 M_0\subseteq M\cap E^+,
 \qquad |M_0|=r-q.                                    \tag{2.1}
\]

### Definition 2.1 (closed rescue module)

A closed rescue module of size at most \(D\) consists of a distinguished
ordered rescue spine

\[
 a=(m_0,m_1,\ldots,m_{\ell}),
                                                               \tag{2.2}
\]

together with an optional terminal fork/circuit block and fixed phase-paired
literal tuples in the **final simultaneous child**, such that:

1. every missing role of a nonterminal mode is supplied by a named later
   spine host or by a named mode of the terminal block;
2. the terminal mode is native, or is closed by an authenticated
   zero-boundary fork/circuit contained in the module; every primitive mode
   and physical resource of that block is included, and the **total** number
   of primitive modes in the spine and block is at most \(D\);
3. every later donor deletion has already been replayed against every
   earlier tuple;
4. endpoint, address, flag, history, reset, residence, upper and compiler
   effects are part of the fixed state; and
5. the module exposes only its declared compensation and gain service claims
   and their actual free-head port menus.

An ordinary rescue chain is the special case with an empty terminal block.
Pairwise rescue edges do not prove Definition 2.1.  They may reuse one
capacity-one row, disagree on a flag, or be destroyed by a later donor
deletion.

A closed module with source role \(e\) is **bilateral at the source** when
two distinct named helper hosts both occur in the union of the source's
selected phase footprints.  This is a property of the literal selected
source option in the final child.  It neither asserts that either helper
role is complete nor substitutes for any terminal-closure clause above.

### Definition 2.2 (coherent private module bank)

A bank \({\cal A}\) of closed modules is coherent and private when:

1. all its primitive modes and fixed states coexist in the same child
   \(\widehat G^+\);
2. all non-supplier closure footprints of distinct modules are disjoint, or
   an overlap is retained as an exact directed capacity gadget preserving
   its full boundary linkage relation, rather than only its singleton
   transitions or a silently duplicated capacity;
3. every shared capacity, including local-versus-supplier interaction, is a
   literal unit-capacity vertex of one network;
4. each exposed boundary is state-transparent: a contracted capacity-one
   module either has a fixed ingress/egress or has a rectangular relation
   between its admissible ingresses and egresses; a nonrectangular module is
   retained in full;
5. every edge of \(M\setminus M_0\) and every non-supplier casualty is
   completely attributed to a module or to an explicit global boundary set;
6. module states and the child do not change when the exposed claim subset
   changes;
7. the complete interface has the capacity-faithful lifting property: for
   every claim subset and every selection of its actual boundary ports,
   simultaneous physical service in this fixed child holds if and only if
   those ports have vertex-disjoint linkages to the supplier sinks in the
   represented network.  Every interaction between a module boundary and
   the rest of the child occurs through that network; and
8. each claim-to-port prefix is private to that occurrence and is contracted
   before its menu is declared.  Every shared capacity lies downstream of
   the declared entry ports and remains in the claim-free base network.  If
   this fails, the full augmented gadget must be retained and the port-Rado
   formula below is not asserted.

The quantifier order is

\[
 \boxed{
 \text{select and terminally close the bank}
 \to\text{materialize one child}
 \to\text{replay suppliers}
 \to\text{form the claim network}.}                  \tag{2.3}
\]

## 3. Private-prefix contraction gives a strict-gammoid restriction

Orient the child supplier graph alternately with respect to \(M_0\), split
every unit-capacity physical vertex, and use the unmatched right vertices as
sink bank \(T\).  For each occurrence-labelled service claim \(u\),
privately contract its claim-side prefix and add an indegree-zero claim
vertex \(x_u\) with arcs to its actual entry menu \(P_u\) of
\(M_0\)-unmatched free-head ports.  Every shared physical capacity lies
downstream of these ports and remains in the network.  Call the resulting
directed graph \({\cal D}_{\cal A}\).

### Theorem 3.1 (bounded-size private-prefix contraction)

For a coherent private module bank, a claim set is jointly serviceable if
and only if its claim vertices are linkable by vertex-disjoint paths to
\(T\) in \({\cal D}_{\cal A}\).  Consequently the serviceable claim sets
form the restriction to the claim vertices of the strict gammoid
\(L({\cal D}_{\cal A},T)\).

Let \(\Gamma\) be the port-gammoid restriction of the entire claim-free
base of \({\cal D}_{\cal A}\), including every retained module-interface and
shared-capacity gadget.  Then
for every claim set \(X\),

\[
 r_{\rm ch}(X)=
 \min_{Y\subseteq X}
 \left(
 |X\setminus Y|+
 r_\Gamma\!\left(\bigcup_{u\in Y}P_u\right)
 \right).                                             \tag{3.1}
\]

Claim-to-port arcs are infinite-capacity and therefore uncuttable.  In the
fully split augmented network, let \(W\) denote the base-network portion of
a sink-avoiding cut, excluding \(T\) and its output terminals.  Claim
vertices are optimized separately: \(x_u\) stays on the source side exactly
when every port in \(P_u\) lies in \(W\).  Let \(c(W)\) count every crossed
finite arc and unit vertex-split arc; assign \(+\infty\) when an infinite
base-network arc leaves \(W\).  Put

\[
 t_X(W)=|\{u\in X:P_u\subseteq W\}|.                  \tag{3.2}
\]

Then

\[
 \boxed{
 r_{\rm ch}(X)
 =|X|-\max_W\bigl(t_X(W)-c(W)\bigr).
 }                                                       \tag{3.3}
\]

#### Proof

All occurrence dependencies have already been closed in the fixed child.
Private prefixes can be expanded or contracted without changing a service
linkage.  Every remaining shared resource is a literal capacity vertex, so
a linkage decodes losslessly to the same number of simultaneous
\(M_0\)-augmenting paths, and every admitted physical service bank gives
such a linkage.  This proves the strict-gammoid statement.  Equation (3.1)
is deficient Rado for the claim menus.  Eliminating the claim starts from
the integral max-flow/min-cut formula gives (3.3). \(\square\)

Bounded depth is not used in the gammoid proof.  Its role is only to make
the module footprint, matching loss and casualty signature uniformly
controllable.

### Proposition 3.2 (rectangular one-vertex interface)

Let an otherwise isolated capacity-one module have ingress set \(I\),
egress set \(O\), no boundary or shared interaction except through
\(I\cup O\), and exact one-traversal relation
\(R\subseteq I\times O\).  Replacing
the module by one unit vertex with every used ingress arc and every used
egress arc represents

\[
               \pi_I(R)\times\pi_O(R).                \tag{3.4}
\]

Thus this contraction is exact only when \(R\) is rectangular.  (A fixed
one-point side is a rectangular special case.)  For example, the diagonal relation
\(\{(i_1,o_1),(i_2,o_2)\}\) acquires two false cross transitions.  Splitting
the two states without a coupling gadget duplicates the physical capacity.

#### Proof

Every incoming arc at the contracted vertex concatenates with every
outgoing arc.  This is exactly the Cartesian product in (3.4). \(\square\)

Address/history-dependent shared gates therefore require their full exact
gadget; a generic anonymous capacity vertex is not proof-safe.

## 4. Bounded-depth Hall cascade

The following separates chain allocation from terminal supplier routing.
Fix one master child containing all occurrence-labelled states used below.
Let

\[
 V_0,V_1,\ldots,V_d,\qquad d<D,                      \tag{4.1}
\]

be disjoint state layers.  Between \(V_{i-1}\) and \(V_i\), let \(H_i\)
be an exact unit-capacity bipartite transition graph.  Its vertices encode
complete states, and the layer interiors use disjoint physical resources;
nonrectangular or shared-capacity behavior has already been retained as an
exact state gadget.  Assume explicitly the capacity-faithful decode: every
family of vertex-disjoint layered paths, and only such a family, gives
jointly coexisting rescue chains in the one child.  Individual edge legality
alone is not this hypothesis.

Write \(r_i(X)\) for the matching rank of \(X\subseteq V_{i-1}\) into
\(V_i\).  Let \(\Gamma_T\) be a terminal supplier-port gammoid on \(V_d\),
whose internal vertices are disjoint from the rescue layers except at
\(V_d\).

### Theorem 4.1 (deficiencies add along a rescue cascade)

Suppose \(C_i,C_S\ge0\) and

\[
 r_i(X)\ge |X|-C_i
 \quad(X\subseteq V_{i-1}),                            \tag{4.2}
\]

and

\[
 r_{\Gamma_T}(Y)\ge |Y|-C_S
 \quad(Y\subseteq V_d).                               \tag{4.3}
\]

Then every \(X\subseteq V_0\) contains at least

\[
 \boxed{|X|-C_H-C_S,\qquad C_H=\sum_{i=1}^d C_i,}    \tag{4.4}
\]

sources which extend through pairwise vertex-disjoint closed rescue chains
and terminal supplier routes.

More generally, if \(0\le\eta\le1\), \(\gamma\ge0\), and

\[
 r_{\Gamma_T}(Y)\ge\eta|Y|-\gamma,
\]

then at least

\[
              \eta(|X|-C_H)-\gamma                    \tag{4.5}
\]

sources obtain terminal routes.

#### Proof

Match all but at most \(C_1\) elements of \(X\) into \(V_1\).  Apply
(4.2) to the image and retain all but at most \(C_2\) of those paths.
Continue through the layers.  The terminal image has size at least
\(|X|-C_H\).  Choose an independent subset in \(\Gamma_T\) using (4.3),
or its fractional analogue, and discard the other layered paths.  Injective
matchings and the disjoint/exact layer hypothesis make the concatenated
paths vertex-disjoint and physically decodable. \(\square\)

The sum in (4.4) is sharp for this information: different layers may lose
disjoint source subsets.

If layer \(i\) is a bounded erosion of the odd-central coatom containment
graph after a protected matching of size \(t_i\), the protected-coatom
lemma gives the sufficient value

\[
              C_i=E_i+\max\{t_i-1,0\}.                \tag{4.6}
\]

Thus bounded depth, bounded literal erosion, and bounded protected-bank
sizes would give \(C_H=O(1)\).  The current Pascal notes prove the abstract
coatom expansion, not that the actual state-labelled rescue layers satisfy
this erosion hypothesis.

Theorem 1.2 shows that this ordinary linear-cascade route cannot instantiate
the canonical K17 H-source face at three modes: its complete single-helper
branch is empty.  A K17 application must instead allocate exact bilateral
fork/circuit modules, or use a deeper terminal closure, before Theorem 3.1
can be invoked.

### Lemma 4.2 (minimal split-bilateral permutation skeleton)

Let \(n\) roles and \(n\) named helper hosts be indexed by
\(\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z\).  Suppose the installed-host footprint selected by
role \(i\) in phase \(\phi\) consists of exactly one nonself helper host
\(h_{\sigma_\phi(i)}\), and every helper host is used exactly once in that
phase.  Any additional base-provider rows are private and outside this
skeleton.  Thus \(\sigma_\phi\) is a derangement.  Require split
bilaterality

\[
 \sigma_0(i)\ne\sigma_1(i)
 \qquad(i\in\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z).
                                                               \tag{4.7}
\]

Then \(n\ge3\).  At \(n=3\), up to relabelling the roles and exchanging the
two phases, the unique solution is

\[
 \boxed{\sigma_0(i)=i+1,\qquad\sigma_1(i)=i-1.}        \tag{4.8}
\]

#### Proof

For \(n=1\) there is no derangement.  For \(n=2\) there is only the
transposition, so the two phases cannot satisfy (4.7).  The only
derangements of three points are the two oppositely oriented 3-cycles;
(4.7) forces the phases to use one each. \(\square\)

This is the smallest and unique skeleton only in the stated
one-host-per-role, phasewise-permutation class.  It does not cover a
same-phase \(G2\) ticket, unused hosts, or the weaker situation in which only
one distinguished H source is bilateral.  In particular, it is a host
incidence pattern, not an occurrence-positive or terminally closed module.
On the all-three-H subface, the exact bilateral quotient has eight host
matrices.  Exactly two are split one-host-per-role matrices--the two ordered
orientations in (4.8); the other six contain a same-phase two-helper/empty
source pattern.  Indeed each host chooses which of its two other roles gets
it in phase 0, with the other role getting it in phase 1, giving \(2^3=8\);
phasewise indegree one at every role occurs only for the two coherent cycle
orientations.  Theorem 1.7 eliminates the other six on the authenticated
triangular K17 ticket face, so an all-three-H source-option candidate there
is forced into one of these two opposed orientations.  It does not prove
that either orientation has literal tickets.

### Lemma 4.3 (opposed triangle has zero named lower current)

Suppose three mixed-coatom packets are prospectively prepared with one
literal aligned base \(B\), the same filler-profile pair \(P_s,S_s\) at
every depth \(2\le s\le d\), and active transitions

\[
              x_0\longrightarrow x_1\longrightarrow x_2
                   \longrightarrow x_0.                         \tag{4.9}
\]

If a transition \(x\to y\) has signed lower-value action

\[
 \Delta_s(x,y)=
 [B\cup P_s\cup\{y\}]+[B\cup S_s\cup\{x\}]
 -[B\cup P_s\cup\{x\}]-[B\cup S_s\cup\{y\}],        \tag{4.10}
\]

then

\[
        \boxed{\Delta_s(x_0,x_1)+\Delta_s(x_1,x_2)
                    +\Delta_s(x_2,x_0)=0}             \tag{4.11}
\]

for every audited depth.

#### Proof

Both rails in (4.10) telescope around the closed 3-cycle. \(\square\)

Equation (4.11) is equality in the free abelian group on named lower target
values.  It is not equality of occurrence-labelled target--cell incidences,
does not create the prospective alignment in (4.9), and does not certify
address/history, supplier, reset, residence or compiler closure.
The canonical local realization is available only for prepared slots with

planting or all-\(k\) regeneration theorem.  Its depth-one counter is
unchanged packetwise.

                         L=q(q-1)                      \tag{4.12}

candidate triangles.  Assume:

   \]

Then the tasks have a full independent transversal of counted U1--U4

 \boxed{

## 5. The exact loss-normalized cut

Fix one selected bank in one replayed child.  Let \(Q\) be its \(q\)
compensation claims, one for every parent-matching unit omitted from
\(M_0\), and let \(G\) be its \(g\) genuine gain claims.  Put

\[
 \Delta(Q\cup G)
 =\max_W\bigl(t_{Q\cup G}(W)-c(W)\bigr).              \tag{5.1}
\]

### Theorem 5.1 (one-exposure loss normalization)

Let \(p\) be the maximum number of claims from this exposed \(Q\cup G\)
bank which are simultaneously serviceable; it is a certified augmentation
count and need not equal the child's total augmentation maximum when other
free heads are unexposed.  Then

\[
 \boxed{p=q+g-\Delta(Q\cup G).}                       \tag{5.2}
\]

Consequently the complete-potential recourse upper bound

\[
             \Phi'\le\Phi+q-p+c_{\rm ns}              \tag{5.3}
\]

becomes

\[
 \boxed{
 \Phi'\le\Phi-g+\Delta(Q\cup G)+c_{\rm ns}.
 }                                                       \tag{5.4}
\]

For a fixed child, exposed bank, and target parameters
\(0\le\eta\le1\), \(\gamma\ge0\), the weakest exact **full augmented
claim-network** cut condition for this bank to satisfy

\[
                         p\ge q+\eta g-\gamma          \tag{5.5}
\]

is

\[
 \boxed{
 \Delta(Q\cup G)\le(1-\eta)g+\gamma.
 }                                                       \tag{5.6}
\]

Equivalently, every trapped-menu cut obeys

\[
 c(W)\ge t_{Q\cup G}(W)-(1-\eta)g-\gamma.             \tag{5.7}
\]

#### Proof

Equation (5.2) is (3.3) with \(|Q\cup G|=q+g\).  Substitution gives
(5.4), and rearranging (5.2) gives (5.6)--(5.7). \(\square\)

Thus arbitrary \(q\) cancels in the exact target.  For a requested
recourse-RHS reduction \(R\), the exact sufficient-and-necessary condition
at the level of the right side of (5.3) is

\[
        \Delta(Q\cup G)+c_{\rm ns}\le g-R.            \tag{5.8}
\]

Because (5.3) is only an upper accounting row, (5.8) is sufficient, not
necessary, for the actual child potential to fall by \(R\).

In particular, for a requested affine decrement \(\rho\Phi-C\), the sharp
same-child condition at the accounting-right-side level is

\[
 \boxed{
 g-\Delta(Q\cup G)-c_{\rm ns}\ge\rho\Phi-C.
 }                                                       \tag{5.9}
\]

Equivalently,
\(\Delta(Q\cup G)+c_{\rm ns}\le g-\rho\Phi+C\).  A
one-transition \(O(1)\) reset asks for (5.9) with \(\rho=1\) and
\(C=O(1)\).  This exact net-surplus condition is weaker than any chosen
separation into \(\eta\)-expansion and \(\chi\)-casualty bounds.

Condition (5.6) is a one-exposure statement.  When the later gain subset is
not known, the following anchored hereditary form is the exact robust row.

## 6. Compensation-first anchored expansion

Let \({\cal N}\) be the claim gammoid of Theorem 3.1.  Choose a set
\(I\subseteq Q\) of compensation claims with

\[
                 |I|\ge q-\beta.                      \tag{6.1}
\]

Here \(\beta,\gamma\ge0\) and \(0\le\eta\le1\).

### Theorem 6.1 (anchored trapped-menu equivalence)

The following two rows together are equivalent to

\[
 I\text{ is independent in }{\cal N},
 \qquad
 r_{\cal N}(I\cup Y)-|I|\ge\eta|Y|-\gamma
       \quad(Y\subseteq G):                           \tag{6.2}
\]

\[
 \boxed{
 \begin{aligned}
 c(W)&\ge t_I(W),\\
 c(W)&\ge t_I(W)+\eta t_G(W)-\gamma
 \end{aligned}
 \qquad\text{for every finite-cut-admissible }W.
 }                                                       \tag{6.3}
\]

Consequently there is one independent claim set containing every member of
\(I\) and at least \(\eta g-\gamma\) gain claims, and hence

\[
 \boxed{
 p\ge q-\beta+\eta g-\gamma.
 }                                                       \tag{6.4}
\]

#### Proof

By (3.3), independence of \(I\) is equivalent to the first row of (6.3).
For \(Y\subseteq G\), (3.3) gives

\[
 r_{\cal N}(I\cup Y)
 =|I|+|Y|-max_W\bigl(t_I(W)+t_Y(W)-c(W)\bigr).        \tag{6.5}
\]

The second rank row in (6.2) is therefore equivalent, for every \(W,Y\),
to

\[
 c(W)\ge t_I(W)+t_Y(W)-(1-\eta)|Y|-\gamma.            \tag{6.6}
\]

For fixed \(W\), the right side is maximized by taking exactly the gain
claims whose whole menus lie in \(W\); each such claim contributes
\(\eta\), and every untrapped claim contributes \(-(1-\eta)\).  The maximum
is \(t_I(W)+\eta t_G(W)-\gamma\), proving (6.3).

Since \(I\) is independent, extend it to a basis of \({\cal N}|(I\cup G)\).
The rank row supplies at least \(\eta g-\gamma\) additional gain elements.
Together with (6.1) this proves (6.4). \(\square\)

The extension and its augmenting paths are recomputed in the same child;
Theorem 6.1 does not assert one fixed linkage works for every \(Y\).

### Corollary 6.2 (fixed compensation linkage)

Fix one linkage \(L_I\) routing every claim of \(I\).  Delete all capacities
and sinks used by \(L_I\), and call the residual gain network
\({\cal D}/L_I\).  Delete unavailable ports from every gain menu and compute
both \(t_G\) and \(c_I\) in this residual network.  If its trapped-menu cuts
satisfy

\[
             c_I(W)\ge\eta t_G(W)-\gamma              \tag{6.7}
\]

for every residual sink-avoiding \(W\), then (6.4) holds.

#### Proof

The residual cut theorem gives at least \(\eta g-\gamma\) disjoint gain
paths.  They are disjoint from \(L_I\), so adjoining \(L_I\) gives (6.4).
\(\square\)

This fixed-linkage factorization is a concrete sufficient certificate.  It
can be stronger than (6.3), because the exact matroid contraction may reroute
the compensation claims when the gain set changes.

### Corollary 6.3 (exact compensation-basis normalization)

Let \(I\) be a basis of the compensation restriction
\({\cal N}|Q\), and put

\[
 \beta_Q=q-r_{\cal N}(Q),\qquad
 h_I=r_{\cal N}(I\cup G)-r_{\cal N}(I).              \tag{6.8}
\]

Then \(I\) spans every claim in \(Q\), and

\[
 \boxed{
 p-q=h_I-\beta_Q=g-\Delta(Q\cup G).
 }                                                       \tag{6.9}
\]

Consequently the complete-potential row is exactly bounded by

\[
 \boxed{
 \Phi'\le\Phi-h_I+\beta_Q+c_{\rm ns}.
 }                                                       \tag{6.10}
\]

#### Proof

Since \(I\) is a basis of \({\cal N}|Q\), it is independent, spans \(Q\),
and \(|I|=r_{\cal N}(Q)=q-\beta_Q\).  Hence

\[
 p=r_{\cal N}(Q\cup G)=r_{\cal N}(I\cup G)=|I|+h_I.
\]

Subtracting \(q\), and comparing with (5.2), gives (6.9).  Substitution in
(5.3) gives (6.10). \(\square\)

Thus \(\beta_Q\) is the exact compensation-rank defect, not an arbitrary
matching-loss price.  An independently chosen anchor as in Theorem 6.1 is
still useful when it has a simpler physical certificate, but then (6.4) is
only a lower bound rather than the identity (6.9).

## 7. Uniform contraction theorems

### Theorem 7.1 (anchored rescue-module contraction)

Suppose one reachable state with complete carried potential \(\Phi\) admits,
in one child and one compatible spine, with all rate and additive parameters
below nonnegative:

1. a bounded-size rescue-module allocation with total deficit \(C_H\),
   producing \(g\) terminally closed modules with one genuine gain claim each
   and

   \[
                  g\ge\alpha\Phi-b_0-C_H;             \tag{7.1}
   \]
2. a fully replayed coherent private bank of those modules;
3. an independent compensation anchor \(I\) and anchored gain expansion
   satisfying
   Theorem 6.1 with \(\beta,\eta,\gamma\);
4. complete non-supplier casualty accounting

   \[
                  c_{\rm ns}\le\chi g+b_{\rm ns};     \tag{7.2}
   \]

5. the complete potential row (5.3).

Put

\[
                         s=\eta-\chi.                 \tag{7.3}
\]

If \(s>0\), then

\[
 \boxed{
 \Phi'\le
 [1-\alpha s]\Phi
 +s(b_0+C_H)+\beta+\gamma+b_{\rm ns}.
 }                                                       \tag{7.4}
\]

#### Proof

By (6.4),

\[
 q-p\le-\eta g+\beta+\gamma.
\]

Use (5.3) and (7.2), then substitute (7.1). \(\square\)

For one finite child, the displayed factorization guarantees strict descent
precisely when

\[
                 sg>\beta+\gamma+b_{\rm ns}.          \tag{7.5}
\]

Thus \(\eta>\chi\) is only the sign condition on the extensive term; it is
not by itself a finite-child descent theorem.  If the anchor is a
compensation basis, replace \(\beta\) in (7.4)--(7.5) by the exact defect
\(\beta_Q\) of (6.8).

Put

\[
 B=b_0+C_H,\qquad
 D=\beta+\gamma+b_{\rm ns},\qquad
 \lambda=\alpha s,\qquad K=sB+D.                     \tag{7.6}
\]

Then (7.4) is \(\Phi'\le(1-\lambda)\Phi+K\).  If
\(0<\lambda<1\), this is an ordinary affine contraction with fixed point
at most \(K/\lambda\).  If \(\lambda=1\), one transition gives
\(\Phi'\le K\).  If \(\lambda>1\), nonnegativity of \(\Phi'\) and (7.4)
force the current parent potential already to satisfy
\(\Phi\le K/(\lambda-1)\); this is not an ordinary contraction regime.
For an a priori unbounded parent, exact cancellation of the parent
coefficient in one transition is
\(\alpha(\eta-\chi)=1\) with \(K=O(1)\).  The familiar endpoint

\[
 \alpha=\eta=1,
 \qquad \chi=0,
 \qquad C_H+\beta+\gamma+b_0+b_{\rm ns}=O(1),         \tag{7.7}
\]

is one sufficient realization.  It is the only such realization only if an
additional bound \(\alpha\le1\) is known.  Rescue depth and \(q\) do not
enter the coefficient.

### Corollary 7.2 (regenerated varying-step criterion)

Suppose the same hypotheses regenerate for steps \(i=0,\ldots,N-1\), on one
compatible spine, with

\[
 0<\lambda_i=\alpha_i(\eta_i-\chi_i)\le1,
 \qquad
 K_i=(\eta_i-\chi_i)(b_{0,i}+C_{H,i})
       +\beta_i+\gamma_i+b_{{\rm ns},i}.              \tag{7.8}
\]

Then

\[
 \boxed{
 \Phi_N\le
 \Phi_0\prod_{i=0}^{N-1}(1-\lambda_i)
 +\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}K_j
       \prod_{i=j+1}^{N-1}(1-\lambda_i).
 }                                                       \tag{7.9}
\]

These two terms being \(O(1)\) are the weakest numerical terminal
requirements furnished by these recurrences.  In particular, pointwise
\(\eta_i>\chi_i\) is insufficient.  A convenient uniform sufficient form
is \(\lambda_i\ge\lambda_0>0\), \(K_i\le K_0=O(1)\), which gives

\[
 \Phi_N\le(1-\lambda_0)^N\Phi_0+{K_0\over\lambda_0}. \tag{7.10}
\]

One still needs \((1-\lambda_0)^{N(k)}\Phi_0(k)=O(1)\).

#### Proof

Iterate (7.4) with the step-dependent parameters.  Bounding every factor
after step \(j\) by the uniform geometric factor gives (7.10). \(\square\)

### Theorem 7.3 (unanchored depth-price fallback)

Suppose instead that only the symmetric combined-claim rank row

\[
              p\ge\eta(q+g)-\gamma                    \tag{7.11}
\]

is available, together with

\[
 q\le\omega g+b_q,
 \qquad
 c_{\rm ns}\le\chi g+b_{\rm ns}.                     \tag{7.12}
\]

Put

\[
              s_D=\eta-(1-\eta)\omega-\chi.           \tag{7.13}
\]

Then

\[
 \Phi'\le
 \Phi-s_Dg+(1-\eta)b_q+\gamma+b_{\rm ns}.            \tag{7.14}
\]

If (7.1) holds and \(s_D>0\),

\[
 \boxed{
 \Phi'\le
 [1-\alpha s_D]\Phi
 +s_D(b_0+C_H)+(1-\eta)b_q+\gamma+b_{\rm ns}.
 }                                                       \tag{7.15}
\]

If every one-gain module has at most \(D\) primitive modes, every primitive
damage set contains at most \(w\) edges of \(M\), and every omitted edge of
\(M\setminus M_0\) is attributed to one of those damage sets or to the
\(b_q\) boundary set, then \(\omega\le wD\).  No such conclusion is valid
from marginal mode damage alone when root/common-basis rematerialization can
delete further matching edges.

The scalar threshold is

\[
 \boxed{
 \eta>{\omega+\chi\over1+\omega}.
 }                                                       \tag{7.16}
\]

Thus bounded depth without compensation-first routing may require
near-perfect combined expansion.  At \(\eta=1\), matching loss cancels and
only \(\chi<1\) remains.

## 8. Sharp bounded-depth obstructions

### Proposition 8.1 (raw chain packing is not a matroid)

Take three individually closed atoms with non-supplier capacity footprints

\[
                 F_a=\{x\},\qquad
                 F_b=\{x,y\},\qquad
                 F_c=\{y\}.                           \tag{8.1}
\]

Call a bank feasible when its footprints are disjoint.  Then \(\{a,c\}\)
and \(\{b\}\) are feasible, but neither \(a\) nor \(c\) can augment
\(\{b\}\).  Matroid augmentation fails.  Padding each atom with private
dummy stages makes every atom depth three without changing the obstruction.
Ground size three and two shared resources are minimal.

Thus selecting a compatible closed-chain bank is a hypergraph-packing gate,
not the claim gammoid of Theorem 3.1.  Selection must precede atomization.

### Proposition 8.2 (one terminal separator gives rank one)

For every \(m\), take \(m\) jointly materialized, individually live
depth-three atoms with private occurrence prefixes and one gain claim each.
Force every gain route through one common unit-capacity supplier vertex
\(z\).  Then

\[
                         r_{\rm ch}(G)=1.              \tag{8.2}
\]

Indeed, in the vertex-split network take \(W\) to contain the upstream side
and \(z_{\rm in}\), but not \(z_{\rm out}\).  The cut crosses the single
unit split arc of \(z\), so \(t_G(W)=m\) and \(c(W)=1\).
Consequently no fixed \(\eta>0\), \(\gamma=O(1)\) satisfies hereditary
expansion as \(m\to\infty\).  Bounded depth and perfect occurrence privacy
do not imply supplier rank.

### Proposition 8.3 (casualty coefficient is necessary)

Even \(q=0\) and \(p=g\) give no complete-potential surplus if every gain
creates one uncancelled non-supplier casualty, so that
\(c_{\rm ns}=g\).  Hence a complete casualty rate strictly below the gain
yield is necessary for Theorem 7.1's contraction mechanism.

## 9. Weakest exact analytic hypothesis after a bilateral bank is fixed

For one fixed child and already exposed bank, (5.9) is the weakest exact
net-surplus condition at the complete-accounting right-hand side.  For a
fixed \((\eta,\gamma)\) augmentation target, (5.6) is the weakest exact full
augmented claim-network cut:

\[
 \max_W(t_{Q\cup G}(W)-c(W))
       \le(1-\eta)g+\gamma.                            \tag{9.1}
\]

For a fixed master bank which must remain robust under later gain exposure,
the weakest exact anchored version after choosing \(I,G\) and the network is
(6.3).  A sufficient all-dimensional package for this bilateral route
separates into a geometric bank lemma and a proved analytic implication.
Uniform constants are required
over the whole quantified parent class, not selected anew after seeing a
favourable child.

1. an occurrence-labelled source exposure
   \(|V_0|\ge\alpha\Phi-b_0\) with a uniform \(\alpha>0\);
2. an exact state-labelled allocation in one master child, producing
   \(g\ge|V_0|-C_H\), \(C_H=O(1)\), either as bounded terminally closed
   modules or as a completed integral rotor bank; every authenticated source
   role is bilateral;

   effects, with an internally valid protected bank, complete continuation
   holonomy and one joint resource packing;

   anchor losing at most \(\beta=O(1)\);
5. the anchored cut (6.3) with \(\gamma=O(1)\), together with complete
   casualties \(c_{\rm ns}\le\chi g+b_{\rm ns}\);

   \[
    \lambda=\alpha(\eta-\chi),\qquad
    K=(\eta-\chi)(b_0+C_H)+\beta+\gamma+b_{\rm ns},   \tag{9.2}
   \]

   require \(0<\lambda_0\le\lambda\le1\) and
   \(K/\lambda=O(1)\); equivalently one may prove the exact normalized
   surplus (5.9) directly; and
7. the complete potential accounting row (5.3).

they are **UNPROVED** all-\(k\).  After a bank is fixed, the analytically
weakest additional row is the exact normalized surplus (5.9), followed by
the potential row (5.3).  Rows 4--6 are a checkable anchored factorization
of that surplus, not logically necessary if (5.9) is proved directly.
Rows 1--7 give the one-child contraction (7.4); that implication is

opposed-triangle/Haxell theorem is a candidate only for part of rows 2--3:

(7.9).  A terminal additive bound still needs the multiplicative product to
die, the additive convolution to remain bounded, and a valid even
terminal-child transfer.  These regeneration/terminal rows are also
**UNPROVED**.

\[
 \partial P_{x,\phi}=

\[

\]

\[
 \operatorname{Pack}(F),\qquad R_W\cap K\ne\varnothing. \tag{9.5}

Zero named current does not imply (9.5): nonempty history relations can have

 g\ge\alpha\Phi-B_0,qquad

Left-total regeneration, bounded route/reset charge and bounded terminal

Once a valid bank is selected, bilateral arity disappears from the
