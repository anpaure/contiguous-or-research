## 1. Legal slab choice groups

Fix a baseline retained packetization and a family \(\mathcal A\) of
pairwise owner-disjoint compatible \(Q_{R+1}\)-slabs.  For \(a\in\mathcal A\),
shore \(0\) is its old two-packet resolution and shore \(1\) is its new
cross-parent resolution.

At signed depth \(c\), the aggregate target images of either shore are
sets of size \(2s\):

\[
 |\operatorname {supp}\Gamma_{a,c}^0|
 =|\operatorname {supp}\Gamma_{a,c}^1|=2s.           \tag{1.1}
\]

The two packets within a shore have disjoint images, so there is no
within-group collision.  All packets outside the selected slabs are
treated as fixed choice groups.  Let

\[
 L_c^0=B_c+\sum_a\Gamma_{a,c}^0                    \tag{1.2}
\]

be the baseline literal load.

Let \(X_a\in\{0,1\}\) select the new shore.  Then the realized load is

\[
 L_c(X)=L_c^0+\sum_aX_a\Delta_{a,c}.                \tag{1.3}
\]

Every realization has the same total occurrence mass \(G\) at every
signed depth.

The owner-disjointness assumption is essential.  An arbitrary collection
of locally valid slabs need not be simultaneously switchable: two slabs
can share an old packet or owner.  In a general atlas, legal simultaneous
slab sets form a packing system.  Its convex hull is denoted
\(\mathcal Z\).  Parent-label flow balance alone does not describe
\(\mathcal Z\); matching and odd-set inequalities can remain.

## 2. Exact independent fractional objective

For an integral load \(L_c\) of mass \(G\), its factorial collision energy
is

\[
 \mathcal C_c(L_c)
 ={1\over2}\bigl(\|L_c\|_2^2-G\bigr).              \tag{2.1}
\]

Because each slab shore is internally target-rainbow, the total collision
energy may equivalently be counted over unordered pairs of distinct choice
groups.

Choose the disjoint slab bits independently with
\(\Pr(X_a=1)=z_a\).  The contribution of one slab against the old
background is

\[
 d_{a,c}
 =\langle L_c^0-\Gamma_{a,c}^0,\Delta_{a,c}\rangle. \tag{2.2}
\]

For two distinct slabs, changing both creates the cross term

\[
 Q_{ab,c}=\langle\Delta_{a,c},\Delta_{b,c}\rangle.   \tag{2.3}
\]

Summing over the protected signed depths gives (0.3)--(0.4).

### Proposition 2.1 (multi-affinity and whole-slab rounding)

For pairwise disjoint slabs, \(\widetilde{\mathcal C}(z)\) is affine in
each individual \(z_a\).  Hence some integral shore choice
\(\widehat X\in\{0,1\}^{\mathcal A}\) satisfies

\[
 \mathcal C(\widehat X)\le\widetilde{\mathcal C}(z). \tag{2.4}
\]

#### Proof

Equation (0.3) is multi-affine.  Expose the slab bits one at a time and
choose the endpoint having no larger conditional expectation. \(\square\)

Thus independent slab fractionalization introduces no new rounding gap.
It also introduces no convex-flow theorem: the off-diagonal Gram
coefficients \(Q_{ab}\) can have either sign, and the zero-diagonal
quadratic form in (0.3) can be indefinite.

## 3. First moments do not determine covariance

Let \(\mu\) be an arbitrary joint distribution on legal integral slab
sets.  Put

\[
 z_a=\mathbb E_\mu X_a,\qquad
 y_{ab}=\mathbb E_\mu(X_aX_b).                    \tag{3.1}
\]

Taking expectations in the exact pair expansion gives (0.5).  The vector
\(z\) may obey every parent-label conservation law while the pair moments
\(y\) vary over a nontrivial interval.

The same point has a load-variance form.  Put

\[
 \bar L_c=\mathbb E_\mu L_c(X)
          =L_c^0+\sum_az_a\Delta_{a,c}.             \tag{3.2}
\]

Then

\[
 \boxed{
 \mathbb E_\mu\mathcal C_c(L_c)
 =\mathcal C_c(\bar L_c)
   +{1\over2}\mathbb E_\mu\|L_c-\bar L_c\|_2^2.}    \tag{3.3}
\]

#### Proof

Use
\(\mathbb E\|L_c\|_2^2
=\|\mathbb EL_c\|_2^2+\mathbb E\|L_c-\mathbb EL_c\|_2^2\)
in (2.1). \(\square\)

Thus minimizing the energy of the mean load is not the fractional
covariance problem.  The omitted variance term can be macroscopic.

For independent slab bits it is

\[
 \mathbb E\|L_c-\bar L_c\|_2^2
 =\sum_az_a(1-z_a)\|\Delta_{a,c}\|_2^2.             \tag{3.4}
\]

For correlated bits, (3.4) acquires the exact cross terms

\[
 2\sum_{a<b}(y_{ab}-z_az_b)
      \langle\Delta_{a,c},\Delta_{b,c}\rangle.       \tag{3.5}
\]

A scalar circulation records none of (3.5).

## 3A. Exact compound exchanges and overlap holonomy

Fix a current legal state.  For every slab \(t\), choose at most one
alternative resolution \(a\), and abbreviate its all-depth literal
derivative by

\[
 \Delta_t=(\Delta_{t,a,c})_c.                         \tag{3.6}
\]

Remove the current shore of \(t\) and let

\[
 d_{t,c}=\langle B_{t,c},\Delta_{t,c}\rangle,
 \qquad d_t=\sum_cd_{t,c}                            \tag{3.7}
\]

be its exact common all-depth one-slab collision score.  If \(S\) is a
simultaneously legal family of such replacements, define its literal
residue

\[
 R_c(S)=\sum_{t\in S}\Delta_{t,c}.                   \tag{3.8}
\]

### Theorem 3A.1 (compound derivative)

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
 \mathcal C(L(X^S))-\mathcal C(L(X))
 &=\sum_{t\in S}d_t+
   \sum_{\{t,u\}\subseteq S}\sum_c
      \langle\Delta_{t,c},\Delta_{u,c}\rangle,\\
 \Phi(X^S)-\Phi(X)
 &=2\sum_{t\in S}d_t+
   2\sum_{\{t,u\}\subseteq S}\sum_c
      \langle\Delta_{t,c},\Delta_{u,c}\rangle .
\end{aligned}}                                                   \tag{3.9}
\]

#### Proof

For one signed depth,

\[
 \mathcal C_c(L+R)-\mathcal C_c(L)
 =\langle L,R\rangle+{1\over2}\|R\|_2^2.            \tag{3.10}
\]

For one slab at the fixed signed depth,

\[
 d_{t,c}=\langle L_c-\Gamma_{t,c}^0,\Delta_{t,c}\rangle
 =\langle L_c,\Delta_{t,c}\rangle
   +{1\over2}\|\Delta_{t,c}\|_2^2,                  \tag{3.11}
\]

because
\(\langle\Gamma_{t,c}^0,\Delta_{t,c}\rangle
=-\|\Delta_{t,c}\|_2^2/2\).
Expand the square of \(R_c(S)\), sum over \(c\), and use
\(\Phi=2(\mathcal C-\mathcal C_{\min})\). \(\square\)

Define the overlap holonomy of \(S\) by

\[
 \mathscr H(S)
 =\sum_{\{t,u\}\subseteq S}\sum_c
       \langle\Delta_{t,c},\Delta_{u,c}\rangle
 ={1\over2}\sum_c\left[
      \|R_c(S)\|_2^2-\sum_{t\in S}\|\Delta_{t,c}\|_2^2\right].
                                                               \tag{3.12}
\]

The sum of the linear score and the holonomy has the residue-only form

\[
\boxed{
 \sum_{t\in S}d_t+\mathscr H(S)
 =\sum_c\left[
    \langle L_c,R_c(S)\rangle
       +{1\over2}\|R_c(S)\|_2^2\right].}             \tag{3.12a}
\]

Consequently two legal compound exchanges with the same literal residue
\(R(S)\) have exactly the same collision and CPCR derivatives, even if
their one-slab scores and pairwise overlap decompositions differ.  This is
the exact residue invariant of compound slab optimization.  In particular,
a compound exchange with \(R(S)=0\) changes no target load and no CPCR
energy; then its negative holonomy exactly cancels its total one-slab
score.

Thus a current state can satisfy \(d_t\ge0\) for every single slab and
still admit compound descent exactly when

\[
                         \mathscr H(S)<-\sum_{t\in S}d_t.        \tag{3.13}
\]

The abstract \(Q_3\) trap gives the calibrated example.  Four simultaneous
flips have

\[
 \sum_{t\in S}d_t=4L,\qquad
 \mathscr H(S)=-8L,\qquad
 \Delta\mathcal C=-4L.                              \tag{3.14}
\]

Every individual derivative is \(+L\), yet the compound exchange reaches
zero collision energy.  Therefore neither nonnegative one-slab scores nor
large derivative scatter proves CPCR local optimality.

For a general joint law, averaging (3.9) gives exactly the pair-moment
formula (0.5).  The entries \(y_{tu}\) are the fractional weights of the
compound holonomy terms.  They must belong to the correlation polytope of
legal simultaneous exchanges; choosing them independently is not
justified.

## 4. The quota-floor variance identity

Write

\[
 \lambda_c={G\over N_c}=k_c+\alpha_c,
 \qquad k_c=\lfloor\lambda_c\rfloor,\quad
 0\le\alpha_c<1.                                   \tag{4.1}
\]

The integer floor energy is

\[
 \mathcal C_{c,\min}
 =N_c\binom{k_c}{2}+(G-k_cN_c)k_c.                 \tag{4.2}
\]

Every realization in (1.3) has mass \(G\).  Applying the elementary
floor identity to (3.3) gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
 \mathbb E\mathcal C_c-\mathcal C_{c,\min}
 ={1\over2}\Big[
  \|\bar L_c-\lambda_c{\bf1}\|_2^2
  +\mathbb E\|L_c-\bar L_c\|_2^2
  -N_c\alpha_c(1-\alpha_c)\Big].                  \tag{4.3}
\end{aligned}
\]

Summing (4.3) proves (0.10).

For the CPCR functional itself, (4.3) is equivalently

\[
 \boxed{
 \mathbb E\Phi_m
 =\sum_c\Big[
  \|\bar L_c-\lambda_c{\bf1}\|_2^2
  +\mathbb E\|L_c-\bar L_c\|_2^2
  -N_c\alpha_c(1-\alpha_c)\Big].}                  \tag{4.4}
\]

The three terms have distinct meanings.

* \(\|\bar L_c-\lambda_c{\bf1}\|_2^2\) is literal zero-mode imbalance.
* \(\mathbb E\|L_c-\bar L_c\|_2^2\) is cross-configuration covariance.
* \(N_c\alpha_c(1-\alpha_c)\) is the unavoidable variance of a
  floor/ceiling integer load.

Exact floor energy is attained if and only if the joint law is supported
on floor/ceiling load vectors.  Balancing only \(\bar L_c\) is neither
necessary nor sufficient.

## 5. What the explicit direction derivative proves

Let \(\Pi_q^\epsilon\) map a literal target vector to its direction-support
incidence vector.  For a slab which activates \(e\) and freezes \(i\), the
new theorem gives

\[
 \Pi_q^\epsilon\Delta_{a,q}^\epsilon
 =2qg_R(e_e-e_i),\qquad g_R={s\over R}.             \tag{5.1}
\]

Orient the active-axis exchange graph from \(i\) to \(e\), and let \(B\)
be its vertex-edge incidence matrix.  If \(D_q^\epsilon\) is the matrix
whose slab columns are the literal derivatives, then (5.1) is exactly

\[
 \Pi_q^\epsilon D_q^\epsilon=2qg_RB.               \tag{5.2}
\]

### Corollary 5.1 (all-depth support cancellation of a circulation)

If \(B\xi=0\), then

\[
 \Pi_q^\epsilon D_q^\epsilon\xi=0
 \qquad(q\le H,\ \epsilon\in\{-,+\}).               \tag{5.3}
\]

The same circulation cancels every depth because the only depth dependence
in (5.2) is the scalar \(q\).  It also cancels both signs.

This is useful, but it places \(D\xi\) in the literal hard kernel.  It does
not imply \(D\xi=0\).  A proof which replaces the literal columns by
\(e_e-e_i\) loses exactly the target-labelled information needed by
(4.3).

More generally, every compound residue obeys the exact simultaneous-depth
relations

\[
 \sum_T R_q^\epsilon(T)=0,                          \tag{5.3a}
\]

\[
 {1\over q}\Pi_q^-\!R_q^-
 ={1\over q}\Pi_q^+\!R_q^+
 =2g_RB{\bf1}_S,                                    \tag{5.3b}
\]

and, after identifying the common active-axis coordinates,

\[
 {1\over q}\Pi_q^\epsilon R_q^\epsilon
 ={1\over q'}\Pi_{q'}^{\epsilon'}R_{q'}^{\epsilon'}
 \quad(q,q'\le H;\ \epsilon,\epsilon'\in\{-,+\}).   \tag{5.3c}
\]

These are exact residue invariants of the slab catalogue.  For a closed
axis exchange \(B{\bf1}_S=0\), all these projected residues vanish, but
the literal residue \(R(S)\) need not vanish.  It lies in the direct-sum
hard kernel

 \[
 \mathcal K_{\rm lit}
 :=\bigoplus_{q\le H,\epsilon=\pm}\ker\Pi_q^\epsilon \tag{5.3d}
 \]

This hard-kernel residue is the unresolved literal cross-parent data on
which the CPCR holonomy terms depend.

The projection also gives a nontrivial size audit.  Since
\(\Delta(T)\in\{-1,0,1\}\), the positive direction change at \(e\) forces
at least \(2qg_R\) positive entries of \(\Delta\), and the negative change
at \(i\) forces at least \(2qg_R\) negative entries.  Therefore

\[
 \boxed{
 \|\Delta_{a,q}^\epsilon\|_2^2
 =\|\Delta_{a,q}^\epsilon\|_1
 \ge {4qs\over R}.}                                 \tag{5.4}
\]

For independent fractional slab bits, (3.4) and (5.4) imply

\[
 \sum_{\epsilon=\pm}\sum_{q\le H}
 \mathbb E\|L_q^\epsilon-\bar L_q^\epsilon\|_2^2
 \ge {4sH(H+1)\over R}\sum_a z_a(1-z_a).            \tag{5.5}
\]

Thus a mean-only circulation calculation omits at least the right-hand
side.  If a positive density of an owner-spanning slab family is mixed
nontrivially, the omitted term is of order

\[
                         {WH^2\over R}.              \tag{5.6}
\]

At the diverse-order scale \(R\asymp\sqrt{mH}\), this is not \(o(W)\)
when \(H\not=o(m^{1/3})\).  This does not by itself rule out a correctly
correlated floor law—the lattice term in (4.3) must also be counted—but
it rules out evaluating a diffuse trade circulation only at its mean
load at the required \(o(W)\) accuracy.

## 6. Exact circulation range and dual

Put all signed depths into the direct-sum target space

\[
 \mathcal V=\bigoplus_{q\le H,\epsilon=\pm}
                   \mathbb R^{\mathcal T_q^\epsilon}.           \tag{6.1}
\]

Let

\[
 D:\mathbb R^{\mathcal A}\longrightarrow\mathcal V,
 \qquad
 D\xi=\left(\sum_a\xi_a\Delta_{a,c}\right)_c.        \tag{6.2}
\]

The formal uncapacitated circulation space is \(\ker B\).

### Theorem 6.1 (literal Hodge alternative)

For \(r\in\mathcal V\), the following are equivalent:

1. there is \(\xi\in\ker B\) with \(D\xi=r\);
2. for every \(y\in\mathcal V\),

   \[
   D^Ty\in\operatorname {im}B^T
   \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
   \langle y,r\rangle=0.                           \tag{6.3}
   \]

#### Proof

The attainable linear space is \(D(\ker B)\).  Its orthogonal complement
is

\[
\begin{aligned}
 D(\ker B)^\perp
 &=\{y:\langle D^Ty,\xi\rangle=0\text{ for all }\xi\in\ker B\}\\
 &=\{y:D^Ty\in(\ker B)^\perp\}\\
 &=\{y:D^Ty\in\operatorname {im}B^T\}.
\end{aligned}                                       \tag{6.4}
\]

Membership in a finite-dimensional linear subspace is equivalent to
annihilating its orthogonal complement. \(\square\)

For example, every direction-support potential pulls back through (5.2)
to a boundary potential \(B^T\phi\).  It therefore gives a necessary
compatibility condition on \(r\).  The common kernel may contain further
dual potentials not visible in the direction census.

For the actual fractional trade polytope \(\mathcal Z\), no linear-space
claim is justified.  The exact separation theorem is instead

\[
 r\in D\mathcal Z
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \langle y,r\rangle\le
 \max_{z\in\mathcal Z}\langle D^Ty,z\rangle
 \quad\text{for every }y,                          \tag{6.5}
\]

which is (0.9).  Determining the support function on the right includes
the slab packing constraints.  It is not supplied by the parent incidence
matrix \(B\).

## 8. Verdict on fractional slab circulation

The following statements are now proved.

* Cross-parent slab trades are legal whole-owner moves and their exact
  literal columns may be used in the covariance program.
* Independent mixing has the exact multi-affine objective (0.3) and can
  be rounded whole-slab without loss.
* General correlated mixing requires the pair moments in (0.5).
* Single-slab local descent can fail at linear CPCR energy; compound
  descent is governed exactly by the holonomy term in (3.9).
* A parent/axis circulation cancels the simultaneous-depth direction
  projection exactly, but leaves an uncontrolled literal kernel vector.
* The exact mean-reachability obstruction is (0.8)/(0.9).
* The exact floor-energy obstruction is the variance ledger (0.10).

What is not proved is a circulation satisfying those two tests.  In
particular, the local bound
\(\|\Delta_{a,c}\|_1\le4s\) supplies neither a sign for \(d_a\), useful
control of \(Q_{ab}\), nor cancellation of the literal kernel on closed
cycles.

The Hamming-two local-minimum theorem sharpens this boundary.  At the
level of the presently proved slab axioms there are strict one-slab local
minima with \(\Phi=\Omega(W)\), although a four-slab correlated exchange
reaches zero.  Hence a proof of CPCR must either:

* construct compound exchanges with sufficiently negative
  \(\mathscr H(S)\);
* prove that literal compiler chronology excludes positive-energy states
  satisfying every compound inequality (3.13) in a sufficiently rich
  exchange family; or
* solve the legal pair-moment/correlation-polytope problem in (0.5).
