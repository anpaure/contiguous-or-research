# Paired receiver squares: exact selector--extension fusion and the even-level TU theorem

**Date:** 2026-08-05  
**Method:** literal double-expansion squares, one ordinary augmented
matching, Hall/Tutte, and parity; no computation  
**Status:** unconditional reduction and an exact even-cut-level theorem.
For a fixed paired-petal bank, quotient receiver selection, endpoint
disjointness, next-level matching extension, the incoming singleton, and
the two reset colours are one matching problem.  At even cut length this
problem is bipartite and therefore has an exact Hall/TU formulation.  At
odd cut length the exact obstruction is a Tutte blossom cut; it is not in
general an ordinary Hall problem.  The required Hall/Tutte inequalities
for every PBBS bank are not proved here.

## 1. Every two-petal menu is a complete bipartite square

Use the literal cut notation of the paired hub-fan theorem.  Let two
endpoint-disjoint shifted-cut petals over the same hub `H` have cut pairs

\[
                         \{p,p+1\},\qquad \{r,r+1\}.
\tag{1.1}
\]

Joint admissibility says that all four double expansions

\[
 z_{\alpha\beta}
   =x_{H\cup\{p+\alpha,r+\beta\}},
 \qquad \alpha,\beta\in\{0,1\},
\tag{1.2}
\]

exist.  Their legal horizontal edges are

\[
\begin{aligned}
 z_{0\beta}z_{1\beta}&\quad(\beta=0,1),\\
 z_{\alpha0}z_{\alpha1}&\quad(\alpha=0,1).
\end{aligned}
\tag{1.3}
\]

Put

\[
 A=\{z_{00},z_{11}\},\qquad
 B=\{z_{10},z_{01}\}.
\tag{1.4}
\]

### Lemma 1.1 (receiver rectangle)

The four possible adjacent receiver pairs are exactly the complete
bipartite graph

\[
                              A\mathbin\square B=K_{2,2}.
\tag{1.5}
\]

After passing to necklace orbits and deleting any already occupied
receiver vertices, let `A_j,B_j` be the sets of surviving orbit images of
the two shores in (1.4).  Repetitions inside either set are coalesced.
Then every two *distinct* orbits

\[
                         a\in A_j,\qquad b\in B_j
\tag{1.6}
\]

are adjacent.  Hence the surviving job menu is still the complete
rectangle on `A_j,B_j`, with the diagonal omitted only when the two orbit
sets overlap.

#### Proof

Two vertices in (1.2) are adjacent exactly when one of `alpha,beta`
changes.  Every pair with one endpoint in (1.4)'s `A` and one in its `B`
differs in exactly one coordinate.  This proves (1.5).

For the quotient statement, choose the displayed representatives in
(1.2).  Every cross-pair of distinct orbit images has adjacent
representatives, so it is an edge of the simple orbit graph.  If two
images coincide, their projected edge is a loop and is correctly omitted.
Deleting vertices merely deletes elements from the two shore sets and
cannot destroy any cross-edge between the survivors.  \(\square\)

The point of Lemma 1.1 is that a job does **not** choose an indivisible
edge-object.  It independently chooses one endpoint from each of two
two-element lists; adjacency between the chosen distinct endpoints is
automatic.

There is also exact literal separation inside one fan.

### Lemma 1.2 (same-hub literal squares are disjoint)

Fix one literal hub `H`, let its shifted petals be pairwise
endpoint-disjoint, and partition some of those petals into disjoint
passive pairs.  Before taking rotations, the four-vertex receiver squares
belonging to distinct passive pairs are vertex-disjoint.

#### Proof

The two possible inserted cuts belonging to distinct endpoint-disjoint
petals are disjoint.  A vertex of one receiver square is `H` together
with one inserted cut from each of that pair's two petals.  Equality with
a vertex from another square would identify the unordered pair of added
cuts, and therefore the unordered pair of parent petals.  The passive
pairs were distinct.  \(\square\)

Thus all receiver collisions occur either between different literal hubs
or when rotations coalesce distinct pointed states.  The augmented graph
below prices both phenomena; no intra-fan pointed collision is hidden.

## 2. Reset-colour inheritance

The deleted-cut hub colours of the four square edges are visible without
calculation:

\[
\begin{aligned}
 \operatorname{hub}(z_{0\beta}z_{1\beta})
   &=x_{H\cup\{r+\beta\}},\\
 \operatorname{hub}(z_{\alpha0}z_{\alpha1})
   &=x_{H\cup\{p+\alpha\}}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2.1}
\]

### Lemma 2.1 (parent-endpoint colour inheritance)

Every receiver-square edge has as its hub colour one of the four critical
endpoints of its two parent petals.

Consequently, if the ordinary lower-level selector contains none of the
critical vertices whose orbit colours are contracted by the special
reset, no edge in any receiver square generated by that selector has a
contracted reset colour.

#### Proof

Deleting the moved cut `p+alpha` from the first line of (1.3) leaves
`H union {r+beta}`.  Deleting `r+beta` from the second line leaves
`H union {p+alpha}`.  These are exactly the endpoints of the other and
the first parent petal, respectively.  This proves (2.1) and the first
claim.

The special reset removes its occupied ordinary fibres before the
ordinary selector is formed.  In particular its two contracted hub
vertices cannot be parent-petal endpoints.  Equation (2.1) then excludes
both colours from every generated receiver square.  \(\square\)

This inheritance is load-bearing.  Arbitrarily deleting edge colours
from a `K_(2,2)` menu need not leave a rectangle.  Here the two reset
colours delete edges from the *background* next-level graph, while the
generated job rectangles avoid those colours automatically.  Reset-used
receiver vertices may still be deleted; by Lemma 1.1 that operation
preserves rectangularity.

## 3. The augmented selector graph

Fix one cut level after the special-reset fibres have been removed.  Let
`G` be the disjoint union of the remaining ordinary capacity-two necklace
sectors at that level.  Delete from `G`

1. every vertex already occupied by a lower-level receiver or by the
   special reset, except for the vertices belonging to the paired bank
   currently being installed; and
2. every edge whose deleted-cut hub colour is one of the two colours
   contracted by the special reset.

Call the resulting graph `G^circ`.  Let `J` be a fixed bank of paired
passive-petal jobs.  For `j in J`, let `A_j,B_j` be its surviving quotient
shore sets from Lemma 1.1.  By Lemma 2.1 every distinct cross-pair in
`A_j times B_j` is an allowed edge of `G^circ`.

Let `I` be the incoming singleton set; throughout this note

\[
                              |I|\le1.
\tag{3.1}
\]

Delete `I` from `G^circ` and from every job list.  For each job `j`, add
two private vertices

\[
                              \alpha_j,\ \beta_j,
\tag{3.2}
\]

and the edges

\[
 \alpha_j a\quad(a\in A_j),
 \qquad
 \beta_j b\quad(b\in B_j).
\tag{3.3}
\]

No edges join private vertices to one another.

Let `O` be the set of physically allowable outgoing singleton sockets,
including the retained-anchor condition in the exceptional fan state.
Put

\[
 n_0=|V(G^\circ)\setminus I|+2|J|,
 \qquad
 \varepsilon=n_0\pmod2.
\tag{3.4}
\]

If `epsilon=1`, add one private socket vertex `omega` and the edges

\[
                              \omega t\qquad(t\in O).
\tag{3.5}
\]

If `epsilon=0`, add no socket vertex.  Denote the final augmented graph by

\[
                              \widehat G(J,I,O).
\tag{3.6}
\]

### Theorem 3.1 (exact selector--extension fusion)

The following are equivalent.

1. For every job `j` one can choose an edge

   \[
                              e_j=a_jb_j,
   \qquad a_j\in A_j,\ b_j\in B_j,
   \tag{3.7}
   \]

   so that the `e_j` are pairwise vertex-disjoint, avoid `I`, and, after
   all their endpoints and `I` are deleted, `G^circ` has a matching of
   every remaining vertex except exactly one freely chosen vertex
   `t in O` when `epsilon=1`, and except none when `epsilon=0`.

2. The augmented graph `widehat G(J,I,O)` has a perfect matching.

Under either condition, replacing the two private edges incident with
`alpha_j,beta_j` by `a_jb_j` for every job produces a parity-optimal
next-level critical matching which contains every prescribed receiver
pair abstractly, respects the two reset colours, and routes at most one
singleton upward.

#### Proof

Suppose first that `widehat G` has a perfect matching `M`.  Each private
vertex `alpha_j` has to be matched to a unique vertex `a_j in A_j`, and
each `beta_j` to a unique `b_j in B_j`.  A matching cannot use one
ordinary vertex twice, so all selected endpoints are distinct.  In
particular `a_j != b_j`; Lemma 1.1 gives the legal receiver edge
`a_jb_j`.

All ordinary vertices not selected by the job terminals are matched by
ordinary edges of `G^circ`, except that when `epsilon=1` the vertex
matched to `omega` is the unique outgoing socket `t in O`.  No ordinary
edge has a reset colour by the definition of `G^circ`, and no selected
job edge has one by Lemma 2.1.  Replacing the two terminal edges for each
job with `a_jb_j` gives the matching in item 1.

Conversely, start with the data in item 1.  Replace every selected job edge
`a_jb_j` by `alpha_j a_j` and `beta_j b_j`.  Retain the residual matching
of `G^circ`.  If `epsilon=1`, use `omega t`; if `epsilon=0`, there is no
socket vertex.  These edges cover every vertex of `widehat G` exactly
once, giving a perfect matching.  \(\square\)

Thus quotient coalescence, receiver endpoint collisions, prescribed-edge
extension, and singleton routing are not four separate gates.  They are
one ordinary matching problem.

## 4. Even cut length: Hall and total unimodularity are exact

Suppose the capacity-two sector has cyclic coordinate length `ell`, fixed
odd digit mass `R`, and `ell` is even.  On the labelled token graph put

\[
                         \chi(t)=\sum_{i=0}^{\ell-1} i t_i\pmod2.
\tag{4.1}
\]

Every legal adjacent transfer toggles `chi`.  This includes the wrap edge,
because `ell-1` is odd.

Let `H<=C_ell` be the rotational stabilizer of the fixed background.  Its
order is odd.  Every shift belonging to `H` is even: if a shift by `s`
has odd order, then

\[
                         {\ell\over\gcd(\ell,s)}
\tag{4.2}
\]

is odd, so `gcd(ell,s)`, and hence `s`, contains the full factor two of
`ell`.  Rotation by such an `s` changes (4.1) by `sR=0 mod 2`.
Therefore `chi` descends to the necklace quotient.

### Theorem 4.1 (even-level TU extension theorem)

At every even cut length, the graph before `omega` is bipartite.  If
`epsilon=1`, a one-socket extension is possible only when its two shore
sizes differ by one; put `omega` on the smaller shore and retain in `O`
only candidates on the larger shore.  (If the imbalance is not one, the
gate is immediately infeasible.)  With that convention
`widehat G(J,I,O)` is bipartite.  Hence the protected paired-receiver
extension exists if and only if

\[
                         |N_{\widehat G}(X)|\ge |X|
\tag{4.3}
\]

for every subset `X` of either fixed shore of `widehat G`.

Equivalently, the natural fractional system on ordinary matching edges,
job-to-endpoint edges, and the possible socket edges is integral: its
constraint matrix is the bipartite vertex--edge incidence matrix and is
totally unimodular.

#### Proof

The preceding parity calculation makes every ordinary sector quotient
bipartite.  In a receiver square,

\[
 \chi(z_{\alpha\beta})
   =\chi(z_{00})+\alpha+\beta\pmod2,
\tag{4.4}
\]

so `A_j` lies on one shore and `B_j` on the other.  Add `alpha_j` to the
shore opposite `A_j` and `beta_j` to the shore opposite `B_j`.  Deleting
vertices or reset-colour edges preserves bipartiteness.  If parity calls
for `omega`, a perfect matching requires equal final shore sizes.  Thus
the pre-`omega` imbalance must be one, `omega` belongs to the smaller
shore, and its possible mates lie on the larger shore.  Candidates on the
other shore cannot be the unique outgoing socket of a perfect completion
and are omitted.

The graph in (3.6) is therefore bipartite.  Hall's theorem proves (4.3).
The ordinary oriented incidence matrix of a bipartite graph is a network
matrix up to row signs and is totally unimodular, proving the fractional
statement.  \(\square\)

This is stronger than testing an SDR of receiver-edge objects and then
asking whether those edges extend.  Equation (4.3) prices both operations
simultaneously and has no post-rounding extension gap.

There is an equivalent router form whenever a background matching is
already known.

### Theorem 4.2 (one-commodity alternating router)

Let `B` be the even-level graph obtained from `G^circ-I`, with `omega`
and its allowable socket edges included when `epsilon=1`, but before the
`2|J|` job terminals are added.  Suppose `B` has a fixed perfect matching
`M_0`.

Choose the global bipartition `L,R`.  Exactly one private terminal of
each job belongs to `L` and the other belongs to `R`; call the resulting
terminal sets `S_J,T_J`.

Form a directed graph `D_(M_0)` by

1. orienting every edge of `B-M_0` from `L` to `R`;
2. orienting every edge of `M_0` from `R` to `L`;
3. orienting every job edge incident with `S_J` away from its terminal;
   and
4. orienting every job edge incident with `T_J` toward its terminal.

Give every ordinary vertex capacity one.  Then

\[
 \widehat G(J,I,O)\text{ has a perfect matching}
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 D_{M_0}\text{ has }|J|\text{ vertex-disjoint directed paths from }
 S_J\text{ onto }T_J.
\tag{4.5}
\]

Hence, conditional only on the displayed base matching, the full
even-level protected extension is one ordinary integral max-flow.  Its
exact obstruction is one minimum vertex cut of value less than `|J|`.

#### Proof

Let `M` be a perfect matching of `widehat G`.  In the symmetric difference
`M triangle M_0`, every ordinary vertex has degree zero or two, while each
job terminal has degree one.  Thus the symmetric difference is a union of
alternating cycles and `|J|` vertex-disjoint alternating paths whose
endpoints are the job terminals.  Bipartiteness forces every such path to
join one terminal in `S_J` to one in `T_J`.  With the orientations above,
all these paths are directed.  Discarding the alternating cycles gives the
right side of (4.5).

Conversely, flip `M_0` along any saturating family of directed
vertex-disjoint paths.  Each path starts and ends with a nonmatching job
edge and alternates thereafter, so the flip matches both job terminals
and rematches every ordinary vertex on the path.  Vertex disjointness
makes the simultaneous flips compatible.  Vertices outside the paths
retain their `M_0` edge.  The result is a perfect matching of `widehat G`.
Integral vertex splitting converts the linkage to one ordinary max-flow.
\(\square\)

The base-matching premise in Theorem 4.2 is a convenience, not an extra
condition in Theorem 4.1.  Job terminals can occasionally repair a base
which was not perfectly matchable by itself; in that case Hall (4.3),
rather than the relative router, remains the exact statement.

The Hall family itself has a useful closed form.  Orient the bipartition
so that `A_j subseteq L` and `B_j subseteq R` for every job; if necessary,
exchange the names `A_j,B_j` independently before adding the private
terminals.  Put

\[
 A(U)=\{j\in J:A_j\cap U\ne\varnothing\},
 \qquad
 B(K)=\bigcup_{j\in K}B_j.
\tag{4.6}
\]

Define `B(V)` and `A(K)` symmetrically on the other shore.

### Corollary 4.3 (exact two-shore cut formula)

When no socket vertex is present, (4.3) is equivalent to the two families

\[
 |N_{G^\circ-I}(U)\cup B(K)|+|A(U)|
      \ge |U|+|K|
 \quad(U\subseteq L,\ K\subseteq J),
\tag{4.7}
\]

and

\[
 |N_{G^\circ-I}(V)\cup A(K)|+|B(V)|
      \ge |V|+|K|
 \quad(V\subseteq R,\ K\subseteq J).
\tag{4.8}
\]

If `omega` lies on the left shore, (4.7) receives one additional optional
terminal whose list is `O`; equivalently allow a bit `delta in {0,1}` and
replace `B(K)` by `B(K) union O` and the right side by `|U|+|K|+1` when
`delta=1`.  The right-shore case is symmetric.

#### Proof

An arbitrary subset of the left augmented shore has the form

\[
                         U\cup\{\beta_j:j\in K\}.
\]

Its ordinary right neighbours are exactly
`N_(G^circ-I)(U) union B(K)`.  Its private right neighbours are the
vertices `alpha_j` for exactly the jobs in `A(U)`.  These two kinds of
vertices are disjoint, giving (4.7).  Every left-shore subset has this
form.  The other shore gives (4.8), and adding `omega` contributes its
list and one unit to the chosen subset exactly as stated.  \(\square\)

Equations (4.7)--(4.8) are the precise quotient-coalesced all-cut rows.
They show where ordinary sector expansion and the two receiver endpoint
lists interact; separate Hall tests on the jobs and on `G^circ` do not
imply them.

For a set `D subseteq R`, define the residual endpoint-list deficiency

\[
 \delta_B(D)=
   \max_{K\subseteq J}
      \left(|K|-\left|\bigcup_{j\in K}(B_j\setminus D)\right|\right).
\tag{4.9}
\]

Then (4.7) for every `K` is equivalently the single inequality

\[
 |N_{G^\circ-I}(U)|+|A(U)|
   -\delta_B\bigl(N_{G^\circ-I}(U)\bigr)
 \ge |U|
 \qquad(U\subseteq L).
\tag{4.10}
\]

There is a symmetric formula with `A,B` exchanged.  This follows from

\[
 |D\cup B(K)|=|D|+|B(K)\setminus D|.
\]

Thus even the exact two-shore Hall family separates into ordinary sector
expansion minus one ordinary transversal deficiency of two-element lists.
No higher-dimensional receiver hypergraph remains at even levels.

Because every list has size at most two, that transversal deficiency has
an exact graphic formula.  Build the endpoint multigraph `Q_B(D)` as
follows.

* Its vertices are the surviving receiver options in
  `bigcup_j(B_j setminus D)`.
* A job with two surviving options is one edge joining them.
* A job with one surviving option is a loop at that option.
* A job with no surviving option is a one-edge component with zero
  vertices.

Parallel edges and loops retain their job labels.

### Lemma 4.4 (bicircular surplus formula)

For every `D subseteq R`,

\[
 \boxed{
 \delta_B(D)=
   \sum_{C\in\operatorname{Comp}(Q_B(D))}
       \bigl(|E(C)|-|V(C)|\bigr)_+ .}
\tag{4.11}
\]

Thus the independent job families are exactly the bicircular-matroid
independent sets: every connected component contains at most one cycle,
where a loop is a cycle.

#### Proof

Matching a job to one surviving endpoint is the same as orienting its
multigraph edge toward a distinct incident vertex; a loop has its unique
possible head.  In a connected component with `e` job edges and `v`
endpoint vertices, at most `v` jobs can be assigned.

If `e<=v`, the connected component is a tree or unicyclic after redundant
edges are omitted.  Orient a tree away from an arbitrary root, assigning
each edge to its child; in the unicyclic case orient the unique cycle
cyclically and the attached trees away from the cycle.  This assigns every
retained edge to a distinct vertex.  If `e>v`, retain a connected
unicyclic spanning subgraph with `v` edges and apply the same orientation;
exactly `v` jobs are assignable.  The zero-vertex convention handles an
empty list.

Hence the maximum number of assignable jobs in a component is
`min(e,v)`, and its deficiency is `(e-v)_+`.  Components are independent,
so the deficiencies add.  By Hall's deficiency formula this sum is
exactly (4.9).  \(\square\)

Combining (4.10) and (4.11), the left-shore obstruction is precisely

\[
 |N_G(U)|+|A(U)|
 -\sum_C\bigl(|E(C)|-|V(C)|\bigr)_+
 <|U|,
\tag{4.12}
\]

with `C` ranging over the endpoint components remaining outside
`N_G(U)`; the other shore is symmetric.  This is the promised
capacity-two form of the quotient-coalesced Hall gate.

For the actual receiver rectangles there is an exact cancellation.  Put

\[
                         D=N_{G^\circ-I}(U),
 \qquad                  J_U=J\setminus A(U).
\tag{4.13}
\]

If `j in A(U)`, some `a in A_j` belongs to `U`; rectangle completeness
gives

\[
                         B_j\subseteq D.
\tag{4.14}
\]

Thus that job has an empty residual `B`-list and contributes exactly one
zero-vertex component to (4.11).  Define

\[
 \delta_B^{\rm inv}(D;U)=
   \max_{K\subseteq J_U}
      \left(|K|-\left|\bigcup_{j\in K}(B_j\setminus D)\right|\right).
\tag{4.15}
\]

### Corollary 4.5 (invisible-job cancellation)

The complete left-shore Hall family is equivalent to

\[
 \boxed{
 |N_{G^\circ-I}(U)|-|U|
   \ge \delta_B^{\rm inv}
          \bigl(N_{G^\circ-I}(U);U\bigr)
 \qquad(U\subseteq L).}
\tag{4.16}
\]

The right-shore family is the symmetric inequality with `A,B` exchanged.

#### Proof

Every job in `A(U)` has empty residual list by (4.14), so each contributes
one independent unit to the Hall deficiency.  Removing those jobs leaves
exactly the optimization (4.15).  Hence

\[
 \delta_B(D)=|A(U)|+\delta_B^{\rm inv}(D;U).
\]

Substitution in (4.10) gives (4.16).  \(\square\)

In the bicircular picture, only jobs whose `A`-lists completely avoid `U`
remain.  A positive term on the right of (4.16) requires either an empty
list among those invisible jobs or a connected endpoint component with
at least two independent cycles.  Trees and unicyclic components have
zero surplus.  Thus the actual even-level theorem has the sharply reduced
form:

> every hub-proper invisible empty list or bicyclic endpoint core is paid
> by one unit of ordinary Hall slack `|N_G(U)|-|U|`.

## 5. Odd cut length: the exact object is a blossom system

When `ell` is odd, the wrap transfer in (4.1) preserves rather than
reverses parity.  The labelled capacity-one face

\[
                         \mathcal T_{\ell,1}=C_\ell
\tag{5.1}
\]

already contains an odd cycle.  Neither the sector nor its augmented graph
is generally bipartite.

### Theorem 5.1 (odd-level Tutte reduction)

For odd cut length, the protected paired-receiver extension exists if and
only if

\[
 \operatorname{odd}\bigl(\widehat G(J,I,O)-S\bigr)\le |S|
 \qquad
 \text{for every }S\subseteq V(\widehat G(J,I,O)),
\tag{5.2}
\]

where `odd(K)` denotes the number of odd-order connected components of
`K`.

Equivalently, the exact fractional relaxation is Edmonds' perfect-matching
polytope with its odd-set inequalities.  It is integral/TDI, but in
general it is not the feasible region of the unsigned network matrix used
at even levels.

#### Proof

Theorem 3.1 reduces the desired construction to a perfect matching of
`widehat G`.  Tutte's one-factor theorem gives exactly (5.2).  Edmonds'
matching-polytope theorem gives the equivalent integral formulation.  The
odd cycle (5.1) rules out a uniform bipartite/TU argument on the original
edge variables.  \(\square\)

In delta-matroid language, the allowable boundary deletions are the
feasible sets of the matching delta-matroid of the augmented sector.
The receiver operation is therefore naturally a delta-matroid transition,
not an ordinary matroid extension.  The strict increase in cut count from
the paired-fan theorem makes these transitions acyclic across levels, but
does not make the within-level blossom inequalities disappear.

## 6. Exact counterexamples to weaker formulations

### 6.1 An SDR of edge-orbits does not price endpoint capacity

Let three jobs all have the four edges of one `C_4=K_(2,2)` as their
receiver menu.  The bipartite graph from jobs to edge-objects satisfies
Hall: every nonempty job set has four neighbours, so three jobs have an
edge-object SDR.  But `C_4` has matching number two, and no three selected
receiver pairs can be vertex-disjoint.

Thus the right side of the selector cannot consist merely of distinct
receiver-edge orbits.  The two private job terminals in Section 3 are
necessary even before next-level extension is considered.

### 6.2 A vertex-disjoint bank need not extend with one socket

The capacity-one face (5.1) gives an exact obstruction.  Let `P` be a
matching of prescribed edges in an odd cycle `C_(2m+1)`, and let `I` be
an incoming singleton set of size at most one, disjoint from `V(P)`.
The components of

\[
                         C_{2m+1}-V(P)-I
\tag{6.1}
\]

are paths.  Its matching deficiency is exactly the number of odd-order
path components.  Therefore `P` extends while exporting at most one
socket if and only if (6.1) has at most one odd component.

For example, in `C_9` the three prescribed edges

\[
                         01,\quad34,\quad67
\tag{6.2}
\]

leave the three isolated vertices `2,5,8`.  They are pairwise
vertex-disjoint, but every completion has deficiency three.  In `C_5`,
the prescribed edge `01` together with incoming singleton `3` leaves the
two isolated vertices `2,4`, so it cannot route that singleton with
bounded state zero or one.

These examples disprove arbitrary prescribed-bank extension.  They do
not assert that the particular bad banks are generated by the PBBS
pairing rule; they prove that a structural theorem about the generated
banks is indispensable.

### 6.3 Reset-colour pruning must use inheritance

Deleting two opposite edges from a `K_(2,2)` leaves two disjoint edges,
which are not one complete rectangle `A' times B'`.  A two-terminal
factorization that ignored this would introduce the two forbidden cross
choices.  Lemma 2.1 is therefore exactly what permits the unpruned
rectangle model in the special-reset application.

### 6.4 No all-level unsigned TU matrix

The unsigned vertex--edge incidence matrix of the triangle
`T_(3,1)=C_3` has determinant two.  Hence the even-level network matrix
cannot extend unchanged to odd cut length.  Odd-set/blossom constraints
are a genuine feature, not a technical weakness of the proof.

## 7. Consequence for the upward parity-flow programme

Fix the passive-petal pairing at each hub fan and process cut levels in
increasing order.  The paired-fan theorem supplies the job bank `J` and
at most one incoming socket.  Lemmas 1.1 and 2.1 supply its quotient
rectangles and protect the two special-reset colours.  Theorem 3.1 then
does all of the following in one step:

1. chooses one of the four pointed receiver pairs for every passive pair;
2. coalesces rotations honestly;
3. prevents receiver-vertex collisions;
4. extends all chosen pairs to the next-level critical matching;
5. avoids both reset hub colours; and
6. chooses the unique outgoing socket when parity requires one.

At an even cut level, the remaining assertion is precisely the Hall family
(4.3), and any fractional flow satisfying it rounds integrally.  At an odd
cut level, the remaining assertion is precisely the blossom family (5.2).
The cut-count potential is strict, so a successful matching at one level
exports data only to the next level and never invalidates an earlier
choice.

This replaces the former pair

\[
 \text{``receiver-edge SDR''}
 \quad+\quad
 \text{``post-hoc prescribed-edge extension''}
\]

by one proof-safe augmented graph.  It does **not** prove that (4.3) or

sharp:

> **Protected augmented-cut theorem.**  For every parity-flow job bank

> `widehat G(J,I,O)` satisfies Hall at even cut length and Tutte at odd cut

Proving this statement, or proving a stronger structural property of the
generated banks which implies its cuts, completes the protected
capacity-two receiver extension row.  No separate quotient SDR,
endpoint-packing lemma, or matching-extension theorem would remain.

## 8. Scope

Proved:

1. every paired-petal receiver menu is a quotient-stable complete
   bipartite square;
2. every receiver-edge hub colour is inherited from a parent endpoint;
3. exact fusion of quotient selection, vertex capacity, extension, reset
   avoidance, and one-socket routing into one ordinary augmented matching;
4. exact Hall/TU formulation at every even cut length;
5. exact Tutte/blossom formulation at every odd cut length; and
6. explicit counterexamples to edge-SDR, arbitrary prescribed-bank, and
   all-level TU shortcuts.

Not proved:

1. Hall (4.3) for every generated even-level bank;
2. Tutte (5.2) for every generated odd-level bank;
3. a canonical passive-petal pairing which forces those inequalities;
4. collision-free PBBS halo planting; or
5. the complete adjacent-necklace theorem or any universal-word bound.
