# PBBS two-row TP2 gives one crossing, but neither one-defect uncrossing nor a uniformly bounded Apéry shoulder

**Date:** 2026-08-07  
**Status:** unconditional total-positivity theorem and exact scope
obstruction.  The parent/child ballot kernel is strictly TP2 and therefore
gives the one-sign-change property of every volume-normalized PBBS tail.
That TP2 statement does not survive as a one-defect reduction through the
min-plus closure.  In particular, there are valid two-denomination
covering prices whose finite Apéry shoulder has \(h-2\) nonzero periods
and remains of leading size when \(h\asymp D\).  No all-depth positivity
or counterexample is claimed.

## 1. The exact Farkas price

Fix a minimum-density denomination \(h\).  After subtracting its linear
slope, the uncompressed cyclic transport LP has Farkas dual

\[
 \sum_{i=1}^{h-1}s_i y_i
 \ge
 \sum_Ld_L
 \min_{x\in\mathcal C_L}x\cdot y
 \qquad(y\ge0),
\tag{1.1}
\]

where

\[
 \mathcal C_L=
 \left\{x\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{h-1}:
 \sum_{i=1}^{h-1}ix_i\le L,\quad
 \sum_{i=1}^{h-1}ix_i\equiv L\pmod h\right\}.
\tag{1.2}
\]

The minimum in (1.1) is a min-plus covering closure.  Thus the proposed
TP2 strategy requires more than a sign theorem for the coefficient row:
it requires an extremal theorem for the nonlinear map

\[
 y\longmapsto
 \left(\min_{x\in\mathcal C_L}x\cdot y\right)_L.
\tag{1.3}
\]

The next section identifies exactly what total positivity supplies before
this nonlinear step.

## 2. Strict TP2 of the ballot parent/child rows

For \(n\ge2\), put

\[
 H_s^{(n)}={n\choose s}-{n\choose s-1}.
\tag{2.1}
\]

The exact two-row ratio is

\[
\boxed{
 {H_s^{(n)}\over H_{s-1}^{(n-2)}}
 ={n(n-1)\over s(n+1-s)}=:R_s.}
\tag{2.2}
\]

On \(1\le s<(n+1)/2\), the denominator

\[
 s(n+1-s)
\tag{2.3}
\]

is strictly increasing, because its increment is \(n-2s>0\).
Consequently \(R_s\) is strictly decreasing.  Hence, whenever \(s<t\)
are in this range,

\[
\boxed{
 H_s^{(n)}H_{t-1}^{(n-2)}
 -H_t^{(n)}H_{s-1}^{(n-2)}>0.}
\tag{2.4}
\]

This is strict TP2 for the two ballot rows.

### Corollary 2.1 (one crossing)

For every \(\lambda\ge0\), the signed sequence

\[
 H_s^{(n)}-\lambda H_{s-1}^{(n-2)}
 =H_{s-1}^{(n-2)}(R_s-\lambda)
\tag{2.5}
\]

changes sign at most once as \(s\) increases through the positive ballot
range.

For the terminal PBBS volume seed,

\[
 \lambda={V_{r,D}\over V_{r-1,D}},
\tag{2.6}
\]

and the negative-length coefficient is, up to the positive factor
\(V_{r-1,D}\), exactly (2.5).  Thus strict TP2 proves the tail sign used
in the depth-five-through-twelve theorems.  This is a complete
variation-diminishing statement for the raw two-row coefficient measure.

It does not classify the minimizers in (1.3).  The minimum of many linear
configuration costs is concave and piecewise linear in \(y\), not a
positive linear transform to which (2.4) can be applied again.

## 3. A cone-level one-defect uncrossing is impossible

The inverse-clock representation of a closed price gives a nondecreasing
superadditive clock.  Its Stieltjes counting measure lies in the
anchored-window cone.  Every strict counting clock is an extreme ray of
that cone.

Indeed, let

\[
 0=x_0<x_1<x_2<\cdots,\qquad
 x_{i+j}\ge x_i+x_j,
\tag{3.1}
\]

and suppose

\[
 \sum_j\delta_{x_j}=\rho_1+\rho_2
\tag{3.2}
\]

with both summands satisfying the anchored-window inequalities.  If
\(\rho_1(\{x_j\})=c_j\), the singleton anchored inequality gives
\(c_j\le c_0\).  Applying the same inequality to \(\rho_2\) gives
\(1-c_j\le1-c_0\).  Hence \(c_j=c_0\) for every \(j\), so both summands
are scalar multiples of the original clock.

There are primitive multidefect extreme clocks.  The first occurs at
residue period five: the increment word

\[
 (0,1,0,1,1)
\tag{3.3}
\]

has shifts

\[
 (0,0,1,1,2,3).
\tag{3.4}
\]

It is an extreme cyclic Apéry ray, has no proper-period density tie, and
is not a one-kink/one-defect ray.  Therefore the price cone is not
generated by one-defect rays, and a convex extreme-ray reduction cannot
reduce every Farkas price to that family.

This does not rule out an **objective-specific nonlinear** uncrossing for
the PBBS coefficient vector.  It proves that such an uncrossing would
have to use more than TP2 and convex extremality: it must exploit the
actual signed binomial/Rayleigh objective.

## 4. An exact family with an \(h-2\)-period shoulder

The finite shoulder itself cannot be bounded by an absolute number of
periods.

Fix an integer \(h\ge3\).  Consider the covering price generated by two
capacities:

\[
 \text{capacity }h\text{ at cost }h,
 \qquad
 \text{capacity }h-1\text{ at cost }h-\frac12.
\tag{4.1}
\]

Explicitly,

\[
 \psi_h(L)=
 \min_{\substack{q,k\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}\\
 qh+k(h-1)\ge L}}
 \left(qh+k\left(h-\frac12\right)\right).
\tag{4.2}
\]

Its restriction \(p_i=\psi_h(i)\), \(1\le i\le h\), is already a closed
depth-\(h\) price table.  Capacity \(h\) has the unique minimum density
one.  Put

\[
 \delta_h(L)=\psi_h(L)-L.
\tag{4.3}
\]

### Theorem 4.1 (literal long shoulder)

For

\[
 L_j=1+jh\qquad(j\ge0),
\tag{4.4}
\]

one has

\[
\boxed{
 \delta_h(L_j)
 =h-1-\frac12\min\{j+1,h-1\}.}
\tag{4.5}
\]

Consequently the stabilized residue-one Apéry distance is

\[
 d_1=\lim_{j\to\infty}\delta_h(L_j)
 ={h-1\over2},
\tag{4.6}
\]

and, more generally, the complete stabilized profile is

\[
\boxed{d_a={h-a\over2}\qquad(1\le a<h).}
\tag{4.6a}
\]

Indeed, after reducing to \(0\le k<h\), zero overshoot in residue \(a\)
is attained by \(k=h-a\) copies of the capacity-\((h-1)\) generator.
Every smaller \(k\) has overshoot \(h-a-k\), and every larger \(k\)
wraps once more; both have larger reduced cost.  Thus even the formal
tail of this example is a single linear directed-residue profile.  The
obstruction below comes from availability, not from a complicated
multidefect formal tail.

while its physical shoulder is

\[
\boxed{
 \eta_{1,j}
 =\delta_h(L_j)-d_1
 ={(h-j-2)_+\over2}.}
\tag{4.7}
\]

Thus the shoulder has exactly \(h-2\) positive periods.

#### Proof

A block of \(h\) copies of the capacity-\((h-1)\) generator has the same
total capacity as \(h-1\) copies of the capacity-\(h\) generator, but
costs \(h/2\) more.  Hence an optimal cover may be chosen with

\[
 0\le k\le h-1.
\tag{4.8}
\]

Fix \(0\le j\le h-2\).  If \(0\le k\le j+1\), take
\(q=j+1-k\).  The resulting capacity is

\[
 (j+1-k)h+k(h-1)=(j+1)h-k\ge1+jh,
\tag{4.9}
\]

and its reduced cost is

\[
 h-1-\frac k2.
\tag{4.10}
\]

This decreases with \(k\).  The choice \(k=j+1,q=0\) is still a cover,
because

\[
 (j+1)(h-1)-(1+jh)=h-j-2\ge0.
\tag{4.11}
\]

Every \(k>j+1\) with \(q=0\) adds the positive cost
\(h-\tfrac12\) while the target is fixed, so it is worse.  Thus the
optimum is \(k=j+1\), giving

\[
 \delta_h(L_j)=h-1-\frac{j+1}{2}.
\tag{4.12}
\]

For \(j\ge h-2\), use \(k=h-1\) and enough capacity-\(h\) pieces.  Its
reduced cost is \((h-1)/2\).  Any other representative has, after the
replacement in (4.8), some \(0\le k\le h-1\).  If \(z\) is its integer
capacity overshoot, then

\[
 z\equiv-k-1\pmod h,
 \qquad
 \delta_h(L_j)=z+{k\over2}.
\tag{4.13}
\]

For \(k<h-1\), the least possible overshoot is \(h-k-1\), so the last
quantity is at least \(h-1-k/2>(h-1)/2\).  For \(k=h-1\), zero overshoot
is possible from \(j=h-2\) onward and gives equality.  Hence
\((h-1)/2\) is optimal and stable.  This proves
(4.5)--(4.7). \(\square\)

## 5. The shoulder survives the PBBS local limit

In the terminal regime,

\[
 h=D,\qquad {D\over\sqrt r}\longrightarrow A.
\tag{5.1}
\]

For every fixed \(j\),

\[
 {L_j\over\sqrt r}\longrightarrow jA,
\qquad
 {\eta_{1,j}\over h}\longrightarrow{1\over2}.
\tag{5.2}
\]

The normalized PBBS coefficient at \(L_j\) converges to
\(-K'(jA)\).  For every fixed \(j\ge1\), this is a nonzero Gaussian-tail
quantity.  Therefore the shoulder contribution in this family is on the
same \(C_rC_{r-1}/\sqrt r\) scale as the complete volume-normalized
functional.  It is not a lower-order conductor artifact.

This disproves both of the following possible shortcuts:

1. every relevant physical price reaches its formal Apéry tail after
   \(O(1)\) critical periods;
2. local CLT normalization automatically removes the finite shoulder.

The family is only a scope obstruction, and the theta machinery actually
signs its complete limit.  If \(L/h\to x>0\) at a continuity point of the
ceiling function, then every selected generator has capacity \(h+O(1)\)
and cost \(h+O(1)\), so

\[
\boxed{
 {\psi_h(L)\over h}\longrightarrow\lceil x\rceil.}
\tag{5.3}
\]

In PBBS coordinates \(y=L/\sqrt r\), the limiting price is therefore

\[
 f(y)=A\left\lceil{y\over A}\right\rceil.
\tag{5.4}
\]

Here \(f(0)=0\) and the right limit at zero is \(A\); values at the other
countably many jump points are immaterial to the absolutely continuous
limit measure.

The local-limit theorem and Gaussian domination give

\[
\boxed{
 {2\over A C_rC_{r-1}}
 \sum_L\widehat\mu_L\psi_h(L)
 \longrightarrow
 \int_0^\infty -K'(y)f(y)\,dy
 =A\sum_{q\ge0}K(qA).}
\tag{5.5}
\]

Poisson summation gives the exact ceiling margin

\[
 \sum_{q\ge0}K(qA)
 ={1\over2}-e^{-\pi/4}
  -2\sum_{m\ge1}e^{-4\pi m^2}>0.
\tag{5.6}
\]

Hence this explicit \(\Theta(h)\)-shoulder family has a strictly positive
asymptotic volume-seed margin.  The example disproves boundedness and
negligibility of the shoulder, not positivity.  It also shows the useful
scope of the August 4 theta calculation: a long shoulder is harmless when
its normalized price collapses to one reciprocal-ceiling train.

## 6. Exact consequence for the all-depth strategy

Strict ballot TP2 gives the raw one-crossing theorem and is already fully
used in the coefficient sign analysis.  It cannot, by itself, turn the
hypergraph Farkas dual into a one-defect or interval LP:

* primitive multidefect prices are genuine extreme rays;
* the min-plus closure is nonlinear;
* carry subadditivity fails at the physical shoulder; and
* the shoulder can occupy \(\Theta(h)\) critical periods at leading
  Gaussian scale.

The viable replacement for a bounded-shoulder theorem is a **signed
weighted shoulder inequality**.  It must compare the adverse part of

\[
 \sum_{a=1}^{h-1}\sum_{j\ge0}
 \widehat\mu_{a+jh}\eta_{a,j}
\tag{6.1}
\]

with the formal Apéry reserve and with the cross-residue capacities
released by the same shortest-path configurations.  Total positivity may
still help order those charges, but an objective-specific inequality of
this form is indispensable.
