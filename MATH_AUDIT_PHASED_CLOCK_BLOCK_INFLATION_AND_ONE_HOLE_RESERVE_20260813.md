# Audit of the proposed phased-clock and block-inflation closures

**Date:** 2026-08-13  
**Scope:** combinatorial/algebraic proof audit of the two supplied candidate
texts

* `2256725a-c930-4c98-8f7f-a2f5f8c2dbe7/pasted-text.txt`; and
* `a21fec92-f4eb-4dd4-9fc7-5fc57477bfa8/pasted-text.txt`.

## Verdict

Neither text proves a new unconditional closure of the whole construction.
The first contains a useful **conditional** clock-dilation lemma, but its
essential phase-refined hypothesis is not supplied by the archived `Q4` or
ordinary all-width-current theorem.  The second contains a useful
owner-level common-reserve identity reducing the special star residue to
one punctured insertion rail.  Its claimed unconditional spanning-host row,
however, is not established by the written proof.  The already frozen
complementary-square theorem remains the proof-safe local theorem closing
residence, all-width current, odd socket action, and phased-host extension.

## 1. Phased clock dilation: conditional only

The clock construction uses the two different offset lists

\[
 J_0=(2q-1,0,\ldots,q-1),\qquad
 J_1=(q-1,q,\ldots,2q-1).
\]

Consequently the clock contribution of a lifted interval depends on the
phase of its first macro-occurrence.  The proof therefore assumes, in its
equation (1.1), equality of the old interval-union current separately in
each phase class:

\[
 \sum_{t:\epsilon(U_t^0)=b}
 e_{\cup_{j<h}U_{t+j}^0}
 =
 \sum_{t:\epsilon(U_t^1)=b}
 e_{\cup_{j<h}U_{t+j}^1}
 \qquad(b=0,1).
\tag{1}
\]

Ordinary all-width current zero gives only the sum of the two equations in
(1).  An alternating ordered two-SDR, proper phasing, or `Q4` transparency
does not imply the two summands vanish separately.  Indeed two nonzero
phase currents can cancel after aggregation.  Thus the displayed lifting
argument is correct **if (1) and its prefix/suffix refinements are assumed**,
but the candidate does not derive (1) for the actual common-mate bank.

The stated exposure inequalities after dilation are also not consequences
of owner count alone; they require a direct radius-one incidence census in
the target Boolean lattice.  Hence this candidate cannot presently be used
to invoke the protected-Ore host theorem.

## 2. Block inflation: the local map is useful, the host step is open

The map

\[
 \Psi(U)=(U\cap(F\cup A))\cup
          \bigcup_{\widehat z_j\in U}D_j,
 \qquad D_j=\{z_j,\ldots,z_{j+q-2}\},
\]

is union-preserving, and on a literal two-consecutive-label horizontal
state it gives

\[
 F+a_i+\widehat z_{j-1}+\widehat z_j
 \longmapsto
 F+a_i+\{z_{j-1},\ldots,z_{j+q-2}\}.
\]

Therefore the displayed normal-form owners have rank `R`, adjacent columns
are Johnson adjacent, and each `z`-coordinate has a positive block of
length `q`.  Conditional on a source bank in which every relevant state and
cell has exactly the asserted consecutive-label normal form, literal
all-width set equalities push forward under `Psi`.

Two steps needed for the advertised Part A are not proved:

1. The text says to create a width-`m` source bank by subdividing eight
   approach arcs.  It does not specify the resulting source owner/cell
   sequence and prove that subdivision preserves every *fixed-width*
   occurrence current.  Inserting identical local paths into two global
   resolutions is not by itself such a proof, because the offsets of
   crossing windows change.
2. Equations (28)--(30) assert that the protected exposures `alpha,beta`
   are inherited up to `O(q)`.  The overlapping map `Psi` is not an
   incidence-graph embedding and can create new unprotected lower sets
   adjacent to many protected owners.  A direct target-ground neighbour
   census is required.  No such census is given.

Accordingly the local dilation is a credible construction target, not yet
an unconditional spanning phased-host theorem.  The complementary-square
bank already has the necessary direct distinctness and exposure audit and
is the proof-safe result to cite.

## 3. The one-hole owner identity is the strongest valid new reduction

For an insertion current, its big and small cyclic decks split as

\[
 Q^+=J\sqcup P,\qquad Q^-=J\sqcup N,
 \qquad |P|=q,\quad |N|=q-1,
\]

and the point-current identity

\[
 \sum_{G\in P}{\bf1}_G-\sum_{G\in N}{\bf1}_G
 ={f1}_D+q e_z
\tag{2}
\]

is correct.  Applying (2) to the insertion current and the star currents
gives the displayed cancellation

\[
 \deg B^+=\deg B^-.
\]

Adding every common deck `J` really does turn one shore into a disjoint
union of complete rails and the other into a disjoint union of complete
rails except for the distinguished insertion rail with its owner `H`
deleted.  The random-order estimate is sufficient to make the same-core
centre decks disjoint on each relevant shore; different star cores are
separated by their intersections with `S`, and `c>q` separates insertion
and star supports.

Thus the following owner-level reduction is proof-safe:

\[
 \boxed{\text{the special conformal star residue reduces to one specified
 punctured insertion rail.}}
\]

It is not yet a positive-semigroup solution.  Restoring the puncture must
be done by one common nonnegative reserve while preserving the contextual
compulsory-ticket current

\[
 \Omega_H=
 \delta\tau(\Delta_x)-\tau(H;\Delta_x^+)
 +\sum_{p\ne x}
   \bigl(\delta\tau(X_p)-\delta\tau(L_p)\bigr),
\]

as well as owner disjointness and the same-state suffix capacities.  No
such decorated absorber is constructed in either candidate.

## 4. Exact proof-safe boundary

The strongest synthesis supported by the two texts is therefore:

1. conditional phase-refined clock dilation;
2. a plausible but not yet host-certified overlapping-block dilation; and
3. an unconditional owner-level reduction of the special star residue to
   one decorated punctured rail.

Neither the full compulsory-ticket semigroup nor the typed common-cap cut
is closed.  In particular these candidates do not strengthen `Q4` into a
joint occurrence-level saturation theorem.
