## 1. The unique typed physical-root seed

Let `V` be the 22 physical upper/root slots of the planted module.  At a

\[
 \operatorname{val}_L(\ell_\epsilon(U))=L_\epsilon(U),\qquad
 \operatorname{val}_T(t_\epsilon(U))=T_\epsilon(U).

Let

\[
 \mu_\epsilon:B_L^\epsilon\longrightarrow V,\qquad
 \mu_\epsilon(\ell)=\text{the slot occupied by lower occurrence }\ell \tag{1.2}

### Theorem 1.1 (forced root-gauge seed)

There is a unique typed-port map fixing `V`, all heads, and all 14 common
atoms, and carrying the old local assignment and per-slot co-middle relation
to the new ones.  It has typed components

\[
 \alpha:B_L^0\longrightarrow B_L^1,\qquad
 \gamma:B_T^0\longrightarrow B_T^1,
\]

and on the two shores it is

\[
 \begin{split}
   \alpha(\ell_0(U))&=\ell_1(U),\\
   \gamma(t_0(U))&=t_1(U).
 \end{split}                                             \tag{1.3}
\]

Both maps are bijections, and with `beta=id_V` one has

\[
                         \mu_0=\mu_1\alpha.              \tag{1.4}
\]

In particular, (0.1) holds and the assignment-only **typed-port reduct** has
zero defect in physical-root gauge.  No full selected-interface defect is
claimed here.

#### Proof

Coefficient-one equality of the lower deck makes the first formula in
(1.3) a bijection: each fixed slot contains one old and one new lower
occurrence.  Likewise coefficient-one equality of the tail deck makes the
second formula a bijection.  For every old lower occurrence `ell_0(U)`,

\[
       \mu_1\alpha(\ell_0(U))=\mu_1(\ell_1(U))=U
                              =\mu_0(\ell_0(U)),
\]

which is (1.4).  Since `U` and `H(U)` are fixed, the cap point
`U-H(U)` is fixed, and (1.1) gives

\[
 \operatorname{val}_T\!\left(\gamma(t_0(U))\right)
 =\operatorname{val}_L\!\left(\alpha(\ell_0(U))\right)
    \cup(U-H(U))=T_1(U).
\]

Thus the typed per-slot co-middle relation is preserved.  Conversely,
fixing a slot forces the image lower occurrence to be the unique new lower
occurrence in that slot, so `alpha=mu_1^{-1}mu_0`; then (1.1) and the value
maps force `gamma`.  Hence the seed is unique.  \(\square\)

 \end{split}                                             \tag{1.5}
\]
