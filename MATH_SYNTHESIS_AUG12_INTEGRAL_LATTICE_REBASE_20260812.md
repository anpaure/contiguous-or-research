## 2. Why the generic trace boxes are not yet rail obstructions

One column of the explicit closed-rail system is a complete cyclic source
component containing (N) owners.  It is not a one-owner open trace.
Every such component regenerates its full literal state and has trace
boundary zero integrally.

Designated lower targets are optional marks on already existing intervals.
An upper witness designation is likewise optional only when selecting that
witness changes no compulsory upper-resource row.  In either genuinely
optional case, comparing the same physical rail with and without one mark
gives the corresponding target unit vector in the integer column lattice.
Thus neither the forced-mark parity box nor the signed open-history square
embeds automatically in the actual whole-rail owner/lower/trace projection.

The actual owner lattice is also less arithmetically rigid than the
maximal-carousel subfamily.  Pure nonmaximal rails exist at every period

\[
 2d+4\le N\le k-R+d+1.
\]

Two consecutive periods kill the component-size gcd.  Their role swaps
generate every first-order coordinate difference.  Moreover all standard
(j)-subset degree gcds of the two pure-rail templates equal one: for
(j\ge2), a chosen pair of toggles at cyclic distance (d) lies in a
unique owner window and can be extended to a (j)-set of template degree
one.

In fact the full statement is now proved: the integer lattice generated by
all named-owner columns of the nonmaximal pure rails is

\[
 \mathbb Z^{\binom{[k]}R}.
\]

The proof uses adjacent toggle transpositions to generate the complete
kernel of the point-incidence map and consecutive rail periods to generate
its full allowable image.  Combined with optional marks and zero
whole-cycle boundary, the owner/lower/trace lattice is

\[
 \mathbb Z^{\binom{[k]}R}\oplus
 \mathbb Z^{\mathcal L_{\rm residual}}\oplus\{0\}.
\]

Thus the generic Smith/parity obstruction is absent in this projection.
Any surviving torsion must use a rigid coordinate omitted from that
projection, such as a compulsory palette, component/topology, residence,
phase/socket, nonoptional upper, or typed-cap row.  This still does not prove
semigroup normality.

### 2.1 Exact augmented-coordinate scope

The preceding direct sum is a statement about the displayed
owner/lower/closed-boundary **projection**.  Its three shores have different
strengths when further compulsory coordinates are appended.

1. **Optional annotations split globally.**  If a lower target (or an upper
   witness) is merely marked on an already-existing interval, downward
   closure gives two complete packet columns differing by

   \[
        (0,\mathbf e_T,0)
   \]

   in the *full* augmented system: every physical, palette, phase, socket,
   cap, and state coordinate is identical.  Hence a modular character of
   any augmentation has coefficient zero on every genuinely optional mark.

2. **Whole-rail boundary is identically zero, not freely saturated.**  Every
   closed rail column has boundary zero and the required demand has boundary
   zero.  Thus open-history alternating squares do not embed in this packet
   family.  If one atomizes a rail into open transitions, however, boundary
   must be restored as a compulsory row; it is not legitimate to discard it.

3. **Named-owner saturation is only proved after forgetting rigid rows.**
   The theorem

   \[
        \operatorname{span}_{\mathbb Z}\{o(C)\}
        =\mathbb Z^{\binom{[k]}R}
   \]

   does not say that \((\mathbf e_A,0)\) belongs to the lattice after
   compulsory palette, component/topology, phase/socket, residence,
   nonoptional upper-ticket, or typed-cap coordinates are appended.  A
   surviving character may couple a nonzero owner coefficient to one of
   those rigid rows.  Likewise, consecutive component sizes eliminate the
   scalar component-count gcd but do not prove the joint owner/component
   lattice or bounded Euler-component realization.

Accordingly an abstract determinant-two alternating square has the following
proof-safe interpretation.  If its alternating row is an optional mark, it
is killed by a literal unit column difference.  If it is an open trace
boundary, it is absent from whole closed rails.  If it uses any rigid row
listed in item 3, it remains a valid warning but is not yet an obstruction
until embedded in the actual complete rail columns.  Finally, even complete
signed saturation of the augmented lattice would not imply a nonnegative,
owner-disjoint, connected decomposition.

The analytic chronology has the same arithmetic conclusion in every
physical row.  If (qn\le m\le(q+1)n), the odd-tropical hierarchy gives
every physical optimum a literal certificate using at most (2q+1) head
denominations.  Hence no scalar carry row hides an independent congruence
obstruction.  Any remaining congruence obstruction must couple rigid

## 3. First remaining integral theorem

Let (A_k^{\rm rail}) have one column for every complete closed rail
component, including compulsory protected data but treating optional
lower/upper witness marks as free annotations.  The first exact target is:

> **Protected owner-cycle normality.**  After at most a constant number of
> terminal corrections, the required all-owner demand lies in the
> nonnegative semigroup of the protected closed-rail columns, and the
> selected rails admit occurrence-compatible lower chain designations.

The lattice test is necessary, but the sharper known static obstruction is
positive chainization.  The Ferrers capacity flow is integral, yet refining
it to one nested depth-(d) flag per owner is equivalent to an equitable
minimum-chain decomposition of a punctured Boolean half.  This FEHC problem
is open at coefficient one.  The best unconditional static replacement has
depth (2d+O(1)), which is not enough for (B(k)+O(1)).

Thus the current first gate may be attacked either by:

1. a protected normality/absorber theorem for the closed-rail orbit; or
2. a direct occurrence-labelled rail factorization that simultaneously
   proves the sharp Ferrers flag structure.

There is now a constructive intermediate result.  For every named owner
(H), an insertion current and a jointly packed star of two-size role
currents give a conformal current (Y_H): its positive and negative sides
are genuine owner matchings, (H) occurs only positively, and

\[
 P_RY_H={\bf1}_H.
\]

Consequently

\[
 e_H-Y_H=\mathcal B^+-\mathcal B^-
\]

for two simple equal-size owner matchings with identical point-degree
sequence.  Thus insertion/role positivity is closed.  The remaining
owner-only step is the conformal rail lift of this zero-point difference.
It cannot be discharged by a generic sequence of simple (2)-switches:
uniform degree fibres can have realizations separated from every
(i)-switch with (i<R).  The proof must exploit the special pair
((\mathcal B^+,\mathcal B^-)), use a compound growing-support rail trade,
or serialize through a reserve.

There is also an actual positive-semigroup boundary on how this may be done.
If one fixes a core (C) and tries to partition its complete petal fibre
independently into pure rails, every toggle point has degree divisible by
(q=d+1) in every rail.  Hence necessarily

\[
 q\mid {M-1\choose q-1},\qquad M=k-|C|.
\]

This already fails at (k=17): (q=4), (M=12), and
({11\choose3}=165).  Allowing every legal nonmaximal period does not remove
the obstruction.  It is not a global obstruction, because different core
roles contribute different point currents; rather, it proves that the
positive theorem must allocate owners across **overlapping cores** before
forming cyclic decks.  Baranyai factorization or a fixed-core wreath theorem
cannot by itself close the gate.

\[
