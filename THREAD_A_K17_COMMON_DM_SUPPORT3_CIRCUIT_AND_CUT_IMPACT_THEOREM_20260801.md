## 1. Per-root resource signatures

Let `V` be the 1,430 rooted rows, let `f_q^0` be the frozen flag at root
`q`, and let `F_q` be any declared finite menu of literal replacement flags
at that root.  Let

\[
             r_q(f)\in\mathbb Z^{\mathcal K}                 \tag{1.1}
\]

be the complete exact resource vector of `f`: its type contribution and
all tight lower-flag target contributions.  The root-row coordinate is
constant over `F_q` and may be included or suppressed.  Define the option
delta

\[
             \partial_q(f)=r_q(f)-r_q(f_q^0).                 \tag{1.2}
\]

A replacement has **support exactly three** when it chooses distinct roots
`q_1,q_2,q_3` and nonincumbent flags `f_i in F_{q_i}`.  Put

\[
                  v_i=\partial_{q_i}(f_i).                    \tag{1.3}
\]

Here and below a resource circuit is a `0/1` zero-sum packet: every named
option is used once.  It is not a claim of minimal linear dependence with
arbitrary integer coefficients.

### Theorem 1.1 (complete support-three normal form)

The three-root replacement preserves every exact static resource if and
only if

\[
                         v_1+v_2+v_3=0.                       \tag{1.4}
\]

Among the triples satisfying (1.4), exactly one of the following holds.

1. **Primitive triple:** all three `v_i` are nonzero.  Then no nonempty
   proper subset of the three options has zero resource sum.
2. **Unary--binary decomposition:** exactly one delta, say `v_1`, is zero,
   and `v_2=-v_3` is nonzero.  The final replacement is the disjoint union
   of one unary and one binary circuit.
3. **Three-unary decomposition:** `v_1=v_2=v_3=0`.

There is no fourth case.  In particular, a support-three zero-sum packet is
primitive if and only if all three option deltas are nonzero.

#### Proof

The total resource change is the sum in (1.4), proving the first assertion.
If some `v_i=0`, the other two sum to zero.  They are either both nonzero
opposites or both zero, giving cases 2 and 3.  Conversely those cases are
decomposable.  If every `v_i` is nonzero, no singleton sums to zero; and if
a pair summed to zero, (1.4) would force the remaining singleton to be
zero.  Hence the triple is primitive.  This also exhausts all cases.
\(\square\)

### Corollary 1.2 (proof-safe catalogue generation)

Bucket every literal nonincumbent option `(q,f)` by its exact vector
\(\partial_q(f)\).  The complete primitive support-three catalogue consists
of the canonically ordered triples on distinct roots

\[
 (q_1,f_1;q_2,f_2;q_3,f_3),\qquad q_1<q_2<q_3,       \tag{1.5}
\]

whose three nonzero bucket labels sum to zero.  Thus it can be generated by
a two-sum join

\[
                  v_1+v_2=-v_3,                              \tag{1.6}
\]

followed only by distinct-root and canonical-order filters.  The complete
decomposable catalogue is the union of

\[
       B_0B_0B_0\quad\hbox{and}\quad B_0B_vB_{-v}\ (v\ne0).  \tag{1.7}
\]

No chronology or liveness approximation enters this enumeration.

The decomposable triples are redundant for an exact joint optimizer which
already permits arbitrary compatible unary/binary options and evaluates the
complete final table.  They are **not** redundant for a strict one-move
greedy sweep: three individually nonimproving constituent moves can have a
joint liveness effect because transition compatibility is an ordered-pair
predicate of final endpoint flags.

## 2. Serial overlapping support-two moves

Primitive triples can sometimes be generated without introducing a
primitive three-root atom, provided one allows the support-two catalogue to
be regenerated after an overlapping move.

Fix a zero-sum triple on distinct roots `p,q,r`, with final flags
`f_p,f_q,f_r` and deltas `v_p,v_q,v_r`.  An intermediate flag `g in F_q`
gives the serial factorization

\[
 (p:f_p,\ q:g),\qquad (q:g\longmapsto f_q,\ r:f_r).   \tag{2.1}
\]

The second pair is evaluated relative to the intermediate table, so its
delta at `q` is \(\partial_q(f_q)-\partial_q(g)\).

### Theorem 2.1 (overlap/commutator factorization criterion)

The factorization (2.1) consists of two resource-exact moves, each supported
on at most two roots, if and only if

\[
                         \partial_q(g)=-v_p.                   \tag{2.2}
\]

Equivalently, after choosing the pivot `q` and which of `p,r` is serviced
first, the required intermediate-delta fibre at `q` must be nonempty.

For a primitive triple, (2.2) automatically makes both moves genuinely
binary and makes `g` different from both the incumbent and the final flag.
Every net three-root factor produced by two palette-neutral binary moves
sharing exactly one root is a zero-sum triple of Theorem 1.1, and it is
primitive precisely when its three final deltas are nonzero.

#### Proof

The first move in (2.1) is exact precisely when

\[
                         v_p+\partial_q(g)=0,
\]

which is (2.2).  Under (2.2), the change of the second move is

\[
 \bigl(v_q-\partial_q(g)\bigr)+v_r
   =v_q+v_p+v_r=0.
\]

This proves sufficiency; exactness of the first move proves necessity.  The
net delta is the telescoping sum of the two moves.  The final assertions
follow from Theorem 1.1.  \(\square\)

Thus a fresh-baseline binary catalogue can miss a primitive triple which is
present as a length-two walk through an intermediate flag fibre.  Conversely,
if none of the three possible pivot roots has the required intermediate
delta for either order, the primitive triple cannot be expressed as two
serial overlapping binary moves inside the declared menus.  This is an
exact menu-relative obstruction, not a global impossibility.

## 3. Common-live graph and the old critical shore

For a final flag table `F`, let an aligned attachment state `s=(q,a)` be
**common-live** when it has at least one literal incoming and at least one
literal outgoing transition under the complete ordered-pair compatibility
test.  Its owner `o(q,a)` is determined by the rooted attachment incidence.
Define

\[
 b_F(q,o)=1
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \text{some common-live state at root }q\text{ has owner }o. \tag{3.1}
\]

The common graph `G_F` has edge `qo` exactly when `b_F(q,o)=1`.  Parallel
physical states must be retained in a later state selection, but they have
one edge for matching cardinality and Hall neighbourhoods.

Let `F'` be the table obtained from any resource-exact circuit packet.  For
a root set `S`, define its distinct-neighbour cut impact

\[
 \kappa_{F'|F}(S)
   =|N_{F'}(S)|-|N_F(S)|                                \tag{3.2}
\]

or equivalently

\[
 \kappa_{F'|F}(S)
 =|N_{F'}(S)\setminus N_F(S)|
  -|N_F(S)\setminus N_{F'}(S)|.                        \tag{3.3}
\]

For the frozen shore put

\[
 A(F')=N_{F'}(P_0)\setminus H_0,
 \qquad
 L(F')=H_0\setminus N_{F'}(P_0).                      \tag{3.4}
\]

### Theorem 3.1 (exact fixed-shore price)

The deficiency of the **same old root set** `P_0` after the circuit is

\[
 |P_0|-|N_{F'}(P_0)|
   =289-\bigl(|A(F')|-|L(F')|\bigr).                  \tag{3.5}
\]

Consequently:

* the old `309 -> 20` shore improves by at least `t` if and only if
  `|A(F')|-|L(F')| >= t`;
* any candidate capable of increasing the common matching by at least one
  must satisfy

\[
                         |A(F')|>|L(F')|.                      \tag{3.6}
\]

The sets in (3.4) count distinct owner neighbours, not live attachment
states, transition arcs, or changed roots.

#### Proof

The old neighbourhood is exactly `H_0`, of size 20.  Equations (3.3)--(3.4)
give

\[
 |N_{F'}(P_0)|=20+|A(F')|-|L(F')|,
\]

and `|P_0|=309`, proving (3.5).  If the maximum matching increases, the
maximum Hall deficiency falls below 289, so in particular the deficiency
of `P_0` falls below 289.  This proves (3.6).  \(\square\)

Equation (3.6) is the strongest safe filter supplied by the one frozen
shore alone: candidates failing it may be discarded before matching.  It is
not a matching-gain certificate.

### Theorem 3.2 (dormant crossing-state anchor)

Let `T` be the changed-root support of `F'`.  If the common matching of
`F'` is larger than 1,141, then there are a root `p in P_0`, an owner
`o notin H_0`, and an aligned attachment state `s` at `p` with owner `o`
such that

\[
 s\text{ is not common-live in }F,
 \qquad
 s\text{ is common-live in }F'.                              \tag{3.7}
\]

For every side on which `s` was dead in the baseline, a newly activated
literal witness exists in the final table:

\[
\begin{array}{ll}
d_F^+(s)=0 &\Longrightarrow
 \text{some new outgoing transition from }s,\\
d_F^-(s)=0 &\Longrightarrow
 \text{some new incoming transition to }s.
\end{array}                                                   \tag{3.8}
\]

Every witness in (3.8) is incident with a root in `T`.  Thus a complete
cut-capable support-three generator may be anchored on

1. one dormant crossing state `(p,o)` with `p in P_0`, `o notin H_0`; and
2. one final witness geometry (ordered endpoint states, phase, and endpoint
   flags) for each of its baseline-zero sides,

followed by the zero-sum resource completion of Theorem 1.1.  There are at
most two such witness geometries, and their number is exactly the number of
baseline-zero sides.  In the frozen common-live model labelled state loops
are excluded, so when both sides are zero they require two nonloop witness
geometries.  This anchor is necessary, not sufficient: other old-head
neighbours may be lost, and another Hall shore may remain critical.

#### Proof

By Theorem 3.1, a matching increase implies
`|A(F')|>|L(F')|`, hence `A(F')` is nonempty.  Choose `o in A(F')`.
By definition of the new neighbourhood, some aligned state `s` at a root
`p in P_0` has owner `o` and is common-live in `F'`.  Since
`o notin H_0=N_F(P_0)`, no such state was common-live in `F`; this proves
(3.7).

If one side of `s` had degree zero in `F` and positive degree in `F'`, any
final witness on that side is a newly activated transition, proving (3.8).
A literal transition whose two endpoint roots both lie outside `T` sees the
same two endpoint flags before and after the replacement, so its legality
cannot change.  Every new witness is therefore incident with `T`.
\(\square\)

## 4. Exact all-cut criterion for matching gain

For every root set `S`, write

\[
                   d_F(S)=|S|-|N_F(S)|.                      \tag{4.1}
\]

The frozen graph has

\[
                   D=\max_S d_F(S)=289,
 \qquad \nu(G_F)=1430-D=1141.                               \tag{4.2}
\]

### Theorem 4.1 (cut-impact min--max)

For any rebuilt final table `F'` and integer `t>=0`,

\[
 \nu(G_{F'})\ge1141+t                                      \tag{4.3}
\]

if and only if, for every root set `S`,

\[
 \boxed{\quad
 \kappa_{F'|F}(S)\ge d_F(S)-289+t.
 \quad}                                                     \tag{4.4}
\]

In particular, the common matching increases by at least one if and only if

\[
 \kappa_{F'|F}(S)\ge d_F(S)-288
 \qquad\text{for every }S\subseteq V.                       \tag{4.5}
\]

Thus every old deficiency-289 shore must gain at least one net distinct
neighbour, every old deficiency-288 shore must suffer no net loss, and all
other cuts remain constrained according to (4.5).  Running an exact maximum
matching and rebuilding its Hall shore is an equivalent separation oracle
for (4.5).

#### Proof

By (3.2),

\[
 |S|-|N_{F'}(S)|=d_F(S)-\kappa_{F'|F}(S).              \tag{4.6}
\]

The deficiency form of Hall's theorem gives

\[
 \nu(G_{F'})
 =1430-\max_S\bigl(d_F(S)-\kappa_{F'|F}(S)\bigr).      \tag{4.7}
\]

Inequality (4.3) is therefore equivalent to requiring every term in the
maximum to be at most `289-t`, which is exactly (4.4).  \(\square\)

### Example 4.2 (old-shore escape is not enough)

Let the left vertices be `1,2,3`, the right vertices be `a,b,c`, and take

\[
 E=\{1a,2a,3b\}.
\]

The maximum matching has size two.  Relative to the matching `1a,3b`, the
canonical alternating shore from unmatched root 2 is
`P={1,2}`, `N(P)={a}`, of deficiency one.  Add the single edge `2b`.
The fixed shore now has neighbours `{a,b}` and deficiency zero, but the
maximum matching is still only two because owner `c` is isolated.  The new
critical set is all three left vertices.  Hence even a positive fixed-shore
impact, with no deleted edge, need not increase the recomputed matching.

This is the precise reason the search may price by (3.6), but every survivor
must be scored by a fresh common attachment-state matching and Hall audit.

## 5. Exact locality and a proof-safe critical-shore enumerator

Let `C` be a support-three circuit with changed-root set `T`.  A literal
transition whose two endpoint roots both lie outside `T` has the same two
endpoint flags before and after `C`, and therefore cannot change.  It follows
that:

1. every changed transition is incident with `T`;
2. a common-liveness bit outside `T` can change only when one of its incoming
   or outgoing witnesses is a transition to or from `T`; and
3. every common edge outside that one-step incidence closure is unchanged.

More precisely, define the **dependency halo**

\[
 \mathcal H_C(T)=T\cup
 \{q:\text{some transition in }\mathcal T(F')\mathbin\triangle\mathcal T(F)
       \text{ has endpoint roots }q\text{ and }t\in T\}.       \tag{5.1}
\]

Here \(\mathcal T(F)\) denotes the complete literal transition set of the
final flag table `F` (and is unrelated to the added-owner set `A(F')` in
(3.4)).

All root--owner common adjacencies at roots outside \(\mathcal H_C(T)\) are
fixed.
Roots in the halo but outside `T` are not fixed: changing a witness at the
other endpoint can activate or kill their common-live attachment state.
Therefore an implementation which updates only changed roots in
\(T\cap P_0\) is unsound.  It must rebuild the transition incidence
halo, OR parallel live states onto each root--owner edge, and only then
evaluate the distinct-neighbour ledger (3.4).

This gives the following complete fail-closed procedure.

1. Either generate all zero-sum triples by Corollary 1.2, or begin with the
   dormant crossing-state and one-or-two-witness anchors of Theorem 3.2 and
   complete their changed-root support by the same zero-sum join.  Record
   whether each result is primitive, unary--binary, three-unary, or serially
   factorable by Theorem 2.1.
2. Materialize the three final rows.  Rebuild every transition incident with
   a changed root and then recompute both in- and out-liveness for every
   affected attachment state.  A cached one-sided arc gain is insufficient.
3. Form `A(F')` and `L(F')` from distinct owner identifiers.  Discard the
   candidate exactly when `|A(F')|<=|L(F')|`.
4. For every survivor, run an exact common root--owner maximum matching and
   derive its new alternating Hall shore.  A strict gain is authenticated
   only when the matching exceeds 1,141, equivalently when (4.5) holds.

A proof-auditable row ledger should therefore contain at least

```text
three root IDs and three literal option IDs;
three exact delta hashes and their zero-sum check;
primitive/decomposable class and any serial pivot witness;
added old-shore owner IDs A and lost old-shore owner IDs L;
fixed-shore impact |A|-|L|;
recomputed common matching;
recomputed Hall-tail and Hall-head identifier hashes;
materialized final-row-table hash.
```

The first five fields are a valid critical-shore prefilter.  Only the last
three distinguish a true matching gain from movement of the old obstruction.

## 6. Proved boundary

The theorem completely closes the **algebraic basis question** for support
three relative to any declared literal option menus.  It also gives an exact
necessary-and-sufficient cut formulation for a common matching gain and a
safe old-shore prefilter specialized to `2e9194...`.

It does not show that a support-three candidate satisfying (3.6) exists, nor
that such a candidate improves the recomputed matching.  Even a factor with
common matching 1,430 would still require a compatible transition selection;
no chronology or downstream gate follows from the results above.

In particular, the frozen strict support-at-most-two sweep accepting no move
does not exclude any of the following: a primitive triple, a serial
overlapping binary pair whose second member exists only after the first, or
a jointly useful decomposable triple whose constituents are individually
nonimproving.
