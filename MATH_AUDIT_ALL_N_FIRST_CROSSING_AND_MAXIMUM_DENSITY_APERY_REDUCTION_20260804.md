# Independent audit: all-slot first-crossing and maximum-density Apéry reduction

**Date:** 2026-08-04  
**Audited theorem:**
`MATH_THEOREM_ALL_N_FIRST_CROSSING_AND_MAXIMUM_DENSITY_APERY_REDUCTION_20260804.md`  
**Verdict:** **PASS** as an exact reduction.  The note does not prove
all-slot Gaussian positivity.

## 1. Exact-fill interpretation

The recurrence

\[
 V_m=\max_{1\le j\le\min(m,n)}(c_j+V_{m-j})
\]

is exactly the maximum over compositions whose ordinary capacities sum to
`m`.  Denomination one is always present, so every capacity is reachable.
Equivalently, an underfilled knapsack may be filled by size-one generators
without decreasing its value because `c_1>=0`.

For `m<=n`, internal superadditivity bounds the value of every such
composition by `c_m`, while the singleton generator `m` attains `c_m`.
Thus the identity `V_m=c_m` used by the first-crossing theorem is valid.

## 2. First-crossing deletion

Let `N` be the first index with `c_N>=A`, and let `W` use only the prefix
through `N`.  The two clocks agree through capacity `N`, and generator
deletion gives `W_m<=V_m` thereafter.  Exact filling gives

\[
 W_m\ge c_N+W_{m-N}\ge A\qquad(m\ge N).
\]

Hence both arguments lie in `[A,infinity)`, where

\[
 K(x)=-e^{-(A+x)^2}
\]

is strictly increasing.  Therefore `K(W_m)<=K(V_m)` term by term.  The
comparison is summable because both clocks grow at least linearly along
copies of their endpoint generator.  The direction of the functional
inequality is correct: deleting all generators after the first threshold
crossing can only make the functional smaller.

This argument is confined to deletion in the monotone Gaussian tail.  It
does not use, and does not imply, the false monotonicity under insertion of
an undominated denomination.

## 3. Endpoint-saturation audit

For `n>=2`, the family defining `P_n` is nonempty (use `n` copies of
denomination one) and finite, so its maximum is attained.  It is exactly
the prefix Bellman value at capacity `n` when denomination `n` is omitted.
For `n=1` the family is empty, consistently with the stated value `P_1=0`.

The alternative value `P_n` dominates every lower-index partition of
capacity `n`, in particular every pair `c_i+c_(n-i)`.  Therefore replacing
`c_n` by `max(A,P_n)` preserves internal superadditivity.  The partition
`(n-1)+1` also shows `P_n>=c_(n-1)`, so endpoint monotonicity survives.
For `n=1`, the replacement is simply `c_1'=A`; then `V_m'=mA<=mc_1=V_m`
and both clocks lie in the increasing tail for every `m>=1`.

Repeated original superadditivity bounds every lower-index partition by
`c_n`, hence `P_n<=c_n`.  Since also `A<=c_n`, the saturated value really
is a lowering: `c_n'=max(A,P_n)<=c_n`.

The new and old clocks agree below `n`; thereafter endpoint lowering gives
`V'<=V`, while one new endpoint generator plus size-one fill gives
`V'>=A`.  Tail monotonicity of `K` therefore has the asserted direction.
If `P_n>=A`, an attaining lower partition replaces every endpoint use, so
the endpoint is genuinely inert rather than merely equal at capacity `n`.
This inertness does not force the saturated endpoint to be `A`: deleting
the endpoint can remove the last displayed value at or above `A`, and
re-extending the same lower-generator clock to its first threshold crossing
adds an inert endpoint again.  The theorem therefore deliberately stops at
`c_n'=max(A,P_n)`.

## 4. Reduced residue graph

With

\[
 \lambda=\max_j c_j/j,\qquad d_j=c_j-j\lambda,
\]

all reduced weights are nonpositive.  Critical denominations have weight
zero and capacity divisible by their gcd `g`, so they are zero-weight loops
modulo `g`.  Every noncritical edge has strictly negative weight.

If `g>1`, denomination one is necessarily noncritical and reaches every
residue.  Deleting the segment between repeated vertices in a noncritical
walk strictly increases its reduced weight.  Thus every residue maximum is
attained by a simple walk of at most `g-1` edges and ordinary capacity at
most `(g-1)n`.  For `g=1`, the empty path gives the sole residue maximum
`beta_0=0`.  No infinite-walk supremum or zero-cycle ambiguity remains.

## 5. Critical-semigroup conductor

Normalize the critical indices by `g` and write

\[
 S'=S/g,\qquad a=\min S',\qquad N_*=\max S'.
\]

Because `gcd(S')=1`, the directed Cayley graph generated by `S'` on
`Z/aZ` reaches every residue.  A shortest path to a residue is simple, so
its represented sum is at most `(a-1)N_*`.  If `h_r` is such a sum and
`k>=max h_r` has the same residue, then

\[
 k=h_r+qa\qquad(q\ge0),
\]

and `a` is itself a critical generator.  Hence every
`k>=(a-1)N_*` is in the normalized critical semigroup.  Scaling gives the
claimed conductor

\[
 C_*=g(a-1)N_*\le(a-1)n.
\]

The inequality is sufficient rather than asserted sharp, and the inclusive
endpoint is justified.

## 6. Exact padding and eventual formula

Fix a residue `r` and an optimal simple reduced path of capacity `ell_r`.
For any

\[
 m\equiv r\pmod g,
 \qquad m\ge\ell_r+C_*,
\]

the difference `m-ell_r` is a multiple of `g` lying beyond the conductor.
It is therefore an exact nonnegative combination of critical
denominations.  Appending that combination fills capacity `m` exactly and
adds zero reduced weight.  This proves the lower bound

\[
 V_m\ge\lambda m+\beta_r.
\]

Conversely, delete the critical loops from any exact-fill configuration.
The remaining noncritical steps form a walk to residue `r`, whose reduced
weight is at most `beta_r`.  This proves the reverse inequality.  The
padding step therefore has neither a divisibility gap nor unused capacity.

The uniform threshold arithmetic is also correct:

\[
 \ell_r+C_*
 \le(g+a-2)n
 \le n(n-1),
\]

because `a<=n/g` and

\[
 g+{n\over g}\le n+1
 \quad(1\le g\le n).
\]

Thus every capacity `m>=n(n-1)` is in its asserted shifted arithmetic
coset, and splitting the Bellman sum at that threshold gives the exact
finite-head-plus-lattices formula.

## 7. Scope

The audited theorem establishes:

* a canonical first-threshold counterexample normalization;
* exact endpoint saturation by the best lower-denomination capacity-`n`
  configuration;
* exact eventual periodicity for every finite Bellman clock;
* an explicit quadratic bound on the nonperiodic head;
* finite simple-path and critical-padding certificates for every tail
  residue.

It does not give an absolute bound on grid size, sign the finite transient
head, prove `Phi(c)>=0`, or construct the Rayleigh coagulation kernel.
