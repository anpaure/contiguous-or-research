# Rectangle positivity: an exact transport obstruction

This note addresses the remaining positive Markov-basis question for the
octahedral rectangle moves supplied by the promotion-packet construction.
The signed lattice theorem says that these moves generate every rank-\(r\)
load difference with zero point marginals.  That does **not** imply an
efficient positive realization.  In fact, even deep inside the positive
orthant, the number of rectangle moves cannot in general be bounded by a
constant multiple of the \(\ell_1\)-defect.

## 1. Johnson transportation is a mandatory lower bound

Let
\[
\Omega_r=\binom{[M]}r,
\]
and give \(\Omega_r\) the Johnson metric
\[
d_J(S,T)=|S\setminus T|=|T\setminus S|.
\]
For two nonnegative integer load vectors \(\mu,\nu\) of equal total mass,
write \(W_1^J(\mu,\nu)\) for the minimum transportation cost using this
metric.

An elementary octahedral rectangle has the form
\[
R=e_{K+a_0+b_0}+e_{K+a_1+b_1}
 -e_{K+a_1+b_0}-e_{K+a_0+b_1},
\]
where the displayed four sets have rank \(r\).  Its positive and negative
parts can be paired along two Johnson edges.  Consequently
\[
W_1^J(R_+,R_-)\le 2.
\]

### Proposition 1

If \(\mu\) is transformed to \(\nu\) by \(L\) signed octahedral rectangle
moves, then
\[
\boxed{L\ge \frac12 W_1^J(\mu,\nu).}
\]

This remains true if every intermediate vector is required to be
nonnegative, and it remains true after adding any common positive baseline
to \(\mu\) and \(\nu\).

### Proof

Each move transports its two units by total Johnson distance at most two.
Concatenating the induced transport plans for the \(L\) moves gives a plan
from \(\mu\) to \(\nu\) of cost at most \(2L\).  Hence
\(W_1^J(\mu,\nu)\le2L\).  Positivity restrictions can only remove legal
sequences, not weaken this lower bound.  Adding the same baseline to both
vectors cancels in the transportation problem. \(\square\)

Equivalently, by Kantorovich duality, every 1-Lipschitz function on the
Johnson graph supplies a lower bound on the required number of rectangles.

## 2. Constant \(\ell_1\)-defect can require linearly many rectangles

Let \(M=r+s\), with \(2\le s\le r\).  Partition the ground set as
\[
[M]=K\sqcup P\sqcup Q,
\qquad |K|=r-s,\quad |P|=|Q|=s.
\]
Put
\[
A=K\cup P,
\qquad B=K\cup Q.
\]
Choose a balanced repartition
\[
P\sqcup Q=P'\sqcup Q',
\qquad |P'|=|Q'|=s,
\]
so that each of \(P',Q'\) contains
\(\lfloor s/2\rfloor\) or \(\lceil s/2\rceil\) elements from each of
\(P,Q\).  Define
\[
C=K\cup P',
\qquad D=K\cup Q'.
\]

Then
\[
\mathbf 1_A+\mathbf 1_B=\mathbf 1_C+\mathbf 1_D
\]
as point-incidence vectors.  Thus
\[
e_A+e_B-e_C-e_D
\]
lies in the zero point-marginal lattice generated by rectangles.

For any integer \(b\ge1\), let
\[
\mu=b\mathbf 1+e_A+e_B,
\qquad
\nu=b\mathbf 1+e_C+e_D.
\]
Both vectors have minimum load at least \(b\), they have identical total
mass and identical point marginals, and
\[
\|\mu-\nu\|_1=4.
\]

### Proposition 2

Every octahedral-rectangle sequence from \(\mu\) to \(\nu\) has length at
least
\[
\boxed{\lfloor s/2\rfloor.}
\]

### Proof

Let
\[
f(S)=d_J(S,\{A,B\}).
\]
This is 1-Lipschitz.  By the balanced choice of \(P',Q'\),
\[
f(C),f(D)\ge\lfloor s/2\rfloor,
\qquad f(A)=f(B)=0.
\]
Using \(-f\) in the Kantorovich dual gives
\[
W_1^J(\mu,\nu)
\ge f(C)+f(D)
\ge2\lfloor s/2\rfloor.
\]
Proposition 1 now gives the result. \(\square\)

## 3. Consequence for the promotion-packet programme

In a top fibre of size
\[
M=m+H,
\]
the central ranks have \(r=m\pm q\), so the complementary Johnson radius is
\[
s=M-r=H\mp q.
\]
For \(|q|\le H/2\), the example above forces
\[
\Omega(H)
\]
rectangle moves despite an \(\ell_1\)-defect of only four and despite an
arbitrarily large common positive baseline.

Therefore the hoped-for theorem
\[
\text{number of positive rectangle moves}=O(\ell_1\text{-defect})
\]
is false, even under uniform positive lower bounds and exact point-marginal
compatibility.

This does **not** kill the segment-rectangle approach.  It changes the
correct target.  A viable theorem must be measured in a geometric quantity,
at least Johnson transportation cost, and then exploit one of the following
forms of global sharing:

1. pack many compatible rectangle moves into one positive segment
   deployment;
2. cancel long transport paths across ranks before literal deployment;
3. prove that the particular load defects arising from top-fibre packet
   selections have \(W_1^J=o(W)\), a property false for arbitrary balanced
   load vectors;
4. use larger Markov moves whose transportation diameter grows with \(H\)
   but whose literal realization still has only \(O(H)\) boundary cost.

Thus signed lattice generation is complete, but positivity is not merely a
local buffer problem.  It has an unavoidable long-range transport component.

## 4. Revised exact gate

A plausible replacement for the disproved \(O(\ell_1)\) gate is:

> For the specific aggregate rank-load discrepancies produced by the
> top-fibre fractional design, find a nonnegative rectangle decomposition
> whose number of *segment deployments*, rather than elementary rectangles,
> is \(o(W/H)\), with total Johnson transport \(o(W)\) after shared routing.

The existing positive segment rectangle realizes one full elementary
rectangle throughout the hard band with only \(O(H)\) duplicated boundary
entries.  The outstanding problem is therefore a congestion/packetization
theorem for many such transports, not an ordinary bounded-length Markov-basis
theorem.
