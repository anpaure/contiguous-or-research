# Stateful status-port fusion: exact robust degrees, Hall cuts, and a history transversal

Date: 2026-07-26

Method: pure mathematics only.  No computation, finite search, solver, or
web input is used.

All logarithms in the quantitative history cut are natural.

## 0. Exact outcome

Let \(X\) be an endpoint port of a rank-\(m\) product-SCD path for a
split \(A\dot\cup B\), \(|A|=|B|=m\).  Fix a perfect matching
\(\pi:A\to B\), and let the split pairs of \(X\) be the raw
status-cube directions.

Three different quantities must not be identified.

1. **Raw directions.**  If \(e(X)\) pairs are split and an arbitrary
   recent queue uses \(B\) physical coordinate labels, at least

   \[
                              e(X)-B                         \tag{0.1}
   \]

   raw directions survive.  This is exact and sharp.  On the
   near-spanning full-status sector, \(e(X)=m/2+O_{\mathbb P}(\sqrt m)\),
   so \(B=O(H)=o(m)\) leaves linearly many raw axes.

2. **Endpoint directions.**  A surviving raw toggle need not land at a
   product-SCD endpoint.  The exact endpoint degree is given in
   Theorems 2.1--2.2 below by four SCD boundary-incidence sets.  No lower
   bound for this degree follows from \(e(X)\).  Against arbitrary
   coordinate queues, its exact one-sided robust Hall condition is
   (3.1); the two-port condition is the capacitated cut (3.4).
   In fact independent arbitrary states on both ports make robustness
   impossible even for budget one (Corollary 3.3).
   At one port, the literal inverse-seam history (2.10)--(2.13) kills
   every candidate whenever \(d_{\rm end}(X)<H\).

3. **Dynamically safe directions.**  Even a queue-disjoint endpoint seam
   can be illegal because a recent label reappears in the first
   \(H\) edges of the outgoing product path.  The exact age-sensitive
   degree is (4.5), and the codegree bound is (4.7).  It is a degree in
   the \(H\)-memory lift, not in the static endpoint graph.

There is an explicit worst-history cut.  Suppose a port has \(d\)
candidate outgoing seams, every seam followed by an internally
two-sided-safe word of between \(r_-\) and \(r_+\) edges, including the
seam.  Then there is a valid, two-sided-safe incoming history of

\[
 K=\left\lceil {m(\log\max\{d,2\}+1)\over2r_-}\right\rceil       \tag{0.2}
\]

edges which kills every candidate, provided

\[
                              K+r_+\le H.                       \tag{0.3}
\]

For a W endpoint on a path of length
\(L\in[c\sqrt m,C\sqrt m]\), all same-side neighboring paths have
length \(L\pm2\), so one may take

\[
 r_-\ge L-1,\qquad r_+\le L+3,\qquad d\le m.                    \tag{0.4}
\]

Consequently, if \(H=\sqrt m\,\omega(m)\) with
\(\omega(m)/\log m\to\infty\), every such port admits an adversarial
history of \(O(\sqrt m\log m)=o(H)\) edges which leaves no dynamically
safe endpoint seam.  A positive limiting fraction of all W ports lie in
every fixed nonempty length band of this form.

This disproves any endpoint-expansion theorem required to hold for
**every** \(H\)-memory state.  It does not disprove a global stateful
fusion whose histories are chosen coherently rather than adversarially.
For merely \(\omega\to\infty\) with
\(\omega=o(\log m)\), the transversal cut does not apply.  In that
regime the missing theorem is precisely an expansion/codegree theorem
for the histories actually generated by the construction.

## 1. Raw status directions after a queue deletion

For a middle owner \(X\), write

\[
 {\cal S}(X)=\bigl\{\{a,\pi(a)\}:
                 |X\cap\{a,\pi(a)\}|=1\bigr\}.                  \tag{1.1}
\]

The pairs in \({\cal S}(X)\) are pairwise coordinate-disjoint.  A recent
queue \(Q\) has support

\[
                         F=\operatorname{supp}Q\subseteq A\cup B. \tag{1.2}
\]

Delete a raw axis when its physical pair meets \(F\).

### Lemma 1.1 (exact raw resilience)

\[
 \#\{p\in{\cal S}(X):p\cap F=\varnothing\}
                         \ge e(X)-|F|.                           \tag{1.3}
\]

For every \(b\le e(X)\), there is an \(F\) of size \(b\) for which
equality holds.

#### Proof

Every coordinate lies in one matching pair, so one forbidden coordinate
can delete at most one raw axis.  Conversely choose one coordinate from
each of any \(b\) split pairs. \(\square\)

For a queue of \(k\) internally two-sided-safe Johnson edges,
\(|F|=2k\).  Thus (1.3) reads \(e(X)-2k\).  The full-status enumerator

\[
                         [x^m](1+x^2+2xy)^m                       \tag{1.4}
\]

gives

\[
 \mathbb E(e\mid |X|=m)={m^2\over2m-1}
                         ={m\over2}+O(1),                        \tag{1.5}
\]

and the exact second-moment calculation gives
\[
 \operatorname{Var}(e\mid |X|=m)
 ={2m^2(m-1)^2\over(2m-1)^2(2m-3)}
 ={m\over4}+O(1).                                  \tag{1.6}
\]
In particular, for
\(H=o(m)\), arbitrary \(O(H)\)-label deletion does not threaten raw
status dimension on the owner-dense sector.

Equation (1.3) is only a direction statement.  It says nothing about
whether the toggled owner is another W endpoint.

## 2. Exact W endpoint degree inside a status cube

For a set \(Z\) in one half, let \(\lambda(Z)\) be the minimum rank of
its SCD chain.  Say that \(Z\) is a bottom when
\(|Z|=\lambda(Z)\), and a top when
\(|Z|=m-\lambda(Z)\).  Define

\[
\begin{aligned}
 {\rm TD}(Z)&=\{z\in Z:Z-z\text{ is a top}\},&
 {\rm TA}(Z)&=\{z\notin Z:Z+z\text{ is a top}\},\\
 {\rm BD}(Z)&=\{z\in Z:Z-z\text{ is a bottom}\},&
 {\rm BA}(Z)&=\{z\notin Z:Z+z\text{ is a bottom}\}.              \tag{2.1}
\end{aligned}
\]

These are boundary-incidence sets of the chosen SCD.  They are not
determined by ranks alone.

Write a port as \(X=I\cup J\), \(I\subseteq A\), \(J\subseteq B\), and
put \(b=\pi(a)\).

### Theorem 2.1 (exact high-port formula)

Suppose \(X\) is a high endpoint at stratum \(l\), so
\[
                         |I|=m-l,\qquad |J|=l.                  \tag{2.2}
\]
The number \(d_{\rm end}(X)\) of status-orientation axes whose other
endpoint is again a product-SCD endpoint is

\[
\begin{aligned}
d_{\rm end}(X)
={}&\sum_{\substack{a\in I\\ \pi(a)\notin J}}
 {\bf1}\{a\in{\rm TD}(I)\ \text{or}\ \pi(a)\in{\rm BA}(J)\}\\
&+\sum_{\substack{a\notin I\\ \pi(a)\in J}}
 {\bf1}\{a\in{\rm TA}(I)\ \text{or}\ \pi(a)\in{\rm BD}(J)\}.
                                                               \tag{2.3}
\end{aligned}
\]

The first sum gives neighbors at stratum \(l+1\); the second gives
neighbors at stratum \(l-1\).

#### Proof

In the first orientation, toggling the pair removes \(a\) from \(A\)
and inserts \(b\) in \(B\).  The new ranks are
\((m-l-1,l+1)\).  A product-SCD vertex at these ranks is the high
endpoint of its rectangle exactly when its \(A\)-part is a chain top or
its \(B\)-part is a chain bottom.  This is the first indicator in
(2.3).  The reverse orientation gives ranks
\((m-l+1,l-1)\), and the identical endpoint criterion gives the second
indicator. \(\square\)

### Theorem 2.2 (exact low-port formula)

Suppose \(X\) is a low endpoint at stratum \(l\), so
\[
                         |I|=l,\qquad |J|=m-l.                  \tag{2.4}
\]
Then

\[
\begin{aligned}
d_{\rm end}(X)
={}&\sum_{\substack{a\notin I\\ \pi(a)\in J}}
 {\bf1}\{a\in{\rm BA}(I)\ \text{or}\ \pi(a)\in{\rm TD}(J)\}\\
&+\sum_{\substack{a\in I\\ \pi(a)\notin J}}
 {\bf1}\{a\in{\rm BD}(I)\ \text{or}\ \pi(a)\in{\rm TA}(J)\}.
                                                               \tag{2.5}
\end{aligned}
\]

The first sum gives the inner neighbor at stratum \(l+1\), and the
second the outer neighbor at stratum \(l-1\).

#### Proof

After increasing the \(A\)-rank, the new vertex is a low endpoint
exactly when its \(A\)-part is a bottom or its \(B\)-part is a top.
After decreasing the \(A\)-rank, the same criterion gives the second
line. \(\square\)

The formulas exhibit the gap between raw degree and endpoint degree.
The raw statistic records only whether a pair is split.  Endpoint
eligibility additionally tests four SCD boundary incidences.

There is a useful rank census, but it is not an endpoint-conditioned
degree theorem.  If
\[
                         n_j=\binom mj-\binom m{j-1},             \tag{2.6}
\]
then
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{|Z|=m-l}|{\rm TD}(Z)|
 &=(l+1)n_{l+1}
  =\binom ml(m-2l-1),\\
\sum_{|Z|=l}|{\rm BA}(Z)|
 &=(l+1)n_{l+1}
  =\binom ml(m-2l-1).                            \tag{2.7}
\end{aligned}
\]

The outward incidences have the equally exact census
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{|Z|=m-l}|{\rm TA}(Z)|
&=(m-l+1)n_{l-1},\\
\sum_{|Z|=l}|{\rm BD}(Z)|
&=(m-l+1)n_{l-1}.                                  \tag{2.7a}
\end{aligned}
\]

Indeed, every top of rank \(m-l-1\), or bottom of rank \(l+1\), has
\(l+1\) adjacent sets of the required rank.  Thus an unconditioned
half-set sees only \(m-2l-1\) such incidences on average.  W ports are
conditioned to have at least one boundary component, and that
conditioning can concentrate (2.7).  It is invalid to infer an
\(O(\sqrt m)\), or a linear, port degree from this census alone.

### Corollary 2.3 (exact queue-disjoint endpoint degree bound)

Let \({\cal C}_{\rm end}(X)\subseteq{\cal S}(X)\) be the axes counted by
(2.3) or (2.5), and let \(F\) be an arbitrary forbidden coordinate
support.  Then
\[
\begin{aligned}
d_{\rm seam}(X;F)
&=\#\{p\in{\cal C}_{\rm end}(X):p\cap F=\varnothing\}\\
&\ge d_{\rm end}(X)-|F|.                           \tag{2.8}
\end{aligned}
\]
The bound is sharp for every
\(|F|\le d_{\rm end}(X)\) in the unrestricted support model.

#### Proof

The candidate axes remain distinct pairs of the perfect matching.
Apply the proof of Lemma 1.1 to this subfamily. \(\square\)

Thus a \(k\)-edge recent queue gives the pointwise bound
\[
                         d_{\rm seam}(X;Q)
                              \ge d_{\rm end}(X)-2k.             \tag{2.9}
\]
This is the strongest conclusion obtainable from pair intersection
alone.  It does not test the outgoing collar word.

### Proposition 2.4 (literal inverse-seam history cut)

Let \(e_1,\ldots,e_d\) be all candidate status seams at \(X\), written
\[
                         e_j:r_j\longrightarrow i_j,
 \qquad r_j\in X,\quad i_j\notin X.                              \tag{2.10}
\]
The matching-pair directions make all \(2d\) displayed labels distinct.
There is an internally two-sided-safe history of \(d\) edges ending at
\(X\) after which every \(e_j\) is illegal.  Explicitly, start at
\[
                         (X\setminus\{r_1,\ldots,r_d\})
                            \cup\{i_1,\ldots,i_d\}               \tag{2.11}
\]
and perform the inverse exchanges
\[
                         i_j\longrightarrow r_j
                         \qquad(1\le j\le d).                    \tag{2.12}
\]
If \(d+1\le H\), every repeated seam label lies in an \(H\)-edge
window.  More generally, the same construction kills any prescribed
\(s\le H-1\) candidate seams with an \(s\)-edge history.

Thus arbitrary-history pointwise survival requires
\[
                         d_{\rm end}(X)\ge H                     \tag{2.13}
\]
if even one seam is to be guaranteed.  This necessity is physical, not
merely an abstract support-deletion adversary.

## 3. Exact static robust Hall cuts

Let \(G=(L,R;E)\) be a directed endpoint-port graph.  At a left port
\(u\), distinct incident status edges use distinct matching-pair
directions.  We first state the exact static **support-deletion** model:
an adversary may name at most \(B\) coordinate labels at each port.
It can then delete an arbitrary prescribed set of at most \(B\)
incident edges, by naming one endpoint of each corresponding split pair.

If the support must itself be realized by a two-sided-safe history ending
at \(u\), it contains equally many labels in \(u\) and \(u^c\).  Since
each split pair offers one choice of each kind, every prescribed set of
\(r\) incident edges can still be deleted by a valid history with support
at most \(r+1\): choose the two kinds as evenly as possible and add one
fresh spectator when \(r\) is odd.  Thus the physical history version
has the same criterion with the harmless replacement \(B\mapsto B-1\);
for even balanced cuts equality is exact.

### Theorem 3.1 (one-sided \(B\)-resilient Hall criterion)

Every deletion of at most \(B\) incident edges at each \(u\in L\)
leaves a matching saturating \(L\) if and only if, for every nonempty
\(S\subseteq L\) and every \(T\subseteq R\) with \(|T|<|S|\), there is
some \(u\in S\) such that

\[
                         |N_G(u)\setminus T|>B.                  \tag{3.1}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
 \min_{\substack{\varnothing\ne S\subseteq L\\
                  T\subseteq R,\ |T|<|S|}}
       \max_{u\in S}|N_G(u)\setminus T|\ge B+1.                  \tag{3.2}
\]

#### Proof

If (3.1) fails, delete from every \(u\in S\) all of
\(N(u)\setminus T\).  At most \(B\) edges are deleted at each \(u\), and
the surviving neighborhood of \(S\) lies in \(T\), so Hall fails.

Conversely, suppose a permitted deletion creates a deficient set \(S\).
Let \(T\) be its surviving neighborhood and enlarge \(T\), if necessary,
to size \(|S|-1\).  Every edge from every \(u\in S\) to
\(R\setminus T\) was deleted, so
\(|N(u)\setminus T|\le B\), contradicting (3.1). \(\square\)

In particular the pointwise necessary condition is

\[
                              d_{\rm end}(u)>B.                  \tag{3.3}
\]

It is not sufficient.  Ordinary Hall plus a minimum-degree bound also
does not imply (3.1).

For a literal seam tested against a past queue at its left port and a
future queue at its right port, an edge may be deleted by either
endpoint.  The exact cut has a capacitated-cover form.

### Theorem 3.2 (two-port robust Hall criterion)

Suppose every vertex on both shores may mark at most \(B\) incident
edges as forbidden, and an edge is deleted when either endpoint marks
it.  The graph remains left-saturating under every such marking if and
only if the following holds:

for every \(S\subseteq L\) and \(T\subseteq R\) with \(|T|<|S|\),
there exist \(U\subseteq S\) and \(V\subseteq R\setminus T\) such that

\[
                         |E(U,V)|>B(|U|+|V|).                   \tag{3.4}
\]

#### Proof

To make the surviving neighborhood of \(S\) lie in \(T\), the adversary
must cover every edge of the cut graph
\[
                         G[S,R\setminus T]                       \tag{3.5}
\]
by assigning it to one of its endpoints, with capacity \(B\) at each
endpoint.  The edge-to-endpoint incidence graph has such a capacitated
assignment exactly when, for every \(U\subseteq S\) and
\(V\subseteq R\setminus T\),
\[
                         |E(U,V)|\le B(|U|+|V|),                 \tag{3.6}
\]
by capacitated Hall.  Negating (3.6) for every deficient pair
\((S,T)\) gives (3.4). \(\square\)

Equations (3.1) and (3.4), not ordinary transversality of raw status
axes, are the exact static all-cuts requirements under arbitrary
coordinate queues.

### Corollary 3.3 (independent two-port robustness is impossible)

If \(B\ge1\), no nonempty simple bipartite endpoint graph is robust in
the sense of Theorem 3.2.

#### Proof

Fix \(u\in L\), take \(S=\{u\}\) and \(T=\varnothing\).  Assign each
edge \(uv\) of the cut star to its right endpoint \(v\).  Every right
endpoint receives only one edge, so capacity \(B\ge1\) covers the whole
star and isolates \(u\).  Equivalently, in (3.4), for
\(U\subseteq\{u\}\) and \(V\subseteq N(u)\),
\[
                         |E(U,V)|\le |V|
                                      \le B(|U|+|V|).
\]
Thus the required violating subcut cannot exist. \(\square\)

Physically, every neighbor can place one label of its own seam direction
in its independently prescribed queue.  Corollary 3.3 does not apply
when the right-port states are outputs of the same global trajectory and
hence cannot be chosen independently.  It proves that such trajectory
correlation is indispensable; no theorem uniform over arbitrary states
at both ports can exist.

## 4. The full \(H\)-memory degree

Write an incoming two-sided-safe queue at a port \(X\) as

\[
 Q=((\alpha_{-r},\beta_{-r}):1\le r\le H-1),                    \tag{4.1}
\]

where age \(r\) means that the edge occurred \(r\) transitions before
the proposed seam.  Let a candidate seam \(e\), followed by its chosen
outgoing atomic path, have direction labels

\[
                         D_j(e)=\{\alpha_j(e),\beta_j(e)\},
 \qquad 0\le j\le H-1,                                          \tag{4.2}
\]

truncated when the atomic path ends.  Position \(j=0\) is the seam.

### Theorem 4.1 (exact age-sensitive legality)

Assume the future word \((D_j(e))_{j\ge0}\) is internally two-sided
\(H\)-safe.  Then the seam and outgoing word are legal from \(Q\) if
and only if

\[
 D_{-r}\cap\bigcup_{0\le j\le H-1-r}D_j(e)=\varnothing
                         \qquad(1\le r\le H-1),                  \tag{4.3}
\]

where \(D_{-r}=\{\alpha_{-r},\beta_{-r}\}\).

#### Proof

Two labels on edges at positions \(-r\) and \(j\) occur together in an
\(H\)-edge window exactly when
\[
                              r+j\le H-1.                        \tag{4.4}
\]
The two-sided residence criterion says that every such pair of edge
labels must be disjoint.  Internal future safety handles pairs with
both indices nonnegative, while incoming queue safety handles pairs with
both negative.  The remaining pairs are exactly (4.3). \(\square\)

Define \({\cal C}_0(X)\) to be the endpoint candidates whose future word
is internally safe, and put

\[
d_H(X,Q)=
\#\left\{e\in{\cal C}_0(X):\text{\((4.3)\) holds}\right\}.       \tag{4.5}
\]

This is the actual dynamic degree.  If \(d_{\rm seam}(X,Q)\) tests only
the seam labels \(D_0(e)\), then
\[
                         d_H(X,Q)\le d_{\rm seam}(X,Q)
                                      \le d_{\rm end}(X).       \tag{4.5a}
\]

For a useful conditional lower bound, define the prefix codegrees

\[
\Delta_{r,x}(X)=
\#\left\{e\in{\cal C}_0(X):
 x\in\bigcup_{0\le j\le H-1-r}D_j(e)\right\}.                   \tag{4.6}
\]

Then the union bound gives the deterministic estimate

\[
 d_H(X,Q)\ge |{\cal C}_0(X)|
   -\sum_{r=1}^{H-1}\ 
      \sum_{x\in D_{-r}}\Delta_{r,x}(X).                         \tag{4.7}
\]

Thus a positive theorem would follow, for example, from endpoint degree
\(D\) and prefix codegree \(\Delta\) satisfying
\[
                              D>2(H-1)\Delta.                    \tag{4.8}
\]
No such estimate is supplied by W's endpoint census or by full-status
raw abundance.  A single old label may occur in the outgoing prefixes
of many endpoint candidates.

The exact history obstruction is the negation of (4.5): an age-labelled
queue \(Q\) for which the sets in (4.3) cover all
\({\cal C}_0(X)\).  Section 5 constructs one quantitatively.

## 5. An explicit balanced history transversal

### Theorem 5.1 (history transversal cut)

Fix a middle port \(X\), and let \({\cal C}\) be a family of \(d\ge1\)
candidate seam-plus-path words.  Assume:

1. every word is internally two-sided safe;
2. every word has \(r_e\) edges, including its seam, with
   \[
                              r_-\le r_e\le r_+;                 \tag{5.1}
   \]
3. every direction label of a word is distinct from every other label
   in that word.

There exists an internally two-sided-safe incoming history of \(K\)
edges ending at \(X\), where

\[
 K=\left\lceil {m(\log\max\{d,2\}+1)\over2r_-}\right\rceil,     \tag{5.2}
\]

such that no word in \({\cal C}\) is legal, provided \(K+r_+\le H\).

#### Proof

For a future word \(e\), two-sided safety and distinctness imply that
its \(r_e\) removal labels form a set
\[
                         R_e\subseteq X,\qquad |R_e|=r_e,       \tag{5.3}
\]
and its insertion labels form
\[
                         I_e\subseteq X^c,\qquad |I_e|=r_e.     \tag{5.4}
\]
Choose independently and uniformly a \(K\)-set \(Y\subseteq X\) and a
\(K\)-set \(Z\subseteq X^c\).  For a fixed candidate,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\Pr(Y\cap R_e=\varnothing,\ Z\cap I_e=\varnothing)
&={\binom{m-r_e}K^2\over\binom mK^2}\\
&\le \exp\left(-{2Kr_e\over m}\right)
 \le \exp\left(-{2Kr_-\over m}\right).             \tag{5.5}
\end{aligned}
\]
By (5.2), the expected number of candidates missed by
\(Y\cup Z\) is less than one.  Hence some choice hits every candidate.

Enumerate \(Y=\{y_1,\ldots,y_K\}\) and
\(Z=\{z_1,\ldots,z_K\}\).  Starting from
\[
                         X_0=(X\setminus Y)\cup Z,               \tag{5.6}
\]
perform the disjoint exchanges
\[
                         z_i\longrightarrow y_i,
                         \qquad 1\le i\le K.                     \tag{5.7}
\]
This is an internally two-sided-safe history and ends at \(X\).

Every candidate future word repeats at least one label from this
history.  The oldest possible history occurrence and the latest possible
future occurrence are separated by at most \(K+r_+-1\) edge positions.
Condition \(K+r_+\le H\) therefore puts the repetition inside an
\(H\)-edge window.  Theorem 4.1 makes every candidate illegal.
\(\square\)

The history in Theorem 5.1 is literal; its labels have the correct
membership signs at \(X\), and no coordinate is repeated inside the
history.

### Corollary 5.2 (W ports in the central length band)

Let \(X\) be a high or low W endpoint on a product path of length \(L\).
Every same-side endpoint neighbor lies at the adjacent stratum, hence its
path has length \(L-2\) or \(L+2\).  After discarding candidates whose
seam already repeats a label in the outgoing path, the remaining
seam-plus-path words satisfy
\[
                         r_-\ge L-1,\qquad r_+\le L+3.           \tag{5.8}
\]
Also \(d\le e(X)\le m\).  Hence a killing history exists with
\[
 K\le
 \left\lceil{m(\log m+1)\over2(L-1)}\right\rceil                \tag{5.9}
\]
whenever the right side plus \(L+3\) is at most \(H\).

For fixed \(0<c<C<\infty\), uniformly over
\[
                         c\sqrt m\le L\le C\sqrt m,              \tag{5.10}
\]
one has
\[
                         K\le
 \left({1\over2c}+o(1)\right)\sqrt m\log m.                     \tag{5.11}
\]
Thus (5.9) is available if
\[
 {H\over\sqrt m}
                         \ge \left({1\over2c}+o(1)\right)\log m+C.
                                                                    \tag{5.12}
\]

Under the uniform product-SCD path census,
\[
 \Pr\left({L\over\sqrt m}>t\right)\longrightarrow e^{-t^2}.     \tag{5.13}
\]
Therefore the fraction of paths in (5.10) tends to
\[
                              e^{-c^2}-e^{-C^2}>0.               \tag{5.14}
\]
Every port in that band admits its own adversarial killing history under
(5.12).

Corollary 5.2 is a worst-state obstruction.  The histories chosen at
different ports need not be simultaneously realizable along one global
cycle factor.  It therefore rules out a robust theorem quantified over
all memory states, not a coherently constructed stateful fusion.

## 6. The precise surviving boundary

The following implications are proved.

* Raw status abundance survives arbitrary \(O(H)\)-label queues whenever
  \(H=o(m)\).
* Queue-disjoint endpoint expansion requires the boundary-incidence
  degree (2.3) or (2.5) and the robust Hall cuts (3.1)/(3.4).
* Full dynamic expansion requires the \(H\)-memory degree (4.5) and
  prefix-codegree control such as (4.8).
* For \(H/\sqrt m\gg\log m\), no theorem uniform over arbitrary memory
  states is possible, by the literal history cut of Theorem 5.1.

For the full coefficient-one regime
\[
                         H=\sqrt m\,\omega(m),\qquad
 \omega(m)\to\infty,\qquad H=o(m),                              \tag{6.1}
\]
the proved and unresolved ranges are sharply distinct.

1. If \(\omega/\log m\to\infty\), arbitrary-history endpoint expansion
   is false on a positive-density central path band.
2. If \(\omega=O(\log m)\), including
   \(\omega=o(\log m)\), the transversal cut need not fit inside the
   \(H\)-queue.  Raw axes remain abundant, but no current theorem bounds
   either the endpoint degree or the prefix codegrees strongly enough to
   verify dynamic Hall.  The boundary case
   \(\omega\asymp\log m\) is decided only when the explicit constant
   inequality (5.12) holds.

A positive route must exploit that the construction controls its own
queue trajectories.  It must prove expansion only on a selected,
invariant family of memory states, or periodically reset histories in
the full status-orbit factor.  Static abundance of split axes and
ordinary endpoint Hall are insufficient.
