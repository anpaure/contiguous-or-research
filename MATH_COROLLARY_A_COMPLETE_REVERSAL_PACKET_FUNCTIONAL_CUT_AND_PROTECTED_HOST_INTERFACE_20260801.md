There are two uses which must be kept separate.  In a **relative local
switch**, the exterior and its physical addresses are frozen while the

The packet is a resource-zero replacement on $4d+2$ roots.  It can

## 1. Exact functional cut price

Suppress the attachment and static rows, which are internally exact by the
imported theorem.  Let the forward predecessor at head $Z_i^+$ be
$Z_{i-1}^-$, and let the reverse predecessor be $Z_{i+1}^-$, with all
indices modulo $M$.

Place the packet in an ambient predecessor-incidence graph under the
following explicit host hypothesis: at packet head $Z_i^+$ the only
phase-dependent candidate incidence is
$Z_{i-1}^-Z_i^+$ versus $Z_{i+1}^-Z_i^+$, and every other candidate edge
is held fixed.  This is the graph alteration certified by the literal
cycle atoms.  It is **not** automatic for a larger option menu whose row
change also creates or deletes further compatibility edges.  For a head
set $Y$, write

\[
 I=\{i:Z_i^+\in Y\},                                      \tag{1.1}
\]

and let $N_0(Y)$ be the set of tail roots adjacent to $Y$ through fixed
edges outside the switched packet.  Put

\[
 I^- =\{Z_{i-1}^-:i\in I\},\qquad
 I^+ =\{Z_{i+1}^-:i\in I\}.                              \tag{1.2}
\]

### Theorem 1.1 (complete-reversal cut formula)

The old and new distinct-neighbour sets of $Y$ are exactly

\[
 N^-(Y)=N_0(Y)\cup I^-,\qquad
 N^+(Y)=N_0(Y)\cup I^+.                                  \tag{1.3}
\]

Consequently the change in Hall surplus is

\[
 \Delta_Z(Y)
 =|N_0(Y)\cup I^+|-|N_0(Y)\cup I^-|.                     \tag{1.4}
\]

In particular, the complete-reversal packet is not intrinsically
augmenting: its price can be positive, zero, or negative according to the
ambient fixed neighbours.

Formula (1.4) prices a relative switch against one fixed exterior.  In the
global-reversal quotient, both the packet and the completed exterior are
reflected, so the two incidence graphs are isomorphic and their Hall
deficiencies agree.  Positive cut price is therefore not required for a
one-orientation existential induction.

#### Proof

At a packet head $Z_i^+$, reversal changes the unique selected packet
predecessor from $Z_{i-1}^-$ to $Z_{i+1}^-$.  No selected edge at a head
outside the packet changes.  Taking distinct unions with the unchanged
neighbours gives (1.3), and subtracting cardinalities gives (1.4).
\(\square\)

The formula is occurrence-sensitive only before projection.  Hall counts
distinct tail roots, so repeated occurrences in $I^\pm$ are collapsed in
(1.3).  Here the packet roots are simple, but a packet root may already lie
in $N_0(Y)$, which is the essential ambient correlation.

### Corollary 1.2 (protected one-root puncture criterion)

Suppose $I=\{i\}$.  If

\[
             Z_{i-1}^-\in N_0(Y),\qquad
             Z_{i+1}^-\notin N_0(Y),                       \tag{1.5}
\]

then

\[
                         \Delta_Z(Y)=1.                    \tag{1.6}
\]

Thus a host which places exactly one packet head in a critical shore,
keeps its old predecessor represented by an unchanged exterior edge, and
places its new predecessor outside the old neighbour set obtains one exact
Hall unit.  All other packet roots may lie in the protected exterior.

Conversely, with $I=\{i\}$, condition (1.6) holds if and only if (1.5)
holds.  Merely replacing one old neighbour by one new neighbour gives
zero, not one, Hall gain.

#### Proof

For a singleton $I$, (1.4) compares adjoining one element to the same
fixed set.  The difference is $1$ exactly in (1.5).  \(\square\)

This is the exact bridge to the protected-puncture theorem for a
**relative fixed-exterior switch** **once** a host
embedding identifies every packet row with an allowed rooted option and
identifies the packet's balanced derivative inventories with the host's
resource deck.  The selected packet head then supplies the external
puncture, while the other $M-1$ changed roots give its protected completion
in the total resource ledger.  Neither that root-admissible identification
nor compression of those $M-1$ changes to the two rooted substitutions of
the literal protected-two-sum theorem follows from the cycle construction.

## 2. No phase-relative collision; two absolute seam obligations

Let ${\cal R}^+$ and ${\cal R}^-$ be the two complete packet atom sets.
They are alternative orientations, never simultaneously selected.  Each
phase uses once every **selected packet** lower colour, upper colour, tail
root and head root.  Hence there is no phase difference in those resources
and no closed-doubleton collision.

Setwise, two absolute palette obligations remain.  The original direct
reset seam $EF$ has

\[
                         L=E\cap F,\qquad U=E\cup F,       \tag{2.1}
\]

and neither value is used by the return rail.  They are common external
targets in both phases.  If the completed reset-return cycle is then opened
at one of its actual edges, that operation omits a second common pair
$(I_*,U_*)$.  These pairs are generally different.

Thus the long rail removes the bare square's orientation conflict, not the
absolute seam targets.  It also does not make the two ordered atom sets
equal: every predecessor and every head--owner incidence is reversed.  The
reversal isomorphism transports the complete selected internal matching.
A genuinely common residual is needed only when the packet is switched
relative to one frozen exterior, not when the complete exterior and its
matching are reflected with the packet.

## 3. Exact root-palette planting gate

Let an ambient phase contain a root set $V$, lower palette ${\cal L}$,
and upper palette ${\cal U}$, each with capacity one.  Fix a proposed
literal topology embedding, including Johnson-legal opening and join atoms.
Conditional on that incidence/topology data, its owner-and-q1 capacity
ledger is exact if and only if all three equalities

\[
 \begin{aligned}
  \{\text{removed roots}\}_{\rm multi}&=\{Z_i:i\in\mathbb Z_M\},\\
  \{\text{removed lower colours}\}_{\rm multi}&={\cal L}_Z,\\
  \{\text{removed upper colours}\}_{\rm multi}&={\cal U}_Z
 \end{aligned}                                             \tag{3.1}
\]

hold after including the declared opening/join seams.  Pairwise
disjointness of the packet palettes from the retained exterior is
necessary but is not, by itself, enough: coefficient-one capacity also
requires that the colours removed from the old support are precisely
replaced.  The local theorem proves simplicity of ${\cal L}_Z,{\cal U}_Z$,
not (3.1).

This is the correct root-palette/global-host ledger.  It is a simultaneous
three-shore selection problem, not a new residence or internal-shadow
problem.  The equalities do not themselves manufacture Johnson-legal join
atoms or the desired component topology.

## 4. A sharp same-socket exterior obstruction

The internal cyclic deck equality does not imply transparency after a
linear opening.  The following elementary criterion isolates the first
boundary obstruction.

For a finite block $B=(B_0,\ldots,B_{M-1})$, put

\[
 P_t(B)=\bigcup_{j=0}^{t-1}B_j,\qquad
 S_t(B)=\bigcup_{j=M-t}^{M-1}B_j.                         \tag{4.1}
\]

### Lemma 4.1 (pointwise fixed-socket reversal criterion)

Place $B$ and its literal reversal
$B^{\rm rev}=(B_{M-1},\ldots,B_0)$ between the same ordered left and
right exterior words.  Universal pointwise transparency means that, for
every set context $C$ (including $C=\varnothing$), every one-boundary
union $C\cup P_t(B)$ equals the corresponding union
$C\cup S_t(B)$.  This holds if and only if

\[
                         P_t(B)=S_t(B)qquad(1\le t\le M).
                                                               \tag{4.2}
\]

For a nontrivial simple-root block this is impossible: at $t=1$, (4.2)
would give $B_0=B_{M-1}$.

#### Proof

A left-crossing interval is the union of a fixed exterior suffix with a
prefix of the inserted block.  Under reversal that prefix is the
corresponding suffix of $B$.  Universal equality includes the empty set
context and is therefore equivalent to (4.2); conversely (4.2) makes every
contextual union equal.  The right boundary is symmetric.  The $t=1$
assertion is immediate. \(\square\)

Therefore the complete-reversal packet cannot be made pointwise
transparent by placing its two phases between the same ordered sockets.
The lemma applies separately to the opened root block, its maximal
antecedent, and every derivative row; internal reversal gives each such
block, but does not identify its two linear boundary profiles.
A positive global host must use at least one of:

1. swapped/reversed exterior sockets;
2. protected duplicate witnesses for the affected crossing targets;
3. the weaker prefix/suffix deck-inclusion criterion rather than
   same-address equality; or
4. an additional boundary collar whose own profile cancels the mismatch.

This obstruction concerns exterior cross-windows only.  It does not
contradict the exact cyclic/internal deck theorem.

It is also a same-socket **relative-switch** obstruction, not an
obstruction to reversal-quotient induction: in the quotient the exterior
sockets and all occurrence addresses reflect with the packet.  The next
theorem is different because it charges deletion of a cyclic edge, and its
loss survives that global reflection.

### Theorem 4.2 (quadratic duplicate-bank obligation)

The frozen theorem
`MATH_THEOREM_RESET_RETURN_RAIL_LINEARIZATION_COLLATERAL_20260801.md`
strengthens Lemma 4.1 from a profile mismatch to a quantitative target
loss.  For every cut $c$ of the packet's maximal depth-$d$ antecedent,
let ${\cal C}_c$ be the OR-values of all cyclic intervals of widths
$2,3,\ldots,2d$ which cross $c$.  Then

\[
 |{\cal C}_c|
   =\sum_{w=2}^{2d}(w-1)
   =d(2d-1),                                               \tag{4.3}
\]

and every value in ${\cal C}_c$ has exactly one witness in the complete
cyclic packet word.  Consequently none has an internal noncrossing witness
after linearization.

If the owner rank is $r=2d+1+h$, the forced family has exact rank
histogram

\[
 \#\{C\in{\cal C}_c:|C|=r-d+t\}=t
       \qquad(1\le t\le2d-1).                              \tag{4.4}
\]

Therefore a fixed-exterior linear host is upper-safe only if its exterior,
its new join collars, or an explicitly reserved duplicate bank supplies at
least one further witness for every member of ${\cal C}_c$.  In
particular, the phase-common q1 two-factor of Corollary 3.1 does not by
itself give an $O(1)$-defect linear packet: since
$d=\Theta(\sqrt m)$, (4.3) is $\Theta(m)$.

#### Proof

In the four-block antecedent normal form, every coordinate has a zero run
of length at least $2d+1$.  Hence extending any interval of width at most
$2d$ introduces one new coordinate at every step.  Its OR has rank
$r-d+w-1$, and the start and width are recovered from the value.  Thus
all such cyclic interval ORs are globally unique inside the packet.
Exactly $w-1$ width-$w$ intervals cross a fixed cut.  Summation gives
(4.3), and putting $t=w-1$ gives (4.4).  \(\square\)

The exact escape trichotomy for an opened linear representative is now:

1. supply the rank-graded ambient duplicate bank (4.4);
2. reverse/reflect a whole compatible exterior component so that the
   relevant global cyclic deck, rather than a bare packet linearization,
   is the invariant object; or
3. retain the packet through a non-linear contraction and postpone any
   opening until a separate guarded absorber is available.

Finding a more favourable bare cut is not a fourth option.
Simultaneous global reversal only bijects this lost family with the family
at the reflected cut; it preserves its size and witness multiplicities and
does not repay it.

### Corollary 4.3 (the frozen twin bank pays the absolute local debt)

The theorem
MATH_THEOREM_RESET_RETURN_DOVERLAP_AND_UPPER_FERRERS_TWIN_BANK_20260801.md
supplies the first alternative in the preceding trichotomy explicitly.
The canonical \(d\)-overlap uses exactly the mandatory \(M+d\) source

Therefore Theorem 4.2 is a sharp obstruction only to a **bare** opening.
It is not an outstanding local witness-supply obstruction.  What remains is
to extend the opened packet and both banks inside one global
upper-complete chronology without destroying exterior old witnesses, and
to retain a terminal compiler.  Global reversal transports such a completed
payment but still does not manufacture it.

## 5. Exact common-residual compiler gate for a frozen local switch

Contract the internally matched packet cells using the reversal bijection
from the frozen theorem.  Let $G^+,G^-$ be the two residual
target--cell graphs on the same exterior target bank and the same literal
exterior cell bank.  If the outside matching is required to be literally
common to both phases, its available graph is

\[
                              G^\cap=G^+\cap G^-.           \tag{5.1}
\]

### Proposition 5.1 (common residual Hall criterion)

A phase-common residual compiler saturating every residual target exists
if and only if

\[
                |N_{G^\cap}(A)|\ge |A|
       \qquad\text{for every residual target set }A.       \tag{5.2}
\]

#### Proof

A literal exterior edge can belong to one common matching exactly when it
is present in both phase graphs, hence exactly when it lies in $G^\cap$.
Hall's theorem applied to that graph gives (5.2). \(\square\)

Separate perfect matchings in $G^+$ and $G^-$ do not imply (5.2).
Internal reversal has already removed all internal incidence discrepancy;
only cells crossing the opening and ambient assignments enter this gate.

### Corollary 5.2 (reversal-quotient correction)

Proposition 5.1 is the exact gate for a **local** phase switch against one
frozen exterior and one fixed-address compiler.  It is not a requirement
for the existential same-parity induction of
`MATH_THEOREM_GLOBAL_REVERSAL_QUOTIENT_INDUCTION_AND_RELATIVE_PHASE_OBSTRUCTION_20260801.md`.

In that quotient, choose one oriented complete child and one perfect
matching in its residual graph $G^+$.  Simultaneous reversal of the whole
source word, exterior, witnesses and matching gives a perfect matching in
the isomorphic graph $G^-$.  No edge must occupy the same address in both
representatives, so Hall in $G^+\cap G^-$ is unnecessary.

The intersection criterion remains necessary when a named one-sided socket
is held fixed, when one packet is flipped relative to other components, or
when the theorem explicitly asks for a deferred local switch without
reflecting the exterior.  Global reversal does not repay the absolute cut
family (4.3) and does not change component count.

## 6. Exact integration boundary

The complete-reversal reset packet now supplies a literal resident,
all-depth, resource-zero role converter of support $4d+2$.  In the
punctured-containment lane it gives:

* the exact all-cut price (1.4);
* the one-unit protected-anchor criterion (1.5);
* an $O(d)$-root protected completion of the puncture delta; and
* no internal lower/upper, residence, derivative-row or compiler-incidence
  debt.

After Corollary 3.1, the remaining theorem is a one-oriented decorated
host/absorption statement which simultaneously chooses:

1. a fixed-$M_0$ lower/root/head-injective, graphic-independent
   Catalan-forest refinement of one oriented root/lower/tail-ticket host,
   with a low-component connector;
2. an opening and topology join;
3. a global embedding of the now-explicit \(d\)-overlap/Ferrers twin bank,
   preserving every exterior all-width target (or, alternatively, a
   whole-component reflection/non-linear contraction);
4. one terminal residual matching in the selected orientation (or the
   stronger common residual Hall inequalities (5.2) only for a named local
   switch); and
5. a same-parity regeneration rule for the protected packet state.

The phase-common root/lower-q1 planting and tail-ticket projection are now
available at the thresholds in Corollary 3.1; reversal-quotient induction
needs only one orientation of them.  The twin-bank theorem repays the
complete local opening leave, but it does not transport the banks through
the ambient chronology.  No fixed-$M_0$ joint lower/root/head/graphic
Catalan forest, low-component connector, exterior all-width preservation,
global matching increase, safe directed join, terminal compiler,
regeneration, or additive bound follows from these local results.
