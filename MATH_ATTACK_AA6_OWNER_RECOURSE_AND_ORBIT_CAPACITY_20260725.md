## 0. Outcome

Put

\[
 n=2m+1,\qquad W=\binom nm,\qquad t=\operatorname{Cat}_m=\frac Wn.
\]

This note gives two exact conclusions about target-specific component
signing.

1. Row recourse and exact-middle ownership recourse differ by the exact
   factor \(n\).  If \(F,G\) are exact wreath factors, then

   \[
   \boxed{d_{\rm own}(F,G)=n\,d_{\rm row}(F,G).}
   \]

   Combining this identity with the audited canonical packet obstruction
   shows that every exact factor satisfying the fixed-window fractional
   survival-packet target is at middle-owner distance

   \[
   \boxed{\left(\frac1{256}-o(1)\right)W}
   \]

   from the canonical MSW factor.  This holds for paths using arbitrary
   coordinate support and arbitrary exact-factor moves.  Hence an AA
   rounding theorem starting from that factor cannot have \(o(W)\)
   exact-middle owner recourse.  This is a statement about ownership-map
   changes, not by itself a lower bound on literal MTF connector length.

2. There is a group-orbit packet lower bound which remains valid when
   target labels move.  Tag source rows by targets they own, with at most
   \(D\) tags on one row, and let \(\Gamma\) be the group generated by
   every coordinate transposition used along an adaptive component-switch
   path.  If \(T_O\)
   tagged incidences start in a target orbit \(O\), while the quota capacity
   of that orbit is \(B_O\), then every integral quota-safe deletion family
   has size at least

   \[
   \frac1D\sum_O(T_O-B_O)_+,
   \]

   and every fractional survival-packet cover has mass at least

   \[
   \boxed{\frac1{DR}\sum_O(T_O-B_O)_+,}
   \]

   where \(R=\max_S(\beta(S)+1)\).  This strictly extends the invariant-
   target argument: invariant labels are simply singleton orbits.

   For the nonaligned reflection group from
   `NONALIGNED_REFLECTION_SUPPORT_SEED_ESCAPE_20260725.md`, however, a
   fixed-split universe with both Dyck sides at least half the reflection
   support occupies at most half of every nontrivial target orbit.  Three
   times one half is
   below the depth-one upper-quota capacity two.  Thus no quota-independent
   obstruction based only on orbit totals can recover the lost
   non-invariant triple packets.  The surviving theorem must use genuine
   component correlations, or a seed of multiplicity at least five, or a
   positive orbit-excess estimate combining several seed types.

The proofs and all scope restrictions follow.

## 1. Exact owner-map distance

Let \(\Omega_m\) be the set of unoriented cyclic orders on \([n]\).  An
exact middle wreath factor is a set \(F\subseteq\Omega_m\) whose cyclic
middle \(m\)-windows partition \(\binom{[n]}m\).  Thus \(|F|=t\), and every
middle set \(S\) has a unique owning wreath, denoted \(o_F(S)\).

For exact factors \(F,G\), define

\[
d_{\rm row}(F,G)
 =|F\setminus G|=|G\setminus F|
 =t-|F\cap G|,
\tag{1.1}
\]

and define the middle-owner Hamming distance

\[
d_{\rm own}(F,G)
 =\left|\left\{S\in\binom{[n]}m:o_F(S)\ne o_G(S)\right\}\right|.
\tag{1.2}
\]

### Theorem 1.1 -- exact conversion

For every pair of exact factors,

\[
\boxed{d_{\rm own}(F,G)=n\,d_{\rm row}(F,G).}
\tag{1.3}
\]

#### Proof

Every common wreath \(E\in F\cap G\) owns the same \(n\) cyclic middle
windows in both factors.  These \(n|F\cap G|\) windows are pairwise
distinct, because already inside \(F\) the wreath windows partition the
middle layer.

Conversely, if one middle set has the same owner in both factors, say
\(o_F(S)=o_G(S)=E\), then the wreath \(E\) belongs to both \(F\) and \(G\).
Therefore the middle sets whose owner is unchanged are exactly the union
of the \(n\)-window families of the common wreaths.  Their number is
\(n|F\cap G|\).  Since \(W=nt\),

\[
d_{\rm own}(F,G)
 =W-n|F\cap G|
 =n(t-|F\cap G|)
 =n d_{\rm row}(F,G).
\]

There is no double counting: exact middle ownership makes the window
families of distinct wreaths in one factor disjoint. \(\square\)

For a finite exact-factor path \(F_0,\ldots,F_L\), define cumulative
owner recourse by

\[
\operatorname{Rec}_{\rm own}(F_0,\ldots,F_L)
 =\sum_{j=1}^Ld_{\rm own}(F_{j-1},F_j).
\tag{1.4}
\]

Because \(d_{\rm row}\) is half symmetric-difference distance, it is a
metric.  Theorem 1.1 therefore gives

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Rec}_{\rm own}(F_0,\ldots,F_L)
 =n\sum_{j=1}^Ld_{\rm row}(F_{j-1},F_j)
 \ge n d_{\rm row}(F_0,F_L).
}
\tag{1.5}
\]

## 2. The canonical packet barrier becomes linear in \(W\)

For completeness, the canonical packet family used here is recalled
explicitly.  In the canonical MSW factor take the three semilength-four
Dyck roots

\[
w^{(1)}=11110000,\qquad
w^{(2)}=11101000,\qquad
w^{(3)}=11001100.
\tag{2.1}
\]

Their MSW flip permutations are

\[
\begin{aligned}
\pi(w^{(1)})&=(8,2,6,4,5,3,7,1),\\
\pi(w^{(2)})&=(8,2,4,3,6,5,7,1),\\
\pi(w^{(3)})&=(4,2,3,1,8,6,7,5).
\end{aligned}
\tag{2.2}
\]

At their second internal states, direct substitution in the MSW colour
formula \(\Gamma(x_i)=x_i\cup\{a_i,b_{i-1}\}\) gives

\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
w&x_2&\Gamma(x_2)\\ \hline
11110000&\{1,3,6,8\}&\{1,3,4,5,6,8\}\\
11101000&\{1,4,5,8\}&\{1,3,4,5,6,8\}\\
11001100&\{3,4,5,6\}&\{1,3,4,5,6,8\}.
\end{array}
\tag{2.3}
\]

Write the common colour as the incidence word

\[
T_0=10111101.
\tag{2.4}
\]

If \(V\) is a Dyck word of semilength \(m-4\), MSW prefix locality keeps
all the displayed moves inside the first eight coordinates and appends
\(V\) unchanged.  Hence the three distinct canonical wreaths indexed by

\[
w^{(1)}V,\qquad w^{(2)}V,\qquad w^{(3)}V
\tag{2.5}
\]

all own the depth-one lower target

\[
S_V=\{\infty\}\cup\bigl([2m]\setminus T_0V\bigr).
\tag{2.6}
\]

Different \(V\)'s give disjoint owner triples: an MSW wreath has one Dyck
root, and a root in (2.5) uniquely records both its first eight bits and
its suffix.  Thus there are exactly \(\operatorname{Cat}_{m-4}\) pairwise
row-disjoint triples.

At depth one every balanced quota is one or two.  Under any such quota,
each intact triple contains a survival packet: choose two of its rows when
the quota is one and all three when it is two.  Therefore, for every exact
factor \(H\), every balanced fixed-window quota system \(\beta\), and
canonical MSW factor \(F_m^{\rm MSW}\), deletion of
\(d_{\rm row}(H,F_m^{\rm MSW})\) canonical rows can hit at most that many
of the disjoint triples, while inserted rows cannot destroy an intact
packet.  Hence

\[
\boxed{
\vartheta(H,\beta)+d_{\rm row}(H,F_m^{\rm MSW})
\ge \operatorname{Cat}_{m-4}.
}
\tag{2.7}
\]

Minimizing over balanced quotas preserves (2.7).  The exact ratio is

\[
\boxed{
\frac{\operatorname{Cat}_{m-4}}{\operatorname{Cat}_m}
=
\frac{(m-2)(m-1)m(m+1)}
 {16(2m-7)(2m-5)(2m-3)(2m-1)}
=\frac1{256}+O(m^{-1}).
}
\tag{2.8}
\]

Indeed, (2.8) follows immediately by inserting
\(\operatorname{Cat}_j=(j+1)^{-1}\binom{2j}j\) and cancelling eight
successive factorial factors.

Let \(H_m\) satisfy the fixed-window FSP endpoint condition

\[
\vartheta_A(H_m)=o_A(t/\sqrt m).
\tag{2.9}
\]

Equations (2.7)--(2.9) imply

\[
d_{\rm row}(F_m^{\rm MSW},H_m)
\ge\left(\frac1{256}-o(1)\right)t.
\tag{2.10}
\]

Theorem 1.1 and (1.5) now give, for every exact-factor path from the
canonical factor to \(H_m\), with no restriction on its coordinate
support or moves,

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Rec}_{\rm own}
\ge d_{\rm own}(F_m^{\rm MSW},H_m)
\ge\left(\frac1{256}-o(1)\right)W.
}
\tag{2.11}
\]

Thus a route which measures switch cost by changed exact-middle owner
assignments cannot both start at the canonical factor, end at an FSP
factor, and have \(o(W)\) switch cost.  The stronger audited cube-wide
constant \(\delta_{\rm inv}\) from
`GLOBAL_SEED_NATIVE_CUBE_INVARIANT_OBSTRUCTION_20260725.md` similarly
upgrades to \((\delta_{\rm inv}-o(1))W\) owner distance from the entire
native cube.

Equation (2.11) does **not** assert that a literal MTF bridge or reset must
pay one letter for every changed owner.  A separate compression theorem
could in principle realize many owner changes with one short physical
operation.  The proved obstruction is to \(o(W)\) ownership recourse, and
to any reset accounting which lower-bounds that recourse by a fixed
positive multiple.

## 3. Orbit excess for moving target labels

The invariant-target method uses only singleton target orbits.  The next
lemma is the exact extension to arbitrary target orbits.

Consider an adaptive exact-factor path built from component switches under
coordinate transpositions \(\tau_1,\ldots,\tau_L\), and put

\[
\Gamma=\langle\tau_1,\ldots,\tau_L\rangle\le S_n.
\tag{3.1}
\]

The row-lineage lemma says that there is a bijection \(\phi:F_0\to F_L\)
such that

\[
\phi(E)=g_EE\qquad\text{for some }g_E\in\Gamma.
\tag{3.2}
\]

This remains true when an overlay has common rows.  In one step, the left
row \(E\) and right row \(\tau E\) lie in the same ownership component,
because some middle window of \(E\) contains both or neither endpoint of
\(\tau\).  Choosing a component side therefore chooses exactly one of
\(E,\tau E\).  Common uncoloured rows may be cancelled and kept fixed;
the componentwise descendant maps on the remaining rows, together with
the identity on the common rows, are still bijective.  Composing the
one-step bijections proves (3.2).

Tags may come from one controlled depth or from several depths; target
orbits are always taken inside one rank.  A tagged owner family is a
collection

\[
\mathcal I=\{(E_i,S_i)\}_i
\tag{3.3}
\]

such that \(E_i\in F_0\) owns \(S_i\), no pair \((E_i,S_i)\) is repeated,
and every source row occurs in at most \(D\) tags.  For a \(\Gamma\)-orbit
\(O\) of target labels, put

\[
T_O=|\{i:S_i\in O\}|.
\tag{3.4}
\]

At the endpoint, the descendant \(\phi(E_i)=g_{E_i}E_i\) owns
\(g_{E_i}S_i\in O\).  Thus the number of tagged owner incidences in each
orbit is invariant, even though their individual target labels move.

Let \(\beta(S)\) be an arbitrary integral quota and put

\[
B_O=\sum_{S\in O}\beta(S),
\qquad
R=\max_S(\beta(S)+1).
\tag{3.5}
\]

### Theorem 3.1 -- moving-label orbit packet bound

Every integral deletion family which makes the endpoint factor quota-safe
has size at least

\[
\boxed{\frac1D\sum_O(T_O-B_O)_+.}
\tag{3.6}
\]

Every fractional survival-packet cover \(x\) of the endpoint satisfies

\[
\boxed{
\|x\|_1
\ge\frac1D
 \sum_{S:h_S\ge\beta(S)+1}\frac{h_S}{\beta(S)+1}
\ge\frac1{DR}\sum_O(T_O-B_O)_+.
}
\tag{3.7}
\]

#### Proof

For an endpoint target \(S\), let \(h_S\) be the number of tagged
descendant rows which own \(S\).  One endpoint row occurs in the tagged
owner sets of at most \(D\) targets, because its unique source row had at
most \(D\) tags and lineage is bijective.  Also

\[
\sum_{S\in O}h_S=T_O.
\tag{3.8}
\]

An integral quota-safe deletion must remove at least
\((h_S-\beta(S))_+\) tagged rows at target \(S\).  After summing over
targets, one deleted row has been counted at most \(D\) times.  Therefore
\(D|B|\) is at least the sum of these positive parts.  Using

\[
\sum_{S\in O}(h_S-\beta(S))_+
\ge\left(\sum_{S\in O}h_S-B_O\right)_+
=(T_O-B_O)_+
\]

proves (3.6).

For the fractional statement, suppose \(h_S\ge k_S:=\beta(S)+1\).
Every \(k_S\)-subset of the \(h_S\) tagged owners is an actual survival
packet.  Sum its fractional-cover inequalities over all such subsets.
Every tagged vertex occurs in \(\binom{h_S-1}{k_S-1}\) subsets, so

\[
\sum_{E\text{ tagged at }S}x_E
\ge
\frac{\binom{h_S}{k_S}}
     {\binom{h_S-1}{k_S-1}}
=\frac{h_S}{k_S}
\ge\frac{(h_S-\beta(S))_+}{R}.
\tag{3.9}
\]

When \(h_S\le\beta(S)\), the last positive part is zero.  Sum (3.9) over
all targets.  Each endpoint row weight occurs at most \(D\) times on the
left, so that left side is at most \(D\|x\|_1\).  Now use the same
positive-part inequality as above.  This proves (3.7). \(\square\)

At depth one, balanced quotas lie in \(\{1,2\}\), so \(R=3\) and
\(B_O\le2|O|\).  If a row-disjoint family has three tagged owners for each
of \(r_O\) seed targets in orbit \(O\), then \(D=1\), and Theorem 3.1 gives

\[
\boxed{
\vartheta\ge
\frac13\sum_O(3r_O-2|O|)_+.
}
\tag{3.10}
\]

For a singleton orbit with one triple, the first bound in (3.7) gives one
when the quota is two and \(3/2\) when it is one, recovering the complete
fixed-target packet constraint.  The coarser orbit-only display (3.10)
gives merely \(1/3\), because it deliberately forgets the endpoint load
distribution and retains only total orbit capacity.  Its useful new feature
is that positive orbit excess can remain after target invariance disappears.

## 4. Why one suspended triple seed has no detected non-invariant orbit excess

This section audits the nonaligned reflection support at the exact orbit
level.  The conclusion concerns a fixed-split target universe and an
orbit-total relaxation; it is not an endpoint-factor construction.

Retain the lower target seed

\[
S_0=01000010.
\tag{4.1}
\]

Fix \(u,v\ge0\) with \(u+v=m-4\), and define the fixed-split suspended
target universe

\[
\mathcal A_{u,v}
=\{\ell(U,V)=US_0\overline V:
      U\in\mathcal D_u,\ V\in\mathcal D_v\}.
\tag{4.2}
\]

For fixed \(u,v\), the displayed word uniquely determines \(U,V\), so
(4.2) is a set rather than a marked multiset.

Let \(1\le s\le\min(2u,2v)\), and let

\[
\Gamma_s
=\left\langle(i,2m+1-i):1\le i\le s\right\rangle
\cong(C_2)^s.
\tag{4.3}
\]

Put \(V^*=\operatorname{rev}(\overline V)\in\mathcal D_v\).  For
\(\ell(U,V)\), write

\[
a=\operatorname{pref}_s(U),
\qquad
b=\operatorname{pref}_s(V^*).
\tag{4.4}
\]

The \(i\)-th generator of \(\Gamma_s\) exchanges \(a_i,b_i\).  Let

\[
p=|\{i:(a_i,b_i)=(1,0)\}|,
\qquad
q=|\{i:(a_i,b_i)=(0,1)\}|,
\qquad
d=p+q.
\tag{4.5}
\]

Equal pairs do not change.  The \(d\) unequal pairs may be exchanged
independently, so the target orbit has exactly \(2^d\) elements.

### Lemma 4.1 -- half-orbit bound

If \(d>0\), then

\[
\boxed{
|\Gamma_s\ell(U,V)\cap\mathcal A_{u,v}|
\le\binom d p\le2^{d-1}
=\frac12|\Gamma_s\ell(U,V)|.
}
\tag{4.6}
\]

If \(d=0\), the orbit is a singleton and the target is
\(\Gamma_s\)-invariant.

#### Proof

Exchange a subset of the unequal coordinate pairs.  The number of ones in
the first \(2u\)-bit block remains \(u\), as required for the transformed
first block to be a Dyck word of semilength \(u\), only if the number of
exchanged \((1,0)\) pairs equals the number of exchanged \((0,1)\) pairs.
The number of exchange subsets satisfying this necessary balance condition
is

\[
\sum_j\binom pj\binom qj
=\binom{p+q}p
=\binom dp,
\tag{4.7}
\]

by Vandermonde's identity.  Nonnegativity of both transformed Dyck paths
can only reduce the count, proving the first inequality in (4.6).

For \(d>0\), every binomial coefficient in row \(d\) is at most half the
row sum \(2^d\).  Equality occurs for \(d=1\), and also at the central
coefficient for \(d=2\); otherwise the inequality is strict.  This proves
the second inequality.  When \(d=0\), every exchanged coordinate pair is
equal and every generator fixes the target. \(\square\)

Now take any row-disjoint family of genuine canonical three-owner seed
triples whose targets lie in one fixed-split universe \(\mathcal A_{u,v}\).
For every nontrivial orbit \(O\), Lemma 4.1 gives

\[
r_O\le\frac{|O|}{2},
\qquad
3r_O\le\frac32|O|\le2|O|.
\tag{4.8}
\]

Thus the quota-independent lower-bound summand in (3.10) vanishes on every
nontrivial orbit covered by the hypotheses of Lemma 4.1.  This does not say
that the actual endpoint orbit excess vanishes; it says that row lineage and
these orbit totals alone do not force a positive contribution.  Only
singleton invariant orbits are detected by this particular triple-seed
orbit-total bound.

There is no hidden shortage of upper depth-one quotas in the corresponding
relaxation.  A row-disjoint triple family uses at most \(t\) source rows,
so after arbitrary rowwise target moves it touches at most \(t\) target
labels.  The number of quota-two labels available is

\[
\rho_1=W-\binom n{m-1}
=\frac{2W}{m+2}
=\frac{2n}{m+2}t>t.
\tag{4.9}
\]

Hence a relaxed assignment may put quota two on every touched target.
Since (4.8) gives enough orbit capacity, the three tracked descendants per
seed can be distributed with load at most two inside each nontrivial orbit.

The last distribution is an **orbit-total relaxation**, not a claim about
an actual exact-factor endpoint.  It allows the group element attached to
each tagged row to be chosen independently.  Genuine component signs may
correlate those choices; arbitrary rowwise choices may also fail row
distinctness or exact middle ownership.  Therefore Lemma 4.1 does not
construct an FSP factor and does not refute a component-correlation
obstruction.  It proves precisely that row lineage, orbit membership,
balanced quota counts, and the canonical three-owner seed alone cannot
force a non-invariant packet.

## 5. Exact surviving statements

The results leave two sharply scoped routes.

1. **Arbitrary-support owner travel.**  Starting from the canonical MSW
   factor, any FSP endpoint requires \(\Omega(W)\) exact-middle owner
   recourse by (2.11).  A proposed \(o(W)\) signing theorem must therefore
   start outside that basin, change its cost notion, or prove a physical
   compression which realizes \(\Omega(W)\) ownership changes with
   \(o(W)\) literal MTF connector cost.

2. **Moving-label orbit excess.**  For a tagged genuine seed family and a
   generated coordinate group \(\Gamma\), prove

   \[
   \sum_O(T_O-B_O)_+=\Omega_A(t/\sqrt m)
   \tag{5.1}
   \]

   with a positive fixed-\(A\) lower constant (more generally, prove that
   this sum is not \(o_A(t/\sqrt m)\)) to obstruct FSP through Theorem 3.1,
   or prove it is small and then use
   genuinely realizable component correlations to disperse the remaining
   tags.  For depth-one \(h\)-owner seeds occupying at most half of each
   nontrivial orbit, orbit totals can force overload against quota two only
   when \(h>4\).  Thus multiplicity five is the first possible uniform
   non-invariant seed threshold.  Alternatively one must combine several
   seed types in the same target orbits or exploit constraints beyond orbit
   totals.

The first item is a genuine exact-factor no-go at the target \(W\)-scale.
The second identifies the exact statement which survives the logarithmic
nonaligned-support escape, without mistaking a relaxed rowwise orbit
assignment for an integral exact factor.
