# Same-parity Pascal task birth: one-gap compound bundling and the exact private-socket gate

**Date:** 2026-08-02
**Status:** exact compound-bundling theorem for the one-star/persistent-
aperture task specification, a sharp payload lower bound, and an exact
lifted path-cover formulation with Hall cut projection for the remaining
global residual gate.  The scalar Pascal
recurrence alone does not control that gate.  No all-dimensional Pascal
host, upper/source antecedent, compiler, or word is claimed.

## 0. Verdict

Literal casualties are not compound tasks.  After cancelling the declared
child baseline and every admissibly transported inherited row, task birth is
counted by independently rooted changed-support modules, not by the number
of requirement rows carried by a module.

For the one-cell Pascal pivot there is at most one such rooted module.  All
affected
owner rows, immediate-upper rows, deadline-qualified deeper-upper rows,
bi-history coordinates, cap current/debt coordinates, the carried exterior
`J` occurrence, and the unique star address belong to one terminal-atomic
compound task specification.  Thus the exact **birth** bound is

\[
                         B_{\rm pivot}^{\rm root}\le1,     \tag{0.1}
\]

with equality whenever at least one uncancelled pivot row is exported.

This does not prove that its completed-ticket atlas is nonempty.  Conditional
on one accepted terminal macro carrying the full physical `J`, backup,
history, topology, and protected-resource support in at most `Ld` resources,
the accepted service count is also one.  If that macro does not exist, the
one task is blocked; it does not split into many births.

If one deliberately refuses to join the address-distinct exterior `J`
socket to the aperture port, the proof-safe count is two rather than one.
This is the only extra root in the frozen one-aperture ledger:

\[
                         B_{\rm pivot}^{\rm root,uncoupled}\le2. \tag{0.2}
\]

The task specification in (0.1) is not an `O(1)`-size atomic row.  On every
sufficiently large deadline plateau there is a sharp example with `d-1` distinct
last-witness casualties at the one insertion gap.  They form the diagonal
of one suffix--prefix boundary relation and require `Theta(d)` literal
payload.  Its directly changed rows use only the `2d+1`-position pivot
collar, so the obstruction itself is exactly within the allowed `O(d)`
payload/support convention.  This collar statement does not bound the full
support of a completed repair ticket.  It is not a counterexample to (0.1).

There is nevertheless no unconditional global `H` theorem in the frozen
scalar state.  The four-sector recurrence supplies `c+4d` endpoint-labelled
residual pieces and three newborn singleton targets, but explicitly does
not determine their endpoint-containment graph or path forest.  The exact
missing coordinate is the number of independently exported residual path
ends in a capacity/product-state-respecting path forest.  Section 6 denotes
the exact lifted optimum by `kappa_sock^*`.  Its uncapacitated endpoint
projection has the Hall lower bound

\[
 \underline\kappa_{\rm sock}
   =\max_{X\subseteq\mathcal P}
                 \bigl(|X|-|N_{B^0_{\rm res}}(X)|\bigr).  \tag{0.3}
\]

This is a socket count, not a mask count.  If one completed ticket contains
at most `Ld` independent private sockets for one absolute constant `L`, then

 \[
 H_{\rm res}\ge
       \left\lceil{\kappa_{\rm sock}^*\over Ld}\right\rceil
       \ge
       \left\lceil{\underline\kappa_{\rm sock}\over Ld}\right\rceil. \tag{0.4}
 \]

The scalar identities do not bound either quantity.  A
scalar/deadline-admissible
endpoint-bank countermodel has `m` mutually private path ends, giving
`kappa_sock^*=underline kappa_sock=m` and hence
`Omega(sqrt(m)/L)` compound tasks because `d=Theta(sqrt(m))`; for the fixed
absolute `L` convention this is `Omega(sqrt(m))`.  This is a countermodel to
deduction from the marginal recurrences, not a claimed four-sector image of
an authenticated Pascal factor.

The exact additional global hypothesis is therefore not “assume the number
of masks is bounded.”  It consists of the lifted socket expansion

\[
                         \kappa_{\rm sock}^*\le LdH_0,    \tag{0.5a}
\]

whose necessary Hall cuts are

\[
 |X|-|N_{B^0_{\rm res}}(X)|\le LdH_0
                  \qquad(X\subseteq\mathcal P),           \tag{0.5b}
\]

together with a fully union-replayed accepting supercolumn containing at
most `H_0` completed product-state tickets.  Under (0.5a), baseline closure,
that accepted supercolumn, and a nonempty accepted pivot atlas, the accepted
service bound is

\[
                         H\le H_0+1,                       \tag{0.6}
\]

or `H<=H_0+2` if `J` is intentionally kept as a separate task.  At the task-
specification level the same inequalities hold without the atlas-nonempty
claim.  The mixed-tag addendum below shows that the standard indivisible
four-sector interface cannot satisfy a uniform `O(d)` version of (0.5a): a
viable global expansion theorem must first split/macro-absorb the mixed
pieces or plant new internal receiver capacity, and must then prove the
full lifted inequality rather than only (0.5b).

## 1. Tasks, payloads, and private sockets

Fix one proposed same-parity transition `pi`, one literal old matching, and
one independently declared child baseline.  Use the disjoint typed
requirement universe

\[
 \mathscr R=\mathscr O\ \dot\cup\ \mathscr U_1\ \dot\cup\
 \mathscr U_{\ge2}\ \dot\cup\ \mathscr H\ \dot\cup\
 \mathscr C\ \dot\cup\ \mathscr J\ \dot\cup\ \mathscr S. \tag{1.1}
\]

The types are literal owner/facet, immediate upper, deeper upper/deadline,
bidirectional history, cap current/debt, exterior aperture, and active-star
rows.  Occurrence addresses are part of the keys.

Perform the following cancellation before counting anything.

1. Delete every baseline-internal row discharged by the declared child
   construction.
2. Delete every inherited row whose selected occurrence has an admissible
   full-block lift.
3. Identify two remaining diagnoses only when one completed physical action
   must discharge both.  Equality of underlying set values is not enough.

A **causal root** is a changed source gap, changed owner edge, or declared
private exterior socket.  Join two roots when a literal transition row, a
Pascal port, or one terminal cap/history state requires them in the same
physical action.  The connected rooted modules are the compound task
specifications.  Every requirement incident with a module is payload of that
task.

This convention separates four quantities:

\[
\begin{aligned}
 B_{\rm lit}&=\#\{\text{uncancelled literal rows}\},\\
 \kappa_{\rm sock}^*&=\min\#\{\text{independent private sockets in an exact
                                  lifted path cover}\},\\
 B_{\rm cmp}&=\#\{\text{rooted compound task specifications born}\},\\
 H_{\rm acc}&=\min\#\{\text{accepted completed tickets servicing them}\}.
\end{aligned}                                               \tag{1.2}
\]

Put `H_acc=infinity` when no completed service exists.  There is no
inequality `B_cmp>=B_lit/O(1)` when tickets are allowed
`O(d)` payload.  If every ticket has at most `Ld` support, there is instead
the proof-safe socket inequality (0.4).  Several rows sharing one gap are
not several sockets.

An incompatible cap/history pair inside one rooted module does not silently
turn into two births.  It means that the atlas of completed tickets for that
task is empty, unless a terminal-atomic compound path returns the product
state.  A genuine negative task-count theorem must exhibit either

* more than `O(d)` independent private sockets per bounded number of tasks;
  or
* a growing family of socket groups which are pairwise incompatible in the
  accepted lifted state.

The plateau bank in Section 4 has neither property.

The completed cap/history recurrence is what makes terminal-atomic grouping
formal.  For a packet word `P`, write its label as

\[
                         \Xi(P)=(z_P,\beta_P,\mathbb W_P), \tag{1.3}
\]

where `z` is cap current, `beta` is maximum prefix debt, and `mathbb W` is
the paired positive/negative boundary-history relation.  Serial composition
is

\[
 \Xi(A)\Xi(B)=
 \bigl(z_A+z_B,\ \max\{\beta_A,\beta_B-z_A\},
                   \mathbb W_B\circ\mathbb W_A\bigr).     \tag{1.4}
\]

Thus several primitive cap or history disturbances belong to one task when
their whole word has one accepted terminal label.  They are not separate
tasks merely because an intermediate primitive is blocked.  Conversely,
failure of terminal acceptance is recorded as an empty completed-ticket
menu or as an incompatibility row in Section 6.

## 2. Exact one-block boundary normal form

Let a word be written

\[
                         W^-=L\,P\,R,                     \tag{2.1}
\]

and replace the consecutive block `P` by a consecutive block `Q`, leaving
`L,R` fixed.  An interval affected by the replacement is exactly one of:

1. an interval internal to the replaced block;
2. a suffix of `L`, followed by a prefix of the block;
3. a suffix of the block, followed by a prefix of `R`; or
4. a suffix of `L`, the whole block, and a prefix of `R`.

Thus its value has one of the exact forms

\[
\begin{array}{ll}
 \operatorname{OR}(I),&I\subseteq P\text{ or }Q,\\[2mm]
 S_u(L)\cup P_v(P),&\text{or }S_u(L)\cup P_v(Q),\\
 S_u(P)\cup P_v(R),&\text{or }S_u(Q)\cup P_v(R),\\
 S_u(L)\cup\operatorname{OR}(P)\cup P_v(R),
   &\text{or }S_u(L)\cup\operatorname{OR}(Q)\cup P_v(R).
                                                               \tag{2.2}
\end{array}
\]

Here `S_u` and `P_v` denote suffix and prefix unions.  Conversely every
displayed expression whose endpoints exist is a physical interval.

### Theorem 2.1 (one-block compound interface)

For a fixed width/deadline band, every old/new row changed by one block
replacement belongs to one of four boundary relations: internal, left-ray,
right-ray, or two-sided grid.  All four have the same causal root, namely
the replaced block.  Hence they form one compound task specification.

#### Proof

An interval meets a consecutive set of word positions.  If it leaves the
replaced block on only one side, it contains a prefix or suffix of that
block.  If it leaves on both sides, it contains the whole block.  These are
the four cases in (2.2), and they are mutually exhaustive.  Every row in
(2.2) is incident with the same changed block, so the causal-root quotient
has one component. \(\square\)

The theorem counts task specifications, not targetwise repair cells.  An
arbitrary two-sided grid may have `Theta(d^2)` literal entries and can exceed
an `O(d)` ticket payload.  The one-star plateau below is smaller: only one
diagonal of the grid leaves the deadline band, so its payload is `d-1`.

## 3. One insertion gap has one rooted task and `d-1` possible deadline rows

Specialize (2.1) to insertion of one nonempty letter `X` at a gap between
`L` and `R`.  Let the last old left letter and the first old right letter
have union containing `X`.  An old crossing interval using `u>=1` left
letters and `v>=1` right letters has value

\[
                         S_u(L)\cup P_v(R).              \tag{3.1}
\]

Its lifted interval has value

\[
                S_u(L)\cup X\cup P_v(R)
                    =S_u(L)\cup P_v(R),                  \tag{3.2}
\]

but its width rises from `u+v` to `u+v+1`.

### Theorem 3.1 (one-gap terminal-atomic bundling)

On a deadline plateau `d'=d`, the only full-block lifts rejected solely by
the width deadline are the old crossing intervals satisfying

\[
                         u+v=d.                           \tag{3.3}
\]

There are exactly `d-1` possible indexed rows.  Every actual casualty among
them, together with every owner, palette, cap, history, exterior-`J`, and
star row caused by the same insertion, is payload of one gap-rooted
compound task.  The entire deadline bank is named by the last `d-1` letters
of `L`, the inserted letter, and the first `d-1` letters of `R`, hence has
`O(d)` support.

On a deadline jump `d'=d+1`, the rows in (3.3) remain admissible and this
deadline contribution is zero.

#### Proof

Every old interval of width at most `d-1` lifts to width at most `d`; every
old interval of width `d` not crossing the gap is unchanged.  Hence (3.3)
is necessary and sufficient for rejection by width alone.  The positive
solutions `(u,v)` are `(1,d-1),...,(d-1,1)`, giving `d-1` rows.

All these intervals meet the same insertion gap.  The new owner windows
and their immediate palettes also meet that gap.  The cap/history state is
the terminal label of that same physical action, while the Pascal port
joins its exterior `J` occurrence to the aperture edge.  The causal-root
graph is therefore connected.  The displayed collar contains every
deadline row, proving the support bound.  On a jump, the lifted width is
the new deadline. \(\square\)

This theorem is the corrected task-birth statement.  A proof that the task
has a nonempty atlas still needs one completed physical ticket carrying all
its product-state rows.  Atlas nonemptiness is a service/expansion gate, not
an additional task birth.

## 4. The sharp plateau construction is a payload lower bound

Put

\[
 W_m={2m-1\choose m},\qquad \Lambda_m=4^{m-1}-1,
 \qquad\tau_q={q+1\choose2},                            \tag{4.1}
\]

and let `d_m` be the least `q` with

\[
                         qW_m+\tau_q\ge\Lambda_m.       \tag{4.2}
\]

The exact odd Pascal recurrence gives

\[
                         d_{m+1}-d_m\in\{0,1\}.         \tag{4.3}
\]

The central binomial estimate gives

\[
                         d_m={\sqrt{\pi m}\over2}(1+o(1)). \tag{4.4}
\]

Consequently there are infinitely many plateaus `d_(m+1)=d_m`, and their
deadlines are unbounded.  Otherwise all sufficiently late increments in
(4.3) would be one, contradicting (4.4).

Fix a sufficiently large plateau and write `d=d_m=d_(m+1)`.  Take a core
`K` of size `m-1`, private coordinates

\[
 u_{-d+1},\ldots,u_0,u_1,\ldots,u_d,                  \tag{4.5}
\]

and a separator `z`, all disjoint.  Since `d=O(sqrt(m))`, assume
`2d<=m-1`, so they fit in the parent ground.  Put

\[
                         A_i=K\cup\{u_i\},              \tag{4.6}
\]

and use the local word

\[
 (A_{-d+1},\ldots,A_0,A_1,\ldots,A_d).                \tag{4.7}
\]

For `2-d<=s<=0`, define

\[
 T_s=K\cup\{u_s,\ldots,u_{s+d-1}\}.                   \tag{4.8}
\]

There are `d-1` such targets.  In (4.7), `T_s` has exactly one interval
witness, namely `(A_s,...,A_(s+d-1))`, of width `d`.  To make uniqueness
global, append one-letter occurrences of every other required target,
separating them and (4.7) by `{z}`, and omit the one-letter occurrences of
the `T_s`.

Insert the single star letter `K` between `A_0` and `A_1`.  Every old OR is
preserved, because `K` is contained in every crossing union.  Each interval
witnessing (4.8) crosses the gap and now has width `d+1`.  Conversely an
interval with value `T_s` must contain every private `u_i` in (4.8), hence
all `d` old endpoint-to-endpoint letters and, by contiguity, the inserted
`K`; separators exclude every remote interval.  Thus all `d-1` targets lose
their deadline-`d` witnesses.

### Theorem 4.1 (sharp payload, one rooted task specification)

The construction has exactly `d-1` deadline casualties, the universal
maximum for one insertion gap.  Its compound task-specification birth count
is nevertheless one.  Any accepted ticket for this task must carry
`Omega(d)` named rows or an equivalent lossless ladder encoding.

#### Proof

The last-witness argument above proves the lower bound.  A gap is crossed
by exactly `d-1` length-`d` intervals, proving sharpness.  Every casualty is
incident with the same gap, so Theorem 3.1 gives one rooted specification.
\(\square\)

The targets have rank

\[
                         |T_s|=m+d-1,                    \tag{4.9}
\]

so for `d>=3` they are deep-upper rows, not the immediate cap, rank-`m-1`
aperture, or star row.  This prevents cross-type double charging while not
turning the rows into separate tasks.

For the exact `k=17 -> 19` scalar step, `m=9`,

\[
 W_9=24310,\quad \Lambda_9=65535,\quad d_9=3,
 \quad \sigma_9=7401,                                  \tag{4.10}
\]

and `d_10=3`.  The sharp bank therefore has two literal casualties in one
compound task.  This numerical calibration uses no claim about the frozen
`k=17` factor, which remains nonresident/`NEED_CEGAR`.

## 5. No-double-charge ledger for the one-aperture module

The following table counts roots, not rows.

| coordinate | literal payload | compound-task contribution |
|---|---:|---:|
| owner/facet | the `d+1` pivot owner windows and their exact owner rows | `0` beyond the gap task |
| immediate upper | the `d` adjacent owner-union colours | `0` beyond the gap task |
| deep upper/deadline | at most the `d-1` plateau diagonal rows from Theorem 3.1 | `0` beyond the gap task |
| bi-history | the positive and negative boundary relations of the terminal macro | `0`; terminal labels of the same task |
| cap debt/backups | `(z,beta)` and the occurrence-labelled backup endpoints used by the macro | `0`; product-state labels/support of the same task |
| exterior `J` | one address-distinct occurrence, joined to the aperture transition by the Pascal port | birth `0`, inventory `1` |
| active star | the unique inserted address after parent consolidation | birth `0`, inventory `1` |

The exterior occurrence and star address are distinct even if their set
values coincide.  They are not double-counted because task grouping follows
the literal port relation, not equality of values.

If no completed ticket is allowed to contain the exterior `J` socket, place
that occurrence in its own rooted task.  The count becomes two, as in (0.2),
not `d` or `d+1`.  If the three singleton births `{x},{y},{x,y}` from the
four-sector recurrence are intentionally excluded from the declared child
baseline, they add at most three further constant tasks.  In the prepared
Pascal recurrence they belong to residual baseline completion and are not
exported sidecar births.

This ledger does not assert that the global owner/q1 factor, cap-compatible
exterior occurrence, history collar, or common compiler exists.  It proves
that failure of one of those rows makes the one compound task unavailable;
it does not manufacture many tasks from one root.

## 6. Exact residual path-cover and cut formulation

The active pivot is not the only object in a whole four-sector transition.
Let `mathcal P` be the nonempty endpoint-labelled residual chain pieces left
after the top-containing child chains and every certified baseline
contraction have been fixed.  Give every available internal child root one
right vertex for each **independent path-capacity slot**.  Several certified
literal gaps on the same slot are labelled alternative edges to that one
right vertex; they are not cloned into independent sockets.  Unused length
inside one root chain is a capacity bound on its attached path, not a bank of
independent ports.  Form the resulting unit-slot acyclic successor
projection

\[
                         B^0_{\rm res}=(\mathcal P^-,V^+;E). \tag{6.1}
\]

The right shore `V^+` consists of successor copies of residual pieces and
the independent internal attachment slots.  Put a port-labelled edge
`P^-Q^+` when the
endpoint projection permits `P` to be followed by `Q`; in particular

\[
                         \max P\subseteq\min Q,           \tag{6.2}
\]

and a rank layer or fixed address order makes the arc acyclic.  Edges to
literal root ports have the analogous endpoint meaning.  At this stage the
graph deliberately forgets cumulative path mass and product state.

A matching in (6.1) chooses at most one successor for each piece and at most
one predecessor for each successor/root port.  Acyclicity turns it into an
uncapacitated vertex-disjoint path forest.  Every unmatched left vertex is
the terminal end of a projected residual path which needs an exterior root
socket.

### Theorem 6.1 (exact Hall deficiency of the endpoint projection)

The least number of independent exterior root sockets in the
uncapacitated endpoint projection is

\[
 \boxed{
 \underline\kappa_{\rm sock}=|\mathcal P|-\nu(B^0_{\rm res})
   =\max_{X\subseteq\mathcal P}
                    \bigl(|X|-|N_{B^0_{\rm res}}(X)|\bigr).} \tag{6.3}
\]

#### Proof

A maximum matching internally continues or terminates exactly
`nu(B_res^0)` residual paths.  Every unmatched left piece needs one new
terminal socket, and adding one private terminal neighbour to each unmatched
piece completes the path cover.  Thus the first equality holds.  The second
is the deficiency form of Hall's theorem. \(\square\)

Formula (6.3) is a lower bound on the physical socket demand.  It is exact
for an already state-materialized unit-capacity DAG, but ordinary matching
does not enforce the cumulative mass of a whole residual path.  The frozen
four-sector theorem supplies neither independent capacity slots nor that
state materialization.

For the exact lifted formulation, let `mathcal C_0` be the finite family of
all literal internally rooted residual chains.  A member records its ordered
pieces, its single certified terminal port, its cumulative owner/boundary
capacity, and its complete state word

\[
 (\text{ordered pieces};\text{remaining capacity};z,\beta;
       \mathcal R^+,\mathcal R^-;J,\text{topology}).       \tag{6.4}
\]

Let `mathcal C_1` be the analogous family of feasible chains which terminate
at one declared exterior socket rather than an internal port.  With
`a_{qC}` the use of any protected resource `q` by chain `C` and `b_q` its
available multiplicity, the exact minimum number of independent exterior
sockets is the finite chain-column problem

\[
 \boxed{
 \kappa_{\rm sock}^*=\min\left\{
    \sum_{C\in\mathcal C_1}x_C:
 \begin{array}{ll}
  \displaystyle
  \sum_{C\in\mathcal C_0\cup\mathcal C_1:P\in C}x_C=1
       & (P\in\mathcal P),\\[3mm]
  \displaystyle
  \sum_C a_{qC}x_C\le b_q
       & (\text{protected resources }q),\\
  x_C\in\{0,1\}
 \end{array}\right\}.}                                 \tag{6.5}
\]

The equality row is the physical path partition: every residual piece is
used exactly once.  Capacity, cap, history, topology, and port acceptance are
inside each column; shared protected resources are enforced by the second
row.  Every physical solution gives such columns and every selected column
family gives the corresponding path forest, so (6.5) is exact.  Forgetting
the lifted rows maps it to (6.1), hence

\[
                 \underline\kappa_{\rm sock}
                       \le\kappa_{\rm sock}^*.             \tag{6.6}
\]

Put `kappa_sock^*=infinity` when (6.5) is infeasible.

Now let `mathcal T` be the family of literal completed compound tickets.
Each `T` contains at most `Ld` exterior chains from `mathcal C_1`, includes
their full product-state returns, and declares every resource it uses.  The
following compact integer formulation is exact on a **prepared commuting
subatlas**: every resource-compatible selected family has no unrecorded
cross term, its exact union replay returns the same full cap/history/
topology/voltage/aperture state, and its aggregate cap cuts pass.  On that
face, replace the exterior-chain columns in (6.5) by ticket columns:

\[
 \boxed{
 H_{\rm res}^{\rm comm}=\min\left\{
      \sum_{T\in\mathcal T}y_T:
 \begin{array}{ll}
  \displaystyle
  \sum_{C\in\mathcal C_0:P\in C}x_C
       +\sum_{T:P\in\operatorname{pieces}(T)}y_T=1
       &(P\in\mathcal P),\\[3mm]
  \displaystyle
  \sum_C a_{qC}x_C+\sum_Ta_{qT}y_T\le b_q
       &(\text{capacity-limited occurrence/state resources }q),\\
  x_C,y_T\in\{0,1\}.
 \end{array}\right\}.}                                  \tag{6.7}
\]

Put `H_res^comm=infinity` when (6.7) is infeasible.

Ticket columns are whole compatible objects, not marginal menus.  Thus the
equality row prevents overlapping socket service, while the resource row
enforces cross-ticket owner, cap, history, topology, and protected-resource
capacity on the prepared commuting face.  Serial tickets with reusable
resources are represented by their ordered lifted product-state column.
Formula (6.7), unlike a bare
set cover of one maximum-matching leave, also permits a nonmaximum endpoint
matching when that produces a more compatible terminal partition.

Without the prepared-commuting property, individual completed tickets are
not enough: disjoint supports can still have topology, voltage, history, or
transported-reserve cross terms.  The fully general exact bounded-state
formulation uses replayed supercolumns.  Let `mathfrak F` be the finite
family of objects

\[
 F=(\text{an internal-chain partition};
       T_1,\ldots,T_{h(F)};
       \text{exact ordered union replay})                \tag{6.8}
\]

which cover every residual piece exactly once, satisfy every occurrence
capacity, every aggregate cap cut, both history relations, topology,
voltage, aperture/`J`, star, and terminal recursive-state row.  Then

\[
 \boxed{
                         H_{\rm res}^{\rm exact}
          =\min_{F\in\mathfrak F}h(F).}                   \tag{6.9}
\]

Put the value equal to infinity when `mathfrak F` is empty.  Enumerating
supercolumns is a definition of the finite lifted state, not a claim that a
small accepted catalogue exists.  On a prepared commuting subatlas,
(6.9) reduces to (6.7).

The support bound gives (0.4).  A growing clique of mutually incompatible
socket states gives an additional lower bound even when (6.3) is small.

The exact positive expansion hypothesis is now:

1. every reachable residual bank satisfies the projected cuts (0.5b) and
   `kappa_sock^*<=LdH_0` in (0.5a);
2. it has one fully replayed supercolumn `F` in (6.8) with `h(F)<=H_0`
   (equivalently, on the commuting face, one solution of (6.7) of cost at
   most `H_0`); and
3. every residual row outside those tickets is closed inside the declared
   child baseline.

Then (6.9) gives `H_res^exact<=H_0`, and Theorem 3.1 gives (0.6).  The Hall
cuts are the exact Farkas obstruction on the uncapacitated endpoint face;
(6.5) is the exact chain lift, and (6.9) is the exact accepted compound lift
which prevents capacity, cross-ticket history, or topology from being
hidden in those cuts.

### Corollary 6.2 (prepared-baseline birth bound)

If the declared child baseline already contains a literal capacity/state-
respecting internal chain partition from `mathcal C_0`, then
`kappa_sock^*=0` and no residual compound task is exported.  The complete
transition therefore births at most the one pivot-rooted task specification
of (0.1).  If that task is nonempty and its accepted atlas exists, one ticket
serves it; if `J` is kept uncoupled, the bound is two.

#### Proof

Choose the internal partition in (6.5), giving objective zero.  Every
residual row is baseline-internal, and Theorem 3.1 supplies the sole
remaining causal root. \(\square\)

## 7. Why the scalar recurrence cannot prove the cuts

The exact scalar state gives

\[
 W'=4W-c,\qquad \Lambda'=4\Lambda+3,qquad
 d'\in\{d,d+1\},                                      \tag{7.1}
\]

\[
 4(W+d)-(W'+d')=c+4d-d',                              \tag{7.2}
\]

and a residual bank of `c+4d` inherited pieces plus three singleton births.
Equation (7.2) is explicitly an address contraction count, not a join count.
None of (7.1)--(7.2) contains a neighbourhood in (6.3).

For a sharp projection countermodel, choose `s` private coordinates
`a_1,...,a_s` and residual one-member pieces `P_i=({a_i})`.  Make every
right-shore successor endpoint, every internal root port, and every padding
endpoint omit the entire private bank `{a_1,...,a_s}`.
Then

\[
                         N_{B^0_{\rm res}}(\{P_i:i\in I\})
                              =\varnothing,              \tag{7.3}
\]

so `underline kappa_sock=kappa_sock^*=s`.  Give each piece one feasible,
address-distinct exterior root containing its private `a_i` and a private

support resources.  The pieces have unit length and therefore pass every

\[
                         H_{\rm res}\ge
                         \left\lceil{m\over Ld_m}\right\rceil

\]

In the ticket convention of this note `L` is one fixed absolute constant,

needed if that convention is weakened.

Adding the one star and the one carried aperture as a separate connected

The smallest private-anchor pattern has two isolated pieces and two private
