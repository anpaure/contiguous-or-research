# Coxeter-flat transport authenticates change of cyclic symmetry

**Date:** 2026-08-03  
**Status:** unconditional groupoid theorem and conditional same-parity
interface.  No finite computation is used.

## 0. Outcome

The fresh-pair transition from `k` to `k+2` naturally uses the old-coordinate
rotation `C_k`, while the next transition wants the full rotation
`C_(k+2)` on the former child.  These are different subgroups of
`S_(k+2)`.  Separate flat gauges for the two cyclic groups do not identify
their packing states.

There is, however, an exact sufficient certificate.  If complete
capacity-faithful packing states admit transports for adjacent coordinate
transpositions satisfying the Coxeter involution, commutation, and braid
relations, then the transport extends uniquely to an `S_n` action groupoid.
Every subgroup action is the restriction of that one groupoid.  Hence,
while the complete physical representative is retained, changing from the
old-coordinate `C_k` description to the full `C_(k+2)` description requires
no new physical packing and creates no additional holonomy.  Calling this a
single **pure gauge** additionally requires trivial stabilizer action on
every complete fibre of a nonfree state orbit.

The conclusion is conditional on one important semantic row: the output
state must be admissible after forgetting or transporting the old fresh-pair
mark.  A seam, root, deadline, or history register that still distinguishes
that pair is not removed by group theory and needs its own authenticated
reset.

## 1. Complete packing fibres

Let `G=S_n` with simple transpositions

\[
                         s_i=(i\ i+1),\qquad 1\le i<n. \tag{1.1}
\]

Let `X` be a `G`-set of complete physical states.  For every `x in X`, let
`Omega_x` be a nonempty set of complete, occurrence-labelled,
capacity-faithful packings over `x`.

For every `i` and `x`, suppose there is a bijection

\[
 R_i(x):\Omega_x\longrightarrow\Omega_{s_ix}.        \tag{1.2}
\]

The maps retain every physical address, history, cap, guard, and packing
identity after applying the coordinate relabelling.  A projected state map
is not enough.

Assume the translated Coxeter relations:

\[
 R_i(s_ix)R_i(x)=\operatorname{id}_{\Omega_x},        \tag{1.3}
\]

\[
 R_i(s_jx)R_j(x)=R_j(s_ix)R_i(x)
 \qquad(|i-j|>1),                                    \tag{1.4}
\]

and

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 R_i(s_{i+1}s_ix)R_{i+1}(s_ix)R_i(x)
  ={}&R_{i+1}(s_is_{i+1}x)\\
    &\mathbin{}R_i(s_{i+1}x)R_{i+1}(x).
 \end{aligned}                                       \tag{1.5}
\]

Composition is read from right to left.

## 2. Exact symmetric-group gauge

### Theorem 2.1 (Coxeter extension)

For every `sigma in S_n` and `x in X`, the composite transport along a word
for `sigma` is independent of the chosen word.  It defines a bijection

\[
 R_\sigma(x):\Omega_x\longrightarrow\Omega_{\sigma x} \tag{2.1}
\]

satisfying

\[
 R_{\sigma\tau}(x)=R_\sigma(\tau x)R_\tau(x).         \tag{2.2}
\]

Thus the complete packing bundle carries a genuine `S_n` action groupoid.

### Proof

The symmetric group has the Coxeter presentation generated by the `s_i`
with exactly the relations (1.3)--(1.5).  Any two words for the same
permutation are related by insertions/deletions of these translated
relations.  Each such move preserves the composite transport by the
corresponding hypothesis, proving word independence.  Concatenating a word
for `tau` with a word for `sigma` proves (2.2). \(\square\)

### Corollary 2.2 (pure-gauge criterion)

Fix one base state `x_0` in a transitive `S_n`-orbit.  If the action on
states is free, define

\[
                         g_x=R_\sigma(x_0)            \tag{2.3}
\]

for the unique `sigma` with `sigma x_0=x`.  Then every edge transport is
of the pure-gauge form

\[
                         R_i(x)=g_{s_ix}g_x^{-1}.      \tag{2.4}
\]

For a nonfree state orbit `S_n/L`, such a representative-independent pure-
gauge trivialization exists if and only if every stabilizer element
`ell in L` acts trivially on the complete fibre over `x_0`.

### Proof

The free case is immediate from (2.2).  In the Schreier case, two choices
of `sigma` differ by a stabilizer element; trivial stabilizer holonomy makes
(2.3) well defined.  Conversely, if (2.3)--(2.4) give a well-defined pure
gauge, then for `ell in L`,

\[
                         R_\ell(x_0)=g_{x_0}g_{x_0}^{-1}
                         =\operatorname{id}_{\Omega_{x_0}},
\]

so the stabilizer action is trivial. \(\square\)

Without this condition the Coxeter relations still give a perfectly valid
`S_n` action groupoid, but its restriction over `S_n/L` carries a nontrivial
Schreier stabilizer representation and is not a pure gauge.

## 3. Change of subgroup inside one complete groupoid

Let `H,K<=S_n` be arbitrary subgroups.  Restricting (2.1) to `H` or to `K`
gives their complete packing transports.

### Theorem 3.1 (subgroup-change theorem)

The `H`- and `K`-transports are automatically compatible: both are
restrictions of the same maps `R_sigma`.  At one retained complete state,
changing only its quotient label from the `H` description to the `K`
description is the identity on its packing: it changes no physical resource
and introduces no additional continuation holonomy.

If an intermediate relabelling `pi in S_n` is desired, the authenticated
change is exactly `R_pi`; subsequent `K`-transport satisfies

\[
 R_\kappa(\pi x)R_\pi(x)
   =R_\pi(\pi^{-1}\kappa\pi x)R_{\pi^{-1}\kappa\pi}(x) \tag{3.1}
\]

for `kappa in K`.

### Proof

All assertions are instances of the composition law (2.2). \(\square\)

Both sides of (3.1) are typed maps

\[
                         \Omega_x\longrightarrow\Omega_{\kappa\pi x};
\]

the left side is \(R_{\kappa\pi}(x)\), and the right side is
\(R_{\pi(\pi^{-1}\kappa\pi)}(x)\) by (2.2).

The theorem does not say that an `H`-quotient state is a `K`-quotient state;
their orbit partitions differ.  Nor does an `H`-orbit label alone determine
the complete representative needed to form its `K`-orbit label.  It says
that, after retaining the complete physical state, both quotient
descriptions have one common authenticated lift.  If quotient sections
replace that representative by canonical orbit representatives, the
resulting stabilizer transports can be nontrivial; representative-independent
pure-gauge re-description then requires Corollary 2.2's trivial Schreier
holonomy condition.

## 4. Fresh-pair application

Put `n=k+2`.  Inside `S_n`, let

* `H=C_k` rotate the first `k` coordinates and fix the fresh ordered pair;
* `K=C_(k+2)` rotate all `k+2` coordinates.

These groups need not be conjugate and generally have different orders.
Theorem 3.1 requires no conjugacy.

### Corollary 4.1 (authenticated same-parity re-gauging)

Suppose one `k -> k+2` physical lift exports a complete child packing in an
`S_(k+2)`-flat bundle satisfying Sections 1--2.  Suppose also that the
child acceptance relation is invariant under coordinate relabelling and
either:

1. the old fresh-pair mark is **semantically dead** at output: deleting its
   register is a specified cost-free equivariant map on complete packed
   states, and acceptance, every future guard, and continuation type factor
   through that map; or
2. it is transported to the next arrow's marked interface by one declared
   `R_pi` map.

Then the old-coordinate `C_k` quotient used inside the transition and the
full `C_(k+2)` quotient used by the next transition are compatible lifts of
one physical output state.  No extra word position or packing resource is
needed for the change of cyclic group itself.  Any nontrivial mark-release
or interface-reset cost remains a separate construction charge.

### Proof

Apply Theorem 3.1 with the displayed subgroups.  In case 1, the stated
forgetful map--not group theory alone--certifies that deleting the mark
preserves the complete admissible continuation type.  In case 2, (3.1)
authenticates the declared relabelling. \(\square\)

## 5. Local proof burden

The global change-of-group row is therefore reduced to local checks:

\[
 \boxed{s_i^2=1,\qquad s_is_j=s_js_i\ (|i-j|>1),
        \qquad s_is_{i+1}s_i=s_{i+1}s_is_{i+1}}.      \tag{5.1}
\]

These identities must hold on the **complete global packing fibres**, not
merely on target values, rank profiles, or independently chosen local
packings.  They suffice for the action groupoid.  If a quotient interface
discards the complete representative and requires a pure-gauge
trivialization on a Schreier orbit, trivial stabilizer holonomy must
additionally be checked as in Corollary 2.2.

The following failures remain possible.

* A coordinate transposition may not preserve a rooted seam or deadline.
* Two local transpositions may require incompatible global packings.
* A braid may close after projection while permuting hidden addresses or
  histories.
* Forgetting the old fresh-pair mark may change future guards.

Such data must be included in `Omega_x` or moved to a bounded terminal
sidecar.  Separate flat `C_k` and `C_(k+2)` gauges do not imply this theorem.

## 6. Exact remaining construction row

Combined with the fresh-pair orbit-growth theorem, a sufficient
same-parity symmetry interface is now:

1. one `S_(k+2)`-flat complete packing groupoid, certified by the translated
   form of (5.1), together with either the retained complete representative
   or trivial stabilizer action whenever a pure-gauge quotient section is
   required;
2. a bounded-charge mark-release or marked-interface transport;
3. capacity-faithful exact-period gates under the old-coordinate `C_k`
   action; and
4. a uniform per-period defect bound.

The orbit-growth theorem then forces the central-band gates to saturate,
while Corollary 4.1 authenticates the output for the next full rotation.
What remains unproved is the construction of such a complete symmetric
packing bundle together with the upper-complete carrier, protected lower
signature, literal router, and regenerative output.
