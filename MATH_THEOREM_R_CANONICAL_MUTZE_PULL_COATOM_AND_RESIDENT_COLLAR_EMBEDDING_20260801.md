# Canonical Mütze pulls versus coatom and resident-square packets

Date: 2026-08-01  
Lane: R, additive-constant topology/packet bridge  
Status: exact GMN forest-extension theorem, exact abstract rail identities,
an occurrence-level embedding criterion, a conditional prospective packet
theorem, and two sharp provenance/filtered-family obstructions.  No
compiler/common-cap theorem is asserted.

## 0. Verdict

The proposed Mütze bridge has one valid part and two missing identifications.

* In the canonical Gregor--Mütze--Nummenpalo (`GMN`) pull family, the
  alternating incidence hexagons are pairwise edge-disjoint and have the
  required noninterleaving property.  A prescribed labelled subset extends
  to a canonical gluing spanning tree **if and only if** its auxiliary
  plane-tree edges form a graphic forest.
* Neither the mixed-coatom packet nor the resident square collar is
  literally one of those six-cycles.  The coatom packet contracts to the two
  decorated Johnson rails of one incidence hexagon.  The square collar's
  switched core contracts to one of those rails, while its resident return
  path is extra physical structure.
* The exact canonical forest theorem is stated for the GMN lexical
  `P_sigma` factor.  The frozen all-six coherence profile
  `110u0v <-> 101u0v` is proved for the later rotational MMM `f`-factor.
  The workspace does not contain a proof that these occurrences, including
  their six untouched external edges, coincide.  A literal occurrence
  provenance check is therefore an explicit hypothesis below.

There is nevertheless an exact occurrence-level bridge.

1. Inside the rotational MMM standard-pull model, a full two-rail,
   phase-transparent coatom packet has the right seam/palette signature on

   \[
             110u0v\longleftrightarrow101u0v
   \]

   if and only if `u` is empty.  Transfer to a GMN pull additionally requires
   the literal provenance predicate.  Full physical embedding additionally
   requires the actual retained-fragment pairing, boundary marks, and
   expanded packet paths.
2. The bare ternary switch of a square collar is abstractly exactly a
   common-delete incidence rail.  A specified pull supplies its socket only
   when its six occurrence-labelled owners and external edges match this
   rail.  Its full resident collar embeds only when the prescribed common
   return rail also occurs literally, with private typed resources and
   correct boundary marks.
3. Consequently a bounded forest of already prepared packet occurrences can
   be extended prospectively to a canonical spanning tree once the remaining
   pull labels form a hereditarily commuting bank for the complete packet
   interface.  The theorem does not create the prepared occurrences or that
   bank.

The restriction `u=empty` is substantial.  The rotational MMM standard
transparent-pull graph is disconnected for every paper parameter `n>=5`.
Thus that standard family cannot be filtered to full-coatom-compatible
pulls and still span.  Under a proved GMN/MMM occurrence identification the
same obstruction transfers; without it, the disconnection is not a theorem
about the GMN lexical auxiliary graph.

Compiler cells, common caps, terminal damage, and regeneration are not used
anywhere below.

## 1. The exact canonical family and its forest theorem

Use the paper parameter `n`, so the middle-levels graph has shores of ranks
`n` and `n+1`.  In the GMN canonical path factor, a flippable pair has the
Dyck normal form

\[
\begin{aligned}
x={}&(1,u_1,1,u_2,\ldots,1,u_t,1,1,0,w,0,
                         v_t,0,\ldots,v_1,0,v_0),\\
y={}&(1,u_1,1,u_2,\ldots,1,u_t,1,0,1,w,0,
                         v_t,0,\ldots,v_1,0,v_0),
\end{aligned}                                                   \tag{1.1}
\]

where all displayed `u_i,v_i,w` are Dyck words.  A cyclic rotation fixes
the physical occurrence.  The corresponding alternating `C_6(x,y)` changes
three canonical factor edges: two edges of `P_\sigma(x)` and one edge of
`P_\sigma(y)`.  If the flippable block begins at the paper's position `a`,
the two `x`-edges occur at `2a-1,2a+4` and the `y`-edge at `2a-1`.

The exact facts imported from GMN Proposition 3 are:

1. canonical pull hexagons are pairwise **edge-disjoint**;
2. two pull edge-pairs on the same canonical path are noninterleaving; and
3. toggling a pull swaps the appropriate terminal path endpoints while
   preserving starts and the total path-vertex set.

They may share physical vertices.  In particular, these facts do not imply
owner, turn-colour, witness, residence, or cap privacy.

Let `H_n` be the connected labelled auxiliary multigraph whose vertices are
the canonical factor components and whose labelled edges are the canonical
flippable pairs.  Loops and parallel labels are retained.

### Theorem 1.1 (exact prescribed-pull extension)

For a labelled set \(A\subseteq E(H_n)\), the following are equivalent.

1. Some canonical labelled spanning tree `T` contains `A`.
2. `A` is a graphic forest in the labelled multigraph: it contains no loop,
   no ordinary cycle, and no two parallel labels forming a two-cycle.

When these conditions hold, the pull hexagons in `A` may be prescribed in
advance and the remaining canonical pulls may be added without changing
any edge of the prescribed pull hexagons.

#### Proof

Necessity is immediate because every subset of a spanning tree is a forest.
For sufficiency, contract every component of `A`.  Connectivity of `H_n`
implies that the contracted multigraph is connected.  Add labelled edges
until the contraction is a spanning tree, and uncontract.  This gives a
labelled spanning tree `T` containing `A`.

Order the edges of `A` first and the added edges afterward in any
forest-growing order.  Every prefix is a forest.  Canonical edge-disjointness
and noninterleaving make every toggle a noninterfering pure component merge,
so the simultaneous symmetric difference over `T` is the canonical
Hamilton join.  Edge-disjointness also says that later pulls do not replace
an edge of an earlier prescribed pull.  It says nothing about shared
vertices or non-edge resources.  \(\square\)

This theorem is special to the canonical GMN family.  A forest in an ECO
two-section, a resource-conflict graph, or the full later MMM labelled
catalogue is not covered unless its labels have first been identified with
this literal family and its compatibility hypotheses.

## 2. The two decorated rails of one incidence pull

Write an arbitrary incidence hexagon as

\[
\begin{array}{lll}
 L_a=H+a,&L_b=H+b,&L_c=H+c,\\
 U_{ab}=H+a+b,&U_{bc}=H+b+c,&U_{ca}=H+c+a,
\end{array}                                                    \tag{2.1}
\]

where `|H|=n-1` and `a,b,c` are distinct and outside `H`.  Orient its old
matching as

\[
       L_aU_{ab},\qquad L_bU_{bc},\qquad L_cU_{ca},              \tag{2.2}
\]

and its new matching as the other alternating matching.

At a lower port let the untouched factor edge insert `d_x`; at an upper
port let the untouched factor edge delete `e_xy`:

\[
 L_x\longleftrightarrow H+x+d_x,qquad
 U_{xy}\longleftrightarrow H-e_{xy}+x+y.                        \tag{2.3}
\]

The all-six transparent-hex theorem gives the exact criterion

\[
 d_a=d_b=d_c=:d,qquad
 e_{ab}=e_{bc}=e_{ca}=:e.                                      \tag{2.4}
\]

Under (2.4), the turns at the lower ports form a Johnson hex switch on

\[
 H+a+d,H+b+d,H+c+d,U_{ab},U_{bc},U_{ca},                        \tag{2.5}
\]

and the turns at the upper ports form another Johnson hex switch on

\[
 L_a,L_b,L_c,H-e+a+b,H-e+b+c,H-e+c+a.                           \tag{2.6}
\]

The two turn-colour triples forced by these rails are

\[
 H+d+a+b, H+d+b+c, H+d+c+a                                  \tag{2.7}
\]

and

\[
 H-e+a, H-e+b, H-e+c.                                       \tag{2.8}
\]

Thus a coherent incidence pull is not one Johnson hex: its decorated
all-six action consists of two disjoint Johnson hexes, one on each turn
shore.

For a standard label in the rotational MMM `f`-factor

\[
             x=110u0v,qquad y=101u0v,                          \tag{2.9}
\]

the literal external-label calculation is

\[
 \{e_{ab},e_{bc},e_{ca}\}=\{0,0,0\},                           \tag{2.10}
\]

and

\[
 \{d_a,d_b,d_c\}=
 \begin{cases}
   \{2n,2n,2n\},&u=\varnothing,\\
   \{2n,2n,\ell(u)+2\},&u\ne\varnothing,
 \end{cases}                                                   \tag{2.11}
\]

where \(\ell(u)\) is the length of the first primitive Dyck factor of `u`.
Hence

\[
 \boxed{\text{standard rotational pull is all-six coherent}
        \iff u=\varnothing.}                                   \tag{2.12}
\]

In cyclic phase `j` of that rotational factor, the common labels are the adjacent pair
`(d,e)=(j-1,j)` modulo `2n+1`.
The formally analogous condition in the full nested GMN normal form (1.1)
would say that the Dyck word `w` inside the flippable `110w0/101w0` block is
empty.  This analogy is not used as a theorem until the occurrence
provenance predicate below is checked.

### Definition 2.1 (literal GMN/MMM pull provenance)

For a GMN occurrence `g` and a rotational MMM standard occurrence `g'`,
write \(\mathsf{Prov}(g,g')\) when one fixed coordinate identification sends

1. the six incidence ports of `g` to those of `g'`;
2. the old three factor incidences, with their `2+1` path positions, to the
   old three incidences of `g'`; and
3. all six untouched external factor incidences, with their directions, to
   the external incidences used in (2.3).

This is a finite literal predicate.  The current workspace has no uniform
proof that every GMN pull, or the particular canonical GMN spanning tree,
has a corresponding rotational MMM standard occurrence satisfying it.

## 3. Exact pull-expansion criterion

A packet with more than six vertices cannot be literally equal to a GMN
pull.  The correct notion is an occurrence-labelled expansion.

### Definition 3.1 (canonical pull expansion)

A two-phase typed packet is an expansion of a specified canonical pull only
when it is supplied with the following literal certificate.

1. **Incidence occurrence.**  Six distinct port occurrences are identified
   with one rotated Dyck pair (1.1), and the old three incidences are the
   literal canonical `2+1` factor edges at positions
   `2a-1,2a+4;2a-1`.
2. **Decorated-role projection.**  There is an occurrence- and
   direction-preserving bijection from the packet's contracted active roles
   to the twelve turn roles obtained by adjoining the six untouched factor
   incidences to those ports.  Under this bijection the phase-changing seams
   project to the claimed one or two Johnson turn rails.  Equality merely
   as an unlabelled multiset is not enough.
3. **Retained fragments.**  The declared phase-common fragments are
   pairwise internally vertex-disjoint paths.  Their intersection with the
   exterior is exactly their declared terminal set `B`; every internal
   vertex has degree two and every terminal has local degree one in both
   phases, in every typed resource graph.  The two phases use the same
   complete physical vertex and typed-owner support; any terminal-free
   component is explicitly declared rather than silently discarded.
4. **Boundary partition.**  After contracting those paths, both phases have
   the declared oriented partition of `B`.  It is the canonical endpoint
   swap for an ordinary pull, the same through-partition for a transparent
   replacement, or that through-partition plus an explicitly declared
   sealed sidecar.  Directions, port marks, clipped run ages, and any
   exported prefix/suffix signature are part of this boundary datum.
5. **Literal host and external roles.**  Every packet edge occurs in the
   retained physical host, all external turn labels agree role by role, and
   the exterior uses no packet interior vertex or typed resource.

The contraction in item 2 is a decorated turn projection, not a claim that
an equal-rank Johnson path is a graph subdivision of the bipartite incidence
hexagon.

### Lemma 3.2 (typed terminal substitution)

Let two finite typed subgraphs meet a fixed exterior only in the same
terminal set `B`.  Suppose they use the same complete internal vertex and
typed-owner support, every internal vertex has typed degree two, every
terminal has local degree one, and the two subgraphs induce the same
oriented path partition of `B`, with possibly a declared collection of
terminal-free sealed components.  Then substituting one for the other
preserves spanning coverage and the exterior component relation; the only
component difference is the declared sealed collection.

If their clipped run ages and all exported prefix/suffix signatures also
agree, every boundary-spanning residence row and every declared
boundary-spanning OR row agrees.

#### Proof

Suppress each internally vertex-disjoint path to an oriented edge on `B`.
The two suppressed graphs are identical by hypothesis, so gluing either to
the exterior gives the same terminal connectivity and orientation.  A
terminal-free component remains sealed.  Boundary runs concatenate from
the two clipped ages, and a boundary-spanning interval is the unchanged
exterior union together with one exported prefix, suffix, or total union.
The final assertion follows.  \(\square\)

### Theorem 3.3 (necessary and sufficient occurrence test)

A two-phase packet is a literal expansion at a specified canonical pull
occurrence if and only if it has a certificate in Definition 3.1.  Transfer
of a rotational-MMM packet to a GMN occurrence additionally requires the
predicate \(\mathsf{Prov}\) of Definition 2.1.

#### Proof

Necessity follows by restricting a literal embedding to the pull ports,
turns, retained fragments, and exterior boundary.  GMN Proposition 3 forces
item 1; literal turn ownership and degree two force items 2--5.

Conversely, items 1--2 identify the exact canonical pull and its decorated
turn action.  Items 3--5 make the local graphs literal typed subgraphs with
the required terminal data.  Lemma 3.2 performs the substitution.  If the
packet came from the rotational factor, \(\mathsf{Prov}\) is exactly the
missing identification of its ports, old edges, and untouched external
edges with this GMN occurrence.  \(\square\)

This criterion is finite and occurrence labelled.  Equality of abstract
component pairs, equality of a six-cycle up to graph isomorphism, or
co-containment in one ECO hyperedge is insufficient.

## 4. Mixed-coatom packet: exact criterion and mismatch

Use the active labels from the zero-defect mixed-coatom packet.  Its two
contracted words have common endpoints `I_ab,ab`.  Cancelling their five
common internal edges leaves exactly

\[
\begin{aligned}
 \Gamma_\infty={}&
 I_{ab}-I_b-I_{bc}-I_c-I_{ca}-I_a-I_{ab},\\
 \Gamma_d={}&
 bc-C_d-ca-A_d-ab-B_d-bc.                                    \tag{4.1}
\end{aligned}
\]

These are two disjoint alternating Johnson `C_6`s.  They are exactly the
two rails (2.5)--(2.6) after the standard relabelling of
`(e,a,b,c,d,infinity)`.  The packet therefore is never literally one GMN
hexagon: before expansion it changes `6` old seams to `6` new seams, and
after tensoring at residence depth `s` it has `12s+35` equal-rank owners.

For the explicit row-one connector, the common contracted matching which
closes both phases is

\[
 M_*={A_dB_d, C_dI_c, bcI_b, caI_a,
                    I_{bc}I_{ca}, I_{ab}ab\}.                  \tag{4.2}
\]

Cutting `I_ab ab` gives the common ordered packet endpoints.

### Theorem 4.1 (full-coatom occurrence embedding)

Fix a rotational MMM standard occurrence `g'` of the form (2.9) and a GMN
occurrence `g`.  The row-one mixed-coatom packet is a phase-transparent
expansion at `g` if and only if all of the following hold.

1. \(\mathsf{Prov}(g,g')\) holds.
2. `u=empty` (equivalently, both turn rails of `g'` are coherent), and the
   cyclic phase has the packet's common adjacent labels `(d,e)`.
3. An occurrence-preserving role bijection identifies the two cycles (4.1)
   with the two decorated turn rails of `g`.
4. The six retained physical fragments contract to `M_*` in (4.2), with
   `I_ab ab` as the declared cut, and their boundary marks alternate.
5. The coatom blocks and mixed screens occur as literal, pairwise
   internally disjoint, typed-private retained paths at those fragments;
   their boundary partition, clipped run ages, and exported signatures
   satisfy Definition 3.1.

The analogous statement holds for the other two authenticated connector
rows with their corresponding common connector.

#### Proof

Literal transfer from the rotational factor to the GMN factor requires
item 1.  The packet changes both rails in (4.1), so (2.4) is necessary.
Equations (2.10)--(2.12) make this exactly `u=empty` for `g'`.  Literal turn
roles require item 3.  The packet's five common edges and common cut force
(4.2); Definition 3.1 forces items 4--5.

Conversely, items 1--3 identify the twelve contracted turn roles with the
two coherent rails of the literal GMN occurrence.  The matching (4.2) makes
each phase one twelve-cycle; cutting its common edge gives the two
common-endpoint active paths.  Item 5 invokes the mixed-coatom theorem,
which supplies owner equality, both immediate palettes, prefix/suffix OR
signatures, internal OR-**support** equality, and the declared clipped
residence state.  Definition 3.1 and Lemma 3.2 complete the physical
substitution.  \(\square\)

For `n>=2`, in the simplified fixed-root rotational catalogue (2.9), a fixed cyclic phase
has \(\operatorname{Cat}_{n-2}\) coherent labels; rotation gives
\((2n+1)\operatorname{Cat}_{n-2}\) labelled seam/palette candidates.  These are not
counts of distinct auxiliary edges or of GMN labels, and are not prepared
packet occurrences: items 1 and 3--5 still have to be realized.

### Corollary 4.2 (filtered-tree no-go)

For every `n>=5`, the **rotational MMM standard-label graph** satisfying
`u=empty` is disconnected.  Hence no proof inside that standard family may
filter to full-coatom-compatible pulls and retain connectivity.  The same
statement transfers to a GMN subfamily only after a uniform provenance
identification.

#### Proof

The condition `u=empty` is exactly the degree-two-parent leaf pull.  Repeated
such pulls have a unique irreducible normal form.  From `n=5` onward the star
and a double star are distinct irreducibles, so they lie in different
components.  \(\square\)

Coherent ECO atoms avoid this static supply obstruction and shadow every
standard pull edge, but may touch three factor components.  Their connected
two-section is not a literal set of canonical pull labels, and no all-`n`
pairwise-compatible/noninterleaving ECO hypertree is currently proved.

## 5. Resident square collar: the abstract rail is exact, the host is not

Work first on the even `2m`-coordinate square-collar host `J(2m,m)`.  Use
the notation

\[
 L,\alpha,\delta,b,c,\qquad K=L-\{b\},
\]

and its six switched owners `A,B,C,D,E,F`.  Put

\[
                         H=K+\delta.                            \tag{5.1}
\]

Then the three internal incidence ports are

\[
 A=H+\alpha,qquad C=H+c,qquad E=H+b,                          \tag{5.2}
\]

the three upper ports are

\[
 H+\alpha+c,qquad H+c+b,qquad H+b+\alpha,                    \tag{5.3}
\]

and their external lower neighbours obtained by deleting the common label
`delta` are

\[
 B=K+\alpha+c,qquad D=K+c+b,qquad F=K+b+\alpha.               \tag{5.4}
\]

Consequently

\[
 O=\{AB,CD,EF\},\qquad N=\{AF,CB,ED\}                          \tag{5.5}
\]

are exactly the two turn matchings on the common-delete rail of an
incidence pull.

### Theorem 5.1 (bare square--incidence-rail equivalence)

The ternary switched core of every square collar is, up to cyclic
relabeling and reversal, exactly the common-delete Johnson rail of an
incidence hexagon; that one shore is coherent.  Conversely every
common-delete rail has the form (5.1)--(5.5).

In the rotational MMM standard factor, the upper external-delete labels are
always equal.  Hence every one of its standard pull occurrences supplies an
abstract rail with the square normal form, without requiring `u=empty`.
A **fixed** square core embeds there only when its six occurrence-labelled
sets, directions, phase, and port marks equal that occurrence.  Transfer to
a GMN canonical pull additionally requires \(\mathsf{Prov}\), or a direct
literal verification of the six external incidences.  Placement on the
other rail requires `u=empty` in the rotational model.

#### Proof

Equations (5.2)--(5.4) give the forward construction and direct substitution
gives (5.5).  Conversely, from a common-delete rail take `delta=e`, let
`K=H-e`, and name its three active letters `alpha,c,b`; then (5.2)--(5.4)
recover all six turn owners.  The rotational-factor statement follows from
(2.10)--(2.11).  The GMN statement is deliberately conditional on
\(\mathsf{Prov}\) or literal replay.  \(\square\)

### Theorem 5.2 (full resident-collar criterion)

Let `1<=s<=m-2`, and let `R_s` be the square collar's common `2s+1`-edge
return path from `F` to `E`.  A full resident square collar embeds at a
specified pull occurrence if and only if:

1. its switched core passes Theorem 5.1 and every provenance/role/mark row
   of Definition 3.1;
2. `R_s` meets the switched support exactly in `E,F` and occurs literally
   in the retained physical factor;
3. its internal owners and all typed lower/upper resources are disjoint from
   the pull and exterior; and
4. its two endpoint degrees, directions, clipped run ages, and boundary
   marks are the required ones.

The formal count

\[
                         ((m-2)_s)^2                            \tag{5.6}
\]

counts set-theoretic resident rails for one square target in the even
`J(2m,m)` host.  It is not a count of rails occurring in a fixed pull factor.
To use this count in the fixed-terminal-zero facet of a `2n+1`-coordinate
GMN occurrence, one must explicitly set `m=n` and require every rail owner
to omit the terminal coordinate; no unrestricted odd-host count is claimed.

#### Proof

Necessity follows by deleting the switched core from an embedding: what
remains between `F` and `E` is the literal common path `R_s`; typed degree
two forces the stated intersection and privacy rows.  Conversely, Theorem
5.1 installs the switched core and items 2--4 supply a typed-private common
return with the exact terminal signature.  Lemma 3.2 and the resident-square
theorem give the old cycle/new two-path decomposition, identical immediate
palettes, and the declared clipped residence state.  \(\square\)

Thus a verified pull occurrence supplies the square **socket**, not its
resident rail.  The later rotating-hole collar shortens the physical return,
but it has the same occurrence/terminal-host obligation; the present theorem
uses `R_s` because its exact square normal form is explicit.

## 6. The exact conditional prospective theorem

For prepared packets, edge-disjointness alone is not enough.  Two canonical
pulls may share a port vertex, and nested pulls on one `P_sigma` path may
change the four-terminal exterior pairing or boundary chronology without
touching a packet-interior edge.

For a prepared set `S`, let `P(S)` be its **complete protected interface**:

* packet interiors, six pull ports, and every incident factor stub;
* the typed owner/lower/upper resources claimed private;
* the ordered terminal partition, directions, marks, and clipped run ages;
* every boundary prefix/suffix signature or witness address claimed to
  survive.

A labelled bank `E_adm(S)` is **hereditarily admissible** when, for every
graphic subset \(R\subseteq E_{\rm adm}(S)\), simultaneous toggling of `R` commutes
with every packet projection, leaves `P(S)` unchanged, and preserves the
same terminal substitution signature in both phases.  This is stronger than
pairwise edge avoidance.

### Theorem 6.1 (bounded prepared packet forest under a Cartesian admissible bank)

Fix a dimension-independent constant `H`.  Let `S` be a set of at most `H`
canonical GMN pull occurrences such that:

1. their labelled auxiliary edges form a graphic forest;
2. each occurrence carries a literal coatom expansion satisfying Theorem
   4.1 or a literal square-collar expansion satisfying Theorem 5.2;
3. their complete protected interfaces are jointly compatible (in
   particular, pairwise-disjoint complete footprints are sufficient); and
4. there is a hereditarily admissible, labelwise bank `E_adm(S)` for which
   \(E_{\rm adm}(S)\cap S=\varnothing\), and the spanning labelled auxiliary
   subgraph `H_n^adm(S)` with edge set **exactly**
   \(S\cup E_{\rm adm}(S)\) satisfies

   \[
                         H_n^{\rm adm}(S)/S
                         \quad\hbox{connected}.                  \tag{6.1}
   \]

Then `S` extends to a canonical spanning tree `T` using only labels in the
admissible bank outside `S`.  A common-endpoint coatom replacement remains a
legal local replacement in the globally joined carrier.  The two square
phases retain their respective canonical old/new four-terminal partitions;
the minus phase additionally has exactly its declared sealed sidecar.  Any
stronger global component claim is obtained only after gluing those explicit
partitions to the certified exterior.

Every packet-internal owner/immediate-palette and interval-OR-support claim
survives.  Boundary-spanning OR and residence claims survive precisely to
the extent included in `P(S)`.  No global upper-surjectivity or compiler
conclusion follows.

#### Proof

Contract `S`.  Equation (6.1) and ordinary graphic augmentation give a
spanning tree in the admissible contraction.  Hereditary admissibility makes
the entire selected extension commute with every packet projection, not
merely each edge separately.  Definition 3.1 and Lemma 3.2 then give the
stated coatom and square phase signatures.  The local packet theorems give
the intrinsic owner, palette, OR-support, and clipped residence rows.
\(\square\)

Within a genuinely labelwise/hereditarily admissible model, the criterion
is exact.  Failure is certified by either

1. a labelled graphic circuit already contained in `S`; or
2. if `S` is a forest, a nontrivial cut in `H_n/S` crossed only by labels
   excluded from `E_adm(S)`:

   \[
    \varnothing\ne X\subsetneq V(H_n/S),\qquad
    \delta_{H_n/S}(X)\cap E_{\rm adm}(S)=\varnothing.            \tag{6.2}
   \]

Without a Cartesian hereditarily admissible bank, (6.2) is not an exact
criterion: joint nesting/chronology conflicts form higher-order constraints.
At the edge-only GMN level the complete interface is empty, every canonical
label is admissible, and Theorem 6.1 reduces to Theorem 1.1.

The theorem is genuinely prospective: the old host, pull occurrence, and
packet expansion are selected together.  The fixed-incumbent coatom fibre
may have size one, and a fixed directed Hamilton target may contain at most
one formal square rail for a given oriented target.  Raw algebraic menu size
does not produce hypothesis 2.

## 7. Exact remaining obstruction

After this audit, **edge-only** canonical spanning-tree augmentation is no
longer an open row.  The exact missing host statement is:

> For each bounded regenerative task, expose a literal canonical pull
> occurrence whose auxiliary labels remain a forest and whose retained
> physical fragments realize either `M_*` plus the coatom blocks/screens, or
> a private resident return rail, with pairwise-private halos and the
> required endpoint marks; prove its GMN/MMM provenance and exhibit a
> hereditarily admissible completion bank for the complete interface.

Within the rotational standard model, the full coatom task is restricted to
the degree-two-parent pulls.  Every rotational standard pull supplies an
abstract square-normal-form rail, but no theorem identifies all of those
rails with GMN occurrences or supplies the resident return.  ECO
co-containment and connected two-sections do not imply either occurrence
statement.

Separately, a finished additive-constant induction still needs upper witness
protection or bounded terminal damage, a final common cap/compiler matching,
and nonaccumulating regeneration.  None is hidden in Theorems 1.1 or 6.1.
