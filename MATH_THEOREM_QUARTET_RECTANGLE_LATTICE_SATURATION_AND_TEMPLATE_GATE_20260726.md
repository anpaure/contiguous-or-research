# Quartet rectangles saturate the complete top-incidence lattice

Date: 2026-07-26

Scope: constant-one promotion coupling. This note isolates the purely
top-index part of the all-depth quartet exchange. It proves an integral
lattice theorem; it does not assume that the required four positional
frame templates are simultaneously present.

## 0. Result

Let \(\Omega\) be an \(N\)-set and let

\[
2\le k\le N-2,\qquad \mathcal X=\binom{\Omega}{k}.
\]

For \(C\in\binom{\Omega}{k-2}\) and four distinct elements
\(a_0,a_1,b_0,b_1\notin C\), put

\[
\begin{aligned}
r(C;a_0,a_1;b_0,b_1)
  ={}&e_{C\cup\{a_0,b_0\}}-e_{C\cup\{a_0,b_1\}}\\
    &-e_{C\cup\{a_1,b_0\}}+e_{C\cup\{a_1,b_1\}} .
\end{aligned}                                                   \tag{0.1}
\]

Let \(\mathcal R\le\mathbb Z^{\mathcal X}\) be the lattice generated by
all vectors (0.1), and let

\[
A:\mathbb Z^{\mathcal X}\longrightarrow\mathbb Z^\Omega,\qquad
(Az)_x=\sum_{U\ni x}z_U                                      \tag{0.2}
\]

be the top--element incidence map.

### Theorem 0.1 (integral rectangle saturation)

\[
\boxed{\mathcal R=\ker_{\mathbb Z}A.}                          \tag{0.3}
\]

Equivalently, every integral signed redistribution of \(k\)-set tops
which preserves every element margin is an integral sum of checkerboard
quartet vectors.

This strengthens the mod-two affine-character calculation in
MATH_THEOREM_PROMOTION_RING_QUARTET_HOLONOMY_EXCHANGE_20260726.md.
There is no additional rational, modular, or torsion obstruction in the
complete top-index lattice.

For the promotion parameters

\[
N=2m,\qquad k=M=m+H,
\]

the hypotheses hold because \(m-H\ge2\). Therefore the only unresolved
quartet gate is **template realizability**: an actual move requires the
four frames to restrict to one common positional word on their
\((M-2)\)-set core, with the two exceptional labels occupying the same
two positions. The abstract lattice theorem does not manufacture this
common word, nor does it guarantee a box-feasible sequence of moves
between two nonnegative integral frame tables.

## 1. The annihilator over an arbitrary field

Let \(\mathbb F\) be any field. Suppose

\[
f:\mathcal X\to\mathbb F                                      \tag{1.1}
\]

annihilates every rectangle:

\[
f(C+a_0+b_0)-f(C+a_0+b_1)
-f(C+a_1+b_0)+f(C+a_1+b_1)=0 .                                \tag{1.2}
\]

Here and below \(C+x+y\) abbreviates \(C\cup\{x,y\}\).

### Lemma 1.1 (swap derivatives are context-free)

For distinct \(x,y\in\Omega\), the difference

\[
f(B+y)-f(B+x),\qquad
B\in\binom{\Omega\setminus\{x,y\}}{k-1},                       \tag{1.3}
\]

is independent of \(B\).

#### Proof

The Johnson graph on
\(\binom{\Omega\setminus\{x,y\}}{k-1}\) is connected because

\[
1\le k-1\le N-3.
\]

It is therefore enough to compare adjacent sets

\[
B=C+p,\qquad B'=C+q,
\]

where \(|C|=k-2\) and \(p,q,x,y\) are distinct and outside \(C\).
The required equality

\[
f(C+p+y)-f(C+p+x)=f(C+q+y)-f(C+q+x)
\]

is exactly (1.2). \(\square\)

Write the common value in (1.3) as \(d_{xy}\). If \(x,y,z\) are
distinct, choose a \((k-1)\)-set \(B\) disjoint from them; this is
possible since \(k\le N-2\). Telescoping gives

\[
d_{xy}+d_{yz}=d_{xz}.                                          \tag{1.4}
\]

Fix \(o\in\Omega\), put \(\alpha_o=0\), and for \(x\ne o\) put
\(\alpha_x=d_{ox}\). Equation (1.4) gives

\[
d_{xy}=\alpha_y-\alpha_x.                                      \tag{1.5}
\]

If two \(k\)-sets \(U,V\) are adjacent in the Johnson graph, say
\(V=U-x+y\), then

\[
f(V)-f(U)=\alpha_y-\alpha_x.
\]

Since \(J(N,k)\) is connected,

\[
f(U)=c+\sum_{x\in U}\alpha_x                                  \tag{1.6}
\]

for some \(c\in\mathbb F\). Conversely every function (1.6)
annihilates the rectangles.

### Corollary 1.2 (field-independent rank)

Over every field \(\mathbb F\), the annihilator of the rectangle span
has dimension exactly \(N\), and hence

\[
\operatorname {rank}_{\mathbb F}\mathcal R=\binom Nk-N.         \tag{1.7}
\]

Indeed, the parameterization \((c,\alpha)\mapsto f\) in (1.6) has the
one-dimensional kernel

\[
\alpha_x=t\quad(x\in\Omega),\qquad c=-kt,
\]

in every characteristic.

## 2. Saturation and identification with the incidence kernel

Every rectangle has zero element margin, so

\[
\mathcal R\subseteq\ker_{\mathbb Z}A.                           \tag{2.1}
\]

Over \(\mathbb Q\), the incidence matrix \(A\) has row rank \(N\).
To see this, if \(\sum_{x\in U}\beta_x=0\) for every \(k\)-set \(U\),
comparison of two sets differing by \(x\leftrightarrow y\) gives
\(\beta_x=\beta_y\) for all \(x,y\), and then \(k\beta_x=0\) gives
\(\beta_x=0\). Thus

\[
\operatorname {rank}_{\mathbb Z}\ker A=\binom Nk-N,             \tag{2.2}
\]

the same rank as \(\mathcal R\).

It remains to rule out finite index. Choose any integer matrix whose
rows are the rectangle vectors. By (1.7), its rank modulo every prime
\(p\) equals its rational rank. Therefore no prime divides the gcd of
its maximal nonzero minors. That gcd is one, so its row lattice
\(\mathcal R\) is saturated in \(\mathbb Z^{\mathcal X}\).

The lattice \(\ker_{\mathbb Z}A\) is also saturated, since

\[
\mathbb Z^{\mathcal X}/\ker A\cong\operatorname {im}A
\]

is a subgroup of the free abelian group \(\mathbb Z^\Omega\).
Now (2.1), (2.2), and saturation of both lattices in the same rational
subspace imply (0.3). \(\square\)

## 3. Exact implication for promotion quartets

Fix two positional slots. Abstractly record by \(z_U\) the signed
number of times the two labels occupying those slots are transposed in
the frame on top \(U\). Any all-depth checkerboard quartet changes
\(z\) by one vector (0.1). Theorem 0.1 says:

> At the top-index level, preservation of every element margin is the
> only integral conservation law for these transpositions.

This removes three possible obstructions:

1. a hidden odd-set character on the complete top system;
2. torsion not detected by the mod-two audit; and
3. a rational codimension beyond the affine element margins.

It does **not** prove connectivity of the physical frame fibre. Two
additional requirements remain.

* **Template compatibility.** The four current frames must share the
  same ordered core and the same two placeholder positions.
* **Nonnegative path connectivity.** A lattice decomposition may pass
  through negative frame multiplicities. A Markov-basis theorem must
  realize the decomposition by moves whose source frames are present at
  every step, allowing only \(o(W)\) exceptional frames.

Thus a future obstruction to quartet connectivity must be genuinely
positional/nonabelian or a nonnegative-fibre obstruction; it cannot live
in the complete integral top-incidence lattice.
