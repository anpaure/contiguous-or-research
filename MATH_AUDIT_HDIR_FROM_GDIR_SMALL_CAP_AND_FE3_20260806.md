## 1. Correct absolute Bonferroni identity

For every resource occurrence `x` in `E`, put

\[
 u_x=1-\rho_i
 \quad\hbox{or}\quad
 u_x=1-\rho_{s,i}
\]

according to its shore, and let `alpha_x=X(i)u_x`.  Define

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 D_i^{\rm prod}(E)
   &=\sum_{x\in E}\alpha_x-X(i)q_i(E),\\
 D_i^{\rm act}(E)
   &=\sum_{x\in E}Y_x(i)-\Lambda_i(E).
 \end{aligned}
\]

The two Bonferroni inequalities give

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 0\le D_i^{\rm prod}(E)
 &\le X(i)\sum_{\{x,y\}\subseteq E}u_xu_y,\\
 0\le D_i^{\rm act}(E)
 &\le \sum_{\{x,y\}\subseteq E}Y_{x,y}(i)
   =\mathcal B_i(E).
 \end{aligned}
\]

Consequently the exact decomposition is

\[
 X(i)\mathcal H_i(E)
 =\sum_{x\in E}(\alpha_x-Y_x)
   +D_i^{\rm act}(E)-D_i^{\rm prod}(E),
\]

and hence

\[
 \boxed{
 \left|X(i)\mathcal H_i(E)
       -\sum_{x\in E}(\alpha_x-Y_x)\right|
 \le \mathcal B_i(E)+D_i^{\rm prod}(E).}
\]

The source's `(1.4)` omits `D_i^{prod}`.  It is false literally when
`B_i(E)=0` but the product density has two or more nonzero hazards.

This is only a bookkeeping correction.  A row has `O(d)` resources and,
on the total-rate interval, `u_x=O(1/X)`, so

\[
 D_i^{\rm prod}(E)=O(d^2/X(i)).
\]

Under `PCAP`, `P_i=O(X(i)/d^2)`, and therefore the cumulative squared
contribution is bounded by

\[
 \sum_i {\mathcal P_i\over X(i)}
          (D_i^{\rm prod})^2
 \le C\sum_i {d^2\over X(i)^2},
\]

which is exponentially small throughout the central regime, even after
summing all process times.  Equivalently it may be placed in the existing
deterministic density-remainder ledger.

## 2. First-order coefficient matching

For the `E` side of one pair row define

\[
 g_{x,E}^{\circ}
 =\sum_{Q,E,F:\,x\in E-Q}c_i(Q,E,F)R_i(E,F;Q).
\]

Then

\[
 g_{x,E}^{\circ}\le g_x^{\circ},
\]

where `g_x^circ` is the union-incidence coefficient in the source.  Thus
row-size Cauchy gives

\[
 \sum_{Q,E,F}c_iR_i
 \left(\sum_{x\in U_T(E)-Q}a_x\right)^2
 \le Cd\sum_xg_x^\circ a_x^2.
\]

\[

As written, `(2.3)` uses `U_T(E)` while `g_x^circ` omits the distinguished

## 3. Nonlinear pair remainder and `FE3`

For pairs of distinct non-slot resources, the good-relation stop gives

\[
 Y_{x,y}\le C/d.
\]

There are `O(d^2)` such pairs in one row, so their contribution to
`B_i(E)` is `O(d)`.  A row has only constantly many slot occurrences.
It has only `O(d)` slot--non-slot pairs and constantly many slot--slot
pairs; bounded root loads therefore make their total contribution
`O(d)` as well.  Hence, after this split,

\[
 \mathcal B_i(E)\le Cd,
 \qquad
 \mathcal B_i(E)^2\le Cd\mathcal B_i(E).
\]

Moreover

\[
 \mathcal B_i(E)\le
 \sum_{\{x,y\}\subseteq E\cup F}Y_{x,y}(i).
\]

With the exact coefficient `c_iR_i/X(i)`, the right side is precisely the
multi-hit term generated by `(FP9)`.  The authenticated `FE3` estimate,
including its separately suppressed slot-fugacity classes, gives
`O(A/d)`.  Multiplication by the preceding factor `d` proves the claimed
`O(A)` square bound.  No maximum higher-codegree theorem is used.

This argument covers the base pair family.  Marked families require the
separately stated normalization replay; the source correctly leaves that
finite interface explicit.

## 4. `HDIR` normalization

On the common total-rate stop,

\[
 h_i=1-\rho_i^2\asymp X(i)^{-1}.
\]

Writing `D_i(E)=X(i)H_i(E)` gives

\[
 {1\over h_i}\mathcal H_i(E)^2

Thus the first-order estimate and the nonlinear square have exactly the
normalization required by `HDIR`; there is no missing factor of `X`.
