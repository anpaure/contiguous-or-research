# Packet Hall and common-threshold recourse for hard wreath quotas

Date: 2026-07-25

Method: pure mathematics only.  No web search, finite search, random
experiment, solver, or asymptotic matching black box is used.

## 0. Outcome

This note does **not** prove MWB.  It does remove the integral-cover gap in
the hard-quota route, up to a harmless constant on every fixed Gaussian
window.

The key observation is exact.  Fix an exact wreath factor and a quota
\(\beta(q,S)\) for a lower target \(S\).  If \(\mathcal O(q,S)\) is the set
of wreaths owning an occurrence of \(S\), then a deleted family \(B\) leaves
at most \(\beta(q,S)\) occurrences precisely when \(B\) meets every
\((\beta(q,S)+1)\)-subset of \(\mathcal O(q,S)\).  These subsets are the
**survival packets**.

For balanced quotas on a fixed window \(q\le A\sqrt m\), every survival
packet has size at most

\[
 R_A=2+\max_{q\le A\sqrt m}c_q=O_A(1).
\]

Consequently the exact integral deletion problem is a bounded-rank vertex
cover problem.  If \(\vartheta_A\) is its ordinary fractional vertex-cover
value and \(\tau_A\) its integral value, then

\[
 \boxed{\vartheta_A\le \tau_A\le R_A\vartheta_A.}
\]

The upper bound is the elementary threshold rounding

\[
 B=\{E:x_E\ge 1/R_A\}.
\]

Thus, for each fixed \(A\), an \(o(\operatorname{Cat}_m/\sqrt m)\)
fractional **packet** cover already gives the required integral exceptional
family.  This packet LP is weaker than the previously recorded fractional
multicover LP \(Ax\ge d\).

There is also a dynamic theorem.  Along any sequence of exact factors and
fractional packet covers, one common threshold produces integral quota-safe
exceptional families at every time, with total symmetric-difference
recourse at most \(R_A\) times the fractional \(\ell^1\) movement.  Hence
bounded-capacity packet rounding itself causes no long-recourse obstruction.
The remaining obstruction is now fractional and geometric: construct a
small packet cover, or a slowly moving sequence of such covers, inside the
exact-factor fibre.

## 1. Wreath quotas and owner sets

Put

\[
 n=2m+1,\qquad
 W=\binom nm,\qquad
 t=\frac Wn=\operatorname{Cat}_m.
\]

Let \(F\) be one exact middle wreath factor, so \(|F|=t\).  At depth \(q\),
put

\[
 r=m-q,\qquad
 N_q=\binom nr,\qquad
 \lambda_q=\frac W{N_q},\qquad
 c_q=\lfloor\lambda_q\rfloor,
\]

and let a balanced quota be

\[
 \beta_q(S)\in\{c_q,c_q+1\},\qquad
 \sum_{S\in\binom{[n]}r}\beta_q(S)=W.
\]

For a resource

\[
 a=(q,S),\qquad S\in\binom{[n]}{m-q},
\]

define its owner set

\[
 \mathcal O_a
 =\{E\in F:S\text{ is a cyclic length-}(m-q)
                    \text{ interval of }E\}.
 \tag{1.1}
\]

The cyclic intervals of one fixed proper length in one coordinate order are
distinct.  Hence a wreath contributes at most one occurrence to a fixed
resource, and

\[
 |\mathcal O_a|=\mu_q^F(S).
 \tag{1.2}
\]

If \(B\subseteq F\) is deleted and \(G=F\setminus B\), then

\[
 \mu_q^G(S)=|\mathcal O_a\setminus B|.
 \tag{1.3}
\]

The hard-quota condition is

\[
 |\mathcal O_a\setminus B|\le \beta_q(S)
 \qquad\text{for every controlled }a.
 \tag{1.4}
\]

## 2. Exact survival-packet equivalence

For every resource \(a=(q,S)\), let

\[
 \mathcal P_a
 =\left\{P\subseteq\mathcal O_a:
                  |P|=\beta_q(S)+1\right\}.
 \tag{2.1}
\]

This family is empty when \(|\mathcal O_a|\le\beta_q(S)\).  Put

\[
 \mathcal P(F,\beta)=\bigcup_a\mathcal P_a.
 \tag{2.2}
\]

It is a hypergraph on the vertex set \(F\).  When the same wreath subset is
generated by two resources, regard the two copies as resource-labelled
parallel hyperedges.  This does not change the vertex-cover constraints or
their fractional relaxation, but it makes the resourcewise dual
decomposition canonical.  Its edges are called survival packets: if all
wreaths in one such packet survive, their common target exceeds its quota.

### Theorem 2.1 — exact packetization

For \(B\subseteq F\), the following are equivalent.

1. The residual core \(G=F\setminus B\) obeys every hard quota:
   \[
    \mu_q^G(S)\le\beta_q(S)
    \quad\text{for every controlled }q,S.
   \]
2. \(B\) is a vertex cover of \(\mathcal P(F,\beta)\):
   \[
    B\cap P\ne\varnothing
    \quad(P\in\mathcal P(F,\beta)).
   \]

Consequently the deletion-transversal number from the hard-quota report is
exactly

\[
 \boxed{
 \tau(F,\beta)=\tau\bigl(\mathcal P(F,\beta)\bigr).
 }
 \tag{2.3}
\]

#### Proof

If (1) fails at \(a\), at least \(\beta_q(S)+1\) wreaths of
\(\mathcal O_a\) survive.  Any such subfamily is a packet disjoint from
\(B\), so (2) fails.

Conversely, if a packet \(P\in\mathcal P_a\) is disjoint from \(B\), all
\(\beta_q(S)+1\) members of \(P\) survive, and (1) fails at \(a\).  This
proves the equivalence and (2.3).  \(\square\)

### Packet rank

Let

\[
 R(F,\beta)=\max_{P\in\mathcal P(F,\beta)}|P|,
 \]

with \(R=1\) if the packet family is empty.  Since a balanced quota has
value \(c_q\) or \(c_q+1\),

\[
 R(F,\beta)
 \le 2+\max_q c_q.
 \tag{2.4}
\]

For a fixed Gaussian window

\[
 1\le q\le K_A:=\lceil A\sqrt m\rceil,
\]

the exact binomial ratio gives

\[
 \log\lambda_q
 =\frac{q(q+1)}m+O_A(m^{-1/2}).
\]

Hence there is a constant \(C_A\), independent of \(m\), such that

\[
 c_q\le C_A\qquad(q\le K_A),
\]

and every packet has size at most

\[
 \boxed{R_A=C_A+2=O_A(1).}
 \tag{2.5}
\]

## 3. Bounded-rank Packet Hall theorem

Define the fractional packet-cover value

\[
 \vartheta(F,\beta)
 =\min\left\{
       \sum_{E\in F}x_E:
       x_E\ge0,\ 
       \sum_{E\in P}x_E\ge1
       \quad(P\in\mathcal P(F,\beta))
             \right\}.
 \tag{3.1}
\]

One may impose \(x_E\le1\), since replacing \(x_E>1\) by one preserves all
packet inequalities.

### Theorem 3.1 — bounded-capacity Packet Hall rounding

For every exact factor, every finite family of integral quotas, and its
survival-packet rank \(R\),

\[
 \boxed{
 \vartheta(F,\beta)
 \le \tau(F,\beta)
 \le R\,\vartheta(F,\beta).
 }
 \tag{3.2}
\]

More generally, with arbitrary nonnegative vertex costs \(w_E\), the
minimum integral quota-safe deletion cost is at most \(R\) times the
fractional packet-cover cost.

#### Proof

The first inequality is relaxation.  Let \(x\) be a fractional packet
cover and put

\[
 B=\{E:x_E\ge1/R\}.
 \tag{3.3}
\]

If a packet \(P\), of size at most \(R\), missed \(B\), then every
\(x_E<1/R\) on \(P\), and therefore

\[
 \sum_{E\in P}x_E<\frac{|P|}{R}\le1,
\]

contrary to (3.1).  Thus \(B\) hits every packet and is quota-safe by
Theorem 2.1.  Moreover

\[
 |B|\le R\sum_E x_E.
\]

With costs, sum \(w_E\le Rw_Ex_E\) over \(E\in B\).  Taking an optimal
fractional cover proves the theorem.  \(\square\)

### Exact dual

Ordinary finite-dimensional LP duality gives

\[
 \boxed{
 \vartheta(F,\beta)
 =\max\left\{
       \sum_{P\in\mathcal P(F,\beta)}y_P:
       y_P\ge0,\quad
       \sum_{P\ni E}y_P\le1\quad(E\in F)
            \right\}.
 }
 \tag{3.4}
\]

Thus the remaining fractional obstruction is a packing of survival packets
subject to unit wreath congestion.  Equations (3.2)--(3.4) are a genuine
bounded-capacity Hall theorem: every such fractional packet packing controls
the integral deletion number within the fixed factor \(R\).

### Relation to the earlier fractional multicover

The earlier LP, with the deletion-variable bounds

\[
 0\le x_E\le1\qquad(E\in F),
\]

asks for

\[
 \sum_{E\in\mathcal O_a}x_E
 \ge
 d_a:=\bigl(|\mathcal O_a|-\beta(a)\bigr)_+
 \qquad(a=(q,S)).
 \tag{3.5}
\]

Every \(x\in[0,1]^F\) satisfying (3.5) is a fractional packet cover.  Indeed,
for \(P\in\mathcal P_a\),

\[
 |\mathcal O_a\setminus P|
 =|\mathcal O_a|-\beta(a)-1=d_a-1,
\]

so

\[
 \sum_{E\in P}x_E
 \ge d_a-\sum_{E\in\mathcal O_a\setminus P}x_E
 \ge d_a-(d_a-1)=1.
 \tag{3.6}
\]

Therefore

\[
 \vartheta(F,\beta)
 \le \tau_{\mathrm{frac}}(F,\beta)
 \le \tau(F,\beta).
 \tag{3.7}
\]

The first inequality can be strict.  For one resource with \(h\) owners
and quota \(\beta<h\), the packet hypergraph is the complete
\((\beta+1)\)-uniform hypergraph on \(h\) vertices.  Hence

\[
 \vartheta=\frac{h}{\beta+1},\qquad
 \tau_{\mathrm{frac}}=\tau=h-\beta.
 \tag{3.8}
\]

For example, \((h,\beta)=(3,1)\) gives

\[
 \vartheta=\frac32<2=\tau_{\mathrm{frac}}=\tau.
\]

Thus packet Hall is not merely a reformulation of the former fractional
Hall theorem; it is a strictly weaker sufficient gate.

### Order-statistic form and the forced prebalance ledger

For a finite list \((z_E:E\in O)\), write

\[
 \operatorname{Bot}_k(z;O)
\]

for the sum of its \(k\) smallest entries.  If \(h_a=|\mathcal O_a|\) and
\(k_a=\beta(a)+1\), then the complete family of packet inequalities for
resource \(a\) is equivalent to the single condition

\[
 \boxed{
 \operatorname{Bot}_{k_a}(x;\mathcal O_a)\ge1.
 }
 \tag{3.9}
\]

Indeed, the left side is the minimum of \(\sum_{E\in P}x_E\) over all
\(k_a\)-subsets \(P\subseteq\mathcal O_a\).

Average the packet inequalities over all
\(\binom{h_a}{k_a}\) packets of one resource.  Every owner belongs to
\(\binom{h_a-1}{k_a-1}\) of them, and therefore

\[
 \boxed{
 \sum_{E\in\mathcal O_a}x_E
 \ge\frac{h_a}{k_a}.
 }
 \tag{3.10}
\]

Let

\[
 \mathcal V(F,\beta)
 =\{a:h_a>\beta(a)\}
 \tag{3.11}
\]

be the over-capacity resource family in the full factor.  One wreath owns
exactly \(n\) resources at each depth, hence at most \(nK_A\) members of
\(\mathcal V(F,\beta)\) throughout the window.  Summing (3.10) gives

\[
 \boxed{
 \sum_{a\in\mathcal V(F,\beta)}
       \frac{h_a}{\beta(a)+1}
 \le nK_A\|x\|_1.
 }
 \tag{3.12}
\]

Since \(h_a/(\beta(a)+1)\ge1\), and
\(\beta(a)+1\le R_A\), this implies

\[
 |\mathcal V(F,\beta)|\le nK_A\|x\|_1,
 \qquad
 \sum_{a\in\mathcal V(F,\beta)}h_a
 \le R_A nK_A\|x\|_1.
 \tag{3.13}
\]

In particular, a cover at the target scale

\[
 \|x\|_1=o_A(t/\sqrt m)
\]

forces both the number of over-capacity cells and their total occurrence
mass, summed over the whole fixed window, to be \(o_A(W)\).  This is not a
construction of the cover.  It is an exact necessary prebalance consequence
of \((\mathrm{FSP}_A)\).

### Theorem 3.2 — compressed packet-flow Hall dual

The exponentially many packet variables in (3.4) can be eliminated.
Retain the over-capacity resource family

\[
 \mathcal V=\{a:h_a\ge k_a\},
 \qquad h_a=|\mathcal O_a|,
 \qquad k_a=\beta(a)+1.
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
 \vartheta(F,\beta)
 =\max\ &\sum_{a\in\mathcal V}Y_a\\
 \text{subject to }\;&Y_a\ge0,\\
 &\sum_{a\in\mathcal V}
 Y_a\bigl(k_a-|\mathcal O_a\setminus Z|\bigr)_+
 \le |Z|
 \qquad(Z\subseteq F).
\end{aligned}}
 \tag{3.14}
\]

Thus a proposed fractional packet mass \(Y_a\) is feasible exactly when
every wreath set \(Z\) can pay the owner capacity that the packets are
forced to place inside \(Z\).

#### Proof

Start from a feasible packet packing \(y_P\) in (3.4).  For every resource,
put

\[
 Y_a=\sum_{P\in\mathcal P_a}y_P,
 \qquad
 z_{a,E}=\sum_{\substack{P\in\mathcal P_a\\E\in P}}y_P.
 \tag{3.15}
\]

Then

\[
 0\le z_{a,E}\le Y_a,\qquad
 \sum_{E\in\mathcal O_a}z_{a,E}=k_aY_a,
 \qquad
 \sum_{a:E\in\mathcal O_a}z_{a,E}\le1.
 \tag{3.16}
\]

Conversely, suppose nonnegative numbers \(Y_a,z_{a,E}\) satisfy (3.16).
When \(Y_a>0\), the vector

\[
 (z_{a,E}/Y_a:E\in\mathcal O_a)
\]

lies in the hypersimplex

\[
 \left\{p\in[0,1]^{\mathcal O_a}:\sum_Ep_E=k_a\right\},
\]

which is the convex hull of the incidence vectors of the \(k_a\)-subsets
of \(\mathcal O_a\).  Decomposing this vector into those vertices and
multiplying the coefficients by \(Y_a\) reconstructs packet weights
\((y_P:P\in\mathcal P_a)\) with induced owner loads \(z_{a,E}\).  The last
inequality in (3.16) is exactly the unit wreath-congestion constraint in
(3.4).  Hence (3.4) is equivalent to maximizing \(\sum_aY_a\) subject to
(3.16).

It remains to eliminate \(z\).  Build a capacitated bipartite flow network:

* the source sends exactly \(k_aY_a\) units to resource \(a\);
* \(a\) sends at most \(Y_a\) units to every owner
  \(E\in\mathcal O_a\);
* every owner sends at most one unit to the sink.

A cut whose source side contains resources \(X\subseteq\mathcal V\) and
owners \(Z\subseteq F\) has enough capacity to saturate all source arcs if
and only if

\[
 \sum_{a\in X}
 Y_a\bigl(k_a-|\mathcal O_a\setminus Z|\bigr)
 \le |Z|.
 \tag{3.17}
\]

For fixed \(Z\), the strongest choice of \(X\) consists exactly of the
resources for which the displayed coefficient is positive.  Thus all cuts
(3.17) are equivalent to the positive-part inequality in (3.14).
Max-flow/min-cut now gives (3.16), completing the proof.  \(\square\)

The coefficient in (3.14) has a direct packet meaning.  A
\(k_a\)-subset of \(\mathcal O_a\) must use at least

\[
 \bigl(k_a-|\mathcal O_a\setminus Z|\bigr)_+
\]

members of \(Z\).  Theorem 3.2 says these pigeonhole cut constraints are
also sufficient fractionally; there is no further fractional obstruction.

### Theorem 3.3 — direct packet-cover-to-overload transfer

Let the controlled depths be \(1\le q\le H<m\), with no fixed-window
assumption.  For one exact factor \(F\), balanced quotas \(\beta\), and any
fractional survival-packet cover \(x\),

\[
 \boxed{
 \sum_{q=1}^H\frac{O_q(F)}{c_q}
 \le 3nH\|x\|_1.
 }
 \tag{3.18}
\]

Consequently,

\[
 \boxed{
 \vartheta(F,\beta)=o(t/H)
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \sum_{q=1}^H\frac{O_q(F)}{c_q}=o(W).
 }
 \tag{3.19}
\]

This implication does not round the packet cover and does not require
bounded packet rank.

#### Proof

For a resource \(a=(q,S)\), put

\[
 h_a=\mu_q^F(S),\qquad
 k_a=\beta_q(S)+1,\qquad
 d_a=(h_a-\beta_q(S))_+.
\]

The chosen balanced quota \(\beta_q\) is an admissible competitor in the
definition of \(O_q(F)\), so

\[
 O_q(F)\le\sum_{a\text{ at depth }q}d_a.
 \tag{3.20}
\]

Only resources with \(h_a\ge k_a\) contribute.  For them,

\[
 d_a=h_a-k_a+1\le h_a.
\]

Also \(\beta_q(S)\in\{c_q,c_q+1\}\), and hence

\[
 k_a\in\{c_q+1,c_q+2\},
 \qquad
 \frac{k_a}{c_q}\le3
 \tag{3.21}
\]

because \(c_q\ge1\).  Therefore (3.20) gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
 \sum_{q=1}^H\frac{O_q(F)}{c_q}
 &\le
 \sum_{a\in\mathcal V}\frac{h_a}{c_q}\\
 &\le
 3\sum_{a\in\mathcal V}\frac{h_a}{k_a}.
\end{aligned}
 \tag{3.22}
\]

By the averaged packet inequality (3.10),

\[
 \frac{h_a}{k_a}
 \le\sum_{E\in\mathcal O_a}x_E.
\]

Each wreath owns exactly \(n\) targets at each depth, so its weight appears
on the right at most \(nH\) times.  Thus

\[
 \sum_{a\in\mathcal V}\frac{h_a}{k_a}
 \le nH\|x\|_1.
\]

This proves (3.18).  Since \(W=nt\), (3.19) follows.  \(\square\)

## 4. Fixed-window implication to MWB

For fixed \(A\), minimize also over balanced quotas:

\[
 \vartheta_A(F)
 =\min_{\beta_1,\ldots,\beta_{K_A}\ \mathrm{balanced}}
       \vartheta(F,\beta).
 \tag{4.1}
\]

### Theorem 4.1 — fractional packet criterion

Suppose that, for every fixed \(A>0\), there is an exact middle wreath
factor \(F_{m,A}\) such that

\[
 \boxed{
 \vartheta_A(F_{m,A})
 =o_A\!\left(\frac{\operatorname{Cat}_m}{\sqrt m}\right).
 }
 \tag{4.2}
\]

Then the hard-quota statement \(HQ_A\) holds for every fixed \(A\).  Hence,
by the already audited hard-quota charging and fixed-window
diagonalization, MWB and the coefficient-one contiguous-OR upper bound
follow.

#### Proof

Choose balanced quotas and a fractional packet cover attaining (4.2).
Theorem 3.1 and (2.5) give a quota-safe integral deletion family \(B\) with

\[
 |B|
 \le R_A\vartheta_A(F_{m,A})
 =o_A(t/\sqrt m),
\]

because \(R_A\) is constant for fixed \(A\).  Put \(G=F\setminus B\).
Theorem 2.1 gives \(\mu_q^G\le\beta_q\) simultaneously throughout the
window, and \(F=G\sqcup B\) is already an exact completion.  This is
\(HQ_A\).  The quoted implication from \(HQ_A\) completes the proof.
\(\square\)

Alternatively, Theorem 3.3 gives the fixed-window MWB estimate directly,
since \(K_A=O_A(\sqrt m)\).  The bounded-rank rounding is stronger in a
different direction: it also produces the genuine quota-safe core
\(F=G\sqcup B\) and supports the recourse theorem below.

### What has been removed

The previously isolated integral common-depth deletion-transversal lemma
asked directly for

\[
 \tau_A(F,\beta)=o(t/\sqrt m).
\]

On every fixed window, Theorem 3.1 proves the equivalence, at the relevant
little-\(o\) scale,

\[
 \boxed{
 \exists(F,\beta):\ \tau_A=o(t/\sqrt m)
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \exists(F,\beta):\ \vartheta_A=o(t/\sqrt m).
 }
 \tag{4.3}
\]

The right side is a fractional bounded-rank packet problem.  Thus generic
set-cover nonideality, including the Fano example in the hard-quota audit,
cannot be the asymptotic obstruction on fixed Gaussian windows.  It can
cost a constant depending on \(A\), and fixed-window diagonalization absorbs
that constant.

This does **not** make the remaining fractional theorem easy.  The packet
owners are constrained by one exact factor, and a small packet cover still
forces the ambient factor to be two-sided prebalanced.  What disappears is
only the final fractional-to-integral absorber gap.

## 5. Common-threshold bounded recourse

The same threshold can round a whole adaptive trajectory.

Let \(\mathscr V\) be a finite common universe.  For times
\(j=0,1,\ldots,T\), let \(\mathcal P_j\) be hypergraphs on \(\mathscr V\)
of rank at most \(R\), and let

\[
 x^{(j)}\in[0,1]^{\mathscr V}
\]

be fractional vertex covers of \(\mathcal P_j\).  Put

\[
 z_E^{(j)}=\min\{x_E^{(j)},1/R\}.
 \tag{5.1}
\]

### Theorem 5.1 — common-threshold recourse theorem

For arbitrary nonnegative holding weights \(a_0,\ldots,a_T\) and recourse
weights \(b_1,\ldots,b_T\), there is one threshold

\[
 0<\theta\le1/R
\]

such that every

\[
 B_j(\theta)=\{E\in\mathscr V:x_E^{(j)}\ge\theta\}
 \tag{5.2}
\]

is an integral vertex cover of \(\mathcal P_j\), and

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
 &\sum_{j=0}^T a_j|B_j(\theta)|
 +\sum_{j=1}^T b_j|B_j(\theta)\triangle B_{j-1}(\theta)|\\
 &\quad\le
 R\left[
 \sum_{j=0}^T a_j\|x^{(j)}\|_1
 +\sum_{j=1}^T b_j
       \|x^{(j)}-x^{(j-1)}\|_1
 \right].
\end{aligned}}
 \tag{5.3}
\]

A sharper right side replaces \(x^{(j)}\) by \(z^{(j)}\) inside the square
brackets.

There is also a pointwise-holding version.  One may choose

\[
 \frac1{2R}\le\theta\le\frac1R
\]

so that every \(B_j(\theta)\) is a vertex cover,

\[
 \boxed{
 |B_j(\theta)|\le2R\|x^{(j)}\|_1
 \qquad(0\le j\le T),
 }
 \tag{5.4}
\]

and

\[
 \boxed{
 \sum_{j=1}^T b_j
 |B_j(\theta)\triangle B_{j-1}(\theta)|
 \le
 2R\sum_{j=1}^T b_j
 \|x^{(j)}-x^{(j-1)}\|_1.
 }
 \tag{5.5}
\]

#### Proof

Choose \(\theta\) uniformly from \((0,1/R]\).  For every packet
\(P\in\mathcal P_j\),

\[
 \sum_{E\in P}x_E^{(j)}\ge1,qquad |P|\le R,
\]

so some \(E\in P\) has \(x_E^{(j)}\ge1/|P|\ge1/R\).  Hence \(B_j(\theta)\)
hits \(P\) for every allowed value of \(\theta\).

For a fixed vertex \(E\),

\[
 \Pr(E\in B_j(\theta))=Rz_E^{(j)}
 \tag{5.6}
\]

and

\[
 \Pr\bigl(E\in B_j(\theta)\triangle B_{j-1}(\theta)\bigr)
 =R|z_E^{(j)}-z_E^{(j-1)}|.
 \tag{5.7}
\]

Multiply by the nonnegative weights and sum.  The expected left side of
(5.3) is exactly

\[
 R\left[
 \sum_j a_j\|z^{(j)}\|_1
 +\sum_j b_j\|z^{(j)}-z^{(j-1)}\|_1
 \right].
\]

Some threshold is no worse than this expectation.  Finally truncation at
\(1/R\) is coordinatewise 1-Lipschitz, so

\[
 \|z^{(j)}\|_1\le\|x^{(j)}\|_1,
 \qquad
 \|z^{(j)}-z^{(j-1)}\|_1
 \le\|x^{(j)}-x^{(j-1)}\|_1.
\]

This proves (5.3).  \(\square\)

For the pointwise version, choose \(\theta\) uniformly from
\([1/(2R),1/R]\).  Every packet is still hit because it contains a
coordinate of value at least \(1/R\).  For every allowed threshold,
membership in \(B_j(\theta)\) implies \(x_E^{(j)}\ge1/(2R)\), which proves
(5.4).  The probability that one coordinate changes membership between two
times is at most

\[
 2R|x_E^{(j)}-x_E^{(j-1)}|.
\]

After summing with the weights \(b_j\), some threshold has recourse no
larger than its expectation.  This proves (5.5).  \(\square\)

### Corollary 5.2 — exact-factor packet recourse

Let \(F_0,\ldots,F_T\) be any exact-factor trajectory, let \(\beta^{(j)}\)
be balanced quotas, and extend fractional packet covers \(x^{(j)}\) by zero
to the union of all wreaths appearing in the trajectory.  Then there are
quota-safe exceptional families

\[
 B_j\subseteq F_j
\]

such that (5.3) holds with \(R=R_A\).  In particular, if

\[
 \max_j\|x^{(j)}\|_1=o(t/\sqrt m)
\]

and the total fractional movement is \(o(t/\sqrt m)\), then the
pointwise-holding version gives

\[
 \max_j|B_j|=o(t/\sqrt m)
\]

and total integral recourse \(o(t/\sqrt m)\).

#### Proof

Use the union of the factor supports as \(\mathscr V\), with
\(x_E^{(j)}=0\) for \(E\notin F_j\).  Since \(\theta>0\), equation (5.2)
gives \(B_j\subseteq F_j\).  Theorem 2.1 turns packet coverage into quota
safety.  Apply Theorem 5.1.  \(\square\)

This does not construct the fractional trajectory.  It proves that once a
slowly moving fractional packet cover is found, integer exceptional-family
recourse is not an additional obstruction.

## 6. Sharpness and scope

### 6.1 The factor \(R\) is unavoidable abstractly

For one resource with \(h\) owners and quota \(\beta\), equation (3.8)
gives

\[
 \frac{\tau}{\vartheta}
 =\frac{(h-\beta)(\beta+1)}h.
 \tag{6.1}
\]

For fixed \(\beta\) and \(h\to\infty\), this tends to \(\beta+1\), the
packet rank.  Therefore no universal factor smaller than \(R-o(1)\) is
possible for arbitrary quota systems.  Wreath geometry may improve the
constant, but fixed-window diagonalization does not require such an
improvement.

### 6.2 Why growing windows are treated diagonally

If \(A=A(m)\to\infty\), then

\[
 \max_{q\le A\sqrt m}c_q
 \asymp e^{A^2}
\]

at the Gaussian scale, and the rank constant \(R_A\) is no longer uniform.
The theorem therefore does not justify a direct growing-window rounding with
an uncontrolled \(A(m)\).  The established fixed-window equivalence is the
right quantifier: prove (4.2) separately for every fixed \(A\), allow
\(R_A\) to deteriorate arbitrarily, and only then diagonalize.

### 6.3 What remains open

The smallest positive gate exposed by this note is:

> **Fractional survival-packet theorem \((\mathrm{FSP}_A)\) — unproved.**  
> For every fixed \(A>0\), find one exact factor \(F_{m,A}\), balanced
> quotas through \(K_A=\lceil A\sqrt m\rceil\), and a fractional cover of
> all survival packets of total mass
> \[
> o_A(\operatorname{Cat}_m/\sqrt m).
> \]

By Theorem 4.1, \((\mathrm{FSP}_A)\) implies MWB.  By (3.4), it is equivalent
to proving that every unit-congestion fractional packing of survival packets
has total mass \(o_A(t/\sqrt m)\).

This gate is strictly weaker than:

1. the integral deletion-transversal lemma;
2. the earlier fractional multicover inequalities \(Ax\ge d\);
3. constructing an exactly balanced factor; and
4. labelled cyclic alignment with \(o(W)\) owner-depth disagreement.

It remains nontrivial because one exact factor determines all owner sets at
all depths.  The ambient factor still must be close enough to balanced that
a very small family of wreaths can intersect every over-capacity survivor
packet.

## 7. Adversarial self-audit

1. **Occurrences are binary per wreath.**  At a fixed proper cyclic length,
   one coordinate order has distinct cyclic intervals.  Thus the owner sets
   in (1.1) are honest sets, not multisets.  Without this fact, packet sizes
   would need occurrence-labelled copies.  Packets themselves may be
   parallel when two different resources have the same owner subset;
   retaining their resource labels does not change any cover value.
2. **Packets encode residual capacity, not overload mass.**  Hitting every
   \((\beta+1)\)-packet is exactly equivalent to retaining at most \(\beta\)
   owners.  No total-mass approximation is used.
3. **All depths use one deletion family.**  The packet hypergraph is the
   union over all controlled \((q,S)\).  One vertex cover \(B\) hits all of
   them simultaneously.
4. **Quota choices remain integral and balanced.**  The packet theorem does
   not manufacture quotas.  It applies after choosing the same balanced
   quota vectors used in the hard-quota formulation.
5. **The threshold proof is deterministic.**  Theorem 3.1 uses the fixed
   threshold \(1/R\).  Randomness in Theorem 5.1 is only an averaging proof
   that one common deterministic threshold exists.
6. **No hidden exact-completion step.**  Since \(B\subseteq F\), the residual
   core and exceptional family already partition one exact factor.
7. **The old fractional Hall LP is not claimed equivalent.**  Equation
   (3.6) proves only that it dominates the packet LP.  Example (3.8) shows
   strictness.
8. **The integrality factor is harmless only for fixed \(A\).**  The proof
   explicitly avoids a growing-parameter matching theorem.  The constant
   \(R_A\) is absorbed only after fixed-window diagonalization.
9. **Two recourse versions are distinguished.**  Equation (5.3) controls an
   aggregate holding-plus-recourse objective with factor \(R\).  Equations
   (5.4)--(5.5) use the narrower threshold interval and factor \(2R\) to
   give pointwise holding bounds together with aggregate recourse.
10. **No proof of FSP is hidden.**  Theorems 2.1--5.1 eliminate bounded-rank
    rounding and recourse as separate obstacles.  They do not construct the
    required small fractional packet cover inside an exact factor.
11. **The order-statistic compression is exact.**  Equation (3.9) replaces
    all packets of one resource by their minimum-weight packet; it does not
    replace different resources or depths by an averaged condition.
12. **The compressed dual is genuinely equivalent.**  The hypersimplex
    decomposition is applied separately inside each owner set, and the
    subsequent Hall inequalities are the complete cuts of one capacitated
    bipartite flow network.
13. **The direct overload transfer uses a chosen balanced quota only as a
    competitor.**  It does not identify that quota with the optimizer in
    \(O_q\), and all inequalities point in the safe direction.

## 8. Final theorem ledger

### Proved

1. Exact survival-packet equivalence for simultaneous hard quotas.
2. Packet rank at most \(2+\max c_q\), hence \(O_A(1)\) on fixed Gaussian
   windows.
3. The integral/fractional sandwich
   \[
   \vartheta\le\tau\le R\vartheta.
   \]
4. The exact unit-congestion fractional packet-packing dual and its
   compressed wreath-cut form (3.14).
5. Strict weakening relative to the former fractional multicover gate.
6. The exact bottom-\((\beta+1)\) order-statistic formulation and the
   forced prebalance ledger (3.12)--(3.13).
7. The direct all-window inequality
   \[
   \sum_{q\le H}O_q/c_q\le3nH\vartheta.
   \]
8. The fixed-window implication \(\mathrm{FSP}_A\Rightarrow HQ_A\Rightarrow
   \mathrm{MWB}\).
9. Common-threshold rounding along an adaptive trajectory: factor \(R_A\)
   for the aggregate objective, and factor \(2R_A\) for simultaneous
   pointwise holding bounds plus aggregate recourse.

### Still open

Construct one exact factor and one balanced quota system for which the
fractional survival-packet cover has mass \(o_A(t/\sqrt m)\) for every fixed
\(A\), or produce a slowly moving fractional cover along an exact-factor
trajectory and use the common-threshold theorem.

Thus the hard-quota lane no longer needs an integral absorber theorem at
fixed Gaussian width.  Its exact remaining content is a correlated
fractional packet-cover theorem inside one exact cyclic-wreath factor.
