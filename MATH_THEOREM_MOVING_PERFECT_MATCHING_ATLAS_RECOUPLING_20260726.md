# Moving perfect-matching atlases: exact recoupling components and the rate-one boundary

Date: 2026-07-26

## 0. Outcome

Let `V=[2h]`, and let `A` be any family of perfect matchings of `V`.
For each matching `M`, its pair-status cells partition the middle layer
`binom(V,h)` into cubes: in a cell, every `M`-edge is declared empty,
full, or split, and the orientations of the split edges vary freely.

The common-support geometry of all these partitions has an exact
description.  Let

\[
                         G_A=(V,\bigcup_{M\in A}M)                \tag{0.1}
\]

and let `B_1,...,B_c` be its connected coordinate components.  Two middle
owners lie in the same cell-overlap component if and only if they have the
same occupancy count in every `B_j`.  Thus the exact common supports are

\[
 U_\kappa=\{X\in\tbinom Vh:|X\cap B_j|=\kappa_j\ (1\le j\le c)\},
 \qquad\sum_j\kappa_j=h.                                      \tag{0.2}
\]

Consequently one may choose an arbitrary matching `M_kappa in A`
independently on every `U_kappa`, factor all its status cells there, and
obtain an exact middle factor.  This is a literal common-support
recoupling theorem; no fractional completion or rounding is present.

It also gives a statewise obstruction.

* If `G_A` is connected, the entire middle layer is one overlap component,
  so the atlas permits only one **global** matching choice.  Componentwise
  mixing of genuinely far matchings is impossible.
* Owner-dependent matching choices are possible only when all atlas
  matchings preserve one common coordinate block decomposition.
* The invariant `kappa` is unchanged by all orientation changes along
  completed matched pairs.  Therefore choosing `M=M(kappa)` contributes
  zero information toward recovering the erased orientation bits.  The
  rate-one completed-support entropy forced by
  `MATH_THEOREM_NONAFFINE_PARITY_LIFT_AND_SUPPORT_ENTROPY_OBSTRUCTION_20260726.md`
  must still be supplied inside each status cell.

On the positive side, for a uniformly random middle owner the number of
split pairs is `(1/2+o(1))h`, uniformly for every invariant selector
`M(kappa)`.  Hence a recursive isometric cell factor completes exactly
`q` matched pairs in every `q`-window for `q<=A sqrt(h)`, outside an
`o(1)` fraction of owners.  The moving atlas therefore solves exact
recoupling and pair-completion density, but not the rate-one trace code or
the remaining cross-cell Hall problem.

## 1. Pair-status cells

For a perfect matching `M` of `V` and `X in binom(V,h)`, define

\[
 C_M(X)=\{Y\in\tbinom Vh:|Y\cap e|=|X\cap e|
                       \text{ for every }e\in M\}.              \tag{1.1}
\]

If `s_M(X)` edges of `M` are split by `X`, then `C_M(X)` is canonically
the cube `Q_{s_M(X)}`: one independently chooses which endpoint of every
split edge is occupied.  The empty and full edges are frozen.  The cells
`C_M(X)` partition the middle layer.

Make a multipartite cell-overlap graph whose vertices are pairs `(M,C)`,
where `C` is an `M`-cell, and join two cells when they contain a common
middle owner.  Equivalently, put an equivalence relation on middle owners
generated by

\[
 X\sim_M Y\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad C_M(X)=C_M(Y).          \tag{1.2}
\]

The equivalence classes are precisely the common supports on which one
may replace all cells of one matching by all cells of another.

## 2. Exact component theorem

### Theorem 2.1 (exclusion-process component classification)

For middle owners `X,Y`, the following are equivalent.

1. `X` and `Y` lie in the same component of the atlas cell overlap.
2. For every connected component `B_j` of `G_A`,
   \[
                              |X\cap B_j|=|Y\cap B_j|.           \tag{2.1}
   \]

Hence the overlap components are exactly the sets `U_kappa` in (0.2).

#### Proof

If `X` and `Y` lie in one `M`-cell, then one obtains `Y` from `X` by a
sequence of orientation flips on split edges of `M`.  Each flip replaces
one occupied endpoint of an edge by its unoccupied endpoint.  It is a
single-particle move along an edge of `G_A`, so it preserves the number of
occupied vertices in every coordinate component `B_j`.  This proves the
necessity of (2.1) along every overlap path.

Conversely, fix one connected graph `G_A[B_j]` and two subsets of `B_j`
of the same size.  The simple exclusion graph on the `k`-subsets of any
connected graph is connected: move particles one at a time along a
spanning tree, starting from the leaves, to transform either configuration
to the same canonical `k`-set.  Every elementary exclusion move toggles a
split edge `e` belonging to some matching `M in A`, and its two endpoints
therefore lie in one `M`-cell.  Applying this independently in all `B_j`
connects `X` and `Y` through atlas cells. \(\square\)

### Theorem 2.2 (exact moving-atlas recoupling)

For every admissible vector `kappa` choose a matching
`M_kappa in A`.  Within `U_kappa`, take all `M_kappa`-status cells, and in
each cell install any exact cycle factor of that cell.  The union over all
`kappa` is an exact middle factor.

More generally, any two choices can be switched independently on an
arbitrary subfamily of the `U_kappa`.

#### Proof

Every edge of every matching in `A` is contained in one coordinate
component `B_j`; hence an `M`-cell is contained in one `U_kappa`.
Conversely the `M`-cells partition `U_kappa`.  Therefore the selected cell
factors partition each `U_kappa` owner by owner, and the sets `U_kappa`
partition the middle layer. \(\square\)

This theorem is stronger than a pairwise component switch: it permits an
arbitrary matching from the whole atlas on every exact common support.

### Corollary 2.3 (connected-atlas rigidity)

If `G_A` is connected, there is only one admissible vector, namely
`kappa=(h)`, and the moving-atlas selection is global.  In particular, if
two matchings `M,M'` have connected union (equivalently their alternating
union is one cycle after common edges are suppressed), their status-cell
partitions have one overlap component on the middle layer.

Thus making the matchings genuinely distant generally destroys, rather
than creates, componentwise freedom.

### Corollary 2.4 (amount of owner-adaptive information)

If the coordinate-component sizes are `b_j=|B_j|`, the number `K_A` of
independent common supports satisfies

\[
 K_A\le\prod_{j=1}^c(b_j+1),\qquad
 \log_2K_A\le\sum_{j=1}^c\log_2(b_j+1).                         \tag{2.2}
\]

Also `K_A<=(2h+1)^(c-1)`, since the last occupancy count is determined by
the first `c-1`.  Hence an atlas with only
`c=o(sqrt(h)/log h)` coordinate components cannot even expose
`sqrt(h)` bits through its owner-component label.

This last information bound is only about the moving-matching selector;
the internal cell factor may carry additional support entropy.

For an equal-scale atlas in which all coordinate components have size
`b` (so `c=2h/b`), the useful upper estimate is

\[
                         \log_2K_A\le {2h\over b}\log_2(b+1)   \tag{2.3}
\]

Unequal blocks are governed by the exact sum in (2.2).  Therefore an
equal-scale argument asking the atlas label
itself to supply `q=Theta(sqrt h)` bits must work at block scale
`b=O(sqrt h log h)`.  A larger connected block has too few occupancy
labels; bounded blocks retain plenty of labels but confine all matching
edges to one fixed microscopic decomposition.

### Corollary 2.5 (the local pair-frame associator is one atlas component)

Suppose `M,M'` differ by the recoupling

\[
                         ab|cd\quad\longleftrightarrow\quad ac|bd
\]

on one four-coordinate block and agree elsewhere.  Then `G_{M,M'}` has
that `B_4` as one component and every common matching edge as a two-vertex
component.  Impose occupancy two on `B_4`, occupancy one on two common
reservoir edges, and fixed occupancy zero or two on all other common
edges.  The corresponding common support is

\[
 \binom{B_4}{2}\times Q_2,
\]

of size `6*4=24`.  It is exactly the support of
`MATH_THEOREM_LOCAL_PAIR_FRAME_ASSOCIATOR_20260726.md`.

Thus the six-for-six associator is not an isolated coincidence: it is the
first nontrivial `U_kappa` in the general exclusion-component theorem.
The connectedness of its cell overlap is also immediate from Theorem 2.1.

At the opposite extreme, the atlas of **all** perfect matchings has union
graph `K_(2h)`, hence one middle overlap component.  Despite its enormous
catalogue, it permits only a global matching choice under componentwise
cell recoupling.

## 3. Typical cells have linear dimension

The exact recoupling theorem would be useless if most selected cells had
too few split pairs.  They do not.

Fix the coordinate components `B_j`, an occupancy vector `kappa`, and any
matching `M in A`.  Conditional on `X` being uniform in `U_kappa`, its
restrictions to the blocks are independent uniform `kappa_j`-subsets.
For an `M`-edge inside a block of size `b` with occupancy `k`, the
probability of being split is

\[
                         {2k(b-k)\over b(b-1)}.                  \tag{3.1}
\]

Since there are `b/2` matching edges, the conditional expected number of
split edges in that block is

\[
                              {k(b-k)\over b-1}.                 \tag{3.2}
\]

Crucially, this value is independent of which perfect matching inside the
block was selected.  Thus a selector `M=M(kappa)` cannot bias the first
moment of the cell dimension.

### Proposition 3.1 (uniform linear split count)

Let `X` be uniform in the full middle layer, and choose
`M=M(kappa(X))` by an arbitrary moving-atlas selector.  Then

\[
 \mathbb E s_M(X)={h^2\over2h-1}={h\over2}+O(1),
 \qquad \operatorname {Var}(s_M(X))=O(h).                       \tag{3.3}
\]

Consequently

\[
                  \Pr\{s_M(X)<h/3\}=O(1/h)=o(1).               \tag{3.4}
\]

#### Proof

Conditional on `kappa`, the **entire distribution** of the split count is
independent of the selected matching: inside each `B_j`, any two perfect
matchings are conjugate by a coordinate permutation, and the uniform
`kappa_j`-subset law is permutation invariant.  Thus the unconditional
split-count distribution under an arbitrary selector is exactly the one
for any fixed reference matching.  For a fixed matching, write
`s_M` as the sum of the `h` split-edge indicators.  Direct two-edge
hypergeometric counting gives bounded individual variances and total
pair covariance `O(h)`; hence `Var(s_M)=O(h)`.  The mean follows from
(3.1) with `b=2h,k=h`, or directly from

\[
 \Pr\{|X\cap e|=1\}
 ={2\binom{2h-2}{h-1}\over\binom{2h}{h}}
 ={h\over2h-1}.
\]

Chebyshev proves (3.4). \(\square\)

### Corollary 3.2 (Gaussian windows complete linearly many pairs)

Install in every selected `Q_s` cell an isometric recursive factor whose
direction word has no repetition in a half-cycle.  Every `q`-window with
`q<=s` then completes exactly `q` distinct matched-pair directions.
Uniformly for `q<=A sqrt(h)`, this holds for all but `o(W)` middle owners.

Thus the moving atlas meets the required `Theta(q)` pair-completion scale;
the obstruction is not lack of active pairs.

## 4. Why moving the matching does not itself supply trace entropy

Fix a coordinate component vector `kappa`, so the selected matching is
`M_kappa`.  Let a `q`-window complete a set `J` of `q` split edges.  Its
`2^q` possible initial orientations all have exactly one occupied endpoint
on every edge of `J`.  They therefore have the same occupancy vector
`kappa`, and hence the **same selected matching**.

For a lower trace `L`, if `q_j` completed edges lie in `B_j`, then

\[
                  \kappa_j=|L\cap B_j|+q_j.                     \tag{4.1}
\]

For an upper trace `U`,

\[
                  \kappa_j=|U\cap B_j|-q_j.                     \tag{4.2}
\]

Therefore the atlas label `M_kappa` is a deterministic function of the
signed trace together with its completed support `J`.  It is not an
additional hidden hash of the erased orientations.

### Theorem 4.1 (support-entropy bound survives the moving atlas)

For a fixed selected cell factor, let `N_q(x)` be the number of completed
edge supports obtainable from an internal start `x` as the erased
orientation context varies.  The aligned trace bound remains

\[
 D_q\le 2^{s-q}\sum_{x\in Q_s}N_q(x),                            \tag{4.3}
\]

inside every `Q_s` cell.  Choosing `M=M(kappa)` does not enlarge
`N_q(x)` on any orientation fibre, because `kappa` is constant on that
fibre.  Consequently near-injectivity still requires

\[
                  2^{-s}\sum_xN_q(x)\ge(1-o(1))2^{q-1}.         \tag{4.4}
\]

#### Proof

Apply Theorem 3.1 of
`MATH_THEOREM_NONAFFINE_PARITY_LIFT_AND_SUPPORT_ENTROPY_OBSTRUCTION_20260726.md`
to the `s` orientation bits of the status cell.  The observation preceding
the theorem shows that the moving matching is fixed throughout this whole
orientation fibre, so it contributes no extra support choices. \(\square\)

Hence a recursive trace-injective cell factor is not optional.  If it is
available, it supplies the required local rate-one library; the moving
atlas preserves it but does not create it.

## 5. Exact remaining gate after recoupling

The moving-matching atlas has now been separated into proved and open
parts.

### Proved

1. The common-support components are exactly the occupancy strata
   `U_kappa`.
2. Any function `kappa -> M_kappa` gives an integral exact middle factor.
3. Typical selected cells have dimension `Theta(h)`, uniformly over that
   function.
4. An isometric cell factor completes `Theta(q)` matched pairs in every
   Gaussian window.
5. Matching selection cannot replace the rate-one internal support code.

### Still required

Choose a block-decomposed atlas, a selector `M_kappa`, and recursive
trace-injective factors in its status cells so that the **cross-cell** and
**cross-kappa** lower and upper target images have aggregate deficit
`o(W)` throughout the protected band.

Theorem 2.1 shows why a fully connected atlas cannot do this by adaptive
mixing: it has only a global matching choice.  Conversely, taking many
small coordinate components restores exact adaptivity but confines every
matching edge to a fixed block system.  Whether a growing block scale can
simultaneously avoid the known fixed-frame Gaussian Hall cuts and keep the
outer target deficiency `o(W)` is the remaining quantitative problem.
