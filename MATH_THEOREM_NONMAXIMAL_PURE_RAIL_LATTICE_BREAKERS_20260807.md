# Nonmaximal pure rails remove the closed-carousel scalar and coordinate gcd barriers

**Date:** 2026-08-07  
**Status:** unconditional local and lattice theorem.  Nonnegative exact packing,
target decoration, and global fusion remain open.

## 1. Every sufficiently long component size is available

Put

\[
 q=d+1,\qquad c=R-q,\qquad M=k-c=k-R+q.
\tag{1.1}
\]

The pure rail is the unified-queue type

\[
 a=d,\qquad p=1,\qquad h_0=1.
\]

For any integer (N) satisfying

\[
 \boxed{2(q+1)\le N\le M,}
\tag{1.2}
\]

choose a core (C) of size (c) and (N) toggle coordinates in a cyclic
order.  Use the period-(N) source

\[
 A_i=C\cup\{x_i\}.
\tag{1.3}
\]

Exactly as in the unified rail theorem, its owner row is

\[
 O_i=C\cup\{x_i,x_{i-1},\ldots,x_{i-q+1}\},
\tag{1.4}
\]

and has the following properties:

* (N) distinct rank-(R) owners forming a Johnson cycle;
* simple lower and upper q1 palettes;
* simple interval values at every proper width;
* toggle owner run (q) and gap (N-q\ge q+2);
* complete literal regeneration after (N) source positions.

The component uses (c+N\le k) coordinates.  Thus the pure-rail family
contains a closed legal component of **every** size in the full interval

\[
 \boxed{N=2d+4,2d+5,\ldots,k-R+d+1.}
\tag{1.5}
\]

For the central parameters, this interval has length (Theta(k)).

## 2. Scalar lattice

### Theorem 2.1

For all sufficiently large (k), the gcd of the available closed-component
sizes is one.  In fact, both (M-1) and (M) occur.

Consequently every sufficiently large integer, in particular the owner count
(W={k\choose R}), is a nonnegative integer combination of legal pure-rail
component sizes.

#### Proof

Since (M=\Theta(k)) and (q=\Theta(\sqrt k)), eventually
(M-1\ge2(q+1)).  Equation (1.5) then contains the consecutive integers
(M-1,M), whose gcd is one.  The two-generator Frobenius theorem represents
every integer greater than (M(M-1)-M-(M-1)).  The central binomial
coefficient (W) is exponentially larger.  \(□\)

This eliminates the obstruction (N\mid W) created by restricting to one
maximal type.

## 3. First-order coordinate-incidence lattice

In a size-(N) pure rail, a ground coordinate contributes to the owner row
with multiplicity

\[
 \begin{cases}
 N,&\text{core role},\\
 q,&\text{toggle role},\\
 0,&\text{unused role}.
 \end{cases}
\tag{3.1}

Let (Lambda_{m diff}) be the lattice generated by differences of
coordinate-incidence vectors of parameterized pure-rail copies, allowing all
available sizes.

### Theorem 3.1 (primitive coordinate-difference lattice)

For all sufficiently large (k),

\[
 \boxed{
 \Lambda_{m diff}supseteq
 \{z\in\mathbb Z^k:\sum_i z_i=0\}.}
\tag{3.2}
\]

Equivalently, every unit difference (e_x-e_y) is an integral signed
combination of legal carousel-copy differences.

#### Proof

Fix distinct coordinates (x,y), and keep all other roles fixed.

* Swapping (x,y) between a core role and an unused role in a size-(N)
  component gives (N(e_x-e_y)).
* Swapping them between a toggle role and an unused role gives
  (q(e_x-e_y)).

The required spare roles exist because (c,q\ge1) and, for a nonmaximal
component, at least one coordinate is unused.  Apply the first operation at
sizes (M-1) and (M-2) (or any two consecutive available nonmaximal
sizes).  Their coefficients are consecutive, hence Bezout gives
(e_x-e_y).  The unit differences generate the zero-sum integer lattice.
□

If one wishes to use the maximal size (M), the same conclusion follows from
(gcd(q,M,M-1)=1), using toggle--unused and core--unused swaps wherever the
roles exist.

### Corollary 3.2

The coordinate congruence

\[
 \gcd(q,N_\theta)\mid {k-1\choose R-1}
\]

from a maximal-only family disappears after adjoining two consecutive
nonmaximal pure-rail sizes.  There is no scalar or first-order coordinate
gcd separator for the enlarged closed-component catalogue.

This is a lattice statement, not a nonnegative packing theorem: an absorber
must realize the required signed generators around a large positive packing.

For (k=17,R=9,d=3),

\[
 q=4,\qquad c=5,\qquad M=12.
\]

The legal pure-rail sizes are

\[
 \boxed{N\in\{10,11,12\}.}
\tag{4.1}
\]

Thus the maximal-only divisibility failure

\[
 12\nmid {17\choose9}=24310

\[
 24310=10+2025\cdot12.
\tag{4.2}

The first-order role coefficients (4,10,11,12) have gcd one, so the

## 5. Correct continuation of the rounding programme

The integral host should therefore be split into two scales:

1. use maximal components for the pseudorandom bulk nibble and full upper
   reach;
2. reserve a sparse bank of nonmaximal sizes (M-1,M-2) (and, when useful,
   other sizes from (1.5)) as lattice generators and absorbers.

The remaining theorem is a **positive realization** of the signed lattice:
construct a bounded-density absorber in which each unit owner/target residue
can be toggled by the adjacent-transposition birail trades, while all chosen
components remain disjoint and the upper/compiler tickets survive.

This note proves that opened components are not required merely to defeat the
elementary closed-component gcd.  Opened components may still be useful for
global path topology or terminal Ferrers boundaries.
