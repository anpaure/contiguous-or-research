# A clean folded-C8 endpoint is full-block exact only after prospective host planting

Date: 2026-08-01  
Lane: R, addressed folded-C8 host embedding  
Status: unconditional full-block transport lemma; primary exact endpoint-seam
census for `2<=d<=12` plus an independent endpoint-source/full-block-interface
replay of its displayed witnesses; and an exact triangular obstruction to
invoking that lemma from the maximal source word.  No all-`d` seam theorem,
two-ended host embedding, zero-charge reset, or `B(k)+O(1)` conclusion is
claimed.

## 0. Verdict

The zero-block theorem removes the antitone/comparator problem.  Once the
literal two-host module is present, its phasewise diagonal
`U_(2d-2,2d-2)` is already stronger than the two cross-perfect-matchings
criterion.  Moreover, if its host letters are **actual letters of the old
source word**, splitting them into union-equal blocks automatically
transports every admissible old interval matching.  The ray side cells are
outside the full-block image, so no separate background Hall theorem is
needed in that case.

The new clean endpoint seam is a genuine positive topology/palette/residence
result.  In each audited depth it gives two resident paths on all `8d+24`
owners, complete sixteen-value upper support, and distinct lower colours on
all retained edges.  Its one-sided endpoint ray is a pure full-block split
of a **prospectively planted** endpoint word at the compiler level.  The
split itself is not owner-neutral: it replaces the first depth-`d` owner
window by a two-window endpoint ear.

It is not, however, a pure refinement of the maximal old source word used to
find the seam.  Before splitting, the construction strictly shrinks source
positions `0,...,d-1`.  Under the natural full-block address map, exactly

\[
                 \{[i,j]:0\le i\le j<d\}                 \tag{0.1}
\]

change value, a triangular bank of size `d(d+1)/2` in each phase.  Every
change is a loss of literal coordinates.  Thus support preservation does
not imply that an old occurrence matching transports.  The exact remaining
host theorem is prospective planting (or an alternative address return) for
this triangle, a reflected second endpoint socket, and payment/recycling of
the raw `+1` split per planted socket.

## 1. Full-block refinement needs no background Hall theorem

Let `A=(A_0,...,A_(n-1))` be a nonempty source word.  At a set `P` of old
positions replace each letter by a nonempty consecutive block

\[
               A_p\rightsquigarrow
               (B_{p,1},\ldots,B_{p,t_p}),
 \qquad \bigcup_{h=1}^{t_p}B_{p,h}=A_p.              \tag{1.1}
\]

All positions not in `P` are unchanged.  For an old interval `I`, let
`I^*` be the new interval which uses the whole replacement block at every
old position of `I`.

### Theorem 1.1 (simultaneous full-block transport)

The map `I -> I^*` is injective and

\[
                       \operatorname{OR}(I^*)
                       =\operatorname{OR}(I).          \tag{1.2}
\]

Consequently every injective old target-to-interval matching transports
injectively.  If the complete old cap state admitted `A_p` at position `p`
and each new piece is contained in `A_p`, transported background rows impose
no new cap restriction.  They reconstruct by (1.1).

Any new interval which uses a proper nonempty part of one replacement block
but not the whole block is outside the image.  Therefore mandatory ray cells
chosen among such side intervals are automatically disjoint from every
transported old matching cell.

#### Proof

The ordered list of old positions met by `I` is recoverable from `I^*`, so
the map is injective.  Equation (1.2) is the union of (1.1) over those
positions.  Every old assigned target containing `A_p` contains each
`B_(p,h)`, and the complete block still contributes `A_p`; hence cap
containment and reconstruction both persist.  A proper-side interval cannot
equal `I^*`, because every image interval contains either all or none of
each block.  \(\square\)

This is the load-bearing distinction from a same-support replacement.
Equality of distinct target supports, or even equality of their counters,
is unnecessary under (1.1) and insufficient without an addressed map.

### Corollary 1.2 (canonical two-host module)

If literal old host letters `X_L,X_R` occur, then

\[
 X_L\mapsto(X_L,L_\epsilon),\qquad
 X_R\mapsto(R_\epsilon,X_R),                         \tag{1.3}
\]

with `L_epsilon subseteq X_L` and `R_epsilon subseteq X_R`, is full-block
exact.  The old matching transports, while the `2d-2` prefix/suffix ray
cells which trim one host block are private.  Thus the phasewise diagonal
already gives a zero-defect local terminal bank; the antidiagonal
cross-matching criterion is not an additional gate after (1.3) exists.

The antidiagonal notation still has one important occurrence correction:
`P_0(j)<->S_1(j+1)` and `P_1(j)<->S_0(j+1)` are paired **two-cell tickets**,
not one physical cell.  In the isolated rail the corresponding addresses
are `[1,j]` and `[j+1,d]`.

## 2. The clean endpoint seam

Suppress the fixed core.  Put

\[
 X=\{z,a_1,a_3\},\quad
 N_0=\{z,a_3\},\quad N_1=\{z,a_1\}.                 \tag{2.1}
\]

The frozen seam catalogue starts with one upper-complete folded cycle in
each phase.  It deletes a common host edge and a second common edge whose
upper colour is repeated, then adds the common Johnson seam joining the two
newly exposed facets.  In the normalized audited example the three edges
are, with `F={f_1,...,f_d}` and fresh boundary fillers `f_0,f_(d+1)`,

\[
\begin{aligned}
 e_H&:\quad X\cup F
      \longleftrightarrow \{z,a_1\}\cup F\cup\{f_{d+1}\},\\
 e_R&:\quad \{z,a_3,f_0\}\cup F[2,d+1]
      \longleftrightarrow \{z,a_3\}\cup F[1,d+1],\\
 e_S&:\quad \{z,a_1\}\cup F[1,d+1]
      \longleftrightarrow \{z,a_3\}\cup F[1,d+1].  \tag{2.2}
\end{aligned}
\]

Remove `e_H,e_R` and add `e_S`.  A further common repeated-upper edge may
be cut in the remaining cycle.

### Theorem 2.1 (authenticated finite endpoint forest)

For every `2<=d<=12`, the displayed operation has exactly two catalogued
seam choices and the following properties in both phases.

1. After the three-edge operation the factor is one path on `d+2` owners
   and one cycle on `7d+22` owners.
2. Both components are cyclic/linear depth-`d` resident as appropriate.
3. The retained lower colours are all distinct, of support `8d+23`.
4. All sixteen upper support values remain present.
5. Every one of `7d+9` common safe edges of the large cycle may be cut to
   give two resident paths of sizes `d+2` and `7d+22`; the resulting
   `8d+22` lower colours remain pairwise distinct and all sixteen upper
   values remain present.
6. On the short path, the endpoint source can be rethreaded into a
   decreasing-filler source `B` with the same owner chronology.  Splitting
   `B_0=X` into the two halves of `X` then makes the new one-sided ray cells
   literal and pairwise distinct, but is **not** owner-neutral: it replaces
   the first owner by a two-owner endpoint ear.  Owner legality of that ear
   is an additional host-embedding condition.

#### Audit basis

Items 1--5 are certified by the primary frozen seam catalogue, whose checker
rebuilds both phase edge sets, degrees and components; traverses the path and
cycle; recomputes lower intersections, upper unions and every coordinate run;
and enumerates all common safe final cuts.  The independent interface replay
reads the displayed witness in each catalogue row, reconstructs its endpoint
path, recomputes the maximal source and endpoint rethread, checks the dilation,
and then checks every old interval under the literal address map.  It does
**not** constitute a second enumeration of all seam choices or all final
cuts.  The all-`d` formula in (2.2) is visible in every row, but the theorem
here deliberately retains the authenticated `2<=d<=12` quantifier.

The separate attempted `d<=64` stress run produced no enlarged artifact
before its capped process ended; it is classified `UNKNOWN`, not evidence
for or against the symbolic extension.

### Theorem 2.2 (the two cross banks do not follow)

On the unmodified common-endpoint cuts, the independent `2<=d<=12` replay
finds

\[
        \texttt{exact\_one\_ray\_comparators}=0              \tag{2.3}
\]

at every depth.  Its best cuts have upper support only `15`, not `16`.
Conversely, the three-edge seam in Theorem 2.1 restores all sixteen upper
values, but it may place a coordinate needed by the transported prefix ray
on the other component.  Therefore Theorem 2.1 proves only a phasewise
one-sided endpoint rethread.  It does not realize both zero-block cross
perfect matchings in one cap/guard state.

This is an occurrence/topology distinction, not a contradiction: the
suffix ray in Section 3 is literal at the compiler-address level, while the
missing simultaneous prefix ray belongs to a different component after the
upper-safe seam.  The endpoint split also has the separate owner-ear effect
recorded in Theorem 3.1 below.

## 3. Prospective endpoint word and automatic transport

Let `A^epsilon` be the maximal source of the short path.  The endpoint
routine first forms a smaller word `B^epsilon` of the same length by setting

\[
 B_0^\epsilon=X,\qquad
 B_i^\epsilon=A_i^\epsilon\cap
       (N_\epsilon\cup\{f_{d+1-i}\})\quad(1\le i<d), 
                                                               \tag{3.1}
\]

and leaving every later letter unchanged.  It verifies

\[
                       D^dB^\epsilon=T^\epsilon.       \tag{3.2}
\]

Now replace `B_0^epsilon=X` by the two consecutive nonempty halves

\[
 (N_{1-\epsilon},N_\epsilon),qquad
 N_{1-\epsilon}\cup N_\epsilon=X.                    \tag{3.3}
\]

Call the resulting word `tilde B^epsilon`.

### Theorem 3.1 (prospective host is full-block exact)

The passage `B^epsilon -> tilde B^epsilon` is exactly Theorem 1.1 with one
split block.  Its natural interval map is

\[
 [0,j]\mapsto[0,j+1],\qquad
 [i,j]\mapsto[i+1,j+1]\quad(i>0).                   \tag{3.4}
\]

Every old `B`-matching transports injectively.  The intervals

\[
                       [1,j],\qquad 2\le j\le d,      \tag{3.5}
\]

are outside the image and give the advertised one-sided ray.  Thus there is
no ambient matching or cap obstruction **provided `B` itself is the old
admissible source word**.

However, full-block transport is not owner transport.  Write
`T=D^dB`.  Directly from the depth-`d` window definition,

\[
 D^d\widetilde B=
 \bigl(B_0\cup\cdots\cup B_{d-1},\ 
       N_\epsilon\cup B_1\cup\cdots\cup B_d,\
       T_1,T_2,\ldots\bigr).                         \tag{3.6}
\]

Thus the old tail beginning at `T_1` is retained verbatim, but the old first
owner `T_0=B_0\cup\cdots\cup B_d` is replaced by two endpoint owners and
the owner-word length increases by one.  In the audited clean seam this is
a genuine change, not a duplicate-window insertion.  A physical embedding
must therefore certify both new endpoint owners and their incident lower
and upper colours; Theorem 1.1 alone does not do so.

Indeed the value at address `[1,j]` is

\[
             N_\epsilon\cup F[d+2-j,d]
             \qquad(2\le j\le d),                    \tag{3.7}
\]

so these addresses enumerate the complete nested suffix ray exactly once.
This part of the argument is symbolic for every `d>=2`; only its simultaneous
placement in the upper-safe owner forest retains the finite audit scope of
Theorem 2.1.

The raw source-length charge of (3.3) is exactly `+1`.  It is zero only if
the two-cell block was prospectively allocated, one old cell is contracted
elsewhere with a proved full-block return, or an exact clipped-boundary
accounting identifies an already counted address.  The owner cut alone does
not prove any of these alternatives.

The union-equal block does recycle the **screen value** for free: every
ambient interval entering from its left and crossing the complete block
contains `N_(1-epsilon) union N_epsilon=X`.  Hence no separate screen letter
is needed for such crossing rows.  This removes a screen sidecar, but it does
not remove the extra physical address created by the split.

#### Proof

Equations (3.3)--(3.5) are Theorem 1.1 at the left endpoint.  An image
interval containing the split block contains both positions `0,1`; an
interval in (3.5) contains the near half and omits the far half, so the two
banks are disjoint.  Sliding a depth-`d` window across the split gives
(3.6).  Length increases from `|B|` to `|B|+1`.  \(\square\)

## 4. Exact triangular obstruction from the maximal source

The previous theorem cannot be applied with `A^epsilon` in place of
`B^epsilon`.  The finite replay gives, in the normalization (2.1),

\[
 A_0^\epsilon=X\cup F[1,d],qquad
 A_i^\epsilon=N_0\cup F[1,d+1-i]\quad(1\le i<d).    \tag{4.1}
\]

For phase zero,

\[
 B_i^0=N_0\cup\{f_{d+1-i}\}\quad(1\le i<d),        \tag{4.2}
\]

whereas for phase one,

\[
 B_i^1=\{z,f_{d+1-i}\}\quad(1\le i<d).             \tag{4.3}
\]

All positions `0,...,d-1` shrink strictly; no coordinate is gained.

### Theorem 4.1 (exact failed full-block triangle)

For every audited `2<=d<=12` and each phase `epsilon`, under the natural
split-address map (3.4), an old interval of `A^epsilon` changes OR value if
and only if its address is in (0.1).  Consequently exactly `d(d+1)/2` old
physical cells fail to transport by this natural map in each phase.  Every
changed value is a strict subset of the old value.

More explicitly, write `0<=i<=j<d`.

* If `i=0`, the lost coordinate set is `F[1,d-j]` in either phase.
* If `i>0`, the phase-zero loss is `F[1,d-j]`, while the phase-one loss is
  `\{a_3\} union F[1,d-j]`.

Every old interval with `j>=d` keeps its value under the natural map.

#### Proof

The letters in (4.1) are nested on the audited prefix.  Taking their unions
and comparing with (4.2)--(4.3) gives the three displayed loss formulas.
Each is nonempty because `j<d`.  The exact replay verifies that the first
unchanged boundary letter at position `d` restores all omitted coordinates;
therefore every interval reaching it, and every interval starting later,
is unchanged.  This proves both directions and the triangular count.
\(\square\)

The construction does preserve the **set** of old support values: lost
values have other witnesses.  That is not enough for a fixed occurrence
matching.  If an old matching used any address in (0.1), its natural image
now reconstructs a proper subset of the assigned target.  This is a
reconstruction failure, not a source-cap overflow.

## 5. Exact remaining two-ended theorem

The active positive route is now sharply scoped.

1. **Prospective host.**  Supply the short endpoint word `B^epsilon`, not
   merely the maximal word `A^epsilon`, as an admissible old source word; or
   give an explicit address permutation returning all matched occurrences
   used in the triangle (0.1).
2. **Owner ear.**  Embed the two new endpoint owners in (3.6) without a
   duplicate or missing middle owner and with their two incident palettes
   exact.  Compiler full-block transport does not imply this row.
3. **Second shore.**  Plant a compatible second endpoint/cross bank so both
   folded ray chains, hence both two-cell antidiagonals, are present in one
   owner/palette/cap/guard state.  A formal reflection alone is insufficient:
   the upper-safe seam may put the required prefix coordinate on the other
   component.
4. **Zero charge.**  Reuse the split blocks or contract one source address
   per socket.  Raw equal phase length is `+1` per split, not `chi=0`.
5. **Cut integration.**  Keep the two common owner seams, one lost lower
   edge per final path opening, all sixteen upper witnesses and every
   residence boundary simultaneously, and accept the two new endpoint-ear
   owners from (3.6).  The finite endpoint forest proves a large local menu
   but not a global Catalan placement.

Once these five rows hold by literal full-block refinement, Theorem 1.1
automatically carries the background matching and common cap.  No separate
comparator ordering and no separate background Hall theorem remain.
