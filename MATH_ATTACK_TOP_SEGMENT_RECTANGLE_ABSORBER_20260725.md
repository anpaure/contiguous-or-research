# Complementary top segments as rank-isolating rectangle absorbers

Date: 2026-07-25

Pure mathematics only.  This note proves the four-order interval identity,
refutes its full-packet 0/2 deployment, and audits the complementary-segment
repair including its exact endpoint counterterm, owner overlap, integral
lattice, and positivity limitations.

## 0. Verdict

Let \(U\) be a calibrated top of size

\[
 M=m+H,\qquad H=(1+o(1))\sqrt{m\log m},\qquad
 N_H=(1+o(1))\frac Wm.                                                     \tag{0.1}
\]

Take two disjoint adjacent position swaps in a cyclic order of \(U\).  If
their cyclic boundary separation is \(r\), the mixed four-order difference

\[
 \pi_{00}+\pi_{11}-\pi_{10}-\pi_{01}                                      \tag{0.2}
\]

has zero interval incidence at every length except \(r\) and \(M-r\).
At length \(r\), it is one elementary rectangle

\[
 e_{Cbc}+e_{Cad}-e_{Cac}-e_{Cbd},\qquad |C|=r-2.                           \tag{0.3}
\]

The complementary length carries the complement rectangle.

The direct use of the two diagonal full packets is fatal.  They differ by
only two adjacent swaps and share at least \(M-4\) of their \(M\) middle
owners.  A 0/2 deployment with total packet count \(N_H\) therefore covers
at most

\[
 \frac{M+4}{2}N_H=(1/2+o(1))W                                             \tag{0.4}
\]

distinct owners even before cross-top collisions.

The proposed complementary-segment repair is algebraically valid.  Let
\(A,B\subseteq\mathbb Z_M\) be two cyclic intervals of phase starts with

\[
 A\cup B=\mathbb Z_M.                                                      \tag{0.5}
\]

Use \(\pi_{00}\) on \(A\) and \(\pi_{11}\) on \(B\) for one diagonal,
and \(\pi_{10}\) on \(A\) and \(\pi_{01}\) on \(B\) for the other.  If
\(A\cap B\) contains the two starts affected by the first adjacent swap at
every protected interval length, then the truncated diagonal difference is
**exactly** the full mixed difference (0.2) at every protected length.

For the hard window \(m-Q,\ldots,m+Q\), the required overlap has size
\(O(Q)\).  It consists of one neighborhood at the swap edge and one near
the antipodal \(m\)-boundary.  If only this overlap is used, lengths outside
the hard window acquire the explicit single-swap counterterm (3.10) below.
Taking an \(O(H)\) overlap instead protects every proper interval rank in
the calibrated band, \(m-H,\ldots,m+H-1\); the rank-\((m+H)\) top is
order-independent.  This stronger choice is still cheap:

\[
 HN_H=o(W).                                                               \tag{0.6}
\]

With the full-band overlap, choosing separation \(r=m-q\) isolates lower
depth \(q\), and choosing \(r=m+q\) isolates upper depth \(q\).  The other
exceptional length is \(H+q\), respectively \(H-q\), which lies below the
controlled band for large \(m\).

The rank-\(r\) signed lattice generated by these absorbers is exactly

\[
 \boxed{
 \ker_{\mathbb Z}U_r
 =\left\{z:\sum_{S\ni i}z_S=0\text{ for every coordinate }i\right\}.}      \tag{0.7}
\]

There is no additional parity or finite-index invariant.  Because either
diagonal may be declared positive, the signed cone is the whole real
kernel.  Coordinate marginals are the exact surviving invariants.

The segment repair also fixes the owner-overlap catastrophe.  Relative to
one full packet, a two-segment diagonal changes or duplicates only
\(O(H)\) middle phases.  After \(K\) further adjacent-swap rectangle steps,
the aggregate owner disturbance is

\[
 O(HN_H+K).                                                              \tag{0.8}
\]

Thus a pre-existing owner near-transversal remains one whenever
\(K=o(W)\).  Two paths per top cost only \(O(HN_H)=o(W)\) resets.

This does not yet prove the absorber theorem needed for coefficient one.
The remaining issues are nonnegative availability and quantitative Markov
length: the desired rank correction must have the same coordinate margins,
must admit an oriented rectangle decomposition whose negative cells are
currently available, and must use \(K=o(W)\) adjacent-swap steps.  The
integral lattice theorem alone is signed and supplies no such short positive
decomposition.  Hence the correct architecture is now rigorous:

1. first construct an owner-only near-perfect packet matching;
2. replace selected packets by complementary segment pairs at an
   \(o(W)\) owner cost; and
3. use rank-isolating rectangles only as a final, margin-compatible
   absorber.

## 1. Adjacent swaps and affected starts

Fix an oriented cyclic order

\[
 \pi=(x_0,x_1,\ldots,x_{M-1})
\]

of \(U\), with positions in \(\mathbb Z_M\).  For \(1\le k<M\), write

\[
 v_k^\pi(t)=e_{I_\pi(t,k)},                                                \tag{1.1}
\]

where \(I_\pi(t,k)\) is the unordered set of the \(k\) cyclic labels
starting at position \(t\).

Let \(s_j\) swap the adjacent labels in positions \(j-1,j\).

### Lemma 1.1 (two affected starts)

For every proper length \(k\),

\[
 v_k^{s_j\pi}(t)=v_k^\pi(t)
 \quad\text{unless}\quad
 t\in E_k(j):=\{j,j-k\}.                                                  \tag{1.2}
\]

At \(t=j\), the window contains position \(j\) but not \(j-1\); at
\(t=j-k\), it contains position \(j-1\) but not \(j\).

#### Proof

A positional cyclic interval changes as an unordered label set precisely
when it contains exactly one of the two swapped positions.  Since those
positions are adjacent, this happens only when their joining edge is one
of the interval's two boundary edges.  The two corresponding starts are
exactly those in (1.2). \(\square\)

## 2. The full four-order rectangle

Take disjoint adjacent swaps

\[
 \sigma=s_j,qquad \tau=s_{j+r},                                          \tag{2.1}
\]

where

\[
 2\le r\le M-2.                                                          \tag{2.2}
\]

They commute.  Put

\[
 \pi_{\varepsilon\eta}=\sigma^\varepsilon\tau^\eta\pi,
 \qquad \varepsilon,\eta\in\{0,1\},                                     \tag{2.3}
\]

and define the full signed length-\(k\) incidence

\[
 \Delta_k=
 \sum_{t\in\mathbb Z_M}
 \left(v_k^{00}(t)+v_k^{11}(t)-v_k^{10}(t)-v_k^{01}(t)\right).             \tag{2.4}
\]

### Theorem 2.1 (exact boundary-separation identity)

One has

\[
 \boxed{\Delta_k=0\quad\text{for }k\notin\{r,M-r\}.}                     \tag{2.5}
\]

Let

\[
 a=x_{j-1},\quad b=x_j,\quad
 c=x_{j+r-1},\quad d=x_{j+r},                                             \tag{2.6}
\]

and let

\[
 C=\{x_{j+1},\ldots,x_{j+r-2}\}.
\]

Then

\[
 \boxed{
 \Delta_r=e_{Cbc}+e_{Cad}-e_{Cac}-e_{Cbd}.}                              \tag{2.7}
\]

The vector \(\Delta_{M-r}\) is the complement image inside \(U\) of
\(\Delta_r\).

#### Proof

The mixed difference of one positional window is zero unless that window
contains exactly one endpoint of each adjacent swap.  Equivalently, its two
boundary edges must be the swap edges in (2.1).  The two cyclic arcs between
those edges have lengths \(r\) and \(M-r\), proving (2.5).

The length-\(r\) arc contains \(b,C,c\).  Applying \(\sigma\) replaces
\(b\) by \(a\), applying \(\tau\) replaces \(c\) by \(d\), and applying
both makes both replacements.  Substitution in (2.4) gives (2.7).
Complementing the two arcs proves the last assertion. \(\square\)

### Corollary 2.2 (rank isolation in the calibrated band)

For the lower choice one may take \(1\le q\le H\), and for the upper
choice one may take \(1\le q\le H-2\):

\[
 r=m-q\qquad\text{or}\qquad r=m+q.                                       \tag{2.8}
\]

The companion exceptional length is respectively

\[
 M-r=H+q\qquad\text{or}\qquad M-r=H-q.                                   \tag{2.9}
\]

Since \(m>3H\) eventually, these companion lengths lie below \(m-H\).
Thus the first choice changes only lower depth \(q\) in the full calibrated
band, and the second changes only upper depth \(q\).  Upper depths
\(H-1,H\) are boundary/top rows and do not admit or require this
four-disjoint-label move: length \(M-1\) interval support is independent of
the cyclic order, and length \(M\) is the top itself.

## 3. Complementary segment truncation

Let \(A,B\subseteq\mathbb Z_M\) be cyclic intervals with \(A\cup B=\mathbb
Z_M\).  Define the two diagonal segment configurations

\[
 \mathcal C_0=(\pi_{00},A)+(\pi_{11},B),                                   \tag{3.1}
\]

\[
 \mathcal C_1=(\pi_{10},A)+(\pi_{01},B).                                  \tag{3.2}
\]

Here \((\pi,A)\) means the promotion path consisting of the phase starts in
the cyclic interval \(A\), with every state retaining its full cyclic flag.
Put

\[
 G_k=
 \sum_{t\in A}(v_k^{00}(t)-v_k^{10}(t))
 +\sum_{t\in B}(v_k^{11}(t)-v_k^{01}(t)).                                 \tag{3.3}
\]

### Theorem 3.1 (exact truncated rectangle criterion)

Let \(\mathcal K\subseteq\{1,\ldots,M-1\}\) be a set of protected
lengths.  If

\[
 \boxed{
 \bigcup_{k\in\mathcal K}E_k(j)\subseteq A\cap B,}                        \tag{3.4}
\]

then

\[
 \boxed{G_k=\Delta_k\quad(k\in\mathcal K).}                              \tag{3.5}
\]

#### Proof

For a fixed start \(t\), put

\[
 a_t=v_k^{00}(t)-v_k^{10}(t),\qquad
 b_t=v_k^{11}(t)-v_k^{01}(t).                                             \tag{3.6}
\]

Both vanish outside \(E_k(j)\) by Lemma 1.1.  At a start in \(A\cap B\),
the contribution to (3.3) is

\[
 a_t+b_t
 =v_k^{00}(t)+v_k^{11}(t)-v_k^{10}(t)-v_k^{01}(t),                        \tag{3.7}
\]

which is the full mixed difference.  Condition (3.4) places every possibly
nonzero start in \(A\cap B\), proving (3.5). \(\square\)

### 3.1 Size and construction of the overlap

By (1.2),

\[
 \bigcup_{k\in\mathcal K}E_k(j)
 =\{j\}\cup\{j-k:k\in\mathcal K\}.                                      \tag{3.8}
\]

If \(Q\le H-1\) and

\[
 \mathcal K_Q=\{m-Q,m-Q+1,\ldots,m+Q\},                                  \tag{3.9}
\]

then (3.8) is one singleton neighborhood near \(j\) and one interval of
\(2Q+1\) starts near \(j-m\).  Two long cyclic intervals \(A,B\) can be
chosen to cover the phase circle and overlap on short neighborhoods around
these two loci.  Thus \(|A\cap B|=O(Q)\).

For completeness, enlarge the two loci to disjoint cyclic intervals
\(N_1,N_2\) of total size \(O(Q)\).  The complement has two gap intervals
\(G_1,G_2\).  Taking

\[
 A=\mathbb Z_M-G_1,\qquad B=\mathbb Z_M-G_2
\]

makes both \(A\) and \(B\) single cyclic intervals, with
\(A\cup B=\mathbb Z_M\) and \(A\cap B=N_1\cup N_2\).  Hence two promotion
paths per top really suffice; no hidden fragmentation into many phase
pieces is being assumed.

To protect the entire proper calibrated band, use instead

\[
 \mathcal K_{\rm full}=\{m-H,m-H+1,\ldots,M-1\}.
\]

Equation (3.8) again has only two cyclic components and total size
\(O(H)\).  This overlap is negligible after summing over tops because
\(HN_H=o(W)\).  The omitted length \(M\) is the common top and is
independent of the cyclic order.

### 3.2 The exact counterterm outside the protected lengths

Condition (3.4) is necessary for a uniform exact assertion.  For an
unprotected length, the truncation error is

\[
 \boxed{
 G_k-\Delta_k
 =-\sum_{t\in E_k(j)\cap(A-B)}b_t
   -\sum_{t\in E_k(j)\cap(B-A)}a_t.}                                      \tag{3.10}
\]

Indeed \(A\cup B=\mathbb Z_M\), so every affected start lies in exactly
one segment or in their overlap.  A start lying only in \(A\) omits the
\(b_t\) half of the full difference, and a start lying only in \(B\) omits
the \(a_t\) half.  Each omitted half is a single-swap difference of two
rank-\(k\) unit vectors.

Thus an \(O(Q)\) overlap proves exact isolation on the hard band
\(\mathcal K_Q\), but not outside it.  If those other calibrated ranks must
remain fixed, the correct repair is the \(O(H)\) overlap, not an assertion
that (3.10) vanishes automatically.

## 4. Why the full-packet diagonal fails

One adjacent swap changes only the two middle starts in (1.2).  Hence two
orders differing by two adjacent swaps have identical middle windows at
least \(M-4\) phase starts.

### Proposition 4.1 (full-diagonal overlap obstruction)

Each diagonal pair \(\{\pi_{00},\pi_{11}\}\), and likewise
\(\{\pi_{10},\pi_{01}\}\), has at least \(M-4\) common middle owners and
therefore at most \(M+4\) distinct middle owners.

If packet multiplicities are restricted to zero or two and their total is
\(N_H\), there are \(N_H/2\) used tops.  The number of distinct owners is
at most

\[
 \frac{M+4}{2}N_H=(1/2+o(1))W.                                           \tag{4.1}
\]

This proves the sharp failure of the direct 0/2 deployment.

## 5. Owner overlap for complementary segments

The segment diagonals use only \(|A|+|B|=M+|A\cap B|\) phase occurrences,
not \(2M\).  Their middle-owner overlap is correspondingly small.

### Proposition 5.1 (one absorber top)

Assume the protected set contains the middle length \(m\).  Then each
diagonal configuration in (3.1)--(3.2) has

\[
 M+O(|A\cap B|)                                                          \tag{5.1}
\]

middle occurrences and differs, as an owner multiset, from one full base
packet at only

\[
 O(|A\cap B|+1)                                                          \tag{5.2}
\]

phase entries.  In particular, a hard-band overlap costs \(O(Q)\) and a
full-band overlap costs \(O(H)\).

#### Proof

The occurrence count is immediate.  Outside the four starts affected at
middle length by \(\sigma\) or \(\tau\), every involved order has the same
middle window as the base order.  All affected starts lie in \(A\cap B\)
under the hypothesis.  On \(A-B\) and \(B-A\), the two segment orders
therefore reproduce the base packet.  Only the overlap and the constant
number of affected starts can change or duplicate owners, proving
(5.2). \(\square\)

There is a useful stability version.  If two cyclic orders are obtained
from a common base by a total of \(d\) adjacent swaps, then at most \(2d\)
middle phase starts in either order differ from their base windows.  Since
the middle windows of one cyclic order are distinct, a common owner at two
different unaltered starts is impossible.  Therefore complementary
segments cut from those orders have owner overlap

\[
 O(|A\cap B|+d).                                                         \tag{5.3}
\]

Consequently, starting from an owner near-transversal and performing a
total of \(K\) rectangle steps over all absorber tops changes the aggregate
owner-hole/duplicate ledger by at most

\[
 O(HN_H+K)                                                               \tag{5.4}
\]

when the full-band overlap is used.

In particular, if the original one-packet-per-top family has
\(W-o(W)\) distinct middle owners, then its complementary-segment
replacement still has

\[
 W-o(W)                                                                  \tag{5.5}
\]

distinct middle owners whenever \(K=o(W)\), because
\(HN_H=o(W)\).  This proves the requested owner count conditional only on
the preceding owner-only near-perfect packet matching; no second owner
matching is needed after absorption.

Two promotion paths at every top have total reset toll

\[
 4HN_H=o(W),                                                              \tag{5.6}
\]

and their extra overlap occurrences number \(O(HN_H)=o(W)\).  Thus the
segment replacement is compatible with coefficient-one accounting whenever
\(K=o(W)\).

There is further splice freedom which can only improve this ledger.  A
radius-\(H\) useful state records the \((m-H)\)-element lower reserve as one
unordered block.  An adjacent pair swap wholly inside that reserve is
therefore invisible at the phase.  Segment endpoints chosen outside all
affected-start neighborhoods can sometimes be joined without a new reset by
permuting this hidden reserve internally.  The argument above does not rely
on this saving; it uses the conservative two-path count.

## 6. The exact rank lattice

Let \(\mathcal V_r=\binom{[2m]}r\), and let

\[
 U_r:\mathbb Z^{\mathcal V_r}\longrightarrow\mathbb Z^{[2m]},\qquad
 U_re_S=\mathbf1_S                                                         \tag{6.1}
\]

be the point-incidence map.  Let \(\mathscr R_r\) be the lattice generated
by all rectangles

\[
 e_{Cbc}+e_{Cad}-e_{Cac}-e_{Cbd},
 \qquad |C|=r-2,                                                         \tag{6.2}
\]

on four distinct labels outside \(C\).

### Theorem 6.1 (integral saturation)

For \(2\le r\le2m-2\),

\[
 \boxed{\mathscr R_r=\ker_{\mathbb Z}U_r.}                                \tag{6.3}
\]

Every generator in (6.2) is realized by a top-segment absorber whenever
\(2\le r\le M-2\).

#### Proof

Every rectangle has zero coordinate margins, so
\(\mathscr R_r\subseteq\ker_{\mathbb Z}U_r\).

Work over an arbitrary field.  If a function \(f\) on \(r\)-sets
annihilates every rectangle, then, for fixed distinct \(a,b\), the
difference

\[
 f(T+a)-f(T+b)                                                            \tag{6.4}
\]

is independent of the \((r-1)\)-set \(T\) avoiding \(a,b\).  Indeed,
rectangle identities equate (6.4) on adjacent vertices of the relevant
Johnson graph, which is connected.  Write the common difference as
\(d_{a,b}\).  The cocycle identity gives

\[
 d_{a,b}+d_{b,c}=d_{a,c},
\]

so there are scalars \(w_i\) and \(c_0\) with

\[
 f(S)=c_0+\sum_{i\in S}w_i.                                               \tag{6.5}
\]

The space (6.5) has dimension \(2m\), in every characteristic.  Hence the
rectangle span has dimension \(\binom{2m}r-2m\) over every field.  The
primitive lattice \(\ker_{\mathbb Z}U_r\) has the same rank.  A nontrivial
finite index of \(\mathscr R_r\) would lower its rank modulo a prime divisor
of that index, contradicting the field calculation.  This proves (6.3).

To realize a prescribed rectangle, choose a top containing
\(C\cup\{a,b,c,d\}\), fill its remaining positions arbitrarily, and place
the two adjacent swap edges at the boundaries of the length-\(r\) arc.
Theorem 2.1 and Theorem 3.1 give the required absorber. \(\square\)

### Corollary 6.2 (invariants and signed cone)

There is no parity or congruence invariant beyond the exact coordinate
margins in (6.1).  Since interchanging the two diagonals negates every
rectangle, the cone generated by oriented signed absorbers is

\[
 \ker_{\mathbb R}U_r.                                                     \tag{6.6}
\]

Using the full-band overlap and the separations in Corollary 2.2, the
signed band image is the direct sum of these kernels over the independently
isolated lower and upper ranks.

## 7. Positivity and sequential absorption

The lattice theorem is exact but signed.  Three further conditions are
unavoidable in an integral packet construction.

### 7.1 Coordinate-margin compatibility

Every rectangle preserves

\[
 g_i(\mu)=\sum_{S\ni i}\mu(S).                                            \tag{7.1}
\]

Therefore a target load \(\mu\) can be moved to a proposed balanced load
\(b\) only if

\[
 U_r(\mu-b)=0.                                                            \tag{7.2}
\]

If (7.2) fails, every rectangle sequence leaves the discrepancy untouched.
More quantitatively, for every final load \(\mu'\) reachable by rectangles,

\[
 \|\mu'-b\|_1
 \ge\frac1r\|U_r(\mu-b)\|_1.                                             \tag{7.3}
\]

For one full packet at every top the coordinate margins at rank \(r\) are
uniform and order-independent: a coordinate of a top lies in exactly \(r\)
of its \(M\) cyclic \(r\)-intervals.  Segment overlaps perturb these
margins by only the same \(o(W)\) ledger as in (5.4), but exact balanced
quota compatibility still has to be arranged.

### 7.2 Nonnegative availability

An oriented rectangle subtracts its two negative target cells.  They must
currently have sufficient multiplicity.  At a floor/ceiling target this is
the checkerboard condition: negative cells should be overloaded and positive
cells underloaded.  The equality of the signed lattice and the margin kernel
does not imply that such a sign-compatible decomposition exists.

At packet level the issue is stronger.  A step replaces one diagonal
segment configuration by the other.  The source diagonal must actually be
present.  Algebraic commutativity of the isolated rank effects does not by
itself produce a nonnegative walk through packet-pair configurations.

### 7.3 Quantitative length

Every rectangle has \(\ell^1\)-norm four, so correcting a discrepancy
\(z\) requires at least \(\|z\|_1/4\) rectangle steps.  The integral
saturation proof gives no matching upper bound independent of \(m\).
If \(K\) steps are used, (5.4) shows that owner near-disjointness survives
provided

\[
 K=o(W).                                                                  \tag{7.4}
\]

With only an \(O(Q)\) overlap, each step also has the counterterms (3.10)
at unprotected lengths.  Across the full calibrated band this can create
an \(O(HK)\) collateral ledger.  The \(O(H)\) overlap removes that factor
entirely and is therefore the clean version for sequential absorption.

## 8. Final audit

The corrected verdict is:

* **Full diagonal packets:** refuted by the \(M-4\) middle overlap.
* **Complementary long segments:** the rank-isolating rectangle identity is
  exact, not heuristic, once every affected phase start is put in the two
  segment overlap.
* **Hard-band overlap \(O(Q)\):** exact inside the hard band, with explicit
  counterterm (3.10) outside it.
* **Full-band overlap \(O(H)\):** exact throughout the calibrated band and
  still costs only \(o(W)\) owner occurrences and resets.
* **Rank lattice:** exactly the zero-coordinate-margin lattice, with no
  additional parity invariant.
* **Coefficient-one status:** still open.  One first needs an owner-only
  near-perfect packet matching, followed by a margin-compatible, positive
  rectangle decomposition of total length \(o(W)\).

Thus the complementary-segment proposal successfully repairs the local
overlap defect and genuinely decouples the signed rank actions.  Its exact
remaining gap is global positivity/availability, not another interval
identity or lattice obstruction.
