## 6. Exact lower cross-Gram and Haar drift

For \(R\in\mathcal D_j^\vee\), write \(D_p(R)\) for its old \(A\)-phase
lower flag.  Every positive-depth dual upper flag is fixed: for
\(1\le p\le K\),

\[
 E_p(e_B^\vee(R))
 =\theta E_p(e_A^\vee(R))
 =E_p(e_A^\vee(R)),
\tag{6.1}
\]

because \(E_p\supseteq E_1=R\supseteq A\cup B\).  At \(p=0\), the owner
\(E_0=D_0\) generally moves and is deliberately excluded from the signed
positive-depth flag energy.  The lower innovation, for \(1\le p\le K\),
is

\[
 (d_R^\vee)_p^-
 =\delta_{\theta D_p(R)}-\delta_{D_p(R)}.
\tag{6.2}
\]

### Lemma 6.1 (dual pair-swap unit-vector identity)

If \(D,D'\) both contain \(A\), then

\[
 \boxed{
 \left\langle
 \delta_{\theta D}-\delta_D,
 \delta_{\theta D'}-\delta_{D'}
 \right\rangle
 =
 2\mathbf1_{\{D=D',\ B\nsubseteq D\}}.}
\tag{6.3}
\]

#### Proof

Expansion gives

\[
 2\mathbf1_{\{D=D'\}}
 -\mathbf1_{\{\theta D=D'\}}
 -\mathbf1_{\{D=\theta D'\}}.
\]

If a mixed equality holds, its common set contains \(A\) from one side and
\(B\) from the other.  It therefore contains both complete pairs, is fixed
by \(\theta\), and lies on the diagonal \(D=D'\).  On the diagonal the
innovation vanishes exactly when \(B\subseteq D\); otherwise its squared
norm is two.  \(\square\)

For arbitrary finite nonnegative weights \(w_p^-\), \(1\le p\le K\),
Lemma 6.1 proves (0.4).

For the global lower band, relative depth is \(p=q+1\).  Thus, for
arbitrary nonnegative global weights \(\omega_q^-\), the exact
specialization is

\[
 \boxed{
 \langle d_R^\vee,d_{R'}^\vee\rangle_{\rm global,-}
 =2\sum_{q=1}^{H}\omega_q^-
 \mathbf1_{\{
 D_{q+1}(R)=D_{q+1}(R'),\ B\nsubseteq D_{q+1}(R)
 \}}
 \ge0.}
\tag{6.3a}
\]

The top-relative term \(p=1\) in (0.4) lies at the central rank \(m\); it
is not one of the global lower modes \(m-q\), \(q\ge1\).

Distinct starts in one row have distinct proper windows \(D_p\) for every
\(1\le p\le K\le m-2\).  Hence, if \(I\) is one maximal changed interval,

\[
 \left\|\sum_{R\in I}d_R^\vee\right\|^2
 =\sum_{R\in I}\|d_R^\vee\|^2.
\tag{6.4}
\]

No curvature is lost by assigning one bit to a whole interval.

Let

\[
 \lambda_{p,D}
 =|\{R\in\mathcal D_j^\vee:D_p(R)=D\}|.
\tag{6.5}
\]

For the coherent endpoint distance \(\mathfrak A_j^\vee\) and independent
interval variance \(\mathfrak V_j^\vee\), one has exactly

\[
 \boxed{
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathfrak A_j^\vee
 &=2\sum_{p=1}^{K}w_p^-
   \sum_{D:B\nsubseteq D}\lambda_{p,D}^2,\\
 \mathfrak V_j^\vee
 &=2\sum_{p=1}^{K}w_p^-
   \sum_{D:B\nsubseteq D}\lambda_{p,D},\\
 \mathfrak A_j^\vee-\mathfrak V_j^\vee
 &=2\sum_{p=1}^{K}w_p^-
   \sum_{D:B\nsubseteq D}
   \lambda_{p,D}(\lambda_{p,D}-1).
 \end{aligned}}
\tag{6.6}
\]

Therefore fair independent interval bits lower the average of the two
coherent endpoint squared energies, equivalently doubled factorial-floor
energies, by

\[
 \boxed{
 \frac12\sum_{p=1}^{K}w_p^-
 \sum_{D:B\nsubseteq D}
 \lambda_{p,D}(\lambda_{p,D}-1).}
\tag{6.7}
\]

For the ordinary undoubled binomial floor excess, the exact descent is
half of (6.7):

\[
 \boxed{
 \frac14\sum_{p=1}^{K}w_p^-
 \sum_{D:B\nsubseteq D}
 \lambda_{p,D}(\lambda_{p,D}-1).}
\tag{6.7a}
\]

Both statements remain exact after subtracting the appropriate
corner-independent adjacent-integer baseline. At autonomous top-relative
depth \(p=1\), the token mass is \(T<W\), so its floor is zero; one must
use finite weights or the factorial floor polynomial, exactly as at the
first upper depth of the source chart.

For a completed mass-\(W\) word, define the adjacent-integer floor
quotients

\[
 c_q^-=\left\lfloor
 \frac{W}{\binom{n}{m-q}}
 \right\rfloor,
 \qquad
 c_r^+=\left\lfloor
 \frac{W}{\binom{n}{m+r}}
 \right\rfloor.
\]

Complementation gives the exact identity

\[
 \boxed{c_q^-=c_{q+1}^+,}
\tag{6.8}
\]

because rank \(m-q\) is complementary to rank \(m+1+q\).  No floor or
factor-of-two correction appears under the depth shift.

### Lemma 7.1 (chartwise \(\theta\)-paired padding)

For each fixed adjacent-swap chart, the \(d\) isolated-owner flags may be
chosen so that every column of the completed literal word, at every rank,

#### Proof

\(Z,\theta Z\).  Theorem 3.1 applied to the single-root flip shows that
when \(Z\) is occupied by its token, \(\theta Z\) is occupied by no other
token; after the flip their occupied and omitted roles are exchanged.
The owner pairs belonging to distinct changed roots are disjoint, again by
independent-root legality.

Choose any isolated saturated radius-\(K\) flag \(\mathcal P_Z\) around
\(Z\), and choose \(\mathcal P_{\theta Z}=\theta\mathcal P_Z\) around its
partner.  Pad whichever member of the pair is omitted.  If \(F\) is the
\(A\)-token flag target and \(P\) is the corresponding target of
\(\mathcal P_Z\), the combined column of one flip is

\[
 (\delta_{\theta F}-\delta_F)
 +(\delta_P-\delta_{\theta P}),
\tag{7.12}
\]

a sum of \(\theta\)-differences.  At the owner layer the two terms cancel
exactly.  Owners untouched by the chart keep identical padding flags in
both phases.  Summing (7.12) over the selected packet flips proves the

This padding is chartwise.  No one padding system is asserted to be

## 8. Why the right-shifted long-interval dual does not share the upper word

Complementation is not an OR-word automorphism:

\[
 c(X\cup Y)=cX\cap cY.
\tag{8.1}
\]

Theorem 5.2 built the right-shifted dual flags from new top-middle
deep-core letters.  It did not complement the letters of the bottom word.

There is also an unavoidable central-layer shift:

\[
 S^{m-1}\subset X^m
 \quad\stackrel c\longmapsto\quad
 cX^{m+1}\subset cS^{m+2}.
\tag{8.2}
\]

Thus independent upper and lower chart bits belong to two separately
literal \(W+o(W)\) words.  Concatenating them costs \(2W+o(W)\), not
constant one.

The most direct attempt to recenter the dual has an exact capacity
obstruction.

### Proposition 8.1 (same-centre deletion-capacity obstruction)

Let

\[
 f:\binom{[n]}{m+1}\longrightarrow\binom{[n]}m
\]

be a bijection with \(f(U)\subset U\).  For every coordinate \(x\), the
number of roots \(U\) for which

\[
 U\setminus f(U)=\{x\}
\]

is exactly

\[
 \boxed{
 \Delta_m
 =
 \binom{2m}{m}-\binom{2m}{m-1}
 =
 \frac1{m+1}\binom{2m}{m}.}
\tag{8.3}
\]

#### Proof

There are \(\binom{2m}{m}\) roots containing \(x\), and
\(\binom{2m}{m-1}\) owners containing \(x\).  An owner containing \(x\)
can only be assigned to a root containing \(x\).  Bijection therefore
forces the difference to equal the number of edges deleting \(x\).
\(\square\)

Consider the stipulated recentering architecture in which every
\(A\)-phase carrier edge on roots containing \(A\cup B\) deletes one of
the two coordinates of \(A\).  Proposition 8.1 bounds its carrier by
\(2\Delta_m\).  But

\[
 \left|\{U\in\binom{[n]}{m+1}:A\cup B\subseteq U\}\right|
 =\binom{2m-3}{m-3},
\tag{8.4}
\]

and

\[
 \binom{2m-3}{m-3}>2\Delta_m
\tag{8.5}
\]

is equivalent to

\[
 m^2-17m+6>0.
\]

Hence (8.5) holds for every \(m\ge17\).  This rules out exactly the
stipulated keep-one-\(A\)-coordinate recentering on the full first
carrier.  It does not prove that an arbitrary recentering must delete an
\(A\)-coordinate, and it does not rule out nonlocal fusion through the two
middle layers.

It also does not apply to the owner-fixed lower spike audited in
MATH_ATTACK_AD13_OWNER_FIXED_LOWER_SPIKE_AUDIT_20260725.md. That spike
does not recenter the top matching: it fixes each bottom central edge and
uses a transposition internal to its lower endpoint. Hence the capacity
obstruction and the same-word spike theorem are fully compatible.

## 9. Exact floor-mode span test

This section tests the fixed-pair-exchange long-interval atlas only. The
owner-fixed spike transpositions are additional generators and need not
fix \(\star\); they are not covered by the cokernel below.

Let

\[
 \Omega=\binom{[n]}{m+1},
\qquad |\Omega|=W,
\]

and split

\[
 \mathcal A=\{U\in\Omega:\star\in U\},
\qquad
 \mathcal C=\Omega\setminus\mathcal A.
\tag{9.1}
\]

Then

\[
 |\mathcal A|=\frac{m+1}{2m+1}W,
\qquad
 |\mathcal C|=\frac{m}{2m+1}W.
\tag{9.2}
\]

Every adjacent exchange \(\theta_j\) fixes \(\star\).  Therefore every
upper-chart innovation preserves the total load on \(\mathcal A\).  The
dual chart has zero positive-depth upper innovation; its moving
rank-\((m+1)\) owner at depth zero is itself a \(\theta_j\)-difference and
also preserves the \(\star\)-subtotal.  After the \(d\) omitted owners are
added, the complete owner layer is exactly constant.  Thus the two
subtotals in (9.1) are exact invariants of every completed one-chart corner
using Lemma 7.1, and hence of the formal coset generated by all columns in
the fixed-pair-partition upper-plus-lower menu.  Simultaneous product-cube
legality across different adjacent charts is not asserted.

### Lemma 9.1 (an integral \(q=1\)-legal blind profile)

There is a set \(E\subseteq\mathcal A\), \(|E|=|\mathcal C|\), and an
integral owner-to-upper-neighbour assignment with load

\[
 \mu(U)=
 \begin{cases}
 2,&U\in E,\\
 1,&U\in\mathcal A\setminus E,\\
 0,&U\in\mathcal C.
 \end{cases}
\tag{9.3}
\]

#### Proof

Every rank-\(m\) owner \(X\) omitting \(\star\) is assigned to

\[
 U=X\cup\{\star\}.
\]

This is a bijection from those owners to \(\mathcal A\), giving one
occurrence at every target in \(\mathcal A\).

An owner containing \(\star\) has the form

\[
 X=\{\star\}\cup Y,\qquad |Y|=m-1,\quad Y\subseteq[2m].
\]

Match every such \(Y\) injectively to an \(m\)-set \(Z\supset Y\).
In this bipartite graph, every left vertex has degree \(m+1\) and every
right vertex has degree \(m\).  For every left family \(\mathcal Y\),
edge counting gives

\[
 (m+1)|\mathcal Y|
 \le m|N(\mathcal Y)|,
\]

so Hall supplies an injection.  Let \(E\) be its image after adjoining
\(\star\).  There are

\[
 \binom{2m}{m-1}=|\mathcal C|
\]

left owners, proving \(|E|=|\mathcal C|\).  Assigning these owners to the
targets in \(E\) gives the second occurrences.  \(\square\)

This is an exact integral \(q=1\) incidence assignment.  Extension to one
exact all-depth factor is not asserted.

At mass \(W\) on \(W\) targets, the factorial floor excess is

\[
 \Phi_1(\nu)=\sum_{U\in\Omega}\binom{\nu(U)}2
 =\frac12\|\nu-\mathbf1\|_2^2.
\tag{9.4}
\]

For (9.3),

\[
 \Phi_1(\mu)=|\mathcal C|
 =\frac{m}{2m+1}W.
\tag{9.5}
\]

Every nonnegative integral mass-\(W\) profile \(\mu'\) for which
\(\mu'-\mu\) lies in the span of the proved upper and dual-lower chart
columns obeys

\[
 \sum_{U\in\mathcal A}(\mu'(U)-1)=|\mathcal C|,
\tag{9.6}
\]

\[
 \sum_{U\in\mathcal C}(\mu'(U)-1)=-|\mathcal C|.
\tag{9.7}
\]

Cauchy--Schwarz gives

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 2\Phi_1(\mu')
 &=\|\mu'-\mathbf1\|_2^2\\
 &\ge
 \frac{|\mathcal C|^2}{|\mathcal A|}
 +\frac{|\mathcal C|^2}{|\mathcal C|}\\
 &=\frac{m}{m+1}W.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{9.8}
\]

This is the exact real orthogonal-projection bound onto the two subtotal
modes.  Integrality gives a sharper floor bound.  Equation (9.7) and
nonnegativity imply

\[
 \mu'(U)=0\qquad(U\in\mathcal C).
\]

For every integer \(t\ge0\), \(\binom t2\ge t-1\).  Therefore

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \Phi_1(\mu')
 &=\sum_{U\in\mathcal A}\binom{\mu'(U)}2\\
 &\ge\sum_{U\in\mathcal A}(\mu'(U)-1)\\
 &=W-|\mathcal A|=|\mathcal C|.
 \end{aligned}
\]

Consequently the sharp integral bound in this invariant coset is

\[
 \boxed{
 \Phi_1(\mu')\ge\frac{m}{2m+1}W.}
\tag{9.9}
\]

The Hall profile (9.3) has only loads one and two on \(\mathcal A\), so it
attains (9.9).  This is a linear floor-excess mode which the combined atlas
cannot burn.

### Theorem 9.2 (exact common cokernel)

Let

 G=\langle\theta_1,\ldots,\theta_{m-1}\rangle\cong S_m,

\[
 \delta_{\theta_j R}-\delta_R.
\tag{9.10}

\[
 \operatorname{span}(\text{proved completed one-chart columns at rank }r)
 \subseteq
 \operatorname{Fix}_G
 \left(\mathbb R^{\binom{[n]}r}\right)^\perp.
\tag{9.11}
\]

For the unrestricted family of all \((I-\theta_j)\)-differences, equality
holds:

\[
 \sum_j\operatorname{im}(I-\theta_j)
 =
 \operatorname{Fix}_G
 \left(\mathbb R^{\binom{[n]}r}\right)^\perp.
\tag{9.12}
\]

#### Proof

Every generator is orthogonal to every \(G\)-invariant vector, proving
(9.11).  The orthogonal complement of the left side of (9.12) is

\[
 \bigcap_j\ker(I-\theta_j)
 =\operatorname{Fix}_G,
\]

because each \(\theta_j\) is an orthogonal involution.  \(\square\)

The completion in (9.11) is chartwise: Lemma 7.1 does not construct one
padding system simultaneously equivariant for all adjacent swaps.  At rank

Writing \(P_i=\{x_i,y_i\}\), the \(G\)-orbits on rank-\(r\) targets are

\[
 (a_0,a_x,a_y,a_2,\epsilon),
\tag{9.13}

\[
 a_0+a_x+a_y+a_2=m,
\qquad
 a_x+a_y+2a_2+\epsilon=r,

 \epsilon\in\{0,1\}.
\tag{9.14}

Hence

\[
 \dim\operatorname{Fix}_G

 =
 [x^mz^r]\,
 \frac{1+z}{(1-x)(1-xz)^2(1-xz^2)}.
\tag{9.15}

After removing the constant vector, this leaves one fewer centered blind

In particular,

\[
 h_r(R)
 =
 \mathbf1_{\{\star\in R\}}-\frac{r}{2m+1}
\tag{9.16}

\[
 \{b_i\}=L_{q-1}(e_i)\setminus L,\qquad

\tag{10.1}
\]

Both transposed coordinates lie in \(S_i\), so \(\sigma_i\) fixes the

 \left\|\sum_i d_i^-\right\|_w^2

\tag{10.2}
\]

The squared, equivalently doubled-factorial, Haar descent is
\(w_q^-t(t-1)/4\). The ordinary undoubled binomial floor descent is
\(w_q^-t(t-1)/8\); this factor-two normalization is an explicit correction

\qquad

\tag{10.3}

At lower depth two, central root saturation automatically makes the spike

More strongly, replacing one transposition by an arbitrary

An integral node-capacitated Boolean-lattice flow therefore gives exact
