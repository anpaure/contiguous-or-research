## 1. Owner--Schreier quotient theorem

Let \(\tau\) be a coordinate transposition.  In the ownership overlay
between \(F\) and \(\tau F\), an edge labelled by the middle set \(A\)
joins

\[
 o_F(A)
 \quad\hbox{to}\quad
 \tau o_F(\tau A).
\]

After the right row \(\tau P\) is identified with the base row \(P\), this
becomes exactly

\[
 o_F(A)\sim o_F(\tau A).
\]

The identification is legitimate inside every overlay component.  The
short missing local proof is as follows.  Each of the two transposed
coordinates belongs to exactly \(m\) of the \(n=2m+1\) cyclic middle
windows of a row.  If every window contained exactly one of the two
coordinates, the total number of incidences would be \(n=2m+1\), whereas
it is \(2m\).  Hence some middle window contains both coordinates or
neither and is fixed by \(\tau\).  That window joins \(P\) to \(\tau P\)
inside the overlay.

Now form the Schreier graph of \(G=\langle T\rangle\) on the middle layer.
Its components are the \(G\)-orbits.  Along every Schreier edge the owners
are related in \(\mathcal P_T\), so all rows meeting one orbit are in one
block.  Conversely every generator of \(\mathcal P_T\) is the owner image
of a Schreier edge, while a row meeting two orbits is exactly an incidence
edge through that row in \(\mathfrak B_G(F)\).  Thus alternating paths in
the row--orbit incidence graph are precisely chains in the joined owner
relation.

This verifies Theorem 1.1, including the assertion that the join depends
only on \(G\), not on the chosen transposition generators.

If the coordinate support graph is connected, its edge transpositions
generate \(S_n\); there is then one middle-layer orbit and every row is
incident with it.  Hence Corollaries 1.2 and 1.3 are correct.

### Counterexample search

The strongest possible converse is false, but the note does not assert it.
The coordinate support can be disconnected and the owner incidence graph
can nevertheless be connected.  The point stabilizer
\(S_{[n]\setminus\{x\}}\) is the canonical example: it has two middle
orbits, and every wreath row meets both.  This is consistent with Theorem
1.1 and is proved later in Theorem 5.4.

## 2. Fixed-word pullback and component collapse

At interface \(i\), write

\[
 \sigma_i=\tau_i\sigma_{i-1},
 \qquad
 \rho_i=\sigma_{i-1}^{-1}\tau_i\sigma_{i-1}.
\]

Pulling a middle-set edge from the overlay
\(\sigma_{i-1}F\) versus \(\sigma_iF\) back by
\(\sigma_{i-1}^{-1}\) gives exactly the owner relation generated by

\[
 A\longleftrightarrow \rho_iA.
\]

The fixed-middle-window argument above also gives a path carrying the same
base row label across every adjacent pair of layers.  Therefore relations
from different interfaces can be concatenated, and the pulled-back
endpoint partition is exactly

\[
 \mathcal P_{\rho_1}\vee\cdots\vee\mathcal P_{\rho_L}.
\]

The group identity

\[
 \langle\rho_1,\ldots,\rho_L\rangle
 =\langle\tau_1,\ldots,\tau_L\rangle
\]

is also correct: at step \(i\), \(\sigma_{i-1}\) belongs to the group
already generated, so adjoining the conjugate \(\rho_i\) is equivalent to
adjoining \(\tau_i\).

Consequently connected letter support gives one layered component.  Within
the canonical endpoint side-choice construction, the only offered sides
are the two whole endpoints \(F\) and \(\sigma_LF\), and every
relabeling-invariant energy has zero drift.

### Required wording qualification

In Theorem 2.2, replace

> consists only of \(F\) and \(\sigma_LF\)

by

> the canonical endpoint component-side mechanism offers at most the two
> whole endpoint sides \(F\) and \(\sigma_LF\).

The endpoints may coincide, and the union of all row supports may possess
additional perfect matchings not generated by endpoint component-side
choices.  The paragraph after Corollary 2.3 already makes the latter caveat;
the local wording should match it.

## 3. Orbit-profile overload floor

For an orbit \(\mathcal O\), put

\[
 y_{\mathcal O}=T_{\mathcal O}-cM_{\mathcal O}.
\]

A global balanced quota assigns an integer number
\(d_{\mathcal O}\in[0,M_{\mathcal O}]\) of bonus units to the orbit, with
\(\sum d_{\mathcal O}=\delta\).  Aggregating the half-\(\ell_1\) distance
gives

\[
 \frac12\|\mu-b\|_1
 \ge \frac12\sum_{\mathcal O}|y_{\mathcal O}-d_{\mathcal O}|.
\]

Clamping \(y_{\mathcal O}\) to \([0,M_{\mathcal O}]\) costs

\[
 A+B,
 \quad
 A=\sum(-y_{\mathcal O})_+,
 \quad
 B=\sum(y_{\mathcal O}-M_{\mathcal O})_+.
\]

The clamped total is \(\delta+A-B\).  Correcting it to \(\delta\) costs
\(|A-B|\).  There is always enough capacity: if a decrease is needed,
the clamped total is at least \(A-B\); if an increase is needed, the total
unused capacity exceeds \(B-A\) because \(\delta\le N\).  Hence the exact
aggregated minimum is

\[
 \frac12(A+B+|A-B|)=\max(A,B).
\]

This verifies every factor in Theorem 3.2.

An exact component switch by \(\tau\in G\) changes the histogram by
\((\tau-I)a\).  Every \(G\)-orbit is \(\tau\)-invariant, so its total is
unchanged.  This remains true after the factor and the components are
recomputed.  Lemma 3.1 therefore has the asserted adaptive scope.

## 4. Quadratic floor and restitution constants

For

\[
 \phi(x)=(x-c)(x-c-1),
\]

the first difference is \(2(x-c)\), so \(\phi\) is discretely convex.
At fixed orbit total \(T=aM+\rho\), the minimum is attained by
\(M-\rho\) loads equal to \(a\) and \(\rho\) loads equal to \(a+1\).
This gives exactly (3.7).

For an integer \(x\),

\[
 \phi(x)\ge 2(c-x)_++2(x-c-1)_+.
\]

Thus \(Q\ge2O\).  Applying this to the orbitwise-balanced minimizer proves

\[
 \mathfrak B_{q,G}\ge2\Omega_{q,G}.
\]

Furthermore \(\phi(x)=0\) exactly for \(x=c,c+1\), so

\[
 \mathfrak B_{q,G}=0
 \iff
 cM_{\mathcal O}\le T_{\mathcal O}\le(c+1)M_{\mathcal O}
 \quad\hbox{for every orbit},
\]

which is also exactly the condition \(\Omega_{q,G}=0\).  Theorem 3.3 is
correct.

Writing \(\lambda=c+\theta\) and
\(\beta=N\theta(1-\theta)\), direct expansion gives

\[
 Q=\|\mu-\lambda\mathbf1\|_2^2-\beta.
\]

At the orbitwise balanced minimizer, the within-orbit variance is

\[
 M_{\mathcal O}
 \{T_{\mathcal O}/M_{\mathcal O}\}
 \bigl(1-\{T_{\mathcal O}/M_{\mathcal O}\}\bigr).
\]

Orthogonal decomposition into orbit means and within-orbit fluctuations
therefore proves (3.11) with the displayed **plus** sign before
\(\Theta_{q,G}\) and the **minus** sign before \(\beta_q\).  These signs
and all floor constants are correct.

## 5. Spanning-tree telescope

Adding a tree edge merges coordinate components and hence coarsens the
target-orbit partition.  Both profile quantities are minima subject to the
old orbit totals, so removing those constraints cannot increase either
minimum:

\[
 \Omega_{q,G_t}\le\Omega_{q,G_{t-1}},
 \qquad
 \mathfrak B_{q,G_t}\le\mathfrak B_{q,G_{t-1}}.
\]

At the trivial subgroup, every target is a singleton orbit.  Hence

\[
 \Omega_{q,G_0}=O_q(F),
 \qquad
 \mathfrak B_{q,G_0}=Q_q(F).
\]

At the final subgroup \(S_n\), there is one orbit whose total is \(W\),
so both floors vanish.  Therefore every release is nonnegative and the
telescopes in (4.3) are exact.  Summing in \(q\) first and then applying
the pigeonhole principle gives exactly the factor \(1/(n-1)\) in (4.4).

The refined identities are also correct:

\[
 \sum_t(\|P_{G_{t-1}}f\|_2^2-\|P_{G_t}f\|_2^2)=\|f\|_2^2,
\]

because the first and last projections are \(I\) and the global mean, and

\[
 \sum_t(\Theta_{G_{t-1}}-\Theta_{G_t})=-\beta,
\]

because the endpoint restitution terms are \(0\) and \(\beta\).  Their
sum is \(Q\).  It is correct that the restitution increments alone need
not be nonnegative.

## 6. Subgroup heat identity

For a joined owner block \(K\), its middle support \(U_K\) is a union of
whole \(G\)-orbits: if \(A\in U_K\), the relation (1.1) puts the owner of
\(gA\) in the same block for every generator and hence every \(g\in G\).
Thus \(gU_K=U_K\).  It follows that independently replacing each row block
\(K\) by \(g_KK\) partitions the same support \(U_K\), and different
blocks still have disjoint supports.  Lemma 5.1 is correct; no completion
step is hidden in it.

For independent uniform \(g_K\),

\[
 \mu'_q=\sum_Kg_Ku_{K,q},
 \qquad
 \mathbb E\mu'_q=P_G\mu_q.
\]

Independence kills cross-block centered products and gives

\[
 \mathbb E\|\mu'_q-P_G\mu_q\|_2^2
 =\sum_K(\|u_{K,q}\|_2^2-\|P_Gu_{K,q}\|_2^2)
 =\mathcal V_{q,G}.
\]

Combining this with the floor identity gives

\[
 \mathbb E Q_q(F_{\mathbf g})
 =\|P_Gf_q\|_2^2+\mathcal V_{q,G}-\beta_q
 =\mathfrak B_{q,G}+\mathcal V_{q,G}-\Theta_{q,G}.
\]

This verifies (5.5).

For a target in orbit \(\mathcal O\), the child load is an integer random
variable of mean \(T_{\mathcal O}/M_{\mathcal O}\).  Its variance is at
least \(\theta_{\mathcal O}(1-\theta_{\mathcal O})\), with equality exactly
for support on the adjacent floor and ceiling integers.  Summing over
targets gives

\[
 \mathcal V_{q,G}\ge\Theta_{q,G},
\]

so \(\mathcal C_{q,G}\ge0\).  Since every tuple of group choices has
positive probability, the equality characterization (5.8) is also exact.

It is not a necessary condition for some nonuniform distribution or some

## 7. Point-stabilizer theorem

At rank \(r=m-q\), \(G_x=S_{[n]\setminus\{x\}}\) has the two target orbits
distinguished by membership of \(x\).  Every cyclic row contains exactly
\(r\) rank-\(r\) intervals through \(x\), so an exact factor gives

\[
 T_1=rW/n.
\]

Since \(|\mathcal O_1|=rN_q/n\), the mean on this orbit is \(W/N_q\).
The complementary orbit has the same mean.  Therefore

\[
 P_{G_x}f_q=0,
 \qquad
 \Theta_{q,G_x}=\beta_q,
 \qquad
 \mathfrak B_{q,G_x}=\Omega_{q,G_x}=0.
\]

On the middle layer, every wreath row owns exactly \(m\) sets containing
\(x\) and \(m+1\) sets avoiding \(x\).  Thus every row vertex is adjacent
to both orbit vertices in the owner--orbit incidence graph, which is
connected.  There is one owner block.  Consequently every child is a
whole relabeling and

\[
 \mathcal C_{q,G_x}=Q_q(F),
 \qquad
 \mathbb E Q_q(F_{\mathbf g})=Q_q(F).
\]

All constants and the one-block conclusion in Theorem 5.4 are correct.

### Important interpretation repair

The point stabilizer is also the exact counterexample to reading
“profile-or-collapse” as two mutually exclusive outcomes.  Its coordinate
support is disconnected, its profile floor is zero, and its owner join
collapses to one block.  The valid statement is:

* disconnected support preserves explicit orbit profiles and hence an
  explicit floor, which may be zero;
* connected support guarantees collapse;
* disconnected support may collapse as well.

The formal Theorems 4.1 and 5.4 already have precisely this content.  Only
the informal use of “sharp dichotomy” should be softened.

## 8. Order-two endpoint normalization

For \(G=\langle\tau\rangle\),

\[
 \|u_K\|_2^2-\left\|\frac{u_K+\tau u_K}{2}\right\|_2^2
 =\frac14\|u_K-\tau u_K\|_2^2.
\]

On a moved pair \(\{S,\tau S\}\), writing
\(d_K=u_K(S)-u_K(\tau S)\), the squared norm contributes \(2d_K^2\).
Hence \(\mathcal V\) contributes

\[
 \frac12\sum_Kd_K^2.
\]

The orbit mean is half-integral exactly when

\[
 z=\sum_Kd_K=\mu(S)-\mu(\tau S)
\]

is odd, because the sum and difference of the two loads have the same
parity.  In that case the two-target restitution is \(1/2\).  Thus the
component excess is exactly

\[
 \frac12\left(\sum_Kd_K^2-\mathbf1_{\{z\ {\rm odd}\}}\right).
\]

There is no missing factor of two or four in (8.2).

## 9. Scope of the no-go

The proved obstruction has the following exact scope.

It rules out:

1. treating all one-transposition component coins as one common legal
   product cube;
2. aggregating a fixed connected-support word and expecting a nontrivial
   endpoint component cut;
3. expecting discrete-floor subtraction alone to create contraction in
   the full-group or point-stabilizer block heat.

It does **not** rule out:

1. sequential switches with components recomputed after every chosen move;
2. a nonuniform or state-dependent choice among subgroup children;
3. new perfect matchings inside the union of the layer supports;
4. a direct adaptive proof of the proposed fresh-fragmentation theorem.

The source states these escape routes explicitly.  Subject to the three
wording/self-containment repairs above, its final no-go claim is therefore
accurate.
