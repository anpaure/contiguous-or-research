# Audit of the unbuffered cylinder giant and transparent-fusion proposal

Date: 2026-08-02  
Status: proof audit and sharp scoped obstruction.  One-support connectivity
is proved; adversarial multi-support connectivity and pure transparent
adjacent-core fusion are refuted.  The genuinely random transverse-support
giant remains open.

## 1. Trace quotient

Let `J(n,a)` be central and fix `E subseteq[n]`, `|E|=b`.  The fibre of a
trace `T subseteq E` is

\[
             \{X:X\cap E=T\}\cong J(n-b,a-|T|).          \tag{1.1}
\]

The exact quotient graph `Lambda_b` joins two traces when they differ by
one inclusion/deletion or by a same-cardinality Johnson swap.  Each fibre
is connected, every physical Johnson edge projects to one such quotient
edge, and every quotient edge has a literal lift.  Hence deleting trace
cylinders indexed by `D subseteq2^E` gives precisely the inverse images of
the components of `Lambda_b-D`.

Since the Boolean cube is a spanning subgraph of `Lambda_b`, cube
edge-isoperimetry proves that deleting `s` traces leaves a quotient
component on at least

\[
                         2^b-(b+1)s                      \tag{1.2}
\]

traces.  In the canonical central slice every trace fibre has
`Theta(2^{-b})` of the cores, uniformly for `b=O(sqrt(n))`.  Thus one
opposing packet, which contributes `s=O(q^2)` traces on one
`b=q+O(1)` support, leaves

\[
                         1-O(q^3 2^{-q})                 \tag{1.3}
\]

of that Johnson reservoir in one component.  The quotient-tree lifts give
explicit rank-legal Johnson connector edges.

There is also an exact multi-support positive face.  If the supports
`E_i` are pairwise disjoint and their total size is below
`min(a,n-a)`, take the giant trace component on every `E_i`.  Their product
lifts to one connected constant-weight Johnson subgraph: a trace inclusion
edge exchanges its changed support coordinate with the still-nontrivial
outside buffer.  Its relative size is at least

\[
                 1-O\!\left(\sum_i |E_i|s_i2^{-|E_i|}\right). \tag{1.4}
\]

This covers only `O(n/q)` disjoint `Theta(q)`-supports, not the
`Theta(2^q/q^2)` overlapping supports of clustered pruning.

## 2. Count and density do not imply a giant

Fix one `b`-set `E` and delete every trace whose cardinality lies in one
residue class modulo `ell`.  Some residue uses at most

\[
                         {2^{b+1}\over\ell}              \tag{2.1}
\]

cylinders and deletes at most `2/ell` of the central slice.  Since every
edge of `Lambda_b` changes trace cardinality by at most one, the survivors
split into bands of fewer than `ell` consecutive levels.  A hypergeometric
local bound gives mass `O(ell/sqrt(b))` per band.

Taking `ell=floor(b^{1/4})` uses fewer than the total
`Theta(2^q)` clustered-pruning cylinders and deletes only `o(1)` of the
cores, yet every fixed union of components has `o(1)` mass.  Therefore no
adversarial theorem based only on:

* the total cylinder count;
* the total deleted density;
* the one-frame estimate (1.3); or
* average surviving Johnson degree

can imply the requested linear giant or `O(1)`-component remainder.

This example is an abstract legal exact-cylinder family.  It is not claimed
to be the trace menu generated by independent packet supports.

## 3. What random sampling actually proves

For `R=alpha 2^q/q^2` independently relabelled packet supports in a
`Theta(q^2)` ground set, standard hypergeometric tails prove, with high
probability:

1. every outside support has size `(1-o(1))q`;
2. no two outside supports are equal; and
3. every pair intersects in `O(q/log q)` coordinates.

For a fixed core the deletion probability is `O(alpha)`.  For a fixed
Johnson edge it is only `O(alpha/q)` that exactly one endpoint is deleted,
because the random support must meet one of the two exchanged coordinates.
The fixed-modulus version of the periodic obstruction has the same
orders—constant deleted density and `Theta(1/q)` cut-edge density—so these
first moments still do not prove a giant.

The exact missing random statement is separator anti-cover.  If `D` is the
sampled cylinder union, a survivor set `S` is a union of components exactly
when

\[
                         N_{J(n,a)}(S)-S\subseteq D.       \tag{3.1}
\]

After positive survivor density is established, a linear giant follows
from proving that no survivor set of measure in a fixed interval
`[epsilon,1/2]` has its entire external boundary covered by `D`.  Pairwise
support dispersion and edge first moments do not establish (3.1)'s
negation.  This is the precise unresolved random-correlation lemma.

## 4. Johnson adjacency is not chain transparency

Now use one corrected unbuffered packet support

\[
 A=\{\beta\}\mathbin{\dot\cup}V
\]

and distinct cores `X,Y subseteq[k]-A`.  In any pure cross-splice, every
mixed source window has outside-support trace

\[
                         X\cup Y.                         \tag{4.1}
\]

Every old packet window has trace `X` or `Y`.  Therefore the new mixed
marked chains cannot equal the old affected marked-chain multiset.  This
holds even when `X,Y` are Johnson-adjacent.  Adjacency supplies the necessary
rank budget, not a protected-label return.

For adjacent cores `X=K+a`, `Y=K+b`, a mixed `j`-window with `o` repeated
private tags and beta indicator `epsilon` has rank

\[
                         c+j-o+\epsilon.                  \tag{4.2}
\]

The exact rank condition is `o=epsilon`.  In the fixed support/order/phase
packet, the unique all-width pure open socket is the co-oriented same-odd-tag
`H-H` kissing seam.  It has one repeated tag and beta at every crossing
width; all crossing labels are injective but fresh, with trace `X union Y`.
It is not transparent.

Moreover, one cut in each alternating packet exposes one `P` and one `H`
endpoint per path.  A cyclic fusion needs two new seams, while only the
single `H-H` seam can be rank-correct at all widths.  Hence one-cut pure
two-cycle fusion is impossible even before palette restoration.  An aligned
owner-level Johnson rectangle is topology-legal, but replaces both lower
and upper q1 labels by fresh traces and has no automatic source-chain lift.

## 5. Corrected fusion target

The proposed implication

\[
 \text{cylinder-pruned Johnson giant}
 \Longrightarrow
 \text{protected transparent packet fusion}
\]

is false.  Two independent new ingredients are required:

1. a transverse-cylinder separator anti-cover theorem, if a random giant is
   to be extracted from the sampled supports; and
2. a prospective port policy with ambient duplicate providers, unmarked
   affected chains, or a source-changing multi-core cancellation packet.

The first is a connectivity theorem.  The second is a labelled chronology
theorem and does not follow from Johnson isoperimetry.  A useful positive
reformulation is to reserve the `H-H` kissing labels prospectively and charge
their fresh `X union Y` traces in the clustered resource hypergraph; calling
those edges transparent would be incorrect.
