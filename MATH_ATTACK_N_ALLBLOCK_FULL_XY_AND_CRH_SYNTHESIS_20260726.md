## 1. Exact full owner relation

Interleave the canonical tokens by

\[
 Z_{2t}(P)=X_t^{F_s}(P),\qquad
 Z_{2t+1}(P)=Y_t^{F_s}(P).
\tag{1.1}
\]

Let \(o_X,o_Y\) denote canonical row ownership in the two token ledgers.
At the even phases write

\[
 i_t:D_s\longrightarrow L_t,
 \qquad i_t(P)=X_t^{F_s}(P).
\tag{1.2}
\]

Each \(i_t\) is a bijection, and \(\eta_s\) preserves every Chung--Feller
layer \(L_t\).  Define

\[
 \sigma_t=i_t^{-1}\eta_si_t\eta_s,
 \qquad 0\le t\le s,
\tag{1.3}
\]

and

\[
 a_t(P)=o_Y\!\left(\eta_sY_t^{F_s}(\eta_sP)\right),
 \qquad0\le t<s.
\tag{1.4}
\]

### Theorem 1.1 (full contracted component theorem)

After contracting the common phase-zero port matching, the full ownership
graph is the graph on \(D_s\) generated by

\[
                         P\sim\sigma_t(P)
 \quad(0\le t\le s),
\tag{1.5}
\]

and

\[
                         P\sim a_t(P)
 \quad(0\le t<s).
\tag{1.6}
\]

Its connected components are exactly the independently switchable full
\(X/Y\)-ownership components.

#### Proof

The row of \(G_s\) rooted at the physical port \(P\) is the coordinate
image of the canonical row rooted at \(\eta_sP\).  Its phase-\(t\)
lower token is

\[
                         \eta_si_t(\eta_sP),
\]

whose canonical owner is (1.3).  The same calculation in the upper ledger
is (1.4).  These are all common-token edges.  After the common port edges
are contracted, connectivity is exactly the equivalence closure of
(1.5)--(1.6).  A whole connected bipartite component owns the same token
set on both shores, so either shore is one literal exact rooted factor
piece. \(\square\)

The \(a_t\) need not be injective.  Thus the full components cannot be
called the orbits of a permutation group unless redundancy of all odd
relations is separately proved.

There is a useful direct owner identity.  For every physical token \(T\),

\[
                    o_G(T)=\eta_s o_F(\eta_sT).
\tag{1.7}
\]

It implies that \(\eta_s\) is an automorphism of the contracted component
partition: components occur either as stable blocks \(K=\eta_sK\) or as
equal-size pairs \(\{K,\eta_sK\}\).

## 2. Component transport and the invariant-dual identity

Let \(U_{K,q}\) be the complete canonical carrier histogram of the rows
in a component \(K\), after any fixed context push-forward, and let
\(V_{K,q}\) be its conjugate-shore histogram.  Coordinate conjugacy gives

\[
                  \boxed{V_{K,q}=\eta_*U_{\eta_sK,q}.}
\tag{2.1}
\]

In particular, a stable component has signed effect

\[
                    \delta_{K,q}=\eta_*U_{K,q}-U_{K,q},
 \qquad \eta_*\delta_{K,q}=-\delta_{K,q},
\tag{2.2}
\]

and therefore preserves every \(\eta\)-orbit total.

The Boolean component constructed in Section 3 is \(\eta_s\)-stable:
\(\eta_s\) permutes its all-horizontal cube, so its image component meets
it and hence equals it.

There is an exact protected-dual consequence.  Fix a nonnegative allowed
dual vector \(z\) which is invariant under the inherited physical
involution, and put

\[
                         b_K=\langle z,U_K\rangle.
\tag{2.3}
\]

Then

\[
                         \langle z,V_K\rangle=b_{\eta_sK}.
\tag{2.4}
\]

Hence a stable component contributes exactly \(b_K\) to the protected
minimum, while a paired component orbit contributes

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 &\min(b_K,b_{\eta K})+\min(b_{\eta K},b_K)\\
 &\hspace{15mm}=b_K+b_{\eta K}-|b_K-b_{\eta K}|.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{2.5}
\]

Thus, relative to the canonical protected mass, the complete component
cube can reduce an invariant dual by exactly

\[
                  \boxed{
                  I_z=\sum_{\{K,\eta K\}\ \mathrm{paired}}
                            |b_K-b_{\eta K}|,}
\tag{2.6}
\]

and by nothing on stable components.  A conjugacy-based CRH proof must
therefore produce coefficient-scale paired-component imbalance against
every relevant invariant protected vector.  Conversely, one invariant
protected vector with an \(\Omega(W)\) canonical certificate and
\(I_z=o(W)\) would close this seed.  Neither estimate is presently known
for the merged critical carriers.

Independently of conjugacy, every complete component effect has zero total
and zero point margins at every depth:

\[
 \sum_T\delta_{K,q}(T)=0,
 \qquad
 \sum_{T\ni x}\delta_{K,q}(T)=0.
\tag{2.7}
\]

This follows because both shores contain the same number of cyclic rows,
each row has the same number of starts, and a fixed coordinate belongs to
the same number of fixed-length cyclic intervals in every row.  Thus only
Johnson harmonic degrees at least two can carry useful component action.

## 3. A literal Boolean component and the failure of the Motzkin invariant

The key owner edge is a seam identity.  For \(A\in D_t\),
\(B\in D_{s-t}\), the MSW concatenation law gives

\[
                         X_t^{F_s}(AB)=\overline A B.
\tag{3.1}
\]

At the seam, \(\overline A\) ends in `1` and \(B\) begins in `1`; the
cross-seam factor of \(\eta_s\) therefore fixes `11`.  Hence

\[
 X_t^{G_s}\!\left(\eta_s(AB)\right)
   =\overline{\eta_tA}\,(\eta_{s-t}B)
   =X_t^{F_s}\!\left((\eta_tA)(\eta_{s-t}B)\right).
\tag{3.2}
\]

This is one literal \(X_t\)-owner edge.  Its two roots agree away from
the seam and differ there by

\[
                             10\longleftrightarrow01.
\tag{3.3}
\]

Whenever a Motzkin horizontal lies at ground, orienting it as `01`
produces exactly the Dyck seam in (3.1).  Therefore every ground-horizontal
orientation can be toggled independently by (3.2).  In particular, all
\(2^{s-1}\) all-horizontal roots lie in one Boolean component.

There is also a uniform arch edge.  Put

\[
 P_0=1(10)^{s-1}0,
 \qquad
 P_1=1\,11\,(10)^{s-3}\,00\,0.
\tag{3.4}
\]

The MSW first-transition recursion gives

\[
                         X_1^{G_s}(P_1)=X_1^{F_s}(P_0).
\tag{3.5}
\]

Their Motzkin skeletons are the two distinct peakless words in (0.3), so
the peak-erasure normal form of the smaller conjugated-leaf graph is not an
invariant of the direct overlay.

Right-concatenating (3.5), and using one seam edge (3.2) to correct the
conjugated suffix, places a further \(2^{s-3}\) distinct roots in the same
component.  These have skeleton

\[
                         UH^{a-3}DH^{s-a}
 \qquad(3\le a\le s),
\tag{3.6}
\]

with arbitrary horizontal suffix colours.  This proves (0.4), and its
mirror supplies a second family.  Indeed, if

\[
             \mu(P)=\overline{\operatorname {rev}(P)},
\]

then the MSW path-reversal identity is

\[
 X_t^F(\mu P)=\varrho_sX_{s-t}^F(P),
 \qquad
 Y_t^F(\mu P)=\varrho_sY_{s-1-t}^F(P),
\tag{3.7}
\]

where \(\varrho_s(i)=2s+1-i\).  Coordinate reversal commutes with
\(\eta_s\), so (3.7) holds on both shores and \(\mu\) is an overlay
automorphism.  It preserves the all-horizontal cube, hence stabilizes its
component.

The mirror arch family has skeletons

\[
                         H^{s-a}UH^{a-3}D.
\tag{3.8}
\]

It is disjoint from (3.6) for \(a<s\), because the latter starts with
\(U\) and the former with \(H\).  At \(a=s\) the unique spanning arch is
common.  The mirror therefore adds \(2^{s-3}-1\) roots.  Together with
the \(2^{s-1}\) horizontal roots, this proves

\[
                         |K_*|\ge3\cdot2^{s-2}-1,
\tag{3.9}
\]

whose square divided by \(C_s\) proves (0.5).

The exponential component has density

\[
 {3\cdot2^{s-2}-1\over C_s}
   =\exp\bigl(-(\log2+o(1))s\bigr).
\tag{3.10}
\]

Thus it destroys bounded second moment without being a positive-mass
giant.

It is not completely profile-inert.  On this stable component the
canonical first-insertion histogram is supported on the even targets
\(2j\), while its conjugate is supported on \(2j+1\) for \(j<s\) and
fixes \(2s\).  Among the all-horizontal cube roots, only the all-`10`
root has top first return.  Therefore the component's intrinsic
first-insertion action satisfies

\[
 {1\over2}\|h_{K_*}^{G,\mathrm{first}}-
                  h_{K_*}^{F,\mathrm{first}}\|_1
       \ge 2^{s-1}-1.
\tag{3.11}
\]

This proves genuine activity at one local boundary statistic.  It still
does not prove activity at \(\Theta(s)\) prefix cuts per row, and the
singleton insertion histogram is not itself the physically merged shallow
carrier tensor of Section 5.

## 4. First-phase cycle and component-count bounds

Let \(R_n\) be defined by

\[
             \sum_{n\ge0}R_nz^n
               ={1\over1-z^2C(z)^2}
               ={C(z)^2\over2C(z)-1}.
\tag{4.1}
\]

The all-block first-return matrix has top diagonal cell
\(R_{s-1}\), and every lower diagonal cell is zero.  If
\(\sigma_1(P)=P\), the canonical and conjugate first lower states agree.
They have the same root, so their inserted coordinates agree.  The
canonical target is even; the conjugate target is odd except at the top
target \(2s\).  Therefore every fixed point of \(\sigma_1\) lies in the
top/top cell, and

\[
                         |\operatorname {Fix}\sigma_1|
                         \le R_{s-1}.
\tag{4.2}
\]

A permutation on \(C_s\) points with at most \(R_{s-1}\) fixed points
has at most \((C_s+R_{s-1})/2\) cycles.  Every full component is a union
of \(\sigma_1\)-cycles, which proves (0.7)--(0.8).  It also gives the
weak universal moment lower bound

\[
                         \chi_s\ge2-{R_{s-1}\over C_s}
                                  ={17\over9}+o(1),
\tag{4.3}
\]

superseded quantitatively by (0.5).

The first phase itself is highly fragmented.  If \(c(\sigma_1)\) denotes
its number of cycles, then

\[
 c(\sigma_1)\ge
 \begin{cases}
 C_{(s-2)/2},&s\text{ even},\\[1mm]
 \lceil C_{(s-1)/2}/2\rceil,&s\text{ odd}.
 \end{cases}
\tag{4.4}
\]

Indeed \(\sigma_1=\beta\eta_s\), where
\(\beta=i_1^{-1}\eta_si_1\); both involutions reverse every
\(\sigma_1\)-cycle.  Their fixed-point counts are

\[
 |\operatorname {Fix}_{D_s}\eta_s|
 =\begin{cases}0,&s\text{ even},\\ C_{(s-1)/2},&s\text{ odd},\end{cases}
\tag{4.5}
\]

and

\[
 |\operatorname {Fix}\beta|
 =\begin{cases}2C_{(s-2)/2},&s\text{ even},\\
                 C_{(s-1)/2},&s\text{ odd}.
   \end{cases}
\tag{4.6}
\]

A reflection of one cycle has at most two fixed points, giving (4.4).
The remaining parity case \(s=5\) is excluded because both involutions on
42 roots have two fixed points, so their product is even whereas a
42-cycle is odd.  Hence \(\sigma_1\) is not one cycle for every
\(s\ge5\).  This does not prevent other \(X\)-phases or the odd
\(Y\)-relations from merging all of its cycles.

## 5. Exact complete carrier tensor

Write the canonical rooted local coordinate word as

\[
 \omega_F(P)=(a_1,\ldots,a_s,b_1,\ldots,b_s,\star),
\tag{5.1}
\]

where \(a_1,\ldots,a_s\) are deleted and
\(b_1,\ldots,b_s\) are inserted.  Then

\[
                  \boxed{\omega_G(P)=\eta_s^\star
                                   \omega_F(\eta_sP),}
\tag{5.2}
\]

where \(\eta_s^\star\) fixes the anchor.  Let \(\Pi_c^H(P)\) be the
set of the first \(c\) symbols of \(\omega_H(P)\).  Before the anchor,

\[
 \Pi_c^H(P)=
 \begin{cases}
  A_c^H(P),&0\le c\le s,\\
  P\cup B_{c-s}^H(P),&s\le c\le2s,
 \end{cases}
\tag{5.3}
\]

and

\[
                    \Pi_c^G(P)=\eta_s^\star
                                      \Pi_c^F(\eta_sP).
\tag{5.4}
\]

Now lift into one first-fringe context \(C\) of a size-\(m\) factor.  Its
local cyclic block has length \(b=2s+1\).  For \(1\le q\le H\), put
\(k_q=m-q\).  If

\[
                         b<k_q<2m+1-b,
\tag{5.5}
\]

no length-\(k_q\) interval can have both boundary cuts strictly inside the
local block.  An affected interval has exactly one internal cut and its
local part is either a proper prefix or the complementary proper suffix.
We orient the inherited local block as in (5.1).  A reversed aligned
context only reindexes \(c\leftrightarrow2s+1-c\), exchanges the two
collar families below, and conjugates \(\eta_s^\star\); none of
(5.6)--(5.10) changes.

Let \(E^-_{C,q,c},E^+_{C,q,c}\) be the two fixed exterior collars at cut
\(c\), let \(J^\star\) be the local coordinate set including the anchor,
and let \(\iota_C\) be the local physical injection.  The complete
row/start-resolved effect is

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
 d_{C,P,q}=\sum_{c=1}^{2s}\Big(&
 e_{E^-_{C,q,c}\cup\iota_C(\Pi_c^G(P))}
 -e_{E^-_{C,q,c}\cup\iota_C(\Pi_c^F(P))}\\
 &+e_{E^+_{C,q,c}\cup
       \iota_C(J^\star\setminus\Pi_c^G(P))}
 -e_{E^+_{C,q,c}\cup
       \iota_C(J^\star\setminus\Pi_c^F(P))}
 \Big).
\end{aligned}}
\tag{5.6}
\]

This formula includes every crossing collar.  Summing over all local roots
in one context gives

\[
                    \boxed{D_{C,q}=(\eta_{C*}-I)U^F_{C,q},}
\tag{5.7}
\]

and resolving by a full component gives exactly

\[
                    \boxed{V_{C,K,q}=\eta_{C*}U_{C,\eta_sK,q}.}
\tag{5.8}
\]

Here \(U^F_{C,q}\) is the complete *affected* prefix/suffix histogram in
(5.6); starts having no internal block cut are common to both shores and
belong to the fixed background.  This distinction is necessary because
the common unchanged histogram need not itself be \(\eta_C\)-invariant.

Different contexts generally induce different physical involutions
\(\eta_C\).  Therefore, after physical target merging,

\[
                         D_q=\sum_CD_{C,q}
\tag{5.9}
\]

is not one global permutation coboundary unless an additional collision
consistency theorem is proved.

The paired upper-shadow tensor is obtained exactly by physical
complementation.  If
\(\mathfrak c(T)=[2m+1]\setminus T\), then

\[
 D^{\rm upper}_{C,q}=\mathfrak c_*D^{\rm lower}_{C,q},
 \qquad
 V^{\rm upper}_{C,K,q}
   =\mathfrak c_*V^{\rm lower}_{C,K,q}.
\tag{5.10}
\]

Complementation commutes with \(\eta_C\), so the same component law,
cut count, and norm estimates hold on the upper side.  Thus (5.6) and
(5.10), rather than (5.6) alone, are the complete two-sided carrier
tensor.

The source-resolved first-insertion coordinate is an internal position of
(5.1).  Under (5.5) no shallow ambient interval can isolate it with both
cuts in the local block.  The ambient profile sees the crossing flag
\(P\cup\{b_1(P)\}\) and all other prefixes/suffixes, not the singleton
\(b_1(P)\) table by itself.  This is why the \(1/9\) quota cannot be
inserted directly into CRH.

## 6. Exact critical constants and the activity gap

Define

\[
 d_\partial(P)=
 \#\{1\le c\le2s-1:c\ne s,
                 \ \Pi_c^F(P)\ne\Pi_c^G(P)\},
\tag{6.1}
\]

and

\[
 \alpha_s={1\over(2s-2)C_s}\sum_{P\in D_s}d_\partial(P).
\tag{6.2}
\]

Every changed cut produces exactly two tagged ambient window replacements.
Thus one row and one depth have physical half-\(\ell^1\) action at most

\[
                         2d_\partial(P)\le4(s-1).
\tag{6.3}
\]

Let \(a_{m,s}\) count size-\(m\) roots avoiding a size-\(s\) fringe, so
the number of first-fringe contexts is

\[
                         {C_m-a_{m,s}\over C_s}.
\tag{6.4}
\]

At \(s=\Theta(\sqrt m)\), \(a_{m,s}=o(C_m)\).  Put

\[
 \Omega_{A,m}=\sum_{q\le\lceil A\sqrt m\rceil}{1\over c_q}.
\tag{6.5}
\]

Uniformly for \(q=x\sqrt m\),

\[
 {\binom{2m+1}m\over\binom{2m+1}{m-q}}
          =\exp(x^2+O_A(m^{-1/2})),
\tag{6.6}
\]

so

\[
                         {\Omega_{A,m}\over\sqrt m}\longrightarrow J_A.
\tag{6.7}
\]

The exact tagged action and physical ceiling are

\[
 \mathscr A^{\rm tag}_{A,m,s}
 =4(s-1)\alpha_s\Omega_{A,m}(C_m-a_{m,s}),
\tag{6.8}
\]

and

\[
 {1\over2}\sum_{q\le H}{1\over c_q}
             \|\mu_q^G-\mu_q^F\|_1
 \le\mathscr A^{\rm tag}_{A,m,s}.
\tag{6.9}
\]

Since \(W=(2m+1)C_m\), if \(s/\sqrt m\to\kappa\) and
\(\alpha_s\to\alpha\) along a subsequence, then

\[
              {\mathscr A^{\rm tag}_{A,m,s}\over W}
                 \longrightarrow2\kappa J_A\alpha.
\tag{6.10}
\]

The first-return table proves that the canonical and conjugate first
insertions differ on exactly \(C_s-R_{s-1}\) certified rows.  It follows
only that

\[
                 \alpha_s\ge{4\over9s}+o(s^{-1}).
\tag{6.11}
\]

Thus the current lower information guarantees only an \(o(W)\)-sized
portion of tagged action; it does not upper-bound the actual tagged action,
which could still be \(\Theta(W)\).
To obtain coefficient-scale retained action, one still needs

\[
                         \liminf_{s\to\infty}\alpha_s>0
\tag{6.12}
\]

or a different physical theorem surviving collisions.  The fact that
\(\eta_s\) moves \(\Theta(s)\) coordinate labels does not prove that the
MSW row word changes at \(\Theta(s)\) prefix cuts.

## 7. Exact hinge and zero-margin obstruction

Within one fixed context and depth, let

\[
                         u=U^F_{C,q},\qquad v=\eta_{C*}u,
\]

and let \(a=\ell_q-\beta_q\) be the effective background minus quota.
On a nontrivial orbit \(\{T,T'\}\), \(T'=\eta_CT\), the exact endpoint
hinge difference is

\[
\begin{aligned}
 \Delta_{T,T'}={}&
 (a_T+u_{T'})_++(a_{T'}+u_T)_+\\
 &-(a_T+u_T)_+-(a_{T'}+u_{T'})_+.
\end{aligned}
\tag{7.1}
\]

If \(I_u\) is the interval between \(u_T,u_{T'}\), and \(I_a\) the
interval between \(-a_T,-a_{T'}\), then

\[
                         |\Delta_{T,T'}|=|I_u\cap I_a|,
\tag{7.2}
\]

with the rearrangement sign, and hence

\[
 |\Delta_{T,T'}|
 \le\min\{|u_T-u_{T'}|,|a_T-a_{T'}|\}.
\tag{7.3}
\]

Thus an \(\eta_C\)-symmetric effective background is exactly hinge-flat.
There is no intrinsic descent sign in a coordinate conjugate.

Finally consider one recoverable target block with two equal-mass endpoint
vectors \(x^0,x^1\).  Choose endpoint one with probability \(t\), and put

\[
 H_e=\sum_T(x^e(T)-\beta(T))_+,
 \quad
 \Phi(t)=\sum_T((1-t)x^0(T)+tx^1(T)-\beta(T))_+.
\tag{7.4}
\]

Let

\[
 e={1\over2}\|x^1-x^0\|_1.
\]

The exact targetwise square-root remainder is

\[
 R(t)=\sqrt{t(1-t)}\,e.
\tag{7.5}
\]

Because the hinge is one-Lipschitz in half-\(\ell^1\) on equal-mass
vectors,

\[
 \Phi(t)\ge H_0-te,
 \qquad
 \Phi(t)\ge H_1-(1-t)e.
\tag{7.6}
\]

Since \(\sqrt{t(1-t)}\ge\min(t,1-t)\),

\[
                         \boxed{\Phi(t)+R(t)\ge\min(H_0,H_1).}
\tag{7.7}
\]

Applying this at every depth with weight \(1/c_q\), and then summing
disjoint context blocks, proves (0.11).  Arbitrary correlations among
context bits do not help because a target in this regime sees only one
bit.

The target-disjointness assumption is not a consequence of disjoint
first-fringe root packets.  If several context/component effects hit one
physical target, covariance can change (7.5); this is precisely the
unresolved escape.

## 8. Exact proved/conditional boundary

The all-block pair now passes the following gates:

* literal exact completion through every \(X/Y\) phase;
* asymptotically complete first-fringe supply at \(s=\Theta(\sqrt m)\);
* extensive row distance, at least
  \((8/9+o(1))C_s\) local rows;
* root-scale rather than bounded carrier width; and
* a locality ceiling which permits, but does not prove, coefficient-scale
  action; the exact upper bound is (0.10).

It fails every criterion requiring uniformly bounded maximum component
size or uniformly bounded size-biased component second moment because of
(0.4)--(0.6).
It is conditionally ruled out by (0.11) if the unresolved full overlay is
connected and the physical carrier targets are recoverable.

The following are unproved and are each indispensable to a positive CRH
claim:

1. the complete full-component distribution after all \(X\)-phases and
   noninjective \(Y\)-owner relations are combined;
2. positive-density linear prefix-cut activity, or an equivalent physical
   retained-action theorem;
3. a directed mean-hinge estimate after physical merging of different
   first-fringe contexts;
4. a targetwise covariance estimate for one common law on the exact
   component cube; and
5. defeat of the invariant protected-dual identity (2.6).

Accordingly the answer to the CRH question is **no, not yet**.  There is
now a proved large-component/second-moment obstruction and a sharp
conditional zero-margin obstruction, but no absolute conjugacy invariant
or full-connectivity theorem.  A positive result must either show that one
deterministic endpoint is already good, or use unresolved fragmented
components and cross-context physical collisions in a quantitatively
directed way; the first-column \(1/9\) result cannot stand in for either
calculation.
