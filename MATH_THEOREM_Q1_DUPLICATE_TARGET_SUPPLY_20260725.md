# A forced supply of depth-one duplicate targets

Date: 2026-07-25

Method: pure mathematics only.

## 0. Outcome

Let \(F\) be an exact middle wreath factor on \(n=2m+1\) coordinates.
At depth one write

\[
 \mu(R)=\mu_{F,1}(R),\qquad
 N_1=\binom{n}{m-1},\qquad
 h=\#\{R:\mu(R)=0\}.
\]

The cyclic endpoint structure gives the pointwise bound

\[
 \mu(R)\le M_m:=\left\lfloor {m+2\over2}\right\rfloor.
\]

Consequently the number \(d_2\) of targets of load at least two obeys

\[
 \boxed{
 d_2\ge {h+W-N_1\over M_m-1}.}
\]

Thus linear hole density forces at least \(\Theta(W/m)\) distinct
duplicate targets; the duplicate excess cannot be hidden on \(o(W/m)\)
targets.

Moreover, averaging over coordinate relabelings shows that some
permutation \(\sigma\) pairs at least

\[
 \boxed{
 {h d_2\over N_1}
 \ge {h(h+W-N_1)\over N_1(M_m-1)}}
\]

old holes with targets of old load at least two.  If \(h\ge\epsilon W\),
this is

\[
 (2\epsilon^2+o(1)){W\over m}.
\]

This is exactly the one-round scale required by an \(O(m)\)-round soft
repair scheme.  It does not by itself give a legal switch: an arbitrary
relabeling overlay can have too few ownership components.  The remaining
gate is to realize this target-level duplicate supply through a
component-rich exact-factor coupling.

## 1. Endpoint multiplicity bound

For a rank-\((m-1)\) target \(R\), let

\[
 E_F(R)=\{x\notin R:
 R\text{ is an endpoint facet of }R\cup\{x\}
 \text{ in its unique owning wreath}\}.
\]

Every cyclic occurrence of \(R\) has exactly two adjacent middle
extensions.  Conversely, the exact middle ownership of an extension
\(R\cup\{x\}\) determines the occurrence which contributes \(x\).
Therefore the extensions contributed by distinct occurrences are
distinct and

\[
 \boxed{|E_F(R)|=2\mu(R).}
\]

There are only \(m+2\) possible extensions of \(R\), whence

\[
 \boxed{
 \mu(R)\le\left\lfloor {m+2\over2}\right\rfloor=M_m.}
 \tag{1.1}
\]

## 2. Distinct duplicate targets

Let

\[
 {\cal D}_2=\{R:\mu(R)\ge2\},\qquad d_2=|{\cal D}_2|.
\]

The positive targets number \(N_1-h\).  Since the total load is \(W\),

\[
 \sum_{R\in{\cal D}_2}(\mu(R)-1)
 =W-(N_1-h)=h+W-N_1.
 \tag{2.1}
\]

By (1.1), every summand in (2.1) is at most \(M_m-1\).  Hence

\[
 \boxed{
 d_2\ge
 \left\lceil{h+W-N_1\over M_m-1}\right\rceil.}
 \tag{2.2}
\]

Because

\[
 W-N_1={2W\over m+2},
\]

linear \(h\) implies \(d_2=\Omega(W/m)\).

## 3. Relabeling and transposition opportunity

For \(\sigma\) uniformly distributed in \(S_n\), put

\[
 A_\sigma^{(2)}
 =\#\{R:\mu_F(R)=0,\ \mu_{\sigma F}(R)\ge2\}.
\]

For every fixed target \(R\), the target \(\sigma^{-1}R\) is uniform on
\(\binom{[n]}{m-1}\).  Therefore

\[
 \mathbb E_\sigma A_\sigma^{(2)}
 =\sum_{R:\mu_F(R)=0}
   \Pr(\sigma^{-1}R\in{\cal D}_2)
 ={h d_2\over N_1}.
 \tag{3.1}
\]

Some \(\sigma\) attains at least the mean.  Combining (3.1) with (2.2)
gives

\[
 \boxed{
 A_\sigma^{(2)}\ge
 {h(h+W-N_1)\over N_1(M_m-1)}.}
 \tag{3.2}
\]

If \(h\ge\epsilon W\), then \(N_1=(1-o(1))W\) and
\(M_m-1=(1+o(1))m/2\), so (3.2) is

\[
 A_\sigma^{(2)}\ge
 (2\epsilon^2+o(1)){W\over m}.
 \tag{3.3}
\]

There is also a pointwise transposition-opportunity bound which uses the
wreath endpoints, not merely Johnson expansion.  If \(R\) is a hole and
\(x\notin R\), consider the unique wreath owning the middle set
\(X=R\cup\{x\}\).  That row has exactly two endpoint facets.  Neither can
be \(R\), since \(R\) is a hole, so both are covered Johnson neighbours
of \(R\).  As \(x\) varies, all \(2(m+2)\) neighbours obtained this way
are distinct: each contains the unique new element \(x\in S\setminus R\).
Therefore

\[
 \boxed{
 |N_{J(n,m-1)}(R)\setminus{\cal Z}|\ge2(m+2)
 \quad(R\in{\cal Z}),}
 \tag{3.4}
\]

where \({\cal Z}\) is the hole family.  Summing over holes gives

\[
 |\partial_J{\cal Z}|\ge2(m+2)h.
 \tag{3.5}
\]

Every Johnson edge is generated by a unique coordinate transposition.
Hence some transposition \(\tau\) satisfies

\[
 \boxed{
 |{\cal Z}\setminus\tau{\cal Z}|
 \ge {2(m+2)h\over\binom n2}
 ={4(m+2)h\over n(n-1)}.}
 \tag{3.6}
\]

This guarantees \(\Theta(W/m)\) hole-to-covered opportunities at linear
hole density.  It does not guarantee that the covered endpoints have
load at least two; that is the distinction between merely transporting
a hole and genuine net repair.

## 4. Exact remaining conversion gate

The soft component-switch drift theorem shows that a coupling of \(F\)
and \(\sigma F\) improves the hole count if the duplicate-pair supply
\(A_\sigma^{(2)}\) dominates

* component coalescence \(\Xi_\sigma\), measuring duplicate occurrences
  trapped in the same ownership component; and
* nonrobust common-coverage leakage \(\Lambda_\sigma\).

The sufficient inequality is

\[
 A_\sigma^{(2)}>\Xi_\sigma+2\Lambda_\sigma.
\]

Equations (2.2)--(3.3) prove that the left side is available at the
correct asymptotic scale at every factor with linear first-shadow defect.
What is not proved is that one may choose a relabeling or a multi-factor
circuit whose ownership components both expose that supply and make
\(\Xi_\sigma+2\Lambda_\sigma\) smaller.  This is now a
component-geometry problem, not a shortage of duplicate targets.
