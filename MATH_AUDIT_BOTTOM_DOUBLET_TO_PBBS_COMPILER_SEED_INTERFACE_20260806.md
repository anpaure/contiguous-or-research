# Audit: can bottom balanced-doublet rounding seed the terminal PBBS compiler?

**Date:** 2026-08-06  
**Method:** exact comparison of resource shores, payload cells, and the
domain of literal compiler transports; no computation or search  
**Status:** conditional bridge theorem and exact incompatibility audit.
Balanced-doublet/macro rounding alone does **not** output a complete literal
strict-lower matching.  It can replace the canonical PBBS deep selector if
it is augmented by its own named-payload atlas and is packed around the
protected PBBS upper bank with common-history source seams.

## 1. What the bottom macro selector actually selects

Put `t=r-d`.  One `h`-fold FIFO macro, `h in {2,3}`, contains

* one fresh path of `d` rank-`t` lower resources;
* `h(d+1)` rank-`r` owner resources;
* one anchor copy of the lower block; and
* `h-1` owner-disjoint duplicate source blocks which may be thinned between
  their mandatory cores and maximal envelopes.

The balanced-doublet hypergraph doubles this atom and adds two mark slots.
Its lower shore is exactly

\[
                         {[k]\choose t},
\tag{1.1}
\]

not the full strict-lower target ideal

\[
                         \mathcal L_{<r}
   =\bigcup_{s=1}^{r-1}{[k]\choose s}.
\tag{1.2}
\]

Thus successful integral macro rounding gives one occurrence of every
rank-`t` lower resource, the required duplicate-block capacity, and an
owner factor up to its separator leave.  It does not choose a named target
for every short source interval.

This is not hidden in the macro matching.  Corollary 3.4 of
`MATH_THEOREM_FRESH_FIFO_BLOCK_CHAIN_KERNEL_AND_PAYLOAD_ATLAS_GATE_20260805.md`
allows the free copies to be thinned independently while leaving every
selected lower and owner macro resource unchanged.  Two payload fillings
can therefore have the identical macro edge set and different strict-lower
decks.  No function of the integral macro matching alone can be a literal
matching of (1.2).

The exact missing bottom-side statement is still the payload criterion of
that note.  In one free block `G`, prescribed values `Z_I` must satisfy,
coordinatewise,

\[
 I\cap\left(G\setminus
   \bigcup_{J:x\notin (Z_J-F(J))}J\right)\ne\varnothing
\tag{1.3}
\]

for every positive target incidence.  Equivalently, the payload residuals
must form one interval-union deck.  Macro rounding supplies the cells, not
this named atlas.

## 2. The positive interface once a payload atlas exists

Suppose the bottom construction is strengthened to produce:

1. one literal FIFO antecedent `A_bot` of its owner factor; and
2. an injective literal matching
   \[
      \mu:\mathcal L_{<r}\hookrightarrow
                 \mathcal C_{<r}(A_{bot}).
   \tag{2.1}
   \]

Then the terminal compiler-functoriality theorem is agnostic about how
`mu` was produced.  Any later sequence of source rewrites carrying
exact-value occurrence injections on the image of `mu` transports it with
zero Hall loss.  Therefore a bottom payload atlas would be an entirely
valid compiler seed; it need not be the canonical PBBS gap-section atlas.

In particular the bottom route is structurally friendlier than the
canonical deep selector in one respect.  Its free payload blocks are
separated and an anchor copy retains the owner row, so payload changes are
owner-invisible.  The global owner anti-cover row `(OC)` of a fully free
canonical antecedent is replaced by the block-local coordinate cut (1.3).
Singletons may be installed at block boundaries by the proved
last-in/first-out equality.  This is a genuine alternative lower compiler
route.

## 3. Why current PBBS functoriality cannot yet consume it

The present PBBS upper construction begins with a specific protected bank
of canonical PBBS collars, pentagons, split blocks, tagged arms, and named
upper-backup paths.  Its proved source transports apply only when their old
phase and complete common history occur literally in the current source
word.

An integral bottom macro factor and the canonical/tagged PBBS factor have
the same ambient rank-`r` owner set, but generally different:

* owner adjacencies;
* order-`d` source histories;
* lower `q1` colours;
* component decompositions; and
* prepared local move sites.

No current theorem gives an occurrence-injective source homotopy from the
bottom FIFO chronology to the canonical PBBS chronology.  Equality of the
owner vertex set is insufficient: changing the successor permutation can
destroy every short source address in (2.1).  Likewise, the owner/`q1`
protected-factor theorem and its unoriented upper backups are graph-level
existence results, not maps on source cells.

Even immediate upper coverage is not determined by the owner set.  In
`J(5,2)`, the two cycles

\[
 12,13,14,15,12
 \quad\hbox{and}\quad
 12,14,13,15,12
\tag{3.1}
\]

use the same four owners, but their union-colour multisets are respectively

\[
 \{123,134,145,125\},
 \qquad
 \{124,134,135,125\}.
\tag{3.2}
\]

Thus replacing the unprotected PBBS completion by a bottom owner factor can
already lose `q1` upper targets, before arbitrary-width witnesses or source
occurrences are considered.

Nor does a fixed owner trace determine its lower source cells.  At depth
one, the two local source triples

\[
 (\{1,3\},\{2\},\{1,4\}),
 \qquad
 (\{3\},\{1,2\},\{4\})
\tag{3.3}
\]

have the same two consecutive owner unions

\[
                         \{1,2,3\},\quad\{1,2,4\},
\tag{3.4}
\]

but different singleton and two-letter decks.  Thus even an owner-identical
completion has no canonical map on lower compiler cells.

Therefore the following inference is invalid:

\[
 \text{integral bottom macro factor}
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \text{PBBS source rethread carrying its compiler}.
\tag{3.5}
\]

There are two separate missing arrows: the payload matching (Section 1)
and a source-level bridge into the protected upper architecture.

## 4. Why the polynomial protected forest is not yet a direct overlay

Before generic factor completion, the tagged PBBS construction has a
polynomial protected path forest:

* the phase-forced PBBS pieces;
* their tagged arms and pair-tag connectors; and
* the rank-stratified upper-backup paths.

It is tempting to reserve that forest first and use the bottom macro
selector to fill its complement.  This does **not** follow from the current
upper theorem.  The polynomial backup bank repairs the target current of
specified PBBS head/collar rethreads relative to the old canonical PBBS
deck.  The exponentially large baseline upper deck is supplied by the old
PBBS chronology itself.  An arbitrary protected-factor completion, and in
particular an arbitrary bottom macro factor on the complementary owners,
need not have that baseline deck.  The polynomial backups cannot witness an
exponential family of otherwise absent targets.

Thus a direct overlay is valid only after adding a complete baseline-upper
hypothesis, as made explicit next.

### Theorem 4.1 (protected macro--PBBS overlay, conditional)

Assume that, for every sufficiently large parameter, there exist:

1. a protected PBBS forest `P_up` together with a complete baseline upper
   witness bank in the proposed combined chronology (not merely the
   polynomial residual-backup bank);
2. an integral `2/3` macro packing on the unused lower/owner resources,
   with its ordinary separator completion, whose free payload blocks carry
   a literal matching `mu` for every strict-lower target;
3. literal resident antecedents for every component of
   `P_up` and every macro/separator component; and
4. a spanning tree of separated common-history sites whose owner seams are
   palette-safe (or whose deleted colours have named external backups).

Then the combined construction has one source chronology with the complete
lower compiler `mu`, all named upper witnesses, and no terminal common-cap
defect.  In particular, no canonical PBBS deep remote-block selector is
needed.

#### Proof

The owner resources are disjoint by item 2 and the prospective protection
of `P_up`.  Item 3 supplies source Euler components.  Fuse them along the
tree in item 4.  The common-history theorem preserves occurrence-injectively
every source cell of width at most `d+1`; palette-safe seams preserve the
owner/`q1` factor and the named backups preserve upper coverage.  Hence the
image of `mu` remains a matching of all strict-lower targets.  Serial PBBS
local moves and the terminal opening then transport it by compiler
functoriality.  All upper witnesses are protected throughout.  The final
word therefore has both decks, and the terminal cap has no remaining
target-coverage job. \(\square\)

## 5. Exact strengthened bottom interface

The direct-overlay route replaces the canonical deep selector by the
following combined theorem.

> **Protected payload macro rounding.**  Round the balanced-doublet factor
> after deleting a prescribed polynomial PBBS upper forest, retain the
> stopped marked-cylinder and connectivity conclusions, assign every
> strict-lower target injectively to the separated free-copy payload
> intervals satisfying the coordinate cuts (1.3), and export resident
> common-history ports on a palette-safe spanning tree.

The robustness to a fixed polynomial forbidden bank is plausibly smaller
than the existing hereditary rounding target, but it is not stated or
proved by the current `JSEC`/cleanup package.  More importantly, two global
rows lie outside the lower/owner/slot degrees used by the doublet clock:

* the named payload atlas; and
* the complete baseline upper deck.

The first may be solved after rounding through the exact block-local cuts.
The second requires either an upper-complete macro chronology or a literal
compiler-preserving homotopy from the macro chronology to the canonical
PBBS upper chronology.  The current polynomial PBBS backup bank does not
replace it.

## 6. Verdict

Successful balanced-doublet/macro rounding **as presently formulated** does
not seed terminal compiler functoriality, because it supplies only
rank-`t` lower resources and unlabelled free payload cells.

It can nevertheless eliminate the canonical PBBS deep-selector gate on a
more integrated route.  The price is not another terminal cap; it is:

1. a complete named payload atlas in the bottom free copies;
2. preservation or reconstruction of the canonical baseline upper deck;
3. macro rounding robust to the polynomial protected upper forest; and
4. resident palette-safe common-history fusion of the two component banks.

Thus the canonical deep selector is not intrinsically load-bearing, but a
literal lower matching is.  Current theory has two alternative ways to
manufacture it, and neither is yet complete.
