# The port-automorphic sealed `D_3` native groupoid is only a matching

**Date:** 2026-08-06  
**Method:** pure mathematics; native normal-form identities  
**Status:** unconditional classification of every port-automorphic
conjugate of the four endpoint-sealed persistent edges.  It excludes a
native-only serial circuit inside this orbit, but not a circuit using a
Tamari/other host transport or an exterior phase.

## 0. Verdict

Let

\[
                H=\langle\alpha=(2\ 3),\beta=(4\ 5)\rangle
                   \cong C_2^2.                              \tag{0.1}
\]

Consider every `H`-conjugate of the four endpoint-sealed native edges

\[
                              12^-,54^-,14^+,32^+.             \tag{0.2}
\]

Their literal state-transition groupoid is a disjoint union of six
two-vertex edges:

* four involution edges in the `12/14` family;
* two involution edges in the `54/32` family.

There is no path of two different native transitions.  Hence every closed
native word in this entire port-preserving orbit reduces to backtracking
and has trivial marked row action.

This explains why the time-zero companion union may be connected on the
five **row names** while the dynamic literal slot graph is not state-closed.

## 1. Native packet notation

For fixed ordered banks `X,Y`, write

\[
 \mathcal P(a,d;b,c)=
 \left\{
 (a,b,X,c,d,Y),
 (b,d,X,a,c,Y)
 \right\}.                                                \tag{1.1}
\]

The native substitution is the involution

\[
                 \mathcal P(a,d;b,c)
                    \longleftrightarrow
                 \mathcal P(d,a;b,c).                       \tag{1.2}
\]

All equalities below are equalities of literal unordered two-row packets,
not only of currents.

## 2. The `12/14` orbit

Let `A` be the negative edge-`12` old packet.  Its roles are

\[
 X=V\mathord\infty,qquad Y=1U6,qquad
 (a,b,c,d)=(4,5,2,3).                                      \tag{2.1}
\]

The positive edge-`14` packet is

\[
                         \alpha\beta A,                       \tag{2.2}
\]

because `alpha beta` sends the active role tuple in (2.1) to
`(5,4,3,2)`, the authenticated `14` normal form.

Put

\[
                         A'=T(A)=\mathcal P(3,4;5,2).          \tag{2.3}
\]

### Lemma 2.1

The two `H`-orbits `HA` and `HA'` are disjoint.  Every native transition in
the `12/14` orbit is exactly

\[
                              gA\longleftrightarrow gA',
                              \qquad g\in H.                   \tag{2.4}
\]

Thus this family is four disjoint edges.

#### Proof

The group `H` preserves the two active-label classes

\[
                              \{2,3\},\qquad\{4,5\}.           \tag{2.5}
\]

In every packet `gA`, the first active endpoint `a` belongs to `{4,5}` and
the second endpoint `d` belongs to `{2,3}`.  In `A'`, these memberships are
reversed.  The ordered banks and four boundary slots are fixed, so the
native parameter-recovery lemma makes these alternatives distinct.
Therefore `HA` and `HA'` are disjoint.  Equivariance of (1.2) gives (2.4).
\(\square\)

## 3. The `54/32` orbit

Let `N` be the negative edge-`54` packet.  Its roles are

\[
 X=U2,qquad Y=V\mathord\infty1,qquad
 (a,b,c,d)=(4,3,6,5).                                      \tag{3.1}
\]

Let `P` be the positive edge-`32` packet.  Its roles are

\[
 X=U3,qquad Y=V\mathord\infty1,qquad
 (a,b,c,d)=(5,2,6,4).                                      \tag{3.2}
\]

Directly from (3.1)--(3.2),

\[
                              P=\alpha\beta N.                 \tag{3.3}
\]

The two native transitions are

\[
                              T_{54}:N\longleftrightarrow\beta N,
       \qquad                 T_{32}:\alpha\beta N
                                      \longleftrightarrow\alpha N.
                                                                  \tag{3.4}
\]

Indeed, (1.2) swaps the active endpoints `(4,5)` of `N`, producing exactly
the role tuple of `beta N`; applying it to (3.2) produces `alpha N`.

### Lemma 3.1

All `H`-conjugates of `54` and `32` give only the two undirected edges

\[
                  N-\beta N,qquad \alpha N-\alpha\beta N.    \tag{3.5}
\]

Each edge is represented twice, once from the `54` description and once
from the inverse `32` description.

#### Proof

Conjugating the first edge in (3.4) by every `g in H` gives
`gN-g beta N`, which is exactly the two edges (3.5), each twice.
Using (3.3), the conjugates of the second edge give the same set.  \(\square\)

## 4. Dynamic consequence

### Theorem 4.1 (native port-orbit serial no-go)

The literal transition graph generated by all port-automorphic conjugates
of the four endpoint-sealed persistent `D_3` moves is a matching of six
edges.  Every serial word which begins and ends at the same literal packet
state is a product of backtracks and acts trivially on every tracked row.

#### Proof

Lemma 2.1 gives four disjoint edges on eight packet states.  Lemma 3.1 gives
two disjoint edges on four further packet states.  The two families cannot
meet: their ordered rail shapes are respectively

\[
       (X,Y)=(V\mathord\infty,1U6)
       \quad\hbox{and}\quad
       (X,Y)=(U\{2\text{ or }3\},V\mathord\infty1),           \tag{4.1}
\]

which have different protected-tail placement and boundary labels.
Hence the full graph is a matching.  Any closed walk in a matching
traverses every used edge equally often in the two directions, so its
literal row action is the identity.  \(\square\)

## 5. Exact remaining escape

The connected sealed phase skeleton proves endpoint-safe **availability**,
but Theorem 4.1 proves that port automorphisms and native moves alone do not
give **reuse**.  A productive dynamic circuit must add a transition between
two of the six matching components.  Such a transition must be one of:

1. a planted Tamari or other zero-central-current slot transport;
2. a phase outside the fixed `D_3` port-automorphism orbit;
3. a fragmented-row bridge between two native packet states; or
4. an exterior halo transition with a priced complete rail current.

The direct Tamari two-slot option is separately blocked, for a nonempty
tail, by the q1 tail-count theorem.  Therefore any successful internal
bridge must expose at least a third slot or change the protected-tail
interface itself.
