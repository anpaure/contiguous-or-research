# Polynomial mixed pair frames: simultaneous literal-face Hall, quota-safe rounding, and the exact cycle gate

Date: 2026-07-26

Method: pure mathematics only. No computation, finite search, solver, or
web input is used.

## 0. Verdict

Put

\[
 {\cal M}=\binom{[2m]}m,\qquad W=|{\cal M}|,
 \qquad {\cal L}_q^-=\binom{[2m]}{m-q},
 \qquad {\cal L}_q^+=\binom{[2m]}{m+q},
\]

\[
 N_q=|{\cal L}_q^\pm|,\qquad
 \rho_q={N_q\over W},\qquad
 \Lambda_q={W\over N_q}={1\over\rho_q}.
\]

Assume

\[
 H\le C_0\sqrt{m\log m}
 \tag{0.1}
\]

for a fixed constant \(C_0\). Thus \(H=\sqrt m\,\omega(m)\) is allowed for
every freely chosen slow \(\omega\to\infty\) with
\(\omega\le C_0\sqrt{\log m}\). There is a polynomial catalogue of
coordinate pair frames and one integral assignment
\(A:{\cal M}\to[J]\) of every
middle owner to a good frame such that the following holds simultaneously
for all \(1\le q\le H\) and both signs.

Let \(G_q^\pm(A)\) be the bipartite graph whose left vertices are middle
owners and whose right vertices are literal rank-\((m\pm q)\) targets, with
an edge precisely when that target is a genuine \(q\)-face of the
orientation cube of the owner in its assigned pair frame. Then

\[
 \boxed{
 \sum_{q=1}^H\left(
   \operatorname {def}G_q^-(A)+
   \operatorname {def}G_q^+(A)\right)
   =O\!\left({W\over\sqrt m}\right)+o(W)=o(W).}
 \tag{0.2}
\]

Here

\[
 \operatorname {def}G
 =\max_{{\cal Z}\subseteq R}\bigl(|{\cal Z}|-|N_G({\cal Z})|\bigr)
 \tag{0.3}
\]

is the exact target-side Hall deficiency. Thus, with one common owner-frame
assignment, independent integral matchings at every depth and on both
sides miss only \(o(W)\) target occurrences in total. In particular every
single depth misses \(o(W)\) literal targets.

There is a stronger quota statement. Write

\[
 c_q=\lfloor\Lambda_q\rfloor.
\]

When \(\Lambda_q\) is integral, \(c_q+1\) below denotes the adjacent
upper cell rather than the literal ceiling; conservation of total mass
then makes zero global defect possible only at the constant load \(c_q\).

At every depth and sign there is an integral assignment of every owner
occurrence to a compatible literal target such that the total mobile
floor/ceiling defect

\[
 \sum_T(c_q-n_T)_+
 +\sum_T(n_T-c_q-1)_+
\]

is at most \(e_q^\pm\), where

\[
 \boxed{\sum_{q\le H,\ \pm}e_q^\pm=o(W).}
 \tag{0.4}
\]

Thus polynomially many mixed pair frames remove not only the fixed-frame
type cut but also every literal-face Hall cut, up to a summable \(o(W)\)
exception.

Moreover, the supported assignments may be chosen so that their total
factorial pair-collision excess above the balanced floor/ceiling baseline,
summed over all these depths and both signs, is \(o(W)\). This is proved by
an integral convex min-cost flow in Section 6.2.

This is not yet a contiguous-OR or complete-cycle theorem. The assignment
\(A\) is vertexwise, while a physical \(C_{2h}\) packet requires all its
owners to use one frame. Moreover, compatible faces selected independently
at different depths need not be nested prefixes of one transition order.
In fact, if the polynomial catalogue has \(J\ge m^{1/2+\delta}\) frames
and \(h\to\infty\), then even after allowing **every** geometric
frame-labelled \(C_{2h}\), the independent assignment used below has only
\(o(W)\) owners lying in any monochromatic cycle with high probability.
Hence it cannot simply be grouped into cycles after the fact.

One exact sufficient postprocessing interface for the linear Hall ledger
is:

1. correlate the owner assignment at cycle level, changing only
   \(r=o(W/H)\) owners from an assignment satisfying (0.2); and
2. lose only \(o(W)\) further incidences when replacing compatible faces
   by the actual consecutive windows of the selected cycles.

Changing \(r\) owner frames increases the sum of the two-sided Hall
deficiencies by at most \(2Hr\). Thus the scale \(o(W/H)\) is exact for
this linear interface. To retain the factorial collision conclusion, the
cycle-level assignment must additionally preserve the first- and
second-moment bounds (6.5) and (6.18); this does not follow from a bare
Hamming bound on the number of recoloured owners. The independent
construction itself is much farther from this interface: with high
probability it would require recolouring \(W-o(W)\) owners. Thus complete
cycles must be selected jointly, or generated by a cycle-correlated law
whose one-owner marginals retain the estimates below. Complete
\(C_{2h}\) bundling and common multidepth consecutivity, not
literal-target Hall, are the remaining gates.

## 1. Pair types and the exact mixed marginal

Let \(P\) be a perfect matching of the \(2m\) coordinates. For a set
\(S\), let \(f_P(S)\) be the number of \(P\)-pairs wholly contained in
\(S\). A middle owner of type \(f\) has

\[
 f\text{ full pairs},\quad f\text{ empty pairs},\quad
 s=m-2f\text{ split pairs}.
 \tag{1.1}
\]

A lower target of rank \(m-q\) and type \(f\) has \(f\) full,
\(f+q\) empty, and \(m-2f-q\) split pairs. The source and target orbit
sizes are

\[
 V_f={m!\,2^{m-2f}\over f!^2(m-2f)!},
 \tag{1.2}
\]

\[
 T_{f,q}={m!\,2^{m-2f-q}
              \over f!(f+q)!(m-2f-q)!}.
 \tag{1.3}
\]

For a source of type \(f\), the number of lower depth-\(q\) faces is

\[
 d^{\rm src}_{f,q}=\binom{m-2f}{q}.
 \tag{1.4}
\]

For a target of type \(f\), the number of compatible sources is

\[
 d^{\rm tar}_{f,q}=2^q\binom{f+q}{q}.
 \tag{1.5}
\]

Consequently

\[
 \lambda_{f,q}
 ={d^{\rm tar}_{f,q}\over d^{\rm src}_{f,q}}
 ={V_f\over T_{f,q}}
 ={2^q\binom{f+q}{q}\over\binom{m-2f}{q}}.
 \tag{1.6}
\]

Let

\[
 \pi_0(f)={V_f\over W},\qquad
 \pi_q(f)={T_{f,q}\over N_q}.
 \tag{1.7}
\]

Then the exact mixed-frame marginal identity is

\[
 \boxed{\pi_q(f)\lambda_{f,q}
        =\Lambda_q\pi_0(f).}
 \tag{1.8}
\]

In particular, for every type set \({\cal B}\),

\[
 {\bf E}_{\pi_q}
 \bigl[\lambda_{F,q}{\bf1}_{F\in{\cal B}}\bigr]
 =\Lambda_q\Pr_{\pi_0}(F\in{\cal B}).
 \tag{1.9}
\]

Complementation gives exactly the same formulas for upper targets, with
full and empty pair types exchanged.

To avoid a notational ambiguity below, define the relevant target type by

\[
 g_P^-(T)=|\{E\in P:E\subseteq T\}|
 \quad(T\in{\cal L}_q^-),
\]

\[
 g_P^+(T)=|\{E\in P:E\cap T=\varnothing\}|
 \quad(T\in{\cal L}_q^+).
 \tag{1.10}
\]

Thus \(g_P^-\) counts full pairs on the lower side, while \(g_P^+\)
counts empty pairs on the upper side. If \(T\) is compatible with a
middle owner \(X\) in frame \(P\), then its relevant type is exactly
\(f_P(X)\) on either side.

## 2. A polynomial uniform and separated frame catalogue

Fix a sufficiently large constant \(A_0\), let

\[
 \mu_0={m(m-1)\over2(2m-1)},
\qquad
 {\cal B}=
 \left\{f:|f-\mu_0|
       \le A_0\sqrt{m\log m}\right\},
 \tag{2.1}
\]

and put

\[
 \tau=\Pr_{\pi_0}(F\notin{\cal B}).
 \tag{2.2}
\]

The standard exposure martingale for a uniform perfect matching has
bounded increments by an absolute constant. Hence

\[
 \tau\le 2\exp\left(-cA_0^2\log m\right)
 \tag{2.3}
\]

for an absolute \(c>0\). We choose \(A_0\) so that
\(\tau\le m^{-10}\).

Let

\[
 \varepsilon=m^{-L_0},
 \tag{2.4}
\]

where the fixed \(L_0=L_0(C_0,A_0)\) is chosen sufficiently large that

\[
 H\varepsilon=o(1),
\qquad
 H\varepsilon^2\Lambda_H=o(1).
 \tag{2.5}
\]

This is possible because \(\Lambda_H\le m^{O_{C_0}(1)}\) under (0.1).
A polynomial multiset

\[
 {\mathscr P}=(P_1,\ldots,P_J),\qquad
 J=m^{K(C_0,A_0,L_0)}
 \tag{2.6}
\]

can be chosen so that, with

\[
 I(X)=\{j:f_{P_j}(X)\in{\cal B}\},\qquad
 a_X=|I(X)|,\qquad
 \alpha=J(1-\tau),
 \tag{2.7}
\]

one has, for every middle owner \(X\),

\[
 (1-\varepsilon)\alpha\le a_X\le(1+\varepsilon)\alpha,
 \tag{2.8}
\]

and, for every \(q\le H\), every lower or upper target \(T\),

\[
 {1-\varepsilon\over\rho_q}\alpha
 \le
 \sum_{\substack{j\\g_{P_j}^{\pm}(T)\in{\cal B}}}
       \lambda_{g_{P_j}^{\pm}(T),q}
 \le
 {1+\varepsilon\over\rho_q}\alpha.
 \tag{2.9}
\]

For completeness, here is why the catalogue remains polynomial. On the
band (2.1), and uniformly for \(q\le H\),

\[
 \log\lambda_{f,q}
 \le C\,{q\bigl(q+\sqrt{m\log m}\bigr)\over m}
 \le C'(C_0,A_0)\log m.
 \tag{2.10}
\]

Thus every summand in (2.9) is polynomially bounded. Independent random
frames, Chernoff for (2.8), Bernstein for (2.9), and a union bound over
fewer than \(4^m(H+1)\) target-depth-sign triples prove (2.6)--(2.9) once
the exponent \(K\) is large enough. Identity (1.9) gives the exact
expectation \(\alpha/\rho_q\) in (2.9).

The restriction (0.1) is the proved polynomial-sampling range, not a
combinatorial obstruction. If \(\omega^2\gg\log m\), the balanced-band
summands can have spread \(\exp(\Theta(\omega^2))\), so this argument does
not produce a polynomial catalogue. Conditional on any deterministic
catalogue satisfying (2.8)--(2.9), the Hall proof below needs only
\(H=o(m)\), \(s_*=\Theta(m)\), and \(H\varepsilon=o(1)\).

The same random choice can be required to be pairwise separated. If
\(\iota(P,P')\) is the number of coordinate pairs common to two frames,
then for a fixed \(P\) and independent uniform \(P'\),

\[
 {\bf E}\binom{\iota(P,P')}r
 =
 {\binom mr\over
  (2m-1)(2m-3)\cdots(2m-2r+1)}
 \le {1\over r!}
 \tag{2.11}
\]

for \(r\le m/2\). Hence

\[
 \Pr(\iota(P,P')\ge r)\le {1\over r!}.
 \tag{2.12}
\]

If \(J\le m^K\), taking

\[
 r_m=\left\lceil{(2K+4)\log m\over\log\log m}\right\rceil
 \tag{2.13}
\]

and union bounding over the frame pairs shows that (2.8)--(2.9) and

\[
 \iota(P_i,P_j)\le r_m\qquad(i\ne j)
 \tag{2.14}
\]

hold simultaneously. Separation will not be used in the Hall proof; this
is important, since pairwise frame separation alone is not an expansion
theorem.

The restriction (0.1) belongs only to the polynomial construction of the
uniform catalogue. Sections 3--6 remain valid for any \(H=o(m)\) whenever
(2.8)--(2.9) hold with \(H\varepsilon=o(1)\) and
\(H\varepsilon^2\Lambda_H=o(1)\). For
\(H=\sqrt m\,\omega(m)\) with \(\omega^2\gg\log m\), the polynomial
summand bound (2.10) used in this proof is no longer available. Thus this
note does not claim either a random or deterministic polynomial catalogue
in that range, and it does not claim one is impossible. In the usual
application one is free to choose
\(\omega\to\infty\) sufficiently slowly, for example
\(\omega\le C_0\sqrt{\log m}\).

## 3. One common random owner assignment

Fix a catalogue satisfying Section 2. Independently for every middle
owner \(X\), choose

\[
 A(X)\quad\hbox{uniformly from }I(X).
 \tag{3.1}
\]

Thus every selected frame is central for its owner. Once \(A\) is fixed,
write

\[
 f_X=f_{P_{A(X)}}(X),\qquad
 s_X=m-2f_X,
\qquad
 d_X(q)=\binom{s_X}{q}.
 \tag{3.2}
\]

Put

\[
 s_*=\min_{f\in{\cal B}}(m-2f)
     ={m\over2}-O(\sqrt{m\log m}).
 \tag{3.3}
\]

In particular \(s_*\ge m/3\) and \(H<s_*/2\) for all sufficiently large
\(m\).

For a lower target \(T\), write \(T\sim_{A(X)}^-X\) when \(T\) is obtained
from \(X\) by deleting the selected endpoint in each of \(q\) distinct
split pairs of the assigned frame. Define the normalized target load

\[
 Z_{T,q}^-=
 \sum_{X:T\sim_{A(X)}^-X}
          {\rho_q\over d_X(q)}.
 \tag{3.4}
\]

Define \(Z_{T,q}^+\) analogously by adding the unselected endpoints of
split pairs.

### Lemma 3.1 (exact conditional mean bounds)

For every target, depth, and sign,

\[
 {1-\varepsilon\over1+\varepsilon}
 \le {\bf E}_A Z_{T,q}^\pm
 \le {1+\varepsilon\over1-\varepsilon}.
 \tag{3.5}
\]

#### Proof

We give the lower-side proof. For a fixed frame \(j\) and a target of type
\(f\in{\cal B}\), summing over all compatible owners gives

\[
 \sum_{X:T\sim_j^-X}{\rho_q\over d^{\rm src}_{f,q}}
 =\rho_q\lambda_{f,q}.
 \tag{3.6}
\]

Moreover, every such owner has the same type \(f\), so \(j\in I(X)\).
Using

\[
 {1\over(1+\varepsilon)\alpha}
 \le {1\over a_X}
 \le {1\over(1-\varepsilon)\alpha}
 \tag{3.7}
\]

and then (2.9), we obtain

\[
 {\rho_q\over(1+\varepsilon)\alpha}
 \sum_{\substack{j\\g_{P_j}^-(T)\in{\cal B}}}
   \lambda_{g_{P_j}^-(T),q}
 \le {\bf E}_A Z_{T,q}^-
 \le
 {\rho_q\over(1-\varepsilon)\alpha}
 \sum_{\substack{j\\g_{P_j}^-(T)\in{\cal B}}}
   \lambda_{g_{P_j}^-(T),q}.
 \tag{3.8}
\]

Equation (3.5) follows. The upper proof is its complement. \(\square\)

### Lemma 3.2 (sharp dependency bound)

Set

\[
 b_q={\rho_q\over\binom{s_*}{q}},
\qquad
 M_\varepsilon={1+\varepsilon\over1-\varepsilon}.
 \tag{3.9}
\]

Then

\[
 \operatorname {Var}_A Z_{T,q}^\pm
 \le b_q\,{\bf E}_A Z_{T,q}^\pm
 \le b_qM_\varepsilon.
 \tag{3.10}
\]

#### Proof

For fixed \(T,q\), the summands in (3.4) indexed by different owners are
independent. No independence between different targets, signs, or depths
is claimed or needed. Each owner summand is nonnegative and at most

\[
 {\rho_q\over d_X(q)}
 \le {\rho_q\over\binom{s_*}{q}}=b_q.
\]

Thus \(Y_X^2\le b_qY_X\). Summing
\(\operatorname {Var}Y_X\le{\bf E}Y_X^2\) proves the first inequality,
and Lemma 3.1 proves the second. \(\square\)

This is the complete dependency audit. All incidences belonging to one
owner may be maximally correlated. The only independence used is that the
frame choices of distinct owners are independent.

## 4. Weighted loads dominate every Hall cut

Fix one realization \(A\), one depth, and one sign, and let
\(G_q^\pm(A)\) be the corresponding literal compatibility graph. For
\({\cal Z}\subseteq{\cal L}_q^\pm\),

\[
\begin{aligned}
 \sum_{T\in{\cal Z}} Z_{T,q}^\pm
 &=
 \sum_{X\in N({\cal Z})}
 {\rho_q\over d_X(q)}
 \bigl|\{T\in{\cal Z}:T\sim_{A(X)}^\pm X\}\bigr|\\
 &\le \rho_q|N({\cal Z})|
 \le |N({\cal Z})|.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4.1}
\]

Therefore

\[
 |{\cal Z}|-|N({\cal Z})|
 \le\sum_{T\in{\cal Z}}(1-Z_{T,q}^\pm)
 \le\sum_T(1-Z_{T,q}^\pm)_+.
 \tag{4.2}
\]

By the deficiency form of Hall's theorem,

\[
 \boxed{
 \operatorname {def}G_q^\pm(A)
 \le D_q^\pm(A):=
       \sum_T(1-Z_{T,q}^\pm)_+.}
 \tag{4.3}
\]

This proves all subset cuts at once. No union bound over target families is
used.

Let

\[
 \gamma_\varepsilon={2\varepsilon\over1-\varepsilon}.
 \tag{4.4}
\]

Lemmas 3.1--3.2 and Cauchy--Schwarz give, targetwise,

\[
\begin{aligned}
 {\bf E}_A(1-Z_{T,q}^\pm)_+
 &\le (1-{\bf E}Z_{T,q}^\pm)_+
       +{\bf E}|Z_{T,q}^\pm-{\bf E}Z_{T,q}^\pm|\\
 &\le \gamma_\varepsilon+
       \sqrt{M_\varepsilon b_q}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4.5}
\]

Consequently

\[
 {\bf E}_A D_q^\pm
 \le N_q\left(
       \gamma_\varepsilon+
       \sqrt{{M_\varepsilon\rho_q\over
                    \binom{s_*}{q}}}\right).
 \tag{4.6}
\]

Since \(N_q=W\rho_q\le W\),

\[
 {\bf E}_A D_q^\pm
 \le W\left(
       \gamma_\varepsilon+
       {\sqrt{M_\varepsilon}\over
                    \sqrt{\binom{s_*}{q}}}\right).
 \tag{4.7}
\]

## 5. Summing all depths and both signs

Put

\[
 a_q=\binom{s_*}{q}^{-1/2}.
\]

For \(q<H\),

\[
 {a_{q+1}\over a_q}
 =\sqrt{{q+1\over s_*-q}}
 \le
 r_m':=\sqrt{{H+1\over s_*-H}}=o(1).
 \tag{5.1}
\]

Hence

\[
 \sum_{q=1}^H a_q
 \le {1\over\sqrt{s_*}(1-r_m')}
 ={\sqrt2+o(1)\over\sqrt m}.
 \tag{5.2}
\]

Summing (4.7) gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
 {\bf E}_A\sum_{q=1}^H(D_q^-+D_q^+)
 &\le
 2W\left(
 H\gamma_\varepsilon+
 \sqrt{M_\varepsilon}
 \sum_{q=1}^H{1\over\sqrt{\binom{s_*}{q}}}\right)\\
 &\le { (2\sqrt2+o(1))W\over\sqrt m}
       +O(WH\varepsilon)\\
 &=O(W/\sqrt m)=o(W).
\end{aligned}
\tag{5.3}
\]

Therefore at least one deterministic realization \(A\) satisfies the
same upper bound. Applying Hall independently in every graph proves
(0.2). Notice that the assignment \(A\), not merely the frame catalogue,
is common to every depth and both signs.

The summation is essential. A fixed tolerance estimate at every depth
would generally lose a factor \(H\). Here the variance at depth \(q\) is
controlled by \(\binom{s_*}{q}^{-1}\), so the whole error series is
dominated by \(q=1\).

## 6. The balanced floor/ceiling quota corollary

The unnormalized load is

\[
 U_{T,q}^\pm={Z_{T,q}^\pm\over\rho_q}
 =\sum_{X:T\sim_{A(X)}^\pm X}{1\over d_X(q)}.
 \tag{6.1}
\]

Every source has total outgoing weight exactly one, and

\[
 {\bf E}U_{T,q}^\pm
 ={1\over\rho_q}{\bf E}Z_{T,q}^\pm
 =(1+O(\varepsilon))\Lambda_q.
 \tag{6.2}
\]

Scaling Lemma 3.2 gives

\[
 \operatorname {Var}U_{T,q}^\pm
 \le {M_\varepsilon\Lambda_q\over\binom{s_*}{q}}.
 \tag{6.3}
\]

Therefore

\[
\begin{aligned}
 {\bf E}\sum_T|U_{T,q}^\pm-\Lambda_q|
 &\le
 N_q\left(
   \gamma_\varepsilon\Lambda_q+
   \sqrt{{M_\varepsilon\Lambda_q\over
                 \binom{s_*}{q}}}\right)\\
 &=W\left(
   \gamma_\varepsilon+
   \sqrt{{M_\varepsilon\over
          \Lambda_q\binom{s_*}{q}}}\right)\\
 &\le W\left(
   \gamma_\varepsilon+
   {\sqrt{M_\varepsilon}\over
          \sqrt{\binom{s_*}{q}}}\right).
\end{aligned}
\tag{6.4}
\]

Thus the realization \(A\) may be chosen so that, in addition to (0.2),

\[
 \boxed{
 \sum_{q\le H,\ \pm}
 \|U_q^\pm-\Lambda_q{\bf1}\|_1
 =O(W/\sqrt m)=o(W).}
 \tag{6.5}
\]

Indeed one chooses \(A\) to minimize the sum of the nonnegative
quantities in (5.3) and (6.5); the expectation of their sum has the same
order.

We now convert (6.5) to an integral quota statement.

### Lemma 6.1 (integral mobile-quota support rounding)

Let \(G=(L,R;E)\) be bipartite. Suppose weights \(w_e\ge0\) satisfy

\[
 \sum_{e\ni x}w_e=1\qquad(x\in L),
 \tag{6.6}
\]

and put \(u_y=\sum_{e\ni y}w_e\). Let

\[
 \lambda={|L|\over|R|},\qquad c=\lfloor\lambda\rfloor.
\]

There is an integral assignment of every left vertex along an edge of
\(G\) such that

\[
 \sum_{y\in R}(c-n_y)_+
 +\sum_{y\in R}(n_y-c-1)_+
 \le \sum_{y\in R}|u_y-\lambda|.
 \tag{6.7}
\]

#### Proof

Minimize over all assignments of the left supply the separable target
cost

\[
 \sum_y\bigl((c-n_y)_++(n_y-c-1)_+\bigr).
 \tag{6.8}
\]

This is an ordinary min-cost bipartite flow. At each target \(y\), give
the first \(c\) units of the target-to-sink arc marginal cost \(-1\), the
next unit marginal cost \(0\), and every later unit marginal cost \(1\).
After adding the constant \(c|R|\), its cost is exactly (6.8). All
capacities and supplies are integral, so the flow polytope has an integral
minimum.

The original weights \(w\) are a feasible fractional flow. Extend it
through the target-to-sink arcs by filling their marginal arcs in
nondecreasing cost order. Its cost after adding the constant is

\[
 \sum_y\bigl((c-u_y)_++(u_y-c-1)_+\bigr).
\]

Because \(c\le\lambda\le c+1\), targetwise

\[
 (c-u_y)_++(u_y-c-1)_+
 \le |u_y-\lambda|.
 \tag{6.9}
\]

The integral minimum has cost no larger than this fractional cost and
proves (6.7). \(\square\)

Apply Lemma 6.1 to the weights \(1/d_X(q)\) on the actual assigned-frame
face edges. Equation (6.5) proves (0.4). This also proves the exact
floor-demand Hall estimate

\[
 \max_{{\cal Z}\subseteq{\cal L}_q^\pm}
 \left(c_q|{\cal Z}|-|N({\cal Z})|\right)
 \le
 \sum_T(c_q-U_{T,q}^\pm)_+
 \le\|U_q^\pm-\Lambda_q{\bf1}\|_1.
 \tag{6.10}
\]

Thus the result respects both sides of the mobile floor/ceiling baseline;
it is not merely a one-cover statement. It controls the exact linear MWB
quota defect. It does not by itself bound a quadratic pair-collision
functional on the exceptional target loads. The next subsection supplies
that additional bound.

### 6.2 Factorial collision excess

Let \(\varphi:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)\) be the piecewise-linear
interpolation of the integer function

\[
 \varphi(k)=\binom k2\qquad(k\in{\mathbb Z}_{\ge0}).
 \tag{6.11}
\]

Equivalently, the successive unit marginal costs of \(\varphi\) are

\[
 0,1,2,3,\ldots.
 \tag{6.12}
\]

For \(\Lambda_q\in[c_q,c_q+1]\), put

\[
 B_{\Lambda_q}(x)
 =\varphi(x)-\varphi(\Lambda_q)
       -c_q(x-\Lambda_q).
 \tag{6.13}
\]

Convexity gives \(B_{\Lambda_q}(x)\ge0\). Since every subgradient of
\(\varphi\) at distance \(t\) from \(\Lambda_q\) differs from \(c_q\) by
at most \(t+1\), integration gives the elementary bound

\[
 \boxed{
 0\le B_{\Lambda_q}(x)
 \le |x-\Lambda_q|+\frac12|x-\Lambda_q|^2.}
 \tag{6.14}
\]

We also have a simultaneous square-error estimate. From (6.2)--(6.3),

\[
\begin{aligned}
 {\bf E}\sum_T(U_{T,q}^\pm-\Lambda_q)^2
 &\le
 N_q\left(
 {M_\varepsilon\Lambda_q\over\binom{s_*}{q}}
 +\gamma_\varepsilon^2\Lambda_q^2\right)\\
 &=
 W\left(
 {M_\varepsilon\over\binom{s_*}{q}}
 +\gamma_\varepsilon^2\Lambda_q\right).
\end{aligned}
\tag{6.15}
\]

Now

\[
 \sum_{q=1}^H{1\over\binom{s_*}{q}}=O(1/m),
 \tag{6.16}
\]

by the same ratio argument as (5.1), and

\[
 \sum_{q=1}^H\Lambda_q\le H\Lambda_H.
 \tag{6.17}
\]

The choice (2.5) therefore implies that the same realization \(A\) may
be chosen with

\[
 \boxed{
 \sum_{q\le H,\ \pm}\sum_T
 (U_{T,q}^\pm-\Lambda_q)^2=o(W).}
 \tag{6.18}
\]

Choose \(A\) by minimizing the sum of the nonnegative objectives in
(5.3), (6.5), and (6.18); their expected sum is \(o(W)\).

For fixed \(q\) and sign, minimize pair-collision cost over integral
assignments of all owners along the actual face edges. Give the successive
units entering a target the marginal costs (6.12). This is a min-cost
network with integral capacities, and hence has an integral optimum.
The fractional edge weights \(1/d_X(q)\) are feasible and have target
loads \(U_{T,q}^\pm\). Route each such aggregate through the
target-to-sink marginal arcs in increasing-cost order; its cost is exactly
\(\varphi(U_{T,q}^\pm)\). Therefore the integral optimum \(n_T\) satisfies

\[
 \sum_T\binom{n_T}{2}
 \le\sum_T\varphi(U_{T,q}^\pm).
 \tag{6.19}
\]

Because \(\sum_TU_{T,q}^\pm=W=N_q\Lambda_q\), the linear terms in
(6.13) sum to zero. The balanced integral baseline is

\[
 P_q^{\min}=N_q\varphi(\Lambda_q).
 \tag{6.20}
\]

Using (6.14),

\[
\begin{aligned}
 \sum_T\binom{n_T}{2}-P_q^{\min}
 &\le\sum_T B_{\Lambda_q}(U_{T,q}^\pm)\\
 &\le
 \|U_q^\pm-\Lambda_q{\bf1}\|_1
 +\frac12\|U_q^\pm-\Lambda_q{\bf1}\|_2^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{6.21}
\]

The collision minimizer may be used for the quota conclusion as well.
Because its total mass is \(N_q\Lambda_q\), its collision excess equals
\(\sum_TB_{\Lambda_q}(n_T)\). For every integer \(n\),

\[
 B_{\Lambda_q}(n)=
 \begin{cases}
  \binom{c_q-n+1}{2},&n\le c_q,\\[1mm]
  \binom{n-c_q}{2},&n\ge c_q+1.
 \end{cases}
 \tag{6.21a}
\]

Consequently

\[
 (c_q-n)_++(n-c_q-1)_+\le B_{\Lambda_q}(n).
 \tag{6.21b}
\]

Thus the single integral assignment minimizing factorial collisions is
also quota-safe with total quota defect \(o(W)\); Sections 6.1 and 6.2 do
not require two incompatible integral choices.

Summing (6.21), then using (6.5) and (6.18), proves

\[
 \boxed{
 \sum_{q\le H,\ \pm}
 \left(
   \sum_T\binom{n_{T,q}^\pm}{2}-P_q^{\min}
 \right)=o(W).}
 \tag{6.22}
\]

This is the balanced factorial collision theorem for the literal-face
graphs. It is still not a statement about consecutive windows of one
exact cycle factor.

## 7. What “literal” means here

Every edge used above is a literal physical inclusion:

* on the lower side, \(T\subset X\), and the elements of \(X\setminus T\)
  are the selected endpoints of \(q\) distinct split pairs of the assigned
  frame;
* on the upper side, \(X\subset T\), and the elements of \(T\setminus X\)
  are the unselected endpoints of \(q\) distinct split pairs.

Thus no abstract type slot is being matched to a target. The theorem
strictly strengthens pair-type marginal allocation.

It does not say that the selected \(q\)-faces occur as consecutive
windows in one common cyclic direction word. Matchings at different
depths may choose incompatible direction sets for the same owner.
Therefore “literal-face Hall” must not be shortened to “literal
contiguous-OR realization.”

## 8. Exact interface with complete \(C_{2h}\) bundling

An isometric \(C_{2h}\) in an \(h\)-cube has transition word

\[
 \pi\pi
 \tag{8.1}
\]

for a permutation \(\pi\) of its \(h\) active directions. Consequently,
every physical window of length at most \(h\) is a face with distinct
directions, but only prefix intervals of this one cyclic order occur.
The implication

\[
 \text{consecutive cycle window}\Longrightarrow
 \text{compatible face}
 \tag{8.2}
\]

is valid; the converse is false for a fixed cycle.

There are three separate bundling requirements: frame monochromaticity,
whole-cycle closure, and a common transition order at every depth.

### 8.1 Independent owner rounding destroys all-cycle supply

Every selected cycle must lie wholly in one frame class \(A^{-1}(j)\).
The independent proof assignment does not have this property. This can be
quantified while allowing every geometric cycle, rather than a
predeclared polynomial subcatalogue.

In one frame a middle owner with \(s\) split pairs lies on exactly

\[
 {(s)_h\over2}\le {m^h\over2}
 \tag{8.3}
\]

geometric isometric \(C_{2h}\)'s: choose and order the \(h\) active pair
directions, modulo reversal. Hence all \(J\) frames together contain at
most

\[
 {JWm^h\over4h}
 \tag{8.4}
\]

frame-labelled cycles. Put

\[
 p_*={1\over(1-\varepsilon)\alpha}.
\]

For a fixed frame-labelled cycle, ownerwise independence gives
probability at most \(p_*^{2h}\) that all its owners choose that frame.
If \(C_h(A)\) is the number of owners lying in at least one such
monochromatic cycle, a union bound over owner--cycle incidences gives

\[
 {\bf E}C_h(A)
 \le {JWm^h\over2}p_*^{2h}
 \le {WJ\over2}\left({4m\over J^2}\right)^h
 \tag{8.5}
\]

for all sufficiently large \(m\), since
\(\alpha=J(1-o(1))\). If \(J=m^K\) with \(K>1/2\) and \(h\to\infty\),
the right side is \(o(W)\). Markov's inequality gives

\[
                              C_h(A)=o(W)
 \tag{8.6}
\]

with probability tending to one.

This failure can coexist with the Hall conclusion. The nonnegative total
defect used in (5.3) has expectation \(o(W)\), so Markov gives bounded
probability of exceeding four times its expectation. Intersecting this
event with (8.6) shows that there is a deterministic assignment satisfying
(0.2) for which only \(o(W)\) owners lie on any monochromatic geometric
cycle. Thus assigned-owner Hall does not imply even approximate cycle
packability. Pairwise frame separation (2.14) does not affect this count.

This is not a no-go for a correlated cycle-level assignment. It proves
that \(2h\)-way positive correlation is indispensable.

### 8.2 Exact Hall-without-cycles obstruction

There is also a deterministic local separation. Fix one frame \(P\), one
source type \(f\), and put \(s=m-2f\). Assume

\[
                              2(f+1)\ge s.
 \tag{8.7}
\]

Bipartition every type-\(f\) orientation fibre by Hamming parity, and let
\(A_0,A_1\) be the two resulting owner classes. Every pair flip reverses
parity, so neither \(A_e\) contains a frame edge and neither contains a
nontrivial pair-flip cycle.

Nevertheless, for every valid \(q\),

\[
 \lambda_{f,q}
 =\prod_{i=0}^{q-1}{2(f+i+1)\over s-i}\ge1.
 \tag{8.8}
\]

The full type-\((f,q)\) face graph is biregular. For any target family
\({\cal Z}\), double-counting its incident edges gives

\[
 |N({\cal Z})|\ge\lambda_{f,q}|{\cal Z}|\ge|{\cal Z}|.
\]

Choose a target-saturating matching \(\iota_q\), and put

\[
 B_{e,q}=\{T:\iota_q(T)\in A_e\}.
 \tag{8.9}
\]

Then \(B_{0,q}\dot\cup B_{1,q}\) is the whole target orbit, and
\(\iota_q|_{B_{e,q}}\) proves every Hall cut from \(B_{e,q}\) into
\(A_e\). Do this independently for every depth and, by complementation,
for the upper targets. The same two owner classes therefore have exact
depthwise literal-face Hall while containing no cycle at all.

Interpreting the two parity shores as two resolution labels of the same
frame gives an exact no-go to post hoc Hall-to-cycle composition. It does
not obstruct a jointly selected cycle-atomic mixed-frame theorem.

### 8.3 The successor and common-order equations

Even a monochromatic cycle cover does not automatically realize the
independent matchings of Sections 5--6. At depth one the distinction is
already exact. If a compatible lower target assigned to owner \(X\) is

\[
 L_X=X\setminus\{x\},
\]

and \(\bar x\) is the mate of \(x\) in the assigned frame, then its
outgoing middle successor is forced:

\[
                         \sigma(X)=L_X\cup\{\bar x\}.
 \tag{8.10}
\]

The paired upper target and every deeper flag are then forced by

\[
 U_X=X\cup\sigma(X),
 \qquad
 L_q(X)=\bigcap_{i=0}^q\sigma^i(X),
 \qquad
 U_q(X)=\bigcup_{i=0}^q\sigma^i(X).
 \tag{8.11}
\]

Up to reversing every orbit, complete \(C_{2h}\) bundling is equivalent
to requiring that \(\sigma\) be a permutation of the retained owners,
that every orbit have length \(2h\), and that every orbit's direction word
be \(\pi\pi\) for \(h\) distinct directions. Separate lower, upper, or
depthwise Hall matchings enforce none of these equations.

### 8.4 Exact recolouring robustness

There is an exact linear robustness ledger. Suppose \(A'\) differs from a
Hall-good assignment \(A\) on \(r\) owners. At a fixed depth and sign,
deleting the old contribution of one changed owner removes total
normalized weight exactly \(\rho_q\le1\). Adding its new contribution
cannot increase a positive target deficit. Hence

\[
 D_q^\pm(A')\le D_q^\pm(A)+r.
 \tag{8.12}
\]

Summing gives

\[
 \sum_{q\le H,\ \pm}D_q^\pm(A')
 \le
 \sum_{q\le H,\ \pm}D_q^\pm(A)+2Hr.
 \tag{8.13}
\]

Therefore a complete-cycle rounding which recolours

\[
                              r=o(W/H)
 \tag{8.14}
\]

owners preserves the summable \(o(W)\) Hall conclusion. Conversely the
present ledger gives no coefficient-one guarantee from merely \(r=o(W)\),
because one recoloured owner can affect one occurrence at every depth and
on both sides.

If restricting compatible faces to actual consecutive cycle windows
deletes altogether \(E\) matched incidences over all depths and signs, the
final unmatched ledger is at most

\[
                              o(W)+2Hr+E.
 \tag{8.15}
\]

Thus one exact sufficient linear interface is

\[
 \boxed{r=o(W/H)\quad\text{and}\quad E=o(W).}
 \tag{8.16}
\]

For the factorial conclusion (6.22), one must also retain

\[
 \sum_{q,\pm}\|U_q^\pm-\Lambda_q{\bf1}\|_1=o(W),
 \qquad
 \sum_{q,\pm}\|U_q^\pm-\Lambda_q{\bf1}\|_2^2=o(W).
 \tag{8.17}
\]

A Hamming bound on recoloured owners alone does not imply the second
estimate, because their signed target changes may align.

### 8.5 A cycle-packet covariance transfer lemma

The concentration proof does tolerate the amount of dependence inside one
short cycle. Suppose a random owner assignment has the same one-owner
marginals as (3.1), and suppose the owners have a deterministic partition
into packets of size at most \(L\), with assignments independent between
distinct packets but arbitrarily correlated inside a packet. Then the
mean calculation (3.5) is unchanged. The contribution of one packet to a
fixed \(Z_{T,q}^\pm\) is at most \(Lb_q\), so

\[
 \operatorname {Var}Z_{T,q}^\pm
 \le Lb_q\,{\bf E}Z_{T,q}^\pm.
 \tag{8.18}
\]

Repeating Sections 4--6 gives

\[
 {\bf E}\sum_{q,\pm}\operatorname {def}G_q^\pm
 =O\left(W\left(H\varepsilon+\sqrt{L/m}\right)\right),
 \tag{8.19}
\]

and the summed square-error term becomes

\[
 O(WL/m)+o(W).
 \tag{8.20}
\]

Thus \(L=o(m)\), in particular fixed disjoint \(2h\)-owner packets with
\(h=o(m)\), is statistically compatible with both face-Hall and the
factorial collision conclusion. This is a conditional transfer theorem,
not a cycle construction: the available packet partition normally depends
on the chosen frame, and selecting a disjoint complete-cycle factor is
itself the unresolved correlated decision.

### 8.6 Exact cycle-bank ledgers

A complete cycle remains inside one frame and one full/empty-pair
occupancy fibre. At every certified depth it supplies exactly \(2h\)
lower and \(2h\) upper consecutive-window occurrences. Hence, if
\(n_{j,f,\ge q}\) selected cycles in frame/type bin \((j,f)\) survive
through depth \(q\), the scalar inequality

\[
 |B_{j,f,q}^\pm|\le2h\,n_{j,f,\ge q}
 \tag{8.21}
\]

is necessary. It is far from sufficient: the targets must be the actual
intersections and unions in (8.11).

Every cycle alternates orientation-fibre parity, so it uses \(h\) owners
of each parity. Its total owner count is divisible by \(2h\), and every
physical coordinate occurs on \(0,h\), or \(2h\) of its owners. Write
\(A_j=A^{-1}(j)\). If \(R_j\) is the residual left after extracting cycles
from this frame class, then

\[
 |R_j|\ge
 \sum_\gamma
 \left|
 |A_j\cap E_\gamma|-|A_j\cap O_\gamma|
 \right|,
 \tag{8.22}
\]

where \(\gamma\) ranges over its occupancy fibres. The corresponding
modulo-\(2h\) and coordinate-star modulo-\(h\) constraints are also
necessary. None is visible in pair-type marginals.

For the usual cycle-wide clipped-radius implementation, put

\[
 a_d=N_d-N_{d+1}\quad(d<H),
 \qquad a_H=N_H.
 \tag{8.23}
\]

If \(n_d\) cycles receive label \(d\), one sufficient coefficient-one
rounding ledger is

\[
 \sum_{d=0}^H(2d+1)|2hn_d-a_d|=o(W).
 \tag{8.24}
\]

The bare nearest-integer divisibility loss in (8.24) is only
\(O(hH^2)\). Enforcing the total cycle count requires at most the same
order of adjustment, so divisibility is not the asymptotic gate. This is a
sufficient cost ledger, not a characterization of feasible cycle labels.
If \(r_0\) middle owners remain outside the cycle bank, direct depth-\(H\)
repair costs \(O(Hr_0)\), requiring

\[
                              Hr_0=o(W).
 \tag{8.25}
\]

Complete blocks have \(O(W/h)\) seam locations. A compiler paying
\(O(H)\) per seam therefore requires \(h/H\to\infty\). Finally, the
target requirement is the summed, not merely pointwise, bound

\[
 \sum_{q\le H}(M_q^-+M_q^+)=o(W).
 \tag{8.26}
\]

The unresolved theorem is to select middle-disjoint, radius-compatible
complete cycles directly from the mixed-frame catalogue so that
(8.24)--(8.26) hold and their actual flags satisfy (8.11). The owner-frame
Hall theorem proved above is a projection of that statement, not a
postprocessing solution to it.

## 9. Precise proved/conditional boundary

The following are proved.

1. A polynomial, pairwise separated pair-frame catalogue has the exact
   mixed-frame weighted marginal uniformly at every target and depth in
   (0.1).
2. One common integral owner-frame assignment makes the sum of all lower
   and upper literal-face Hall deficiencies \(O(W/\sqrt m)=o(W)\).
3. The same assignment admits, at every depth and sign, an integral
   assignment of all owner occurrences whose total mobile
   floor/ceiling defect is \(o(W)\) after summing the depths.
4. The supported integral assignments can be chosen with total factorial
   pair-collision excess \(o(W)\).
5. The proof uses only ownerwise independence and is valid despite
   arbitrary correlations among targets and depths.
6. Owner recolouring has the exact stability cost \(2H\) per owner across
   the full two-sided depth ledger.
7. Independent owner rounding leaves only \(o(W)\) owners on any
   monochromatic geometric \(C_{2h}\), even when every such cycle in the
   catalogue frames is allowed, provided \(J\ge m^{1/2+\delta}\) and
   \(h\to\infty\).
8. Exact depthwise face-Hall can coexist with zero cycle supply, by the
   parity-shore construction of Section 8.2.

The following are not proved.

1. that the owner classes are unions of complete \(C_{2h}\)'s;
2. that the independently selected depthwise faces are nested or
   consecutive in one cycle order;
3. that a correlated complete-cycle selection retains the mixed-frame
   concentration;
4. that the \(o(W)\) exceptional face occurrences can be compiled into a
   literal contiguous-OR word at \(o(W)\) additional length.

Conditionally, packetwise independence with packets of size \(L=o(m)\)
does retain the Hall and factorial-collision estimates. What is missing is
an integral cycle-factor law realizing such packets with the required
one-owner marginals and common consecutive flags.

Hence the fixed-pair Gaussian Hall obstruction is genuinely removed by
mixed frames at the full literal-face and balanced-quota level. The
minimal remaining lemma is a correlated complete-cycle/nested-prefix
rounding satisfying the actual-flag equations (8.11), the moment ledger
(8.17), and the cycle-bank bounds (8.24)--(8.26), not another marginal or
ordinary Hall theorem.

## 10. Independent proof audit

The decisive inverse-source-degree argument was independently checked in
both equivalent forms:

1. the direct cut inequality (4.1)--(4.3); and
2. target capping followed by bipartite matching-polytope integrality.

Both audits recover the same mean bounds (3.5), variance bound (3.10), and
summed \(O(W/\sqrt m)\) deficiency. They also confirm that no independence
between targets, depths, or signs is used.

The convex-flow extension in Section 6 was audited separately. The audit
verified the greedy fractional extension through marginal-cost arcs, the
exact baseline \(N_q\varphi(\Lambda_q)\), the Bregman bound (6.14), and the
common quota/collision minimizer via (6.21a)--(6.21b). It also confirmed
the polynomial quantifiers and the range
\(H\le C_0\sqrt{m\log m}\).

Finally, the all-geometric-cycle count, parity obstruction, and packet
covariance calculation were checked independently. The packet result
requires a deterministic packet partition and independence between
packets; adaptive selection of the packets from their frame choices is
not covered. No audit promotes the face theorem to a complete-cycle or
contiguous-OR theorem.
