## 1. Exact simultaneous provider state

Fix a current physical state S.  For an old immediate-upper colour R, let
P_R(S) be its complete set of incumbent provider occurrences.  A socket
option a deletes old occurrences D_a and installs occurrences B_a.  For a
selected family A put

\[
  D(A)=\bigcup_{a\in A}D_a,\qquad B(A)=\bigcup_{a\in A}B_a. \tag{1.1}
\]

Let h_A(R) count only additional final provider occurrences disjoint from
both P_R(S)\D(A) and B(A).  A named retained old witness is already counted
in the first term and is not added again.  Then the literal remaining count
is

\[
 c_A(R)=|P_R(S)\setminus D(A)|
       +|\{e\in B(A):\bigcup e=R\}|+h_A(R).              \tag{1.2}
\]

Consequently the exact exported child set is

\[
                  \Gamma(A)=\{R:c_A(R)=0\}.             \tag{1.3}
\]

The unions in (1.1) are essential.  Adding singleton child counts is
unsound: two individually clean sockets can delete two different providers
of the same R.

### Theorem 1.1 (minimal provider-cover clauses)

For a colour R, call H a minimal casualty cover if

\[
 \bigcup_{a\in H}(D_a\cap P_R(S))=P_R(S)                 \tag{1.4}
\]

and no proper subfamily has this property.  Let y_R be the constrained OR
of all genuinely selected and surviving new-provider variables for R.
(A named surviving old provider instead prevents (1.4).)  Then exact
preservation of R is equivalent to the clauses

\[
             \bigvee_{a\in H}\neg x_a\ \vee\ y_R        \tag{1.5}
\]

for every minimal casualty cover H.

#### Proof

If all members of H are selected and y_R=0, (1.4) and (1.2) give c_A(R)=0.
Conversely, if c_A(R)=0, the selected deletions cover P_R(S); deleting
redundant selected options leaves a minimal cover H, and y_R=0.  Thus one
clause (1.5) fails.  \(\square\)

Individual child-cleanliness says only that (1.5) has no singleton H.  A
sound catalogue must store the sets D_a intersected with every live P_R,
or name a private survivor b_R not touched by any selected option.

### Typed demands

Residence makes the active demand more than its mask.  Write it as

\[
                       (R,\beta),                         \tag{1.6}
\]

where beta contains the clipped boundary ages, forced component/guard
state, residual provider sets, and any protected upper-history state.  A
clean socket for (R,beta_0) in the base chronology need not repair the
child state (R,beta_a) exported by a parent option a.  Section 3 gives the
literal k=17 example.

## 2. The formal child row is rooted branching

Ignore physical correlations temporarily and let Dhat be a finite set of
typed demands.  Represent a child-clean option repairing U by an arc

\[
                           star -> U,                     \tag{2.1}
\]

and an option repairing U while exporting the unique child V by

\[
                            V -> U.                       \tag{2.2}
\]

Let M_head be the partition matroid allowing at most one arc with head U,
and let M_cyc be the graphic matroid of the underlying undirected option
multigraph on Dhat union {star}.

### Theorem 2.1 (exact terminating-dependency min-max)

A one-option-per-demand terminating formal cascade exists if and only if
M_head and M_cyc have a common independent set of size |Dhat|.  Equivalently,

\[
 \max|I|=\min_{X\subseteq E}
   \{r_{\rm head}(X)+r_{\rm cyc}(E\setminus X)\}=|\widehat D|, \tag{2.3}
\]

or, in the directed formulation, every nonempty S subset Dhat has an
entering allowed arc.

#### Proof

A common independent set of the displayed size has one incoming arc at
every demand and is an undirected forest with |Dhat| edges on
|Dhat|+1 vertices.  It is therefore one tree containing star, oriented
outward by the unique-head condition.  This gives a terminating dependency
order.  Conversely, any rooted dependency arborescence has both
independences.  Formula (2.3) is the matroid-intersection min-max theorem;
the cut form is the rooted-branching theorem.  The arcs reverse causal
dependence: V -> U means that repairing U exports V, so physical processing
runs from the leaves back toward star.  \(\square\)

For the published singleton v7 rows this *formal* test passes: there are 99
root arcs, one arc 14309 -> 75749, and a root arc to 14309.  Thus the colour
dependency has depth two.  This does not solve (1.5), option consistency,
facet/guard packing, or typed residence.

## 3. Exact shared-facet residence calculus

Let

\[
 P=(A_0,A_1,\ldots,A_h=F),\qquad
 Q=(B_0,B_1,\ldots,B_g=F)                                \tag{3.1}
\]

be two individually resident facet sockets which are to be concatenated as
P followed by the reverse of Q, with the repeated F identified.  Suppose a
coordinate x in F is omitted once in P at A_p and once in Q at B_q.  Put

\[
        d_P(x)=h-p,\qquad d_Q(x)=g-q.                     \tag{3.2}
\]

### Lemma 3.1 (junction formula)

The new bounded positive run of x between these two zero owners has length

\[
                         L_x=d_P(x)+d_Q(x)-1.             \tag{3.3}
\]

All coordinate runs not omitted on both sides are inherited or extended.
Hence, for residence floor s, the concatenation is resident at the
junction if and only if every double-omitted x has

\[
                         L_x=0\quad\hbox{or}\quad L_x\ge s, \tag{3.4}
\]

in addition to the two outer guard conditions.

#### Proof

After the zero at A_p, P contributes h-p positive owners through F.  Before
the zero at B_q, reversed Q contributes g-q positive owners including F.
The shared copy of F was counted twice, giving (3.3).  If x is omitted on
only one side, the other socket adds positives to an already certified
boundary run.  Only a double omission creates two new bounding zeros.
This proves (3.4).  \(\square\)

Define the exact spacer deficit

\[
 b_x=\begin{cases}s-L_x,&1\le L_x<s,\\0,&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases} \tag{3.5}
\]

If the two bounding zero owners and their order are retained, every
resident refinement must insert at least b_x additional owners containing
x between them, or alter/remove one of those zero owners.

Two useful dimension-free consequences follow.

* Since 1 <= d_P(x) <= h and 1 <= d_Q(x) <= g, every double omission has
  1 <= L_x <= h+g-1.  If s>h+g-1, every double omission is fatal.
* For one two-socket junction, disjoint omission banks on the shared core
  are sufficient.  This pairwise condition is not hereditary for three or
  more sockets: zeros in the first and third blocks can bound a short run
  through an all-positive middle block.

The exact prospective invariant is the clipped suffix-age state beta.  After
identifying the shared endpoint, index only the nonduplicated appended owners
of the next socket by j=0,1,... .  Let a_beta(x) in {0,1,...,s} be the
positive-run age immediately before that appended word, clipped at s, and
let C_j be the permitted omission labels at appended position j.  A zero
for x at position j is forbidden precisely when

\[
            1\le a_\beta(x)+j<s.                        \tag{3.6}
\]

Put B_j(beta) equal to this position-specific forbidden set (together with
the fixed guard/protection exclusions).  The exact next-socket Hall row is

\[
 \left|\bigcup_{j\in J}(C_j\setminus B_j(\beta))\right|
       \ge |J|\quad\text{for every position set }J.      \tag{3.7}
\]

After choosing the representatives, scan the literal socket trace to update
beta.  Requiring (3.7) for every reachable beta is a genuine hereditary
greedy theorem along a dependency path.  Merely deleting the preceding
socket's omission bank is insufficient: for h=2 and s=4, three L3 blocks
can have zeros at global positions 1 and 5 and no middle zero, producing a
run of length 3 despite both adjacent omission-bank intersections being
empty.  A branching dependency must first be linearized or supplied with
distinct physical copies, because an owner path has degree at most two.

### Corollary 3.2 (the displayed v7 parent/child gluing is impossible)

The published L3 parent for 75749 and the clean round-one L3 child for
14309 share the facet owner 10213.  Their direct joined owner word is

\[
       74725,67557,10213,13285,14308.                    \tag{3.8}
\]

Its upper colours are

\[
       75749,75749,14309,14309,                           \tag{3.9}
\]

and its lower colours are the four distinct values

\[
       66533,2021,9189,13284.                             \tag{3.10}
\]

For coordinate 1024 the trace is

\[
                         0,1,1,0,1.                       \tag{3.11}
\]

Thus L_1024=2 while the depth-three residence floor is s=4.  At least two
additional owners containing 1024 are necessary if the two zero owners are
kept in this order.  The same bounded run occurs for all four combinations

## 5. Common-state selection and the exact positive face

Let a complete physical column record its parent and typed children,
component-option map, facets, guards, ports, deleted and added occurrences,
signed lower/upper interval decks, residence relation, topology column,
endpoint fibre, and compiler addresses.  For a partial family A, freeze one
common component/guard state (omega,g).  Suppose the remaining compatible
columns lie in a single aligned matroid M_(omega,g)/A; this includes all
resource and graphic restrictions being claimed on that face.

### Theorem 5.1 (common-state Rado condition)

A simultaneous one-column transversal of the remaining target lists exists
on this aligned face if and only if there is one common (omega,g) such that

\[
 r_{M_{\omega,g}/A}\left(
       \bigcup_{U\in Y}{\cal C}_{\omega,g}(U\mid A)\right)
       \ge |Y|                                            \tag{5.1}
\]

for every remaining target set Y.

#### Proof

For fixed (omega,g), (5.1) is exactly Rado's transversal theorem after
contraction by A.  Taking the disjunction over common states proves the
claim.  The quantifiers are

\[
                 \exists(\omega,g)\ \forall Y,           \tag{5.2}
\]

not a different state for every target.  \(\square\)

Outside the one-matroid aligned face, separate facet and guard Hall tests
are unsound.  For example, the two lists

\[
 \{(f_0,g_0),(f_1,g_1)\},\qquad
 \{(f_0,g_1),(f_1,g_0)\}                                 \tag{5.3}
\]

pass both marginal Hall systems but have no joint disjoint choice.  The
literal finite CSP, or fully decorated conflict columns, is then required.

A useful sufficient implementation is to select a formal dependency tree
first and assign to each tree arc a list of complete private realizations.
On a face where every non-pairwise row is automatic -- for example, all
connectors lie in one fixed ambient forest and all provider paths/compiler
cells are private -- let the remaining pairwise conflict graph have maximum
degree Delta.  If every list has size at least 2 Delta, Haxell's
independent-transversal theorem selects one realization per arc.  If each
list has average external degree at most barDelta, deleting vertices of
degree greater than 2 barDelta and starting with list size at least
8 barDelta gives the same conclusion.  A pairwise conflict graph cannot by
itself encode a graphic cycle or a higher common-basis cut, so this paragraph
does not replace (5.1) outside the automatic/private face.

## 6. Buffered cascade compression

Consider a component of the typed dependency graph which has been oriented
as a path of facet sockets.  At each shared-facet junction compute the
deficit vector (3.5).

### Theorem 6.1 (buffered cascade compression)

Suppose every junction has a resource-disjoint bridge which

1. joins the prescribed endpoint facets;
2. has O(d) owners, is internally resident, and its full clipped-age
   relation composes with both socket boundary states while dominating every
   b_x in (3.5);
3. has literal signed lower/upper cancellation for the internal
   parent--child rows and, after taking the union of all cuts, satisfies
   every provider-cover clause (1.5), exporting no undeclared typed child;
4. is palette-injective relative to both sockets;
5. is deep-upper transparent, or exports its exact accumulated-union state;
6. preserves the selected option, topology, endpoint and compiler guards.

Then concatenating the sockets and bridges and contracting the dependency
path gives one zero-child macro column.  For socket lengths h_a and
residence depth d, its footprint is

\[
                  O\left(\sum_a h_a+dJ\right),           \tag{6.1}
\]

where J is the number of junctions.

#### Proof

The complete clipped-age relation, not only the old deficits, certifies
every coordinate run introduced inside or across the bridge.  Literal
signed cancellation and the compound provider-cover clauses remove every
internal typed child.  Deep transparency or the stored automaton state
composes the longer interval row.  The common resource, topology, endpoint
and compiler guards make the concatenation one legal physical column.  Its
only exterior demands are those of the two ends, so contraction removes
every internal child.  Each socket contributes O(h_a) owners and the stated
bridge-size hypothesis contributes O(d) per junction, proving (6.1).
\(\square\)

The proved pivot-rich geodesic packet is a natural bridge candidate.  Its
published explicit residence collar, however, has the sufficient range

\[
                            k\ge r+3h.                   \tag{6.2}

For the literal k=17 owner rank r=9 and packet parameter h=3, (6.2) asks
17 >= 18.  Thus one local packet is available as a sufficiently-large
bridge candidate but does not, without a tighter endpoint-specific replay,
certify the finite junction (3.8).  Moreover, its two far boundary flags
remain clipped and must still be discharged by the ambient host.  A bank of
many protected copies additionally needs the published planting inequality
and resource-disjointness; local existence is not the availability theorem
required below.

There is also an exact extraction saving when sockets are amalgamated.  For
any selected owner set X and retained old incident-edge set K,

\[
 |E_F(X,V(F))\setminus K|
   =\sum_{x\in X}\deg_F(x)-|E_F(X)|-|K|.                 \tag{6.3}
\]

The internal-edge term is essential once adjacent sockets share a facet or
retain a child-provider edge.  For a chain of T equal h-sockets sharing
T-1 endpoint facets in a two-regular host, suppose the only old internal
X-edges are those T-1 retained junctions and K consists of those edges plus
the two outer guards.  Then |X|=Th+1 and

\[
                         q=2(h-1)T+2.                    \tag{6.4}
\]

Thus compression saves physical cuts and guards, but the next section shows
that it does not by itself erase the repeated-target tax.

## 7. Hereditary regenerative theorem

A regenerative state must contain at least:

* the common component options and consumed facet/guard resources;
* typed demands (R,beta), residual provider sets, and named survivors;
* clipped residence relations at every live port;
* the accumulated-union automaton state for protected deep targets;
* the exact lower-q1 multiset and one common compiler basis;
* the contracted topology and the two-stratum endpoint fibre.

Let Tcal be the processed target set.  Write m_Tcal for the number of
genuinely new edges in F' minus F whose upper colour lies in Tcal, and
tau_Tcal for the number of previously present colours in Tcal whose complete
old provider set is actually deleted.  The resulting scalar casualty lower
bound outside Tcal is

\[
 |\Gamma_{\rm out}(A)|\ge
 \left(m_{\mathcal T}-\mu-\rho-\tau_{\mathcal T}-\kappa\right)_+.
                                                               \tag{7.1}
\]

Here mu is the component-merger credit, rho the repeated-provider credit,
and kappa the genuinely auxiliary provider credit.  If T=|Tcal| and H_0 of
those targets were initially missing, then tau_Tcal <= T-H_0, so (7.1)
implies the weaker but sometimes convenient row

\[
 |\Gamma_{\rm out}(A)|\ge
 \left(m_{\mathcal T}-T+H_0-\mu-\rho-\kappa\right)_+.    \tag{7.2}
\]

If every h_a socket edge is genuinely new, then
m_Tcal=sum_a h_a and (7.2) recovers the independent-socket ledger.  In
general let s_Tcal be the number of target-coloured socket edges actually
reused from F.  Then

\[
                  m_{\mathcal T}=\sum_a h_a-s_{\mathcal T}. \tag{7.3}
\]

For an equal-h chain, the specialization
m_Tcal=(h-1)T+H_0 follows only when exactly T-H_0 child-provider edges are
reused and there are no other target-coloured new edges.  Typically
tau_Tcal=0 for those retained child colours, so the lower bound remains
linear.  Every version of this inequality must hold for every reachable
prefix, not just the final bank.

To prove (7.1), let q=|D|.  The deleted edges kill exactly q-rho old
colours.  Exactly tau_Tcal of those killed colours lie in Tcal, so
q-rho-tau_Tcal lie outside it.  Since |B|=q+mu and m_Tcal genuinely new
edges are target-coloured, at most q+mu-m_Tcal new edges and kappa auxiliary
slots can restore outside colours.  Subtracting proves (7.1); using
tau_Tcal<=T-H_0 proves (7.2).

Let the provider potential be

\[
             \Phi(S)=\sum_{q\in\mathcal D(S)}w(q),
             \qquad w(q)\ge w_{\min}>0,                  \tag{7.4}
\]

where D(S) is the current finite typed-demand multiset.  The positive weight
floor makes the potential well-founded at zero.

### Theorem 7.1 (conditional hereditary regeneration)

Suppose every reachable state satisfies all of the following.

1. The minimal provider-cover clauses (1.5) and the common-state rank cuts
   (5.1) hold for the remaining typed lists.
2. Every selected dependency path has a buffered compression from Theorem
   6.1, and branching nodes are physically linearized by private copies or
   a larger multi-target macro.
3. The provider potential contracts as

\[
                         \Phi_{t+1}\le\theta\Phi_t+\beta,
                         \qquad 0\le\theta<1,             \tag{7.5}
\]

   and the physical footprint of round t is at most
   C(h_max+d)Phi_t.

4. For beta>0 there is a literal terminal atlas for every state with
   Phi<=Phi_star, where Phi_star>=2beta/(1-theta), of maximum footprint
   C_term(Phi_star).  For beta=0 the zero-demand state is terminal.
5. The lower, deep-upper, topology and common-cap rows reproduce the same
   state class.
6. The endpoint vector obeys the exact two-stratum fibre condition

\[
 d_{\mathcal H_0}+\sum_{a\in A}(h_a-1){\bf1}_{U(a)}+\gamma(A)
      \in {\cal D}_C(A_L\setminus Z_L),                  \tag{7.6}
\]

   where Hcal_0 is the incumbent lower-hole family and U(a) is the target of
   column a.

Then for beta=0 the iteration terminates and its total footprint is

\[
       O\left((h_{\max}+d){\Phi_0\over1-\theta}\right).   \tag{7.7}
\]

For beta>0 let L be the first integer with

\[
 \theta^L\Phi_0\le {\beta\over1-\theta}.                 \tag{7.8}
\]

Then the iteration reaches the terminal region Phi<=Phi_star by round L,
and its total footprint is at most

\[
 O\left((h_{\max}+d){\Phi_0+L\beta\over1-\theta}
          +C_{\rm term}(\Phi_*)\right).                  \tag{7.9}
\]

Thus the result is O(1) when all displayed state parameters are bounded,
and is o(W) whenever the complete right side of (7.7) or (7.9) is o(W).

#### Proof

Iteration of (7.5) gives

\[
 \Phi_t\le\theta^t\Phi_0+{\beta(1-\theta^t)\over1-\theta}. \tag{7.10}
\]

When beta=0, the weight floor in (7.4) forces Phi_t=0 once
theta^t Phi_0<w_min; summing the geometric per-round cost proves (7.7).
When beta>0, (7.8), (7.10), and the bound on Phi_star reach the terminal
atlas, while summing (7.10) through L proves (7.9).  At each step (1.5)
preserves all unactivated providers, (5.1) selects one common compatible
physical family, and Theorem 6.1 removes its internal typed children.
Conditions 5 and 6 reproduce every nonlocal state row, so induction applies
to the next state.  \(\square\)

The theorem is deliberately conditional.  For k=17 the open finite object
is a prospective re-selection or enlarged multi-target catalogue satisfying
these rows.  For all k, the weakest missing availability statement is:
every reachable typed junction-deficit signature has enough private
pivot-rich (or equivalent) bridge realizations that the contracted common
rank cuts survive.
