# Exact decoding, opening, and literal splicing of an even equivariant factor

## 1. Scope and result

Let

\[
 K=2r,\qquad n=K-1,\qquad W={K\choose r},\qquad N=W/n,
\]

and let `rho` rotate the `n` old coordinates and fix the top coordinate.
Start with the exact slot--voltage datum

\[
 (U_i,\sigma,p_i)_{i\in[N]},\qquad
 U_i\sim\rho^{p_i}U_{\sigma(i)},                         \tag{1.1}
\]

of Theorem 2.1 in
`MATH_THEOREM_AD_EVEN_MULTICOMPONENT_CT_WAKSMAN_NORMAL_FORM_20260729.md`.
Thus the datum represents an arbitrary rotation-equivariant spanning simple
degree-two factor, not merely a unit-voltage or connected subclass.

This note proves four exact statements.

1. Every physical cycle, including zero- and nonunit-voltage lifts, has an
   explicit quotient-coordinate decoding.
2. All choices of independent physical-cycle orientations, one cut per
   physical cycle, component order, and Johnson seams admit a polynomial
   exact encoding.  The resulting seam catalogue has one variable only for
   a directed Johnson adjacency, not for every ordered pair of states.
3. For components at least as long as the audited window width, the effect
   of opening and joining is the exact old-minus-cut-plus-seam identity.
   For arbitrary short components, a direct partial successor gives the
   exact replacement and avoids an unsound locality assumption.
4. The decoded path composes with the exact one-core Hall theorem to give a
   literal contiguous-OR word.  This is an exact equivalence inside the
   declared one-core architecture and only a sufficient theorem for
   unrestricted antecedents.

No claim is made that cyclic coverage, component count, endpoint adjacency,
or a bounded-width shadow catalogue alone implies compiler feasibility.

## 2. Physical-cycle decoder

Put

\[
 \Omega=[N]\times\mathbb Z_n,\qquad
 V(i,a)=\rho^aU_i,
\]

and define the lifted successor

\[
 F(i,a)=(\sigma(i),a+p_i).                               \tag{2.1}
\]

Freeness of the middle-owner action makes `V` a bijection from `Omega` to
the `W` middle sets.  The cycles of `F` are exactly the oriented physical
cycles of the factor.

### Theorem 2.1 (explicit voltage-lift decoding)

Let

\[
 C=(i_0,i_1,\ldots,i_{\ell-1}),\qquad i_{j+1}=\sigma(i_j),
\]

be a quotient cycle, put

\[
 P_0=0,\qquad P_j=\sum_{t=0}^{j-1}p_{i_t},\qquad
 v=P_\ell\pmod n,
\]

and write

\[
 g=\gcd(n,v),\qquad m=n/g.                               \tag{2.2}
\]

For each coset `h` of the subgroup generated by `v` in `Z_n`, one physical
cycle is

\[
 \Gamma_{C,h}=\bigl(
   (i_j,h+qv+P_j):
   q=0,\ldots,m-1,\ j=0,\ldots,\ell-1
                    \bigr),                              \tag{2.3}
\]

in the displayed lexicographic order.  These `g` cycles are disjoint, have
length

\[
                         L_C=m\ell={n\ell\over g},        \tag{2.4}
\]

and exhaust the lift over `C`.  In particular `v=0` gives `n` cycles of
length `ell`.

#### Proof

Within a fixed `q`, equation (2.1) successively changes

\[
 (i_j,h+qv+P_j)\longmapsto(i_{j+1},h+qv+P_{j+1}).
\]

The last state in that quotient lap maps to

\[
 (i_0,h+qv+P_\ell)=(i_0,h+(q+1)v).
\]

Translation by `v` has `g` phase orbits, each of length `m`.  This proves
the cycle formula, length, disjointness, and exhaustion.  QED.

Changing section representatives changes the phase address in (2.3) but
not the physical middle sets, physical cycles, or their lengths.  Thus the
decoder is gauge-covariant.  It does not normalize any component voltage.

## 3. Complete opening and seam normal form

Although `F` is an equivariant orientation, a literal splice may reverse
different physical cycles independently.  Let `epsilon_Gamma in {+1,-1}`
for every physical cycle and define on that cycle

\[
 H=F^{\epsilon_\Gamma}.                                  \tag{3.1}
\]

Choose one directed `H`-edge `e_Gamma -> H(e_Gamma)` to cut.  The opened
path is

\[
 Q_\Gamma=(H(e_\Gamma),H^2(e_\Gamma),\ldots,e_\Gamma).  \tag{3.2}
\]

It contains every state of `Gamma` exactly once.  A choice of order
`Gamma_1,...,Gamma_c`, where

\[
 c=\sum_C\gcd(n,v_C),                                    \tag{3.3}
\]

is literally joinable when

\[
 V(e_{\Gamma_j})\sim V(H(e_{\Gamma_{j+1}}))
       \qquad(1\le j<c).                                 \tag{3.4}
\]

Call these `c-1` adjacencies the seams.

### Theorem 3.1 (decode/open/splice equivalence)

A linear Johnson ordering of all `W` middle sets obtained by opening the
given undirected factor once in every physical component is equivalent to:

* one orientation sign per physical cycle;
* one cut edge per physical cycle;
* one directed Hamilton path through the physical components whose arcs
  satisfy (3.4).

The resulting middle word is exactly

\[
 T=V(Q_{\Gamma_1})V(Q_{\Gamma_2})\cdots V(Q_{\Gamma_c}). \tag{3.5}
\]

#### Proof

Removing one edge from a simple cycle produces a path and its two possible
orders are exactly (3.1)--(3.2).  Since the physical cycles partition
`Omega`, concatenation contains every middle set exactly once.  It is a
Johnson path exactly when each new consecutive cross-component pair is a
Johnson adjacency, which is (3.4).  Conversely, any one-cut opening has a
unique orientation, cut tail, and component order after choosing the first
state of its global path.  QED.

This theorem allows independent reversal of physical lifts lying over the
same quotient cycle.  Restricting all such lifts to the common equivariant
orientation is a sufficient subclass, not WLOG for the splice problem.

### Proposition 3.2 (polynomial exact SAT annotation)

There is an exact formulation with no component-by-voltage selector.
First relabel the artificial quotient slots by their actual owner-orbit IDs,
and then fix one canonical representative of every orbit by a section gauge.
The first operation conjugates `sigma`; the second changes its dart phases.
Together they leave the physical factor unchanged.  A section gauge alone
does not permute owner slots.  Now index the `W` physical states by fixed
addresses `x=(i,a)`; in this canonical section the literal middle set `V(x)`
is fixed as well.  This qualification is essential for the `Wr^2` fixed seam
census below.  If owner IDs or representatives remain variable in a joint
model, channel the owner ID and phase, together with every cut/root flag, to
these canonical literal-state addresses first.

1. A bit `epsilon_x`, with `epsilon_(F x)=epsilon_x`, selects the physical
   cycle orientation, and a two-way mux defines `H x` as `F x` or
   `F^(-1)x`.
2. Give every state a root address `b_x` and an ordinary bounded-integer
   order `h_x in {0,...,W-1}`.  Impose

   \[
   b_{Hx}=b_x,\qquad R_x\Longleftrightarrow b_x=\operatorname{id}(x),
                                                                    \tag{3.6}
   \]

   \[
   R_x\Rightarrow h_x=0,\quad
   \neg R_x\Rightarrow h_x>0,\quad
   \neg R_{Hx}\Rightarrow h_{Hx}=h_x+1.                  \tag{3.7}
   \]

   Then every `H`-cycle has exactly one root.  Set

   \[
               X_x\Longleftrightarrow R_{Hx};             \tag{3.8}
   \]

   `X_x` is exactly the selected cut-tail bit.
3. For every directed Johnson adjacency `(x,y)` introduce a seam bit
   `Y_(x,y)`, guarded by `X_x` and `R_y`.  Give every state a component
   potential `o_x in {0,...,W-1}`, constant on its `H`-cycle, and require

   \[
   Y_{(x,y)}\Rightarrow o_y>o_x.                           \tag{3.9}
   \]

   Require at most one outgoing seam at every cut tail, at most one incoming
   seam at every root, and

   \[
             \sum_{(x,y)}Y_{(x,y)}=\sum_xX_x-1.           \tag{3.10}
   \]

The equalities and inequalities involving `h_x,o_x` are ordinary integer
relations on the displayed bounded domains, not modular bit-vector
arithmetic; wraparound would invalidate the rootless-cycle and acyclicity
arguments.

These rows are feasible exactly for the opening/splice data of Theorem 3.1.
They use `O(W log W)` stored root/order/potential bits.  Since `J(K,r)` has
degree `r(K-r)=r^2`, the complete directed seam catalogue has at most

\[
                         Wr^2=WK^2/4                     \tag{3.11}
\]

seam variables, rather than `W^2` endpoint-pair variables.  Routing the
fields through a variable `F` is polynomial shared-control circuitry; this
proposition is an encoding-size theorem, not an exact CNF census.

#### Proof

On an `H`-cycle, (3.6) permits at most one root because `b` is constant and
state addresses are distinct.  If there were no root, (3.7) would force a
strict increase all the way around the cycle, impossible for bounded
integers.  Hence there is exactly one root and (3.8) cuts precisely its
incoming edge.

Contract each opened physical cycle to one vertex.  The seam rows give a
directed graph with indegree and outdegree at most one.  The strict
potential (3.9) excludes a directed cycle.  Such a graph is a disjoint
union of directed paths.  If it has `c` vertices, (3.10) gives `c-1` arcs,
so it is one path.  Conversely, number the components along any splice to
satisfy (3.9).  The seam census (3.11) is the directed degree sum of the
Johnson graph.  QED.

The complete seam catalogue is WLOG for literal one-cut splicing.  No claim
of inclusion-minimality is made: its point is the exact `Wr^2`, rather than
`W^2`, fixed upper bound.  A
quotient-equivariant or bounded-support seam subcatalogue is only
sufficient unless reverse/endpoint closure is separately proved.

### Theorem 3.3 (selector-free component-record Waksman splice)

The directed seam variables of Proposition 3.2 are optional.  After the
orientation and root rows (3.6)--(3.8), attach to every physical state `x`
the record

\[
 \mathsf R_x=
 \bigl(R_x,\operatorname{id}(x),\operatorname{id}(H^{-1}x),
             V(x),V(H^{-1}x)\bigr).                       \tag{3.12}
\]

Only a record with `R_x=1` is active.  Its two set fields are exactly the
head and tail of the path obtained by cutting the edge
`H^(-1)x -> x`.

Route all `W` records through one arbitrary-permutation Waksman network,
using the same switch controls for every field.  Write the output records
as

\[
 (a_j,h_j,t_j,A_j,B_j),\qquad 0\le j<W,                  \tag{3.13}
\]

where `a_j` is the active bit and `A_j,B_j` are the head and tail sets.
Impose

\[
                 a_j\ge a_{j+1}\qquad(0\le j<W-1),       \tag{3.14}
\]

and

\[
                 a_{j+1}=1\Longrightarrow B_j\sim A_{j+1}.
                                                                    \tag{3.15}
\]

Then (3.12)--(3.15) are feasible if and only if the chosen independently
oriented and cut physical components admit a literal splice.  Every
component order occurs.  In particular arbitrary quotient successor
permutations and arbitrary component voltages require no
component-order-by-voltage or seam-by-component selectors.

Let `S(W)` be the number of switches in the chosen Waksman construction and
put `b_W=ceil(log_2 W)`.  Routing the active bit, two addresses, and two
`K`-bit endpoint sets uses record width

\[
                         q_R=1+2b_W+2K.                  \tag{3.16}
\]

With the standard two-output mux accounting, the ordering layer has at
most

\[
       S(W)\text{ controls},\qquad 2q_RS(W)\text{ mux variables},     \tag{3.17}
\]

plus `W-1` prefix implications and `O(KW)` guarded Johnson comparators.
Since `S(W)=O(W log W)`, its size is

\[
                       O((K+\log W)W\log W).              \tag{3.18}
\]

This census is for the component-order layer.  Forming
`H^(-1)x` uses one backward pass through the already declared
successor/phase controls (and the orientation mux); it is polynomial
shared-control decoding and is not an endpoint selector table.

This is the preferred topology-oblivious, selector-free complete splice
catalogue.  It is not uniformly smaller in raw Boolean variables: at
`K=16` the explicit `Wr^2` directed seam catalogue may have the better
constant.  That sparse alternative also remains useful when individual seam
identities or linear seam-budget cuts are desired.

The width-`N` Waksman in the factor normal form and this width-`W` network
serve different purposes.  The first chooses the equivariant quotient
successor `sigma`; the second orders independently oriented and cut
**physical** lift components.  Their controls cannot in general be
identified, because the latter choices need not be `rho`-equivariant.

#### Proof

The routing network applies one permutation to whole records, so a head can
never be detached from its own cut tail.  Equation (3.14) moves all and only
the root records to an active prefix.  The active prefix therefore lists
every physical component exactly once in an arbitrary order.  Equation
(3.15) is precisely the Johnson seam test between each consecutive pair,
so Theorem 3.1 gives a splice.

Conversely, given any splice, route its component-root records to the active
outputs in their splice order and route inactive records afterward.
Waksman universality realizes that permutation, (3.14) holds, and its actual
seams satisfy (3.15).  The size bound is the bit width of a shared-control
record times the ordinary switch count.  QED.

The seam map can also be decoded without an arc selector table.  At an
active output `j` which is not last active, attach the next output's root
address and head word

\[
             Z_j=(h_{j+1},A_{j+1}),                      \tag{3.19}
\]

At the unique last-active output, characterized by `a_j=1` and either
`j=W-1` or `a_(j+1)=0`, attach `bottom`; values of `Z_j` on inactive outputs
are ignored.  Route `Z_j` backward through
the same Waksman switch settings.  The original root record `x` then receives
the root address and head word of its successor component.  One predecessor
pass through `H` transfers this field from `x` to its cut tail `H^(-1)x`.
Together with `H` on uncut states, this is precisely the partial global
successor (4.7).  The backward field has width `b_W+K` and costs at most
`2(b_W+K)S(W)` further mux variables, plus the shared predecessor pass.
Thus decoding the selected seams does not reintroduce `Wr^2` or `W^2`
selectors.

The physical-state width `W` in this direct formulation is an upper bound,
not an assertion of minimal circuit size.  One may first compact the at
most `W/3` roots of a simple factor and use a smaller second ordering
network, but an exact smaller universal construction is not proved here.

## 4. Exact cut and seam ledger

For `1<=w<=W`, a physical cycle `Gamma` of length at least `w`, and a start
`x in Gamma`, define the cyclic labels

\[
 I_w(x)=\bigcap_{j=0}^{w-1}V(H^jx),\qquad
 U_w(x)=\bigcup_{j=0}^{w-1}V(H^jx).                       \tag{4.1}
\]

Reversal permutes the collection of cyclic intervals, so their label
multisets do not depend on the chosen sign.  Let

\[
 \mu_w^\pm(S)=\#\{x:I_w(x)=S\}\quad\hbox{or}\quad
               \#\{x:U_w(x)=S\},                         \tag{4.2}
\]

where only cycles of length at least `w` contribute.

For a selected cut tail `e` in its cycle, let

\[
 L_{e,w}^\pm(S)=
 \#\{x:\lambda_w^\pm(x)=S,
       e\in\{x,Hx,\ldots,H^{w-2}x\}\},                   \tag{4.3}
\]

with `lambda^- = I` and `lambda^+ = U`.  This is the exact multiset of old
width-`w` witnesses destroyed by that cut.

For a seam `a`, let `G_(a,w)^\pm(S)` be the number of width-`w` intervals of
the assembled word (3.5) which cross `a`, cross no other seam, and have
label `S`.

### Theorem 4.1 (old minus cuts plus seams)

Fix `w` and suppose every physical component has length at least `w`.  If
`m_(T,w)^\pm(S)` is the literal multiplicity of `S` among the width-`w`
intersections or unions of the spliced linear word `T`, then

\[
 \boxed{
 m_{T,w}^\pm(S)=\mu_w^\pm(S)
       -\sum_{e\in\mathcal X}L_{e,w}^\pm(S)
       +\sum_{a\in\mathcal Y}G_{a,w}^\pm(S).}
                                                                    \tag{4.4}
\]

Moreover every cut destroys exactly `w-1` width-`w` occurrences, every
seam creates exactly `w-1`, and therefore

\[
 \sum_Sm_{T,w}^\pm(S)
 =W-c(w-1)+(c-1)(w-1)=W-w+1.                              \tag{4.5}
\]

#### Proof

For `w=1`, the cut-loss and seam-gain families are empty and the identity is
immediate.  Assume `w>=2` below.

A width-`w` cyclic interval survives in its opened path if and only if none
of its `w-1` transition edges is the selected cut.  Because there is one
cut in that physical cycle, (4.3) enumerates the lost occurrences exactly.

Every interval of the assembled word is either contained in one opened
component or crosses a seam.  A width-`w` interval cannot cross two seams:
that would contain the whole intervening component and at least one state
on each side, requiring at least `w+2` states when that component has length
at least `w`.  Thus the seam-gain classes are disjoint and exhaustive.
There are `w-1` cyclic starts whose `w-1` transition edges contain a fixed
cut.  At a seam there is one interval for each split into `a` states on the
left and `w-a` on the right, `1<=a<w`; the length assumption makes all of
them available.  This proves (4.4)--(4.5).  QED.

The formula is literal, not merely rankwise.  For a target orbit `O`, summing
(4.4) over `S in O` gives the exact orbit ledger.  If the old cyclic factor
covers a required literal family `R_w^\pm`, the post-splice hole set is

\[
 \mathcal H_w^\pm=
 \{S\in\mathcal R_w^\pm:
   \mu_w^\pm(S)-L_w^\pm(S)+G_w^\pm(S)=0\}.                \tag{4.6}
\]

Multiplicity matters: deleting one witness is not a hole when another old
or new witness remains.  An orbit-only loss count is insufficient for a
literal compiler unless it is refined to its individual rotated targets.

For depth `q`, take `w=q+1`.  If the assembled path `T` is strongly
`q`-resident (every non-endpoint-truncated positive run has at least `q+1`
states), then its width-`q+1` intersection labels have rank exactly `r-q`.
Upper labels must still be tested literally; positive residence alone does
not assert rank `r+q` or upper completeness.

### Proposition 4.2 (short-component correction)

Without the length hypothesis in Theorem 4.1, the single-seam gain sum can
double count a window crossing two or more seams, and periodically repeating
a short cycle is not a literal interval.  The exact replacement is the
partial global successor

\[
 Gx=
 \begin{cases}
 Hx,&X_x=0,\\
 y,&Y_{(x,y)}=1,\\
 \bot,&X_x=1\text{ and }x\text{ is the final tail}.
 \end{cases}                                               \tag{4.7}
\]

The rows of Proposition 3.2, or equivalently the active-record ordering of
Theorem 3.3 with (3.19), make `G` one directed Hamilton path.  For every
fixed `1<=w<=W`, the exact active starts are

\[
 A_{x,w}=1\Longleftrightarrow G^j x\ne\bot
                  \quad(0\le j<w),                        \tag{4.8}
\]

and the exact labels are the intersection or union of
`V(x),V(Gx),...,V(G^(w-1)x)`.  There are exactly `W-w+1` active starts.
Let `E_J^rightarrow` be the directed physical Johnson-adjacency set; it has
exactly `Wr^2` members.  Successive guarded payload passes through `G`
encode all widths through a fixed `Hmax` in the conservative bound

\[
 O\bigl(H_{\max}K(W+|\mathcal E_J^\rightarrow|)\bigr)
   =O(H_{\max}WK^3)                                       \tag{4.9}
\]

elementary guarded bit relations before the target-cover networks.  A
shared word-routing implementation can improve this conservative upper
bound, but no smaller exact circuit census is claimed here.
This is complete for the declared finite widths and does not rely on a
minimum component length.  It does not make a fixed finite width set WLOG
for unlisted widths.

## 5. Literal compiler composition

Fix `1<=d<r`.  Let `T=(T_0,...,T_(W-1))` be the path decoded by Theorem 3.1
and suppose it is strongly `d`-resident.  Define its maximal erosion

\[
 P_p=\bigcap_{i=\max(0,p-d)}^{\min(W-1,p)}T_i,
       \qquad 0\le p<W+d.                                 \tag{5.1}
\]

Then `D^dP=T`.  Put

\[
 \mathcal K^+(P)=\bigcup_{j=1}^{d-1}\operatorname{supp}(D^jP),
 \qquad
 \mathcal F(P)=\{S:1\le|S|<r,\ S\notin\mathcal K^+(P)\}. \tag{5.2}
\]

A core is a word `C=(C_p)` satisfying

\[
 C_p\subseteq P_p,qquad
 C_p\cup C_{p+1}=P_p\cup P_{p+1}.                         \tag{5.3}
\]

For `S in F(P)`, define

\[
 N_C(S)=\{p:C_p\subseteq S\subseteq P_p\}.               \tag{5.4}
\]

### Theorem 5.1 (exact one-core decode/splice/compiler theorem)

The decoded opening has a nonempty literal source word
`A=(A_0,...,A_(W+d-1))` satisfying

\[
                         DA=DP,qquad D^dA=T,              \tag{5.5}
\]

and covering every nonempty proper subset of `[K]` if and only if both of
the following hold.

1. Every target of rank greater than `r` is the union of a literal interval
   of `T`.  This may be checked exactly by applying (4.4) at every relevant
   width for which its length hypothesis applies, and using the global
   successor (4.7)--(4.8) for all remaining widths.  No bounded list of
   widths is silently promoted to all-width completeness.
2. There is a core `C` satisfying (5.3) and

   \[
   \left|\bigcup_{S\in X}N_C(S)\right|\ge|X|
             \qquad\text{for every }X\subseteq\mathcal F(P).          \tag{5.6}
   \]

For prescribed distinct pins `S@p`, additionally require
`C_p subseteq S subseteq P_p`, delete every pinned position, delete every
target already supplied by a pin, and apply (5.6) to the residual instance.
This contracted Hall condition is necessary and sufficient for the fixed
core and pins.

#### Proof

Hall's theorem gives a matching of every member of `F(P)` to a distinct
eligible literal position.  Put that target at its matched position and put
`P_p` at every unmatched position.  The sandwich

\[
                         C\subseteq A\subseteq P
\]

and (5.3) force `DA=DP`; hence `D^dA=T`.  The derivative rows below `T`
cover every lower target outside `F(P)`, and the matching supplies all of
`F(P)`.  The middle row is `T`.  If an upper target is the union of
`T_i,...,T_j`, then it is the union of `A_i,...,A_(j+d)`, so item 1 supplies
all upper targets.  Nonemptiness follows from `d<r`, the erosion rank
profile, and nonempty matched targets.

Conversely, within `DA=DP`, any target in `F(P)` cannot be represented by a
positive interval of length at least two and therefore needs a distinct
literal occurrence.  Taking `C=A` gives (5.3), and those occurrences give
the matching whose Hall inequalities are (5.6).  Upper-spectrum equality
forces item 1 by the following exact interval identity.  An `A`-interval
`[a,b]` of rank greater than `r` must have `b-a+1>=d+1`, since every shorter
cell lies in an erosion row of rank at most `r`.  For such an interval,

\[
 \bigcup_{p=a}^{b}A_p
 =(D^{b-a}A)_a
 =(D^{b-a}P)_a
 =(D^{b-a-d}T)_a
 =\bigcup_{i=a}^{b-d}T_i.                               \tag{5.7}
\]

Here the second equality uses `DA=DP`, and the third uses `D^dP=T`.
Thus every upper occurrence in `A` comes from a literal `T` interval, while
the forward identity used above shows every such `T` interval lifts back to
`A`.  The pin statement is ordinary contracted Hall.  QED.

Theorem 5.1 is WLOG only inside the one-core architecture `DA=DP`.  A
positive certificate is a globally valid literal word.  Failure of its
Hall system is not a lower bound against arbitrary `A` satisfying only
`D^dA=T`; that broader class requires the full interval-witness compiler.

Cut losses do not automatically become endpoint pins.  They are first
computed by the literal ledger (4.4) or (4.7); the erosion/core system then
determines whether a missing lower target is forced to a boundary position.
For a squarefree lower-`q=1` deck, the known two-endpoint theorem is a
special additional deduction, not part of the generic ledger.

## 6. Exact completeness and sufficiency boundary

The following are exact, without a unit-voltage or connectedness
restriction:

* physical-cycle decoding (2.3);
* independent reversal and one cut per physical cycle;
* the full directed Johnson seam catalogue (3.11);
* the global Hamilton-successor audit (4.7)--(4.8);
* the additive ledger (4.4) under its explicit length hypothesis;
* Theorem 5.1 inside `DA=DP`.

The following are only sufficient unless separately proved complete:

* common orientation for all lifts of one quotient component;
* quotient-equivariant, bounded-support, or recycled-colour seams;
* any predetermined ordering of physical components;
* any finite width catalogue as a surrogate for unlisted upper intervals;
* cyclic all-depth coverage as a surrogate for the post-cut literal ledger;
* component count or boundary cardinality as a surrogate for Hall;
* the one-core compiler as a surrogate for the unrestricted compiler.

Thus arbitrary successor permutations and voltages create no decoding or
component-selector obstruction.  The remaining exact splice burden is
endpoint/seam choice plus literal post-cut chronology and the common
compiler.  Those layers are polynomially representable, but they are not
consequences of the cyclic Waksman cover certificate alone.
