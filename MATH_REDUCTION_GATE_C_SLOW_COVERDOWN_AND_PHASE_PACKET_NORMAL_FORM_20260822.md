# Gate C: arbitrarily slow cover-down and the exact phase-packet normal form

**Status (2026-08-22).** Every assertion labelled theorem or lemma below is
proved.  The note does not close Gate C.  It weakens two quantitative
demands in the smallest presently known sufficient antecedent.

First, after replacing quota overflow by literal band holes, the residual
middle density need only be `o(1)`.  It need not be
`o(b^(-1/3))`.  More quantitatively, for any

\[
 a_b\longrightarrow\infty,\qquad a_b=o(\log b),                 \tag{0.1}
\]

one may stop at density `exp(-a_b)`, use a fixed fragment length

\[
 L_b=\left\lfloor {c b\log b\over a_b}\right\rfloor,
 \qquad 0<c<2,                                                  \tag{0.2}
\]

and require only `O(a_b)` fixed-fraction recursive matching rounds.  The
total literal block-seam cost is then `O(a_b W/log b)=o(W)`, while the
annealed labelled fragment support remains
`exp((2-c+o(1))b log b)` even at the terminal density.  For example,
`a_b=log log log b` leaves `W/log log b` middle vertices and asks for only
`O(log log log b)` rounds.

Second, one period of a phase fragment has an exact punctured-chain normal
form.  Every aligned `b`-transition packet is obtained from an ordered
partition of `Omega-{u,v}` into two `(b-1)`-tuples, and every such ordered
packet embeds in a phase-refined atom.  This gives a concrete prefix unit for
future extension or switching arguments; no probabilistic or asymptotic
claim is hidden in that description.

The balanced-junta obstruction survives the weaker stopping scale.  Thus the
result is a genuine reduction, not a positive matching theorem: density,
complement symmetry, and point balance still cannot supply the required
recursive fragments.

Here \(\nu(n)\) denotes the minimum length of a word in nonempty subsets of
an \(n\)-element coordinate set such that every nonempty subset is the
union of a contiguous subword.

## 1. The direct-hole compiler

Let

\[
 b=2h+1,\qquad |\Omega|=2b,\qquad
 {\cal V}={\Omega\choose b},\qquad W=|{\cal V}|.                \tag{1.1}
\]

Put

\[
 H=\left\lceil\sqrt{2b\log(2b)}\right\rceil,
 \qquad g=b+H.                                                  \tag{1.2}
\]

A phase-refined atom is the cyclic singleton stream obtained by splitting
`Omega=A dotcup B`, choosing a directed cyclic order on each shore, and
repeating the type period

\[
                  B,A,B,A,\ldots,B,A,B                         \tag{1.3}
\]

of length `b`.  At an event of one type the next coordinate on that shore is
emitted.  One type period advances the two stream roots by
\((h,h+1)\in\mathbb Z_b^2\).  Retain the initial root pair modulo the
cyclic subgroup generated by this vector; there are exactly \(b\) phase
cosets.  The resulting word has period \(b^2\).  Its middle windows are

\[
                  C_t=\{w_t,\ldots,w_{t+b-1}\}.                 \tag{1.4}
\]

These \(b^2\) middle windows are distinct.  At time \(t=qb+s\), their
shore starts have the form

\[
 (i_A(s),i_B(s))+q(h,h+1)\pmod b.
\]

For fixed \(s\), varying \(q\) traverses one full diagonal coset because
\((h,b)=1\).  Increasing \(s\) advances exactly one stream start, so the
sum of the two start coordinates increases by one; the \(b\) values of
\(s\) traverse all \(b\) cosets.  Hence all of \(\mathbb Z_b^2\) occurs.
The two shore intersections of a middle window recover its two proper
cyclic intervals and hence their starts, proving distinctness.

Every block of at most `2b-2` emissions is injective.  Indeed a coordinate
recurs only after `b` events on its shore; the same-type gaps in (1.3) imply
that the recurrence distance is at least `2b-2`.

A core fragment of length `L` and start `a` consists of
`C_a,...,C_(a+L-1)`.  At rank `s<=g` it supplies the `L` designated tokens

\[
 T_s(a,j)=\{w_{a+j},\ldots,w_{a+j+s-1}\},\qquad 0\le j<L.       \tag{1.5}
\]

For all sufficiently large `b`, (1.2) has `g<=2b-2`, so these tokens are
genuine sets.

Choose fragments, possibly in several rounds and with several lengths
\(1\le L_i\le b^2\), whose simple middle cores are mutually disjoint.  Write
`M` for the number of covered
middle vertices, `t_i` for the number of fragments of length `L_i`, and

\[
 h_s={2b\choose s}-
 \left|\{T_s(a,j):\hbox{selected }(a,L_i),\ 0\le j<L_i\}\right|.
                                                                    \tag{1.6}
\]

Linearizing each fragment separately and appending missing targets gives

\[
 \nu(2b)\le M+(g-1)\sum_i t_i
 +\sum_{s=b-H}^{b+H}h_s
 +\sum_{|s-b|>H}{2b\choose s}.                                \tag{1.7}
\]

To prove (1.7), write the singleton block
`w_a,...,w_(a+L_i+g-2)` for each selected fragment.  It costs
`L_i+g-1`, and every token (1.5) is internal to it.  Append every absent band
target and every target outside the band as one set-valued letter.  Summing
the block lengths proves (1.7).

The binomial tail in (1.7) is `o(W)`.  If
`X~Bin(2b,1/2)`, the exponential-moment estimate gives

\[
 \Pr(|X-b|\ge H)\le 2e^{-H^2/b},                               \tag{1.8}
\]

while `W>=2^(2b)/(2b+1)`.  With (1.2), (1.8) is enough.
Consequently

\[
 W-M=o(W),\qquad (g-1)\sum_i t_i=o(W),\qquad
 \sum_{s=b-H}^{b+H}h_s=o(W)                                   \tag{1.9}
\]

imply `nu(2b)=(1+o(1))W`.  The reverse inequality follows because all
interval unions ending at one word position form a chain, and therefore at
most one distinct middle target can be charged to that position.

Notice that (1.9) asks only `W-M=o(W)`.  The stronger
`W-M=o(W b^(-1/3))` arose solely from the older balanced-quota deficit term;
it is absent from the literal-hole compiler (1.7).

## 2. Arbitrarily slow cover-down

Fix constants `0<c<2` and `0<theta<1`, and let `a_b` satisfy (0.1).  Use
the single length (0.2).  Starting with `U_0=V`, suppose that whenever

\[
                  |U_j|>e^{-a_b}W,                              \tag{2.1}
\]

one selects a core matching of length `L_b`, contained in `U_j`, which
covers at least `theta |U_j|` vertices, and deletes its cores to obtain
`U_(j+1)`.  The choices are required to be made so that the union of all
their designated tokens has literal **off-middle** band-hole sum `o(W)`.
The middle hole is the final residual and is handled separately below.

### Theorem 2.1 (slow cover-down gate)

Under the preceding hypothesis,

\[
                         \nu(2b)=(1+o(1))W.                     \tag{2.2}
\]

The number of required rounds is at most

\[
 R_b\le\left\lceil {a_b\over-\log(1-\theta)}\right\rceil,      \tag{2.3}
\]

and their total seam cost is

\[
 (g-1)\sum_i t_i
 \le {gW\over L_b}
 =O\left({a_b\over\log b}W\right)=o(W).                        \tag{2.4}
\]

#### Proof

Every round multiplies the current residual size by at most `1-theta`, so
(2.3) follows.  At termination,

\[
 W-M=|U_(R_b)|\le e^{-a_b}W=o(W).                               \tag{2.5}
\]

Core disjointness gives `sum_i t_i=M/L_b<=W/L_b`.  Since
`H=o(b)`, (0.1)--(0.2) imply `L_b/g -> infinity`, and (2.4) follows.
The assumed literal band-hole estimate, (2.4), and (2.5) are exactly (1.9),
so Section 1 proves (2.2).  \(\square\)

The terminal-leave and round-count demands are weaker than stopping at
`b^(-gamma)` for a fixed positive `gamma`: here `a_b` may diverge
arbitrarily slowly.  The complete antecedents are not logically ordered,
because the present fixed fragment length can be longer than the earlier
density-adapted length.

### Lemma 2.2 (the labelled support is still far from extinction)

Let a random independent residual retain each middle vertex with probability
`x>=e^(-a_b)`.  The expected number `Z_x` of surviving labelled
length-`L_b` phase fragments satisfies

\[
 \log \mathbb E Z_x\ge(2-c+o(1))b\log b.                       \tag{2.6}
\]

#### Proof

There are

\[
 \widehat N=bW((b-1)!)^2
\]

phase-refined labelled atoms: choose the first shore, the two directed
cyclic orders, and one of the \(b\) phase cosets.  Each atom has \(b^2\)
cyclic starts, so the number of labelled length-\(L_b\) fragments is

\[
 \widehat N b^2=bW(b!)^2=b(2b)!.
\]

Equivalently, with \(\widehat D=b(b!)^2\), the exact labelled support
identity is

\[
 W\widehat D={2b\choose b}\,b(b!)^2=b(2b)!.                    \tag{2.7}
\]

The \(L_b\) middle vertices of a simple core are distinct, so a fragment
survives with probability `x^(L_b)`.  Stirling gives

\[
 \log(W\widehat D)=2b\log b+(2\log2-2)b+O(\log b).             \tag{2.8}
\]

Moreover `L_b log(1/x)<=c b log b+o(b log b)` by (0.2) and
`log(1/x)<=a_b`.  Subtraction proves (2.6).  This is annealed support only;
it is not a matching theorem.  \(\square\)

For the concrete choice `a_b=log log log b`, interpreted for sufficiently
large `b`, the terminal density is `1/log log b`, the round count is
`O(log log log b)`, and the seam ratio is
`O(log log log b/log b)`.

## 3. The obstruction persists at the slower scale

For disjoint coordinate pairs `(z_i,y_i)`, put

\[
 {\cal E}_m=\bigcap_{i=1}^m
 \{C\in{\cal V}:\mathbf1_{z_i\in C}=\mathbf1_{y_i\in C}\}.     \tag{3.1}
\]

It is complement-closed and hence every coordinate belongs to exactly half
of its members.  Its exact enumerator is

\[
 |{\cal E}_m|=[z^b](1+z^2)^m(1+z)^{2b-2m}.                     \tag{3.2}
\]

If `m=o(sqrt(b))`, comparison with the uniform fixed-size slice gives

\[
                  {|{\cal E}_m|\over W}
       =2^{-m}\exp(O(m^2/b)).                                  \tag{3.3}
\]

Indeed a prescribed membership pattern of weight `r` on the `2m`
distinguished coordinates has probability
`(b)_r(b)_(2m-r)/(2b)_(2m)=2^(-2m) exp(O(m^2/b))`, uniformly in
`r`; exactly `2^m` patterns satisfy the pair equalities.

Every phase-atom run lying in one equality family has length at most `b`.
For if `C_t,...,C_(t+b)` all have equal membership of `z,y`, every one of
the `b` transitions preserves the parity of the two membership bits.  Thus
the membership indicator of the first `b` emissions agrees positionwise
with that of the next `b` emissions.  Any length-`2b` type interval contains
`b-1` consecutive emissions of the `h`-event shore and `b+1` consecutive
emissions of the `(h+1)`-event shore.  Its coordinate multiset is therefore
\(\Omega\) with one coordinate omitted and a distinct coordinate duplicated.
The first half is an injective middle window and has zero or two
\(\{z,y\}\)-coordinates.  Positionwise agreement would make the full block
contain zero or four distinguished occurrences, whereas the
omission/duplication description gives one, two, or three.  This is a
contradiction.  Therefore `E_m`, being contained in its first equality
family, contains no length-`L` phase fragment for any `L>=b+1`.

Take

\[
                  m_b=\left\lfloor {a_b\over\log2}\right\rfloor-1.\tag{3.4}
\]

Since `a_b=o(log b)`, (3.3) applies and gives

\[
 |{\cal E}_{m_b}|/W=e^{-a_b+O(1)+o(1)}
 \ge(2-o(1))e^{-a_b}.                                         \tag{3.5}
\]

Thus complement symmetry and exact point balance fail at essentially the
new terminal scale as well.  The slow cover-down theorem removes an
unnecessary quantitative demand; it does not remove the need for a genuine
higher-order anti-junta or track-expansion invariant.

## 4. Exact aligned phase-packet normal form

The following finite statement exposes the smallest natural prefix unit of
the phase construction.

### Theorem 4.1 (punctured-chain packet)

Consider `2b` emissions beginning at the first position of the type period
(1.3).  There are unique distinct coordinates `u,v` and ordered disjoint
tuples

\[
 X=(x_1,\ldots,x_{b-1}),\qquad
 Y=(y_1,\ldots,y_{b-1}),                                      \tag{4.1}
\]

whose entries partition `Omega-{u,v}`, such that the emission block is

\[
       u,x_1,\ldots,x_{b-1},y_1,\ldots,y_{b-1},u.               \tag{4.2}
\]

Its `b+1` middle windows are

\[
 C_0=\{u\}\cup X,\qquad C_b=\{u\}\cup Y,                     \tag{4.3}
\]

and, for `1<=i<=b-1`,

\[
 C_i=
 \left(X\setminus\{x_1,\ldots,x_{i-1}\}\right)
 \cup\{y_1,\ldots,y_i\}.                                    \tag{4.4}
\]

In particular `C_0` and `C_b` meet only in `u`, their union omits only
`v`, and all internal windows avoid both `u` and `v`.

Conversely, every choice of `u,v,X,Y` as in (4.1) occurs as an aligned
packet of some phase-refined atom.

#### Proof

Across two copies of (1.3), there are `b+1` events of type `B` and `b-1`
events of type `A`.  The `B` stream therefore traverses all `b` shore
coordinates and repeats its first one, at the final position of the block;
call it `u`.  The `A` stream emits `b-1` distinct coordinates and omits one,
call it `v`.  Every other coordinate occurs exactly once.  Splitting the
middle `2b-2` emissions after position `b-1` gives (4.1)--(4.2).  Sliding a
length-`b` window through (4.2) gives (4.3)--(4.4) directly.

Conversely, assign to shore `B` the coordinates occupying the `B`-type
positions in (4.2); these are `b` distinct coordinates because only `u`
repeats.  Assign the `A`-type coordinates together with `v` to shore `A`.
Put the distinct `B` emissions in their encountered cyclic order.  Put the
`b-1` encountered `A` coordinates consecutively in their encountered order
and insert `v` in the one unvisited place.  Choosing the corresponding
stream origins makes the first `2b` emissions exactly (4.2); their class
modulo \(\langle(h,h+1)\rangle\) supplies the phase label.  Hence the packet
extends to a full phase-refined atom.  \(\square\)

An aligned long phase fragment can therefore be cut into consecutive
`b`-transition packets of the form (4.2), losing fewer than `2b` core
positions at its two ends.  Consecutive packets share their boundary middle
window.  Since (0.2) has `b/L_b=o(1)`, this endpoint loss is negligible at
the slow-cover scale.  The remaining positive problem can consequently be
viewed as a compatible packing and chaining problem for these explicit
punctured Boolean-chain packets, with literal offset images retained.  The
normal form is a structural reduction only; it does not assert that the
packets admit the required global packing.

## 5. Exact revised Gate-C antecedent

The following is the strongest conclusion justified here.

Fix any `a_b` satisfying (0.1), constants `0<c<2` and `0<theta<1`, and
`L_b` from (0.2).  It is sufficient to construct at most (2.3)
residual-disjoint length-`L_b` phase-fragment matchings, each removing at
least a `theta` fraction of the current residual until its density is at
most `e^(-a_b)`, such that their **union** has

\[
 \boxed{\sum_{s=b-H}^{b+H}h_s=o(W).}                            \tag{5.1}
\]

This is a literal-hole statement, not a quota-overflow surrogate.  Its
terminal-density and round-count demands are strictly weaker than those of
the previous fixed-`gamma` cover-down, because both may tend to their limits
arbitrarily slowly; the complete antecedents are not claimed to be ordered.
Equations (3.1)--(3.5) prove that an anti-junta or
equivalent track-expansion hypothesis is still indispensable.
