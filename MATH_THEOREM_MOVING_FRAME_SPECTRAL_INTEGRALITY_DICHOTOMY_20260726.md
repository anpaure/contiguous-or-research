# Moving pair frames: spectral completeness versus integral component rigidity

Date: 2026-07-26

Method: pure mathematics only.  The valid recursive context-array compiler is
used only through its exact pair-frame ownership and literal face properties.
No crossed recursion is used.

## 0. Verdict

Let \(V=[2m]\), let \({\cal A}\) be any nonempty finite catalogue of
perfect matchings of \(V\), and give every member of \({\cal A}\) positive
weight.  There is an exact dichotomy for the canonical moving-frame
pair-status heat.

Let

\[
                         G_{\cal A}=(V,\bigcup_{M\in{\cal A}}M)
\tag{0.1}
\]

and let \(B_1,\ldots,B_c\) be its coordinate components.

1. On every target layer \(\binom Vk\), the fixed space of the heat is
   exactly the set of functions of
   \[
                   (|T\cap B_1|,\ldots,|T\cap B_c|).
   \tag{0.2}
   \]
2. If \(G_{\cal A}\) is disconnected and
   \(2\le k\le2m-2\), a nonzero **pure Johnson degree-two** function is
   fixed.  Thus the heat has zero gap even after constants and all point
   margins have been removed.
3. If \(G_{\cal A}\) is connected, the exact common pair-status owner
   support on \(\binom Vm\) is the whole middle layer.  Hence an integral
   component selector must choose one pair frame globally.  Arbitrary
   recursive context-array choices inside that frame do not change the
   pair-type ledger.

The connected branch has a linear literal Hall deficit.  Put

\[
 q=\lfloor A\sqrt m\rfloor,\qquad A>0\text{ fixed},\qquad
 W=\binom{2m}{m},\qquad q\le H.
\tag{0.3}
\]

Every integral globally selected frame misses, at each sign,

\[
 \boxed{
 D_q^\pm\ge (\delta_A-o(1))W,
 \qquad
 \delta_A=e^{-A^2}\Phi(A/2)-\Phi(-3A/2)>0.}
\tag{0.4}
\]

By contrast, averaging a valid exact factor over all \(S_{2m}\)-frames
gives the uniform fractional **raw occurrence** load

\[
                         \Lambda_q={W\over\binom{2m}{m-q}}>1
\tag{0.5}
\]

at every lower or upper target, with the same whole-factor weights at all
depths. Thus the raw occurrence covering deficit is zero while the
integral pair-status selector has combined missing-shadow deficit at least

\[
                         (2\delta_A-o(1))W
\tag{0.6}
\]

at one protected depth. This is not a zero-deficit statement for the
whole-support configuration LP: after repeated occurrences inside a
state are collapsed, the all-ones target weight sees the same linear
deficit (0.4).

Consequently no sparse or dense catalogue in the pair-status component
model can simultaneously have

* a positive gap on every nonconstant relevant target harmonic, and
* an integral common-choice rounding with \(o(W)\) all-depth holes.

The only remaining moving-frame escape is strictly finer: the exact
cycle-factor overlays must split the common pair-status component, and
their component-effect covariance must be quantitatively smaller than the
continuous frame Dirichlet form.  The theorem does not assert that every
possible finer cycle overlay is connected.

## 1. The pair-status heat

Fix \(M\in{\cal A}\) and \(T\in\binom Vk\).  Its \(M\)-status cell is

\[
 C_M(T)=\{T'\in\tbinom Vk:|T'\cap e|=|T\cap e|
                                  \text{ for every }e\in M\}.
\tag{1.1}
\]

Let \(E_{M,k}\) be uniform averaging on this cell:

\[
 (E_{M,k}f)(T)={1\over|C_M(T)|}\sum_{T'\in C_M(T)}f(T').
\tag{1.2}
\]

On \(\ell^2(\binom Vk)\) with uniform measure, \(E_{M,k}\) is the
orthogonal projection onto the functions constant on every \(M\)-cell.
For positive weights \(w_M\), \(\sum_Mw_M=1\), define

\[
                         K_k=\sum_{M\in{\cal A}}w_ME_{M,k}.
\tag{1.3}
\]

### Theorem 1.1 (exact fixed algebra)

The fixed space of \(K_k\) consists exactly of the functions in (0.2).

#### Proof

For an orthogonal projection \(E\),

\[
                  \langle f,(I-E)f\rangle=\|(I-E)f\|_2^2.
\tag{1.4}
\]

Therefore

\[
 \langle f,(I-K_k)f\rangle
 =\sum_Mw_M\|(I-E_{M,k})f\|_2^2.
\tag{1.5}
\]

Since every weight is positive, \(K_kf=f\) if and only if \(f\) is
constant on every cell of every matching.

Two members of \(\binom Vk\) lie in the equivalence relation generated by
these cells precisely when they have the same occupancy in every
coordinate component \(B_i\).  Necessity is immediate: changing the
orientation of a split matching edge moves one occupied point along an
edge of \(G_{\cal A}\), so it preserves every component occupancy.

For sufficiency, the simple exclusion graph on the \(r\)-subsets of a
connected graph is connected.  Indeed, root a spanning tree and move
particles along tree edges, from the leaves inward, until they occupy a
fixed canonical \(r\)-set.  Every such move toggles a split edge belonging
to some matching in \({\cal A}\), and hence stays inside one of its status
cells.  Apply this independently in every \(B_i\).  Thus the generated
classes are exactly the fibres of (0.2), proving the claim. \(\square\)

## 2. A fixed pure degree-two harmonic in the disconnected branch

Assume that \(G_{\cal A}\) is disconnected.  Because every vertex lies on
an edge of every perfect matching, each coordinate component has size at
least two.  Choose a proper union component \(B\), put \(b=|B|\), and let

\[
                         Z(T)=|T\cap B|.
\tag{2.1}
\]

For \(2\le k\le2m-2\), the hypergeometric random variable \(Z\) under a
uniform \(T\in\binom Vk\) has at least three support points.  Choose the
unique monic quadratic

\[
                         P_2(z)=z^2-az-b_0
\tag{2.2}
\]

which is orthogonal to \(1\) and \(z\) for this hypergeometric law.  It is
nonzero because a nonzero quadratic cannot vanish on all three support
points.

### Theorem 2.1 (degree-two spectral obstruction)

The function \(T\mapsto P_2(Z(T))\) is a nonzero pure Johnson
degree-two function and

\[
                         K_kP_2(Z)=P_2(Z).
\tag{2.3}
\]

#### Proof

The function is fixed by Theorem 1.1.  It is the restriction of a
coordinate polynomial of degree at most two.  It remains to check
orthogonality to all degree-zero and degree-one functions.

Orthogonality to constants is part of the definition of \(P_2\).  If
\(v\in B\), exchangeability within \(B\) gives

\[
 \mathbb E[P_2(Z){\bf1}_{\{v\in T\}}]
 ={1\over b}\mathbb E[P_2(Z)Z]=0.
\tag{2.4}
\]

If \(v\notin B\), exchangeability in the complement gives

\[
 \mathbb E[P_2(Z){\bf1}_{\{v\in T\}}]
 ={1\over2m-b}\mathbb E[P_2(Z)(k-Z)]=0.
\tag{2.5}
\]

Thus it is orthogonal to the span of constants and point indicators.
Since it has degree at most two, it lies entirely in Johnson degree two.
Equation (2.3) proves that the degree-two spectral gap is zero. \(\square\)

This removes a possible loophole: exact factor effect vectors have zero
point margins, but the unmixed obstruction above already lies beyond the
point modes.

## 3. Integral common-support rigidity in the connected branch

Apply the equivalence relation of Theorem 1.1 to the middle layer
\(\binom Vm\).  If \(G_{\cal A}\) is connected, it has one class.  More
explicitly, the cells of every matching in \({\cal A}\), linked through
common owners, form one common support.

Here a *pair-status component selector* means exactly the integral heat
variable supplied by this overlap: on each common support it chooses one
complete matching shore.  The statement below does not classify arbitrary
cell mosaics which are not obtainable by whole-component switching.

### Theorem 3.1 (global integral frame lock)

In the pair-status recoupling model, if \(G_{\cal A}\) is connected then
an integral exact-owner selector must choose one matching \(M\in{\cal A}\)
on the whole middle layer.  It may choose arbitrary valid recursive
context-array factors inside the \(M\)-cells, but it cannot choose a
different matching on a proper nonempty owner subset.

#### Proof

For a disconnected coordinate union, the exact independent owner supports
are the occupancy fibres (0.2): every matching cell refines such a fibre,
and the cells of all matchings are connected within it by Theorem 1.1.
An integral recoupling must choose a whole shore on each such connected
support; otherwise some owner is used zero or twice.  When the coordinate
union is connected there is only the fibre \(\binom Vm\), so the matching
choice is global.  Once it is fixed, its cells partition the middle layer,
and any exact factor installed separately inside those cells preserves
ownership. \(\square\)

## 4. The fixed-frame literal Hall deficit

Fix a perfect matching \(M\).  For a middle owner \(X\), let \(f_M(X)\)
be its number of full \(M\)-pairs.  The number of owners of type \(f\) is

\[
 V_f={m!\,2^{m-2f}\over f!^2(m-2f)!}.
\tag{4.1}
\]

A lower rank-\((m-q)\) target of type \(f\) has \(f\) full pairs,
\(f+q\) empty pairs, and \(m-2f-q\) split pairs.  Hence its type count is

\[
 T_{f,q}={m!\,2^{m-2f-q}\over
                 f!(f+q)!(m-2f-q)!}.
\tag{4.2}
\]

Every genuine lower \(q\)-face of the orientation cube of \(X\) is
obtained by emptying \(q\) split pairs.  Consequently

\[
                         f_M(L_q(X))=f_M(X).
\tag{4.3}
\]

This identity is independent of the internal context array, order, phase,
or cycle.  Therefore all \(V_f\) owner occurrences of type \(f\) can cover
at most \(V_f\) distinct targets among the \(T_{f,q}\) targets of that
type.  Every integral fixed-frame factor obeys

\[
                         D_q^-\ge\sum_f(T_{f,q}-V_f)_+.
\tag{4.4}
\]

Complementation gives the identical upper bound.

### Lemma 4.1 (Gaussian evaluation)

If \(q=A\sqrt m+O(1)\), then

\[
 {1\over W}\sum_f(T_{f,q}-V_f)_+
 \longrightarrow
 e^{-A^2}\Phi(A/2)-\Phi(-3A/2).
\tag{4.5}
\]

#### Proof

First,

\[
 {N_q\over W}
 ={\binom{2m}{m-q}\over\binom{2m}m}
 =\prod_{i=1}^q{m-i+1\over m+i}.
\tag{4.6}
\]

Taking logarithms and using \(q=O(\sqrt m)\) gives

\[
 \log{N_q\over W}
 =-{q^2\over m}+O\left({q\over m}+{q^3\over m^2}\right)
 =-A^2+o(1).
\tag{4.7}
\]

For a uniform \(r\)-subset of \([2m]\), the number \(F_r\) of full
matched pairs has

\[
 \mathbb EF_r={r(r-1)\over2(2m-1)}.
\tag{4.8}
\]

Writing \(F_r\) as the sum of the \(m\) full-edge indicators gives

\[
 \operatorname {Var}F_r
 =m p_2(1-p_2)+m(m-1)(p_4-p_2^2),
\quad
 p_j={(r)_j\over(2m)_j}.
\tag{4.9}
\]

Uniformly for \(r=m+O(\sqrt m)\), expansion of (4.9) yields

\[
                         \operatorname {Var}F_r={m\over16}+o(m).
\tag{4.10}
\]

Stirling's formula applied to (4.1)--(4.2), uniformly on every bounded
standardized interval, now gives the local limits

\[
 {V_f\over W}={4\over\sqrt m}\phi(z)(1+o(1)),
\tag{4.11}
\]

\[
 {T_{f,q}\over N_q}={4\over\sqrt m}\phi(z+2A)(1+o(1)),
\tag{4.12}
\]

when

\[
                         f=\mathbb EF_m+{z\sqrt m\over4}+O(1).
\tag{4.13}
\]

For completeness, the logarithmic remainder in Stirling is uniform here:
the third derivative of every factorial entropy term is \(O(m^{-2})\),
while \(|f-\mathbb EF_r|=O(\sqrt m)\), so the cubic remainder is
\(O(m^{-1/2})\).  Exposing the matching edges gives bounded differences;
the resulting Gaussian tail bound supplies summable domination outside a
fixed standardized interval.  Thus (4.11)--(4.12) may be summed to obtain
the corresponding distribution-function limits.

The ratio

\[
 {V_f\over T_{f,q}}
 =2^q{(f+1)\cdots(f+q)\over
          (m-2f-q+1)\cdots(m-2f)}
\tag{4.14}
\]

is strictly increasing in \(f\), so there is one crossing.  Put
\(f=m/4+u\sqrt m+O(1)\).  Expanding the logarithm of (4.14) gives

\[
                         \log{V_f\over T_{f,q}}
 =8Au+3A^2+o(1).
\tag{4.15}
\]

Hence a crossing index \(a_m\) satisfies

\[
                         a_m=\mathbb EF_m-{3q\over8}+o(\sqrt m).
\tag{4.16}
\]

Below it \(T_{f,q}>V_f\).  Relative to the middle law, (4.16) has
standardized location \(-3A/2+o(1)\).  By (4.8), the target mean is

\[
 \mathbb EF_{m-q}=\mathbb EF_m-{q\over2}+O_A(1),
\tag{4.17}
\]

so the same crossing has target standardized location \(A/2+o(1)\).
Equations (4.7), (4.11)--(4.17) give

\[
 {1\over W}\sum_{f\le a_m}(T_{f,q}-V_f)
 \longrightarrow e^{-A^2}\Phi(A/2)-\Phi(-3A/2).
\tag{4.18}
\]

The one-crossing property makes the left side equal to the positive-part
sum up to a vanishing boundary term, proving (4.5). \(\square\)

The constant is strictly positive.  Indeed, in the middle standardized
coordinate \(z\), the limiting target-minus-middle density below the
crossing is

\[
                         e^{-A^2}\phi(z+2A)-\phi(z).
\tag{4.19}
\]

Its log likelihood ratio is

\[
 \log{e^{-A^2}\phi(z+2A)\over\phi(z)}
 =-2Az-3A^2,
\tag{4.20}
\]

which is positive precisely for \(z<-3A/2\).  Integrating this strictly
positive function proves \(\delta_A>0\).  Equations (4.4)--(4.5) prove
(0.4).

## 5. Raw occurrence averaging versus whole-support Hall

Let \(F\) be any valid exact recursive context-array factor in one physical
pair frame, decomposed into its legal whole packet/cycle columns.  Conjugate
the complete column family by every \(\sigma\in S_{2m}\), and give every
conjugated copy weight \(1/|S_{2m}|\).  Owner transitivity gives total
owner load one.

At a fixed sign and depth there are exactly \(W\) physical occurrences.
The action is transitive on the \(N_q\) targets.  Double-counting pairs
\((\sigma,\text{occurrence})\) which land at a prescribed target gives

\[
                         {|S_{2m}|W\over N_q}
\tag{5.1}
\]

such pairs.  Therefore its fractional occurrence load is exactly
\(W/N_q\), simultaneously at every depth because the same conjugated
whole columns are used throughout. Since \(N_q\le W\), every target has
raw fractional occurrence load at least one.

In the connected status-selector branch, Theorem 3.1 rounds this symmetric
point only to one global frame. Lemma 4.1 together with (4.4) then gives
(0.6). This is a linear gap for the raw occurrence covering relaxation.
It is **not** an integrality gap for the whole-support configuration LP:
the latter replaces each within-state multiplicity by its support
indicator, and the constant weight on the target layer already certifies
the linear deficit fractionally.

## 6. General giant-overlay cancellation

There is a companion obstruction which does not refer to status cells.
Let \(F\) be any exact factor and let \(\Sigma\subset S_{2m}\) be a
symmetric frame-generator set.  Suppose that for every orbit state \(gF\)
and every \(\sigma\in\Sigma\), the exact ownership interaction graph of
\(gF\) and \(\sigma gF\) is connected.  Then a component heat step has
one component and chooses either \(gF\) or \(\sigma gF\).  By induction,
every integral trajectory remains a single relabel \(hF\).

Consequently every relabel-invariant statistic, including every weighted
centered \(\ell^2\) energy, every floor-corrected energy, and the total
number of holes on a full target layer, is constant along the integral
trajectory.  This remains true even if the Markov averaging operator

\[
                         P=\sum_{\sigma\in\Sigma}w_\sigma\sigma
\tag{6.1}
\]

has a uniform spectral gap on all nonconstant target harmonics.

At one step the cancellation is exact.  If \(f\) is a centered target-load
vector, continuous averaging would gain

\[
                         {1\over4}\|f-\sigma f\|_2^2.
\tag{6.2}
\]

The single integral component has effect \(\Delta=\sigma f-f\), so its
rounding covariance costs the identical quantity (6.2).  The net expected
gain is zero.

Thus a spectral moving-frame proof needs more than a sparse expander of
coordinate frames.  It must prove a **strict fragmentation inequality**

\[
 \sum_K\|\Delta_{\sigma,K}\|_H^2
 \le(1-\varepsilon)
       \|f-\sigma f\|_H^2
\tag{6.3}
\]

on the relevant simultaneous-depth direct sum, for enough generators and
for every state reached by the iteration.  Connected overlays have equality
in (6.3).  The pair-status model above shows that imposing full harmonic
mixing at that level lands exactly in this equality branch.

## 7. Exact proved boundary

The following lane is closed:

> Average a sparse family of physical perfect-matching frames, use whole
> pair-status components as the integral variables, and invoke a spectral
> gap of the resulting frame heat to round the uniform fractional point.

Disconnected catalogues have an unmixed degree-two mode.  Connected
catalogues have one global owner component and a linear fixed-frame Hall
deficit.

What is not ruled out is a catalogue of valid recursive factors whose
**cycle-level** interaction components are much finer than their common
pair-status support.  For that escape, the next exact theorem is (6.3),
uniformly across all protected depths, together with literal placement of
the component effects.  Merely proving a spectral gap for the fractional
frame operator cannot substitute for this fragmentation estimate.
