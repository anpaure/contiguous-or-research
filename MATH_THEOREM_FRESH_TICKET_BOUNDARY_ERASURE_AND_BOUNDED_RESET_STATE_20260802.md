# Fresh-ticket boundary erasure and bounded reset state

**Date:** 2026-08-02  
**Status:** exact abstract factorization theorem and exact scoped `k=17`
no-go.  The theorem explains when a phase-specific ticket bank may be
rebuilt as bulk child data while only a bounded literal connector/reset
interface is carried.  It does not construct the missing all-dimensional
hosts.  On the present planted `k=17` host, exhaustive canonical host and
flag retuning leaves `782/783` short rows locally empty in the two s7 owner
phases.  Thus no complete phase-specific canonical bank exists on the
fixed `P*/mu^0/S/HOST` instance even before endpoint packing.  No
chronology, history, residence, supplier, compiler, word, or bound on
`nu(k)` is inferred.

## 0. Outcome

Let `P*` be the reconstructed inverse outer parent and let `S` be its frozen
shore of `1,748` selected short rows.  There are three logically different
questions.

The two **s7 owner phases** in this note use the same planted `HOST` target
rows and roots; only their owner maps change.  They must not be confused
with the two local `B_5` child tables `T^0,T^1`, whose eight lower rows
differ.  Every finite claim below uses the common planted target geometry
for both s7 phases.

1. **Static source regeneration.**  In local `B_5` mode `epsilon`, once a
   long endpoint host `h` is selected, its source token is derived from the
   outer matching as `(mu^epsilon)^(-1)(h)`.  It is not a carried ticket
   coordinate.  This is already proved for `P*`.  No outer matching indexed
   by the s7 owner phase is asserted.
2. **Fresh phase bank.**  In owner phase `phi`, one must choose a new physical
   ticket at every `s in S`, with private endpoint resources.  This is an
   occurrence-labelled three-partite hypermatching, not a flag choice on
   the old records.
3. **Carried state.**  Even if the two phase banks differ on all `1,748`
   records, their interior identities need not be carried if every exterior
   dependency factors through a bounded complete boundary and the boundary
   history is reset.

The exact frozen-record replay gives

\[
 (|V_0|,|V_1|,|V_0\cap V_1|)=(520,501,332).             \tag{0.1}
\]

Exhausting the `20` canonical choices
`q in {7,8}`, `0<=beta<=alpha<4` on each fixed host triple gives no extra
record: in phase zero exactly the same `520` triples have one choice and
the other `1,228` have none; in phase one the corresponding numbers are
`501` and `1,247`; the common `332` have the same unique choice.  Therefore
any phase-zero fresh bank on the same short shore must change at least one
long host on at least `1,228` inherited records, and any phase-one bank must
do so on at least `1,247`.  The union of inherited records requiring a host
change in at least one phase has size
`1,748-332=1,416`; this is not a claim of `1,416` changes separately in each
phase.  A bounded edit of the inherited bank is refuted.

This is not a lower bound on the carried state.  The theorem below permits
all these identities to be existentially reconstructed and forgotten on a
replacement host which actually has the required phase-local factors.

## 1. Exact phase read-set of one canonical ticket

Write a canonical ticket as

\[
                 t=(s,p,u;q,\alpha,\beta),             \tag{1.1}
\]

where `s` is its short row, `p,u` are the predecessor and successor long
hosts, `q in {7,8}`, and `alpha,beta in {0,1,2,3}` with
`beta<=alpha`.  The target rows of the planted child table determine the
three predecessor boxes, the short boxes, and the three successor boxes.

For an s7 owner phase `phi`, put

\[
 a_\phi(s)=O_\phi(s)\setminus R(s),\qquad
 b_\phi(u)=O_\phi(u)\setminus R(u).                    \tag{1.2}
\]

Each is one coordinate because the owner has rank nine and its root has
rank eight.  Direct expansion of the five-cell replay gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
L_0&=P^-_0\cup\{a_\phi(s)\},\\
L_1&=P^-_1\cup S^-_0\cup\{b_\phi(u)\},\\
L_2&=P^-_2\cup S^-_1\cup U^-_0,\\
L_3&=S^-_2\cup U^-_1,\\
L_4&=U^-_2,                                             \tag{1.3}\\
H_0&=P^+_0\cap O_\phi(s),\\
H_1&=P^+_1\cap S^+_0\cap O_\phi(u),\\
H_2&=P^+_2\cap S^+_1\cap U^+_0,\\
H_3&=S^+_2\cap U^+_1,\\
H_4&=U^+_2 .                                            \tag{1.4}
\end{aligned}
\]

The replay predicate says that all five boxes are nonempty,
`L_i` is a subset of `H_i`, and the declared inner and total short covers
have exactly the short lower and root values.  Consequently:

### Proposition 1.1 (complete mutable phase interface)

For fixed child target rows and fixed tuple (1.1), canonical ticket validity
depends on the s7 phase only through the two one-point markers

\[
                         (a_\phi(s),b_\phi(u)).         \tag{1.5}
\]

It does not read the predecessor owner, either source-token label, the
unused rows of the outer matching, or any other owner row.

#### Proof

Equations (1.3)--(1.4) list every occurrence of an owner value in the exact
replay.  The short owner appears only as `O_phi(s)` or its one-point
difference from `R(s)`, and the successor owner appears only in the same two
forms at `u`.  No predecessor owner or token label occurs.  All remaining
terms are functions of the three target rows and `(q,alpha,beta)`.
\(\square\)

Thus the minimal **phase-dependent** host interface of one local replay is
two owner-extension points.  The row identities `(s,p,u)` and the three
flags are still the complete child-local ticket choice.  “Two-point phase
interface” does not mean that the full bank changes on only two rows.

Both points are genuinely necessary on the frozen data.  Holding the
successor marker fixed and changing only the short marker flips validity on
`135` regenerated records; holding the short marker fixed and changing only
the successor marker flips validity on `136`.  If both markers are fixed,
validity is fixed.  Thus (1.5) is not merely a sufficient read-set that can
be reduced to one anonymous phase bit.

## 2. Exact fresh-bank problem on a fixed inverse materialization

Fix a local materialization mode `epsilon`, an s7 owner phase `phi`, a short
shore `S`, a child target table `T^epsilon`, its exact outer matching
`mu^epsilon`, and a set `L` of eligible long endpoint hosts.  For `s in S`,
let `C_(epsilon,phi)(s)` contain every occurrence-labelled witness

\[
 c=(s,p,u;q,\alpha,\beta;W),\qquad p,u\in L,\quad p\ne u, \tag{2.1}
\]

which satisfies:

1. the directed owner/root incidence required of `p,s,u`;
2. the canonical five-cell predicate (1.3)--(1.4);
3. the declared address and flag restriction; and
4. the exact endpoint-source pins
   `mu^epsilon((mu^epsilon)^(-1)(p))=p` and
   `mu^epsilon((mu^epsilon)^(-1)(u))=u`, with the fixed-soft convention where
   applicable.

Here `W` contains the chosen five physical cells and every other
occurrence-labelled private resource.  Write `Res(c)` for its complete
capacity-one footprint, including hosts, sources, addresses, and cells.
If a local geometry has several physical cell realizations, each realization
is a separate member of `C_(epsilon,phi)(s)`.

The last row is tautological for a complete outer matching, but it binds the
physical source labels to the selected endpoint occurrences and prevents an
old source label from being silently imported.

### Theorem 2.1 (private-bank hypermatching equivalence)

There is one `(epsilon,phi)`-valid private canonical ticket at every short in `S`,
with all predecessor and successor hosts globally distinct, if and only if
there are variables `x_c in {0,1}` satisfying

\[
 \sum_{c\in C_{\epsilon,\phi}(s)}x_c=1\quad(s\in S),   \tag{2.2}
\]

and

\[
 \sum_{c:\ h\in\{p(c),u(c)\}}x_c\le1\quad(h\in L).     \tag{2.3}
\]

In addition, every declared private resource obeys

\[
 \sum_{c:\ r\in\operatorname{Res}(c)}x_c\le1
       \quad(r\text{ a capacity-one private resource}). \tag{2.3a}
\]

Any required exact number of soft endpoints is imposed by its corresponding
cardinality row.  If opposite directed ports of one host may be shared,
(2.3) is replaced by the two directed capacity rows plus the common-flag
row; the equivalence is unchanged.

#### Proof

Read `x_c=1` from a private bank.  One ticket at each short gives (2.2),
endpoint privacy gives (2.3), and all remaining private rows give (2.3a).
Conversely, (2.2) selects one literal valid ticket at each short, (2.3)
makes all endpoint hosts private, and (2.3a) enforces every other declared
private row.  The source tokens are uniquely reconstructed from
`mu^epsilon`; every cell/address witness is present in `W`.  Hence the
selected records are exactly a private ticket bank.  \(\square\)

This is a labelled three-role hypermatching problem on the resources `S`, a
predecessor copy of `L`, and a successor copy of `L`, with the two
copies of a physical long host coupled by
(2.3), together with the resource-set-packing rows (2.3a).  Nonempty
individual menus are necessary but not sufficient.  There
is no ordinary two-shore Hall theorem before one host side or the flag state
has been fixed.

There is nevertheless one exact anonymous flag quotient.  For a selected
bank `B`, put

\[
 A_i=|\{t\in B:\alpha(t)=i\}|,
 \qquad B_i=|\{t\in B:\beta(t)=i\}|.                   \tag{2.4}
\]

The number of downward flag crossings at cut `t`, `0<=t<=2`, is

\[
 R_t=\sum_{x\in B}{\bf1}\{\beta(x)\le t<\alpha(x)\}
    =\sum_{i\le t}(B_i-A_i).                           \tag{2.5}
\]

Indeed, the right side counts flags entering the prefix minus flags leaving
it.  Hence `(R_0,R_1,R_2)` is the complete anonymous flag-conservation
imbalance.  It is not a direct-arc or endpoint Hall certificate: those rows
still read the actual host graph.

### Theorem 2.2 (current `P*/mu^0/S` fresh-bank no-go)

Fix all of the following:

1. the planted `HOST` target rows and roots in both s7 owner phases;
2. the current short shore `S` of size `1,748`;
3. the lower value delivered by the reconstructed phase-zero outer
   materialization at every long endpoint;
4. every rank-adjacent predecessor and successor long host determined by
   the root/owner bijections; and
5. the canonical reset-monotone menu
   `q in {7,8}`, `0<=beta<=alpha<4`.

Project each set `C_(0,phi)(s)` to the six geometry fields
`(s,p,u,q,alpha,beta)`.  Exhausting that complete geometric projection gives

\[
\begin{array}{c|rrr}
 &\text{shorts with a geometry}&\text{locally empty}&
   \text{candidate geometries}\\ \hline
\phi=0&966&782&1432\\
\phi=1&965&783&1428.
\end{array}                                               \tag{2.6}
\]

Across the two phases, `799` shorts are positive in both, `167/166` are
positive only in phase zero/one, and

\[
 616\text{ are zero in both},\qquad
 |Z_0\cup Z_1|=949.                                      \tag{2.7}
\]

Consequently neither phase admits a complete canonical ticket bank on this
fixed shore, even without endpoint privacy, flag consistency between
different tickets, long--long completion, or any later protected row.

Relative to the inherited host triple, the minimum endpoint-host movement
partition is

\[
\begin{array}{c|rrrrr}
 &\text{fixed}&\text{predecessor changes}&
   \text{successor changes}&\text{both change}&
   \text{zero even after both change}\\ \hline
\phi=0&520&152&136&158&782\\
\phi=1&501&165&129&170&783.
\end{array}                                               \tag{2.8}
\]

Thus the exact current-host obstruction is stronger than flag rigidity:
even arbitrary motion of both endpoint hosts cannot repair the final
`782/783` rows.  If the target table, outer-delivered lowers, and owner maps
are fixed, a shore locally positive in both phases must delete at least all
`949` members of `Z_0 union Z_1`; at most the `799` currently positive-in-
both rows can survive.  This is a necessary bulk-shore replacement count,
not a carried-state lower bound.

#### Proof

For each short, the root and owner bijections enumerate every rank-adjacent
predecessor and successor long host.  The audit enumerates all `20`
canonical flag/address choices for every such ordered host pair and applies
the exact five-envelope and cover predicate (1.3)--(1.4).  The local-interface
theorem proves that this predicate is equivalent to a constructive
five-cell replay.  A separate C++ implementation reevaluates the literal
predicate and reproduces every aggregate count and an identical zero-set
TSV.  A short with an empty geometric menu has no occurrence-labelled
extension `W`, so it cannot satisfy its exact-one row (2.2).  Equation (2.6)
therefore refutes a complete bank in either phase, and set arithmetic gives
(2.7).  \(\square\)

The scope is sharp.  The theorem does not forbid a replacement construction
which changes at least one of:

* the short shore or a short target row;
* the long lower/middle geometry delivered by a different outer
  rematerialization; or
* the s7 owner allocation.

It also does not address a noncanonical short-address/flag architecture.
Changing only ticket host identities and canonical flags on the current
instance is exhausted and cannot work.

## 3. Exact bounded-boundary erasure theorem

The size of the solution of (2.2)--(2.3) is not automatically the size of
the carried sidecar.  We now state the exact separation criterion.

For one regenerative transition, let

* `X` be all child-local bulk variables: the phase-specific ticket bank,
  its internal endpoint identities, and every internal cell;
* `Y` be a declared literal boundary of at most `c` occurrence/cell
  identities together with its declared aggregate port signature; and
* `Z` be the exterior data read by the next transition.

The aggregate signature may contain an exact cap-current/debt pair or an
exact directed-history relation.  It is not an arbitrary oracle: every
global row represented by it must have a proved composition law.

Call `Y` a **complete boundary** if the full protected predicate admits the
following equivalence for every phase `phi` and every assignment
`X,Y,Z`:

\[
 \mathcal F_\phi(X,Y,Z)
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \mathcal I_\phi(X,Y)\wedge\mathcal E_\phi(Y,Z),        \tag{3.1}
\]

after all declared aggregate rows have been replaced by their exact monoid
signatures.  In particular, no equality, address, history, residence,
supplier, upper/source, cap, compiler, or topology relation may still have
one argument in `X-Y` and another in `Z-Y`.

The boundary schema and the projection to `Y` are fixed before `X` or `Z`
is selected; `Y` may not be chosen retrospectively from a successful full
witness.  The interior variables `X` are disjoint from the boundary
variables `Y`.  Correctness of every aggregate/signature value in `Y` is an
explicit conjunct of `I_phi(X,Y)`.

Here “at most `c`” counts every non-baseline connected boundary component
and all of its non-baseline literal occurrence identities.  A long ordered
rail may be baseline structure, but an undeclared family of `d` exceptional
addresses on that rail is not one boundary resource.  Every actual
nonadjacent overlap, endpoint aperture, and propagated history guard belongs
to (3.1); none may be inferred from a marginal local ticket replay.

### Theorem 3.1 (existential boundary erasure)

If `Y` is a complete boundary, define

\[
 Q_\phi(Y)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
             \exists X\ \mathcal I_\phi(X,Y).           \tag{3.2}
\]

Then, exactly,

\[
 \exists X\ \mathcal F_\phi(X,Y,Z)
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 Q_\phi(Y)\wedge\mathcal E_\phi(Y,Z).                  \tag{3.3}
\]

Therefore the complete bulk bank may be reconstructed independently in
each phase and then forgotten.  Only the `c` literal boundary resources and
the declared compositional signature are carried.  This remains true if
the two phase banks differ at every ticket identity.

#### Proof

Existentially quantify `X` in (3.1).  Since `X` does not occur in
`E_phi(Y,Z)`, it factors out of the quantifier and the remaining first
factor is precisely (3.2).  \(\square\)

### Proposition 3.2 (sharp test for a proposed quotient)

Fix one phase `phi` and a proposed boundary projection `partial(X)=Y`.
Call an interior continuation-legal when its continuation set is nonempty.
Erasure is exact on the continuation language if and only if any two
continuation-legal interiors in the same projection fibre have the same set
of legal exterior continuations:

\[
 \partial(X_1)=\partial(X_2)
 \Longrightarrow
 \{Z:\mathcal F_\phi(X_1,\partial(X_1),Z)\}
 =\{Z:\mathcal F_\phi(X_2,\partial(X_2),Z)\}.          \tag{3.4}
\]

#### Proof

If (3.1) holds in the fixed phase, both continuation sets are the set defined by
`E(Y,Z)`.  Conversely, define `E(Y,Z)` to hold when one (equivalently every)
continuation-legal interior in the fibre over `Y` accepts `Z`; (3.4) makes
this well-defined.  Set `E(Y,Z)` false on a fibre with no continuation-legal
interior.  Let `I(X,Y)` assert continuation-legality and
`partial(X)=Y`.
Then (3.1) follows.  \(\square\)

Thus bounded erasure is a theorem about phase-fixed continuation
equivalence, not about the Hamming distance between the two ticket TSV
files.  Cross-phase carry additionally requires the explicit conjugacy in
Corollary 3.3.

### Corollary 3.3 (two-phase boundary replacement)

Suppose phases zero and one have complete boundaries `Y_0,Y_1`, and let

\[
                       \theta:Y_0\longrightarrow Y_1       \tag{3.5}
\]

be a bijection.  Suppose `theta` is an isomorphism of the complete boundary
signature, is injective on every capacity-one physical cell and actual
address, and transports every overlap/history value occurring in the
exterior predicate.  More formally, for the declared exterior transport
`beta`, assume

\[
 \mathcal E_0(Y,Z)
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \mathcal E_1(\theta Y,\beta Z),                         \tag{3.6}
\]

and assume both `Q_0(Y)` and `Q_1(theta Y)`.
Then the phase-zero and phase-one interiors may be selected independently,
and replacing one by the other transports only `Y` through `theta`; no bulk
ticket identity is carried.

#### Proof

Theorem 3.1 replaces each existential interior by `Q_phi` and its exterior
predicate.  The two `Q` hypotheses supply the phase-local interiors, while
(3.6) identifies their complete exterior traces.  Injectivity prevents two
capacity-one occurrences or addresses from being identified by the
transport.  \(\square\)

The full-boundary and injectivity hypotheses are load-bearing.  Equality of
the two anonymous reset vectors (2.5), or equality of local owner markers,
does not imply (3.6).

## 4. Literal separator obstruction

There is also a direct way to refute a claimed bounded literal boundary.
Make the dependency graph whose vertices are occurrence-labelled resources
and whose edges join two vertices that occur together in one exported
relation.  Functional data already proved reconstructible from one side is
first eliminated.

### Proposition 4.1 (disjoint exported-dependency lower bound)

Suppose the reduced dependency graph contains `r` vertex-disjoint edges

\[
                     x_i z_i\qquad(1\le i\le r),        \tag{4.1}
\]

with every `x_i` a bulk ticket resource and every `z_i` an exterior
resource.  Every literal boundary separating the bulk from the exterior
has size at least `r`.

#### Proof

Each edge in (4.1) must meet the separator.  The edges are vertex-disjoint,
so these choices require at least `r` distinct boundary vertices.
\(\square\)

Hence exporting one independent actual-address or unreset history equality
for every ticket would force an unbounded boundary.  Three mechanisms can
legitimately remove such edges:

1. derive the value again from the new child, as `P*` does for endpoint
   source tokens;
2. compose all interior contributions into an authenticated aggregate
   monoid signature; or
3. pass through a literal reset whose output is independent of the incoming
   interior history.

The `520/501/332` census alone proves none of these lower bounds.  It refutes
the old fixed interior, not a bounded complete boundary.

## 5. Reset composition and the conditional additive consequence

Suppose an all-dimensional construction satisfies the following clauses.

1. For the **actual carried boundary value** `Y_k`, every required local
   mode/owner phase satisfies `Q_(epsilon,phi)(Y_k)`: equivalently,
   (2.2)--(2.3a), or its exact nonprivate replacement, has a solution with
   that boundary and extends to the required complete phase-local factor on
   the new child.  Unconditioned existence of some bank is insufficient.
2. The complete protected dependency structure factors through a boundary
   `Y_k` with at most `c` literal connector/reset resources, uniformly in
   `k`, and consecutive phases satisfy Corollary 3.3 with all nonadjacent
   overlap/address/history guards included.
3. Its positive and negative residence relations accept the connector and
   a pivot maps the allowed entrance domain to one fixed output history.
   The ordered `(d-1)` rail is baseline structure; only the bounded terminal
   connector/reset occurrence is charged as sidecar.
4. The cap signature is returned (or lies in a bounded invariant slack
   state), and the required literal birail cross matchings exist in one
   compatible terminal state.  The zero-block theorem then leaves only the
   actual zero-to-zero pairs, not a positive-threshold sorting debt.
5. The local monotone pivot is owner-legal and supplies its exact two rays;
   on its flat transported bank it creates zero old arbitrary-width upper
   damage.
6. One integral owner-rainbow chronology realizes the selected bulk state.
   The stationary pull clock supplies only fractional membership and does
   not discharge this clause.
7. In the terminal compilation, the complete damage set together with all
   newly claimed compiler cells meets at most `C` cells of one reference
   matching.  Thus bounded eviction creates at most `C` total old-target
   casualties; they are paid once and are not carried to the next child.
8. The choices above form one mutually compatible infinite odd spine and
   compatible even terminal children.  The aggregate signature has a
   uniformly bounded literal serialization, rather than merely a bounded
   number of formal coordinates.

### Corollary 5.1 (bounded fresh-bank sidecar)

Under clauses 1--8, the phase-specific ticket banks contribute no bulk
carried identity.  The regenerative state consists of at most `c` literal
connector/reset resources and the declared bounded aggregate signature.
If `a` is the full literal serialization/source charge of these boundary
resources, their aggregate signature, every other non-baseline connector,
and all newly claimed compiler cells, the bounded-spine and bounded-eviction
theorems give

\[
                         \nu(k)\le B(k)+a+C.            \tag{5.1}
\]

In particular, fixed absolute `a,c,C` give `B(k)+O(1)`.

#### Proof

Theorem 3.1 erases each bulk bank after reconstructing it in its own child.
The reset makes its outgoing history independent of the erased incoming
identities; the cap and birail hypotheses return the other declared
compositional coordinates.  Thus the odd transition maps the bounded
boundary family into itself.  The terminal compiler damage is paid once by
bounded eviction and is absent from the next auxiliary state.  Apply the
bounded-cost odd-spine theorem.  \(\square\)

For an honest `B+1`, one still needs `a=1`, `C=0`, and a one-resource
boundary in which the sole relay output is simultaneously the outgoing
pivot/reset/aperture token.  No theorem here proves that co-location.

## 6. Exact present boundary

The following statements are **PROVED** in their stated scopes.

1. `P*` exactly rematerializes both local `B_5` modes, and endpoint sources
   are derived from its complete outer matchings.
2. Proposition 1.1 is the exact phase read-set of the canonical five-cell
   ticket predicate.
3. Both one-point owner markers in that read-set are genuinely necessary on
   the frozen data.
4. The fixed triples have singleton-or-empty canonical flag menus, giving
   the lower bounds `1228/1247/1416` in Section 0.
5. Theorem 2.2 refutes a complete canonical bank on the current
   `P*/mu^0/S/HOST` instance already at the local-menu row, with
   `782/783` phasewise zeros and zero-set union `949`.
6. The reset identity (2.5), Theorems 2.1 and 3.1, Corollary 3.3, and
   Propositions 3.2 and 4.1 are exact abstract
   equivalences/lower bounds.
7. The positive-history pivot reset, monotone-pivot zero-old-damage theorem,
   zero-block birail collapse, fractional stationary pull clock, and bounded
   compiler eviction remain available exactly in their original scopes.

The following statements remain **UNPROVED**.

1. a replacement host, short shore, outer-delivered long geometry, or owner
   allocation on which every required phase has a complete bank satisfying
   (2.2)--(2.3a);
2. an `O(1)` complete boundary for the selected `z=6` occurrence closure,
   including every actual address, aggregate positive and negative history,
   reset, residence, supplier, upper/source/common-cap, and non-evicted
   compiler dependency;
3. a phase-common protected host carrying that boundary and the bare s7
   aperture with correct orientation;
4. an integral one-copy chronology and an all-dimensional regenerative
   family; and
5. any unconditional `B+1`, `B(k)+O(1)`, or new value of `nu(k)`.

The exact next theorem is therefore not preservation or host retuning of the
`1,748` old ticket identities.  It is either:

* a new host/short shore with phasewise solutions of (2.2)--(2.3a) and a
  bounded complete boundary; or
* a literal dependency obstruction of the form Proposition 4.1 showing
  that every such host must export an unbounded number of independent
  ticket resources.
