# Lane N: the all-block ownership relation and a uniform non-Motzkin crossing

Date: 2026-07-26

Method: pure mathematics only. No finite search, computation, solver, or
web input is used.

Let \(C_s=\operatorname {Cat}_s\), let \(D_s\) be the Dyck roots of
semilength \(s\), and let \(F_s\) be the canonical MSW/Chung--Feller
anchored \(D_s\)-port factor. Put

\[
 \eta_s=(2\ 3)(4\ 5)\cdots(2s-2\ 2s-1)
\]

and

\[
 G_s(P)=\eta_sF_s(\eta_sP).
\]

The purpose of this note is to determine what can and cannot presently be
claimed about the full \(X/Y\)-ownership overlay of \(F_s\) and \(G_s\).

## 0. Outcome

There are four exact conclusions.

1.  The full components are the equivalence classes generated by the
    explicit phase-owner maps in Theorem 1.1 below. At the \(X\)-phases
    these are the permutations

    \[
       \sigma_t=i_t^{-1}\eta_si_t\eta_s,
       \qquad 0\le t\le s,
    \]

    where \(i_t(P)=X_t^{F_s}(P)\). The odd \(Y\)-owner maps must also be
    retained and need not be permutations.

2.  The direct all-block overlay does **not** preserve the peak-erasure
    Motzkin normal form which classifies the smaller
    \(H_s\)-conjugated leaf graph. For every \(s\ge4\), one literal
    \(X_1\)-owner edge joins the two roots

    \[
    \begin{aligned}
       P_0&=1(10)^{s-1}0,\\
       P_1&=1\,11\,(10)^{s-3}\,00\,0.
    \end{aligned}
    \]

    Their uncoloured Motzkin words are respectively

    \[
       H^{s-1},\qquad UH^{s-3}D,
    \]

    which are distinct and already peakless. Thus the direct overlay is
    strictly stronger than the conjugated-leaf graph.

3.  One full component contains the entire Boolean cube of
    all-horizontal two-coloured Motzkin roots. In particular,

    \[
                         \max_K|K|\ge3\cdot2^{s-2}-1
                         \qquad(s\ge3).
    \]

    The component also contains the nonhorizontal root in Item 2. This is
    an exponential packet lower bound, although
    \(2^{s-1}/C_s\to0\) exponentially.

    In particular, the size-biased component second moment satisfies

    \[
       {1\over C_s}\sum_K|K|^2
       \ge {\bigl(3\cdot2^{s-2}-1\bigr)^2\over C_s}
       =\left({9\sqrt\pi\over16}+o(1)\right)s^{3/2}.
    \]

    At a critical scale \(s/\sqrt m\to\kappa>0\), this lower bound is
    \((9\sqrt\pi\kappa^{3/2}/16+o(1))m^{3/4}\).

4.  Let \(R_n\) count sequences of non-singleton primitive Dyck
    components, equivalently

    \[
       \sum_{n\ge0}R_nz^n={1\over1-z^2C(z)^2}.
    \]

    Then

    \[
       \#\pi_0(F_s\cup G_s)
       \le1+{C_s-(3\cdot2^{s-2}-1)+R_{s-1}\over2}
       \le {C_s+R_{s-1}\over2}
       =\left({5\over9}+o(1)\right)C_s,
    \]

    and at most \(R_{s-1}=(1/9+o(1))C_s\) full components can be
    singletons. Equivalently, at least

    \[
       C_s-R_{s-1}=(8/9+o(1))C_s
    \]

    roots lie in non-singleton components.

5.  This is not a positive-density component theorem. It permits linearly
    many further components, and the certified component has exponentially
    vanishing density in \(D_s\). Full connectivity, an \(o(C_s)\)
    component-count bound, and a useful upper bound on the largest
    component all remain unproved. Consequently the all-block pair has not
    met the CRH rounding hinge merely from its ownership structure.

The component action itself is exact: switching one full component \(K\)
replaces its canonical carrier vector \(\mu_K\) by

\[
                         \eta_{s*}\mu_{\eta_sK}.
\]

Thus its signed action is

\[
             \Delta_K=\eta_{s*}\mu_{\eta_sK}-\mu_K.
\]

## 1. Exact contracted owner graph

Interleave the canonical row tokens as

\[
 Z_{2t}(P)=X_t^{F_s}(P),\qquad
 Z_{2t+1}(P)=Y_t^{F_s}(P).
\]

Let \(o_X(T)\) and \(o_Y(T)\) be the roots of the unique canonical rows
owning an \(X\)-token and a \(Y\)-token, respectively. For the
Chung--Feller layer \(L_t\), write

\[
                   i_t:D_s\longrightarrow L_t,
                   \qquad i_t(P)=X_t^{F_s}(P).
\]

Every \(i_t\) is a bijection and \(\eta_s\) preserves every \(L_t\).
Define

\[
 \sigma_t(P)=i_t^{-1}\eta_si_t\eta_s(P),
 \qquad 0\le t\le s,
 \tag{1.1}
\]

and

\[
 a_t(P)=o_Y\!\left(\eta_sY_t^{F_s}(\eta_sP)\right),
 \qquad 0\le t<s.
 \tag{1.2}
\]

### Theorem 1.1 (full owner relation)

After contracting the common phase-zero port edge between the two rows
rooted at each physical port \(P\), the full ownership graph is the graph
on \(D_s\) with edges

\[
                  P\ --\ \sigma_t(P)
                  \qquad(0\le t\le s)
 \tag{1.3}
\]

and

\[
                  P\ --\ a_t(P)
                  \qquad(0\le t<s).
 \tag{1.4}
\]

Its connected components are exactly the independently switchable full
\(X/Y\)-ownership components.

#### Proof

The row of \(G_s\) with physical root \(P\) is the coordinate image of
the canonical row rooted at \(\eta_sP\). Its phase-\(t\) \(X\)-token is

\[
       \eta_si_t(\eta_sP).
\]

The canonical owner of this token is

\[
       i_t^{-1}\eta_si_t\eta_s(P)=\sigma_t(P),
\]

which proves (1.3). The same ownership statement for the union colour is
exactly (1.2), proving (1.4). The \(X\)- and \(Y\)-ledgers contain no
other tokens. Connectivity after the phase-zero contractions is therefore
precisely the equivalence closure of (1.3)--(1.4). A complete connected
bipartite component is exactly a closed old/new row trade. \(\square\)

The maps \(a_t\) cannot in general be discarded: a canonical row may own
several transformed \(Y\)-tokens in one column while another row owns
none, so the odd owner maps need not be injective.

### Proposition 1.2 (anchor automorphism and component action)

The involution \(\eta_s\) permutes the full component blocks. If \(K\)
is one component and \(\mu_K\) is any complete coordinate-equivariant
carrier sum over the canonical rows rooted in \(K\), switching \(K\) has
the exact action

\[
                  \Delta_K=\eta_{s*}\mu_{\eta_sK}-\mu_K.
 \tag{1.5}
\]

#### Proof

Before contraction, an ownership edge witnessed by a token \(T\) joins
the canonical row containing \(T\) to the conjugate row whose underlying
canonical token is \(\eta_sT\). Applying \(\eta_s\) to the witness and
exchanging the two shores is a graph automorphism. On contracted physical
ports it acts as \(P\mapsto\eta_sP\), proving the first assertion.

The new rows with physical roots in \(K\) are

\[
                 \{\eta_sF_s(\eta_sP):P\in K\}.
\]

Their aggregate is therefore the \(\eta_s\)-image of the canonical rows
rooted in \(\eta_sK\). Subtracting the old canonical aggregate on \(K\)
gives (1.5). \(\square\)

In particular, replacing \(\eta_sK\) by \(K\) in (1.5) requires the
additional theorem \(\eta_sK=K\); it is not automatic componentwise.

## 2. A uniform edge crossing the peak-erasure invariant

We use one elementary consequence of the MSW first-return recursion. If

\[
                         W=1A0B
\]

and \(A\ne\varnothing\), the first inserted coordinate is the displayed
closing zero. The first deleted coordinate is the opening one of the last
primitive component of \(A\). This follows immediately from

\[
 \rho(1A0B)=
 (|A|+2,\ |A|+2-\rho(\operatorname {rev}A),\ 1,
                         |A|+2+\rho(B)).
 \tag{2.1}
\]

### Theorem 2.1 (root-scale arch erasure edge)

For every \(s\ge3\), the roots \(P_0,P_1\) in Section 0 belong to one
common full ownership component. In fact

\[
                    X_1^{G_s}(P_1)=X_1^{F_s}(P_0).
 \tag{2.2}
\]

#### Proof

Put \(n=s-1\). Since \(\eta_s\) exchanges the two orientations of every
horizontal Motzkin pair,

\[
 \eta_sP_1=1\,11\,(01)^{n-2}\,00\,0=:Q.
 \tag{2.3}
\]

The root \(Q\) is primitive. Its inner Dyck word

\[
                         11(01)^{n-2}00
\]

is also primitive. Formula (2.1) therefore says that its first canonical
transition deletes coordinate \(2\) and inserts coordinate \(2s\). Hence

\[
 X_1^{G_s}(P_1)
  =\eta_s\bigl(Q-\{2\}+\{2s\}\bigr)
  =P_1-\{3\}+\{2s\}.
 \tag{2.4}
\]

The root \(P_0=1(10)^n0\) is primitive, and its inner word is a forest of
\(n\) singleton primitive components. The last component begins at
coordinate \(2s-2\). Thus its first canonical transition deletes
\(2s-2\) and inserts \(2s\):

\[
 X_1^{F_s}(P_0)=P_0-\{2s-2\}+\{2s\}.
 \tag{2.5}
\]

Turning the indicated one in either root into zero gives the same word:

\[
             P_1-\{3\}=P_0-\{2s-2\}
             =1(10)^{n-1}00\,0.
 \tag{2.6}
\]

Equations (2.4)--(2.6) prove (2.2). \(\square\)

Under the paired-coordinate Motzkin encoding, \(P_0\) and \(P_1\) have
uncoloured words \(H^n\) and \(UH^{n-2}D\). For \(n\ge3\), neither word
contains an adjacent \(UD\), so both are already normal and are distinct.
This proves that the direct all-block overlay crosses the complete
peak-erasure invariant, not merely a colour refinement of it.

The edge (2.2) is one crossing family. It does not prove that arbitrary
patterns \(UH^kD\) can be erased in arbitrary contexts, and it therefore
does not prove transitivity.

### Lemma 2.2 (exact Dyck-seam owner edge)

For every \(1\le t<s\), \(A\in D_t\), and \(B\in D_{s-t}\), the
phase-\(t\) \(X\)-suboverlay contains the edge

\[
       \eta_s(AB)\quad --\quad (\eta_tA)(\eta_{s-t}B). \tag{2.7}
\]

The two endpoint words agree away from the concatenation seam and have
respectively `10` and `01` at that seam.

#### Proof

Put \(P=\eta_s(AB)\), so that the underlying canonical source of the
conjugate row is \(AB\). The MSW concatenation law gives

\[
                         X_t^{F_s}(AB)=\overline A B.  \tag{2.8}
\]

At the seam in this state, \(\overline A\) ends in `1` and \(B\) starts
in `1`. The crossing transposition of \(\eta_s\) fixes `11`, while all
other transpositions restrict to \(\eta_t\) and \(\eta_{s-t}\). Therefore

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 X_t^{G_s}(P)
  &=\eta_s(\overline A B)\\
  &=\overline {\eta_tA}\,(\eta_{s-t}B)\\
  &=X_t^{F_s}\bigl((\eta_tA)(\eta_{s-t}B)\bigr).
 \end{aligned}                                         \tag{2.9}
\]

This proves the owner edge. In the initial root \(AB\), the seam is
`01`; applying \(\eta_s\) changes it to `10`. The separately transformed
concatenation retains `01`, and all nonseam coordinates agree. \(\square\)

### Theorem 2.3 (one complete Boolean component)

More generally, fix an uncoloured Motzkin skeleton \(M\), fix the
orientations of all its nonground horizontal steps, and let \(g_0(M)\) be
the number of its horizontal steps at Motzkin height zero. All
\(2^{g_0(M)}\) choices of the ground-horizontal orientations lie in one
full component, and the corresponding Boolean-cube edges are literal
\(X_t\)-owner edges.

Let \(\mathcal H_s\subset D_s\) be the roots whose length-\((s-1)\)
Motzkin word is entirely horizontal, with each horizontal independently
oriented as `10` or `01`. Then all \(2^{s-1}\) members of
\(\mathcal H_s\) lie in one component of the direct \(F_s/G_s\)
ownership overlay. More precisely, every edge of the Boolean cube on the
\(s-1\) horizontal orientations is a literal \(X_t\)-owner edge for the
corresponding \(t\in\{1,\ldots,s-1\}\).

#### Proof

Fix a physical root \(P\) whose \(t\)-th pair is a ground-level `10`, and
put \(Q=\eta_sP\). The \(t\)-th pair of \(Q\) is `01`. Since the
Motzkin height there is zero, the first bit of this pair is a top-level
Dyck return. Hence there is a factorization

\[
                         Q=AB,
 \qquad A\in D_t,\quad B\in D_{s-t}.                 \tag{2.10}
\]

Apply Lemma 2.2. If

\[
                         A'=\eta_tA,
 \qquad B'=\eta_{s-t}B,                               \tag{2.11}
\]

the contracted phase-\(t\) owner edge joins \(P\) to \(P'=A'B'\).
To compare these roots, apply \(\eta_s\) to the initial concatenation
\(AB\). Away from the boundary it acts exactly as \(\eta_t\) on \(A\)
and \(\eta_{s-t}\) on \(B\). At the boundary it swaps the ending `0` of
\(A\) with the starting `1` of \(B\). Consequently \(P=\eta_s(AB)\)
and \(P'=A'B'\) agree in every coordinate except the boundary pair:

\[
                         10\quad\longleftrightarrow\quad01. \tag{2.12}
\]

Every other coordinate, including every nonground horizontal orientation,
is unchanged. Thus every ground-horizontal coordinate can be toggled
independently. This proves the general cube statement. Taking
\(M=H^{s-1}\), for which every horizontal is at ground, proves the final
assertion. \(\square\)

The all-`10` root in \(\mathcal H_s\) is \(P_0\) of Theorem 2.1.
Therefore that theorem also puts \(P_1\), whose Motzkin skeleton is
\(UH^{s-3}D\), in this same component. In particular,

\[
                         \max_K|K|\ge2^{s-1}+1
                         \qquad(s\ge3).                \tag{2.13}
\]

The weaker round-number bound \(2^{s-1}\) is valid for every \(s\ge1\).

### Lemma 2.4 (suffix lifting of the arch edge)

For \(a\ge3\), write

\[
 P_0^{(a)}=1(10)^{a-1}0,
 \qquad
 P_1^{(a)}=1\,11\,(10)^{a-3}\,00\,0.
 \tag{2.14}
\]

For every \(b\ge0\), every \(S\in D_b\), and \(s=a+b\), the two roots

\[
                         P_0^{(a)}S,
 \qquad                  P_1^{(a)}S                  \tag{2.15}
\]

belong to one full component of the size-\(s\) direct overlay.

#### Proof

The case \(b=0\) is Theorem 2.1. Suppose \(b>0\), put
\(R=\eta_bS\), and define

\[
                         L=\eta_s\bigl((\eta_aP_1^{(a)})R\bigr).
 \tag{2.16}
\]

Lemma 2.2, applied at the seam after the first \(a\) coordinates, gives

\[
                         L\ \sim\ P_1^{(a)}S.          \tag{2.17}
\]

For the phase-one edge, MSW concatenation gives

\[
 X_1^{F_s}\bigl((\eta_aP_1^{(a)})R\bigr)
   =X_1^{F_a}(\eta_aP_1^{(a)})R.                      \tag{2.18}
\]

The local state on the right contains its final coordinate \(2a\),
because that is its first inserted coordinate, while \(R\) starts in one.
Thus the global \(\eta_s\) fixes the `11` seam and factors internally.
Using Theorem 2.1 and \(\eta_bR=S\),

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 X_1^{G_s}(L)
  &=\eta_s\!\left(
       X_1^{F_a}(\eta_aP_1^{(a)})R\right)\\
  &=\eta_aX_1^{F_a}(\eta_aP_1^{(a)})\,S\\
  &=X_1^{F_a}(P_0^{(a)})S
   =X_1^{F_s}(P_0^{(a)}S).
 \end{aligned}                                         \tag{2.19}
\]

Hence \(L\sim P_0^{(a)}S\). Combining this with (2.17) proves (2.15).
\(\square\)

### Corollary 2.5 (sharpened exponential component)

For every \(s\ge3\), one full component has at least

\[
 \boxed{2^{s-1}+2^{s-3}=5\cdot2^{s-3}}                \tag{2.20}
\]

roots.

#### Proof

Start with the \(2^{s-1}\) all-horizontal roots from Theorem 2.3. For
each \(a\in\{3,\ldots,s\}\), put \(b=s-a\) and let \(S\) range over
all-horizontal roots in \(D_b\). The root \(P_0^{(a)}S\) is itself
all-horizontal, so Lemma 2.4 places every \(P_1^{(a)}S\) in the same
component.

These added roots are distinct: their Motzkin skeleton has one initial
\(U\), followed by horizontals, and its unique \(D\) is in position
\(a-1\). For \(b\ge1\) there are \(2^{b-1}\) choices of \(S\), while
\(b=0\) contributes one. Hence their number is

\[
                         1+\sum_{b=1}^{s-3}2^{b-1}
                         =2^{s-3}.                    \tag{2.21}
\]

None is all-horizontal, proving (2.20). \(\square\)

### Lemma 2.6 (mirror stability)

Let \(\varrho_s(i)=2s+1-i\) be coordinate reversal and let
\(\mu(P)=\overline{\operatorname {rev}(P)}\) be the Dyck-word mirror.
Then \(P\mapsto\mu(P)\) is an automorphism of the contracted full
\(F_s/G_s\) owner graph. In particular,

\[
                         \mu(K_*)=K_*.                 \tag{2.22}
\]

#### Proof

The MSW recursion for \(\rho\) gives the path-reversal identity

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 X_t^{F_s}(\mu P)&=\varrho_sX_{s-t}^{F_s}(P),\\
 Y_t^{F_s}(\mu P)&=\varrho_sY_{s-1-t}^{F_s}(P).
 \end{aligned}                                         \tag{2.23}
\]

Thus coordinate reversal and phase reversal preserve every canonical
ownership incidence. The permutations \(\eta_s\) and \(\varrho_s\)
commute: reversal merely reverses the list of adjacent pairs
\((2,3),(4,5),\ldots,(2s-2,2s-1)\). They also commute with
complementation. Applying (2.23) inside
\(G_s(P)=\eta_sF_s(\eta_sP)\) proves the same identity for \(G_s\), and
hence an automorphism of the two-shore overlay.

The mirror maps the all-horizontal cube to itself: on Motzkin words it
reverses the step order, exchanges \(U,D\), and preserves each horizontal
colour. Since that cube lies in \(K_*\), the components \(\mu(K_*)\) and
\(K_*\) intersect and are equal. \(\square\)

### Corollary 2.7 (mirror-sharpened packet size)

For every \(s\ge3\),

\[
 \boxed{|K_*|\ge
       2^{s-1}+2^{s-2}-1=3\cdot2^{s-2}-1.}            \tag{2.24}
\]

#### Proof

The extra roots in Corollary 2.5 have Motzkin skeletons

\[
                         UH^{a-3}DH^{s-a},
                         \qquad3\le a\le s.           \tag{2.25}
\]

Their mirrors have skeletons

\[
                         H^{s-a}UH^{a-3}D.             \tag{2.26}
\]

For \(a<s\), a word in (2.25) starts with \(U\), while its mirror in
(2.26) starts with \(H\), so the two families are disjoint. At \(a=s\)
the spanning arch \(UH^{s-3}D\) is common to both. Corollary 2.5 counted
\(2^{s-3}\) roots in the first family. Mirror stability supplies another
\(2^{s-3}-1\), while Theorem 2.3 supplies the disjoint all-horizontal
cube of size \(2^{s-1}\). Summing proves (2.24). \(\square\)

### Corollary 2.8 (diverging component second moment)

If

\[
                         \chi_s={1\over C_s}\sum_K|K|^2,
 \tag{2.27}
\]

where the sum runs over full ownership components, then

\[
 \boxed{
 \chi_s\ge{(3\cdot2^{s-2}-1)^2\over C_s}
       =\left({9\sqrt\pi\over16}+o(1)\right)s^{3/2}.} \tag{2.28}
\]

Consequently, if \(s/\sqrt m\to\kappa\in(0,\infty)\),

\[
 \chi_s\ge
 \left({9\sqrt\pi\kappa^{3/2}\over16}+o(1)\right)m^{3/4}.
 \tag{2.29}
\]

#### Proof

Corollary 2.7 supplies one component of size \(3\cdot2^{s-2}-1\), so its
one summand gives the first inequality. The
Catalan asymptotic

\[
                         C_s\sim{4^s\over\sqrt\pi s^{3/2}}
\]

gives (2.28), and substitution of \(s\sim\kappa\sqrt m\) gives (2.29).
\(\square\)

This divergence rules out any proposed rounding lemma whose hypothesis is
a uniformly bounded unweighted component second moment. It is not by
itself a lower bound for the actual CRH remainder: the carrier vector may
vanish or cancel on a large component, and physical targets from different
contexts may merge.

Likewise, an exact hard packet requirement \(|K|\le p\) for every local
component fails whenever

\[
                         p<3\cdot2^{s-2}-1.            \tag{2.30}
\]

No comparison between \(p\) and \(3\cdot2^{s-2}-1\) is implicit in the
critical scale assumption \(s=\Theta(\sqrt m)\); (2.30) must be checked in the
particular lift.

### Proposition 2.9 (the exponential component is first-boundary active)

Let \(K_*\) be the full component containing the all-horizontal cube. Then

\[
                         \eta_sK_*=K_*.                \tag{2.31}
\]

If \(h_{K_*}^F\) is its canonical first-insertion histogram and
\(\Delta_{K_*}^{\rm FI}\) is the switched-minus-canonical histogram, then

\[
 \boxed{
 {1\over2}\|\Delta_{K_*}^{\rm FI}\|_1
   =\sum_{j=1}^{s-1}h_{K_*}^F(2j)
   \ge2^{s-1}-1.}                                     \tag{2.32}
\]

The same lower bound holds for the last-deletion histogram:

\[
 {1\over2}\|\Delta_{K_*}^{\rm LD}\|_1
                         \ge2^{s-1}-1.                 \tag{2.33}
\]

#### Proof

The involution \(\eta_s\) permutes full components by Proposition 1.2
and maps the set of all-horizontal roots to itself by complementing all
horizontal colours. Hence \(\eta_sK_*\) intersects \(K_*\), so the two
component blocks are equal.

The canonical first-insertion histogram is supported on the even targets
\(2,4,\ldots,2s\). Component stability and (1.5) say that the new
histogram is its \(\eta_s\)-push-forward. For \(j<s\), \(\eta_s\) maps
\(2j\) to the distinct odd target \(2j+1\), while it fixes \(2s\).
Therefore all non-top even entries are lost and reappear on disjoint odd
entries, proving the equality in (2.32).

Among the \(2^{s-1}\) all-horizontal roots, exactly the all-`10` root has
no ground `01` and hence first-return class \(s\). Every other one has
class below \(s\). These roots alone contribute \(2^{s-1}-1\) to the sum,
proving the first-insertion inequality.

By Lemma 2.6, \(K_*\) is also stable under the Dyck mirror. Path reversal
identifies first insertion in the mirrored row with coordinate-reversed
last deletion in the original row. It intertwines both the canonical and
conjugate shores. Therefore

\[
                         \Delta_{K_*}^{\rm LD}
              =\varrho_{s*}\Delta_{K_*}^{\rm FI}
\]

exactly, after reindexing the rows by the bijection \(P\mapsto\mu P\).
Their \(\ell^1\)-norms agree. This proves (2.33). \(\square\)

Thus the certified exponential component is not a zero-action ownership
packet. Proposition 2.9 certifies the two endpoint cuts only; it
does not prove that a positive density of its rows changes
\(\Theta(s)\) positions of the complete rooted coordinate word.

## 3. Exact first-phase component-count ceiling

Let \(M^{(s)}_{jk}\) be the all-block first-return matrix. Its diagonal
below the top class vanishes, and its top diagonal entry is

\[
                         M^{(s)}_{ss}=R_{s-1}.
 \tag{3.1}
\]

The canonical first target of a class-\(j\) root is \(2j\). If
\(\eta_sP\) has class \(k<s\), the conjugate physical target is \(2k+1\);
if \(k=s\), it is \(2s\).

### Theorem 3.1 (singleton and component bounds)

The fixed-point set of \(\sigma_1\) has size at most \(R_{s-1}\).
Consequently

\[
 \begin{aligned}
  \#\{\text{singleton full components}\}&\le R_{s-1},\\
  \#\pi_0(F_s\cup G_s)
   &\le1+{C_s-(3\cdot2^{s-2}-1)+R_{s-1}\over2}\\
   &\le{C_s+R_{s-1}\over2}.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{3.2}
\]

#### Proof

If \(\sigma_1(P)=P\), then

\[
                         X_1^{G_s}(P)=X_1^{F_s}(P).
\]

Both states differ from the common root \(P\) by one deletion and one
insertion, so their inserted coordinates are equal. An even canonical
target \(2j\) can equal an all-block target only in the top case
\(j=k=s\): all targets with \(k<s\) are odd, and the remaining target is
\(2s\). Hence every fixed root lies in the \((s,s)\)-cell, whose size is
\(R_{s-1}\) by (3.1).

A singleton full component must in particular be fixed by its \(X_1\)
edge, proving the first line of (3.2). A permutation on \(C_s\) points
with at most \(R_{s-1}\) fixed points has at most

\[
       R_{s-1}+{C_s-R_{s-1}\over2}
       ={C_s+R_{s-1}\over2}
\]

cycles. Every full component is a union of \(\sigma_1\)-cycles, because
all \(\sigma_1\)-edges occur in the full graph. This proves the second
coarse inequality.

For the sharper inequality, remove the component \(K_*\), of size at
least \(L=3\cdot2^{s-2}-1\). It is a union of \(\sigma_1\)-cycles.
Outside it, let \(f\le R_{s-1}\) be the number of fixed points. The number
of remaining \(\sigma_1\)-cycles is at most

\[
                         f+{C_s-L-f\over2}
                         \le{C_s-L+R_{s-1}\over2}.
\]

Each outside full component is a union of these cycles. Adding \(K_*\)
proves the sharper bound. \(\square\)

Since \(R_{s-1}/C_s\to1/9\), the constants in Section 0 follow.

### Proposition 3.2 (many first-phase cycles)

The number \(c(\sigma_1)\) of cycles of the first \(X\)-owner
permutation obeys

\[
 c(\sigma_1)\ge
 \begin{cases}
   C_{(s-2)/2},&s\text{ even},\\[1mm]
   \left\lceil C_{(s-1)/2}/2\right\rceil,&s\text{ odd}.
 \end{cases}                                             \tag{3.3}
\]

Consequently \(\sigma_1\) is not one \(C_s\)-cycle for every \(s\ge5\).
The numerical cycle bound alone permits one cycle at \(s=5\), but the
sign calculation at the end of the proof excludes it. The bound does not
exclude one cycle at \(s=4\).

#### Proof

Put

\[
                 \beta=i_1^{-1}\eta_si_1.
\]

Then \(\beta\) and \(\eta_s\) are involutions and

\[
 \sigma_1=\beta\eta_s,
 \qquad
 \eta_s\sigma_1\eta_s=\sigma_1^{-1},
 \qquad
 \beta\sigma_1\beta=\sigma_1^{-1}.                  \tag{3.4}
\]

The two-coloured Motzkin encoding shows that a Dyck root is fixed by
\(\eta_s\) precisely when its length-\((s-1)\) Motzkin word has no
horizontal step. Thus

\[
 f_0:=|\operatorname {Fix}_{D_s}(\eta_s)|=
 \begin{cases}
   0,&s\text{ even},\\
   C_{(s-1)/2},&s\text{ odd}.
 \end{cases}
 \tag{3.5}
\]

We also need the fixed points of \(\eta_s\) on the first flaw layer
\(L_1\). Since \(i_1\) is a bijection, their number is exactly
\(f_1=|\operatorname {Fix}(\beta)|\). Pair-fixed balanced words have equal
bits in all internal pairs.

Suppose first that \(s=2r\). The two endpoints agree. If both are zero,
the compressed internal \(U/D\)-word has \(r\) up-steps and \(r-1\)
down-steps. Exactly one original up-step is below the axis precisely when
the compressed word is \(UW\), with \(W\in D_{r-1}\). If both endpoints
are one, the final endpoint up-step supplies the unique flaw precisely
when the compressed word is \(WD\), again with \(W\in D_{r-1}\). Hence

\[
                         f_1=2C_{r-1}\qquad(s=2r).     \tag{3.6}
\]

If \(s=2r+1\), the endpoints are opposite. The initial-one/final-zero
case compresses bijectively to balanced \(U/D\)-words of semilength \(r\)
with exactly one Chung--Feller flaw, of which there are \(C_r\). In the
initial-zero/final-one case the final up-step is already flawed and every
balanced internal word forces at least one further flawed up-step. Thus

\[
                         f_1=C_r\qquad(s=2r+1).        \tag{3.7}
\]

By (3.4), each of \(\eta_s\) and \(\beta\) maps every \(\sigma_1\)-cycle
to a cycle of the same length and acts as a reflection on every cycle
which it stabilizes. A reflection of one cycle has at most two fixed
points. Therefore

\[
                         c(\sigma_1)\ge
             \left\lceil\max(f_0,f_1)/2\right\rceil.  \tag{3.8}
\]

Substitution of (3.5)--(3.7) gives (3.3). For even \(s\ge6\), its lower
bound is at least \(C_2=2\); for odd \(s\ge7\), it is at least
\(\lceil C_3/2\rceil=3\).

It remains only \(s=5\). Here \(C_s=42\) and
\(f_0=f_1=C_2=2\). An involution on \(N\) points with \(f\) fixed points
has sign \((-1)^{(N-f)/2}\). Hence

\[
 \operatorname {sgn}(\sigma_1)
 =(-1)^{(42-2)/2+(42-2)/2}=+1,
\]

whereas one 42-cycle has sign \(-1\). Thus one cycle is impossible also
at \(s=5\). \(\square\)

The fixed-layer argument, rather than a parity shortcut, is necessary.
The involutions \(\eta_s\) on the root layer and on the first flaw layer
can have different signs. Treating \(\sigma_1\) as automatically even
would therefore be erroneous.

## 4. Exact implication boundary for CRH

The uniform crossing theorem eliminates the proposed conjugacy invariant:
one cannot classify the direct components by the peak-erasure normal form,
nor use the conjugated-leaf component generating function as their size
distribution.

On the other hand, (3.2) is only a constant-factor component-count bound.
Corollary 2.7 gives an exponential packet and Corollary 2.8 forces a
diverging unweighted component second moment, but the packet still has
exponentially vanishing density in \(D_s\). There is no packet-size upper
bound and no positive-density lower bound on one component. The following
remain unproved:

* transitivity of the group generated by all \(\sigma_t\);
* redundancy or component-merging power of the noninjective \(Y\)-owner
  relations;
* an \(o(C_s)\) bound on the number of full components;
* a \(p\)-bound on every component at the critical local scale; and
* positive-density full row-word activity inside the actual component
  packets.

Therefore neither side of the CRH dichotomy is presently certified. If
the full overlay is connected in the critical lift and physical context
targets remain recoverable, the one-bit mean-versus-remainder inequality
gives a sharp no-go. If the overlay fragments, Theorem 1.1 and (1.5), not
the peak-erasure graph, are the exact data which must be rounded. The
large component already rules out any argument requiring uniformly bounded
unweighted component second moment, but actual CRH depends on its
targetwise carrier vector and on cross-context collisions. The all-block
construction is a legitimate critical candidate but does not yet prove the
constant-one theorem.
