# The affine-group obstruction to exact middle wreath resolution

Date: 2026-07-25

Method: pure mathematics only.  No computation, solver, or web input is
used.

## 0. Result

Let

\[
 p=2m+1
\]

be prime, let

\[
 G=\operatorname{AGL}(1,p)
   =\{x\mapsto ux+v:u\in\mathbb F_p^\times,
                         v\in\mathbb F_p\},
\]

and let

\[
 W=\binom pm,\qquad B=W/p=\operatorname{Cat}_m.
\]

The arithmetic-progression rows form one (G)-orbit of (m) rows and
are a genuine middle packing.  In fact, throughout every fixed Gaussian
window their lower and upper interval shadows are globally rainbow inside
this whole affine seed.

They cannot, however, be completed to an exact (G)-invariant wreath
factor once (p\ge7).  There are two independent exact obstructions.

1. The possible unoriented-row orbit sizes are

   \[
   m,\qquad pm,\qquad 2pm.
   \]

   The first size occurs only for the single AP orbit.  Hence every
   invariant row family has size

   \[
   \varepsilon m+pmk,qquad \varepsilon\in\{0,1\}.
   \]

   This is incompatible with

   \[
   B\equiv2(-1)^m\pmod p
   \]

   for every prime (p\ge7).

2. Every pointed wreath occurrence has affine stabilizer of order at most
   two.  The middle set (Q\) of nonzero quadratic residues has stabilizer
   of order (m) and affine orbit of size (2p).  Thus no invariant
   middle packing can cover (Q), or any of its (2p) affine images.
   The same set also forbids a fully affine-equivariant integral first
   deletion.

More strongly, an exact (G)-invariant (t)-fold middle wreath multicover
can exist only if

\[
 \begin{cases}
  t\ge m/2,&m\text{ even},\\
  t\ge2m,&m\text{ odd},
 \end{cases}
\]

and the first permitted values are only arithmetic possibilities, not
existence assertions.  Thus bounded-multiplicity exact affine resolution
is impossible.

These obstructions do **not** rule out an affine-invariant near-factor.
Their forced one-cover defect is polynomial, hence (o(W)), even after it
is charged through (O_A(\sqrt m)) depths.  The surviving problem is an
actual large-orbit packing/completion theorem, not an orbit-divisibility
problem.

## 1. The AP orbit is a globally rainbow seed

For (d\in\mathbb F_p^\times), let (C_d) be the unoriented cyclic order

\[
 (0,d,2d,\ldots,(p-1)d).
\tag{1.1}
\]

Rotation removes an additive starting point and reversal identifies (d)
with (-d).  Hence there are (m=(p-1)/2) such rows.

### Lemma 1.1 — affine AP intervals are unique

Fix (2\le r\le m), and put

\[
 I_r=\{0,1,\ldots,r-1\}\subseteq\mathbb F_p.
\]

If

\[
 a+dI_r=b+eI_r,qquad d,e\ne0,
\tag{1.2}
\]

then (e=\pm d).  Consequently, as (d) ranges modulo sign and (a)
ranges over (mathbb F_p), the (pm) sets (a+dI_r) are all distinct.

#### Proof

For (t\ne0), let

\[
 \Delta_r(t)=|I_r\cap(I_r+t)|.
\]

Because (r\le(p-1)/2), there is no wraparound ambiguity at the top
overlap: the largest nonzero value of (Delta_r(t)) is (r-1), attained
exactly at (t=1) and (t=-1).  An affine self-map (x\mapsto sx+c) of
(I_r) must preserve this difference multiplicity, and therefore sends
the pair ({1,-1}) to itself.  Thus (s=\pm1).  Applying this after
normalizing (1.2) gives (e/d=\pm1).  The remaining translation is the
identity when the sign is (+), and the reversal (x\mapsto r-1-x) when
the sign is (-).  \(square\)

Thus the complete AP orbit is a middle packing of (m) rows, covering
exactly (pm) owners.  At every lower depth (q\) with
(m-q\ge2), all its (p m) lower-shadow occurrences are distinct.
Complementation gives the same statement for the upper shadow.  In
particular this is true uniformly for (q\le A\sqrt m), for every fixed
(A) and all sufficiently large (m).

Its size is nevertheless negligible:

\[
 \frac mB\longrightarrow0,
 \qquad
 \frac{pm}{W}\longrightarrow0.
\tag{1.3}
\]

## 2. Exact stabilizers and row-orbit sizes

A row means an unoriented cyclic order modulo rotation and reversal.

### Lemma 2.1 — row-stabilizer classification

Let (C) be a row and (G_C\) its stabilizer in (G).

* If (C) is AP, then

  \[
  |G_C|=2p.
  \tag{2.1}
  \]

* If (C) is not AP, then

  \[
  |G_C|\in\{1,2\}.
  \tag{2.2}
  \]

The order-two case is generated by an affine reflection
(x\mapsto -x+v).

#### Proof

Every element of (G_C) induces a dihedral automorphism of the (p)
cyclic positions.  The orientation-preserving kernel acts by rotations.
A nonidentity rotation of a (p)-cycle has order (p), so the
corresponding affine map has order (p).  The unique Sylow-(p) subgroup
of (G) is the translation group.  Hence a nontrivial
orientation-preserving stabilizer contains a nonzero translation.

If translation by (b\ne0) acts on the positions by rotation through
(s\ne0), a cyclic representative (z_i) satisfies

\[
 z_{i+s}=z_i+b.
\]

Iteration shows that (z_i) is an arithmetic progression.  Thus a
non-AP row has trivial orientation-preserving stabilizer.  Its full
stabilizer maps injectively to the two orientation signs and consequently
has order at most two.

For (C=C_d), the map (x\mapsto ux+v) sends (C_d) to (C_{ud}).
This is the same unoriented row exactly when (u=\pm1), while every
translation (v) merely rotates it.  Hence the AP stabilizer has order
(2p).  \(square\)

Since (|G|=p(p-1)=2pm), the row orbits have sizes

\[
 \boxed{
 |GC|=
 \begin{cases}
 m,&C\text{ AP},\\
 pm,&C\text{ non-AP and reflection-symmetric},\\
 2pm,&C\text{ generic}.
 \end{cases}}
\tag{2.3}
\]

All AP rows form the one orbit in the first line.

### Corollary 2.2 — invariant row-count lattice

Every (G)-invariant set of rows has cardinality

\[
 R=\varepsilon m+pmk,
 \qquad \varepsilon\in\{0,1\},\quad k\ge0.
\tag{2.4}
\]

Here (arepsilon=1) exactly when the AP orbit is selected.

## 3. No exact affine-invariant factor

### Lemma 3.1 — Catalan residue

For (p=2m+1) prime,

\[
 \boxed{B=\operatorname{Cat}_m\equiv2(-1)^m\pmod p.}
\tag{3.1}
\]

#### Proof

We have

\[
 B=\frac1{m+1}\binom{p-1}{m}.
\]

The product formula gives
(inom{p-1}{m}\equiv(-1)^m\pmod p), while
(2(m+1)=p+1\equiv1\pmod p).  Thus
((m+1)^{-1}\equiv2\pmod p).  \(square\)

### Theorem 3.2 — exact row-orbit obstruction

For every prime (p=2m+1\ge7), there is no (G)-invariant exact middle
wreath factor.

The only prime cases in which such a factor exists are (p=3) and
(p=5); there the AP orbit itself is exact.

#### Proof

An exact factor has (B) rows.  By (2.4), invariance would give

\[
 B\equiv\varepsilon m\pmod p,
 \qquad \varepsilon\in\{0,1\}.
\tag{3.2}
\]

The choice (arepsilon=0) is impossible because (3.1) is nonzero.  If
(arepsilon=1), then

\[
 2(-1)^m\equiv m\pmod p.
\]

Since (2m\equiv-1\pmod p), this is equivalent to

\[
 4(-1)^m\equiv-1\pmod p.
\tag{3.3}
\]

For even (m), (3.3) forces (p\mid5), hence (p=5).  For odd (m),
it forces (p\mid3), hence (p=3).  Lemma 1.1 shows directly that the
AP orbit is exact in these two small cases.  \(square\)

## 4. Near-factor congruences

Let an invariant partial factor have (B-b) rows.  Corollary 2.2 gives
the exact congruence

\[
 \boxed{b\equiv B-\varepsilon m\pmod{pm}.}
\tag{4.1}
\]

In particular, if the AP seed is retained, then

\[
 b\equiv
 \begin{cases}
  m-1\pmod p,&m\text{ odd},\\
  m+3\pmod p,&m\text{ even and }m>2.
 \end{cases}
\tag{4.2}
\]

If the AP orbit is omitted, then

\[
 b\equiv
 \begin{cases}
  2m-1\pmod p,&m\text{ odd},\\
  2\pmod p,&m\text{ even}.
 \end{cases}
\tag{4.3}
\]

Consequently every invariant partial factor satisfies

\[
 b\ge
 \begin{cases}
  m-1,&m\text{ odd},\\
  2,&m\text{ even and }m>2,
 \end{cases}
\tag{4.4}
\]

and an AP-containing one satisfies the stronger bounds displayed in
(4.2).  The number of uncovered middle owners is exactly (pb).

Let

\[
 \delta_\varepsilon=[B-\varepsilon m]_{pm}
\]

be the least nonnegative residue.  Pure orbit arithmetic permits a row
defect (delta_\varepsilon<pm=O(m^2)); this sentence is only a numerical
compatibility statement and does not construct the required packing.
Even if every omitted row is charged at every one of
(H=O_A(\sqrt m)) depths, the resulting arithmetic-scale occurrence
budget is

\[
 Hp\delta_\varepsilon=O_A(m^{7/2})=o(W).
\tag{4.5}
\]

Thus divisibility kills exact completion but not the desired (o(W))
near-completion scale.

## 5. Pointed owner orbits

A pointed occurrence is a pair ((C,X)), where (X) is one of the (p)
middle intervals of (C).

### Lemma 5.1 — pointed stabilizers

For every pointed occurrence,

\[
 \boxed{|G_{C,X}|\le2.}
\tag{5.1}
\]

More precisely:

* the AP pointed occurrences form one orbit of size (pm), with
  stabilizer two;
* a reflection-symmetric non-AP row orbit contributes one pointed orbit
  of size (pm) and (m) pointed orbits of size (2pm);
* a generic row orbit contributes (p) pointed orbits, all of size
  (2pm).

#### Proof

For an AP row, its stabilizer is the dihedral group of order (2p) acting
transitively on the (p) interval starts; a point stabilizer has order
two.  For a non-AP row, (5.1) follows from Lemma 2.1.  In the
reflection-symmetric case, the reflection acts on the odd set of (p)
interval starts with one fixed start and (m) pairs.  In the generic case
all starts have trivial stabilizer.  Orbit-stabilizer gives the listed
sizes.  \(square\)

In particular every affine orbit of pointed occurrences has size (pm)
or (2pm).  Hence an invariant selection of arbitrary pointed
occurrences, even without the whole-row condition, has cardinality
divisible by (pm).  An exact pointed one-cover would therefore require

\[
 m\mid B.
\tag{5.2}
\]

The next obstruction applies even when this divisibility happens to hold.

## 6. The quadratic-residue owner obstruction

Let

\[
 Q=\{x^2:x\in\mathbb F_p^\times\}.
\tag{6.1}
\]

Then (|Q|=m).  Regard (Q) also as the subgroup of square multipliers.

### Lemma 6.1 — exact stabilizer and orbit of (Q)

For (m\ge2),

\[
 \boxed{\operatorname{Stab}_G(Q)
   =\{x\mapsto ux:u\in Q\},}
\tag{6.2}
\]

so

\[
 \boxed{|GQ|=2p.}
\tag{6.3}
\]

#### Proof

Square multipliers give a subgroup (H\le\operatorname{Stab}_G(Q)) of
order (m).  Put (K=\operatorname{Stab}_G(Q)).  If (p\mid|K|), then
the unique Sylow-(p) subgroup of (G), namely all translations, lies in
(K).  A nonempty proper subset of (mathbb F_p) cannot be invariant
under every translation.  Hence (p\nmid|K|).

Every (p')-subgroup of the affine group has a common fixed point.  One
elementary proof is to average the orbit of (0): since (|K|) is
invertible in (mathbb F_p),

\[
 c=\frac1{|K|}\sum_{g\in K}g(0)
\]

is fixed by (K).  Because (H\le K) and a nonidentity square
multiplier fixes only (0), this common fixed point is (0).  Therefore
every element of (K) is a pure multiplier.  Such a multiplier preserves
(Q) exactly when its slope belongs to (Q).  Thus (K=H), and
orbit-stabilizer gives (6.3).  \(square\)

### Theorem 6.2 — mandatory owner holes

Let (mathcal F) be a (G)-invariant middle wreath packing.  If a middle
set (X) is covered by the unique row (Cin\mathcal F), then

\[
 \operatorname{Stab}_G(X)le\operatorname{Stab}_G(C,X),
\tag{6.4}
\]

and consequently

\[
 |\operatorname{Stab}_G(X)|\le2.
\tag{6.5}
\]

In particular (mathcal F) leaves every member of (GQ) uncovered, so
it has at least

\[
 \boxed{2p}
\tag{6.6}
\]

mandatory middle-owner holes.

#### Proof

If (gX=X), then (gCin\mathcal F) is another selected row covering
(X).  Packing uniqueness forces (gC=C).  Thus (g) fixes the pointed
occurrence ((C,X)), which proves (6.4).  Lemma 5.1 gives (6.5), while
Lemma 6.1 has (|\operatorname{Stab}_G(Q)|=m>2) for (p\ge7).  Invariance
then omits the whole orbit (GQ), of size (2p).  \(square\)

This proves exact noncompletion at the owner level, independently of the
row-count congruence.

## 7. No fully affine-equivariant deletion flag

### Theorem 7.1 — selector obstruction

For (p\ge7), there is no (G)-equivariant map

\[
 d:\binom{\mathbb F_p}m\longrightarrow\mathbb F_p,
 \qquad d(X)\in X.
\tag{7.1}
\]

Hence there is no fully (G)-equivariant integral nested deletion
resolution on all middle owners.

Any (G)-invariant domain on which such a selector is defined omits all
(2p) members of (GQ).

#### Proof

The square-multiplier group (H=Q) acts regularly, in particular
transitively and without a global fixed element, on (Q).  Equivariance
would give

\[
 d(Q)=d(hQ)=h,d(Q)
 \qquad(h\in H).
\]

This asks for an (H)-fixed element of (Q), which does not exist.  The
same argument applies to every affine image of (Q).  A nested deletion
resolution supplies (7.1) at its first step, so it is also impossible.
\(square\)

## 8. Exact invariant multicovers require growing multiplicity

Allow rows to occur with multiplicity and suppose every middle set has
load exactly (t\).  Invariance makes the multiplicity of every AP row a
common integer (k).  Since the AP rows are a middle packing,

\[
 0\le k\le t.
\tag{8.1}
\]

### Lemma 8.1 — global row-count congruence

Every invariant exact (t)-fold multicover satisfies

\[
 \boxed{tB\equiv km\pmod p,}
\tag{8.2}
\]

or equivalently

\[
 \boxed{k\equiv-4(-1)^m t\pmod p.}
\tag{8.3}
\]

#### Proof

Counting middle incidences gives (tW=ptB), so the multicover contains
exactly (tB) rows with multiplicity.  Every non-AP row orbit has size
divisible by (p), while the AP orbit contributes (km) rows.  This
proves (8.2).  Use (3.1) and (m^{-1}\equiv-2\pmod p) for (8.3).
\(square\)

### Lemma 8.2 — local pointed-fibre divisibility

Every invariant exact (t)-fold multicover satisfies

\[
 \boxed{
 \begin{cases}
  m\mid t,&m\text{ odd},\\
  m/2\mid t,&m\text{ even}.
 \end{cases}}
\tag{8.4}
\]

#### Proof

Look at the multiset of selected pointed occurrences lying over (Q).
It has total multiplicity (t) and is invariant under
(H=\operatorname{Stab}_G(Q)), of order (m).  By Lemma 5.1, the
stabilizer in (H) of any pointed occurrence has order at most two.
Thus, when (m) is odd, every (H)-orbit in the fibre has size (m).
When (m) is even, every such orbit has size (m) or (m/2).  Summing
the orbit sizes with their invariant multiplicities proves (8.4).
\(square\)

### Theorem 8.3 — multiplicity barrier

For (p\ge7), an invariant exact (t)-fold middle wreath multicover can
exist only if

\[
 \boxed{
 t\ge
 \begin{cases}
  2m=p-1,&m\text{ odd},\\
  m/2=(p-1)/4,&m\text{ even}.
 \end{cases}}
\tag{8.5}
\]

#### Proof

If (m) is even, (8.4) makes the least positive candidate (t=m/2).
At this value (8.3) permits (k=1), so the two exact arithmetic
conditions do not force a larger value.

If (m) is odd, (8.4) makes the first candidate (t=m).  Equation (8.3)
then gives

\[
 k\equiv4m\equiv p-2\pmod p.
\]

But (0\le k\le t=m<p-2), impossible.  The next multiple allowed by
(8.4) is (t=2m); at that value (8.3) permits (k=p-4\le2m).  Hence
the necessary lower bound is (2m).  No existence is asserted at either
boundary value.  \(square\)

Thus taking a bounded number of affine orbits, or a bounded-load affine
multicover and hoping to resolve it afterward, cannot give a coefficient-
one middle skeleton.

## 9. Exact boundary of the obstruction

The results above rule out:

1. an exact (G)-invariant wreath factor for (p\ge7);
2. a full (G)-equivariant integral deletion flag;
3. an exact affine multicover of bounded middle multiplicity;
4. an affine-invariant packing with fewer than (2p) owner holes, and the
   stronger row-congruence holes of Section 4 when whole rows are used.

They do not rule out a family with (o(W)) middle holes and (o(W)) total
shallow-shadow defect through a fixed Gaussian window.  The compulsory
quadratic-residue orbit has only (2p) owners.  The least arithmetic row
residue is below (pm), and its worst all-depth charge in (4.5) is still
(o(W)).  More generally, subsets with affine stabilizer larger than two
form an exponentially negligible portion of the middle layer: a
nontranslation affine element of order (d\ge3) has one fixed point and
((p-1)/d) cycles, hence fixes at most

\[
 2^{1+(p-1)/3}
\]

subsets, and a union bound over (O(p^2)) affine maps is (o(W)).

Accordingly, the exact conclusion is:

\[
 \boxed{
 \begin{minipage}{0.84\linewidth}
 Full affine equivariance is arithmetically and stabilizer-theoretically
 impossible.  Orbit arithmetic alone leaves only a polynomial exceptional
 set, so it does not obstruct an affine-invariant near-factor with
 (o(W)) Gaussian-window defect.  Constructing such a near-factor still
 requires a genuine integral packing of exponentially many compatible
 non-AP row orbits.
 \end{minipage}}
\]
