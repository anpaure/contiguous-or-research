## 0. Outcome

Put

\[
 n=2m,\qquad R=2r+1=m-q_0,
 \qquad h=m-2r=q_0+1=\Theta(\sqrt m),
\tag{0.1}
\]

and work in the simple domino-twin catalogue.  A packet is an
unoriented cyclic ordered perfect matching of \([n]\), written as a
necklace of \(m\) unordered dominoes.

The critical half-exponent family independently repartitions two
length-\(\ell\) position intervals \(I,I+r\).  The proposed response is
to contract or canonically choose within these paired-corridor families.
The exact audit is negative for the natural equivalence-class route.

### Theorem A (paired-corridor transitive closure is universal)

Fix any \(\ell\ge2\).  For every cyclic interval \(I\) of length
\(\ell\), declare two packets locally related when:

1. they agree outside \(I\cup(I+r)\);
2. each of the two segments keeps its own label set; and
3. inside the two segments the labels may be independently repartitioned
   into ordered unordered dominoes.

The equivalence relation generated by these local relations has one
class: every simple domino necklace is equivalent to every other one.
Consequently the global paired-corridor quotient has shell exponent zero
only because it collapses the whole catalogue.  Choosing one canonical
representative leaves one packet, not a near-factor-capable quotient.

### Theorem B (sparse canonical corridors leave the half exponent)

If one avoids the universal closure by contracting only a fixed family
\({\cal A}\) of corridor starts, then every untranslated start \(i\)
supports the original double-segment family on

\[
                         I_i\cup(I_i+r).
\]

In particular, if the chosen corridor intervals and their \(r\)-mates
cover fewer than all cyclic starts, the quotient still has a shell with
exponent \(1/2-o(1)\).  More quantitatively, if
\(|{\cal A}|\ell=o(m)\), a translate disjoint from every contracted
corridor exists, and its entire half-exponent family survives injectively
in the quotient.

Thus there is a contraction-versus-coverage dichotomy:

\[
 \boxed{
 \begin{array}{c}
 \text{contract all translated critical corridors}
       \Longrightarrow\text{one universal class},\\
 \text{contract a sparse compatible subfamily}
       \Longrightarrow\text{a surviving }1/2-o(1)\text{ shell}.
 \end{array}}
\tag{0.2}
\]

### Theorem C (the critical incidence is not statically small)

For every entrance incidence \(X\in F\), and every
\(1\ll\ell=o(m)\), there is a nontrivial paired-corridor neighbour
\(G\ni X\) with

\[
 |F\setminus G|le8\ell+4h+O(1).
\tag{0.3}
\]

Hence every raw packet incidence lies in a critical corridor.  Under the
uniform fractional perfect matching the total critical entrance-incidence
mass is exactly the full entrance mass \(N_R=\Theta(W)\), not \(o(W)\).
Likewise any entrance near-factor covers \(N_R-o(N_R)=\Theta(W)\)
incidences using packets which all belong to nontrivial critical classes.

If “critical incidence” is instead defined to mean a pair of critical
neighbours which are simultaneously retained, ordinary conflict
thinning makes that incidence zero, but pays the full half-exponent
degree loss.  This change of definition does not provide a quotient
near-factor.

### Exact consequence

The exact joint local-minimum probability remains useful once a genuine
partition into moderate clusters is available:

\[
 \rho_Q^2\le
 \Pr(C,D\text{ both retained})\le2\rho_Q^2.
\tag{0.4}
\]

It would prevent a second entropy payment on a quotient conflict graph.
The obstruction is prior to (0.4): the critical paired corridors form an
overlapping cover, not a moderate equivalence partition.  The current
inputs therefore prove neither a quotient shell exponent below \(1/2\)
nor an \(o(W)\) stopped-incidence theorem.

The only live cluster-aware route is a bounded-overlap, non-equivalence
cover together with a trajectory theorem assigning each actually live
critical pair to one cluster.  Such an assignment is not supplied by
raw corridor geometry or by static local-minimum thinning.

## 1. Paired-corridor families

Let

\[
                         B=(B_0,B_1,\ldots,B_{m-1})
\tag{1.1}
\]

be an ordered cyclic list of unordered two-sets partitioning \([n]\).
For a cyclic interval \(I\subset\mathbb Z_m\) of length \(\ell\), put

\[
                         J=I+r.
\tag{1.2}
\]

When \(\ell+h<r\), the intervals are disjoint.  The local paired
corridor through \(B\) consists of all words obtained by:

- keeping every block outside \(I\cup J\) unchanged;
- keeping the union of labels on \(I\) and the union of labels on \(J\)
  separately fixed; and
- replacing the blocks on \(I\), and independently those on \(J\), by
  arbitrary ordered perfect matchings of their respective label sets.

The word “independently” is load-bearing.  The identity repartition is
allowed on either segment.  Indeed the critical family counted in the
double-segment theorem is the Cartesian product of the two full
repartition sets and contains its base packet.

One such local family is a legitimate cluster.  The collection over all
packets and all translated intervals is not a partition: a packet belongs
to many of them, and two clusters overlap whenever their supporting
segments overlap or one repartition is the identity on the overlap.

## 2. Local paired corridors contain every adjacent switch

We first record two elementary moves on ordered domino lists.

### Lemma 2.1 (adjacent block transposition)

For every \(i\), the move

\[
 (\ldots,B_i,B_{i+1},\ldots)
 \longmapsto
 (\ldots,B_{i+1},B_i,\ldots)
\tag{2.1}
\]

belongs to one paired-corridor relation.

#### Proof

Choose a length-\(\ell\) interval containing \(i,i+1\).  On its first
segment use the ordered repartition which only interchanges these two
dominoes and fixes the other \(\ell-2\) blocks.  On the \(r\)-translated
segment use the identity repartition.  All defining conditions in
Section 1 hold. \(\square\)

### Lemma 2.2 (adjacent two-domino re-pairing)

If

\[
                         B_i=\{a,b\},\qquad
                         B_{i+1}=\{c,d\},
\]

then either switch

\[
 (\{a,b\},\{c,d\})
 \longmapsto
 (\{a,c\},\{b,d\})
\tag{2.2}
\]

or

\[
 (\{a,b\},\{c,d\})
 \longmapsto
 (\{a,d\},\{b,c\})
\tag{2.3}
\]

belongs to one paired-corridor relation.

#### Proof

Use the same interval as in Lemma 2.1.  Repartition only the four
displayed labels on its first segment, fix every other block there, and
use the identity on the translated segment. \(\square\)

Thus every adjacent switch is already present for any fixed
\(\ell\ge2\); one does not need to pass to small geometric corridors.

## 3. Connectivity of ordered perfect matchings

### Lemma 3.1 (switch connectivity)

The graph of ordered perfect matchings of \([n]\), with moves
(2.1)--(2.3), is connected.

#### Proof

Fix a target ordered perfect matching

\[
                         T_0,T_1,\ldots,T_{m-1}.
\]

Starting from any ordered perfect matching, construct the target from
left to right.  Suppose positions before \(k\) already equal their
targets, and write \(T_k=\{a,c\}\).  The current dominoes containing
\(a,c\) occur at positions at least \(k\).  Use adjacent block
transpositions to bring them to positions \(k,k+1\).  If they are
already paired together, move that block to position \(k\).  Otherwise
write the two blocks as \(\{a,b\},\{c,d\}\), and apply (2.2) to create
\(\{a,c\}\).  Put it at position \(k\), and continue on the remaining
labels.

After \(m-1\) steps every target block and its order have been obtained.
Hence the move graph is connected. \(\square\)

Rotation and reversal only identify vertices of this connected graph.
Therefore its quotient by the global dihedral action is also connected.

### Proof of Theorem A

Lemmas 2.1--3.1 show that the transitive closure of the paired-corridor
relations connects all ordered domino words.  Passing to simple packet
supports only divides by the dihedral action and cannot disconnect the
relation.  Hence there is one equivalence class. \(\square\)

This is not merely a statement that some classes are large.  It says
that **every** additive invariant of the proposed equivalence relation
is constant on the full simple catalogue.  There is no nontrivial class
label on which to run a quotient shell theorem.

## 4. Why a sparse canonical subfamily does not suffice

One can avoid Theorem A by selecting, in advance, only some corridor
starts.  Let \({\cal A}\subseteq\mathbb Z_m\) be those starts, and
contract only the local families supported on

\[
                         I_a\cup(I_a+r),\qquad a\in{\cal A}.
\tag{4.1}
\]

The union of their position supports has size at most

\[
                         2\ell|{\cal A}|.
\tag{4.2}
\]

If this is \(o(m)\), choose a start \(i\) whose two segments
\(I_i,I_i+r\) avoid that union.  Independently repartitioning these two
segments changes no contracted corridor.  Therefore distinct members of
the double-segment family remain in distinct quotient states.  The exact
count and defect audit from the source theorem gives

\[
 \log |{\cal F}_\ell|
 =4\ell\log\ell+O(\ell+\log m),
\]

\[
 s_\ell=8\ell+4\min\{\ell,h\}+O(1).
\tag{4.3}
\]

For \(h=o(\ell)=o(m)\), its quotient exponent is still

\[
                         {1\over2}-o(1).
\tag{4.4}
\]

This proves the quantitative clause of Theorem B.

More generally, every untranslated corridor leaves a critical family.
Covering all translations forces overlapping contracted families.  If
one then closes those overlaps transitively, Section 3 applies and the
quotient is universal.  A nontransitive choice of one representative in
each overlapping local family is not well-defined: one packet may be the
chosen representative of one cluster and a discarded nonrepresentative
of another.

There is a symmetry version of the same wall.  A coordinate-equivariant
rule cannot select one rooted corridor from an unrooted transitive packet
orbit without adding extra root data.  A label-rooted rule is possible,
but it treats only that rooted corridor; choose the double-segment
modification away from its \(O(\ell)\) rooted support and (4.4) remains.

## 5. What a genuine quotient would gain

Suppose, counterfactually, that the packet set had a partition
\({\mathscr P}\) into moderate paired-corridor classes, and that the
resulting class-conflict graph were \(\Delta_Q\)-regular.  Give classes
independent priorities and retain a class when it is the minimum in its
closed class neighbourhood.  Put

\[
                         \rho_Q={1\over\Delta_Q+1}.
\]

For two nonadjacent classes \(C,D\), the exact local-minimum integral
gives

\[
 \boxed{
 \rho_Q^2\le
 \Pr(C,D\text{ retained})
 \le {2\over(\Delta_Q+1)(\Delta_Q+2)}
 \le2\rho_Q^2.}
\tag{5.1}
\]

Hence every quotient shell would be reduced proportionally in
incidence expectation, and normalization by the quotient entrance
degree would cancel \(\rho_Q\).  This is exactly the desired
no-double-entropy mechanism.

For the actual all-corridor equivalence relation, however,
\({\mathscr P}\) has one member.  Choosing one packet from it leaves
one \(K\)-target edge and zero degree at almost every entrance target.
Treating the entire class as one superedge instead gives a superedge
whose support is essentially the whole entrance layer, so it is not a
bounded-rank matching object.  Formula (5.1) has no useful graph on
which to act.

For a sparse corridor partition, (5.1) is valid inside the chosen
classes, but the half-exponent family in Section 4 lies between classes.
Thus the exact joint probability is not the missing step; the missing
step is a moderate, shell-covering partition.

## 6. Critical incidence is full at time zero

Fix an entrance target \(X\in F\).  It lies in one cell
\({\cal S}_j(F)\).  For a paired interval start \(i\), the only cells
which can change lie in three bands of total size

\[
                         2(\ell-1)+\min\{\ell-1,h\}+4=o(m).
\tag{6.1}
\]

As \(i\) ranges over \(\mathbb Z_m\), choose it so that none of these
bands contains \(j\).  Perform a nonidentity repartition on the two
segments.  The cell \({\cal S}_j\) remains literally unchanged, so the
resulting packet \(G\) contains \(X\).  It is distinct from \(F\) and
obeys (0.3).

Therefore every raw entrance incidence \((X,F)\) has a critical
paired-corridor neighbour in the same entrance link.

The simple catalogue is regular, so assigning weight \(1/D\) to every
packet gives the uniform fractional perfect matching.  Its total
critical incidence is

\[
 \sum_F{1\over D}|F|
 ={1\over D}\sum_Xd(X)
 =N_R.
\tag{6.2}

At the Gaussian entrance \(N_R=\Theta(W)\).  Thus no static
incidence-weighted deletion of all critical-class packets has
\(o(W)\) cost.

Likewise, if an integral packet matching has leave \(L=o(N_R)\), its
selected packets contain exactly

\[
                         N_R-L=\Theta(W)
\tag{6.3}
\]

entrance incidences, and every selected packet belongs to the universal
critical equivalence class.  Marking packets by raw class membership
therefore marks the whole near-factor, not an exceptional part of it.

This does not prove that a critical neighbour of a selected packet is
still live at its selection time.  Establishing that stronger stopped
statement would require the current-residual hazard information absent
from all static shell counts.  Conversely, no static input proves that
the co-live critical incidence is \(o(W)\).

## 7. Exact boundary

Proved:

1. all translated paired-corridor relations generate one equivalence
   class;
2. the generating moves are only adjacent block swaps and adjacent
   two-domino re-pairings;
3. sparse fixed corridor contractions leave a quotient shell of exponent
   \(1/2-o(1)\);
4. the exact class-level joint local-minimum probability would avoid a
   second entropy payment if a moderate partition existed; and
5. every raw entrance incidence is critical, giving total fractional
   critical mass \(N_R=\Theta(W)\).

Not proved:

1. a bounded-overlap non-equivalence cluster cover with a canonical
   live-pair assignment;
2. an exponent below \(1/2\) after such an assignment;
3. an \(o(W)\) bound for co-live critical incidence along the stopped
   process; or
4. a domino-twin near-factor.

The natural equivalence-class pivot is therefore closed negatively.
The only surviving cluster-aware formulation must be dynamic and
nontransitive: assign each live critical pair to one of its overlapping
corridors, then prove that the selected-intensity mass of assigned
clusters is \(o(W)\) or that their quotient shell has a strict exponent
gain.  Static contraction cannot supply that assignment.
