## 0. Verdict

**Pass with an exact scope qualification.**

On the language retaining all local PBBS word identities between two
phases, the two seam factorizations are equivalent by free-monoid
cancellation.  At either phase, the complete terminal first-passage word
accepts every record state generated by that phase's renewal segment.
Therefore the pullback intersection of the two reset sink sets is the
entire exact transition language.

Consequently:

\[
 \boxed{
 \mathbf1_{A_t\cap A_u}
 =\mathbf1_{A_t}
 =\mathbf1_{A_u}}
\]

on the exact common-base language, and the joint reset condition has
exactly the same coefficientwise mass as either single reset condition.
There is no subcritical intersection and no second reciprocal-height
factor.

For a common renewal atomization, the joint maximum-record Green function
telescopes to the scalar factor \((1-\rho)^{-1}\).  For an interlaced
atomization the product-poset formula is unavailable, but the reset
predicates still remove no exact words.  Any two-phase gain must occur
**before** reset acceptance, by proving that few formal phase-\(t\)
corridors admit all transported phase-\(u\) PBBS identities.

The scope qualification is that the resulting \(\Theta(s)\) mass is
proved for the common-atom/free-corridor relaxation.  It is not proved
for the full actual two-phase PBBS compatibility language; that language
might already be sparse.

## 1. Adjacent-phase seam equivalence

Retain

\[
 Q_{j+1}=S_j1Q_j,\qquad
 V_j=V_{j+1}1\overline T_j,
\]

and

\[
 S_j1P_j=P_{j+1}1\overline T_j.
\]

Let

\[
 E_j:\quad P_j=Q_jV_j.
\]

If \(E_j\) holds, then

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 P_{j+1}1\overline T_j
 &=S_j1P_j\\
 &=S_j1Q_jV_j\\
 &=Q_{j+1}V_{j+1}1\overline T_j.
 \end{aligned}
\]

Right cancellation gives \(E_{j+1}\).

Conversely, if \(E_{j+1}\) holds, then

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 S_j1P_j
 &=P_{j+1}1\overline T_j\\
 &=Q_{j+1}V_{j+1}1\overline T_j\\
 &=S_j1Q_jV_j.
 \end{aligned}
\]

Left cancellation gives \(E_j\).  Thus

\[
 \boxed{E_j\Longleftrightarrow E_{j+1}},
\]

and iteration gives

\[
 \boxed{E_t\Longleftrightarrow E_u.}
 \tag{1.1}
\]

The extra “right-cancel” phrase in the converse proof of the attack
report is unnecessary after the displayed substitutions, but the result
is correct.

For every nonnegative length weight \(w(\xi)\),

\[
 \sum_\xi w(\xi)\mathbf1_{E_t(\xi)}\mathbf1_{E_u(\xi)}
 =\sum_\xi w(\xi)\mathbf1_{E_t(\xi)}.
\]

Taking \(w(\xi)=x^{|\xi|}\) makes this coefficientwise.  Phase transport
is a length-preserving bijective word update, so the corresponding
phase-\(t\) and phase-\(u\) enumerators agree.

## 2. The terminal equation is transported, not independent

The one-step tail equation is

\[
 \overline T_j0R_j=R_{j+1}0S_{j+1}.
\]

Induction from \(j=t\) through \(u-1\) yields

\[
 \boxed{
 (\overline T_{u-1}0)\cdots(\overline T_t0)R_t
 =R_u(0S_u)\cdots(0S_{t+1}).}
 \tag{2.1}
\]

Every intermediate tail cancels literally.  Therefore the transported
terminal equation is an iterate of the adjacent identities, not a second
event whose probability can be multiplied by the first phase's carrier
probability.

The two block parsings can have different numerical record maxima and
interlaced separators.  Equation (2.1) does not identify those maxima; it
makes the second parsing a deterministic function of the same literal
word and the intervening block data.

## 3. Exact reset sink sets

At one phase, write the reversed terminal corridor as

\[
 \operatorname {rev}F_b\,
 (0\operatorname {rev}U_k1\operatorname {rev}L_k)
 \cdots
 (0\operatorname {rev}U_11\operatorname {rev}L_1)\,
 0\operatorname {rev}F_A,
\]

where \(A+b=s-1\).

The first word resets every admissible incoming record \(p\le b\) to
zero.  The renewal segment generates records

\[
 0\le q\le A+1.
\]

The last word has net and minimum \(-(A+1)\), so it resets every such
\(q\) to zero.  Hence the physical sink set at phase \(j\) is

\[
 \boxed{
 S_j^{\rm rec}
 =\{\text{every record state generated at phase }j\}.}
 \tag{3.1}
\]

Let \(\Theta_{t,u}\) be the deterministic parsing transport induced by
the exact local identities.  On their common exact language,

\[
 \boxed{
 \Theta_{t,u}^{-1}(S_u^{\rm rec})
 =S_t^{\rm rec}
 =\mathscr L_{t,u}.}
 \tag{3.2}
\]

This is a set-theoretic identity.  It does not require the two scalar
record coordinates to agree.  Therefore

\[
 \boxed{
 S_t^{\rm rec}\cap
 \Theta_{t,u}^{-1}(S_u^{\rm rec})
 =\mathscr L_{t,u}.}
 \tag{3.3}
\]

The reset/sink intersection has full mass inside the exact transported
language, not \(o(s)\) mass.

## 4. Common-atom product records

Suppose one-pair words \(\omega\) form a common atomization for \(d\)
transported phases.  Let

\[
 h(\omega)=(h_1(\omega),\ldots,h_d(\omega))
\]

and update the joint record by coordinatewise maximum.  For the product
of chains

\[
 P=\prod_{i=1}^d\{0,\ldots,B_i\},
\]

put

\[
 K(q)=\sum_{h(\omega)\le q}w(\omega).
\]

After \(k\) atoms, the cumulative mass below \(q\) is \(K(q)^k\).
Möbius inversion gives the exact-state Green series

\[
 G(q)=\sum_{r\le q}{\mu_P(r,q)\over1-K(r)}.
\]

For a down-set rectangle \([0,B']\),

\[
 \sum_{q\le B'}G(q)={1\over1-K(B')}.
\]

The physical reset sink is the full rectangle \([0,B]\), and every atom
lies below \(B\).  Therefore

\[
 K(B)=\sum_\omega w(\omega)=\rho,
\]

so

\[
 \boxed{
 \sum_{q\in P}G(q)={1\over1-\rho}.}
 \tag{4.1}
\]

At a central seam, \(\rho(1/2)=1-\Theta(1/s)\), and (4.1) has critical
mass \(\Theta(s)\).  Correlation between the phase record depths does not
change this telescope.

## 5. Interlaced atomizations

If phase-\(t\) and phase-\(u\) renewal atoms interlace, there may be no
common sequence to which the product-chain Möbius calculation applies.
An explicit joint automaton would need an additional “currently open
phase atom” state.

This does not make the reset intersection subcritical.  Equations
(1.1) and (3.2) are literal on every exact transported word and imply
that both reset predicates accept it.  Thus interlacing can help only by
making the compatibility language

\[
 \mathscr L_{t,u}
 =\{\text{formal phase-}t\text{ corridors admitting every transported
 phase-}u\text{ block identity}\}
\]

sparse inside the one-phase free-corridor language.

The exact surviving statement is therefore a transported-compatibility
bound such as

\[
 [x^{2m}]\mathscr L_{t,u}(x)
 =o\!\left([x^{2m}]\mathscr L_t(x)\right)
\]

on the Gaussian band, or an aggregate quotient-packing analogue.  Reset
geometry alone neither proves nor refutes this statement.

## 6. Precise boundary

Proved:

1. Two exact phase seam equalities are equivalent, not independent.
2. Both phase reset sinks accept their entire generated record ranges.
3. Their transported intersection is the full exact compatibility
   language.
4. With a common atomization, the full product sink rectangle telescopes
   exactly to the scalar Green factor of critical mass \(\Theta(s)\).

Not proved:

1. That the exact two-phase compatibility language itself has
   \(\Theta(s)\) critical mass.
2. That a genuinely interlaced phase automaton is subcritical.
3. Any coefficient-one conclusion.

This closes the two-reset shortcut and isolates transported compatibility,
not reset intersection, as the smallest remaining two-phase gate.
