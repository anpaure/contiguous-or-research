# Complete \(q=1\) trade quotient and legal \(C_6\) mobility

Date: 2026-07-26

Method: pure mathematics only.  No computation, search, solver, or external
input is used.

## 0. Outcome

Let

\[
 \mathcal L=\binom{[n]}{m-1},\qquad
 \mathcal M=\binom{[n]}m,\qquad
 \mathcal U=\binom{[n]}{m+1},
\]

and let \(\mathscr E=E(J(n,m))\).  For a Johnson edge \(e=XY\), put

\[
 L(e)=X\cap Y,\qquad U(e)=X\cup Y.
\]

Let \(B_0,B_-,B_+\) record, respectively, the two middle endpoints, the
lower intersection colour, and the upper union colour.  Define the fixed-owner,
fixed-lower integral trade lattice

\[
 \mathcal T_{n,m}=\ker_{\mathbb Z}B_0\cap\ker_{\mathbb Z}B_-.
 \tag{0.1}
\]

For a cycle \(C\), write \(u_C(U)\) for the number of its edges of upper
colour \(U\), and define

\[
 R^+(C)=\sum_{U\in\mathcal U}(u_C(U)-1)_+,
 \qquad
 M^+(C)=|\{U\in\mathcal U:u_C(U)=0\}|.
\]

For \(m\ge2\) and \(n\ge m+3\), the exact upper image is

\[
 \boxed{B_+(\mathcal T_{n,m})=\ker_{\mathbb Z}P_{m+1},}
 \tag{0.2}
\]

where \(P_{m+1}\) is point-versus-\((m+1)\)-set incidence.  Thus upper
point margins are the complete integral additive invariants.  There is no
further rational, parity, or finite-index invariant in the signed trade
module.

Every direction in (0.2) is generated by upper rectangles

\[
 \rho(C;a,b,c,d)
 =e_{Cbc}+e_{Cad}-e_{Cbd}-e_{Cac},
 \qquad |C|=m-1,                                    \tag{0.3}
\]

and every such rectangle has a five-coordinate alternating \(C_6\) lift
whose two shores use three distinct lower colours once each.  No nontrivial
two-edge-versus-two-edge packet has this one-copy lower property.  Hence the
\(C_6\) has minimum shore size among nonzero binary owner-neutral packets
whose two shores use the same distinct lower colours.

The union defect is genuinely mobile in the legal lower-rainbow cycle space.
Two explicit simple \(15\)-cycles in \(J(6,3)\), on the same owner set and
using every lower two-set exactly once, differ by one legal \(C_6\) switch and
satisfy

\[
 (M^+,R^+)=(4,4)\longmapsto(3,3).                  \tag{0.4}
\]

Thus neither upper holes nor upper repeat excess is a legal-switch invariant.

On even ground \(n=2m\), if \(C\) is a lower-rainbow saturating cycle of
length

\[
 N=\binom{2m}{m-1},
\]

then its all-one upper ledger is reachable in the signed fixed-owner fibre if
and only if its owner set is point-balanced:

\[
 \boxed{|\{X\in V(C):v\in X\}|=N/2\quad(v\in[2m]).} \tag{0.5}
\]

This is an exact algebraic criterion, not a legal connectivity theorem.  The
remaining gate is precisely the conformal saturating-cycle Markov problem:
produce enough eligible \(C_6\)'s or larger alternating ported circuits while
retaining one cycle.

## 1. The universal point identity

For \(j\ge1\), let \(P_j\) be point-versus-\(j\)-set incidence.  On the
whole Johnson edge module,

\[
 \boxed{P_{m-1}B_-+P_{m+1}B_+=P_mB_0.}             \tag{1.1}
\]

Indeed, for every point \(v\) and edge \(XY\),

\[
 \mathbf 1_{v\in X\cap Y}+\mathbf 1_{v\in X\cup Y}
 =\mathbf 1_{v\in X}+\mathbf 1_{v\in Y}.
\]

Consequently

\[
 B_+(\mathcal T_{n,m})\subseteq\ker_{\mathbb Z}P_{m+1}.    \tag{1.2}
\]

For an oriented cycle \(X_0X_1\cdots X_{s-1}X_0\), write

\[
 X_{i+1}=X_i-a_i+b_i
\]

and let \(t_v\) count deletions of \(v\).  Return to the initial state implies
that \(t_v\) also counts insertions of \(v\).  If \(r_v\) is the number of
owners containing \(v\), while \(\ell,u\) are the lower and upper histograms,
then

\[
 (P_{m-1}\ell)_v=r_v-t_v,\qquad
 (P_{m+1}u)_v=r_v+t_v.                              \tag{1.3}
\]

For an intersection-complete cycle, (1.3) shows directly that its union point
margins are forced by its owner set.

## 2. The point kernel is generated integrally by rectangles

We prove a rank-independent lemma.  Let \(2\le k\le n-2\), and let
\(F_k\) be the free abelian group on the \(k\)-subsets of \([n]\).  For a
\((k-2)\)-set \(C\) and distinct \(a,b,c,d\notin C\), define

\[
 q(C;a,b,c,d)
 =e_{Cab}+e_{Ccd}-e_{Cac}-e_{Cbd}.                  \tag{2.1}
\]

Let \(Q_k\) be their integral span.

### Lemma 2.1

\[
 \boxed{Q_k=\ker_{\mathbb Z}P_k.}                   \tag{2.2}
\]

#### Proof

Every generator (2.1) has zero point margin, so only the reverse inclusion
needs proof.  Work in \(G=F_k/Q_k\), writing \([A]\) for the class of a
\(k\)-set.

For distinct \(i,j\) and a \((k-1)\)-set \(T\) avoiding them, put

\[
 \delta_{ij}(T)=[Ti]-[Tj].                           \tag{2.3}
\]

This is independent of \(T\).  Indeed, if
\(T=C\cup\{a\}\) and \(T'=C\cup\{b\}\) are adjacent in
\(J(n-2,k-1)\), the rectangle on \(C,a,b,i,j\) gives

\[
 [T i]-[T j]=[T'i]-[T'j].                            \tag{2.4}
\]

The graph \(J(n-2,k-1)\) is connected.  Write the common class as
\(\delta_{ij}\).  Choosing one \((k-1)\)-context avoiding three distinct
points gives

\[
 \delta_{ij}+\delta_{j\ell}=\delta_{i\ell}.          \tag{2.5}
\]

Fix a point \(p\), put \(\gamma_p=0\), and put
\(\gamma_i=\delta_{ip}\).  Then

\[
 \delta_{ij}=\gamma_i-\gamma_j.                      \tag{2.6}
\]

The Johnson graph \(J(n,k)\) is connected, so (2.3)--(2.6) imply that one
class \(\alpha\in G\) satisfies

\[
 [A]=\alpha+\sum_{i\in A}\gamma_i                  \tag{2.7}
\]

for every \(k\)-set \(A\).

Now take \(z=\sum_Az_Ae_A\in\ker_{\mathbb Z}P_k\).  Summing all point
equations gives \(k\sum_Az_A=0\), hence \(\sum_Az_A=0\).  Equation
(2.7) then gives

\[
 \sum_Az_A[A]
 =\alpha\sum_Az_A+
  \sum_i\gamma_i\sum_{A\ni i}z_A=0.
\]

Thus \(z\in Q_k\).  The proof took place in an abelian-group quotient and
used no division, so it proves exact integral generation and excludes a
finite-index gap. \(\square\)

## 3. Algebraic and one-copy-conformal lifts

Fix \(|C|=m-1\) and distinct \(a,b,c,d\notin C\).

### 3.1 The direct signed lift

All owners \(C+a,C+b,C+c,C+d\) lie in the lower-colour clique over \(C\).
Set

\[
\begin{aligned}
 z_{\square}={}&e_{(C+b)(C+c)}+e_{(C+a)(C+d)}\\
 &-e_{(C+b)(C+d)}-e_{(C+a)(C+c)}.
\end{aligned}                                                \tag{3.1}
\]

Each owner has one positive and one negative incidence; all four edges have
lower colour \(C\), with total signed coefficient zero.  Therefore

\[
 B_0z_{\square}=B_-z_{\square}=0,
\qquad
 B_+z_{\square}=e_{Cbc}+e_{Cad}-e_{Cbd}-e_{Cac}.     \tag{3.2}
\]

Equations (1.2), (2.2), and (3.2) prove (0.2).  This direct lift is not
conformal at an exact lower-rainbow cycle: its negative shore needs two old
edges of the same lower colour \(C\).

### 3.2 A one-copy-conformal alternating \(C_6\)

Choose \(p\in C\) and put \(K=C\setminus\{p\}\).  On the five active
points \(p,a,b,c,d\), define six owners in cyclic order by

\[
\begin{array}{lll}
 X_1=Kab,&X_2=Kbc,&X_3=Kbp,\\
 X_4=Kpd,&X_5=Kap,&X_6=Kac.
\end{array}                                                \tag{3.3}
\]

Here, for example, \(Kab=K\cup\{a,b\}\).  Remove

\[
 E^-=\{X_1X_2,X_3X_4,X_5X_6\}
\]

and insert

\[
 E^+=\{X_2X_3,X_4X_5,X_6X_1\}.
\]

The full ledger is

\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c}
 &\text{edge}&\text{lower}&\text{upper}\\ \hline
 -&X_1X_2&Kb&Kabc\\
 -&X_3X_4&Kp&Kbpd\\
 -&X_5X_6&Ka&Kapc\\ \hline
 +&X_2X_3&Kb&Kbcp\\
 +&X_4X_5&Kp&Kapd\\
 +&X_6X_1&Ka&Kabc.
\end{array}                                                \tag{3.4}
\]

Thus the two shores are perfect matchings on the same six owners, use the
same three pairwise distinct lower colours, and have upper difference

\[
 e_{Cbc}+e_{Cad}-e_{Cbd}-e_{Cac}.                    \tag{3.5}
\]

So every upper rectangle has a one-copy-conformal \(C_6\) lift.

### 3.3 Minimality

There is no nontrivial two-edge-versus-two-edge one-copy-conformal packet.
After common edges are cancelled, owner neutrality would make its overlay an
alternating four-cycle \(Y_0Y_1Y_2Y_3Y_0\).  If the two negative lower
colours are \(S\ne T\), equality of the negative and positive lower
multisets has two possible pairings.  In either pairing, two opposite
Johnson two-paths have equal consecutive intersections.  The elementary
fact for three distinct equal-rank sets on a Johnson two-path,

\[
 AB,BC\in E(J(n,m)),\quad A\cap B=B\cap C=S
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad A\cap C=S,
\]

follows by writing \(A=S+a,B=S+b,C=S+c\) with distinct extras.  The
required distinctness holds in the cancelled binary four-cycle; otherwise a
red and blue edge would coincide and would already have been cancelled.
Applying the fact to the two opposite paths gives
\(Y_0\cap Y_2=S=T\) or \(Y_1\cap Y_3=S=T\), a contradiction.
One edge per shore is necessarily the same edge.  Hence three edges per
shore is minimal.

### 3.4 Exact one-cycle legality of the \(C_6\)

Label the six owners cyclically \(1,\ldots,6\), so

\[
 \mathfrak m^-=\{12,34,56\},\qquad
 \mathfrak m^+=\{23,45,61\}.                       \tag{3.6}
\]

Deleting \(\mathfrak m^-\) from an ambient simple cycle produces three paths and
therefore an outside endpoint matching \(R\).  The old cycle is connected
exactly when \(R\cup\mathfrak m^-\) is a six-cycle.  The replacement is connected
exactly when \(R\cup\mathfrak m^+\) is a six-cycle.  Given the old cycle,
the latter condition is equivalent to

\[
 R\cap\mathfrak m^+=\varnothing.                     \tag{3.7}
\]

Indeed, two disjoint perfect matchings on six vertices have union one
alternating six-cycle; a common edge makes a two-cycle component.  The four
simultaneously legal outside matchings are

\[
 \{13,25,46\},\quad \{14,25,36\},\quad
 \{14,26,35\},\quad \{15,24,36\}.                  \tag{3.8}
\]

Thus eligibility is a literal port-order condition, not an automatic
consequence of the ledger identities.

## 4. Normal form for exact lower-rainbow factor differences

Let \(F,F'\) be binary two-factors with the same owner set, each using every
lower colour exactly once.  Cancel their common edges; colour the remaining
edges of \(F\) red and those of \(F'\) blue.  Then

1. red degree equals blue degree at every owner;
2. every touched lower colour occurs on exactly one red and one blue edge;
3. the overlay decomposes into alternating closed trails.

The first two assertions are the owner and lower ledger equations.  For the
third, pair red and blue half-edges at each owner and follow the pairings.
Conversely, a binary overlay satisfying the first two assertions preserves
both ledgers; it gives another Hamilton cycle exactly when the toggled
two-factor is connected.

This is the exact one-copy trade space.  Equation (0.2) characterizes its
ambient signed lattice, while the alternating-trail support and connectivity
conditions are the nonlinear part.

## 5. A legal defect-changing lower-rainbow \(C_6\)

Consider the two cyclic lists

\[
\begin{aligned}
 C_{\rm old}=(&126,236,136,156,125,256,456,245,234,123,\\
              &135,345,346,146,124),                         \tag{5.1}\\
 C_{\rm new}=(&126,136,236,256,125,156,456,245,234,123,\\
              &135,345,346,146,124).                         \tag{5.2}
\end{aligned}
\]

Both lists use the same fifteen distinct middle owners.  Their cyclic lower
intersection lists are, respectively,

\[
\begin{aligned}
 &(26,36,16,15,25,56,45,24,23,13,35,34,46,14,12),\\
 &(16,36,26,25,15,56,45,24,23,13,35,34,46,14,12).
\end{aligned}                                                \tag{5.3}
\]

Each line is exactly \(\binom{[6]}2\), so both cycles are lower-rainbow.
All adjacent owners differ by one exchange, and the owners are distinct, so
these are simple Johnson cycles.  Equivalently, (5.3) is a Hamilton cycle on
the lower Johnson layer, with distinct middle transition owners.  It is not a
full-owner Hamilton cycle of \(J(6,3)\); it is exactly the length-
\(\binom62=15\) saturating object relevant to the two-sided \(q=1\) gate.

Take in (3.3)

\[
 K=\{6\},\quad p=5,\quad a=1,\quad b=2,\quad c=3,\quad d=4.
\]

Then

\[
 (X_1,X_2,X_3,X_4,X_5,X_6)
 =(126,236,256,456,156,136).                         \tag{5.4}
\]

The old cycle contains \(X_1X_2,X_3X_4,X_5X_6\), with the last edge
traversed in reverse.  After their deletion the outside endpoint matching is

\[
 R=\{X_1X_4,X_2X_6,X_3X_5\}=\{14,26,35\}           \tag{5.5}
\]

in the quotient labelling (3.6).  This is the third matching in (3.8), so
inserting \(X_2X_3,X_4X_5,X_6X_1\) gives one cycle, namely (5.2).

The old upper targets of load two are

\[
 1236,\quad1256,\quad2456,\quad1246,
\]

and its holes are

\[
 1245,\quad1456,\quad2346,\quad2356.                \tag{5.6}
\]

For a literal check, the two cyclic upper lists are

\[
\begin{array}{c|lllllllllllllll}
 C_{\rm old}
 &1236&1236&1356&1256&1256&2456&2456&2345&1234&1235
 &1345&3456&1346&1246&1246\\
 C_{\rm new}
 &1236&1236&2356&1256&1256&1456&2456&2345&1234&1235
 &1345&3456&1346&1246&1246.
\end{array}                                                \tag{5.7}
\]

Thus every upper four-set not listed as a hole or a repeat has load one.
The switch has upper action

\[
 e_{2356}+e_{1456}-e_{2456}-e_{1356}.                \tag{5.8}
\]

Here the two positive targets are old holes, \(2456\) has old load two, and
\(1356\) has old load one.  Therefore the new load-two targets are

\[
 1236,\quad1256,\quad1246,
\]

and its holes are

\[
 1245,\quad1356,\quad2346.                           \tag{5.9}
\]

Since both target layers have size fifteen, upper holes equal upper repeat
excess.  Equations (5.6)--(5.9) prove (0.4).

More generally, for one forward \(C_6\), let

\[
 A=\{Cbc,Cad\},\qquad D=\{Cbd,Cac\}.
\]

For a current upper load \(u\), the exact repeat change is

\[
 \boxed{\Delta R^+
 =|\{U\in A:u(U)\ge1\}|-|\{U\in D:u(U)\ge2\}|.}   \tag{5.10}
\]

Thus a legal packet can improve by two, improve by one as in (5.1), remain
flat, or worsen.  There is no monotone sign without an occupancy theorem.

## 6. Exact formal two-sided criterion

Let \(n=2m\) with \(m\ge3\), and put

\[
 N=\binom{2m}{m-1},
\]

Let \(C\) be a lower-rainbow simple \(N\)-cycle.  Write \(v\) for its
owner indicator, \(r=P_mv\), and \(u=B_+x_C\).  From (1.1),

\[
 P_{m+1}u=2r-P_{m-1}\mathbf1_{\mathcal L}.           \tag{6.1}
\]

The binomial identity

\[
 P_{m-1}\mathbf1_{\mathcal L}
 +P_{m+1}\mathbf1_{\mathcal U}=N\mathbf1_{[2m]}     \tag{6.2}
\]

and the complete image theorem (0.2) show that an integral
\(z\in\mathcal T_{2m,m}\) with

\[
 B_+(x_C+z)=\mathbf1_{\mathcal U}
\]

exists if and only if

\[
 2r=N\mathbf1_{[2m]},
\]

which is (0.5).  Thus point balance is both necessary and sufficient in the
integral signed fibre.

There is an immediate universal parity obstruction.  By Lucas' criterion,

\[
 \binom{2m}{m-1}\text{ is odd}
 \iff (m-1)\mathbin{\&}(m+1)=0
 \iff m=2^a-1.                                      \tag{6.3}
\]

For the last equivalence, an even \(m\) makes both \(m-1\) and \(m+1\)
odd.  If \(m\) is odd and \(2^a\mathrel\Vert(m+1)\), subtraction of two
leaves all bits above position \(a\) unchanged in \(m-1\).  The bitwise
intersection therefore vanishes exactly when \(m+1=2^a\).

For these \(m\), (0.5) is impossible, so an exact two-sided rainbow cycle
does not exist.  This obstruction is already one of the point margins; (0.2)
proves that no further additive congruence obstruction exists.

For a general proposed upper histogram \(b\in\mathbb Z^{\mathcal U}\), the
complete signed-fibre criterion is simply

\[
 \boxed{b-u\in B_+(\mathcal T_{n,m})
 \iff P_{m+1}b=P_{m+1}u.}                            \tag{6.4}
\]

On odd ground, an injective upper target is a zero-one vector of the required
mass, and (6.4) is its exact algebraic feasibility test.

## 7. Proved boundary

The following points are closed exactly.

1. Upper point margins are the complete integral, rational, and modular
   invariants of fixed-owner, fixed-intersection signed trades.
2. Upper rectangles generate the quotient, and the five-coordinate \(C_6\)
   has minimum shore size among nonzero binary owner-neutral packets with
   the same distinct lower colours on both shores.
3. Union holes and repeat excess are not invariants of the legal
   lower-rainbow cycle space: (5.1)--(5.9) give a literal counterexample.
4. On even ground, owner point balance is necessary and algebraically
   sufficient for the exact two-sided upper ledger.

What is not proved is connectivity of the legal lower-rainbow
saturating-cycle fibre, or a descent theorem from an arbitrary saturating
cycle.  A signed sum of
\(C_6\) rectangles may fail to be conformal at intermediate states, and an
eligible packet may have the wrong port order or the wrong occupancy sign.
The exact surviving statement is therefore:

> Given a point-balanced lower-rainbow saturating cycle, prove that its legal
> alternating-circuit component contains an upper-rainbow cycle, or identify a
> genuinely nonlinear component invariant.  No linear or lattice invariant can
> supply such an obstruction.

No coefficient-one conclusion is claimed.
