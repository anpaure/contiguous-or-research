# Prescribed-design cycle joining: exact octahedral trades and alpha absorption

Date: 2026-07-27

## 0. Outcome

Assume the static inverse-design problem has been solved up to connectivity:
there is a transition 2-factor on the middle layer which

1. uses every lower colour exactly once;
2. has degree two at every middle vertex; and
3. has the prescribed upper loads (in particular, the loads coming from a
   simple regular Catalan excess design).

This note gives the exact local cycle-joining algebra for that object.

Inside a four-coordinate Boolean octahedron, the full integer kernel of the
three marginal maps is the circulation lattice of `K_4`.  Its primitive
simple trades are:

* a triangular circulation, which is the familiar alternating six-cycle
  (the **alpha trade**); and
* a four-cycle circulation, which is an eight-edge octahedral trade.

The distinction is decisive.  An alpha trade can merge three factor cycles
into one, reducing the component count by two.  More importantly, if two of
its removed edges lie in one factor cycle in the correct cyclic order and
the third lies in another cycle, the same alpha trade merges those **two**
cycles into one, reducing the component count by one.  Thus alpha itself is
the smallest parity-breaking join; no nonlocal macro trade is needed for
component parity.  The four-cycle trade does not join components at all:
before and after the switch it connects the same two pairs of external ports
and merely exchanges the two internal middle vertices.  There is no
nontrivial two-row trade.

Thus the local cycle-joining problem has a sharp answer:

> arbitrary component count can be reduced to one by alpha charts, provided
> one has enough **absorbing charts**: either one cut in each of three
> components, or two correctly interlaced cuts in the current component and
> one cut in a new component.

Every such switch preserves the lower, middle, and upper marginals exactly,
and in fact preserves the multiset of unordered coordinate-swap pairs.
Residence and deeper hazard statistics are seam-local: a `t`-cut
reconnection changes at most `tq` depth-`q` windows and changes the block
count of any fixed section by at most `t`.  This yields an exact decorated
joining theorem: an `H`-neutral absorbing alpha tree joins the factor without
changing any shadow through depth `H`.

What remains open is the global supply theorem for such decorated absorbing
alpha charts.  The ordinary octahedral four-trade contributes chronology
freedom but not component joining.

## 1. The sigma normal form

Put

\[
 v=2r-1,
 \qquad
 \mathcal L=\binom{[v]}{r-1},
 \quad
 \mathcal M=\binom{[v]}r,
 \quad
 \mathcal U=\binom{[v]}{r+1}.
\]

A lower-rainbow chord selection is exactly a map

\[
 \sigma:\mathcal L\longrightarrow\mathcal U,
 \qquad C\subset \sigma(C).
 \tag{1.1}
\]

If

\[
 \sigma(C)\setminus C=\{a,b\},
\]

then `C` selects the Johnson edge

\[
 e_\sigma(C)=
 \big\{C\cup\{a\},\ C\cup\{b\}\big\}.
 \tag{1.2}
\]

The three relevant marginals are now transparent.

* The lower load of every `C` is one because `sigma` is a function.
* The upper load of `U` is

  \[
  |\sigma^{-1}(U)|.
  \tag{1.3}
  \]

* The degree of a middle vertex `X` is

  \[
  d_\sigma(X)=
  \big|\{C\in\mathcal L:C\subset X\subset\sigma(C)\}\big|.
  \tag{1.4}
  \]

Hence the selected chords form a spanning 2-factor if and only if

\[
 d_\sigma(X)=2\qquad(X\in\mathcal M).
 \tag{1.5}
\]

Equivalently, replace every chord (1.2) by the two incidence edges

\[
 C--(C\cup\{a\}),
 \qquad
 C--(C\cup\{b\}).
\]

This gives a 2-regular bipartite graph on
`mathcal L sqcup mathcal M`.  Its components are exactly the components of
the Johnson 2-factor, with every Johnson edge subdivided once.  This
incidence form is useful because a cycle join is literally a reassignment
of upper values in (1.1), subject to (1.5).

For the Catalan excess target, (1.3) is one on ordinary upper colours and
two precisely on the prescribed simple excess family.  Every trade below
preserves the complete vector (1.3), not merely its total or its energy.

## 2. The exact four-coordinate kernel

Fix an `(r-2)`-set `R` and four points

\[
 Q=\{1,2,3,4\}\subseteq[v]\setminus R.
\]

For `i,j in Q`, `i ne j`, put

\[
 C_i=R\cup\{i\},
 \qquad
 U_j=R\cup(Q\setminus\{j\}),
\]

and let

\[
 e_{ij}:=e(C_i,U_j).
 \tag{2.1}
\]

If `Q setminus {i,j}={k,l}`, then

\[
 e_{ij}=
 \big\{R\cup\{i,k\},\ R\cup\{i,l\}\big\}.
 \tag{2.2}
\]

Consider an integral signed change `z=(z_ij)` supported on these twelve
chords, with `z_ij>0` meaning insertion and `z_ij<0` deletion.

### Theorem 2.1 (octahedral kernel theorem)

The change `z` preserves every lower load, every upper load, and every
middle degree if and only if

\[
 \boxed{
 z_{ij}=-z_{ji},
 \qquad
 \sum_{j\ne i}z_{ij}=0\quad(i\in Q).
 }
 \tag{2.3}
\]

Thus the local integer kernel is canonically the circulation lattice of the
complete graph on `Q`.  It has rank three and is generated by oriented
triangles.

#### Proof

Lower preservation is exactly

\[
 \sum_{j\ne i}z_{ij}=0.
 \tag{2.4}
\]

Upper preservation is the analogous column equation.  For a middle vertex

\[
 X_{ik}=R\cup\{i,k\},
\]

(2.2) gives

\[
 \Delta d(X_{ik})
 =\sum_{j\in Q\setminus\{i,k\}}
   (z_{ij}+z_{kj}).
 \tag{2.5}
\]

Using the two row equations (2.4), the right side of (2.5) is

\[
 -z_{ik}-z_{ki}.
\]

Consequently middle-degree preservation is equivalent to skew symmetry.
Under skew symmetry the column sum at `j` is the negative of the row sum at
`j`, so the upper equations follow automatically from (2.4).  Conversely,
the preceding calculation proves necessity.  Skew matrices with zero row
sums are precisely integral circulations on `K_4`.  The integral cycle
lattice of a graph is generated by its oriented cycles, and the cycle space
of `K_4` is generated by triangles.  QED.

### Corollary 2.2 (primitive simple trades)

If `z_ij in {-1,0,1}` is support-minimal, then its unoriented support is a
simple cycle of `K_4`.  Hence it is either

1. an oriented triangle, giving three deleted and three inserted chords; or
2. an oriented four-cycle, giving four deleted and four inserted chords.

There is no other primitive simple trade in one Boolean octahedron.

This is the exact Markov basis of the local three-marginal problem.

### Corollary 2.3 (swap-pair preservation)

The chords `e_ij` and `e_ji` flip the same unordered pair of coordinates,
namely

\[
 Q\setminus\{i,j\}.
\]

Therefore every local trade satisfying (2.3) preserves, coordinate pair by
coordinate pair, the number of selected Johnson transitions having that
symmetric difference.  The trade changes chronology, not the first-order
swap inventory.

## 3. The alpha trade is the genuine local join

Take a directed triangle `i -> j -> k -> i` and let `ell` be the fourth
point.  Insert

\[
 e_{ij},e_{jk},e_{ki}
\]

and delete

\[
 e_{ji},e_{kj},e_{ik}.
 \tag{3.1}
\]

### Lemma 3.1 (alternating-six-cycle form)

Each side of (3.1) is a perfect matching of the six middle vertices

\[
 R\cup\{a,b\},\qquad \{a,b\}\in\binom Q2,
\]

and the union of the two matchings is one alternating 6-cycle.

#### Proof

Relabel the triangle as `(1,2,3)` and its spare point as `4`.  The inserted
edges are

\[
 13--14,
 \qquad
 12--24,
 \qquad
 23--34,
\]

and the deleted edges are

\[
 23--24,
 \qquad
 13--34,
 \qquad
 12--14,
\]

where `ab` abbreviates `R union {a,b}`.  These are two perfect matchings,
and their union is the displayed alternating hexagon.  QED.

### Lemma 3.2 (exact cut-matching formula)

Let `M_-` be the matching of the three deleted alpha edges and `M_+` the
matching of the three inserted alpha edges on their six middle endpoints.
Delete `M_-` from the current factor.  Its path pieces pair the six exposed
ends; denote this **retained-path matching** by `P`.  (If one deleted edge is
the only cut in its factor cycle, `P` contains that same endpoint pair.)

For two perfect matchings `A,B` on the same six points, let

\[
 \kappa(A,B)
\]

be the number of alternating cycles in the 2-regular multigraph `A union B`,
counting a common pair as one doubled 2-cycle.  Then

\[
 \boxed{
 c(F')-c(F)=\kappa(P,M_+)-\kappa(P,M_-).
 }
 \tag{3.2}
\]

Consequently:

1. if the three deleted edges lie in three distinct cycles, the switch
   merges all three into one and `Delta c=-2`;
2. if two deleted edges lie in one cycle and the third lies in another,
   then `Delta c` is either `-1` or `0`; it is `-1` exactly when
   `P union M_+` is one alternating 6-cycle; and
3. if all three deleted edges lie in one cycle, the switch may retain or
   split that cycle, according to the same formula.

Equivalently, the complete component-count table on the affected region is

\[
 \begin{array}{c|c|c}
 \text{distribution of the three old edges}&c_{\rm old}&c_{\rm new}\\ \hline
 1+1+1&3&1\\
 2+1&2&1\text{ or }2\\
 3&1&1,2,\text{ or }3.
 \end{array}
 \tag{3.2a}
\]

All listed outcomes occur for suitable retained-path matchings.

#### Proof

Every affected component of `F` alternates between a retained path from
`F setminus M_-` and an edge of `M_-`.  Contracting the retained paths gives
exactly the alternating cycles of `P union M_-`.  After the switch the
retained paths are unchanged and the joining matching is `M_+`, giving the
alternating cycles of `P union M_+`.  All unaffected components cancel from
the difference, proving (3.2).

In the first case `P=M_-`, so `kappa(P,M_-)=3`; Lemma 3.1 says
`M_- union M_+` is one alternating 6-cycle, giving `kappa(P,M_+)=1`.

In the second case the singleton-cut cycle contributes one common pair to
`P` and `M_-`, while the twice-cut cycle contributes one of the two cross
pairings on its four ends.  Hence `kappa(P,M_-)=2`.  Directly, or from the
matching formula, `kappa(P,M_+)` is one or two.  This proves the remaining
claims.  For the last row of (3.2a), under the canonical matchings in
Corollary 3.3 the choices

\[
 (13)(25)(46),\qquad (23)(15)(46),\qquad M_+
\]

for `P` give respectively one, two, and three new components while each
gives one old component.  Thus every listed outcome occurs.  QED.

The second case is the crucial correction: alpha is not parity-locked on
closed unoriented factors.  Rooted-strand endpoint monodromy and closed
factor component parity are different invariants.

### Corollary 3.3 (smallest parity-breaking trade)

There are alpha configurations for which two current factor cycles are
merged into one.  For example, after labelling the six alpha ports so that

\[
 M_-=(12)(34)(56),
 \qquad
 M_+=(23)(45)(61),
\]

take

\[
 P=(12)(35)(46).
\]

Then

\[
 \kappa(P,M_-)=2,
 \qquad
 \kappa(P,M_+)=1.
\]

Thus one three-row alpha trade changes component-count parity.  By Theorem
5.1 below no one- or two-row marginal-preserving trade is nontrivial, so
three rows are sharp.

### Corollary 3.4 (exact interlacement dichotomy)

In the two-component case, fix the singleton-cycle pair as `12`.  Up to
relabelling, the two possible retained-path matchings for the twice-cut
cycle are

\[
 P_1=(12)(35)(46),
 \qquad
 P_2=(12)(36)(45).
\]

For

\[
 M_+=(23)(45)(61),
\]

one has

\[
 \kappa(P_1,M_+)=1,
 \qquad
 \kappa(P_2,M_+)=2.
\]

Hence exactly one of the two cyclic interlacements of the alpha edges on the
twice-cut cycle is absorbing.  This turns “binary alpha join” into a static
order test, not an additional existence problem on the six ports.

In the canonical Boolean chart this favourable interlacement can itself be
local.  With the concrete middle vertices from Lemma 3.1, the retained pairs
`35` and `46` may be the Johnson edges

\[
 (R+12)--(R+13),
 \qquad
 (R+14)--(R+34).
\]

Together with the corresponding two deleted alpha edges they form a Johnson
4-cycle.  Thus a visible square on one factor cycle plus the third alpha
edge on another component is a constant-radius certificate for the bare
component topology.

It is **not** the residence-safe certificate.  All three deleted alpha edges
flip the spare coordinate `ell`: their unordered swap pairs are

\[
 \{i,\ell\},\quad\{j,\ell\},\quad\{k,\ell\}.
 \tag{3.3}
\]

In a two-sided `H`-fresh factor, two transitions flipping the same coordinate
cannot occur within `H` cyclic steps.  Therefore any two alpha edges lying
on one `H`-fresh component are automatically separated by more than `H`.
The constant-radius Johnson square above is consequently forbidden as soon
as the required freshness reaches its separation.  A useful binary alpha
absorption is local in its six chord choices but necessarily **nonlocal in
chronology**: its favourable retained paths must be long.  This is exactly
why the port-interlacement criterion and the seam decorations both matter.

## 4. The octahedral four-trade is component-inert

Take the directed four-cycle

\[
 1\to2\to3\to4\to1
\]

and replace its reverse by its forward orientation.  Explicitly, insert

\[
 e_{12},e_{23},e_{34},e_{41}
\]

and delete

\[
 e_{21},e_{32},e_{43},e_{14}.
 \tag{4.1}
\]

### Theorem 4.1 (four-trade inertness)

The trade (4.1) preserves the number of components.  More strongly, after
the four external degree-one ports are fixed, its old and new internal
networks induce exactly the same pairing of those ports.

#### Proof

The inserted side consists of the two length-two paths

\[
 14--13--23,
 \qquad
 12--24--34.
 \tag{4.2}
\]

The deleted side consists of

\[
 14--24--23,
 \qquad
 12--13--34.
 \tag{4.3}
\]

Both sides pair `14` with `23` and pair `12` with `34`; they only exchange
which of the internal vertices `13,24` lies on the two routes.  Replacing
(4.3) by (4.2) therefore cannot join or split any outside path components.
QED.

This audits the most tempting four-variable candidate: it is an exact
three-marginal trade, but it is not a cycle join.

It is nevertheless a useful **port transporter**.  If the two external
routes belong to different factor components, (4.1) keeps those two
components separate but exchanges which of the internal middle vertices
`13,24` belongs to each route.  Thus four-trades may be used to alter the
component ownership or cyclic placement of later alpha charts before an
absorbing alpha switch is applied.  Any such preparatory use still pays only
the seam-local bounds of Section 8 and never changes the prescribed design.

## 5. No two-row join exists

The ordinary 2-switch would be the natural parity-changing join.  The
Boolean incidence constraints exclude it.

### Theorem 5.1 (two-row rigidity)

Suppose two lower colours `C_1,C_2` are assigned two upper colours
`U_1,U_2`, and interchange the two upper assignments.  If both assignments
are legal containments and the middle degrees are unchanged, then the
interchange is trivial.

#### Proof

Assume the upper colours are distinct; otherwise the interchange changes
nothing.  Cross-containment gives

\[
 C_1\cup C_2\subseteq U_1\cap U_2.
\]

If `|C_1 union C_2|=r+1`, both `(r+1)`-sets `U_1,U_2` equal that union, a
contradiction.  Thus distinct `(r-1)`-sets must have intersection of size
`r-2`.  Write

\[
 C_1=A\cup\{a\},\quad C_2=A\cup\{b\},
\]

and

\[
 U_1=A\cup\{a,b,x\},\quad
 U_2=A\cup\{a,b,y\},
 \qquad x\ne y.
\]

Before the interchange the middle vertex `A union {a,x}` is an endpoint of
the chord `(C_1,U_1)`.  After the interchange it is an endpoint of neither
new chord: it is not contained in `U_2`, and it does not contain `C_2`.
Its degree therefore drops by one, contradicting middle-degree preservation.
QED.

Hence the smallest nontrivial marginal-preserving local join uses three
rows, and Corollary 3.3 shows that this minimum already changes component
parity.

## 6. Open-boundary alpha absorption

Delete a designated chord `e(C_*,U_*)` from the 2-factor.  This produces one
path and leaves all other cycles untouched; its exact boundary ledger is

\[
 d(C_*) : 1\to0,
 \qquad
 d(U_*): d(U_*)\to d(U_*)-1,
\]

and the two middle endpoints acquire degree one.  This is precisely the
cycle-break normalization used by the endpoint-rooted OR--Pascal factor.

After further factor edges are cut, the retained pieces on the alpha ports
form a partial matching together with one distinguished open strand.  This
has an exact matching formulation.  Adjoin symbols `s,t` for the two global
path endpoints.  Contract each retained path piece to an edge.  The result is
a perfect matching `P^circ` on the six alpha ports together with `s,t`: the
two outer retained pieces pair `s,t` to alpha ports, and every other retained
piece pairs two alpha ports.

For a matching `M` on the six alpha ports, the graph

\[
 P^\circ\cup M
\]

has one `s`--`t` path and some number of alternating cycles.  Let
`kappa^circ(P^circ,M)` be its number of connected components.

### Theorem 6.1 (open cut-matching formula)

For an alpha switch in a path/cycle factor,

\[
 \boxed{
 c^\circ(F')-c^\circ(F)
 =\kappa^\circ(P^\circ,M_+)-
  \kappa^\circ(P^\circ,M_-),
 }
 \tag{6.1}
\]

where `c^circ` counts the distinguished path plus all cycles.  In
particular, the switch absorbs every affected cycle into the distinguished
path if and only if `P^circ union M_+` is connected.

#### Proof

Exactly as in Lemma 3.2, every affected component alternates between a
retained path piece and a joining edge.  Contracting retained pieces gives
`P^circ union M_-` before and `P^circ union M_+` after.  The global endpoints
force exactly one component to be a path; every other component is a cycle.
This proves (6.1) and the connectivity criterion.  QED.

There are two useful absorption modes.

1. One removed edge lies on the distinguished path and the other two lie on
   two different cycles.  The alpha reconnection absorbs both cycles into
   the path whenever the contracted port graph is connected.
2. Two removed edges lie on the distinguished path and the third lies on
   one cycle.  In the favourable interlacement, the alpha reconnection
   absorbs that single cycle into the path.

For the labels of Corollary 3.3, the second mode is witnessed explicitly by

\[
 P^\circ=(12)(s3)(46)(5t).
 \tag{6.2}
\]

With `M_-=(12)(34)(56)`, the union has one `s`--`t` path and the doubled
pair `12`, hence two components.  With
`M_+=(23)(45)(61)`, it is the single path

\[
 s-3-2-1-6-4-5-t.
\]

Thus one local alpha switch absorbs one cycle into the open Hamilton path.

The second mode is the open-boundary version of Corollary 3.3.  It preserves
the missing lower colour `C_*`, the decremented upper colour `U_*`, and the
two physical path endpoints, because the alpha circuit is disjoint from the
designated cut and preserves every degree and colour marginal on its own
support.

## 7. A conditional connectedness theorem

The positive side is also exact.

Let the cycles of the initial factor be `Gamma_1,...,Gamma_s`.  A family of
flippable alpha charts is an **absorbing alpha tree** if its charts can be
ordered so that, at each step, either

1. one removed edge lies in the already merged component and the other two
   lie in two untouched components (a ternary absorption); or
2. two removed edges lie in the already merged component, the third lies in
   one untouched component, and the retained-path matching satisfies the
   connected criterion in Lemma 3.2 (a binary absorption).

Require additionally that the six-edge supports of chosen charts are
mutually vertex-disjoint, or more generally that every chart remains
dynamically flippable when its turn is reached.

### Theorem 7.1 (alpha absorption theorem)

If a factor admits an absorbing alpha tree spanning its components, then
switching its charts in the stated order produces one Hamilton cycle,
regardless of the parity of `s`.
Throughout the process all lower loads, all middle degrees, all upper loads,
and all unordered swap-pair counts remain exactly unchanged.

#### Proof

Start from any initial component.  A ternary absorption enlarges the merged
component by two initial components; a binary absorption enlarges it by one.
Both assertions are exactly Lemma 3.2.  Induction reaches one component.
The marginal and swap-pair assertions follow from Theorem 2.1 and Corollary
2.3 at every step.  QED.

The unresolved combinatorial supply statement is therefore concrete:

> prove that every proper merged component exposes either a ternary alpha
> chart through two untouched components or a correctly interlaced binary
> alpha chart through one untouched component.

Ordinary connectivity of the abstract alpha-support hypergraph is not by
itself enough: the charts must be simultaneously/dynamically flippable and
must hit distinct current components in the required order.

There is a useful component-level sufficient criterion.  For a current
merged union `S`, call an available chart **outward** if it is either ternary
with one removed edge in `S` and two outside, or binary-absorbing with two
correctly interlaced removed edges in `S` and one outside.

### Corollary 7.2 (one-or-two-outward expansion criterion)

Suppose that for every proper current merged union `S` there is an outward
alpha chart.  If these charts can be selected with mutually vertex-disjoint
physical supports, then the factor can be joined into one Hamilton cycle.

The same conclusion holds with `H`-safe or `H`-neutral charts if this condition
holds in the corresponding decorated support hypergraph after every set of
previously used physical vertices is forbidden.

#### Proof

Apply an outward chart.  The merged set grows by two or one components.
Physical disjointness guarantees that previous switches do not destroy
future charts, so iteration reaches every component and Theorem 7.1 applies.
The decorated variants follow identically from Theorem 9.1.
QED.

This outward-chart condition, rather than ordinary support connectedness, is
the useful cut condition.  The binary alternative is precisely what removes
component parity from the gate.

## 8. Seam-local residence and hazard damage

Write a directed Johnson transition as

\[
 X_i\longrightarrow X_{i+1},
 \qquad
 a_i=X_i\setminus X_{i+1},
 \quad
 b_i=X_{i+1}\setminus X_i.
 \tag{8.1}
\]

A positive coordinate residence of length `ell` is exactly an equality

\[
 b_i=a_{i+\ell}.
 \tag{8.2}
\]

Thus delay `H` means that (8.2) never occurs for `1<=ell<=H`.  The stronger
two-sided `H`-freshness condition says that every block of at most `H`
consecutive swaps is a Johnson geodesic (all its deletion and arrival labels
are distinct).

### Lemma 8.1 (exact seam criterion)

Cut a collection of directed factor cycles into paths and reconnect the
paths.  If the old paths are delay-`H`, then the new factor is delay-`H` if
and only if, at every new seam,

\[
 b_i\ne a_{i+\ell}
 \tag{8.3}
\]

for every pair in the seam neighbourhood whose forward interval crosses
that seam and has `1<=ell<=H`.

The analogous statement for two-sided `H`-freshness is: every block of at
most `H` swaps crossing a new seam has pairwise distinct flip labels.

#### Proof

Every forbidden equality or non-geodesic block lying wholly inside a
retained path segment is unchanged (possibly read in reverse, which does not
change the residence lengths of the underlying set sequence).  Hence every
new violation crosses a new seam, and every cross-seam violation is exactly
one of the tests in (8.3), or its two-sided version.  QED.

This is a finite static test involving only the last `H-1` swaps on one side
and the first `H-1` swaps on the other.  It should be attached as a
decoration to every port of the alpha-support hypergraph.

### Lemma 8.1a (the spare coordinate is automatically safe)

For an alpha chart on triangle coordinates `i,j,k` with spare coordinate
`ell`, all three deleted edges and all three inserted edges flip `ell`.
If the old factor is two-sided `H`-fresh, then none of the retained
`(H-1)`-transition collars at the six cut ports flips `ell`.  Consequently,
provided no `H`-window in the new factor crosses two new seams, no new short
residence involving `ell` can be created by the alpha reconnection.  Under
that natural seam-separation condition, the nontrivial tests concern only
the reassigned triangle coordinates and coincidences between the two
retained collars.

#### Proof

Each deleted edge has swap pair `{ell,x}` for one of `x=i,j,k`.  In an
`H`-fresh transition word, no coordinate occurring in that edge can occur in
any of the preceding or following `H-1` transitions which share an
`H`-window with it.  Hence `ell` is absent from every retained collar.  Each
new edge again flips `ell` exactly once.  If an `H`-window crosses at most one
new seam, it therefore contains no second occurrence of `ell`.  QED.

This removes one apparently global part of residence checking: the common
spare coordinate which makes the alpha trade possible is not itself the
source of seam failure.

### Lemma 8.2 (window locality)

Suppose a switch removes `t` factor edges and reconnects the resulting path
pieces using `t` new edges.  For every `q>=1`:

1. at most `tq` old depth-`q` windows and at most `tq` new depth-`q` windows
   cross a changed seam;
2. the `ell_1` distance between either the intersection-shadow load vectors
   or the union-shadow load vectors before and after the switch is at most

   \[
   2tq;
   \tag{8.4}
   \]

3. for every fixed section `S`, its number of containing blocks changes by
   at most `t`, and its truncated local time at depth `q` changes by at most
   `tq`.

If `H_q` denotes the number of zero-load targets and

\[
 O_q(c)=\sum_T(L_q(T)-c)^+,
\]

then also

\[
 |H_q(F')-H_q(F)|\le tq,
 \qquad
 |O_q(c;F')-O_q(c;F)|\le tq.
 \tag{8.5}
\]

If all loads before and after the switch are at most `B`, the factorial
energy

\[
 \Phi_q(c)=\sum_T(L_q(T)-c)(L_q(T)-c-1)
\]

changes by at most `O(tq(B+c+1))`.

#### Proof

A depth-`q` window changes only if it crosses a deleted or inserted seam.
For each seam there are at most `q` possible starting positions.  All other
windows lie inside retained path pieces and are identical up to reversal.
Replacing one target occurrence by another changes the load-vector
`ell_1` norm by at most two, proving (8.4).

For a fixed section, the number of containing blocks is the number of
`0->1` boundaries in its cyclic indicator word.  Only the `t` replaced
adjacencies can change that count.  The truncated local time counts
all-containing windows, so the first assertion gives the bound `tq`.

For completeness, pair the at most `tq` deleted target occurrences with the
same number of inserted occurrences.  A single replacement changes the
number of holes and every one-sided overload by at most one, proving (8.5).
On the interval of relevant loads the discrete derivative of the factorial
energy is `O(B+c+1)`, which gives the final bound.
QED.

For an alpha switch `t=3`; for the direct octahedral four-trade `t=4`.
In particular one binary alpha absorption satisfies

\[
 \Delta L_1^-=\Delta L_1^+=0,
 \tag{8.6a}
\]

exactly, by the three-marginal theorem, while for every `q>=2`

\[
 \|\Delta L_q^-\|_1,\ \|\Delta L_q^+\|_1\le6q,
 \qquad
 |\Delta H_q|,|\Delta O_q(c)|\le3q,
 \tag{8.6}
\]

and over all `q<=H` its total load-vector disturbance is at most

\[
 3H(H+1).
 \tag{8.7}
\]

The same estimates hold for open-boundary absorption, since the designated
opening edge is untouched and the alpha switch still replaces exactly three
seams.
Consequently `J` uncontrolled alpha joins can create at most `O(JH^2)`
aggregate occurrence discrepancy over depths `q<=H`.  At the critical
scale `H=Theta(sqrt r)`, this is `o(W)` only if `J=o(W/r)`.  Merely knowing
that the factor has `O(W/r)` components is critical, not sufficient.

## 9. Decorated cycle joining

The seam bounds isolate the exact strengthening that avoids paying the
critical error.

For a cut port, record its oriented `H`-boundary decoration: the two
length-`H` transition neighbourhoods, together with every intersection and
union target of a window of depth at most `H` crossing that port.  Call an
alpha chart:

* **`H`-safe** if all three new seams pass Lemma 8.1; and
* **`H`-neutral** if, in addition, the multiset of all decorated crossing
  windows removed by the chart equals the multiset inserted by it.

### Theorem 9.1 (decorated joining theorem)

An `H`-safe absorbing alpha tree produces a Hamilton cycle with the same three
immediate marginals and with no new residence violation through depth `H`.

An `H`-neutral absorbing alpha tree produces a Hamilton cycle whose complete
intersection- and union-shadow load vectors are identical to those of the
initial factor at every depth `q<=H`.

#### Proof

Apply Theorem 7.1.  Safety is preserved inductively by Lemma 8.1 because the
interiors of all retained path pieces remain safe.  Under neutrality, every
changed crossing-window occurrence is replaced by an occurrence of the same
target, while every interior occurrence is unchanged by Lemma 8.2.  Hence
all load vectors through depth `H` agree exactly.  QED.

Theorem 9.1 is deliberately an exact reduction, not an existence claim.
It says what a successful cycle-joining theorem must count: a spanning
absorbing tree in the hypergraph of **decorated**, dynamically flippable
alpha charts.
Counting undecorated octahedra or proving ordinary support connectivity does
not establish it.

## 10. Final diagnosis

Under the assumed existence of a prescribed-design transition 2-factor:

* lower rainbowness, central degree two, and the complete Catalan upper-load
  vector survive every alpha or octahedral trade exactly;
* the local signed kernel and its primitive moves are completely classified;
* alpha charts merge either three components or, under the exact
  interlacement criterion, two components;
* the tempting four-variable octahedral trade is component-inert;
* there is no two-row switch;
* the three-row alpha chart is the sharp smallest parity-breaking join; and
* residence/hazard damage is confined to explicitly testable seam collars.

The remaining theorem is therefore not “find some local cycle switch.”  It
is the following precise supply statement:

> prove that the `H`-safe (ideally `H`-neutral) alpha charts contain an
> absorbing spanning tree, allowing both ternary and correctly interlaced
> binary absorptions.

This separates the static design problem from the only genuine global
chronology problem and rules out the ordinary octahedral four-trade as a
false shortcut.
