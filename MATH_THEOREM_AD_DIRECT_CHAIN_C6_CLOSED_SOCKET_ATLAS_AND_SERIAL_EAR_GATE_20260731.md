## 2. Exact installed-line characterization

Fix

\[
 K\in{\Omega\choose n-1},\qquad s\notin K,\qquad
 a,b,c\in\Omega\setminus(K+s)                         \tag{2.1}
\]

with `a,b,c` distinct.  Put

\[
\begin{array}{lll}
D_a=K+a,&D_b=K+b,&D_c=K+c,\\
V_{ac}=K+a+c+s,&V_{ab}=K+a+b+s,&V_{bc}=K+b+c+s.
\end{array}                                                   \tag{2.2}
\]

The two palette phases are

\[
 E^0=\{(D_a,V_{ac}),(D_b,V_{ab}),(D_c,V_{bc})\},       \tag{2.3}
\]

\[
 E^1=\{(D_a,V_{ab}),(D_b,V_{bc}),(D_c,V_{ac})\}.       \tag{2.4}
\]

### Theorem 2.1 (installed C6 line)

The phase `E^0` is installed in `M` if and only if

\[
 f_M(V_{ac})=D_a,\qquad
 f_M(V_{ab})=D_b,\qquad
 f_M(V_{bc})=D_c.                                     \tag{2.5}
\]

The opposite phase `E^1` is installed if and only if

\[
 f_M(V_{ac})=D_c,\qquad
 f_M(V_{ab})=D_a,\qquad
 f_M(V_{bc})=D_b.                                     \tag{2.6}
\]

For a strict direct host, `E^1` is a native terminal phase exactly when all
three pairs in (2.4) occur in its direct occurrence catalogue.  Outer
containment alone does not imply this last condition.

#### Proof

The displayed equations simply state which selected lower colour is paired
with each of the three displayed upper colours.  They are equivalent to
membership of all three atoms in the matching.  Direct nativity additionally
requires that every Boolean diamond be a one-coordinate lift or projection
of an authenticated child edge. `square`

### Corollary 2.2 (three target ports)

If `E={x_0,x_1,x_2}` is installed, then for every `i`

\[
                         O_i=E-\{x_i\}                 \tag{2.7}
\]

is a two-atom matching whose complete typed leave inside `res(E)` is
exactly `x_i`.  All three choices have the same virtual old phase `E` and
the same terminal phase.  Moving the puncture from `i` to `j` has signed
covered-resource displacement

\[
                         \chi_{x_i}-\chi_{x_j}.         \tag{2.8}
\]

Activating the terminal phase fills the chosen target.  The lower-to-upper
outer pairing changes by the fixed-point-free 3-cycle (2.3)--(2.4).

#### Proof

The three installed atoms are resource-disjoint.  The suspended-hex identity
says that `E` and its opposite phase use the same complete typed resource
multiset.  Removing one atom therefore omits exactly its four resources,
and subtraction gives (2.8). `square`

### Lemma 2.3 (local `A_3` phase cosets)

Let the cyclic permutation `rho=(a b c)` fix `K,s`.  The six atoms in
(2.3)--(2.4) split into exactly two `rho`-orbits, namely `E^0` and `E^1`.
Every odd permutation of `{a,b,c}` interchanges these two orbits.
Consequently:

1. local cyclic symmetry only rotates the three target ports of one phase;
   it neither creates another installed line nor proves terminal nativity;
2. if the **direct candidate host** restricted to this six-atom support is
   invariant under one odd local permutation, then installation of either
   phase implies that the opposite phase is native.

The second assertion concerns the candidate host, not the selected matching:
an exact matching cannot install both phases simultaneously because they use
the same three lower and three upper resources.

#### Proof

Applying `rho` cyclically permutes the three atoms in (2.3), and separately
the three atoms in (2.4).  Swapping `b,c`, for example, maps every atom of
(2.3) to one atom of (2.4).  This proves the orbit and host-invariance
claims. `square`

This `S_3/A_3` split is the first finite bottleneck in (0.1): most installed
phases lie wholly in one available coset while their complementary coset is
not native in the strict direct host.

## 3. Literal closure criterion

Let `A` be the three old physical edges of an installed line and `B` the
three terminal physical edges.  Remove `A` from the complete three-rail
forest.  The path orientation exposes one outgoing role slot at every old
tail and one incoming role slot at every old head.

Adjoin one root `rho` and one root edge to the declared start of every old
path.  This turns the old forest into a spanning tree `T`.  For `a in A`
and `b in B`, let

\[
 X(a,b)=1\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 a\text{ lies on the }T\text{-path between the endpoints of }b. \tag{3.1}
\]

### Theorem 3.1 (closed socket test)

An installed line is a literal closed socket for the declared state if and
only if all of the following hold.

1. Its opposite phase is native in the direct occurrence host.
2. `B` uses exactly the six role-labelled slots exposed by deleting `A`.
3. `det_(F_2) X=1`.
4. The terminal side matching respects every anchor cap.
5. The terminal outer pairing agrees on any declared protected cap domain.

Under these conditions the complete terminal support is a path forest with
the same declared start bank, exactly one start per component, and an
endpoint permutation.  Conversely every constant-support replacement which
leaves all unedited oriented fragments fixed must satisfy these five rows.

#### Proof

Row 1 is the exact strict occurrence condition.  Row 2 is exactly the
pointwise incoming/outgoing capacity equation; it also makes every unchanged
fragment retain its orientation.  After virtually completing any planted
target, old and new phases have equal size.  The rooted fundamental-cycle
basis theorem gives row 3 as the necessary and sufficient spanning-tree
condition.  Removing the root then gives one declared start per component.
Rows 4--5 are the remaining literal capacity resources.  Every listed row
is necessary for a constant-support word, and together they are sufficient.
`square`

The source does not contain a protected pointwise pairing or compiler cap.
The finite field `unprotected_local_capacity_compatible=true` therefore
means only that the terminal physical degree and side-anchor capacities
pass.  It is not a common-cap certificate, a preassigned cap map, or a
global compiler certificate.

### Theorem 3.2 (endpoint parity)

Every role-closed C6 socket acts on the declared path labels either as the
identity or as a 3-cycle.  In particular its endpoint permutation is even,
and no word consisting only of such sockets can correct an odd endpoint
permutation.

#### Proof

Deleting the three old directed edges exposes three tail slots and three
head slots.  Contract every unchanged directed fragment.  Before the new
edges are inserted, those fragments give a fixed bijection

\[
 \{\hbox{old sources}\}\sqcup\{\hbox{three exposed heads}\}
 \longrightarrow
 \{\hbox{old terminals}\}\sqcup\{\hbox{three exposed tails}\}. \tag{3.2}
\]

The opposite C6 phase composes the last three labels with a 3-cycle.  In a
determinant expansion, eliminating the three temporary cut labels
contributes the same factor `(-1)^3` before and after the replacement.
Hence the relative sign of the decoded source-to-terminal bijection is the
sign of that 3-cycle, namely `+1`.  Only the old path components containing
the three deleted edges can acquire different terminal labels, so the
relative action has support at most three.  An even permutation supported
on at most three labels is the identity or a 3-cycle. `square`

This endpoint parity is separate from the outer-pairing parity, although a
full C6 is even on both.  A frozen old outer pairing is incompatible on all
three changed lower colours, because the relative outer action has no fixed
point.

## 4. Banks and the all-parameter orbit lemma

Let `L(X)` be any family of installed lines in one fixed decorated state
`X`, after any chosen individual filters.  It is a linear 3-uniform
hypergraph on the incumbent atoms: two distinct suspended-C6 lines share at
most one atom.  Put

\[
                         h=|L(X)|,\qquad
 \Delta_X=\max_x d_{L(X)}(x).                           \tag{4.1}
\]

### Lemma 4.1 (exact greedy bank bound)

`L(X)` contains a phase-disjoint bank of order at least

\[
                  \left\lceil{h\over3\Delta_X-2}\right\rceil.  \tag{4.2}
\]

If every line in the family is role-closed, a phase-disjoint bank is also
resource- and role-slot-private.

#### Proof

One line meets at most `3(Delta_X-1)` other lines.  A maximal matching of
lines therefore covers the family by closed neighbourhoods of order at most
`3Delta_X-2`, proving (4.2).  The complete resource union of a C6 line is
the union of its three incumbent atoms.  Distinct incumbent atoms of `M`
are resource-disjoint, proving the last assertion. `square`

Resource privacy alone does not factor the rooted graphic row.  For socket
blocks `(A_i,B_i)`, form

\[
 X_{ij}=X[A_i,B_j].                                      \tag{4.3}
\]

Call a bank **block closed** when its old phases are disjoint and
`X_ij=X_ji=0` for every distinct pair.  Its total transfer matrix is block
diagonal, so every subbank is a rooted basis in any order.  More generally,
if the directed block-dependency graph `i->j` defined by `X_ij != 0` is
acyclic, every subbank is legal in a topological order.  These are exact
sufficient composition rows; arbitrary simultaneous rooted legality is a
full determinant condition and is not pairwise.

### Theorem 4.2 (internal stabilizer-orbit atlas)

Let

\[
                         G\le\operatorname {Aut}(X)     \tag{4.4}
\]

be the genuine stabilizer of the matching, roots, role slots, protected cap
and endpoint decoder.  Let `O=G E` be an orbit of one legal installed line,
and suppose the atoms incident with `O` form one `G`-orbit `W`, every member
of which lies in exactly `r>=1` orbit lines.  Then

\[
                         |O|={|W|r\over3}               \tag{4.5}
\]

and `O` contains a resource-private bank of order at least

\[
             {|W|r\over3(3r-2)}\ge {|W|\over9}.         \tag{4.6}
\]

Suppose additionally that every orbit line has at most `delta` extra
rooted cross-block conflicts beyond phase intersections.  Then there is a
block-closed bank of order at least

\[
                     {|W|r\over3(3r-2+\delta)}.         \tag{4.7}
\]

In particular, if `|W|>=beta P` and `delta<=kappa r`, its density is at
least

\[
                         {\beta\over3(3+\kappa)}.        \tag{4.8}
\]

Targets and slot signatures are relabelled by `G`, endpoint actions are
conjugated, and rooted determinants and protected-cap compatibility are
invariant.

#### Proof

Double-count incident pairs `(x,E)` to obtain (4.5).  Lemma 4.1 gives
(4.6).  The block-conflict graph has maximum degree at most
`3(r-1)+delta`; its greedy independent-set bound gives (4.7), and (4.8)
follows by enlarging the denominator to `3(3+kappa)r`.  Every descriptor is
defined from state resources stabilized by `G`, proving equivariance.
`square`

The group must be the **internal** decorated-state stabilizer.  The full
`Sym(Omega)` orbit sends `(X,E)` to `(gX,gE)` and does not install `gE` back
in `X`.  From `n=4` onward the authenticated selected side forests already
have components of several sizes, so their stabilizer is not transitive on
all selected edges.  Theorem 4.2 is a usable all-parameter sufficient lemma,
not a property proved for the stored chain.

## 6. Weakest viable replacement

### Theorem 6.1 (serial C6-ear criterion)

Let `mathfrak X` be a guarded class of reachable decorated states, closed
under inverse legal moves.  For an endpoint permutation `sigma` on `k`
labels, let `omega(sigma)` be the number of odd cycles, including fixed
points.  If `sigma` is even, its minimum unrestricted 3-cycle word length is

\[
                         d_3(\sigma)={k-\omega(\sigma)\over2}.    \tag{6.1}
\]

Suppose that whenever `X in mathfrak X` has nonidentity even endpoint
permutation `sigma_X`, there is a legal C6 ear with endpoint action `tau`
such that

\[
 \omega(\tau\sigma_X)=\omega(\sigma_X)+2,              \tag{6.2}
\]

its successor remains in `mathfrak X`, and its actual slot, root, reserve,
cap and compiler guards pass.  Then repeated ears reach identity in exactly
`d_3(sigma_X)` steps.  This is optimal among C6 endpoint words.

#### Proof

A 3-cycle changes `omega` by at most two.  Equation (6.2) decreases (6.1)
by one, so induction reaches zero in exactly the displayed number of steps.
The lower bound follows from the same two-unit bound.  The standard cycle
decomposition of an even permutation into `(k-omega)/2` 3-cycles proves
that (6.1) is the unrestricted metric. `square`

If the endpoint permutation is odd, no C6 word can reach identity by
Theorem 3.2.  One odd actuator is mandatory.  The authenticated vocabulary
contains two scoped candidates, neither proved uniformly available:

* a representative/common-bank-changing serial ear such as the strict
  five-edge `n=3` packet; or
* an even-half-length sparse circuit, first the `ell=6`/`C12` family in its
  known dimension range.

For target mobility, a static disjoint bank is also stronger than necessary.
The authoritative multislice theorem supplies clean loose C6 trees with one
movable hole per component.  The constructive replacement target is
therefore:

1. correlate the body leave with one port of each planted loose tree;
2. activate/route those holes by overlapping C6 ears;
3. use a state-dependent odd ear if endpoint parity demands it; and
4. enforce the full mixed endpoint/pairing/slot/root/cap/compiler state after
   every serial step.

This replacement is weaker than a fixed positive-density word-closed atlas
and is not refuted by (0.1)--(0.4).  Its uniform state-by-state availability
is the precise remaining theorem.
