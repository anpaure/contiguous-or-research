# Protected refinement can quarantine a small upper-zero bank exactly when
# its absorbers preserve a robust functional core

**Date:** 2026-08-01  
**Lane:** H3 / recuttable protected host / residual upper absorption  
**Status:** exact conditional absorber theorem, exact robust-Hall
characterization, exact zero-damage canonical refinement lift, and sharp
projection/matching obstructions.  The first authenticated `k=17` dense bank
has 187 isolated sockets and 78 rank-ten zeros.  A later bank closes both
singleton families, but its exact coloured q1 selector is UNSAT on a
four-edge bow tie.  No q1-complete refined bank, topology join, exact product
residence, deeper deck or compiler is constructed here.

## 0. Verdict

The first literal `7,612`-piece refinement is a real change of regime.  Under the
relaxed necessary two-block residence test it has no physical endpoint or
lower-colour singleton zero, and each of its three physical two-shore
projections has a perfect matching.  The former `1,289`-colour obstruction is
therefore not stable under simultaneous refinement.

It does not yet enter an absorber theorem.  Exactly `78` cut-killed rank-ten
targets have no relaxed-resident atomic seam provider.  They must be served
by a further recut, a resident facet/socket macro, or an interior rethread.
Moreover, three perfect projections do not imply a common
tail--head--colour matching.  Here the stronger failure is literal: exactly
`187` pieces have incoming support only in one orientation and outgoing
support only in the other, so the fixed bank's common-orientation q1 CNF is
UNSAT by unit propagation.

Further cut choice has now removed all 187 isolated-socket rows and all 78
rank-ten zero rows simultaneously.  This proves that pure refinement can
close the singleton support layer.  It still does not enter the functional
absorber theorem: the resulting `7,612`-piece bank has an exact four-seam
bow-tie UNSAT core in the simultaneous socket/socket/lower-colour selector.

There are two exact positive theorems beyond this obstruction.  First, once
a coloured socket selector exists, every later pure split has a canonical
self-closing lift: retain all old seams and insert the old factor edge across
each new cut.  After contracting these forced split modules, the functional
core, component topology and physical owner chronology are unchanged.  This
is a zero-damage (`a=0`) regenerative theorem and needs no robust-Hall margin.

Second, for a boundary-changing absorber, first choose the
exceptional upper absorbers by ordinary Hall in a **closed functional
target--site atlas**.  Contract them.  If they delete at most `h` balanced
task/slot pairs and the already oriented residual functional graph is
`h`-robust under balanced deletion, then the residual graph still has a
perfect matching.  For a balanced graph on `N+N` vertices, that robustness
has the exact neighbourhood characterization

\[
 |N_G(X)|\ge \min\{N,|X|+h\}
 \qquad(\varnothing\ne X\subseteq L).                 \tag{0.1}
\]

Thus an `O(1)` or `o(N)` exceptional bank can be absorbed with `O(1)` or
`o(N)` support only when the local packets are closed and the functional
core has the corresponding deletion margin.  Singleton support and perfect
projections alone prove neither condition, and support monotonicity alone
gives no bounded time to reach the first coloured selector.  A bounded
rank-improvement hypothesis is necessary.

## 2. Closed exceptional absorbers

Let `Z` be a bank of exceptional upper targets.  More generally, one may
adjoin a bank `O` of orientation-conflict pieces and treat
\(D=Z\sqcup O\) as the primary defect bank; a packet credited to
`O` must export one common incoming/outgoing orientation.  Let `S` be a bank of
physical refinement sites disjoint from the fixed protected packet.  A
column `gamma(z,s)` (or `gamma(o,s)`) is a **closed protected absorber** when it records all of
the following in one literal packet.

1. It changes only a declared owner interval at `s`, covers the one credited
   primary target `z`, and retains every old owner exactly once.
2. Every new lower or upper casualty is restored internally or by a named
   child contained in the same packet.  After closure the packet exports no
   additional target demand.
3. Its exact boundary-age transition is the same on every legal incoming
   state as the slot it replaces, or its new state is included literally in
   the outer functional configuration.
4. It preserves the required internal, prefix, suffix, total and exterior
   interval decks, or carries a literal occurrence ticket for each declared
   exception.
5. Its fixed-`M_0` roots, head/tail roles, protected resources and quotient
   topology delta are declared.  Topology is neutral or belongs to one
   pre-certified potential-ordered linear-forest family.
6. After the packet is contracted, it removes equally many vertices from
   the two shores of the residual functional matching problem.  All
   remaining old functional atoms outside those removed vertices stay legal
   with the same orientation and labels.

The last sentence is the **functional persistence** condition.  A packet
that repairs `z` but changes distant endpoint states or deletes unrelated
candidate atoms is not closed for this theorem.

The absorber atlas is **functional by site** when the target labels and
sites both have capacity one, the non-target resource footprint of every
`gamma(z,s)` lies in a site block `R_s`, the blocks `R_s` are pairwise
disjoint, and every partial target--site injection installs simultaneously
with all six certificates above.

Form the bipartite absorber graph (written for the upper-target-only case)

\[
 H=(Z,S;E_H),\qquad zs\in E_H
       \Longleftrightarrow \gamma(z,s)\text{ is closed}.       \tag{2.1}
\]

On this deliberately factorized face, Hall is exact.  It is not a claim
about the raw multi-resource socket catalogue.

## 3. Robust functional cores

Let `G=(L,R;E)` be a balanced bipartite functional graph with
`|L|=|R|=N`.  Orientations, protected contractions and every nonfunctional
resource row are fixed before `G` is defined.

### Definition 3.1 (balanced-deletion robustness)

For `0<=h<N`, call `G` **h-robust** when, for every

\[
 A\subseteq L,\quad B\subseteq R,
 \qquad |A|=|B|\le h,                                  \tag{3.1}
\]

the graph `G-(A union B)` has a perfect matching.

This condition also extends every forced matching of size at most `h`:
contract its edges and match the remaining balanced deletion.

### Theorem 3.2 (exact robust-Hall characterization)

The graph `G` is `h`-robust if and only if

\[
 \boxed{
 |N_G(X)|\ge \min\{N,|X|+h\}
 \quad\text{for every nonempty }X\subseteq L.}          \tag{3.2}
\]

#### Proof

Assume (3.2), choose `A,B` as in (3.1), and put `a=|A|`.  For every nonempty
`X subseteq L-A`,

\[
 |N_{G-A-B}(X)|
 \ge \min\{N,|X|+h\}-a.                               \tag{3.3}
\]

If `|X|+h<=N`, the right side is at least `|X|`; otherwise it is
`N-a>=|X|`.  Hall gives a perfect matching.

Conversely, suppose `G` is `h`-robust.  If `|X|<=N-h`, delete `h` left
vertices outside `X`.  Were `|N_G(X)|<|X|+h`, one could delete `h` right
vertices containing as much of `N_G(X)` as possible and leave `X`
deficient.  Hence `|N_G(X)|>=|X|+h`.

If `|X|>N-h`, put `a=N-|X|<h` and delete all `a` left vertices outside
`X`.  If `N_G(X)` omitted a right vertex, deleting `a` right vertices from
`N_G(X)` as far as possible would leave fewer than `N-a=|X|` neighbours.
Thus `N_G(X)=R`.  These are exactly the two cases of (3.2).  \(\square\)

For a known restricted absorber family, arbitrary-deletion robustness is
stronger than necessary.  The exact weaker condition is simply Hall in
`G-(A_I union B_I)` for every selectable absorber family `I`.  Theorem 3.2
is useful because it removes the dependence on which sites Hall selects.

## 4. Protected small-bank absorption

### Theorem 4.1 (protected refinement/absorber theorem)

Assume:

1. the closed absorber graph `H` satisfies
   \[
                    |N_H(X)|\ge |X|\qquad(X\subseteq Z); \tag{4.1}
   \]
2. every absorber removes or contracts at most `a` pairs of vertices from
   the functional core;
3. different selected sites have disjoint deletion pairs and their packets
   commute;
4. after every absorber family is installed, the surviving functional graph
   contains the corresponding vertex deletion of one fixed graph `G`; and
5. `G` is `h`-robust for
   \[
                              h=a|Z|<N.                  \tag{4.2}
   \]

Then all targets in `Z` can be absorbed simultaneously and the residual
functional graph still has a perfect matching.  If every packet uses at
most `sigma` protected owner positions and names at most `c` new cuts, the
whole exceptional bank uses at most

\[
                  \sigma|Z|\text{ owners},\qquad
                  c|Z|\text{ cut positions}.            \tag{4.3}
\]

Every palette, residence, deck, root and topology conclusion included in
the packet and fixed-core certificates is retained.

#### Proof

Hall in `H` selects distinct sites for all targets.  Functional-site closure
installs their packets simultaneously and covers the entire exceptional
bank without creating a child demand.  Contract the local packets.  Their
union deletes equal core shores of some size at most `a|Z|`.  Theorem 3.2
gives a perfect matching of the residual core, and functional persistence
makes every selected core edge legal after installation.  The support ledger
is additive because the site blocks are disjoint.  \(\square\)

The statement takes the fixed protected bank as already contracted.  If a
later protected reservation deletes at most `p` additional balanced core
pairs and otherwise preserves `G`, replace (4.2) by
`h=p+a|Z|<N`.

### Corollary 4.2 (a checkable absorber-site condition)

If every target of `Z` has at least `D` closed sites and every site belongs
to at most `D` target menus, then (4.1) holds by double counting.  More
generally the exact obstruction is the Hall deficiency of `H`; individual
nonempty menus are insufficient.

### Corollary 4.3 (asymptotic support)

For a family with core size `N=N_m` and exceptional size `z_m`, bounded
`a,sigma,c` gives an `o(N)` absorber bank whenever `z_m=o(N)`.  For the
resident facet/socket scale `sigma=O(d)`, the exact requirement is

\[
                             z_md=o(N_m).               \tag{4.4}
\]

If (N_m=O(C_md)=O(W_md/m)) and (d=O(\sqrt m)), then
(z_m=o(N_m)) and \(\sigma=O(d)\) imply \(\sigma z_m=o(W_m)\).  For
\(z_m=O(1)\), the charge is \(O(d)\).

These are support conclusions only.  They do not produce the sites, the
robust margin, or the terminal compiler.

### Corollary 4.4 (existence versus local rethread cost)

Theorem 4.1 may replace a previously chosen core matching by a completely
different one.  Suppose instead that, after every intermediate contraction,
each unmatched core task has an alternating augmenting path of radius at
most `r`, and one core column has physical support at most `lambda`.  At most
`h=a|Z|` augmentations are needed, so the additional changed-core ledger is
bounded by

\[
              h(2r+1)\text{ columns},\qquad
              h(2r+1)\lambda\text{ support}.            \tag{4.5}
\]

Without this alternating-radius hypothesis, robust Hall proves existence but
not a local edit bound.  A bidiagonal functional graph can have a perfect
matching while a defect-one state has a unique augmenting path through all
`N` tasks.

### Corollary 4.5 (canonical pure refinement is a zero-damage absorber)

Suppose a cut factor already has a coloured socket selector: every physical
socket is used once and every selected deleted lower colour is used once.
Make any allowed set `D` of additional internal cuts while retaining every
old cut.  Keep every old selected seam on its persistent outer sockets and,
for each `g in D`, select the old factor adjacency across `g` between the two
new sockets.  This canonically lifts the selector and uses exactly the new
lower colour `g`.

After contracting these canonical edges, the selected functional core is
literally the old core.  Component count is unchanged, and every factor
upper occurrence moved from an internal edge to a seam is restored by the
same adjacency.  Expanding the split children consecutively gives the same
physical owner word.  Hence every residence, all-width interval-deck,
erosion or compiler certificate which is functorial in that word transports
literally.  A certificate naming the old piece as an indivisible protected
atom requires the cut to be declared transparent for that atom.

Thus Theorem 4.1 has `a=0` for canonical pure refinements: no robust-Hall
margin is needed.  Robustness must be measured only after the forced
self-closing modules are contracted; their uncontracted degree-one sockets
would give a false expansion failure.

For an opened directed forest with strict potential `phi`, order the children
of each split piece in its actual traversal direction and use the
lexicographic potential `(phi(P),j)`.  Old external arcs increase the first
coordinate and canonical child arcs increase the second.  An unopened cycle
has only cyclic order, not a strict global potential.

### Theorem 4.6 (bounded regenerative rank-and-ticket descent)

For a cut bank `C` with `P(C)` pieces, let `A(C)` be the three-resource seam
hypergraph: one atom consumes two physical sockets and its selected deleted
lower colour.  Let `B` be the prescribed protected selector atom bank, and
let `nu_3(C)` be the maximum size of a three-resource matching which extends
`B` (equivalently, contract `B`, delete its conflicts, and add `|B|` to the
residual matching rank).  Put

\[
 \delta_3(C)=P(C)-\nu_3(C).                              \tag{4.6}
\]

After `q` pure cuts, canonical self-edges give

\[
 \nu_3(C')\ge\nu_3(C)+q,
 \qquad \delta_3(C')\le\delta_3(C).                     \tag{4.7}
\]

Assume hereditarily that every reachable state with `delta_3>0` has an
allowed bundle of at most `kappa_0` further cuts which strictly decreases
`delta_3`.  Then at most

\[
                         \kappa_0\delta_3(C)             \tag{4.8}
\]

cuts produce a lower-colour-exact socket perfect matching.

Once `delta_3=0`, let `eta(C)` be the minimum number of required immediate-
upper targets left uncovered by such a perfect matching, counting both
uncut internal factor edges and selected seam labels.  Canonical refinement
never increases `eta`: it lifts an old perfect matching and self-restores
the upper value of every newly cut factor edge.  If every reachable state
with `delta_3=0<eta` has an allowed bundle of at most `kappa_1` cuts which
keeps `delta_3=0` and lowers `eta`, then from the first perfect-matching state
`C_1` another at most

\[
                         \kappa_1\eta(C_1)              \tag{4.9}
\]

cuts give an immediate-upper-complete coloured selector.

#### Proof

Every new cut contributes two new sockets, one new lower colour and one
canonical atom disjoint from the old resource ground, proving (4.7).
The two claimed bounds are repeated descent of nonnegative integer
potentials.  The upper monotonicity follows from the canonical lift described
above.  \(\square\)

The rank-improvement assumptions are the load-bearing regenerative input.
They are not consequences of nonisolation or of raw provider support.  If
one packet has owner support at most `sigma`, the corresponding protected
charge is at most
`sigma(delta_3(C)+eta(C_1))` when the two `kappa` values are absorbed into
that packet bound.  Bounded packets and `o(N)` initial debts therefore give
`o(N)` support.

### Corollary 4.7 (conditional two-cut singleton support cover)

Let `I_0` be the isolated physical sockets and `U_0` the immediate-upper
labels with no relaxed provider.  Ignore only the age inequalities and call
a Johnson socket edge **raw** when its lower colour is already selected.
For a raw edge `e=st`, cut the first factor gap inward from each nonsingleton
endpoint socket; require both gaps to be allowed and unprotected.  This uses
at most two cuts and makes both endpoint owners singleton children, so every
present coordinate has age `infinity` and `e` becomes relaxed-legal.

If a raw edge family `E_*` has endpoints covering `I_0` and upper labels
covering `U_0`, the union of these first-inward cuts repairs every singleton
row, creates none, and has size at most

\[
                              2|E_*|.                   \tag{4.10}
\]

In particular, when every task has such a raw witness, choosing one witness
per task gives at most `2(|I_0|+|U_0|)` cuts.  This is monotone labelled set
cover, not Hall: the support edges are not yet selected capacity-one seams.
For the first dense bank it gives the conditional number
`2(187+78)=530`; the required protected-gap cover has not been audited.

## 5. Sharp obstructions

### Proposition 5.1 (three perfect projections do not synchronize)

Use sources `s_1,s_2`, heads `h_1,h_2`, colours `a,b`, and atoms

\[
 (s_1,h_1,a),\quad(s_1,h_2,b),\quad
 (s_2,h_1,b),\quad(s_2,h_2,a).                         \tag{5.1}
\]

Each of the source--head, source--colour and colour--head projections has a
perfect matching.  Every source--head perfect matching in the original atom
set, however, repeats its colour: the diagonal uses `a,a` and the
off-diagonal uses `b,b`.  There is no common three-resource matching.

Thus the three perfect projection ranks in (1.1) do not define the
functional core `G` of Theorem 4.1.  One common orientation and one literal
three-resource selection, or a proved functional contraction leaving one
Hall row, are still required.

The later zero-singleton dense bank realizes this obstruction literally.
Its four-edge bow tie uses three pieces and two degree-two lower colours.
Either choice for the first colour forces the central piece into the
orientation incompatible with the only remaining choice for the second.
All four seams have the same upper label, so even immediate-upper singleton
closure cannot detect the contradiction.

### Proposition 5.2 (singleton absorbers can have linear defect)

If every target in `Z` has the same single site, then every target has a
closed local packet but at most one can be installed.  The absorber defect
is `|Z|-1`.  Hence the size `78` is not by itself an absorber theorem.

### Proposition 5.3 (a perfect core need not survive one absorber)

Let `G` be the identity matching on two left and two right vertices.  Delete
the first left vertex and the second right vertex.  The residual two
vertices are nonadjacent.  Thus ordinary Hall at `h=0` does not imply even
one-step balanced-deletion robustness.

This is why a perfect projection, or even one completed functional matching,
cannot simply be frozen before the exceptional absorbers are planted.

### Proposition 5.4 (monotonicity gives no bounded repair time)

For every `m`, there is an abstract self-closing refinement system with
initial coloured-socket defect one in which the defect remains one for the
first `m-1` refinements and vanishes only at the `m`-th.

#### Proof

Take the direct sum of a fixed defect-one bow tie and `m` private canonical
split atoms.  Every split adds its two sockets, private lower colour and
self-edge, so all old support persists and the defect never increases.
Expose the one alternative bow-tie edge only with the final split.  Before
then the bow tie remains a direct summand of defect one; afterward the new
alternative resolves it.  \(\square\)

This abstract construction is not asserted to be a Boolean factor family.
It proves that the monotonicity axioms alone cannot supply a dimension-free
`kappa_0` in Theorem 4.6.  A Boolean theorem must prove bounded
obstruction-breaking expansion from its actual socket geometry.

## 6. Exact interpretation of the two dense banks

For the first bank, the socket theorem turns the `187+78` ledger into one
monotone support-cover problem.  Every isolated socket has a raw incident
edge and every missing rank-ten target has a raw provider.  If these tasks
admit a raw witness cover whose first inward gaps avoid the protected bank,
Corollary 4.7 closes them with at most 530 further cuts.  This is now an exact
conditional cut bound, rather than a hypothetical private-absorber count.
It is not yet audited that all 265 tasks possess freely isolable protected-
safe witnesses.

The later cut-choice bank closes both singleton families with no increase in
piece count.  It therefore proves that the support obstruction can disappear
inside the protected factor family.  Its exact q1 selector is nevertheless
UNSAT by the four-edge bow tie.  In the notation of Theorem 4.6, this bank
has no singleton debt but still has `delta_3>0`.  Thus no bounded theorem can
use `|I_0|+|U_0|` as its only potential.

The sharp finite continuation is a monotone cut CEGAR on the returned
coloured obstruction.  A refinement column must add an alternative for one
of the two bow-tie colours, change the central socket incidence, or replace a
selected colour; the exact q1 selector is then resolved again.  Theorem 4.6
turns such a loop into an `O(delta_3)` refinement theorem only after a
uniform bounded rank-improvement constant `kappa_0` is proved.  The current
Boolean socket geometry supplies no such bound yet.

The first CEGAR step confirms this distinction.  A single global cut change
preserves every singleton row and raises one bow-tie colour's degree from two
to ten, destroying the first core.  The new q1 instance is still UNSAT and
returns a second constant-size core.  Therefore “break one returned core” is
not the rank-improvement hypothesis of Theorem 4.6; the packet must increase
`nu_3` or be charged to another separately proved well-founded potential.

Once a q1 selector is found, Corollary 4.5 changes the situation completely:
all later pure refinements preserve that selector, its components and every
chronology-functorial certificate with `a=0`.  Robust Hall is needed only for
an absorber that changes outer boundaries or consumes old functional
resources.  In that case the weakest exact condition is Hall in each actual
contracted residual graph; (3.2) is the uniform sufficient substitute.

The relaxed socket predicate remains necessary only.  A new selector which
uses noncanonical cross edges must still pass the exact product residence
automaton, subtour/graphic connectivity, ranks 11--17, arbitrary exterior
windows and the terminal compiler.  Canonical lifting preserves those rows
only after they have already been certified on a physical owner chronology.

Accordingly the live order is

\[
 \boxed{
 \begin{array}{c}
 \text{break the returned coloured bow-tie and solve the exact q1 selector;}\\
 \text{freeze/contract its functional core;}\\
 \text{use canonical zero-damage refinement whenever possible;}\\
 \text{use robust Hall only for boundary-changing closed absorbers;}\\
 \text{then impose topology, exact residence, deep decks and compiler.}
 \end{array}}                                             \tag{6.1}
\]
