## 2. Three half-shifts and the marked-height convention

For every integer `j`, define

\[
 H_j=\{h\in\mathbb Z_k:d_{j+hC}=0\}.
                                                        \tag{2.1}
\]

Every `H_j` has size `m`.  There is exactly one gap-three zero departure in
each residue `j`; let its height be `lambda_j`.  Then

\[
 \lambda_j\in H_j,
 \qquad H_{j+C}=H_j-1,
 \qquad \lambda_{j+C}=\lambda_j-1.                    \tag{2.2}
\]

Write

\[
 \rho=2^{-1}\pmod k,
 \qquad \phi(h)=-\rho h\pmod k.                       \tag{2.3}
\]

There are three different half-shifts:

\[
 t={W+1\over2},\qquad
 s={W-1\over2},\qquad
 \delta={C+1\over2}.                                 \tag{2.4}
\]

Here `t` is inverse two modulo `W`, `s` is the physical complement shift,
and `delta` is the quotient-controller self-duality shift.  None of these
may be interchanged.

The elementary height relations are

\[
 H_j\cap H_{j+1}=\varnothing,                         \tag{2.5}
\]

\[
 \mathbb Z_k\setminus(H_j\cup H_{j+1})
     =\{\lambda_{j-1}\},                              \tag{2.6}
\]

and

\[
 H_{j+2}=(H_j\setminus\{\lambda_j\})
             \cup\{\lambda_{j-1}\}.                 \tag{2.7}
\]

Indeed, adjacent zero heights would give `00`.  Since two adjacent fibres
are disjoint and have total size `2m=k-1`, their unique missing coordinate
has pattern `11`; it lies in the unique `0110` block whose first zero is the
gap-three departure in residue `j-1`.  A zero in residue `j` advances by two
unless it is the unique gap-three departure, while the unique new zero came
from residue `j-1`.  This proves (2.5)--(2.7).

## 3. Exact run-boundary and controller theorem

Let

\[
 T_i=\{x\in\mathbb Z_k:c_{i-xC}=1\}.
\]

Then

\[
 T_i=\overline{\phi(H_{2i})},
 \qquad T_i\cap T_{i+1}=\phi(H_{2i+1}).              \tag{3.1}
\]

### Theorem 3.1 (the common start/end label word)

If

\[
 T_{i+1}=T_i-\{\alpha_i\}+\{\beta_i\},
\]

then

\[
 \boxed{
 \alpha_i=\phi(\lambda_{2i-1}),
 \qquad
 \beta_i=\phi(\lambda_{2i}).}                       \tag{3.2}
\]

Moreover,

\[
 \alpha_{i+C}=\alpha_i+1,
 \qquad \beta_{i+C}=\beta_i+1,                      \tag{3.3}
\]

and

\[
 \alpha_{i+t}=\beta_i,
 \qquad \beta_{i+t}=\alpha_{i+1}.                  \tag{3.4}
\]

For the generic run-transversal parametrization, let `sigma_j` be the
coordinate whose scalar `c`-run starts in physical residue `j`, and let
`epsilon_j` be the coordinate whose run ends there.  Then

\[
 \boxed{
 \sigma_j=\beta_{j-1}=\phi(\lambda_{2j-2}),
 \qquad
 \epsilon_j=\alpha_j=\phi(\lambda_{2j-1}).}         \tag{3.5}
\]

The literal start and end positions are respectively

\[
 j-\sigma_jC,
 \qquad j-\epsilon_jC\pmod W.                       \tag{3.6}
\]

#### Proof

Apply (2.7) from `2i` to `2i+2`.  Removing `lambda_(2i)` from the absent
set inserts `phi(lambda_(2i))` into `T`, while adjoining
`lambda_(2i-1)` to the absent set deletes its `phi`-image.  This is (3.2).
Equation (2.2) gives (3.3).  Since `2t=W+1` and a shift by `W=kC` changes a
height by `-k=0` modulo `k`, (3.4) follows.

A scalar start `p=j-xC` is the insertion seen in the transition
`T_(j-1)->T_j`; hence `x=beta_(j-1)`.  A scalar end is the deletion in
`T_j->T_(j+1)`; hence `x=alpha_j`.  This gives (3.5)--(3.6).  In
particular, the two endpoint functions cannot be optimized independently.
∎

### Corollary 3.3 (exact first-hit criterion)

Fix `i` and define, for `x in G_i`,

\[
 \tau_i(x)=\min\{a\ge0:\lambda_{2i+2a+1}=x\},       \tag{3.16}

## 4. Static puncture Hall theorem

The first marked-shadow obstruction disappears completely after forgetting
chronology.

### Theorem 4.1 (cyclic-necklace puncture matching)

For every odd `k=2m+1`, form a bipartite graph whose left vertices are
cyclic necklaces of rank `m-1`, whose right vertices are cyclic necklaces
of rank `m`, and where two necklaces are adjacent when some representatives
`A subset B`.  This graph has a matching saturating every left vertex.

#### Proof

Let a family of left necklaces contain `a` free necklaces and `b` necklaces
with stabilizer order three.  These are the only possibilities because a
stabilizer order divides both the ground-cycle length and the set size, and

\[
 \gcd(k,m-1)=\gcd(3,m-1).                            \tag{4.1}
\]

Every rank-`m` necklace is free because `gcd(k,m)=1`.

Lift the family to actual subsets.  It has size

\[
 ak+{bk\over3}.                                     \tag{4.2}
\]

Every rank-`m-1` set has `m+2` rank-`m` extensions, while a rank-`m` set
contains at most `m` members of the lifted family.  The lifted upper shadow
is exactly the lift of the quotient neighborhood, and every upper orbit has
size `k`.  Hence

\[
 |N|\ge {m+2\over m}\left(a+{b\over3}\right).       \tag{4.3}
\]

There is a second bound.  All short sets are invariant under the unique
order-three subgroup.  Two distinct such rank-`m-1` sets differ by a
nonempty union of three-point subgroup orbits on each side, so their union
has size at least `m+2`.  No rank-`m` set contains two of them.  Their lifted
upper shadows are therefore pairwise disjoint, and

\[
 |N|\ge {m+2\over3}b.                               \tag{4.4}
\]

If `a >= (m-1)b/3`, (4.3) gives

\[
 |N|-(a+b)
 \ge {2\over m}\left(a-{m-1\over3}b\right)\ge0.
\]

If `a <= (m-1)b/3`, (4.4) gives `|N|>=a+b`.  Hall's
condition follows.  The case `k=3,m=1` is included: its sole rank-zero
necklace matches its sole rank-one necklace.  ∎

### Corollary 4.2 (full static `q2` marking)

Take one rank-`m` representative from every necklace.  One can choose a
marked element in each representative so that the punctures contain every
rank-`m-1` necklace.  Match every lower necklace to a distinct incident
upper necklace, rotate representatives to make literal containment, and
mark the unique difference.  Mark unused upper necklaces arbitrarily.

Thus a complete middle necklace transversal has enough static marked
capacity for all lower `q2` and upper `q1` orbits.  This corollary does not
order the upper necklaces and does not impose (3.8), (3.9), the helical
closure, residence, or any wider `J`-tower.

## 6. A deterministic height-sorted Euler schema

There is first a complete nonlinear placement form.

### Theorem 6.1 (marked-height circular placement)

Choose an arbitrary function

\[
 \lambda:\mathbb Z_C\longrightarrow\mathbb Z_k
\]

and represent its `C` marked special zeros by

\[
 L_j=j+\lambda(j)C\pmod W.                          \tag{6.1}
\]

These positions are automatically distinct.  List them in cyclic lifted
order as

\[
 L_{\pi_0}<L_{\pi_1}<\cdots<L_{\pi_{C-1}}
   <L_{\pi_0}+W
\]

and let `Delta_a` be the consecutive lifted gaps.  There is a unique
class-balanced NAND word having exactly these marked heights if and only if
every `Delta_a` is odd and at least three.  It is obtained by placing zeros
in the half-open block from one marked position to the next at offsets

\[
 \{0\}\cup\{3+2q:q\ge0,\ 3+2q\le\Delta_a-2\}.      \tag{6.2}
\]

Its physical one-run paired with this block has length

\[
 {\Delta_a+1\over2}.                                \tag{6.3}
\]

Consequently the word has minimum physical run at least `h>=2` if and only
if

\[
 \boxed{\min_a\Delta_a\ge2h-1.}                    \tag{6.4}
\]

#### Proof

Necessity follows by listing the actual gap-three departures.  Between two
of them, the first zero gap is three and every remaining zero gap is two,
so their distance is odd and the block is forced.  Equation (6.3) is the
literal run calculation following (3.6).

Conversely, (6.2) gives one gap-three departure in every residue and only
gap-two departures otherwise.  If `b_v` counts the `+2` departures from
residue `v`, flow balance gives `b_v=b_(v-2)`.  Since `C` is odd, `b_v` is
constant, and its total is

\[
 {W-3C\over2}=(m-1)C.
\]

Thus every residue has exactly `m-1` gap-two departures in addition to its
one gap-three departure.  The zero walk is the exact Euler circuit of the
NAND multigraph, all class sums follow, and uniqueness is forced block by
block.  Equation (6.3) proves (6.4).  ∎

Thus `lambda` is not merely an auxiliary owner word: subject to the
odd-gap placement condition, it determines the entire trace.  The remaining
nonlinearity is that the induced recurrence (2.7) may recycle the same
necklace many times.

The following explicit sufficient form makes the placement inequalities
transparent.

### Theorem 6.2 (height-sorted partition construction)

Fix `h>=2`.  Let `R_0,...,R_(k-1)` be a partition of `Z_C` into nonempty sets.  Write
each `R_a` in increasing order, and concatenate the `k` lists in cyclic
height order.  Suppose:

1. at every transition from `R_a` to `R_(a+1)`, including the last to the
   first, the first element of the next list is smaller than the last
   element of the current list;
2. every internal positive difference and every cross difference

   \[
   C+\min R_{a+1}-\max R_a                           \tag{6.5}
   \]

   is odd and at least `2h-1`.

Let `pi_0,...,pi_(C-1)` be the resulting residue order and let `Delta_a` be
its positive cyclic differences.  Choose a special zero `L_0` congruent to
`pi_0` modulo `C`, and put

\[
 L_{a+1}=L_a+\Delta_a                               \tag{6.6}
\]

and, in the half-open block `[L_a,L_(a+1))`, place zeros at

\[
 \{L_a\}\cup
 \{L_a+3+2q:q\ge0,\ L_a+3+2q\le L_{a+1}-2\}.       \tag{6.7}
\]

Put ones elsewhere and set `c_p=d_(2p)`.  Then:

* `d` has no `00` and no `111`;
* it is a lifted Euler circuit with one gap-three departure in every
  residue and `m-1` gap-two departures there;
* all `C` class sums of `c` equal `m+1`;
* every physical seam is Johnson;
* run starts and run ends are both transversals of `Z_C`;
* every physical one-run has length at least `h`.

The marked height of a residue in `R_a` is `a`, up to one common cyclic
height shift.

#### Proof

There are no descents inside an increasing list and exactly one descent at
each of the `k` cross transitions.  Telescoping the positive cyclic
differences gives

\[
 \sum_a\Delta_a=kC.                                 \tag{6.8}
\]

All `Delta_a` are odd, so (6.7) tiles a length-`W` binary cycle by blocks

\[
 0\,11\,(01)^{(\Delta_a-3)/2}.                      \tag{6.9}
\]

Every residue appears exactly once as a special departure.  Between
special departures, all remaining zero gaps are two.  If `b_v` is the
number of `+2` departures from residue `v`, flow balance of the closed
projected walk gives `b_v=b_(v-2)`.  Since `C` is odd, all `b_v` are equal;
their total is `(W-3C)/2=(m-1)C`.  Thus the projected zero walk uses once
every `+3` edge and `m-1` copies of every `+2` edge;
Theorem 4.1 of
`MATH_THEOREM_CSPACE_NAND_EULER_OWNER_TRANSFER_20260729.md` applies.

For consecutive special zeros `L_a<L_(a+1)`, the associated physical run
starts at

\[
 t(L_a+2)
\]

and ends at

\[
 t(L_{a+1}+1).
\]

The sampled `d`-positions in that run are

\[
 L_a+2,L_a+4,\ldots,L_{a+1}+1,
\]

so its inclusive length is

\[
 \boxed{\ell_a={\Delta_a+1\over2}.}                \tag{6.10}
\]

This is at least `h`.  Since the special residues are a permutation and
`t` is invertible modulo `C`, both endpoint residue lists are permutations.
Finally, every cross transition increases the lift height once and internal
transitions do not, proving the marked-height assertion.  ∎

This theorem is constructive but conditional on the partition.  It permits
many moving height sectors.  It contains no claim that different sectors
produce different necklaces: that is the nonlinear collision gate.

## 9. Phase changes are necessary but not sufficient

If `gcd(p,C)=1` and `F` is nonconstant, deleting `E_p(F)` from the unique
`p`-cycle gives exactly `|E_p(F)|` constant components.  Hence

\[
 |\operatorname{im}F|\le |E_p(F)|.                 \tag{9.1}
\]

Full middle coverage therefore requires every edge to change for every
coprime comparison step used in this way.  The retained candidate attains
equality in (9.1) for `p=14`.

The rotor proves the converse false.  At the natural block step `p=15` it
has

\[
 |E_{15}(F_H)|=118,
 \qquad |E_{15}(F_K)|=116,                          \tag{9.2}
\]

but only `82` and `80` labels.  More sharply, over every step coprime to
`429`,

\[
 \min |E_p(F_H)|=405
 \quad\text{at }p=206,223,                          \tag{9.3}
\]

and

\[
 \min |E_p(F_K)|=401
 \quad\text{at }p=178,251.                          \tag{9.4}
\]

Thus even near-complete phase separation can recycle the same necklaces
nonlocally.  The true nonlinear state is the accumulated product of the
successive transpositions in (3.8), not the number of local rotor states or
phase boundaries.

## 10. Exact remaining simultaneous lift

The static results show what would be enough if chronology were free.  The
actual Euler problem asks for one indexed family

\[
 (G_i,\lambda_{2i},\lambda_{2i+1})_{i\in\mathbb Z}
\]

such that simultaneously:

1. (3.8) and the helical rule (3.9) hold;
2. the `G_i` traverse every rank-`m` necklace with the required physical
   multiplicities;
3. the punctures `K_i` cover every rank-`m-1` necklace with physical orbit
   weights;
4. every lower target occurs in some first-hit interval (3.17), so by
   (3.14) every upper target also occurs;
5. the odd-distance mark exclusions required by physical residence hold.

Theorem 4.1 pays item 3 only after deleting items 1, 4, and 5.  The finite
atlas pays all rankwise supports only after deleting their common order.
The parity rotor pays items 1 and 5 exactly but not items 2--4.  Therefore
the smallest remaining hypothesis is a chronology-preserving lift of the
static puncture/chain assignment through the recurrence (3.8).  No result
in this note implies a `k=15` carrier or coefficient one.
