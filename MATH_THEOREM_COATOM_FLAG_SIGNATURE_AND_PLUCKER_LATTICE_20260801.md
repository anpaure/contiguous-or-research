## 0. Outcome

The canonical mixed-screen packet does not create unrelated defects at
depths `2,...,d`.  Its complete lower-support exchange is one
antisymmetrized maximal-chain signature on a `d`-element filler set.

The integer lattice generated by all filler orderings has only the obvious
constraints:

1. complementary depths carry complementary coefficients; and
2. every noncentral layer has total coefficient zero.

There are no further congruence or lattice-index obstructions **inside this
fixed-filler flag lattice**.  Its complementary-depth constraint is
substantive, however: inside the common-order subcatalogue it becomes a
reflected pair-degree invariant, recorded in Corollary 2.3 below.  The
one-block-order breaker later shows that this is not an invariant of the
enlarged planted catalogue.  At depth two alone the packet specializes to an ordinary
Johnson-square, or quadratic Pluecker, switch.  Varying the labels realizes
every such switch, and these switches generate the complete integer lattice
of rank-`(r-2)` occurrence vectors with fixed coordinate degrees.

This does not yet prove bounded terminal compiler deletion.  It proves that
the local flag lattice is saturated, while also identifying the exact
reflection fibre in which the nonlinear terminal compiler must be reached.

## 1. Flag form of one packet

Let

\[
 F=\{f_1,\ldots,f_d\}
\]

be the internal filler set, in some order `pi`.  Write

\[
 P_t(\pi)=\{\pi_1,\ldots,\pi_t\},\qquad
 S_t(\pi)=\{\pi_{d-t+1},\ldots,\pi_d\}.              \tag{1.1}
\]

In the canonical coatom tensor, put

\[
 G=K\cup\{\mathord\infty,c\},\qquad t=d+1-q.
\]

At lower depth `q`, `2<=q<=d`, the old-to-new support exchange is, up to
the global choice of orientation,

\[
 \Delta_q(\pi)=
 e_{G\cup\{a\}\cup P_t}
 +e_{G\cup\{b\}\cup S_t}
 -e_{G\cup\{b\}\cup P_t}
 -e_{G\cup\{a\}\cup S_t}.                            \tag{1.2}
\]

Thus all depths are coupled by one maximal chain in `2^F`.

## 2. Exact maximal-chain lattice

For `1<=t<=d-1`, let `B_t` be the free abelian group on the `t`-subsets of
`F`, and set

\[
                         B=\bigoplus_{t=1}^{d-1}B_t.
\]

For a permutation `pi`, define its antisymmetric chain signature

\[
 A(\pi)_t=e_{P_t(\pi)}-e_{S_t(\pi)}.                 \tag{2.1}
\]

Define `U_d` to be the integer vectors `x in B` satisfying

\[
 x_{d-t}(F\setminus X)=-x_t(X)                       \tag{2.2}
\]

for every proper `X subset F`, and

\[
 \sum_{|X|=t}x_t(X)=0\qquad(1\le t<d/2).             \tag{2.3}
\]

### Theorem 2.1

\[
                 \boxed{\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb Z}
                   \{A(\pi):\pi\in S_d\}=U_d.}       \tag{2.4}
\]

In particular the lattice is saturated, and its rank is

\[
                  2^{d-1}-1-\left\lfloor{d-1\over2}\right\rfloor.
                                                               \tag{2.5}
\]

#### Proof

Every `A(pi)` satisfies (2.2), since

\[
 S_t(\pi)=F\setminus P_{d-t}(\pi),
\]

and it satisfies (2.3) because each layer contains one `+1` and one `-1`.
Hence the left side of (2.4) is contained in `U_d`.

Fix `t<d/2` and let `pi'` be obtained from `pi` by interchanging positions
`t` and `t+1`.  All prefix sets except the rank-`t` prefix are unchanged,
and all suffix sets except the rank-`(d-t)` suffix are unchanged.  Therefore

\[
                         A(\pi)-A(\pi')                \tag{2.6}
\]

is one Johnson-edge difference `e_X-e_Y` in layer `t`, together with exactly
its forced complement partner in layer `d-t`.

Conversely every edge `X,Y` of `J(d,t)` is obtained this way: order their
common `(t-1)`-set first, put the two exchanged elements in positions `t`
and `t+1`, and order the remaining elements arbitrarily.  The Johnson graph
is connected, so its edge differences generate the complete zero-sum
integer lattice on layer `t`.  Equation (2.6) therefore generates every
paired zero-sum block in layers `t,d-t`, independently for every `t<d/2`.

If `d` is odd, those paired blocks are all of `U_d`.  If `d` is even, one
middle layer remains.  Given a `d/2`-set `X`, choose `pi` whose first half is
`X` and whose second half is `F-X`.  The middle component of `A(pi)` is

\[
                             e_X-e_{F\setminus X}.     \tag{2.7}
\]

All its other components lie in the paired zero-sum blocks already
generated, so subtracting them isolates (2.7).  These vectors generate the
entire complement-antisymmetric middle-layer lattice.  This proves (2.4)
over the integers, not merely over the rationals.  Counting one coordinate
per complement pair and subtracting the layer-sum constraints gives (2.5).
\(\square\)

### Corollary 2.2

### Corollary 2.3 (common-order reflected pair invariant)

Let `mu_q` be the occurrence vector of depth-`q` owner intersections and put

\[
 \deg_q^{(2)}(x,y)=\sum_{S\supseteq\{x,y\}}\mu_q(S).
\]

Every serial walk using the **common-order, unplanted** canonical coatom
packets preserves, for `q'=d+2-q`,

\[
             \deg_q^{(2)}(x,y)-\deg_{q'}^{(2)}(x,y).             \tag{2.9}
\]

Indeed, in (1.2) only pairs `{a,f_i}` and `{b,f_i}` can have nonzero
signed change.  For `{a,f_i}` that change is

\[
 \epsilon_t(i)={\bf1}_{i\le t}-{\bf1}_{i>d-t},
\]

and the reflected depth has prefix length `d-t`, for which
`epsilon_(d-t)(i)=epsilon_t(i)`.  The `b` row has the opposite sign.  Point
degrees are separately conserved at every depth.  Thus the all-depth move
space is not the product of its depthwise degree kernels, even though the

## 3. Depth two is a Pluecker switch

At `q=2`, put

\[
 H=K\cup\{\mathord\infty,c\}
       \cup\{f_2,\ldots,f_{d-1}\}.
\]

Then (1.2) becomes

\[
 e_{H\cup\{a,f_1\}}+e_{H\cup\{b,f_d\}}
 -e_{H\cup\{b,f_1\}}-e_{H\cup\{a,f_d\}},            \tag{3.1}
\]

the standard square relation in `J(k,r-2)`.

When `r>=d+4` and `k-r>=4`, every rank-`(r-2)` Johnson square can be labelled
as (3.1).  Given its common `(r-4)`-set `H`, partition `H` into `K`, the two
active labels `infinity,c`, and the `d-2` internal fillers.  Assign its four
external labels to `a,b,f_1,f_d`; the remaining four coordinates required by
the tensor are available from `k-r>=4`.

The following standard lattice fact is included for completeness.

### Theorem 3.1 (uniform-degree lattice)

Let `Omega` have size `v`, let `2<=s<=v-2`, and let

\[
 \partial_s:\mathbb Z^{{\Omega\choose s}}\longrightarrow\mathbb Z^\Omega,
 \qquad e_X\longmapsto\sum_{x\in X}e_x.              \tag{3.2}
\]

Then `ker(partial_s)` is generated over the integers by the Johnson squares

\[
 e_{Hax}+e_{Hby}-e_{Hay}-e_{Hbx},                    \tag{3.3}
\]

where `|H|=s-2` and `a,b,x,y` are distinct outside `H`.

#### Proof

Induct on `v`.  The case `v=s+1` has zero kernel: its incidence matrix is
the square matrix `J-I`, which is nonsingular.  Split a vector in
`ker(partial_s)` according to whether its sets contain a fixed last point
`z`.  Identifying the containing part with coefficients on `(s-1)`-sets of
`Omega-z`, the `z`-degree equation says those coefficients sum to zero.

The Johnson graph on the `(s-1)`-sets is connected, so that coefficient
vector is an integer sum of edge differences

\[
                             e_{H+b}-e_{H+a}.          \tag{3.4}
\]

Choose `c` outside `H,a,b,z`, which is possible because `v>=s+2`.  The
square

\[
 e_{Hac}+e_{Hbz}-e_{Haz}-e_{Hbc}                     \tag{3.5}
\]

has `z`-containing part (3.4), up to orientation.  Subtracting such squares
kills the entire containing part.  What remains lies in
`ker(partial_s)` on `Omega-z` and is generated by induction.  \(\square\)

Hence the depth-two projection of the packet catalogue has no hidden
integer invariant beyond the coordinate degrees.

## 4. Exact remaining gap

Theorems 2.1 and 3.1 are catalogue statements.  They do not assert that a
fixed global carrier contains a physically plantable copy of every desired
labelled packet, nor that a sequence of algebraically prescribed moves can
be embedded without exhausting owner tokens.

They nevertheless sharpen the additive-constant target.  A failure of
bounded terminal compiler reachability can no longer be attributed to an
unsaturated fixed-flag lattice or to an incomplete depth-two degree lattice.
It must come from one of:

1. physical planted-move availability in the safe carrier component;
2. nonlinear positivity/nonzeroness constraints of the common compiler; or
3. failure of the enlarged common-order-plus-breaker action to generate the
   compiler augmentations required at the terminal carrier.
