# Giant promotion-ring couplings: exact target-star floor resolutions and the full signed trade lattice

Date: 2026-07-26

Method: pure mathematics only. No computation, finite search, solver, or
web input is used.

## 0. Outcome

Put

\[
 n=2m,\qquad M=m+H,\qquad
 W=\binom{2m}m,\qquad
 N_H=\binom{2m}{m-H},
\tag{0.1}
\]

where

\[
 H=(1+o(1))\sqrt{m\log m},\qquad H=o(m).
\tag{0.2}
\]

A root is \(A\in\binom{[n]}{m-H}\), and a frame is a directed cyclic
order of \(U_A=[n]\setminus A\).  Its promotion-ring deck is

\[
 e(A,\pi)=\{A\cup I_\pi(i,H):i\in\mathbb Z_M\}
       \subseteq\binom{[n]}m.
\tag{0.3}
\]

For a fixed middle target \(D\), its root star and one-root hit
probability are

\[
 \mathcal R(D)=\binom D{m-H},\qquad
 R:=|\mathcal R(D)|=\binom mH,
\tag{0.4}
\]

\[
 p=\frac M{\binom MH},\qquad
 \theta=Rp=\frac{MN_H}{W}=1+o(1).
\tag{0.5}
\]

The block-factor hole theorem proves that any successful product coupling
must have blocks of size \((1-o(1))R\).  This note proves that the same
giant scale is sufficient for one literal target and identifies exactly
what remains when all target stars overlap.

1. **Exact giant star component.**  For every fixed \(D\), there is a
   joint law on one frame for each of the \(R\) roots in
   \(\mathcal R(D)\) such that:

   * every root frame has the exactly uniform marginal;
   * the load \(L_D\) is always \(1\) or \(2\); and
   * \(\mathbb EL_D=\theta\).

   Hence \(D\) is never missed and its collision energy is at the exact
   load-\(\theta\) floor:

   \[
                         \mathbb E\binom{L_D}{2}=\theta-1.
   \tag{0.6}
   \]

   The total cross-root covariance at \(D\), relative to independent
   uniform frames, is

   \[
      \boxed{
      \sum_{A<B}\operatorname {Cov}(X_{A,D},X_{B,D})
      =-\frac12+o(1).}
   \tag{0.7}
   \]

   Thus the \(S_{2m}\)-orbit sum of these components has precisely the
   missing negative covariance \(-(1/2+o(1))W\) at the middle-owner
   scale.

2. **Exact finite resolution array.**  The law is rational.  After
   clearing denominators, it becomes a finite array of integral
   configurations, one frame per root in every column, in which every
   frame occurs equally often at every root and every column hits \(D\)
   once or twice.  This is an actual orbit-scale alternating resolution,
   not a fractional choice inside a column.

3. **No linear or congruence invariant beyond point degrees.**  Let
   \(\partial\) send an integer middle-layer vector to its vector of
   ground-coordinate degrees.  Let \(\mathscr T_H\) be the lattice
   generated by differences of two complete promotion-ring decks with
   the same root.  For all sufficiently large \(m\),

   \[
                         \boxed{
                         \mathscr T_H=\ker_{\mathbb Z}\partial.}
   \tag{0.8}
   \]

   Moreover the lattice is generated by the \(S_{2m}\)-orbit of one
   adjacent-transposition four-term atom.  Therefore no additive,
   parity, or finite-character invariant of the owner load survives
   root-balanced frame trades beyond the forced coordinate degrees.

4. **The unresolved obstruction is positive overlap, not signed
   reachability.**  Two target stars share a root exactly when their
   Johnson distance is at most \(H\), and

   \[
    |\mathcal R(D)\cap\mathcal R(E)|
      =\binom{m-d}{H-d}qquad(d=|D\setminus E|\le H).
   \tag{0.9}
   \]

   Every root belongs to \(\binom MH=(M/\theta)R\) target stars.  Thus
   the one-target giant components overlap massively.  Since disjoint
   stars consume \(R\) roots each, a disjoint packing services at most
   \(N_H/R=O(W/(mR))=o(W)\) targets.  The orbit sum giving (0.7) is
   consequently a valid signed covariance direction but is not itself
   one positive joint law.

5. **Exact global gate.**  What remains is to amalgamate the overlapping
   star arrays into one distribution, or one deterministic orbit, on
   full root resolutions such that all but \(o(W)\) middle loads lie in
   \(\{1,2\}\), while the inherited tagged shorter-window vectors also
   have aggregate floor excess \(o(W)\).  Equation (0.8) rules out a
   linear invariant obstruction.  The block theorem and (0.9) rule out
   products of bounded, polynomial, or merely disjoint star components.

Thus an orbit-scale circuit with the correct covariance exists and has
the provably necessary size \(\Theta(\binom mH)\).  The exact remaining
problem is simultaneous positive compatibility across the dense
root--target incidence geometry.

## 1. Root-frame incidence and the forced invariant

Let

\[
 \mathcal M=\binom{[n]}m,
 \qquad
 \mathcal A=\binom{[n]}{m-H}.
\tag{1.1}
\]

For every root \(A\), let \(\Omega_A\) be its \((M-1)!\) directed cyclic
frames modulo rotation.  Write

\[
                         v_{A,\pi}=\mathbf1_{e(A,\pi)}
                         \in\mathbb Z^{\mathcal M}.
\tag{1.2}
\]

A root-balanced signed trade is an integer vector \(z=(z_{A,\pi})\)
satisfying

\[
                         \sum_{\pi\in\Omega_A}z_{A,\pi}=0
                         \qquad(A\in\mathcal A).
\tag{1.3}
\]

Its middle-owner direction is

\[
                         Bz=\sum_{A,\pi}z_{A,\pi}v_{A,\pi}.
\tag{1.4}
\]

Define the point-degree map

\[
 \partial:\mathbb Z^{\mathcal M}\longrightarrow\mathbb Z^{[n]},
 \qquad
 (\partial f)_x=\sum_{X\ni x}f_X.
\tag{1.5}
\]

### Lemma 1.1 (the forced degree invariant)

Every root-balanced trade satisfies

\[
                         \partial(Bz)=0.
\tag{1.6}
\]

Every complete one-frame-per-root selection has the same coordinate
degree

\[
                         \frac{MN_H}{2}=\frac{W+E}{2}
\tag{1.7}
\]

at every ground coordinate, where \(E=MN_H-W=o(W)\).

#### Proof

Fix a root \(A\).  In every frame deck, a coordinate \(x\in A\) belongs
to all \(M\) owners, while a coordinate \(x\notin A\) belongs to exactly
\(H\) of the cyclic \(H\)-windows.  Thus \(\partial v_{A,\pi}\) depends
only on \(A\), not on \(\pi\).  Equation (1.3) proves (1.6).

After summing over all roots, symmetry makes the coordinate degrees equal.
The selection has \(MN_H\) owner occurrences, each of size \(m=n/2\),
so their common degree is \(MN_H/2\). \(\square\)

The excess \(E/2\) over the all-one owner vector is unavoidable.  It is
only \(o(W)\), and therefore does not obstruct coefficient one.  The next
sections prove that it is the only signed lattice invariant.

## 2. The local adjacent-transposition atom

Fix a root \(A\).  Let \(S,T\subset U_A\) be disjoint
\((H-1)\)-sets, and let \(x,y\in U_A\setminus(S\cup T)\) be distinct.
Arrange a cyclic frame with the consecutive local pattern

\[
                         S,\ x,\ y,\ T,
\tag{2.1}
\]

where the elements inside \(S,T\) are put in arbitrary orders.  Swap the
adjacent labels \(x,y\).  Every \(H\)-window is unchanged except the one
ending at the first swapped position and the one starting at the second.
After adjoining the root, the deck difference is

\[
\begin{aligned}
 \rho(A;S,T;x,y)
  ={}&[A\cup S\cup\{y\}]+[A\cup T\cup\{x\}]\\
    &-[A\cup S\cup\{x\}]-[A\cup T\cup\{y\}].
\end{aligned}
\tag{2.2}

It is the difference of two legal complete frames at one root.  In
particular it is a root-balanced integral trade direction.

For later use, if \(S,T\) are not disjoint but avoid \(x,y\), choose an
\((H-1)\)-set \(Q\) disjoint from
\(S\cup T\cup\{x,y\}\).  This is possible when \(M\ge3H\), which holds
in the present range.  The formal rectangle satisfies the exact
telescoping identity

\[
 \rho(A;S,T;x,y)
 =\rho(A;S,Q;x,y)+\rho(A;Q,T;x,y).
\tag{2.3}
\]

Thus every same-root exchange rectangle, without a disjointness
assumption, belongs to the lattice generated by adjacent frame swaps.

## 3. The full signed trade-lattice theorem

We use one elementary switching fact.

### Lemma 3.1 (uniform-set degree lattice)

Let \(V\) be a finite set and \(2\le k\le|V|-2\).  On
\(\mathbb Z^{\binom Vk}\), the kernel of the point-degree map is generated
by symmetric two-row exchanges

\[
 [P\cup\{x\}]+[Q\cup\{y\}]
 -[P\cup\{y\}]-[Q\cup\{x\}],
\tag{3.1}
\]

where \(|P|=|Q|=k-1\) and \(x,y\notin P\cup Q\).

#### Proof

Split an integer kernel vector into positive and negative multisets of
\(k\)-sets.  Their point degrees are equal, and therefore the two
multisets have the same cardinality.  Regard each multiset as a zero-one
matrix whose rows are its sets and whose columns are the points of \(V\).
The two matrices have the same row sums and column sums.

The standard alternating-cycle argument for binary matrices now applies.
After permuting rows to maximize agreement, if the matrices differ, the
symmetric difference of their one-entries contains an alternating cycle.
A chord of a shortest such cycle gives a \(2\times2\) switch which
increases agreement while preserving all row and column sums.  That switch
is exactly (3.1).  Iteration transforms the positive matrix into the
negative one.  Summing the switches expresses the original kernel vector.
\(\square\)

### Theorem 3.2 (root-balanced ring trades generate the full lattice)

Assume \(H\ge2\) and \(M\ge3H\).  Let

\[
 \mathscr T_H
 =\{Bz:z\text{ satisfies }(1.3)}
 \subseteq\mathbb Z^{\mathcal M}.
\tag{3.2}
\]

Then

\[
                         \boxed{
                         \mathscr T_H=\ker_{\mathbb Z}\partial.}
\tag{3.3}
\]

Indeed, \(\mathscr T_H\) is generated by the coordinate orbit of the
single adjacent-transposition atom (2.2).

#### Proof

Lemma 1.1 gives the inclusion \(\mathscr T_H\subseteq\ker\partial\).
By Lemma 3.1, it suffices to realize every global symmetric exchange

\[
 [P\cup\{x\}]+[Q\cup\{y\}]
 -[P\cup\{y\}]-[Q\cup\{x\}],
\tag{3.4}
\]

where \(P,Q\) are \((m-1)\)-sets avoiding the distinct points \(x,y\).

First suppose \(|P\cap Q|\ge m-H\).  Choose a root

\[
                         A\subseteq P\cap Q,
                         \qquad |A|=m-H.
\]

Put \(S=P\setminus A\) and \(T=Q\setminus A\).  These are
\((H-1)\)-sets in \(U_A\), avoiding \(x,y\).  Formula (3.4) is the
same-root rectangle \(\rho(A;S,T;x,y)\), which belongs to the adjacent
swap lattice by (2.3).

For arbitrary \(P,Q\), use the connected Johnson graph on the
\((m-1)\)-subsets of \([n]\setminus\{x,y\}\) to choose a chain

\[
                         P=P_0,P_1,\ldots,P_s=Q
\]

in which consecutive sets differ by one exchange.  Then
\(|P_i\cap P_{i+1}|=m-2\ge m-H\), so every consecutive rectangle is
already in \(\mathscr T_H\).  The exact telescoping identity

\[
 \rho(P,Q;x,y)=\sum_{i=0}^{s-1}\rho(P_i,P_{i+1};x,y)
\tag{3.5}
\]

proves that (3.4) belongs to \(\mathscr T_H\).  Lemma 3.1 completes the
proof. \(\square\)

### Corollary 3.3 (no further additive invariant)

Let \(F:\mathbb Z^{\mathcal M}\to G\) be a homomorphism to any abelian
group which is constant on all one-frame-per-root resolutions.  Then
\(F\) factors through the coordinate-degree vector \(\partial\).

In particular there is no additional parity, modular, Walsh-character,
or orbit-congruence obstruction to a giant signed promotion-ring trade.

#### Proof

Constancy makes \(F\) vanish on \(\mathscr T_H\).  Theorem 3.2 identifies
this lattice with \(\ker\partial\), so the universal property of the
quotient gives the factorization. \(\square\)

This is a signed statement.  The representation (3.5) may use the same
root many times and may require negative copies of frames absent from a
given selection.  It proves reachability in the integral trade lattice,
not a positive bounded-support absorber.

## 4. Exact giant target-star floor resolution

Fix \(D\in\mathcal M\).  A root can select \(D\) if and only if
\(A\in\mathcal R(D)\).  For such a root, put \(J=D\setminus A\).  A
uniform frame on \(U_A\) contains \(J\) as an \(H\)-window with
probability

\[
                         p=\frac M{\binom MH}.
\tag{4.1}
\]

There are \(R=\binom mH\) roots in the star, so the mean target load is
\(\theta=Rp\).  At the covering-side calibration, \(1\le\theta<2\) for
all sufficiently large \(m\).

### Theorem 4.1 (one-target giant floor coupling)

There is a joint law \(\mathbb P_D\) on one frame
\(\pi_A\in\Omega_A\) for every \(A\in\mathcal R(D)\) such that:

1. each \(\pi_A\) is exactly uniform on \(\Omega_A\);
2. the total number
   \[
                         L_D=|\{A:D\in e(A,\pi_A)\}|
   \]
   belongs to \(\{1,2\}\) surely; and
3. \(\Pr(L_D=2)=\theta-1\), so \(\mathbb EL_D=\theta\).

#### Proof

Choose a random integer \(K\) by

\[
                         \Pr(K=1)=2-\theta,
                         \qquad
                         \Pr(K=2)=\theta-1.
\tag{4.2}
\]

Conditional on \(K\), choose a uniform \(K\)-subset
\(S\subseteq\mathcal R(D)\).  For \(A\in S\), choose \(\pi_A\)
uniformly among frames which contain \(D\); for \(A\notin S\), choose it
uniformly among frames which avoid \(D\).  These conditional frame choices
may be independent.

For every root,

\[
                         \Pr(A\in S)=\frac{\mathbb EK}{R}
                         =\frac\theta R=p.
\tag{4.3}
\]

If a particular frame contains \(D\), its probability under the mixture
is

\[
                         p\frac1{p|\Omega_A|}
                         =\frac1{|\Omega_A|}.
\]

If it avoids \(D\), its probability is similarly

\[
                         (1-p)\frac1{(1-p)|\Omega_A|}
                         =\frac1{|\Omega_A|}.
\]

Thus every root marginal is exactly uniform.  By construction
\(L_D=|S|=K\), proving the other assertions. \(\square\)

### Corollary 4.2 (exact covariance cancellation)

Let

\[
                         X_{A,D}=\mathbf1_{\{D\in e(A,\pi_A)\}}.
\]

Under \(\mathbb P_D\),

\[
\begin{aligned}
 \sum_{A<B}\operatorname {Cov}(X_{A,D},X_{B,D})
 &=\mathbb E\binom{L_D}{2}-\binom R2p^2\\
 &=\theta-1-\frac{\theta^2}{2}\left(1-\frac1R\right)\\
 &=-\frac12+o(1).
\end{aligned}
\tag{4.4}
\]

Moreover \(D\) has no hole and

\[
                         \mathbb E\binom{L_D}{2}=\theta-1,
\tag{4.5}
\]

which is the minimum possible pair-collision energy among integer loads
of mean \(\theta\in[1,2]\).

#### Proof

Equation (4.5) follows because \(\binom K2\) is one exactly when \(K=2\).
The independent pair baseline is \(\binom R2p^2\).  Subtraction gives
(4.4).  Finally, for an integer \(L\) of mean \(\theta\in[1,2]\), convexity
of \(\binom L2\) makes the floor mixture on \(1,2\) optimal. \(\square\)

This is exactly the covariance scale missing from independent roots: one
unit-order negative contribution per target.  Summing the formal
\(S_{2m}\)-orbit of (4.4) over all \(D\) gives

\[
                         -\left(\frac12+o(1)\right)W.
\tag{4.6}
\]

### Theorem 4.3 (finite integral resolution array)

There is a positive integer \(Q\) and a \(Q\)-column array of frame
configurations on the roots \(\mathcal R(D)\) such that:

1. every column chooses exactly one frame at every root;
2. for every root, every one of its frames occurs exactly
   \(Q/|\Omega_A|\) times; and
3. every column hits \(D\) once or twice, with exactly
   \((\theta-1)Q\) double-hit columns.

#### Proof

All probabilities in Theorem 4.1 are rational: \(p,\theta\) are ratios
of integers, the subset choices are uniform on finite sets, and the
conditional frame laws are uniform on finite sets.  Take a common
denominator \(Q\) for the finitely many atomic probabilities and repeat
each atomic configuration according to its numerator.  The marginal and
load assertions of Theorem 4.1 become the displayed exact column counts.
\(\square\)

The array is the promised giant alternating component.  Its root support
has size

\[
                         R=\frac\theta p
                         =(1+o(1))\frac1p,
\tag{4.7}

matching the necessary block scale from the block-factor hole theorem.

## 5. Why the star components do not tensor

The root stars of two middle targets have an exact overlap profile.

### Lemma 5.1 (star intersection)

Let \(D,E\in\mathcal M\) have Johnson distance
\(d=|D\setminus E|\).  Then

\[
 |\mathcal R(D)\cap\mathcal R(E)|
 =
 \begin{cases}
  \displaystyle\binom{m-d}{H-d},&d\le H,\\[2mm]
  0,&d>H.
 \end{cases}
\tag{5.1}
\]

In particular, for fixed \(d\le H\),

\[
 \frac{|\mathcal R(D)\cap\mathcal R(E)|}{R}
 =\frac{(H)_d}{(m)_d}.
\tag{5.2}
\]

#### Proof

A common root is an \((m-H)\)-subset of \(D\cap E\), whose size is
\(m-d\).  This gives (5.1), and factorial simplification gives (5.2).
\(\square\)

Every fixed root belongs to

\[
                         \binom MH=\frac M\theta R
\tag{5.3}
\]

different target stars.  Thus even though the relative overlap of two
distance-one stars is only \(H/m=o(1)\), the full star system is highly
overcomplete.

The components of Theorem 4.1 can be applied independently only to a
family of targets at mutual Johnson distance greater than \(H\).  A greedy
packing gives such a family of size at least

\[
 \frac{W}{B_H},
 \qquad
 B_H:=\sum_{d=0}^H\binom md^2
       \le(H+1)\binom mH^2=(H+1)R^2.
\tag{5.4}
\]

This services at least \(W/((H+1)R^2)\) targets, but only an
\(O(R^{-2})\) guaranteed fraction of the middle layer.  In the other
direction, disjointness of the root stars gives

\[
                         |\mathcal C|R\le N_H,
                         \qquad
                         |\mathcal C|\le\frac{N_H}{R}
                         =O\left(\frac{W}{mR}\right)=o(W).
\tag{5.5}
\]

Thus no disjoint star packing can service a positive fraction of the
targets.  Repeating the components for all Johnson colours would reuse
every root in \(\Theta(MR)\) incompatible component arrays.

There is a useful side-effect locality.  A \(D\)-star coupling can affect
the hit law of another target \(E\) through its coupled roots only when
\(d(D,E)\le H\).  Thus the interaction graph is exactly the \(H\)-th
power of the Johnson graph, with degree \(B_H-1\).  The missing global
construction is an amalgamation on this entire graph, not a product over
its colour classes.

## 6. Exact global resolution gate

Let a full resolution be a choice \(\pi_A\in\Omega_A\) for every root.
For a middle target \(D\), put

\[
                         L_D=|\{A\in\mathcal R(D):
                                  D\in e(A,\pi_A)\}|.
\tag{6.1}
\]

Every resolution has

\[
                         \sum_D L_D=MN_H=W+E,
\tag{6.2}
\]

and the coordinate-degree invariant of Lemma 1.1.  The exact middle
floor target is

\[
                         L_D\in\{1,2\}
                         \quad\text{for all but }o(W)\text{ targets},
\tag{6.3}
\]

with \(E+o(W)\) double loads and \(o(W)\) holes.

Equivalently, seek a joint law on full resolutions such that:

1. every root frame marginal is uniform;
2. \(\sum_D\Pr(L_D=0)=o(W)\); and
3. \(\sum_D\bigl[\mathbb E\binom{L_D}{2}-(\theta-1)\bigr]=o(W)\).

Theorem 4.1 solves these equations exactly for any one chosen \(D\).
The formal orbit sum solves their linearized covariance ledger at all
\(D\)'s.  Theorem 3.2 proves that every integral degree-preserving owner
direction is signedly reachable.  What neither theorem supplies is one
positive measure satisfying all overlapping star constraints
simultaneously.

For the coefficient-one application, the same resolution must carry one
tagged phase word per root.  Its inherited lower and upper interval
vectors must additionally satisfy the all-depth floor condition

\[
 \sum_{q\le H}\sum_{\sigma\in\{-,+\}}
 \sum_T\binom{L_{q,T}^\sigma}{2}=o(W),
\tag{6.4}
\]

after subtracting the exact unavoidable floor at each rank.  The giant
middle-star component does not automatically cancel these shorter-window
side effects; an all-depth component must refine Theorem 4.1 by coupling
the conditional frames through their entire interval traces.

## 7. Audited boundary

Proved:

1. a giant \(R=\binom mH\)-root component with exact uniform root-frame
   marginals and floor-optimal load at one literal middle target;
2. an exact finite integral resolution-array form of that component;
3. the order-one negative covariance per serviced target and its formal
   order-\(W\) orbit sum;
4. the exact integral trade-lattice identity
   \(\mathscr T_H=\ker_{\mathbb Z}\partial\);
5. the exact target-star overlap kernel; and
6. the \(O(W/(mR))\) ceiling for a disjoint packing of giant star
   components.

Not proved:

1. positive amalgamation of the star components across their overlaps;
2. a full-resolution law with \(o(W)\) middle holes;
3. a deterministic global resolution; or
4. simultaneous cancellation of the nested tagged interval colours.

The strongest exact verdict is:

\[
\boxed{
\begin{minipage}{0.86\linewidth}
The necessary orbit-scale negative covariance is locally realizable with
exact one-frame-per-root marginals, and the signed frame-trade lattice has
no hidden invariant beyond coordinate degrees.  The remaining obstruction,
if one exists, must be a positive integral incompatibility among the
densely overlapping \(\binom mH\)-root target-star components; it cannot
be a local circuit, a block-product cut, or an additive congruence.
\end{minipage}}
\]
