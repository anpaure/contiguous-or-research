For the full original profile R put

    N_u=binom(ell_u+p_u-1,p_u-1),
    M_u=binom(ell_u+p_u-u-2,p_u-u-2),
    a_u=M_u/N_u=(p_u-1)_(u+1)/(ell_u+p_u-1)_(u+1).

These are respectively the full row count, its base-triangle-zero count,
and the probability of u+1 specified zeros in an UNFORCED row. Define

    A_v=product_(u<v) M_u,
    qbar_v(R,E_v)=A_v/Cat_r,

This identity is before normalization. In contrast, under a specified

## 2. Exact conditional thinning by actual shifted triangles

Let K_(v,H) be the core C_v^v T_v phase congestion at the current edge,
as in the stationary-core note. Define Khat_(s->t,H)(E_s) to count the
phases of K_(s,H) whose ACTUAL translated triangle vanishes in every row
u=s,...,t-1. Equivalently, short forward/reverse grouping gives

    Khat_(s->t,H)
      =sum_(d deep-t eligible at H)
          1{Z_(u,lambda_(u+1)(2d)-i)=0
                  for s<=u<t, 0<=i<=u}.                (3)

The same physical time 2d occurs at every depth. In particular

    Khat_(s->t,H)<=K_(s,H),
    Khat_(s->t,H)<=K_(t,H).                             (4)

The second inequality concerns phase counts, without identifying labels
across depths. For s=2 the first count is the actual depth-two zero-clock
count with an additional exposed-tail-zero restriction.

For EACH fixed deep phase in (3), reveal the entire rows t-1,...,s in
that order. Before row u is revealed, its selected original label is
determined by the already exposed deeper rows. Its u+1 queried indices
are distinct by the common safe domain. Because this row is unforced,
its exact zero probability is a_u, independent of that selected label.
Iterated conditioning proves

    E_ref[Khat_(s->t,H) | R,E_t]
                        =theta_(s,t) K_(t,H).           (5)

This is an equality, not independent thinning. Different deep phases
can share many queried coordinates, and their lower-row labels depend
on the full previously revealed rows.

For the decreasing sigma-fields G_v generated by (R,E_v), (4)-(5) give
the exact one-step transport inequality

    E_ref[K_(v,H)|G_(v+1)] >= a_v K_(v+1,H).             (6)

Multiplying by theta_(s,v) and iterating preserves this inequality.
It is not generally a martingale equality: shallower short clocks with
positive intermediate gaps contribute the nonnegative difference.

## 3. The actual Palm law is size-biased thinning

Write H_c<=H_C, and let mu_(v,c) be the exact base raw-incidence mass
after the zero triangle through v and the common safe domain. Moving
the sampled edge to zero gives density

    qbar_v(R,E_v) K_(v,H_c)(E_v)/mu_(v,c).               (7)

In the same edge frame, the depth-t base Palm law on the more detailed
E_s space has density

    qbar_s(R,E_s) Khat_(s->t,H_c)(E_s)/mu_(t,c).         (8)

To verify (8), choose the base birth at each allowed age. Its triangle
in the intermediate rows must actually be zero, and its upper rows
below s have exactly A_s completions. Summing those base choices gives
the count in (3), with no additional row weight. Integrating (8) using
(5) and then (2) recovers (7) at depth t.

Thus, conditional on (R,E_t), the ACTUAL depth-t Palm law of the
intermediate rows has density

    Khat_c/(theta_(s,t) K_(t,H_c))                     (9)

relative to their unforced reference law. The denominator is positive
on this Palm support. The row law has not remained its reference law.

For equal base and partner cutoffs H, abbreviate Khat=Khat_(s->t,H)
and m=theta_(s,t) K_(t,H). Exact finite identities are

    E_Palm[1/Khat |R,E_t]=P_ref(Khat>0|R,E_t)/m,
    E_Palm[Khat |R,E_t]=m+Var_ref(Khat|R,E_t)/m.         (10)

Under Palm, Khat>=1. Formula (10) is just the size bias in (9); it needs
no independence or approximation. For unequal cutoffs the reciprocal is

    E_Palm[1/Khat_C |R,E_t]
        =E_ref[Khat_c/Khat_C |R,E_t]
                                      /(theta_(s,t)K_(t,H_c)), (11)

with 0/0 interpreted as zero. Since K_(s,H_C)>=Khat_C, this also supplies
an upper bound on the shallower reciprocal.

## 4. Exact loss and the first-moment cancellation

Integrating (4)-(5) gives the exact nonnegative loss

    integral qbar_s (K_(s,H)-Khat_(s->t,H))
                                      =mu_(s,H)-mu_(t,H). (12)

For fixed s,t and Gaussian cutoff, the accepted sampled-base triangle
retention says the right side is o(1). After normalizing qbar_s, whose
mass has a positive fixed-depth limit, it follows that

    E_ref[K_(s,H)|G_t]-theta_(s,t)K_(t,H) ->0 in L1.     (13)

The difference is nonnegative. Equation (13) is an asymptotic conditional-
expectation relation at FIXED depths. It is not a growing-depth
martingale theorem or a uniform-integrability claim in the depth index.

For s=2 and fixed t, original-depth concentration gives

    theta_(2,t) -> rho_t/rho_2,
    rho_t=(t+2)^t/(t+1)^(t+1),       rho_2=16/27.        (14)

Here theta depends on the full finite profile; replacing it by (14) is
only the displayed fixed-depth limit. The stationary-core theorem gave
an upper reciprocal bound of order rho_t for the relaxed K_(t,H).
Using (11) pays the reciprocal factor theta_(2,t)^(-1), of order t.
Consequently that bound returns only order rho_2, rather than a bound
tending to zero. Equations (5), (9), and (10) identify the cancellation
exactly: relaxed abundance and the zero-thinning probability have
reciprocal scales.

There can still be additional information in the thinning correlations.
For example (10) isolates its conditional variance and occupied
probability. A large Palm count can come from rare large clusters even
when the reference mean m stays bounded. Neither a variance lower bound
nor an occupied-probability decay follows just from (5).

No convex-order assertion turning K_(2,H) into an independent thinning
of K_(t,H) is justified. No L_0 divergence is claimed. The useful exact
relations are (2), (5), the Palm kernel (9)-(11), and the loss identity
(12)-(13), all with the actual shifted slots and fixed-r weights.
