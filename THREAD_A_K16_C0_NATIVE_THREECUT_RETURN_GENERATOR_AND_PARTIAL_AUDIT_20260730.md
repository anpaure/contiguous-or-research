## 2. Exact enumeration theorem

The five genuine standard three-cut patterns are numbered 3 through 7 as
in `audit_a_k16_fiveopt_surplus_local_atoms_20260730.py`.  For cuts
`a<b<c`, their three new joining seams have endpoint expressions

\[
\begin{array}{c|ccc}
3&(a,b)&(a+1,c)&(b+1,c+1)\\
4&(a,b+1)&(c,a+1)&(b,c+1)\\
5&(a,c)&(b+1,a+1)&(b,c+1)\\
6&(a,b+1)&(c,b)&(a+1,c+1)\\
7&(a,c)&(b+1,b)&(a+1,c+1).
\end{array}                                             \tag{2.1}
\]

### Theorem 2.1 (complete forced-facet generation)

Every genuine standard three-cut return on `S0` which restores `U` is
generated by the following finite procedure.

1. List the nine occurrence-labelled rank-eight rows of `S0` contained in
   `U`.
2. Choose one of the 72 ordered pairs of distinct occurrences.
3. Impose that pair in one of the joining-seam slots in (2.1).
4. Range the one remaining cut through `0,...,12871`, retaining exactly
   `0<=a<b<c<12872`.

The same chronology may be described more than once; this does not affect
completeness.

#### Proof

Let `I` be a new interval with union `U`.  Every row in `I` is a rank-eight
subset of the rank-nine set `U`, hence is one of its facets.  As `S0` has no
`U` provider, a retained source subinterval cannot contain two distinct
`U`-facets: two distinct rank-eight facets already have union `U`.
Therefore `I` must cross a new joining seam at which the facet changes.
The two occurrence-labelled endpoints of that seam form one of the 72
ordered pairs in step 2.

For every joining seam in (2.1), imposing its two endpoints fixes two cut
variables.  The exceptional expression `(b+1,b)` in pattern 7 uses one
variable, but it is the reversal of an old adjacent seam.  It cannot join
two distinct `U`-facets, since that old adjacent pair would already have
provided `U` in `S0`.  Thus every relevant imposition leaves exactly one
cut variable, which step 4 exhausts.  The five rows of (2.1) are the five
genuine standard three-cut patterns.  This proves the claim.  \(\square\)

## 3. Safety of the local prefilter

The generator does not assume that isolated-seam legality is sufficient.
It first uses a necessary filter and then performs whole-word replay.

For a candidate chronology `Q`, let `f_1<f_2<f_3` be its adjacent equal
pairs.  Then

\[
 d(r)=3-|\{i:f_i<r\}|,
 \qquad
 \sum_r d(r)=3+f_1+f_2+f_3.                 \tag{3.1}
\]

At envelope position `p`, the literal maximal envelope is

\[
 E_p=\bigcap_{\substack{\max(0,p-3)\le r\le p\\p\le r+d(r)}}Q_r. \tag{3.2}
\]

### Lemma 3.1 (no false rejection)

Every tuple rejected by `necessary_local_replay` fails at least one
necessary global carrier condition.

#### Proof

Internal adjacencies of each retained segment are source adjacencies,
possibly reversed.  Hence the candidate's complete equal-adjacency list is
exactly the retained subset of the three source flats together with equal
new joins.  The code constructs precisely this list.  A list of size other
than three fails `G0`; if (3.1) is below 26,332 it fails scalar capacity.

For each sampled row, the code evaluates (3.2) literally through the exact
segment accessor, and then evaluates

\[
 \bigvee_{p=r}^{r+d(r)}E_p=Q_r.              \tag{3.3}
\]

A zero `E_p`, an out-of-range deadline, or a failure of (3.3) is a genuine
global failure.  Sampling fewer than all rows can create false positives
but cannot create false negatives.  Every tuple surviving this necessary
test is then materialized, all envelopes and all rows are replayed, scalar
capacity is checked again, and every upper interval mask is recomputed.
\(\square\)

Thus a completed zero-exit run with its `DONE` footer is proof-safe for the
entire standard three-cut face.
