## 0. Outcome

The saturated flag/Pluecker theorem removes the signed integer-lattice gate.
It does not manufacture a nonnegative bounded word; the occurrence-labelled
Markov obstruction makes that a separate hypothesis.  Fix such a bounded
word of desired antisymmetric flag moves.  For each move, one
active label of the coatom tensor is absent from its complete all-depth lower
action and from both physical endpoints.  On a ground set of size `k`,
varying that label gives exactly

\[
                         q_{\rm act}=k-r-3                      \tag{0.1}
\]

endpoint-compatible physical packets, all with the **same prescribed
all-depth flag action**.  On `J(2r,r)` this is `r-3`; on `J(2r-1,r)` it is
`r-4`.

After the endpoint label is fixed, the exact candidate-dependent resource
counts are

\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
 \text{resource}&\text{variable addresses}\ \hline
 \text{rank-}r\text{ owners}&3d+8\\
 \text{adjacent intersections}&3d+6\\
 \text{adjacent unions}&3d+10.
\end{array}                                                     \tag{0.2}
\]

Every fixed noncommon value occurs in at most one candidate.  A deadline
jump introduces twelve new lift-shadow owners, of which exactly three are
candidate dependent.  Thus a one-step-regenerative packet has variable
owner budget

\[
                              3d+11.                            \tag{0.3}
\]

For `H` prescribed moves with strongly private common banks, candidate-
specific forbidden-token count at most `t`, and unit-capacity owner tokens,
greedy selection therefore succeeds whenever

\[
             k-r-3>t+(H-1)(3d+11).                            \tag{0.4}
\]

If adjacent-intersection and adjacent-union tokens must also be private, the
corresponding sufficient row is

\[
             k-r-3>t+(H-1)(9d+33).                            \tag{0.5}
\]

For bounded `H`, `t=O(d)`, `d=o(r)` and `k-r=Theta(r)`, both inequalities
hold in every sufficiently large dimension.  This closes the exact **owner-token
selection** row for a bounded prescribed flag word.

The endpoint-planted one-block reflection breakers do **not** inherit this
fixed-action `z`-menu: in that planted schedule, changing `z` changes the
complete lower action.  They nevertheless give an independent exact advance.
All adjacent twists form a lower-triangular family with a full
Johnson-square diagonal.  Consequently the enlarged catalogue surjects onto
the product of all reflected pair-current degree kernels.  More strongly, a
four-column boundary-role commutator isolates one primitive Johnson square
at any chosen depth, so the **full labelled formal occurrence lattice** is
the integral product of the per-depth point-degree kernels.  Reflection and
every other coupled linear invariant are gone.  A physical menu for a
prescribed breaker, nonnegative serial reachability and compiler linkage
remain open.

It does not yet put the candidate-dependent old words into one Pascal child.
A fixed incumbent word has fibre one.  The single remaining physical theorem
is a bounded prospective-host extension: construct one safe child chronology
which contains the selected old phases as literal endpoint-compatible slots,
with their residence collars and complement already completed.  Once that
holds, serial switching preserves U1--U4 automatically and the compiler is
solved only on the terminal chronology.

There is a precise cross-fibre mismatch inside that statement.  The linear
prescribed-action menu is proved for the canonical common-order schedule,
whereas the explicit nested deadline-jump collar is proved for the corrected
endpoint-planted schedule.  The planted action depends on the variable
active label.  Thus the two ingredients cannot yet be multiplied into one
menu count; one needs either a canonical collar or a planted fixed-action
menu before the global completion step.

## 1. The fixed-action orbit

Use residence depth `d>=1`, owner rank `r`, and the canonical zero-defect
mixed-screen packet.  Throughout Sections 1--4 assume the admissible range
`r>=d+4` and `k-r>=4`.  In the notation of the exact complementary
active-label orbit, fix:

* a rank-`(r-2)` frame `H`;
* the filler flag and core split;
* the active labels `a,b,c,infinity`;
* the endpoint label `e`; and
* both ordered physical endpoints.

Let the remaining active role called `d` in the twelve-owner word be denoted
by `z`, to avoid confusion with residence depth.  The fixed frame, four
other active labels and one outer filler use `r+3` coordinates, so exactly
`q_act=k-r-3` exterior labels are available for `z`.  The two packet phases
have the same endpoints for every such choice.

### Theorem 1.1 (prescribed-flag physical fibre)

All `q_act` choices of `z` induce exactly the same signed lower occurrence
action at every depth `2<=q<=d`.

#### Proof

The authoritative flag theorem writes the complete action as

\[
 \Delta_q(\pi)=
 e_{G\cup\{a\}\cup P_t}
 +e_{G\cup\{b\}\cup S_t}
 -e_{G\cup\{b\}\cup P_t}
 -e_{G\cup\{a\}\cup S_t},\qquad t=d+1-q.              \tag{1.1}
\]

Here `G=K union {infinity,c}` and `P_t,S_t` belong to the fixed filler
flag.  The active role `z` does not occur in (1.1).  It also occurs in
neither active endpoint `Iab` nor `eab`.  Hence varying `z` changes the
physical old/new words but neither their endpoints nor any component of the
prescribed signed action.  Counting the remaining exterior labels gives
`q_act`; the complementary active-label audit checks its central-even
specialization `r-3`.  \(\square\)

The word map `z -> X_d(z)` is injective: the blocks `Ad,Bd,Cd` recover `z`.
Thus (0.1) is a prospective physical menu, not several replacements of one
incumbent fragment.

## 2. Exact resource dependence

Before fixing the endpoint label, the complementary orbit varies an ordered
pair `(e,z)`.  Its addresswise dependence is

\[
\begin{array}{c|ccc}
 &\text{constant}&e\text{-only}&z\text{-only}\\ \hline
 \text{owners}&6d+17&3d+10&3d+8\\
 \text{intersections}&6d+17&3d+11&3d+6\\
 \text{unions}&6d+16&3d+8&3d+10.
\end{array}                                                     \tag{2.1}
\]

Every fixed nonconstant exact value has label-cover number one.  Once the
physical endpoints fix `e`, the first two columns become the common bank and
the third column is precisely the candidate-dependent bank.  This proves
(0.2).

The distinction is load-bearing.  A common owner shared by two task frames
creates a complete cross-list conflict and kills the argument.  We therefore
use the following strong privacy condition.

> A task frame is **source private** when every resource common to all of
> its `z`-candidates is disjoint from every resource used by every other
> task frame.

Literal disjointness is required here because Theorem 4.1 and `PHE_disj`
construct simultaneous owner-disjoint slots.  A declared shared incumbent
bank may be harmless in a weaker capacity model, but then the conclusion is
only compatibility modulo that bank and a separate shared-owner resolver is
required; that weaker variant is not used below.

Under source privacy, only the `z`-dependent columns can create new
cross-task conflicts.

The table and cover-one conclusion are statements about the canonical
fixed-action orbit only.  One can count candidate-dependent addresses after
relabeling an endpoint-planted breaker, but those candidates no longer have
one prescribed action and therefore do not form a menu for the selection
theorem below.

## 3. Regenerative lift shadows

The exact local template recurrence has two same-parity branches.

* If the deadline does not jump, a fresh coordinate is put into the core;
  the complete slot cones and no new collision class appears.
* If the deadline jumps, a fresh filler coatom is inserted in every active
  block.  The twelve new owners are

  \[
                         K\cup V\cup F,                         \tag{3.1}
  \]

  one for each active triple `V`.

The variable active role `z` occurs only in `Ad,Bd,Cd`.  Therefore nine
owners in (3.1) are common to the fixed-action orbit and exactly three are
`z`-dependent.  Declare the common nine source private and add the three
variable lift shadows to the `3d+8` variable parent owners.  This gives the
exact one-jump budget (0.3).

The filler boundary staircase is independent of `z`.  It is consequently a
common frame guard, not a candidate-dependent exclusion.  It is still a
real physical requirement: the local recurrence exports the continuation
profile proved in the local template-recurrence theorem, and source privacy does not
construct the required exterior collar.

## 4. Bounded-bank owner selection

Let `T={1,...,H}` be unit-demand prescribed flag moves.  Task `i` has a
fixed-action candidate set `Z_i` of size `q_act=k-r-3`.  Assume:

1. the common owner and lift-shadow bank of every task is source private;
2. every fixed variable owner/lift-shadow token occurs in at most one
   candidate of every other list; and
3. at most `t_i` candidates of task `i` are removed by its declared external
   candidate-specific guards.

### Theorem 4.1 (exact bounded owner transversal)

There are choices `z_i in Z_i` whose complete owner and one-step lift-shadow
banks are pairwise disjoint provided

\[
      k-r-3>\max_i t_i+(H-1)(3d+11).                           \tag{4.1}
\]

#### Proof

Choose the tasks in any order.  A previously selected task has at most
`3d+11` variable owner/lift-shadow tokens.  By hypothesis 2, each kills at
most one candidate in the current list.  Source privacy removes every
common token from the conflict count.  After fewer than `H` choices, at most
`(H-1)(3d+11)` candidates are lost to earlier tasks, and at most `max t_i`
are lost to external guards.  Inequality (4.1) leaves a candidate.  Induct.
\(\square\)

If both immediate palettes in the **lifted child** are also unit-capacity
private resources, their common banks are source private, and their lifted
variable banks retain the cover-one property, replace `3d+11` by

\[
       (3d+11)+(3d+9)+(3d+13)=9d+33.                           \tag{4.2}
\]

The extra three intersection and three union addresses arise because each
of the three `z`-active blocks splits one old internal edge into two at a
deadline jump.  This proves (0.5) by the same argument.  Upper-coverage support values need
not be made private merely to apply a move: the local packet preserves their
complete decks.  Any construction imposing additional upper witness
ownership must list those tokens separately rather than hide them in U1.

### Corollary 4.2 (asymptotic bounded-bank availability)

For fixed `H`, `t_i<=Ad+B`, `d=o(r)` and `k-r=Theta(r)`, the owner-only and
optional immediate-palette selections exist for every sufficiently large
`r`.  Also `q_act` grows by exactly one under `k->k+2,r->r+1`.

This is an exact label/resource theorem.  It does not assert that the
selected candidate-dependent old words occur contiguously in a previously
fixed carrier.

## 5. Serial U1--U4 composition

Suppose one safe child carrier contains the selected old phases as
pairwise-disjoint contiguous slots with their certified exterior residence
collars.  Apply the prescribed moves in any order.

### Theorem 5.1

Every intermediate carrier has the same rank-`r` owner set, simple topology,
complete interval-OR coverage support and residence state as the starting
carrier.  In particular the entire prescribed flag word is physically
realized and U1--U4 remain exact.

#### Proof

Each local replacement has equal owner set and endpoints, equal
prefix/suffix/internal OR decks, and equal clipped boundary state with the
internal run floor.  Hence it is one edge of the safe-move graph.  Disjoint
slots remain present after other replacements, although disjointness is
stronger than serial safety requires.  Induction using the serial-safe-move
theorem proves the claim.  \(\square\)

### Theorem 5.2 (triangular breakers kill every reflected pair-current row)

Assume `d>=3` and the packet relabelling range
`r>=d+4,k-r>=4`.  Let `Omega=[k]` be the coordinate ground and put
`h=floor((d+1)/2)`.  For `2<=q<=h`, let

\[
 D_q(\Delta)=\deg_q^{(2)}(\Delta_q)
       -\deg_{d+2-q}^{(2)}(\Delta_{d+2-q}).                    \tag{5.1}
\]

Let `K_2(Omega)` be the integer kernel of the point-degree map on unordered
coordinate pairs.  The projection of the lattice generated by common-order
packets and all endpoint-planted single-block adjacent twists onto

\[
                         \prod_{q=2}^{h}K_2(\Omega)             \tag{5.2}
\]

via `(D_2,...,D_h)` is surjective.

#### Proof

Transpose omission positions `s,s+1` in the label-attached `Ica` block,
where `1<=s<h`.  Direct window geometry gives

\[
 D_q=0\quad(q>s+1),                                           \tag{5.3}
\]

while the diagonal `q=s+1` is the integral square

\[
 [f_0f_s]-[f_0f_{s+1}]-[pf_s]+[pf_{s+1}].                    \tag{5.4}
\]

Indeed, beyond the diagonal each relevant window contains both transposed
coatoms or neither, so its reflected pair contribution cancels; on the
diagonal exactly one boundary crosses the transposition.  Relabelling the
four displayed coordinates realizes every `K_4` alternating square.
Johnson squares generate `K_2(Omega)` integrally.

Now prescribe arbitrary elements of `K_2(Omega)` at depths `h,h-1,...,2`.
At depth `q`, use twists with `s=q-1` to set the desired value.  Equation
(5.3) leaves every already fixed later depth unchanged; the changes at
earlier depths are handled at subsequent steps.  Descending induction proves
surjectivity over the integers.  \(\square\)

Theorem 5.2 by itself is a quotient statement.  It removes every reflected
pair-current invariant; Theorem 5.4 below supplies the stronger formal
occurrence-lattice conclusion.  Neither theorem says that a nonnegative
physical walk realizes every signed combination.

### Corollary 5.3 (full coupled quadratic projection)

At every reflected pair of depths, common-order packets supply the diagonal
copy of `K_2(Omega)`, while Theorem 5.2 supplies an arbitrary difference.
At a central self-reflected depth the maximal-chain packet supplies
`K_2(Omega)` directly.  Hence the enlarged signed catalogue has

\[
 \operatorname{im}\!\left(
   L_d^{\rm packet}\xrightarrow{(\deg_q^{(2)})_{q=2}^{d}}
   \prod_{q=2}^{d}\mathbb Z^{\binom{\Omega}{2}}\right)
             =\prod_{q=2}^{d}K_2(\Omega).                       \tag{5.5}
\]

Thus every obstruction which factors linearly through these quadratic
currents is gone.  This is the strongest coupled-span conclusion currently
visible at quadratic level; the following theorem closes the formal
occurrence span itself.

### Theorem 5.4 (full labelled all-depth occurrence lattice)

Assume `d>=3`.  Let `L_d` be the integer span of all admissibly relabelled endpoint-planted
common-order packets and adjacent `Ica` twists, with both orientations.  If
`r>=d+4` and `k-r>=4`, then

\[
             L_d=\bigoplus_{q=2}^{d}\ker_{\mathbb Z}
             \left(\partial_{r-q}:\mathbb Z^{\binom{[k]}{r-q}}
             \longrightarrow\mathbb Z^k\right).              \tag{5.6}
\]

In particular this lattice is saturated.

#### Proof

Let `C_s=Delta^s-Delta^0` be the action difference between an adjacent
twist at omissions `f_s,f_(s+1)` and its untwisted planted packet.  The
exact window formula has a square depending on the extreme role `p` only
at depth `s+1`; its reflected square depends on `f_0`, and its remaining
triangular tail depends on neither `p`, `f_0` nor the active role `b`.

Swap the global roles `p,b` in a second labelled copy.  Subtraction gives

\[
 C_s-(p\,b)C_s=\mathbf e_{s+1}\otimes
 Q_H(b,p;f_s,f_{s+1}),                                      \tag{5.7}
\]

with zero action at every other depth.  Swapping `f_0,b` instead isolates
the reflected depth `d+1-s`.  Taking
`1<=s<=floor(d/2)` covers every depth `2,...,d`.  The role `b` reuses an
existing coordinate, which is why `k-r>=4` suffices.

Arbitrary admissible relabelling realizes every Johnson square in the
rank-`(r-q)` layer.  Johnson squares generate
`ker_Z(partial_(r-q))` integrally, so the right side of (5.6) is contained
in `L_d`.  Every packet action is point balanced at every depth, giving the
reverse inclusion.  \(\square\)

Equation (5.7) is a four-packet signed aperture commutator.  Its columns
generally have different endpoint roles and owner slots.  Therefore (5.6)
is not a fixed-slot native column, a nonnegative safe walk, or an edge in
the planted compiler return graph.  The local `q=1` palette is unchanged.

The saturated flag lattice guarantees that there is no missing integer
generator inside the fixed filler flag.  Theorem 5.1 says what follows once
the desired generators have literal slots.  Neither theorem constructs the
starting safe child carrier.

## 6. The exact prospective-host gate

For a bounded prescribed flag word `w`, define the **disjoint protected-host
extension** `PHE_disj(w)` to be the following statement.

> There exist candidate labels satisfying Theorem 4.1 and one literal
> same-parity Pascal child safe carrier which contains their old packet
> phases as endpoint-compatible slots, completes the complementary owner
> chronology, supplies every exported residence collar, and reserves the
> declared one-step lift shadows.

The universal statement “every algebraically valid packet is present in a
fixed child” is unnecessary and is generally the wrong quantifier.  The
fixed-incumbent fibre-one theorem shows why `PHE_disj(w)` does not follow
from (0.1): choosing a different `z` chooses a different old host word.

The owner part of `PHE_disj(w)` is closed by Theorem 4.1 for canonical
common-order moves.  Their residence collar is **not** closed.  The exact
nested-collar theorem applies to the corrected endpoint-planted order and
screen schedule `{0,2,4,6,8,10}`.  That planted fibre has different boundary
profiles from the canonical fixed-action `z`-orbit used in Theorem 4.1.
Conversely, the planted breaker has no action-invisible `z`-menu.

This is an exact interface mismatch, not a matter of scalar label supply:

* canonical common-order packets have the proved prescribed-action menu but
  still export their unresolved deadline-jump boundary staircase;
* corrected endpoint-planted packets have a constructive private nested
  collar, producing an owner-disjoint protected path forest, but no proved
  prescribed-action menu.

One endpoint-planted packet uses `r+4` coordinate labels, so even one such
private collar needs the necessary reservoir inequality

\[
                              k-r-4\ge d+1.                    \tag{6.1}
\]

For `H` simultaneous collars the literal hypothesis is stronger:
`Omega minus union_i supp(P_i)` must contain `H(d+1)` labels.  Separate
per-packet surplus does not imply this common-complement condition.

A positive theorem may close either side: construct a collar for the
canonical menu, or construct a fixed-action planted menu.  Only after that
identification may one reserve private collar labels and subtract their
coordinate cost from `q_act`.

Its exact remaining content is therefore the **prospective off-state
completion**:

1. identify the chosen candidate family with a validated collar fibre and
   place the collared old phases contiguously;
2. extend that protected forest to one simple owner chronology with
   a connected contracted quotient; and
3. make the residual immediate palettes and concatenated interval-OR deck
   accepting.

For fixed component orientations and clipped boundary states, item 2 is
exactly a state-expanded physically-simple port matching leaving the two
global ports unmatched and whose contracted quotient is one path.  This is
strictly stronger than ordinary Hall.  Items 2--3 are not implied by the
bare Pascal attachment Hall theorem or by the local template recurrence.

This is narrower than a quadratic fixed-slot menu theorem and narrower than
one compiler matching common to every phase.  It is a convenient sufficient
face, but it is not the logically weakest serial quantifier.

Define `PHE_walk(w)` to mean that there is a safe-graph walk whose `i`th
edge is a literal planted packet with the `i`th prescribed flag action; only
that next slot must exist after the first `i-1` moves.  Slots at different
times may overlap.  Then `PHE_walk(w)` is the exact necessary-and-sufficient
physical reachability statement for the prescribed word, while

\[
                         PHE_{disj}(w)\Longrightarrow PHE_{walk}(w). \tag{6.2}
\]

Theorem 4.1 proves the owner-selection row on the stronger disjoint face.
An availability theorem may instead attack `PHE_walk` directly by exposing
each next slot in the current chronology.

## 7. Weakest terminal-compiler implication

Let `lambda_d(T)` be the exact terminal compiler deletion number.  The
serial theorem permits the compiler to be absent at intermediate carriers.

### Theorem 7.1 (bounded prescribed route criterion)

Assume the starting child is an upper-complete safe, factor-resident
`B(k)`-carrier, and that every reachable regenerative state admits a flag
word `w` of length at most an absolute `H_0` such that:

1. `PHE_walk(w)` holds in one same-parity child; and
2. the terminal carrier `T_w` satisfies `lambda_d(T_w)<=C` for one absolute
   constant `C`.

If the starting child is upper complete, then

\[
                              \nu(k)\le B(k)+C.                  \tag{7.1}
\]

If the resulting protected slot/collar interface is again exported, the
same statement iterates on the odd regenerative spine.

#### Proof

Every edge in `PHE_walk(w)` is a literal safe coatom move, so induction by
the serial-safe-move theorem preserves upper completeness and residence even
when successive slots overlap.  Compile only the terminal carrier and append
its at most `C` omitted lower targets.  This is exactly the serial
component-reachability bound.  Export
is a separate hypothesis because terminal repair cells are paid, not
carried.  \(\square\)

The new flag theorem removes linear-lattice saturation from this criterion.
What remains is physical `PHE_walk(w)` (or the stronger `PHE_disj(w)`) and
the nonlinear condition in item 2.  No coupled formal occurrence invariant
remains by Theorem 5.4.  The exact local collar is
available on the corrected endpoint-planted fibre, but has not been
transferred to the canonical fixed-action menu.  No marginal Hall or scalar
unused-cell surplus proves that transfer, the connected accepting port
completion, or the compiler row.

There is also an exact fixed-basis warning.  Occurrence-labelled Pluecker
squares preserve the set of used compiler cells, so they route alternating
cycles but cannot close an alternating-path endpoint.  On the two native
nested chains, a concrete sufficient terminal row is an exterior guarded
Ferrers bank with

\[
 \beta_{\rm end}=
   \max_j(j-|N^-_j|)^+ + \max_j(j-|N^+_j|)^+=O(1),             \tag{7.2}
\]

with every required four-cycle physically certified.  Equation (7.2)
handles the basis-changing return endpoints; higher-depth cap compatibility
and the terminal matching remain nonlinear rows.  The local planted return
graph is empty, so this exterior bank cannot be omitted.
