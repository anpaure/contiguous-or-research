# GMM lower-complete cycles: the exact C6 upper-perfection and Markov gate

**Date:** 2026-08-14
**Status:** exact invariant/reduction; no all-parameter upper-perfect cycle

## 0. Outcome

Let `m>=2` and

\[
 n=2m+1,\qquad
 \mathcal X={ [n]\choose m},\quad
 \mathcal L={ [n]\choose m-1},\quad
 \mathcal U={ [n]\choose m+1},
\tag{0.1}
\]

and put

\[
 W=|\mathcal X|=|\mathcal U|,\qquad
 N=|\mathcal L|={m\over m+2}W,\qquad
 D=W-N={2W\over m+2}.
\tag{0.2}
\]

Contracting a GMM tight enumeration of levels `m-1,m` gives a Hamilton
cycle `C` of `J(n,m)` with complete lower-intersection support.  Write

\[
 \ell_C=\mathbf1_{\mathcal L}+d,
 \qquad d\ge0,\qquad \sum_Rd(R)=D,                 \tag{0.3}
\]

and let `u_C` be its upper-union load.  This note settles what alternating
Johnson `C_4/C_6` flips can prove at the invariant level.

1. Any sequence which preserves the entire lower load `(0.3)` also
   preserves the point-degree vector `P_{m-1}d`.  Exact upper perfection
   is therefore possible in that fixed lower-load fibre only if

   \[
   P_{m-1}d=\eta\mathbf1,
   \qquad \eta={D(m-1)\over2m+1}.                  \tag{0.4}
   \]

   This is an invariant of every lower-neutral switch, not merely of a
   proposed monotone algorithm.
2. Condition `(0.4)` is the **complete integral additive obstruction**.
   Once it holds, the desired upper correction lies in the saturated
   signed lattice generated by five-coordinate, one-copy lower-neutral
   alternating `C_6` packets.  Equivalently, it is already generated by
   lower-neutral two-edge `C_4` rectangles with a repeated lower colour.
   Thus there is no further additive congruence invariant in the upper-load
   quotient.
3. The explicit `C_6` generator has upper action one elementary rectangle
   and uses three distinct lower colours on each shore.  It is
   support-minimal only within that injective-lower catalogue: without the
   injectivity constraint the same rectangle has a two-edge `C_4` lift.
   Signed generation does not imply a conformal sequence on the GMM cycle.
   A literal `C_6` flip is legal only when its three negative edges occur,
   its three positive edges are absent, and its six ports have a
   Hamilton-safe cyclic order.
4. Preserving **coverage**, rather than the full lower load, enlarges the
   problem.  A flip may remove a lower occurrence only if another copy
   remains or the same colour is reinserted.  Such flips can change `d` and
   therefore must first route it to a regular one-design `(0.4)`.  This is a
   lower-surplus transport problem, not a matroid basis-exchange theorem.
5. The exact positive statement is consequently a Markov-fibre theorem:
   choose a GMM tight cycle whose surplus is point-regular, then prove that
   its lower-covered Hamilton component under legal alternating `C_4/C_6`
   moves contains an upper-perfect state.  Existing lattice and matching
   theorems do not supply conformality or chronology.
6. There are two noninterchangeable `C_6` notions.  A hexagon exchange of
   the second perfect matching in gate `(4.6)` preserves degree one at
   every upper vertex and therefore preserves an already all-one upper
   ledger.  A GMM cycle with repeated and missing upper colours is not a
   point of that paired-matching polytope.  Its repair requires a Johnson
   switch which changes the upper ledger while preserving the lower ledger;
   the injective-lower `C_6` in item 2 is one such generator, while a
   repeated-lower `C_4` is another.  Gate `(4.6)` characterizes the desired
   endpoint but is not itself a reconfiguration path from the GMM source.

This gives a sharp answer to gate `(4.6)` of
`MATH_THEOREM_GMM_MIDDLE_LEVELS_DOUBLE_RAINBOW_FUSION_AUDIT_20260726.md`:
ordinary matching/Hall feasibility is automatic in the regular residual
graph, while the colour-covering equations have a uniform fractional point.
An exact switch upgrade is controlled first by the lower-surplus one-design
and then by a nonlinear Hamilton Markov problem.

## 1. Three ledgers and the forced design

For a Johnson edge `e=XY`, define

\[
 L(e)=X\cap Y,\qquad U(e)=X\cup Y,
\tag{1.1}
\]

and let `B_0,B_-,B_+` record respectively its two owner endpoints, lower
colour, and upper colour.  Let `P_j` be point-versus-rank-`j` incidence.
On every edge,

\[
 \boxed{P_{m-1}B_-+P_{m+1}B_+=P_mB_0.}             \tag{1.2}
\]

This is the coordinatewise identity

\[
 \mathbf1_{X\cap Y}+\mathbf1_{X\cup Y}
 =\mathbf1_X+\mathbf1_Y.                            \tag{1.3}
\]

Because `C` is Hamilton on the full owner layer,

\[
 B_0\mathbf1_C=2\mathbf1_{\mathcal X}.             \tag{1.4}
\]

Substitute `(0.3)` and subtract the all-one upper target.  Elementary
binomial incidence counts give

\[
 \boxed{
 P_{m+1}(u_C-\mathbf1_{\mathcal U})
 =\eta\mathbf1-P_{m-1}d,
 \qquad \eta={D(m-1)\over2m+1}.}                   \tag{1.5}
\]

The following equivalent binomial expression proves that `eta` is integral:

\[
 \eta=
 2{2m\choose m-1}-{2m\choose m-2}-{2m\choose m}.   \tag{1.6}
\]

### Theorem 1.1 (fixed-lower-load obstruction)

Suppose `C'` is any spanning two-factor on the same owner layer with

\[
 B_-\mathbf1_{C'}=B_-\mathbf1_C.                   \tag{1.7}
\]

If `C'` is upper-perfect, then `(0.4)` holds.  Consequently, if the GMM
surplus `d` is not point-regular, no sequence of lower-load-preserving
alternating `C_6` flips can make it upper-perfect.

#### Proof

Apply `(1.2)` to `C'-C`.  Both `B_0` and `B_-` differences vanish, so the
upper point-margin difference vanishes.  If \(u_{C'}=\mathbf1\), equation
`(1.5)` must be zero. \(\square\)

This obstruction is stronger than scalar lower completeness.  Tightness
fixes only `d>=0` and `sum d=D`; it does not prove `(0.4)`.

Since every spanning two-factor has \(W=|\mathcal U|\) edges,

\[
 \|u-\mathbf1\|_1=2R^+(u),
 \qquad R^+(u)=\sum_U(u(U)-1)_+.                    \tag{1.8}
\]

### Corollary 1.2 (point-discrepancy lower bound)

Every spanning two-factor with fixed lower load \(\mathbf1+d\) satisfies

\[
 \boxed{
 R^+(u)\ge
 \max\left\{
 {\|P_{m-1}d-\eta\mathbf1\|_1\over2m},
 \|P_{m-1}d-\eta\mathbf1\|_\infty
 \right\}.}                                         \tag{1.9}
\]

#### Proof

Equation `(1.5)` identifies the discrepancy with
`P_{m+1}(u-\mathbf1)`.  Pair each positive unit of `u-\mathbf1` with a
negative unit.  Two rank-`(m+1)` sets in `[2m+1]` differ in at most `m`
points on either side, so one pair contributes at most `2m` to the
\(\ell_1\) norm of its point-incidence difference.  This gives the first
bound.  For each point, positive and negative mass are separately at most
`R^+(u)`, giving the second. \(\square\)

## 2. The complete signed C4/C6 image

Define the fixed-owner, fixed-lower integral trade lattice

\[
 \mathcal T=\ker_{\mathbb Z}B_0\cap\ker_{\mathbb Z}B_-.
\tag{2.1}
\]

Equation `(1.2)` gives

\[
 B_+(\mathcal T)\subseteq\ker_{\mathbb Z}P_{m+1}.  \tag{2.2}
\]

For an `(m-1)`-set `C` and distinct `a,b,c,d` outside it, put

\[
 \rho(C;a,b,c,d)
 =e_{Cbc}+e_{Cad}-e_{Cbd}-e_{Cac}.                  \tag{2.3}
\]

The elementary integer-kernel theorem says

\[
 \ker_{\mathbb Z}P_{m+1}
 =\langle\rho(C;a,b,c,d)\rangle_{\mathbb Z}.        \tag{2.4}
\]

Every generator `(2.3)` has a one-copy lower-neutral alternating `C_6`
lift when `C` is nonempty.  Choose `p in C`, put `K=C-p`, and take the cyclic owners

\[
 Kab,\ Kbc,\ Kbp,\ Kpd,\ Kap,\ Kac.                \tag{2.5}
\]

Replace the alternating matching in positions `12,34,56` by that in
positions `23,45,61`.  The two shores use the same three pairwise distinct
lower colours

\[
 Kb,\quad Kp,\quad Ka,                              \tag{2.6}
\]

and their upper difference is exactly `(2.3)`.

There is also a smaller repeated-colour lift.  Take the cyclic owners

\[
 Cb,\ Cd,\ Ca,\ Cc.                               \tag{2.6a}
\]

Replace edges `12,34` by `23,41`.  Both shores have lower multiset
`{C,C}`, and the upper difference is again `(2.3)`.  Thus the `C_6`
catalogue is valuable for keeping the three lower occurrences distinct,
but it is not the unrestricted support-minimal lift of a rectangle.

### Theorem 2.1 (complete additive invariant theorem)

\[
 \boxed{B_+(\mathcal T)=\ker_{\mathbb Z}P_{m+1}.}   \tag{2.7}
\]

Moreover the lattice is saturated and is generated on the upper quotient
by the `C_4` packets `(2.6a)`; when `m>=2`, the same rectangles also have
the occurrence-distinct `C_6` lifts `(2.5)`.

#### Proof

The inclusion is `(2.2)`.  Integral rectangle generation `(2.4)` and the
explicit lifts `(2.5)--(2.6a)` give the reverse inclusion over
\(\mathbb Z\). \(\square\)

### Corollary 2.2 (exact algebraic criterion)

There is an integral signed fixed-owner, fixed-lower trade sending `u_C`
to the all-one upper ledger if and only if `(0.4)` holds.

#### Proof

By `(1.5)`, the desired difference \(\mathbf1-u_C\) has zero point margins
exactly under `(0.4)`.  Apply Theorem 2.1. \(\square\)

Thus point margins are complete and torsion-free in the signed upper-load
quotient.  This does not exclude parity or component invariants of a
restricted **legal** move graph.

## 3. Exact local legality and chronology

The `C_6` in `(2.5)` is support-minimal among one-copy lower-neutral
binary packets whose lower shore consists of distinct colours used once
each.  A one-edge exchange is trivial.  In a two-edge `C_4`, matching two
distinct negative lower colours to two distinct positive lower colours
forces the opposite-path colours to coincide, so the injective condition
fails.  Without injectivity, `(2.6a)` is the support-minimal rectangle.

Not every lower-neutral `C_6` has rectangle action.  For example, at
`m=3,n=7`, the cyclic owners

\[
 256,567,456,145,245,235                         \tag{3.0}
\]

have lower shore `{56,45,25}` in both orientations, but upper shores
`{2567,1456,2345}` and `{4567,1245,2356}` with no cancellation.  The
claims below about four upper targets and the distance-two lock concern
the explicit rectangle packets `(2.5)`, not arbitrary neutral hexagons.

For a literal flip at a Hamilton cycle, three additional conditions are
load-bearing.

1. The negative matching is contained in the current cycle.
2. The positive matching is absent from the retained cycle.
3. Deleting the three negative edges leaves three paths whose outside
   endpoint matching, together with the positive matching, is a six-cycle.

The third condition is exact chronology.  On the six quotient ports the
outside matching has four simultaneously Hamilton-safe possibilities;
generic port order splits the factor.

The upper rectangle in `(2.3)` has two positive targets `A` and two
negative targets `Q`.  Its exact repeat-excess derivative is

\[
 \Delta R^+
 =|\{U\in A:u(U)\ge1\}|-|\{U\in Q:u(U)\ge2\}|.     \tag{3.1}
\]

Hence even an eligible rectangle `C_6` need not descend.  If every upper
hole is at Johnson distance at least three from every repeated target,
neither orientation of any explicit rectangle packet is a strict descent,
because its four upper targets have diameter at most two.

These are nonlinear support restrictions.  They are invisible to the
saturated lattice theorem.

## 4. Coverage-preserving flips are a larger problem

The fixed-load result does not say that every useful flip must preserve
`d` pointwise.  Lower coverage asks only

\[
 B_-\mathbf1_{C'}\ge\mathbf1_{\mathcal L}.          \tag{4.1}
\]

For a signed move `z`, its exact legality on the lower shore is

\[
 \mathbf1+d+B_-z\ge\mathbf1,
 \quad\hbox{equivalently}\quad d+B_-z\ge0.          \tag{4.2}
\]

If the final state is upper-perfect, applying `(1.2)` shows that its final
surplus `d'=d+B_-z` must satisfy

\[
 d'\ge0,\qquad \sum_Rd'(R)=D,\qquad
 P_{m-1}d'=\eta\mathbf1.                            \tag{4.3}
\]

Thus allowing coverage-preserving but lower-moving `C_6` flips does not
remove the design law; it turns it into a transport target.  The exact
problem separates into two stages which may have to be interleaved:

1. move the `D` surplus tokens to a regular rank-`(m-1)` multidesign while
   never making a lower load zero;
2. within that fixed final lower fibre, realize the point-neutral upper
   correction conformally and preserve one Hamilton cycle.

Ordinary matching or matroid intersection does not encode this.  A cycle
edge simultaneously consumes two owner incidences and one lower/upper
colour pair; the natural grouped incidence matrix already contains a
determinant-two minor, so the direct total-unimodularity route is
unavailable.  The Hamilton one-cycle condition is not encoded by ordinary
matching or matroid intersection, and basis exchange alone gives neither
`(4.2)` nor the one-cycle port order.

The regular design target is not arithmetically empty.  Among all
`D`-element subfamilies of \(\mathcal L\), minimize the sum of squared point
degrees.  If selected degrees satisfy `r_x>=r_y+2`, selected blocks
containing `x` but not `y` outnumber selected blocks containing `y` but not
`x`.  The bijection `R\mapsto R-x+y` therefore sends some former block to
an unselected block, and that swap decreases the square sum.  Hence all
degrees differ by at most one; their common average is the integer `eta`,
so they all equal `eta`.  Thus a simple `D`-block solution of `(4.3)`
exists for `m>=2`.  What is missing is its realization as the lower surplus
of one Hamilton factor.

There is also an exact transport lower bound.  Let

\[
 \mathcal D=\{d'\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{\mathcal L}:
 \sum_Rd'(R)=D,\ P_{m-1}d'=\eta\mathbf1\}.          \tag{4.4}
\]

One `C_6` flip removes three Johnson edges and inserts three, so its lower
load change has \(\ell_1\) norm at most six.  Therefore any coverage-preserving
`C_6` path from surplus `d` to an upper-perfect state has length at least

\[
 \boxed{{1\over6}\min_{d'\in\mathcal D}\|d-d'\|_1.} \tag{4.5}
\]

This bound allows edge reuse and arbitrary chronology, so it applies to
every conformal implementation which uses only `C_4/C_6` moves: a `C_4`
changes the lower load by at most four and a `C_6` by at most six.  For a
`C_4`-only path, the denominator six can be sharpened to four.

## 5. Why gate `(4.6)` is an endpoint theorem, not a GMM repair engine

An upper-perfect, lower-complete Johnson Hamilton cycle lifts to a spanning
two-factor of the middle-level incidence graph `B_m`.  Its alternating
edges split into two perfect matchings `M_0,M_1`; fixing `M_0`, retaining
lower coverage, and retaining one-cycle monodromy gives exactly gate
`(4.6)`.  An alternating incidence `C_6` merely permutes three `M_1`
partners.  It keeps one selected incidence at every upper vertex, so the
projected upper load remains `\mathbf1_{\mathcal U}` throughout.

By contrast, the edge-occurrence incidence lift of the GMM cycle before
repair gives upper vertex `U` degree `2u_C(U)`: repeated colours have
degree at least four and holes have degree zero.  (In the simple incidence
graph, two occurrences may reuse an incidence edge.)  Unless
`u_C=\mathbf1`, this occurrence lift is not the union of two perfect
matchings on the upper shore.  In particular there is no residual
`m`-regular matching instance `B_m-M_0` whose ordinary matching exchanges
encode the GMM state.

Thus the two directions are dual but distinct:

\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
\text{source}&\text{local switch}&\text{protected ledger}\\ \hline
\text{upper-perfect paired matchings}&
\text{incidence }C_6&\text{upper exactness}\\
\text{GMM lower-complete cycle}&
\text{Johnson-edge }C_4/C_6&\text{lower load or coverage}.
\end{array}                                           \tag{5.1}
\]

The paired-matching fractional point proves endpoint marginal feasibility.
It gives no alternating path from a non-upper-perfect GMM source.

## 6. The exact Markov theorem still needed

Let \(\mathfrak G\) be the graph whose vertices are lower-covered Hamilton
cycles on \(\mathcal X\), with adjacency given by legal alternating Johnson
`C_4/C_6` flips.  Let \(\mathfrak G_d\) be the induced subgraph on cycles
having one fixed lower load \(\mathbf1+d\).  One may also study its
restricted `C_6`-only subgraph when distinct lower occurrences are
required.

The strongest theorem supported by the exact algebra is:

> **GMM switch Markov gate.**  Choose a GMM tight enumeration whose contracted
> cycle has point-regular surplus `(0.4)`.  Prove that the component of that
> cycle in \(\mathfrak G_d\) contains a cycle with upper load
> `\mathbf1_{\mathcal U}`.

A more flexible version allows paths in \(\mathfrak G\) which first transport
the surplus to some point-regular `d'` satisfying `(4.3)`.

Theorem 2.1 proves that these targets lie in the same signed affine lattice.
It does not prove that they lie in the same conformal Hamilton component.
The precise missing ingredients are:

* a GMM selection theorem producing point-regular lower surplus, or a legal
  surplus-transport theorem;
* a dense/connected eligible `C_4/C_6` atlas on the chosen cycle;
* a batch rule defeating the distance-two defect lock; and
* chronological control of the six ports so every intermediate state is
  one cycle.

There is no remaining additive congruence obstruction in the unrestricted
signed upper-load quotient after these reductions.  This does not erase
the monodromy-sign/component-parity invariant of incidence-`C_6` matching
exchanges or possible support-state invariants of the legal Johnson move
graph.  Hall/cut conditions still govern prescribed physical support and
extension, but the uniform fractional point does not imply their integral
conformal solution.  Any sharper no-go must therefore live outside the
upper-histogram lattice, while any positive proof must be a conformal
Markov-basis theorem for this specific Boolean incidence host.

## 7. Scope

This note proves no upper-perfect GMM cycle.  It freezes the exact first
obstruction `(0.4)`, proves that it is complete in the signed upper-load
image (generated already by `C_4`s and also by injective-lower `C_6`s), and
identifies the conformal/chronological gate.  It does not infer
point-regularity or switch occurrence from the word “tight.”  The excluded
case `m=1` is elementary and is not covered by the nontrivial lift/design
statements.
