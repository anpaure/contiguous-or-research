# PBBS height pentagons admit an exact three-screen common-history lift

**Date:** 2026-08-05  
**Method:** literal matching-exchange normal form, interval-cell transport,
and complete exterior-current calculus; no computation or search  
**Status:** unconditional head-ring algebra and symmetry obstruction; the
exact compiler transport is conditional on prospective source planting.
The explicit PBBS height pentagon is a five-head common-history rethread
with rank-three screens.  Once that history is literally planted, every
strict-lower occurrence and every ordinary lower compiler assignment
transports with zero terminal loss.  Thus the raw matched cut-load
`e_H(M)` is not the correct remaining quantity.  Positive residence is
automatic.  Arbitrary-width upper support, zero-gap residence, and the
typed common cap reduce to one complete exterior-state circulation; that
circulation is not supplied here.

## 0. Outcome

pentagons from
`MATH_THEOREM_PBBS_AH_PENTAGON_COMPONENT_LOAD_AND_REGENERATION_20260805.md`.
That note transported a reference compiler only on cells avoiding `D_H`,
and consequently bounded its loss by the raw cut-load

\[
 e_H(\mathcal M)
 =|\{X:E(J_X)\cap D_H\ne\varnothing\}|.                 \tag{0.1}
\]

The main result below replaces (0.1) by an explicit local cell exchange.
Under the exact prospective history realization stated in Section 3,

\[
 \boxed{\lambda_{\rm lower}^{\rm terminal}=0}             \tag{0.2}
\]

for the whole simultaneous pentagon ladder.  This remains true even when
`e_H(M)` itself is large.

The remaining upper term is not another lower Hall problem.  It is the
signed complete exterior current of the five-head rethreads.  In
particular the former combined target

\[
 |\mathcal E_H|+\min_{\mathcal M}e_H(\mathcal M)=O(1)
                                                               \tag{0.3}
\]

should be replaced, on this decorated face, by

\[
 \boxed{|\mathcal E_H^{\rm ext}|+\lambda_{\rm cap}^{\rm typed}=O(1).}
                                                               \tag{0.4}

Here `E_H^ext` contains only arbitrary-width exterior upper casualties and

## 1. Every matching-exchange cycle is a head rethread

Work in the height-pentagon notation.  Put

\[
 n=2r+1,
 \qquad R=r+1,
\]

so the physical owner shore has rank `R`.  Fix one height `h` and abbreviate
the five exchange states by

\[
 Z_0,Z_1,Z_2,Z_3,Z_4,
 \qquad Z_i\longrightarrow Z_{i+1}\quad(i\in\mathbb Z_5).
                                                               \tag{1.1}
\]

Let

\[
 Q_i=[n]\setminus Z_i
\]

be the old `M_1` owner, and let

\[
 \ell_i=M_1(Q_i),
 \qquad P_i=M_0^{-1}(\ell_i).                              \tag{1.2}
\]

Thus the old contracted owner edge is `P_i Q_i`.  The meaning of the
exchange arrow in (1.1) is precisely that the incidence formerly ending at
`Q_i` is moved to `Q_(i+1)`.  Hence

\[
 \ell_i=Q_i\cap Q_{i+1},                                  \tag{1.3}
\]

and the old and new owner shores are

\[
       P_iQ_i
 \quad\longleftrightarrow\quad
       P_iQ_{i+1}.                                        \tag{1.4}
\]

Equation (1.4) is a literal cyclic head rethread.  It uses no property
special to a triangle or a clean `C6`.

## 2. The height pentagon has one common rank-`R-3` core

The five exchange states have the form

\[
 Z_i=L_h\cup S_i,
 \qquad |L_h|=r-2,
 \qquad |S_i|=2,                                         \tag{2.1}
\]

where every `S_i` is contained in the five-label survivor set

\[
 A_h=\{z_0,z_1,c,a,z_2\}.                                \tag{2.2}
\]

Define the complementary common core

\[
 G_h=[n]\setminus(L_h\cup A_h).                          \tag{2.3}
\]

Then

\[
 |G_h|=(2r+1)-(r-2)-5=r-2=R-3.                           \tag{2.4}
\]

Every head owner is

\[
 Q_i=G_h\cup(A_h\setminus S_i),                          \tag{2.5}
\]

so it contains `G_h`.  The forward survivors listed in the pentagon
theorem all belong to `A_h`; consequently the old facet

\[
 \ell_i=f(Z_i)=Q_i-\{p_+(Z_i)\}                          \tag{2.6}
\]

also contains `G_h`.  Since `P_i` contains `ell_i`, every tail owner
contains `G_h` as well.

Put

\[
 X_i=P_i\setminus G_h,
 \qquad Y_i=Q_i\setminus G_h.                            \tag{2.7}
\]

All these screens have rank three.  Notice that no special cyclic-adjacent
normal form for the screens is being asserted or needed.

### Theorem 2.1 (three-screen head-ring normal form)

For every height pentagon, the old and new owner edges are exactly

\[
 (G_h+X_i)(G_h+Y_i)
 \quad\longleftrightarrow\quad
 (G_h+X_i)(G_h+Y_{i+1}),                                  \tag{2.8}
\]

with all four displayed owners of rank `R` and both edges Johnson-legal.
The lower facet at role `i` is pointwise fixed.

#### Proof

Equations (1.4), (2.5), and (2.7) give (2.8).  Both owners in either edge
contain the rank-`R-1` set `ell_i`; because the endpoints are distinct
rank-`R` sets, their intersection is exactly `ell_i`.  Thus both edges are
Johnson-legal and the lower facet is unchanged.  \(\square\)

## 3. Exact strict-lower interval transport

Assume

\[
                         1\le d\le r-2.                   \tag{3.1}
\]

Choose any ordered partition into nonempty source letters

\[
 G_h=C_{h,1}\mathbin{\dot\cup}\cdots
          \mathbin{\dot\cup}C_{h,d},
 \qquad \mathcal C_h=(C_{h,1},\ldots,C_{h,d}).            \tag{3.2}
\]

For role `i`, form the old and new literal fragments

\[
 W_i=(X_i,\mathcal C_h,Y_i),
 \qquad
 \widehat W_i=(X_i,\mathcal C_h,Y_{i+1}).                 \tag{3.3}
\]

Their two length-`d+1` owner windows are exactly the two sides of (2.8).
As usual, the complete right context attached to `Y_i` moves with `Y_i`.

### Theorem 3.1 (rank-three-screen common-history theorem)

The rethread (3.3) has an occurrence-preserving bijection

\[
                  \Phi_h:\mathcal C_{<R}^{\rm old}
                           \longrightarrow
                           \mathcal C_{<R}^{\rm new}      \tag{3.4}
\]

between **all** source-interval cells whose OR has rank strictly below
`R`.  The map preserves the interval OR value and its width, and gives a
declared bijection of the complete role/occurrence addresses (right-side
addresses are transported with their heads rather than fixed literally).

#### Proof

Consider an interval meeting the displayed neighbourhood.  If it meets
both screen letters `X_i` and `Y_i`, it contains the complete common
history and hence contains

\[
                  G_h\cup X_i=P_i,
\]

which already has rank `R`.  A strict-lower interval therefore meets at
most one screen.

Intervals meeting the left screen are fixed role by role.  Intervals
meeting the right screen move with the complete pair consisting of `Y_i`
and its right context.  Since the ordered history `C_h` is identical in
every role, this is a cyclic permutation preserving width, value, and
address.  Intervals wholly in the history or in an unchanged context are
fixed or permuted identically.  These cases are disjoint and exhaustive;
reversing the head cycle gives the inverse map.  \(\square\)

The proof used only that the screens complete one rank-`R` owner.  Thus the
usual two-screen common-history theorem was not special to screen rank two.

### Corollary 3.2 (zero-loss lower compiler)

Let `M` be any matching of strict-lower targets to distinct valid
occurrence-labelled source cells before the rethread.  Then

\[
                         \Phi_h(M)                         \tag{3.5}
\]

is a matching of the same targets after the rethread.  In particular the
actual terminal lower deficiency is zero, even for assignments whose old
cells cross a deleted factor edge.

#### Proof

Theorem 3.1 fixes the cell value and is injective on occurrence addresses.
Transport every matching edge.  \(\square\)

### Corollary 3.3 (the whole height ladder)

Suppose all pentagons `2<=h<H` are prospectively realized with the literal
histories (3.2), with their complete moved contexts, in one source
chronology.  Then their simultaneous or serial composite transports every
strict-lower compiler with zero terminal deletion.

No commutativity is needed in the serial formulation: compose the
occurrence bijections in the factor state in which each move is used.
For a simultaneous formulation, require the declared source occurrence
supports to be disjoint or the maps to agree on their overlap.

This is the exact prospective-host premise.  Pairwise disjoint exchange
vertices alone do **not** prove that all histories (3.2) occur in one
already fixed antecedent.

## 4. Residence and the typed cap

### Proposition 4.1 (positive residence)

On a cyclic source chronology, every source occurrence in (3.3) belongs to
`d+1` consecutive owner windows.  Hence the decorated pentagon cannot create
a positive owner run shorter than `d+1`.  Overlapping occurrences merely
merge such runs.

For a linear chronology the fragment must be at least `d` source positions
from an endpoint or use the usual clipped endpoint collar.

This does not prove minimum zero-gap residence.  A head permutation can
join two positive runs which were formerly separated, thereby splitting a
zero gap.  The exact zero-gap premise is that every new seam has matching
length-`d` membership traces on its two sides, or is furnished with a
declared residence collar.

### Proposition 4.2 (typed-cap functoriality criterion)

Let `Gamma_old` be the complete typed cap network: its vertices include
literal cells, type/phase states, route resources, and sinks with their
capacities.  If the cell bijection `Phi_h` extends to a capacity-preserving
network isomorphism

\[
                  \widehat\Phi_h:\Gamma_{old}
                         \longrightarrow\Gamma_{new}       \tag{4.1}
\]

which fixes target demands, then every integral cap routing or matching
transports with zero deficiency.

#### Proof

Apply `widehat Phi_h` to every edge of the integral routing.  Incidence,
types, disjointness, and all capacities are preserved by the network
isomorphism.  \(\square\)

Ordinary compiler data encoded solely by target, cell value, width, and
occurrence address satisfy this criterion by Theorem 3.1.  The current
maximal common cap has additional phase flags and shared route capacities;
no theorem presently shows that they extend under `Phi_h`.  Therefore the
typed-cap row remains an explicit premise rather than a consequence of the
owner pentagon.

## 5. Raw cut avoidance is the wrong lower metric

The canonical height ladder actually forces many of its deleted edges into
the fixed gap-potential section.  This gives a useful obstruction to the
strategy `D_H cap S=emptyset`.

Fix `h>=2`, put `s=h+1`, and use the explicit state

\[
 Z_h^1=0_\rho\,1^s0^s(10)^{r-s}.                         \tag{5.1}
\]

Let `p` be the last up-step of the initial mountain and let its down-steps
be `v_1,...,v_s`.  The outgoing PBBS edge changes `p` to the root zero
`rho`.  Its q1 core is

\[
 K=Z_h^1-\{p\}
  =0_\rho,1^{s-1}0_p0_{v_1}\cdots0_{v_s}(10)^{r-s}.      \tag{5.2}
\]

The unit factors cancel in both directions.  The three forward-unmatched
zeros are

\[
                         \rho,v_{s-1},v_s,                 \tag{5.3}
\]

and the three reverse-unmatched zeros are

\[
                         p,v_1,v_2.                        \tag{5.4}
\]

With the required shared-coordinate order `C,A`, the forward-mark counts
in the three open reverse gaps beginning at `p,v_1,v_2` are

\[
                              (0,0,3).                     \tag{5.5}
\]

Thus the gap potential has its unique maximum at `p`, and the last forward
mark before it is `rho`.

### Theorem 5.1 (one forced selected cut at every ladder height)

The contracted owner edge corresponding to the deleted old `M_1` incidence
at `Z_h^1` belongs to the fixed gap-potential q1 section, independently of
every tie rule.  Consequently, writing `bar D_H` for the contracted owner
edges of the incidence set `D_H`,

\[
                     |\overline D_H\cap\mathcal S|\ge H-2.\tag{5.6}
\]

The same statement holds after any common ground-coordinate rotation of
the complete ladder.

#### Proof

Equation (5.5) gives gap-potential values `0,-1,-2` at the three reverse
marks.  The section therefore chooses the boundary

\[
                         K+p\longrightarrow K+\rho,
\]

which is the outgoing edge at `Z_h^1`.  These are precisely one of the five
deleted old incidences for each `h=2,...,H-1`.  Their literal cores have
different initial mountain lengths, so the edges are distinct.  The unique
maximum calculation is covariant under a common rotation.  \(\square\)

On the `d`-resident maximal-antecedent face, the row-identity theorem turns
each selected q1 occurrence into its declared ordinary source cell.  The
canonical gap-section compiler therefore uses the selected occurrence at
each of these colours, and its raw cut-load is at least `H-2`.  Corollary
3.3 nevertheless transports those cells with zero terminal loss after
common-history decoration.  This proves rigorously, on the same literal
source-realization face, that `e_H(M)` counts cells requiring transport, not
unavoidable compiler holes.

The theorem does not prove a lower bound on `min_M e_H(M)`: another old
occurrence of the same q1 colour may exist.  It does rule out eliminating
the lower issue merely by a common phase rotation of the canonical section.

## 6. The exact remaining upper current

Cut the old factor at the five old edges of one pentagon.  For role `i`,
let

\[
 A_i(u)=\text{union of the last `u` owners of its incoming arc},
\]

\[
 B_i(v)=\text{union of the first `v` owners of its outgoing arc},       \tag{6.1}
\]

with the endpoint owners included under one fixed convention.  The old
one-cut crossing deck has cells

\[
                         A_i(u)\cup B_i(v),                \tag{6.2}
\]

while the head rethread has cells

\[
                         A_i(u)\cup B_{i+1}(v).            \tag{6.3}
\]

The indices `u,v`, the physical role, and the target value must all be
retained.  Define the complete one-cut current

\[
 \partial^+_h
 =\sum_{i,u,v}[i,u,v,A_i(u)\cup B_{i+1}(v)]
  -\sum_{i,u,v}[i,u,v,A_i(u)\cup B_i(v)].                  \tag{6.4}
\]

Intervals contained in one cut arc are fixed.  Intervals traversing more
than one cut are described by the analogous complete arc-state tensor; they
cannot be discarded unless a full-union shield proves that their value is
the full ground set.

### Theorem 6.1 (upper-current reduction)

After the common-history lift, every remaining old upper casualty belongs
to the negative support of the sum of the complete exterior currents

\[
                         \sum_{h=2}^{H-1}\partial_h^+.     \tag{6.5}
\]

A target in that negative support is not a hole if it has an unchanged old
witness or appears in the positive support.  There is no cross-target
capacity coupling.  Thus upper closure is exactly a support-domination
problem for (6.5), not a lower compiler matching problem.

#### Proof

Every interval internal to a retained arc is copied verbatim.  Every other
old interval is encoded by the ordered arc suffixes, complete intervening
arcs, and ordered arc prefixes it traverses.  Replacing the old head
permutation by the new one gives the corresponding positive tensor term.
Subtracting yields (6.5).  A target survives precisely when at least one
of its old or new occurrence terms remains.  Distinct target values do not
share one exact-value interval cell, so no Hall coupling occurs.  \(\square\)

This theorem is exact but does not bound the negative support.  The
component-load-seven estimate and scalar PBBS multiplicities do not imply
such a bound, as established by the upper-multiplicity obstruction note.

## 7. Why a local or phase-only argument cannot close the upper row

The common-history lift is not context-free upper-transparent.  To see the
sharp issue for all sufficiently large `r`, note that the five tail screens
use at most five coordinates of `L_h`, while the head screens and common
history use none.  Choose distinct

\[
 p_0,\ldots,p_4\in L_h\setminus\bigcup_iX_i
\]

and give role `i` the private left-context singleton `p_i`; take the other
displayed contexts to omit all five private labels.  Since the old and new
owners at role `i` are three distinct rank-`R` supersets of the same facet,

\[
 (X_i\cup Y_i)\ne(X_i\cup Y_{i+1}).                       \tag{7.1}
\]

The old interval through the full fragment has value

\[
 T_i=\{p_i\}\cup G_h\cup X_i\cup Y_i,                    \tag{7.2}
\]

whereas the corresponding new value in the only role containing `p_i` is

\[
 \widehat T_i=\{p_i\}\cup G_h\cup X_i\cup Y_{i+1}.       \tag{7.3}
\]

They differ by (7.1).  In the truncated displayed family, the only intervals
containing `p_i` lie in role `i`, and that new role omits the distinguishing
element of `Y_i` altogether.  Thus `T_i` occurs in no other displayed role.
Hence a five-head common-history pentagon can have five genuine exterior
upper casualties.

This is an arbitrary-exterior counterexample, not a claim about the actual
canonical PBBS histories.  It proves that owner legality, the common source
history, and positive residence alone cannot imply `|E_H|=O(1)`.

A common ground rotation only conjugates the current:

\[
                         \partial^+_{h,t}
                         =\rho^t\partial^+_{h,0}.          \tag{7.4}
\]

Therefore rotation cannot turn a nonzero occurrence-labelled local current
into the zero current.  It may still move that current onto targets having
independent PBBS witnesses; proving that is exactly a factor-relative
corridor theorem, not a local phase identity.

## 8. A sufficient paired-pentagon completion

The current calculus gives one concrete positive target.

### Theorem 8.1 (inverse exterior-state companion)

Suppose every ladder pentagon is accompanied by a second legal rethread
whose complete exterior state, including all occurrence addresses, source
histories, residence seam types, and typed-cap resources, satisfies

\[
 D_{comp}^{-}=D_h^{+},
 \qquad
 D_{comp}^{+}=D_h^{-}.                                  \tag{8.1}
\]

Assume the two source occurrence maps are jointly injective and the two cap
maps preserve capacities.  Then the paired macro has

1. zero strict-lower compiler loss;
2. zero complete upper current;
3. zero positive-residence charge, with zero-gap residence preserved by
   the matched seam types; and
4. zero typed-cap deficiency.

#### Proof

The lower maps are bijections by Theorem 3.1 and compose.  Equation (8.1)
cancels the complete upper currents, including multi-cut terms.  The seam
types identify every run/gap boundary, and the cap isomorphisms transport
the integral routes.  \(\square\)

The theorem does not assert that the canonical PBBS factor contains such a
companion, nor that a companion has the required nonzero topology.  It
states the exact whole-packet object which would finish the local ladder
integration without any multiplicity estimate.

### Theorem 8.2 (rotation, complement, and reflection boundary)

None of the three canonical symmetries supplies a positive,
owner-simple, topology-changing inverse-state companion for the height
pentagon.

1. **Ground rotation.**  For every coordinate rotation `rho^t`,

   \[
                         \partial^+(\rho^tP)
                          =\rho^t\partial^+(P).             \tag{8.2}
   \]

   Thus rotation conjugates the complete state; it does not reverse the
   head permutation or its current.  A rotation orbit cancels only under
   the separate signed-necklace condition that every rotation orbit of
   target occurrences has coefficient sum zero.

2. **Set complement.**  Complement sends an interval union to the
   complement of an interval intersection.  Here it also sends a rank-`R`
   owner (`R=r+1` on `2r+1` coordinates) to rank `r`, the opposite Middle
   Levels shore.  Therefore it is a dual lower/intersection transport, not
   a same-shore upper-current inverse.

3. **Complete typed reflection.**  Suppose a second occurrence system is
   paired with the first by a value-preserving involution `eta` which
   reverses every complete cut-path word, including source histories,
   exterior contexts, residence seam types, and cap types.  If `s` is the
   initial successor permutation on the cut paths, exact inverse-current
   pairing forces the terminal permutation to be

   \[
                         s^\dagger=\eta s^{-1}\eta.          \tag{8.3}
   \]

   Hence `s` and `s^dagger` have the same cycle type.  The reflected
   companion is all-width transparent but topology-neutral.

Moreover, a value-preserving reflected copy uses the same owner words in
reverse and therefore repeats their owner sets.  Two such copies cannot
coexist as disjoint occurrences in a simple owner-once factor.  Reusing the
same support serially and applying the inverse rethread merely undoes the
original pentagon.  Applying a nontrivial coordinate relabelling makes the
owners disjoint only by changing every literal target `T` to its relabelled
target; cancellation then holds merely up to that global twist and still
has the cycle-type invariance (8.3).

#### Proof

The first identity is equivariance of the interval deck under coordinate
rotation.  The complement assertion is De Morgan's law together with
`(2r+1)-R=r`.

For reflection, let

\[
 X_0\to X_1\to\cdots\to X_{q-1}\to X_0
\]

be one cycle of `s`.  Under (8.3) the corresponding terminal cycle is

\[
 \eta X_0\to\eta X_{q-1}\to\cdots\to\eta X_1\to\eta X_0.
\]

It is the value-preserving reversal of the initial component word.  This is
a bijection on cycles and on all cyclic interval occurrences, proving both
all-width transparency and cycle-type equality.  Value-preserving reversal
uses exactly the same owner sets.  Owner simplicity and the serial-inverse
claim are then immediate.  \(\square\)

### Corollary 8.3 (no local bijective companion can do both jobs)

Within the following scope:

1. two copies only;
2. no independent upper witness outside the copies;
3. arbitrary privately labelled two-sided contexts;
4. cancellation by a bijection of complete context occurrences; and
5. owner-once simplicity,

there is no phase-paired or complement-paired height-pentagon macro which
both cancels the complete upper current and decreases the component count.

#### Proof

Private context labels force every lost boundary occurrence to be paired
with its same-value reversed context occurrence; otherwise that private
target has no replacement.  Theorem 8.2(3) then forces anti-conjugacy and
cycle-type equality.  A value-identical second copy violates owner
simplicity, while complement does not lie on the owner shore.  \(\square\)

The scope is load-bearing.  A PBBS-specific alternative occurrence outside
the two copies, a non-bijective upper reservoir, or a globally accepted
coordinate twist can evade the private-context argument.  None is supplied
by the present height ladder.

## 9. Exact remaining lemma

The lower cut-load row is closed once the prospective three-screen histories
are planted.  The remaining theorem can be stated without `e_H(M)`.

> **PBBS pentagon exterior-state circulation lemma.**  Plant the explicit
> height pentagons with their rank-three common histories in one biresident
> source antecedent and one typed cap.  Supply a PBBS-specific alternative
> witness for all but `O(1)` negative targets of (6.5), or a larger
> non-bijective state circulation not covered by Corollary 8.3, while the
> typed cap maps have `O(1)` deficiency.  The seam-state output must
> regenerate the same bounded interface.

Under this lemma, the component-load theorem and Corollary 3.3 give the
desired `B(k)+O(1)` ladder graft.  Zero unsupported values and zero cap
deficiency would give the exact `B(k)` version on this face.

What is no longer needed is a compiler chosen to avoid every pentagon cut.
The exact interval-cell exchange transports it through those cuts.
