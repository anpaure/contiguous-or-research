# Odd capacity-two sectors: exact rooted-path imbalance and the forced wrap-blossom gate

**Date:** 2026-08-05  
**Method:** signed generating functions and path-component matching; no
computation  
**Status:** unconditional no-go and sharp reduction.  Choosing one radial
socket and deleting the unique odd wrap direction does not reduce an odd
capacity-two sector to the even Hall theorem: the rooted path sector has
an explicit unbounded parity imbalance.  Any odd-level proof must retain
enough same-shore wrap ears (or equivalent odd blossoms) to cancel this
imbalance before the even augmented Hall router can apply.

## 1. The rooted path sector

Let

\[
 \mathcal T_{\ell,R}
 =\left\{t\in\{0,1,2\}^{\{0,\ldots,\ell-1\}}:
             \sum_i t_i=R\right\},
\tag{1.1}
\]

and join two states when one token is moved between consecutive
coordinates.  Put

\[
                         \ell=2m+1,
 \qquad                  R\text{ odd}.
\tag{1.2}
\]

Delete the wrap transfers between coordinates `2m` and `0`.  The remaining
graph `P_(ell,R)` is the capacity-two token graph of a path.  It is
bipartite under

\[
                         \chi(t)=\sum_{i=0}^{2m} i t_i\pmod2.
\tag{1.3}
\]

Write `Delta_(m,R)` for the number of `chi=0` states minus the number of
`chi=1` states.

### Theorem 1.1 (exact rooted-path imbalance)

For every odd `R`,

\[
 \boxed{
 \Delta_{m,R}
   =[z^{R-1}](1+z^2+z^4)^m.}
\tag{1.4}
\]

In particular `Delta_(m,R)>0` throughout the nonempty odd-mass range, and
it is unbounded with `m`.  At central mass `R=2s+1`, it equals the
coefficient of `u^s` in `(1+u+u^2)^m`.

#### Proof

The signed mass enumerator is

\[
\begin{aligned}
 \sum_R\Delta_{m,R}z^R
 &=\prod_{i=0}^{2m}
       \left(1+(-1)^i z+z^2\right)\\
 &=(1+z+z^2)^{m+1}(1-z+z^2)^m.
\end{aligned}
\tag{1.5}
\]

The elementary identity

\[
 (1+z+z^2)(1-z+z^2)=1+z^2+z^4
\tag{1.6}
\]

turns (1.5) into

\[
 (1+z+z^2)(1+z^2+z^4)^m.
\tag{1.7}
\]

The second factor has only even powers.  For odd `R`, only the middle
term `z` in the first factor contributes, proving (1.4).  Positivity and
the central coefficient statement are immediate.  For example, already
`[u^m](1+u+u^2)^m` grows without bound.  \(\square\)

### Corollary 1.2 (one root cannot balance the path)

Every matching using only nonwrap transfers leaves at least
`Delta_(m,R)` vertices unmatched.  Deleting one prescribed incoming
socket and allowing one outgoing socket can lower this bound by at most
two.  Thus the rooted-path reduction has deficiency at least

\[
                         \Delta_{m,R}-2,
\tag{1.8}
\]

which is unbounded.

#### Proof

Every path-transfer edge joins opposite `chi` shores, so a matching leaves
at least the absolute shore difference.  Deleting one vertex changes the
difference by exactly one in absolute value at best.  Two socket deletions
therefore change the lower bound by at most two.  \(\square\)

This disproves the proposed route

\[
 \text{choose the odd socket}\longrightarrow
 \text{cut the wrap}\longrightarrow
 \text{apply even Hall}.
\]

It does not disprove factor-criticality of the full cyclic token graph;
it proves that any such theorem must use wrap transfers essentially.

## 2. What the wrap direction contributes

A wrap transfer moves a token between coordinates `0` and `2m`.  Both
indices are even, so it **preserves** `chi`.  Thus wrap edges are exactly
same-shore edges relative to the rooted path bipartition.

Let `W` be any matching of wrap edges, with `W_0` edges on the `chi=0`
shore and `W_1` on the `chi=1` shore.  After deleting their endpoints, the
residual shore difference is

\[
                         \Delta_{m,R}-2|W_0|+2|W_1|.
\tag{2.1}
\]

### Proposition 2.1 (necessary wrap current)

If the remaining nonwrap graph is to have matching deficiency at most one,
then necessarily

\[
 \left|\Delta_{m,R}-2|W_0|+2|W_1|\right|\le1.
\tag{2.2}
\]

In particular, when wrap edges are chosen only on the majority shore,
at least

\[
                         \left\lfloor{\Delta_{m,R}\over2}\right\rfloor
\tag{2.3}
\]

same-shore wrap edges are required, up to the parity-one terminal state.

#### Proof

Every selected same-shore edge removes two vertices from its shore.  All
remaining nonwrap edges cross the shores, so their matching deficiency is
at least the residual absolute difference.  This gives (2.2)--(2.3).
\(\square\)

The wrap bank is therefore not a bounded exceptional correction.  It is
the macroscopic parity current which pays the Euler characteristic (1.4).
The odd circulation blossoms in the adjacent-necklace construction have
exactly this role: their direct and counter-circulation routes have
opposite parity and create an effective same-shore relation after their
interiors are matched.

## 3. Interaction with the augmented receiver jobs

Fix the root boundary between coordinates `2m` and `0`.  An actual
receiver square is generated by two parent shifted petals.

* If neither petal shifts across the root boundary, all four receiver
  edges are nonwrap edges.  Its two local square shores agree with the
  rooted `chi` shores.  The two private job terminals add one vertex to
  each shore, so this **root-regular job** changes the signed difference by
  zero.
* At most one parent petal in a hub fan can shift across the fixed root
  boundary.  Pair it, if passive, with one other petal.  The resulting
  **mixed job** has two opposite nonwrap receiver edges, each consuming
  one vertex from each shore, and two opposite wrap receiver edges.  One
  wrap edge lies on each rooted shore, because changing the fixed endpoint
  along the other, nonwrap petal toggles `chi`.

Thus a mixed job has three current modes:

\[
                    -2,\qquad0,\qquad+2,
\tag{3.1}
\]

according as it chooses the minority-shore wrap edge, a nonwrap edge, or
the majority-shore wrap edge.  There is at most one mixed job per literal
hub fan.  All remaining jobs are root-regular and belong to the bipartite
augmented Hall graph.

#### Proof of the classification

In a receiver square, the two sides parallel to a parent petal shift in
that petal's coordinate direction.  A nonwrap shift toggles (1.3), while
the unique wrap shift preserves it.  Two endpoint-disjoint petals cannot
both be the unique shifted edge across the same root boundary.  The four
claims follow directly.  \(\square\)

An incoming or outgoing singleton changes the signed difference by at
most one.  Consequently the imbalance (1.4) must be paid by the selected
mixed-job currents together with ordinary wrap/circulation ears; the
root-regular private terminals do not pay any of it.

The odd-level Tutte theorem can therefore be sharpened to the following
two-stage target.

> **Wrap-current protected extension theorem.**  Choose modes for every
> mixed job and a vertex-disjoint bank of additional wrap/circulation
> ears, compatible with every root-regular receiver rectangle and both
> reset colours, such that:
>
> 1. the total signed same-shore current satisfies (2.2);
> 2. after the ear endpoints are deleted and their interiors matched, the
>    mixed jobs are installed in their selected modes and the residual
>    root-regular augmented graph is bipartite; and
> 3. that residual graph satisfies the even Hall rows (4.7)--(4.8), or
>    equivalently the sector-expansion-minus-bicircular-surplus rows
>    (4.10)--(4.12).

If this theorem holds, adjoining the ear matchings to the residual Hall
matching gives the required odd-level matching with at most one socket.
Conversely, merely naming a concentrated or adaptive radial socket cannot
replace item 1 because it changes (1.4) by only one.

This is an exact reduction of the odd Tutte gate to a macroscopic
same-shore current plus the already isolated even Hall gate.  It does not
construct the required current bank.

## 4. A smaller exact cycle warning

The capacity-one face `T_(2m+1,1)=C_(2m+1)` is the smallest version of the
same phenomenon.  Removing the wrap edge leaves a path with one more
vertex on one shore.  A single root is enough there because (1.4) equals
one at `R=1`.  For general odd `R`, the coefficient (1.4) is larger; the
capacity-one intuition does not scale to the central sectors.

This explains why the concentrated-socket cycle and adaptive monomer lift
correctly transport one existing parity socket but do not by themselves
prove the baseline odd-sector matching.  Baseline matching requires the
full wrap/blossom current first.

## 5. Scope

Proved:

1. the exact rooted-path shore imbalance for every odd capacity-two
   sector;
2. an unbounded lower bound after deleting one incoming and one outgoing
   socket;
3. the exact signed current which any wrap-edge bank must supply; and
4. reduction of the odd-level problem to compatible wrap ears followed by
   the even augmented Hall theorem.

Not proved:

1. a wrap/circulation-ear bank satisfying (2.2) for every actual sector;
2. endpoint-disjoint packing of that bank with the receiver rectangles;
3. the residual even Hall inequalities; or
4. the complete adjacent-necklace or universal-word theorem.
