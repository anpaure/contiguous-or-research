# Quartet toric moves: exact determinant quotient and a dense Markov no-go

Date: 2026-07-26

Method: pure mathematics only. No finite search, computation, solver, or
web input is used.

## 0. Exact outcome

Let \(n=2m\), \(M=m+H\), and \(3\le M\le n-2\). Work with rooted
labelled cyclic frames on the \(M\)-tops.

1. After the natural factor-\(2\) normalization of rooted permutation
   parity on zero-top-margin frame differences, the unrestricted physical
   quartet lattice has Smith quotient

   \[
   \boxed{(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^n.}
   \]

   These are exactly the audited affine top characters. There is no odd
   torsion and no \(4\)- or higher \(2\)-power torsion in this
   determinant projection.
2. This differs from the untwisted checkerboard top-count lattice. The
   latter is saturated and has a free rank-\(n\) quotient. The two
   matrices agree modulo two but are not integrally gauge-equivalent.
3. In a fixed ordered-core/two-slot chart, the equal-score circulation
   lattice modulo quartets is \(\mathbb Z/2\). A parity-neutral
   double-triangle pair has two quartet-isolated endpoints. Thus quartets
   are not a Markov basis even after the chart torsion charge is cleared.
4. More strongly, a three-placeholder cube gives two squarefree
   one-frame-per-top tables with identical target loads phase by phase at
   every interval length. Its degree-eight difference is an integral sum
   of four quartets, yet both endpoints can be made globally
   quartet-isolated. The obstruction is conformal/nonnegative, not a
   further lattice character.
5. Disjoint cubes cover \(N_H-o(N_H)\) tops, where

   \[
   N_H=\binom{2m}{M},\qquad MN_H=(1+o(1))W.
   \]

   Hitting every intact cube by permanently discarded top rows costs
   \((1/8-o(1))W\) retained phase incidences. This is not a lower bound
   against temporary catalysts which may be recycled through many cubes.

Therefore quartet-only Markov connectivity is false. A coefficient-one
proof must add higher placeholder/cycle moves, or prove a literal
catalyst-routing theorem with an \(o(W)\) occurrence ledger. No claim of
coefficient one is made here.

## 1. The normalized physical parity projection

For each \(U\in\binom{[n]}M\), use its increasing ground-label word as a
rooted reference order. Let \(\mathcal F_U\) be its rooted frame set and
write \(\operatorname{sgn}_U(\pi)\in\{\pm1\}\) for frame parity. If

\[
z\in\bigoplus_U\mathbb Z^{\mathcal F_U},
\qquad
\sum_{\pi\in\mathcal F_U}z_{U,\pi}=0
\quad\text{for every }U,
\tag{1.1}
\]

then

\[
\rho_U(z)=\sum_\pi\operatorname{sgn}_U(\pi)z_{U,\pi}
\]

is even. Define the normalized parity current

\[
\bar\rho(z)=\frac12\sum_U\rho_U(z)e_U
\in E_M:=\bigwedge^M\mathbb Z^n,
\tag{1.2}
\]

where \(e_U\) is the canonical \(M\)-wedge. Rooting is essential: when
\(M\) is even, a one-step cyclic rotation changes the parity of a linear
representative.

Choose an \((M-2)\)-set \(C\), four distinct labels
\(a_0,a_1,b_0,b_1\notin C\), and one common positional word on
\(C\cup\{A,B\}\). Let \(q\) be its physical quartet exponent
difference.

### Lemma 1.1 (physical wedge image)

Up to one overall sign depending only on the common positional word,

\[
\boxed{
\bar\rho(q)
=e_C\wedge(e_{a_0}-e_{a_1})
       \wedge(e_{b_0}-e_{b_1}).}
\tag{1.3}
\]

#### Proof

Let \(\pi^+_{ij}\) put \(a_i,b_j\) in slots \(A,B\), and let
\(\pi^-_{ij}\) swap them. Their signs are opposite. Moving the two
placeholder positions to the end of the common positional word has one
sign \(\gamma\), independent of \(i,j\), and gives

\[
\operatorname{sgn}(\pi^+_{ij})e_{C+a_i+b_j}
=\gamma\,e_C\wedge e_{a_i}\wedge e_{b_j}.
\]

The checkerboard contributes \((-1)^{i+j}\), while replacing
\(\pi^+_{ij}\) by \(\pi^-_{ij}\) contributes the factor \(2\) removed
in (1.2). Expansion proves (1.3). \(\square\)

Let \(D_M\le E_M\) be generated by all wedges in (1.3). In the
unrestricted rooted quartet library,

\[
\bar\rho(L_{\rm quartet})=D_M.
\tag{1.4}
\]

This equality is not asserted for a fixed mechanical-shore sublibrary,
which may omit some cores or outside labels.

## 2. Exact Smith quotient

### Theorem 2.1 (no determinant torsion beyond affine parity)

For \(3\le M\le n-2\),

\[
\boxed{E_M/D_M\cong(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^n.}
\tag{2.1}
\]

The normalized physical wedge matrix has Smith factors

\[
1\quad\text{repeated }\binom nM-n\text{ times},
\qquad
2\quad\text{repeated }n\text{ times}.
\tag{2.2}
\]

An element \(z=\sum_Uz_Ue_U\) belongs to \(D_M\) exactly when

\[
\sum_Uz_U\equiv0\pmod2,
\qquad
\sum_{U\ni x}z_U\equiv0\pmod2
\quad(x\in[n]).
\tag{2.3}
\]

There are \(n\) independent equations in (2.3).

#### Proof

Put \(Q=E_M/D_M\). Fix an \((M-3)\)-set \(D\) and five labels
\(1,2,3,4,5\notin D\). In \(Q\), write

\[
x_{ijk}=[e_D\wedge e_i\wedge e_j\wedge e_k]
\]

with alternating subscripts, and abbreviate

\[
\begin{gathered}
A=x_{123},\ B=x_{124},\ C_0=x_{125},\ D'=x_{134},\\
E=x_{135},\ F=x_{145},\ G=x_{234},\ J=x_{345}.
\end{gathered}
\]

Four legitimate quartet-wedge relations are

\[
\begin{aligned}
B-C_0-D'+E&=0,\\
A-C_0+D'+F&=0,\\
A+E-G+J&=0,\\
B+F+G-J&=0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2.4}
\]

Adding the first two and subtracting the last two gives
\(-2C_0=0\). Relabelling the five auxiliary labels shows

\[
2x_{ijk}=0
\]

for every basis \(M\)-wedge; \(M\le n-2\) supplies the two auxiliary
labels. Hence \(2Q=0\).

Modulo two, orientation signs disappear and the generators become the
four-corner rectangle vectors. A function

\[
w:\binom{[n]}M\to\mathbb F_2
\]

annihilates every rectangle if and only if

\[
w(U)=\alpha_0+\sum_{x\in U}\alpha_x.
\tag{2.5}
\]

Indeed, for distinct \(x,y\), the difference
\(w(S+x)+w(S+y)\) is constant as the \((M-1)\)-set \(S\) moves in the
connected Johnson graph on \([n]\setminus\{x,y\}\); the differences
satisfy the three-label cocycle identity. The affine space (2.5) has
dimension \(n\). Therefore \(Q\), already of exponent two, has
\(\mathbb F_2\)-dimension \(n\). This proves (2.1)-(2.3).
\(\square\)

### Remark 2.2 (normalization)

The raw map \(E_{U,\pi}\mapsto\operatorname{sgn}(\pi)e_U\) sends a
quartet to \(2D_M\). On the zero-top-margin lattice (1.1), division by
two is integral. Theorem 2.1 concerns this natural normalized difference
coordinate \(\bar\rho\).

## 3. Reconciliation with top-index saturation

The untwisted top-count rectangle is

\[
r=e_{C+a_0+b_0}-e_{C+a_0+b_1}
  -e_{C+a_1+b_0}+e_{C+a_1+b_1}.
\tag{3.1}
\]

Let \(R_M\) be generated by all such vectors, and let \(B\) record total
mass and point degrees. The arbitrary-field annihilator proof gives

\[
R_M=\ker_{\mathbb Z}B,
\qquad
\mathbb Z^{\binom{[n]}M}/R_M\cong\operatorname{im}B\cong\mathbb Z^n.
\tag{3.2}
\]

Thus the untwisted matrix has \(\binom nM-n\) Smith factors \(1\) and
\(n\) free factors. The normalized physical matrix of Theorem 2.1 has
full rank and \(n\) factors \(2\). Both reduce modulo two to the same
affine character space, but they are not integrally equivalent.

The fixed-core tournament chart makes the distinction concrete.
Top-count saturation sees the kernel of unsigned vertex-edge incidence;
physical score changes see signed incidence. A local four-cycle can be
bipartitely oriented so the vectors agree, but no single bipartition
exists on a complete graph containing triangles. The residual triangle
class is exactly

\[
\mathcal L_{\rm cyc}/\mathcal L_4\cong\mathbb Z/2.
\tag{3.3}
\]

### 3.1 A larger free chronology census

The determinant quotient is not asserted to be the full frame-variable
quotient. For a rooted frame table \(F=(\pi_U)_U\), a ground-label set
\(S\), and a slot set \(T\subseteq\mathbb Z_M\) with
\(|S|=|T|\), define

\[
I_{S,T}(F)
=\bigl|\{U\supseteq S:\pi_U^{-1}(S)=T\}\bigr|.
\tag{3.4}
\]

Every physical quartet preserves every \(I_{S,T}\) integrally. To check
this, intersect \(S\) with the four varying labels
\(a_0,a_1,b_0,b_1\). A touched top contains at most one \(a\)-label and
one \(b\)-label. Core labels do not move; one varying label occupies the
two placeholder slots once on each shore across its two companion tops;
and two varying labels occur together on only one top, where their
unordered slot set is unchanged.

The cases \(|S|=1\) and \(|S|=2\) give the label-slot and pair-slot
censuses. These are free additive chronology margins, not torsion. A
whole-frame replacement changes the level-\(k\) census by
\(\ell_1\)-norm at most \(2\binom Mk\), so a discrepancy \(\Delta_k\)
requires at least

\[
\frac{\Delta_k}{2\binom Mk}
\tag{3.5}
\]

permanent frame replacements. No theorem here proves a large
same-target-fibre discrepancy in (3.4); the cube endpoints below have
equal values because their difference already lies in the quartet
lattice. Thus (3.4) records additional full-table structure but does not
replace the conformal obstruction.

## 4. The squarefree three-placeholder cube

For Sections 4-6 assume \(M\ge7\), as in the promotion regime. Fix an
\((M-3)\)-set \(C\) and disjoint pairs

\[
\{a_0,a_1\},\quad\{b_0,b_1\},\quad\{c_0,c_1\}
\]

outside \(C\). Put

\[
U_{ijk}=C\cup\{a_i,b_j,c_k\},
\qquad i,j,k\in\{0,1\}.
\tag{4.1}
\]

Choose a rooted positional word on \(C\cup\{A,B,D\}\), with the
placeholder positions in three distinct gaps of the cyclic order on
\(C\). Let \(\pi^1_{ijk}\) use the identity assignment and let
\(\pi^\tau_{ijk}\) use the three-cycle \(\tau=(A\ B\ D)\).
For every phase \(s\) and proper interval length \(\ell\), write
\(v^g_{ijk}(s,\ell)\) for the target basis vector.

### Theorem 4.1 (phasewise cube identity)

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{i,j,k}(-1)^{i+j+k}
\bigl(v^1_{ijk}(s,\ell)-v^\tau_{ijk}(s,\ell)\bigr)=0}
\tag{4.2}
\]

for every \(s,\ell\). Thus choosing \(1\) at even cube corners and
\(\tau\) at odd corners, or reversing all eight choices, gives literal
tables with identical top rows and phasewise interval loads at every
length.

#### Proof

Fix an interval and inspect which placeholder positions it contains. If
it contains all three, its image set is independent of the placeholder
permutation. If it contains fewer than three, then for each fixed
permutation its target is independent of at least one of \(i,j,k\);
summing over that bit cancels the alternating sum. \(\square\)

Because (4.2) holds separately for every phase and length, it survives
one common deleted phase, any common retained-phase set, and any common
nested phase-to-depth schedule on the eight corners.

### Theorem 4.2 (lattice-zero but Markov-isolated)

The degree-eight difference in Theorem 4.1 is an integral sum of four
physical quartet vectors, but neither endpoint monomial contains a
quartet divisor.

#### Proof

Write \(\tau=st\) as two transpositions. Telescope from \(1\) to \(s\),
then from \(s\) to \(st\). For each transposition, fix the bit of the
untouched label family. The four remaining corners form a physical
quartet face. There are two fixed-bit values for each transposition,
hence four quartet vectors.

Every four-top rectangle contained in (4.1) is a two-dimensional face:
a physical rectangle has common intersection of size \(M-2\), so one of
\(i,j,k\) is fixed. Its fixed third label belongs to that common core.
The three-cycle moves this label between two distinct gaps of the
\(C\)-order on the two cube shores. The four current frames therefore
do not share one ordered \((M-2)\)-core, which every physical quartet
requires. \(\square\)

The two variants are distinct modulo rotation and reversal. They fix all
\(M-3\ge4\) core labels in the same rooted positions; a nonidentity
rotation fixes none and a reflection fixes at most two.

## 5. Dense global isolation

Partition the \(2m\) labels into \(m\) pairs. For an \(M\)-top, let
\(f,e,s\) be its numbers of full, empty, and singleton ground pairs.
Then

\[
f-e=H,\qquad f+e+s=m.
\tag{5.1}
\]

For each fixed pattern with \(s\ge3\), singleton choices form
\(\{0,1\}^s\). Fix three singleton coordinates and split this cube into
disjoint three-cubes. Tops with \(s<3\) number at most

\[
O(m^2 2^m)=o(N_H),
\tag{5.2}
\]

because \(H=o(m)\) gives \(N_H=4^{m-o(m)}\). Hence there are

\[
\frac{N_H-o(N_H)}8
\tag{5.3}
\]

disjoint gadgets.

Choose each gadget's common core order independently and uniformly, with
its placeholders in distinct core gaps, and give excluded tops
independent uniform frames. A top rectangle not internal to one gadget
involves at least two independent words. Conditional on all but one,
quartet compatibility prescribes the cyclic order of at least \(M-5\)
random core labels. Its probability is at most

\[
\frac{\operatorname{poly}(M)}{(M-6)!}.
\tag{5.4}
\]

There are at most

\[
\binom{2m}{M-2}(2m)^4=\exp(O(m))
\tag{5.5}
\]

top rectangles. The union bound tends to zero. Thus a deterministic
choice exists for which neither endpoint has a cross-gadget quartet;
Theorem 4.2 excludes internal quartets. The global tables are distinct
isolated vertices of the same exact all-phase/all-length,
one-frame-per-top toric fibre.

## 6. Exceptional-frame ledger

The phrase “\(o(W)\) exceptional frames” is ambiguous because

\[
N_H=(1+o(1))\frac WM=o(W).
\]

Allowing \(o(W)\) whole top columns permits discarding every top. A
meaningful literal error ledger counts retained phase occurrences, or
requires \(o(N_H)\) exceptional tops.

For deletion/ignored-row exceptions, hitting every intact cube needs at
least one top per gadget. A repaired top frame has \(M-1\) retained
middle phases, so the hitting cost is

\[
(M-1)\frac{N_H-o(N_H)}8
=\left(\frac18-o(1)\right)W.
\tag{6.1}
\]

Thus an \(o(W)\) permanent exceptional-incidence budget does not hit all
cube witnesses. Equation (6.1) is a catalogue/hitting statement. After
one corner is deleted, the two seven-column residuals need not have equal
loads, so it is not an approximate-fibre theorem.

Endpoint isolation also does not bound recyclable temporary catalysts.
An arbitrary exceptional frame might enable a quartet, be restored, and
then be reused elsewhere. No \(\Omega(W)\) lower bound is proved for
that stronger dynamic exception model.

## 7. Exact boundary

Proved:

1. the complete Smith quotient of the normalized unrestricted rooted
   determinant projection;
2. its exact reconciliation with untwisted top-index saturation and
   fixed-chart circulation parity;
3. a parity-neutral degree-six fixed-chart Markov obstruction;
4. a degree-eight squarefree cube relation in the full quartet lattice
   whose endpoints are nonnegative-isolated;
5. a dense global packing of cube witnesses; and
6. the \((1/8-o(1))W\) permanent deletion-incidence toll.

Not proved:

1. a Smith form for the complete frame-variable quartet lattice outside
   the normalized determinant projection or a complete independence
   classification of the flag censuses (3.4);
2. connectivity after adjoining all higher placeholder-cycle moves;
3. an obstruction to recyclable \(o(W)\)-incidence catalysts;
4. a dense theorem for a smaller fixed mechanical-shore sublibrary; or
5. coefficient one.

The surviving question is conformal: whether a controlled higher-move
or catalyst library can traverse the desired balanced physical fibre
while preserving one literal common permutation and paying only
\(o(W)\) occurrence mass. No further abelian determinant torsion remains
in the unrestricted quartet library.
