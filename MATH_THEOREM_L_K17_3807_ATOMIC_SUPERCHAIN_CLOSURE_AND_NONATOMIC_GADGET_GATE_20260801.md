# The fixed 3,807-block endpoint obstruction is closed under atomic superchains

**Date:** 2026-08-01  
**Lane:** L, endpoint chronology / terminal compiler  
**Status:** exact theorem for the deterministic rightmost-greedy
protected-avoiding minimum cut family emitted by the cited audit on the frozen
`k=17` seven-factor.  It classifies intact
block superchains and the necessary interface of any escaping whole-seam
gadget.  It does not quantify all alternative minimum cut families and does
not claim a terminal compiler.

## 0. Outcome

Delete the canonical 3,807 protected-avoiding factor edges.  The 24,310
rank-nine owners split into 3,807 internally depth-three-resident paths.
Treating those paths as indivisible blocks, in either orientation, gives
56,790 raw directed Johnson endpoint atoms.  The necessary relaxed residence
screen retains 13,174 atoms and has

\[
 \boxed{1289\text{ deleted rank-eight colours of degree zero},\qquad
        368\text{ isolated physical blocks}.}                 \tag{0.1}
\]

The 368 zero-out and zero-in block sets are the same set, by reversal.  A
Hamilton path on the blocks needs 3,806 joins and may omit only one deleted
lower colour.  Thus at least

\[
                              1288                              \tag{0.2}
\]

lower-colour obligations must be supplied non-atomically.

Cutting also removes the last within-block witness of 2,423 rank-ten
targets.  Exactly 1,456 of those targets have no provider in the necessary
relaxed endpoint atlas.  Hence the canonical decomposition fails both its
lower-colour and its immediate-upper singleton cuts before topology,
strict-gammoid or common-cap constraints are imposed.

The key classification is exact:

* concatenating, regrouping or taking the transitive closure of intact
  blocks cannot repair (0.1), even through arbitrarily many all-one blocks;
* a successful whole-seam gadget must change an old block interior, cut or
  interface and export a closed residence-and-shadow signature;
* with this fixed decomposition, some such non-atomic recut/rethread is
  necessary;
* this does **not** prove that more than 3,807 cuts are globally necessary,
  because another protected-avoiding minimum transversal changes the blocks,
  endpoints, ages and deleted-colour rows.

No compiler graph exists yet for these endpoint atoms.  After a successful
new chronology, its maximal antecedent, cap graph and perfect compiler
matching must be newly certified; no fixed-graph matching is automatically
transportable without an explicit transparent/isomorphic transport theorem.

## 1. Fixed blocks and the necessary relaxed atlas

Let `C_0` be the deterministic protected-avoiding minimum circular interval
transversal emitted by the cited rightmost-greedy audit on each of the seven
factor cycles, and let
`B=F-C_0` be its path blocks.  For an oriented block `P` and coordinate `x`,
write

* `all_x(P)` if every owner of `P` contains `x`;
* `pre_x(P)` for its leading positive age; and
* `suf_x(P)` for its trailing positive age.

Ages are capped at four.  A raw atom

\[
                  e=(P^\epsilon,Q^\eta,L)                      \tag{1.1}
\]

joins the last owner of `P^epsilon` to the first owner of `Q^eta` by a
Johnson edge whose rank-eight intersection is a deleted colour `L in C_0`.

Put `e` in `E_rel` unless some coordinate gives one of the following
definite two-block defects, with neither relevant block all-one:

\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
\text{endpoint bits}&\text{necessary condition}\\ \hline
1,1&\operatorname{suf}_x(P)+\operatorname{pre}_x(Q)\ge4,\\
1,0&\operatorname{suf}_x(P)\ge4,\\
0,1&\operatorname{pre}_x(Q)\ge4.
\end{array}                                                    \tag{1.2}
\]

If either block is all-one in `x`, impose no test for `x`.  This deliberately
allows an arbitrary future block to rescue the run.  Therefore `E_rel` is a
necessary relaxation, not a pairwise sufficient residence catalogue.

## 2. Atomic superchains cannot add a forbidden seam

### Theorem 2.1 (superchain closure)

Every seam of every globally depth-three-resident concatenation of the
fixed blocks belongs to `E_rel`.  Consequently no ordering, orientation,
parenthesization, transitive closure or longer intact-block superchain can
use a seam outside `E_rel`.

#### Proof

Suppose neither adjacent block is all-one in a coordinate.  A failed row of
(1.2) is bounded on its left and right by zeros already lying in those two
blocks.  It therefore creates an internal positive run of length at most
three wholly inside their two-block concatenation.  Blocks farther away
cannot extend it.  If either adjacent block is all-one, the atom was retained
without restriction.  Thus every seam of a resident global concatenation is
in `E_rel`.  Grouping several atoms does not change its first constituent
seam.  \(\square\)

This theorem already includes the strongest possible endpoint-superchain
lookahead: the exact capped run automaton may later delete paths in
`E_rel`, but it cannot add a seam rejected by (1.2).

## 3. Reversal makes the 368 blocks genuinely isolated

### Lemma 3.1 (reversal anti-automorphism)

The map

\[
 (P^\epsilon\mathbin{\longrightarrow}Q^\eta,L)
 \longmapsto
 (Q^{-\eta}\mathbin{\longrightarrow}P^{-\epsilon},L)          \tag{3.1}
\]

is an anti-automorphism of `E_rel`.

#### Proof

Reversal preserves the Johnson intersection `L` and all-one flags, while
interchanging prefix and suffix ages.  It swaps the departure and arrival
versions of every row in (1.2).  \(\square\)

Hence a physical block has an outgoing atom in some orientation iff it has
an incoming atom in the reversed orientation.  The audited 368 zero-tail
and 368 zero-head blocks are therefore the same physical set.  None can
occur even as a global endpoint of a nontrivial atomic Hamilton path: a
first block needs an outgoing seam and a last block needs an incoming seam.

### Lemma 5.1 (rank ten is seam-additive)

If an interval of rank-nine owners crosses a block seam and has union `U` of
rank ten, then the two owners adjacent to that seam have union exactly `U`.

#### Proof

Both adjacent owners are rank-nine subsets of the interval union `U`.  They
are distinct Johnson neighbours, so their union has rank ten and is a subset
of `U`; equality follows.  \(\square\)

Therefore a missing rank-ten target can have a crossing witness in an atomic
resident chronology only if some atom of `E_rel` has that target as its
endpoint union.  Among the 2,423 casualties, the exact provider histogram is

```text
0:1456, 2:681, 4:204, 6:63, 8:17, 10:1, 12:1.
```

So 1,456 rank-ten targets require a non-atomic provider.  This number must
not be added to 1,288 or 368: the same whole gadget may repair a dead block,
a lower row and one or more upper rows simultaneously.

Ranks eleven and above are not edge-additive.  For oriented blocks
`P_i,...,P_j`, a crossing interval has union

\[
 \operatorname{suf}(P_i)\ \cup\
 \bigcup_{i<t<j}\operatorname{full}(P_t)\ \cup\
 \operatorname{pre}(P_j).                                    \tag{5.2}
\]

Thus marginal endpoint labels do not decide the 3,116/1,297/249/12 rows.
They require an ordered suffix/full/prefix union automaton through rank
seventeen, or literal replay of the final chronology.

## 6. Exact gadget dichotomy

Call a proposed macro **atomic** if it uses only the fixed blocks, retains
every block interior and connects them only by direct endpoint Johnson
seams.  Theorem 2.1 applies to every atomic macro, regardless of its length.

The frozen factor already uses every rank-nine owner once.  Hence a genuine
whole-seam escape cannot insert a fresh owner occurrence while retaining all
old owner occurrences.  It must do at least one of the following:

1. split a fixed block by deleting a retained interior edge;
2. shift or replace a cut, producing a different block decomposition; or
3. replace a larger interior by a closed rethread/socket gadget.

For the fixed `C_0` decomposition, some operation in this list is necessary.
Every accepted macro must state literally:

* its owner occurrences and two external Johnson ports;
* deleted and restored rank-eight colours;
* its exact capped residence transfer, including all-one carry state;
* the named rank-ten providers and its suffix/full/prefix union decks through
  rank seventeen;
* orientation, component and protected-29 effects; and
* which physical chronology/cap state is passed to the terminal compiler.

Only macros with this closed signature may be used as ground elements of a
new path/graphic/gammoid master.  Raw endpoint arcs plus topology and upper
service do not automatically form a matroid intersection.

The theorem does **not** show that the global optimum cut cardinality exceeds
3,807.  A different protected-avoiding minimum transversal can shift cuts,
change endpoints and eliminate some or all singleton rows while keeping
3,807 cuts.  The next proof-safe outer master is therefore

\[
 \text{cut choice}\ +\ \text{one-waiver lower Hall/nonisolation}\ +
 \text{rank-ten service},                                    \tag{6.1}
\]

followed by the exact residence automaton, topology and ranks eleven through
seventeen.

### 6.1 Quantitative refinement bounds with `C_0` retained

There is a sharper exact statement if all 3,807 old cuts are retained and
the only operation is to add internal splits.  Suppose `s` new cuts touch
`p<=s` original blocks.  Relative to the baseline, all changed endpoint
states lie on at most `2p+2s<=4s` owner masks.  The exact owner-incidence
census gives at most five baseline zero colours below any rank-nine owner.
Since any newly served baseline row must use a changed endpoint and a path
may waive one row,

\[
                    s\ge\left\lceil{1288\over20}\right\rceil
                     =65.                                      \tag{6.2}
\]

This bound permits repeated cuts within the same original block.  In the
narrower face where every original block is split at most once, one split
replaces one block by two and can introduce states on its old first/last
owners and the two owners adjacent to the new cut.  The exact H100 census
over every such split finds that these four owners meet at most nine of the
1,289 baseline zero rows.  Therefore

\[
                    s\ge\left\lceil{1288\over9}\right\rceil
                     =144.                                     \tag{6.3}
\]

Within that same at-most-once-per-original-block face, if splits are
restricted to the 368 isolated blocks, the maximum influence is seven,
giving

\[
                    s\ge\left\lceil{1288\over7}\right\rceil
                     =184.                                     \tag{6.4}
\]

The retained audit prints `185` because its diagnostic field charges all
1,289 rows.  Equation (6.4) is the theorem-level linear-path refinement with
one global colour waiver, namely `ceil(1288/7)=184`.

Even the incompatible superatlas containing every possible one-split state
of every isolated block leaves 568 original zero rows.  Therefore “split
each dead block once” cannot suffice, regardless of how those individual
choices are combined.  These are still fixed-`C_0` refinement bounds; they
say nothing about a shifted size-3,807 transversal or a macro that replaces
several old cuts simultaneously.

## 7. Terminal compiler consequence

This obstruction is strictly pre-compiler.  Any fixed one-pivot compiler
atlas has 65,535 targets and 65,535 cells.  Conditional on a perfect fixed-cap
matching, its cell transversal matroid is free and its dual strict gammoid
has rank zero.  This rules out deletion-only reuse of that fixed graph; no
such graph or matching currently exists for the 3,807 blocks.

More importantly, the 3,807 blocks do not yet define physical source cells:
there is no final owner order, maximal antecedent or cap state.  After a
successful recut or whole-gadget construction, one must reconstruct that
antecedent and solve its literal graph

\[
 \sum_{c\in\operatorname{Adm}_A(S)}z_{S,c}=1,
 \qquad \sum_S z_{S,c}\le1,                                  \tag{7.1}
\]

together with the final common-cap guards.  No fixed-graph matching is
automatically transportable as a strict-gammoid reserve.  An explicitly
proved transparent/isomorphic transport is allowed: global reversal is one
such transport for a completed one-oriented certificate, but it does not
remove the need to certify one final oriented chronology.
