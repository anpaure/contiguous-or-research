# Fixed-slot reciprocal \(C_8\) cubes: provenance, exact moment, and the carrier boundary

Date: 2026-07-26

Method: pure mathematics only.

## 0. Audited verdict

Put

\[
 B=C_s=\operatorname {Cat}_s.
\]

Choose \(u\) pairwise disjoint length-four coordinate intervals in a
Dyck word of length \(2s\).  At interval \(j\), toggle

\[
                  1100\longleftrightarrow1010                 \tag{0.1}
\]

whenever either word occurs there.

The proposed atlas is a genuine common-base exact-factor construction.
For fixed \(u\):

1. every interval supports exactly \(C_{s-2}\) literal reciprocal
   \(C_8\)'s;
2. all interval layers compose exactly, even when their root supports
   overlap;
3. every full ownership component is a Boolean cube of size at most
   \(2^u\); and
4. installing all layers has exact edit moment
   \[
   \boxed{
   \Xi_u={4\over C_s}\sum_{h=1}^u
          {u\choose h}h2^hC_{s-2h}}
   \longrightarrow
   \boxed{{u\over2}\left({9\over8}\right)^{u-1}}.     \tag{0.2}
   \]

Thus every fixed \(4\le u\le7\) passes
\(\Xi_u=o(\sqrt s)\).  Seven intervals fit for \(s\ge14\).

There are two different carrier conclusions.

* In the marked local-boundary quotient, odd-start intervals are
  coherently orientable: every rectangle contributes an
  even-to-following-odd unit, and the raw marked mass is exactly
  \(uC_{s-2}\).
* In the literal physical all-depth carrier on the canonical parent, the
  conclusion is false.  Every reciprocal \(C_8\) has two entrance arms
  and two oppositely signed exit arms, and its complete column is a
  coordinate-pair derivative.  Hence all layers preserve the total load
  on every orbit of the coordinate group generated by their disjoint
  middle pairs.  If the first slot is used, the fatal orbit
  \(\{2,3\}\) starts with total load
  \(C_s+C_{s-1}>2C_s/\theta\); if it is not used, the load \(C_s\) at
  target \(2\) is fixed.  Either case leaves \(\Omega(C_s)\) excess.

Accordingly the construction settles exactness, density, component size,
and sparse-edit variance, but it is rigorously excluded as a repair of the
canonical physical PCap gate.  The next primitive must cross a coordinate
target orbit; another coordinate-conjugate leaf layer cannot do so.

## 1. Literal \(C_8\) provenance at an arbitrary gap

Let \(R\in D_{s-2}\), let \(g\) be any one of its \(2s-3\) gaps, and
write

\[
 R^{(0)}=\operatorname {ins}_g(1100,R),\qquad
 R^{(1)}=\operatorname {ins}_g(1010,R).              \tag{1.1}
\]

Both inserted blocks are nonnegative excursions of net height zero.
Hence both words are in \(D_s\).

### Lemma 1.1 (insertion--erasure order)

In the canonical MSW omitted-label orders of \(R^{(0)}\) and
\(R^{(1)}\), the four inserted coordinates form one consecutive block.
After erasing that block and standardizing the remaining labels, both
orders become the canonical order of \(R\), with the block in the same
gap.

#### Proof

Use the first-return recursion

\[
 \rho(1a0b)=
 \bigl(2j,\ 2j-\rho(\mu a),\ 1,\ 2j+\rho(b)\bigr),  \tag{1.2}
\]

where \(j\) is the semilength of \(1a0\) and \(\mu\) is
reverse-complement.  The two inserted words satisfy

\[
 \mu(1100)=1100,\qquad \mu(1010)=1010,              \tag{1.3}
\]

and

\[
 \rho(1100)=(4,2,3,1),\qquad
 \rho(1010)=(2,1,4,3).                              \tag{1.4}
\]

Induct on the semilength of \(R\).  If the insertion is before the first
primitive component, the concatenation law

\[
 \rho(PQ)=(\rho(P),\,|P|+\rho(Q))                   \tag{1.5}
\]

gives the assertion.  If it lies in the suffix \(b\), apply induction in
the last block of (1.2); the affine shift preserves a consecutive block
and its position.  If it lies in \(a\), reverse-complement reflects the
gap inside \(\mu a\); induction followed by the affine reversal in the
second block of (1.2) again preserves one consecutive block.  Erasing
the four labels changes the enlarged first-return label and every later
label back by four, recovering (1.2) for \(R\).  These cases exhaust all
gaps. \(\square\)

After rotating the common outside order behind the four-letter block and
relabeling its four coordinates as
\(\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\delta\), the two old orders have the form

\[
\begin{aligned}
 C&=(\delta,\beta,\gamma,\alpha,T),\\
 D&=(\beta,\alpha,\delta,\gamma,T),                 \tag{1.6}
\end{aligned}
\]

with one common outside order \(T\).  Define

\[
\begin{aligned}
 C'&=(\delta,\gamma,\beta,\alpha,T),\\
 D'&=(\gamma,\alpha,\delta,\beta,T).                \tag{1.7}
\end{aligned}
\]

The long paths after the four-letter block agree crosswise:
\(V_i(C)=V_i(D')\) and \(V_i(D)=V_i(C')\) from the end of the block
onward.  Inside the block, the two old and two new short paths use the
same six lower states and the same four adjacent-union states.  The
symmetric difference is therefore one alternating \(C_8\), with four
old and four new incidences.  In particular \(C,D\mapsto C',D'\) is a
literal factor-to-factor switch with fixed ports.

This proves that an arbitrary positive-height occurrence is legitimate;
one does not need the prefix before the block to be a complete Dyck word.
The smaller count

\[
 C_aC_{s-a-2}                                       \tag{1.8}
\]

is the count obtained after imposing the extra condition that the block
starts after a complete top-level prefix of semilength \(a\).  It is a
strict sublibrary, not the fixed-coordinate layer considered here.

For one fixed four-coordinate interval, deletion of the block is a
bijection from its \(1100\)-roots to \(D_{s-2}\), and likewise for
\(1010\).  Pairing the two insertions proves:

\[
 \boxed{\text{one fixed interval has exactly }C_{s-2}
        \text{ reciprocal rectangles}.}             \tag{1.9}
\]

## 2. Simultaneous legality and full components

Let \(I_1,\ldots,I_u\) be disjoint intervals and let \(\tau_j\) toggle
(0.1) at \(I_j\).  Because the two local words have the same net height,
\(\tau_i\) changes neither the bits nor the entrance height at \(I_j\).
Thus

\[
 \tau_i\tau_j=\tau_j\tau_i,\qquad
 \mathbf1_{\{j\ {\rm eligible}\}}(\tau_i x)
 =\mathbf1_{\{j\ {\rm eligible}\}}(x).              \tag{2.1}
\]

In the MSW recursion, the flips belonging to one aligned Dyck subword
form a contiguous phase slab, possibly in reverse order.  Disjoint
four-coordinate subwords are incomparable in the recursion tree, so
their slabs have disjoint interiors.  Each reciprocal rectangle fixes
the two slab-boundary states.

For two slab transformations \(A,B\), write \(C^{\epsilon\eta}\) for the
four versions of one common row.  Their complete state-and-colour
incidence columns obey the rowwise cubical identity

\[
 {\bf a}(C^{11})+{\bf a}(C^{00})
   ={\bf a}(C^{10})+{\bf a}(C^{01}),                \tag{2.2}
\]

because the \(B\)-choice is invisible in the \(A\)-slab, the \(A\)-choice
is invisible in the \(B\)-slab, and both fix the gluing states.
Summing (2.2) over every local rectangle shows that either layer remains
ledger-balanced after the other.  Induction proves simultaneous exactness
for all \(u\) layers.  This also explains why overlapping root supports
do not double-own tokens: the common row is replaced by the one row
carrying both slab changes, rather than being counted twice as two old
trade columns.

For a root \(x\), let \(J(x)\subseteq[u]\) be its eligible interval set.
Equation (2.1) makes \(J(x)\) constant on its orbit, and the orbit is

\[
                    \{\tau_Tx:T\subseteq J(x)\}.     \tag{2.3}
\]

Every coordinate edge of this Boolean cube carries a nonempty
cross-owner state or colour from the corresponding reciprocal rectangle.
No owner edge leaves the orbit.  Hence the direct full ownership
component is exactly (2.3), of size \(2^{|J(x)|}\le2^u\).

## 3. Exact support and edit moment

For any prescribed \(h\) intervals, deleting all \(h\) blocks and
recording their \(1100/1010\) choices is a bijection

\[
 \{x:x\text{ is eligible at all prescribed intervals}\}
 \longleftrightarrow D_{s-2h}\times\{0,1\}^h.       \tag{3.1}
\]

Thus the intersection has \(2^hC_{s-2h}\) roots.  In particular, two
layers meet in \(C_{s-4}\) commuting squares.

One rectangle makes one adjacent transposition in each half of either
root order.  For \(r=|J(x)|\), the disjoint slabs give

\[
                         d(x)=2r.                    \tag{3.2}
\]

Put

\[
 Z_u(t)=\sum_{x\in D_s}t^{|J(x)|}.
\]

Since

\[
 t^{|J|}=\sum_{T\subseteq J}(t-1)^{|T|},
\]

(3.1) yields

\[
 Z_u(t)=\sum_{h=0}^u{u\choose h}
           \bigl(2(t-1)\bigr)^hC_{s-2h}.            \tag{3.3}
\]

Weighting (3.2) by the component size \(2^{|J(x)|}\) gives

\[
 C_s\Xi_u
 =\sum_x2^{|J(x)|}\,2|J(x)|
 =4Z_u'(2),
\]

which proves (0.2).  Here \(C_{s-2h}/C_s\to16^{-h}\) for fixed \(h\).
The limits for \(u=4,5,6,7\) are respectively

\[
 {729\over256},\quad {32805\over8192},\quad
 {177147\over32768},\quad {3720087\over524288}.     \tag{3.4}
\]

One may pack \(u\le\lfloor s/2\rfloor\) disjoint intervals.  Moreover

\[
 {C_{s-2}\over C_s}
 ={s(s+1)\over4(2s-1)(2s-3)}
 ={1\over16}\left(1+{3\over s}+O(s^{-2})\right).    \tag{3.5}
\]

Thus the exact raw marked threshold is

\[
 u\ge
 \left\lceil\delta_\theta{C_s\over C_{s-2}}\right\rceil,
 \qquad
 \delta_\theta={1\over2}-{1\over\theta}.             \tag{3.6}
\]

It lies between four and seven for all sufficiently large \(s\) and
\(4\le\theta<16\).  Formula (3.6), rather than
\(\lceil16\delta_\theta\rceil\), is the finite-\(s\) statement.

## 4. Exact carrier column

For one rectangle use (1.6)--(1.7), write the outside order as
\(T=(t_0,\ldots,t_{2s-4})\), and split it into its even- and odd-position
lists \(E,O\).  For a set \(H\) avoiding \(\beta,\gamma\), put

\[
 \partial H=e_{H\cup\{\gamma\}}-e_{H\cup\{\beta\}}. \tag{4.1}
\]

At a shallow rank \(r\), let \(\ell=r-1\).  Directly matching every
unchanged window in the four cyclic orders leaves exactly

\[
 \boxed{
 \Delta_r=
 \partial\suf_\ell(O)+\partial\suf_\ell(E)
 -\partial\pre_\ell(E)-\partial\pre_\ell(O).}       \tag{4.2}
\]

Indeed a changed window contains exactly one of the two exchanged labels.
At the two entrance cuts its old-to-new change is
\(\beta\to\gamma\); at the two exit cuts it is
\(\gamma\to\beta\).  These are the four terms of (4.2).  Reversing the
rectangle multiplies the whole column by \(-1\); it cannot reverse an
entrance arm without also reversing its compensating exit arm.

At first-shadow rank the two \(O\)-terms coincide and cancel.  The
remaining column is the elementary square

\[
 e_{K\cup\{x,\beta\}}-e_{K\cup\{x,\gamma\}}
 -e_{K\cup\{y,\beta\}}+e_{K\cup\{y,\gamma\}},       \tag{4.3}
\]

up to overall orientation.  Its positive and negative masses are both
two.  Formula (4.2), pushed forward by the exterior context, is the
literal physical column; the one-unit direction \(e_3-e_2\) is only one
marked entrance projection of it.

If the fixed intervals start at odd coordinates
\(1,5,9,\ldots,4u-3\), their marked entrance projections all point from

\[
 (2,3),(6,7),\ldots,(4u-2,4u-1),                   \tag{4.4}
\]

with the same even-to-odd orientation.  Hence

\[
                         G_u^{\rm tag}=uC_{s-2}.     \tag{4.5}
\]

This is coherent in the tagged direct sum.  The pairs in (4.4) are
different physical pairs, and (4.2) contains their reverse collateral.
Thus (4.5) is not yet physical cap descent.

For slot \(j\), let \(\{\beta_j,\gamma_j\}\) be its middle coordinate
pair, and let

\[
 \Gamma_{\mathcal I}
   =\langle(\beta_j\ \gamma_j):1\le j\le u\rangle.                \tag{4.6}
\]

The pairs are disjoint because the four-coordinate slots are disjoint.
For odd-start slots, \(\Gamma_{\mathcal I}\) is a subgroup of
\(H_s=\langle(2\ 3),(4\ 5),\ldots\rangle\).  For a
\(\Gamma_{\mathcal I}\)-orbit \(\mathcal O\) of physical targets, put

\[
                         \Pi_{\mathcal O}(v)
                           =\sum_{T\in\mathcal O}v(T).
\]

### Theorem 4.1 (all-rank orbit cancellation)

For every fixed-slot layer \(j\), every protected rank \(r\), and every
\(\Gamma_{\mathcal I}\)-orbit \(\mathcal O\),

\[
                         \Pi_{\mathcal O}(\Delta_{j,r})=0.        \tag{4.7}
\]

The same conclusion holds for the literal simultaneous factor, including
all crossing collars and all mixed-window terms.

#### Proof

The two cells in every elementary dipole \(\partial H\) in (4.2) differ
by the generator
\((\beta_j\ \gamma_j)\in\Gamma_{\mathcal I}\).  They therefore lie in
one orbit and have coefficients \(+1,-1\), proving (4.7) for an isolated
layer.

For the simultaneous statement, install the layers one at a time.  On
one reciprocal packet, the two new rows are the
\((\beta_j\ \gamma_j)\)-coordinate images of the two old rows, with the
root labels reanchored.  A coordinate permutation maps every pointed
window, exterior collar included, to a pointed window in the same
\(\Gamma_{\mathcal I}\)-orbit.  Thus the total number of targets in each
orbit is equal before and after that packet.  Summing packets and
inducting over the layers proves the assertion. \(\square\)

## 5. Exact limits on physical coherence

### Proposition 5.1 (two-boundary visibility)

A fixed consecutive physical intersection or union window depends on at
most two of the \(u\) layer bits.

#### Proof

In one changed slab, let \(L,M,R\) be the old three consecutive states
and \(M'\) the new middle state.  The two octahedral identities give

\[
                         L\cap M\cap R
                         =L\cap M'\cap R,
 \qquad
                         L\cup M\cup R
                         =L\cup M'\cup R.            \tag{5.1}
\]

If the changed state lies strictly inside a window, the window contains
\(L,M,R\), so (5.1) cancels the change on either shadow side.  A slab
outside the window is irrelevant.  Only slabs meeting the two window
boundaries can survive, one at each boundary. \(\square\)

Thus a factor component may have \(u\) Boolean directions while one
physical target sees at most two.  In particular the marked sum (4.5)
cannot be credited \(u\) times to one fixed target occurrence.

### Proposition 5.2 (no background-independent sign)

Let

\[
 \Phi_p(\mu)=\sum_S(\mu(S)-p)_+
\]

be the cap tail at an integer cap \(p\).  No orientation of a reciprocal
\(C_8\) square is nonincreasing for every background \(\mu\).

#### Proof

Let \(v\) be (4.3), and set the loads of its four support cells equal to
\(p\).  Outside those cells choose arbitrary fixed loads.  Both \(v\) and
\(-v\) raise two support cells to \(p+1\) and lower the other two to
\(p-1\).  Hence

\[
             \Phi_p(\mu+v)-\Phi_p(\mu)
 =\Phi_p(\mu-v)-\Phi_p(\mu)=2.                      \tag{5.2}
\]

Thus even one rectangle has no geometry-only favourable orientation.
\(\square\)

The same example gives a positive increase for either orientation of the
floor-corrected square energy.  Consequently disjoint coordinate support,
commutation, and the common tagged direction (4.4) cannot by themselves
prove a common physical sign.

### Theorem 5.3 (fatal parent orbit)

In the standard one-step parent, the canonical distinguished subprofile
contains

\[
                         C_s\text{ occurrences at }2,\qquad
                         C_{s-1}\text{ occurrences at }3.          \tag{5.3}
\]

Every factor obtainable from any pairwise disjoint fixed-slot atlas has
one of the following two invariant obstructions.

1. If the interval beginning at coordinate \(1\) is absent, coordinate
   \(2\) belongs to no active middle pair.  Thus
   \[
                             \mu(2)\ge C_s.           \tag{5.4a}
   \]
2. If that interval is present, its middle pair is \(\{2,3\}\).
   Disjointness forbids every other interval whose middle pair meets
   \(2\) or \(3\).  Hence \(\{2,3\}\) is one complete
   \(\Gamma_{\mathcal I}\)-orbit and
   \[
                             \mu(2)+\mu(3)
                                  \ge C_s+C_{s-1}.    \tag{5.4b}
   \]

Consequently, for \(p=C_s/\theta\), \(4\le\theta<16\),

\[
\boxed{
 K_p\big|_{\Gamma_{\mathcal I}\cdot2}
 \ge
 \begin{cases}
 C_s-p,&[1,4]\notin\mathcal I,\\[2mm]
 C_s+C_{s-1}-2p,&[1,4]\in\mathcal I,
 \end{cases}
 \ge {1\over2}C_s.}                                  \tag{5.5}
\]

#### Proof

The target-\(2\) distinguished occurrences in the canonical one-step
boundary ledger
are indexed by all roots in \(D_s\), giving \(C_s\).  The companion
target-\(3\) occurrences are indexed by the roots obtained after deleting
the forced initial leaf, hence by \(D_{s-1}\), giving \(C_{s-1}\).  This
proves (5.3).  Other physical starts can only increase the two full loads.

The only valid interval whose middle pair contains coordinate \(2\)
starts at coordinate \(1\).  If it is absent, Theorem 4.1 fixes the
singleton orbit of \(2\), and transports the \(C_s\) distinguished
occurrences inside it, proving (5.4a).  If it is present, every
interval beginning at \(2,3,\) or \(4\) overlaps it, while every later
middle pair avoids \(2,3\).  Theorem 4.1 transports all
\(C_s+C_{s-1}\) distinguished occurrences inside this orbit, proving
(5.4b).  Finally,

\[
 (x-p)_++(y-p)_+\ge x+y-2p,
\]

and

\[
 {C_{s-1}\over C_s}={s+1\over2(2s-1)}.
\]

Also \(C_s-p\ge3C_s/4\).  Substitution gives (5.5). \(\square\)

In the second case, equivalently,

\[
 \max\{\mu(2),\mu(3)\}
 \ge {C_s+C_{s-1}\over2}
 =C_s\,{5s-1\over4(2s-1)}
 >p.                                                  \tag{5.6}
\]

Only an interval beginning at the first global root boundary can change
the distinguished rooted first-insertion/deletion occurrence.  Later
intervals can contribute only through their own cyclic starts or through
an explicitly transported parent context.  This is consistent with
Proposition 5.1 and prevents treating (4.5) as repeated repair of one
rooted boundary cell.

## 6. Precise proved boundary and redirect

The fixed-slot construction proves a bounded-component atlas with enough
raw all-start mass and exact sparse-edit accounting:

\[
 |\mathcal E_j|=C_{s-2},\qquad
 b_{\max}\le2^u,\qquad
 \Xi_u\longrightarrow {u\over2}(9/8)^{u-1}.
\]

Its physical orientation is nevertheless impossible for the canonical
PCap consumer.  The obstruction is not an uncontrolled collision error:
Theorem 4.1 survives every collision, collar, mixed window, and legal
component choice, while Theorem 5.3 leaves an \(\Omega(C_s)\) excess on
the actual fatal orbit.

Thus this lane is exhausted with an exact split verdict:

* fixed literal positions do realize the proposed \(4\)--\(7\) exact
  Boolean layers;
* their tagged entrance arms are coherent;
* their complete physical columns cannot clear the parent cap.

The next constructive lemma must use a packet whose row replacement is
not contained in a disjoint-pair coordinate orbit module.  Equivalently it
must transfer positive mass between distinct coordinate-group orbits while
retaining fixed ports, exact \(X/Y\) ownership, and
\(\Xi=o(\sqrt s)\).  A non-coordinate \(D_4\)-ported Haar packet is an
example of the required type of primitive; another shifted
\(1100\leftrightarrow1010\) layer is not.

No coefficient-one conclusion is claimed: only this reciprocal-layer
candidate is closed.
