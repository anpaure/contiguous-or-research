# Chung--Feller path-cover rewiring with fixed Dyck ports

Date: 2026-07-26

Method: pure mathematics.  No enumeration or solver is used.

## 0. Outcome

The exact replacement-seed problem has a particularly rigid matching
normal form.  Put

\[
 J=[2r],\qquad {\cal X}=\binom Jr,\qquad
 {\cal Y}=\binom J{r+1},\qquad {\cal D}=D_{2r}^0,
 \qquad \overline{\cal D}=\{J\setminus P:P\in{\cal D}\}.
\]

A `D`-port-transversal local factor is exactly a pair of perfect
matchings

\[
 M^\uparrow: {\cal X}\setminus\overline{\cal D}
                 \longleftrightarrow {\cal Y},
 \qquad
 M^\downarrow:{\cal Y}
                 \longleftrightarrow {\cal X}\setminus{\cal D},
\tag{0.1}
\]

in the inclusion graph, subject to one nonlinear condition: their union
has no cycle and joins every `P in D` to its own complement `J minus P`.
The global adjacent-union colour ledger is therefore not an extra
constraint after (0.1); it is exactly the assertion that both matchings
saturate the `Y`-class.

For the canonical Chung--Feller factor the phase layers are

\[
 L_t=f^t({\cal D})=D_{2r}^t,\qquad
 C_t=g(L_t)\quad(0\le t<r).                              \tag{0.2}
\]

All have size `Cat_r`, the `L_t` partition `X`, and the `C_t` partition
`Y`.  A fixed-layer alternative is exactly a sequence of permutations of
the Catalan roots satisfying one explicit adjacent-union equation; see
Theorem 2.1 below.

There are two new rigidity results.

1. Every legal **two-row, one-phase transposition** is necessarily the
   spectator extension of the six-state `K_4`/octahedral rectangle

   \[
   (12,14,34),\ (13,23,24)
   \longleftrightarrow
   (12,23,34),\ (13,14,24).                              \tag{0.3}
   \]

   Thus there is no other two-row generator at one phase.  In the two
   matching coordinates, (0.3) is a coordinated pair of alternating
   six-cycles.

2. Neither side of the known completed `m=4` nonlocal Haar circuit is a
   Dyck-port-transversal factor, under **any** coordinate relabelling.
   The obstruction is statewise: every Dyck port family has a pair of
   coordinates separated by every complementary port cut, whereas the
   fourteen port cuts of each Haar factor have no such pair.

The second result closes the most immediate attempt to use the known
nonleaf circuit as the local replacement in the port-substitution theorem.
It does not exclude a different completion or a coordinated collection of
nonleaf circuits.  The full fixed-port reconfiguration fibre is not proved
to be generated by (0.3); longer coordinated alternating cycles remain the
precise open object.

## 1. The two-matching normal form

Let `M(J)` be the bipartite inclusion graph on `X union Y`.  Notice that

\[
 |{\cal X}\setminus\overline{\cal D}|
 =|{\cal X}\setminus{\cal D}|
 =r\operatorname {Cat}_r=|{\cal Y}|.                    \tag{1.1}
\]

### Theorem 1.1 (perfect-matching form of a rooted complement path factor)

There is a bijection between the following objects.

1. Vertex partitions of `M(J)` into paths

   \[
   P=X_0\subset Y_0\supset X_1\subset\cdots\subset
   Y_{r-1}\supset X_r=J\setminus P,qquad P\in{\cal D};  \tag{1.2}
   \]

2. pairs `(M^up,M^down)` of perfect matchings in (0.1) such that
   `M^up union M^down` has no cycle and its endpoint bijection is

   \[
                         P\longmapsto J\setminus P.       \tag{1.3}
   \]

#### Proof

From (1.2), put every edge `X_t--Y_t` into `M^up` and every edge
`Y_t--X_(t+1)` into `M^down`.  Every `Y`-vertex occurs once and has one
edge of each kind.  A nonterminal `X`-vertex occurs once in `M^up`, while
a noninitial `X`-vertex occurs once in `M^down`.  The omitted domains are
therefore exactly `bar(D)` and `D`, respectively, proving that both are
perfect matchings as in (0.1).

Conversely, in the union of two matchings every `Y`-vertex and every
`X`-vertex outside `D union bar(D)` has degree two.  Vertices of `D` and
`bar(D)` have degree one.  Hence every component is an alternating cycle
or an alternating path with endpoints in `D union bar(D)`.  The stated
component and endpoint conditions turn these components into exactly the
paths (1.2).  The constructions are inverse. \(\square\)

Theorem 1.1 separates the linear and nonlinear parts of the problem.
Exact ownership of all `X`-states and all `Y`-colours is just two perfect
matchings.  Exclusion of alternating cycles and the specified pairing of
the `2 Cat_r` unmatched vertices is the nonlinear port condition.

### Corollary 1.2 (alternating-cycle coordinates)

Fix one rooted path factor `(M_0^up,M_0^down)`.  For every other pair of
perfect matchings, each of

\[
 M^\uparrow\mathbin\triangle M_0^\uparrow,
 \qquad
 M^\downarrow\mathbin\triangle M_0^\downarrow          \tag{1.4}
\]

is a disjoint union of even alternating cycles.  Conversely, toggling any
collection of alternating cycles in the two matchings preserves both
ownership ledgers.  It gives a valid rooted factor if and only if the
resulting union has no cycle and still induces (1.3).

Thus ordinary matching reconfiguration is completely linear, but the two
collections of cycles must be coordinated through the endpoint monodromy.

## 2. Chung--Feller phase layers

Let `g,h,g',h'` be the MSW maps.  Recall that

\[
 g^{-1}=h',\qquad h^{-1}=g',\qquad f=h\circ g.           \tag{2.1}
\]

The canonical matchings are

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 M_{CF}^\uparrow
   &=\{\,X--g(X):X\in{\cal X}\setminus\overline{\cal D}\,\},\\
 M_{CF}^\downarrow
   &=\{\,Y--h(Y):Y\in{\cal Y}\,\}.                    \tag{2.2}
 \end{aligned}
\]

Their paths have `X_t=f^t(P)` and `Y_t=g(f^t(P))`.  Consequently (0.2)
holds, by the minimum-change Chung--Feller theorem.

### Theorem 2.1 (fixed-layer permutation form)

Put `i_t=f^t:D -> L_t`.  The rooted complement path factors whose
phase-`t` state set is exactly `L_t` are in bijection with permutations

\[
 s_0,s_1,\ldots,s_r\in\operatorname {Sym}({\cal D}),
 \qquad s_0=s_r=\mathrm{id},                             \tag{2.3}
\]

such that, for every `P in D` and `0<=t<r`,

\[
 |i_t(s_tP)\mathbin\triangle i_{t+1}(s_{t+1}P)|=2,      \tag{2.4}
\]

and

\[
 \boxed{
 \biguplus_{t=0}^{r-1}\biguplus_{P\in{\cal D}}
 \{,i_t(s_tP)\cup i_{t+1}(s_{t+1}P),}
       ={\cal Y}.}                                      \tag{2.5}
\]

#### Proof

Label every rooted path by its prescribed initial port `P`.  At phase
`t`, its state belongs to `L_t`.  Since all states in `L_t` occur once,
there is a unique permutation `s_t` satisfying
`X_t(P)=i_t(s_tP)`.  Fixed initial and complementary terminal ports are
exactly `s_0=s_r=id`.  Johnson adjacency is (2.4), and exact use of the
`Y`-class is (2.5).

Conversely (2.3)--(2.5) give `Cat_r` legal alternating paths with the
correct endpoints.  The phase permutations partition the `X`-class and
(2.5) partitions the `Y`-class. \(\square\)

The full port fibre of Theorem 1.1 is larger: an alternative factor need
not use the canonical flaw class `L_t` at phase `t`.  Thus a no-go proved
only in the permutation model (2.3) must not be promoted to a no-go for
all port-transversal factors.

## 3. Classification of a two-row one-phase switch

Consider two paths whose three consecutive `X`-states are

\[
                         A,B,C,qquad D,E,F,              \tag{3.1}
\]

with `A,D` in one phase, `B,E` in the next, and `C,F` in the following
phase.  Suppose we exchange `B` and `E`, fixing every other state.  Thus
the proposed paths are

\[
                         A,E,C,qquad D,B,F.              \tag{3.2}
\]

Assume both old and new consecutive pairs are Johnson edges and that the
four adjacent-union colours are preserved as a multiset.

### Theorem 3.1 (octahedral rigidity)

Every nonidentity switch (3.1)--(3.2) has the following form, up to a
coordinate relabelling and adjoining a common `(r-2)`-set `R`:

\[
 \begin{array}{c|ccc}
 -&R12&R14&R34\\
 -&R13&R23&R24
 \end{array}
 \quad\longleftrightarrow\quad
 \begin{array}{c|ccc}
 +&R12&R23&R34\\
 +&R13&R14&R24.
 \end{array}                                             \tag{3.3}
\]

In particular the two exchanged middle states have Johnson distance two.
There is no distance-one switch.

#### Proof

The states `A` and `C` are common Johnson neighbours of `B` and `E`, so
`d_J(B,E)<=2`.  The same is true using `D,F`.  The four outer states are
distinct: two distinct rows cannot share a state in one phase, and states
from different flaw phases are disjoint.

First suppose `d_J(B,E)=1`.  Write

\[
 B=H\cup\{b\},\qquad E=H\cup\{e\},\qquad |H|=r-1.
\]

Every common neighbour of `B,E`, distinct from them, has one of the two
forms

\[
 H\cup\{z\}\quad(z\notin H\cup\{b,e\}),
 \qquad
 (H\setminus\{h\})\cup\{b,e\}\quad(h\in H).            \tag{3.4}
\]

For an outer state `Q`, put

\[
 \delta_Q={\bf e}_{Q\cup B}-{\bf e}_{Q\cup E}.           \tag{3.5}
\]

For the first type in (3.4), this is

\[
 {f e}_{H\cup\{b,z\}}-{f e}_{H\cup\{e,z\}},         \tag{3.6}
\]

and distinct outer states give distinct `z`.  For the second type,
`delta_Q=0`, and every such state gives the same old colour
`H union {b,e}`.  Exactness of the old colour ledger therefore permits at
most one outer state of the second type.

Colour preservation is exactly

\[
                         \delta_A+\delta_C
                         -\delta_D-\delta_F=0.            \tag{3.7}
\]

At least three terms in (3.7) are consequently nonzero, and their basis
colours in (3.6) have distinct `z`.  No one of them can cancel.  This
contradiction excludes distance one.

Now let `d_J(B,E)=2`.  Write

\[
 B=R\cup\{b_1,b_2\},\qquad
 E=R\cup\{e_1,e_2\}.                                    \tag{3.8}
\]

The four common neighbours are exactly

\[
                         Q_{ij}=R\cup\{b_i,e_j\}
                         \quad(i,j\in\{1,2\}).           \tag{3.9}
\]

The four distinct outer states therefore use all four cells.  Put

\[
 u_j=R\cup\{b_1,b_2,e_j\},\qquad
 v_i=R\cup\{e_1,e_2,b_i\}.
\]

Then `delta_(Q_ij)=e_(u_j)-e_(v_i)`.  Give `Q_ij` sign `+1` when it is
one of `A,C` and sign `-1` when it is one of `D,F`.  Equation (3.7) says
that every row sum and every column sum of this `2 by 2` sign matrix is
zero.  Its signs are therefore checkerboard.  Relabelling the four active
coordinates gives exactly (3.3). \(\square\)

### Corollary 3.2 (the two matching cycles)

The switch (3.3) is a coordinated pair of alternating six-cycles in the
two matchings of Theorem 1.1.  Suppressing `R`, they are

\[
 12-124-14-134-13-123-12,                                \tag{3.10}
\]

and

\[
 14-124-24-234-34-134-14.                                \tag{3.11}
\]

Toggling both cycles preserves the complement endpoint pairing.  Toggling
only one generally does not.

Theorem 3.1 classifies transpositions at one phase, not longer phase
permutations and not changes of the phase layers themselves.  A proof that
the whole fixed-layer fibre is generated by the certified leaf rectangles
would additionally need to show that every directed cycle in the
alternative-centre graph decomposes into the reciprocal pairs (3.3).

### 3.3 The exact zero-label alternating-cycle gate

The last sentence has a finite exact formulation.  For a fixed phase
`1<=t<r`, write

\[
 A_P=i_{t-1}(P),\qquad B_P=i_t(P),\qquad C_P=i_{t+1}(P).
\]

Form the bipartite graph `G_t` with left class a copy of `D` (the rows)
and right class `L_t`, putting

\[
 P\sim Q
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 |A_P\mathbin\triangle Q|=|Q\mathbin\triangle C_P|=2.   \tag{3.12}
\]

Label this edge by the two-colour vector

\[
 \chi_t(P,Q)
   ={f e}_{A_P\cup Q}+{f e}_{Q\cup C_P}
      \in\mathbb Z^{\cal Y}.                             \tag{3.13}
\]

The identity edges `P--B_P` form one perfect matching of `G_t`.

### Proposition 3.3 (one-phase reconfiguration as a labelled matching)

A permutation `sigma in Sym(D)`, inserted only at phase `t`, gives a legal
fixed-port path factor if and only if

1. `P--B_(sigma P)` is an edge of `G_t` for every `P`; and
2.

   \[
   \boxed{
   \sum_{P\in{\cal D}}\chi_t(P,B_{\sigma P})
    =\sum_{P\in{\cal D}}\chi_t(P,B_P).}                 \tag{3.14}
   \]

Consequently every one-phase alternative is an alternating-cycle
decomposition against the identity matching whose total signed
`chi_t`-label is zero.  A nonleaf one-phase generator exists precisely
when one of these zero-label alternating cycles has more than two row
vertices and is not a conformal sum of the two-row cycles of Theorem 3.1.

#### Proof

Condition 1 is exactly the pair of Johnson adjacencies through the new
phase-`t` state.  A permutation uses every member of `L_t` once, so the
`X`-ledger remains exact.  All unchanged transitions cancel from the
global `Y`-ledger, and the two transitions incident with phase `t` give
exactly (3.14).  Finally, the symmetric difference of two perfect
matchings is a union of alternating cycles; additivity of (3.13) gives the
last assertion. \(\square\)

Proposition 3.3 is the promised decidable leaf-generation gate.  It is
strictly smaller than searching over local wreath factors: it asks for
zero-labelled alternating cycles in one explicit Catalan-by-Catalan
bipartite graph.  Theorem 3.1 settles all alternating cycles with two row
vertices.  Cycles of length at least three remain unclassified.

## 4. A universal-separator obstruction for Dyck ports

Every Dyck word starts with an up-step and ends with a down-step.  This
gives a relabelling-invariant obstruction on the complementary endpoint
pairs of a proposed local factor.

### Lemma 4.1 (universal separator)

If a complement-path factor is `D`-port-transversal after some coordinate
relabelling, then there are two coordinates `a,b in J` such that **every**
complementary port pair separates `a` from `b`.

#### Proof

In the relabelled coordinate order, every member of `D` contains the first
coordinate `a` and omits the last coordinate `b`.  Its complement does the
opposite.  Hence the two sides of every port pair separate `a,b`. \(\square\)

The condition is only necessary, not sufficient.  It is nevertheless
enough to audit the known nonlocal Haar completion.

## 5. The completed `m=4` Haar factors fail the port test

Use the omitted-label orders from `NONLOCAL_HAAR_M4.md` and take coordinate
`9` as `infinity`.  If

\[
 q=(q_0,\ldots,q_8),
\]

the corresponding interval order is

\[
 (q_0,q_2,q_4,q_6,q_8,q_1,q_3,q_5,q_7).                 \tag{5.1}
\]

The port pair consists of the four entries immediately after `9` in
(5.1) and their complement in `[8]`.

For the four negative trade rows the port cuts are

\[
 1346|2578,\quad1246|3578,\quad1236|4578,\quad1235|4678. \tag{5.2}
\]

For the four positive rows they are

\[
 1578|2346,\quad1246|3578,\quad1236|4578,\quad1235|4678. \tag{5.3}
\]

The first three common completion rows have cuts

\[
 1368|2457,\qquad1345|2678,qquad2356|1478.              \tag{5.4}
\]

### Theorem 5.1 (the known Haar completion is not a Dyck-port factor)

Neither of the two fourteen-wreath exact factors in
`NONLOCAL_HAAR_M4.md` is `D_4`-port-transversal under any permutation of
the eight non-infinity coordinates.

#### Proof

For the negative side, the four cuts in (5.2) imply that a coordinate pair
separated by all four must be one of

\[
                         \{1,7\},\quad\{1,8\},\quad\{5,6\}.
\tag{5.5}
\]

The first cut in (5.4) puts `1,8` on the same side.  The third puts both
`1,7` and `5,6` on the same side.  Hence no pair is separated by all port
cuts of the negative factor.

For the positive side, (5.3) leaves only

\[
                         \{2,7\},\quad\{2,8\},\quad\{5,6\}.
\tag{5.6}
\]

The first cut in (5.4) puts `2,7` on the same side, the second puts `2,8`
on the same side, and the third puts `5,6` on the same side.  Again there
is no universal separator.  Lemma 4.1 proves the claim. \(\square\)

This is a no-go for the displayed **completed factors**, not for the
four-row ledger identity in isolation.  A different common completion can
change the other ten port cuts.  It also does not rule out combining
several associator packets so that their port defects close.

## 6. Exact remaining reconfiguration problem

The current evidence can be summarized without ambiguity.

* In the fixed Chung--Feller phase layers, every two-row one-phase move is
  the octahedral leaf rectangle of Theorem 3.1.
* Longer coordinated alternating cycles in the two matching factors of
  Theorem 1.1 are not classified.
* The known four-row nonleaf Haar circuit preserves the two ownership
  ledgers, but its known exact completions fail the necessary port
  separator invariant.
* A complete replacement seed may leave the canonical flaw layers, so
  even a full generation theorem inside (2.3) would not classify the
  entire port fibre.

Accordingly the sharp finite target is:

> Construct a coordinated pair of nonleaf alternating-cycle systems in
> the two incidence matchings (0.1) whose union has no cycle, has identity
> complement endpoint monodromy, and changes the rooted depth-window
> profile; or prove that no such system exists for the required growing
> local scales.

The matching ledgers themselves are automatically integral.  The only
remaining exactness condition in this local problem is endpoint monodromy,
which is precisely why an unrooted four-for-four factor trade need not be
a valid recursive context substitute.
