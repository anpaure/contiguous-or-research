# Singleton absorption rounds flatten, and the deep ideal is too large

Date: 2026-07-28

Status: unconditional round-flattening and capacity obstruction, plus an
exact terminal weighted certificate.  The full trace-two residual extension
and the required non-singleton bulk skeleton remain open.

## 0. Verdict

The private-reserve singleton theorem is a valid terminal absorber, but it
cannot be iterated to cover the full residual deep ideal in a fixed Pascal
flag package.

There are two independent reasons.

1. Properly composed singleton rounds flatten to one final injection into
   the physical singleton positions.  They have no more capacity than one
   global terminal matching.  Separately certified rounds need not compose,
   even when they use disjoint positions, because a later absorption can
   remove the last private witness reserved by an earlier round.
2. A length-`W+d` package has only `W+d` singleton intervals.  For odd
   `k=2r-1`, the residual ideal strictly below rank `r-d` has size

   \[
    D_k=\sum_{s=1}^{r-d-1}\binom{k}{s}
      =2^k\left(\Phi(-\sqrt{\pi/2})+O(k^{-1/2})\right),
   \]

   whereas

   \[
    W+d=2^k\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi k}}(1+o(1)).
   \]

   Thus `D_k/(W+d)` grows as a positive constant times `sqrt(k)`.  Only an
   `O(k^(-1/2))` fraction of the deep ideal can ever be assigned to
   singleton cells.

The constructive consequence is sharp.  A future skeleton theorem must
generate all but `O(W)` deep targets on intervals of length at least two,
while preserving a common reserve.  The singleton theorem can then absorb
the terminal `O(W)` family, including every hot position.  Successive
singleton rounds alone cannot be the bulk construction.

## 1. Exact flattening of singleton rounds

Fix a Pascal source position set `P`.  Let `U subseteq P` be the singleton
positions not already assigned to fixed targets.  At position `p`, let

\[
 C_p=E_p\cap\bigcap_{F\in\mathcal F_p}F
 \tag{1.1}
\]

be the meet of all fixed owners; assume `C_p` is nonempty.  Let
`P_0` be the fixed protected pairs `(L,J_L)`, and assume

\[
 C_p\subseteq L\qquad(p\in J_L).
 \tag{1.2}
\]

Call `p` **hot** when the fixed owners generating `C_p` have meet dimension
greater than two, and write `H` for the hot set.

A monotone singleton-absorption sequence consists of disjoint residual
families `R_1,...,R_t` and injections

\[
 \phi_j:R_j\longrightarrow U
 \]

with pairwise disjoint images, such that `S subseteq C_(phi_j(S))`.  Once a
singleton target is installed, its cell remains a required occurrence and
its position is not subsequently cut by another residual owner.

### Theorem 1.1 (terminal singleton-round flattening)

Let `R` be the disjoint union of the `R_j`, and let

\[
 \phi=\bigsqcup_j\phi_j:R\longrightarrow U.
 \tag{1.3}
\]

The final source word is independent of the round order and equals

\[
 A_p=
 \begin{cases}
 S,&p=\phi(S),\\
 C_p,&p\notin\operatorname {im}\phi.
 \end{cases}
 \tag{1.4}
\]

The complete sequence is a valid trace-two singleton extension if and only
if all three conditions below hold:

1. `S subseteq C_(phi(S))` for every `S in R`;
2. `H subseteq im(phi)`; and
3. for every protected pair `(L,J_L)`,

   \[
    L=\bigcup_{\substack{p\in J_L\\p=\phi(S)}}S
       \ \cup\!
       \bigcup_{p\in J_L\setminus\operatorname {im}\phi}C_p.
    \tag{1.5}
   \]

Consequently a successful sequence exists if and only if one terminal
global injection satisfying Conditions 1--3 exists.  Successive rounds give
no additional feasibility.

#### Proof

At a used singleton position `p=phi(S)`, every fixed owner contains `C_p`
and `S subseteq C_p`.  Intersecting the fixed owners with the singleton owner
`S` therefore gives exactly `S`.  This meet is generated by one residual
owner.  At an unused position no residual owner is active, so the final meet
is `C_p`.  Residual owners at different singleton positions have disjoint
supports, proving (1.4) and order independence.

All used positions have meet dimension one.  An unused position has its
original fixed-owner dimension, so the final word is trace two exactly when
every hot position is used.  Assumption (1.2) and `S subseteq C_p` put every
entry on `J_L` inside `L`; its union is exactly the right-hand side of (1.5).
Thus (1.5) is necessary and sufficient for the protected equality.  All
entries are nonempty.  These observations prove both directions and the
flattening conclusion.  \(\square\)

### Corollary 1.2 (final reserve and private-provider form)

For an injection `phi` satisfying core containment, put `M_p=A_p` using
(1.4).  Then `phi` satisfies all protected equalities if and only if `M` is
one private reserve:

\[
 \varnothing\ne M_p\subseteq C_p,
 \qquad
 \bigcup_{p\in J_L}M_p=L.
 \tag{1.6}
\]

Equivalently, for `(L,J_L)` and `x in L`, define the fixed provider set

\[
 P_{L,x}=\{p\in J_L:x\in C_p\}.
\]

Then the exact private-hit condition is

\[
 P_{L,x}\not\subseteq
 \{\phi(S):x\notin S\}.
 \tag{1.7}
\]

Thus adaptively changing reserves cannot enlarge the final feasible class:
every successful sequence has the single final reserve (1.6).

#### Proof

The first assertion is just (1.5) with `M_p=A_p`.  A provider position
retains `x` in its final entry unless it is occupied by a singleton target
omitting `x`.  Hence every provider loses `x` exactly when the containment
in (1.7) holds.  Negating that failure gives (1.7).  \(\square\)

### Corollary 1.3 (exact singleton capacity)

Every valid monotone singleton extension satisfies

\[
 |H|\le |R|\le |U|\le |P|.
 \tag{1.8}
\]

If the source word has the optimal Pascal length `|P|=W+d`, no sequence of
singleton rounds can install more than `W+d` distinct residual targets.

#### Proof

The first inequality is Condition 2 of Theorem 1.1, the second is
injectivity of (1.3), and the third is `U subseteq P`.  \(\square\)

The monotonicity hypothesis is not cosmetic.  Reusing a position changes
the unique value of its singleton union and destroys the earlier target's
occurrence.  Moving that earlier occurrence to a longer interval is already
a non-singleton bulk operation and lies outside the singleton theorem.

### Proposition 1.4 (independent reserve certificates do not compose)

Two disjoint singleton rounds can each admit a private reserve against the
original cores while their union admits none.

#### Proof

Take two positions `p_1,p_2`, put

\[
 C_{p_1}=C_{p_2}=L=\{a,b,c\},
 \]

and protect `L` on `J_L={p_1,p_2}`.  In the first round install
`S_1={a}` at `p_1`.  The other position remains `C_(p_2)=L`, so the
protected union is still `L`; equivalently one may reserve `{a}` at `p_1`
and `{b,c}` at `p_2`.  Symmetrically, the second round by itself may install
`S_2={c}` at `p_2`, with the unused first core reserving the missing
coordinates.

Install both targets.  Formula (1.4) gives the final entries `{a}` and
`{c}`, whose union omits `b`.  Thus the two original-core certificates do
not compose.  If the second round is correctly recertified after the first,
it fails because `p_2` is then the sole remaining private location for `b`.
\(\square\)

Therefore roundwise applications of the weighted normalized-matching
theorem are sound only when they share one final reserve, or when each round
is recertified against the fully evolved state.  In the latter case Theorem
1.1 still flattens the completed sequence.

## 2. The strongest terminal weighted certificate

Fix one private reserve `M_p subseteq C_p` satisfying

\[
 M_p\ne\varnothing,
 \qquad
 \bigcup_{p\in J_L}M_p=L
 \quad((L,J_L)\in P_0).
 \tag{2.1}
\]

Join a residual target `S` to `p in U` when

\[
 M_p\subseteq S\subseteq C_p.
 \tag{2.2}
\]

Assume `H subseteq U`.  If a hot position lies outside the available
singleton set, no terminal singleton extension can absorb it.

### Theorem 2.1 (exact hot-saturating fractional certificate)

There is a matching of the compatibility graph which saturates every target
in `R` and every hot position in `H` if and only if there are nonnegative
edge weights `w_(S,p)` satisfying

\[
 \sum_p w_{S,p}=1\qquad(S\in R),
 \tag{2.3}
\]

\[
 \sum_Sw_{S,p}\le1\qquad(p\in U),
 \tag{2.4}
\]

and

\[
 \sum_Sw_{S,p}=1\qquad(p\in H).
 \tag{2.5}
\]

Any such matching installs the terminal singleton family and preserves all
protected equalities and trace two.

#### Proof

The incidence vector of the desired matching satisfies (2.3)--(2.5),
proving necessity.  Conversely these equations define a nonempty face of
the bipartite matching polytope: target degree constraints and the hot
position constraints are tight valid degree inequalities, while every
other position has capacity one.  The bipartite matching polytope is
integral, so this face has an integral vertex.  That vertex is the required
matching.  The private-reserve extension theorem then gives every protected
equality; saturation of `H` absorbs every fixed meet of dimension greater
than two.  \(\square\)

This yields the correct cumulative normalized-matching certificate.  For
rank `t`, put

\[
 n_{p,t}=\#\{S\in R:|S|=t,\ M_p\subseteq S\subseteq C_p\}.
\]

If weights `lambda_(p,t)>=0`, with `lambda_(p,t)=0` whenever `n_(p,t)=0`,
satisfy

\[
 \sum_t\lambda_{p,t}\le1,
 \quad\text{with equality for }p\in H,
 \tag{2.6}
\]

and, for every rank-`t` target `S`,

\[
 \sum_{p:M_p\subseteq S\subseteq C_p}
       \frac{\lambda_{p,t}}{n_{p,t}}=1,
 \tag{2.7}
\]

then `w_(S,p)=lambda_(p,t)/n_(p,t)` satisfies Theorem 2.1.  For several
proposed rounds, define `n_(p,t)` from the one final residual family and use
one cumulative `lambda`; then its cumulative position loads must obey (2.6)
and its final target loads must obey (2.7).  If different rounds use
different denominators, one must instead add their actual edge weights
`w^(j)` and check (2.3)--(2.5) directly.  Separate per-round capacity bounds
or target loads merely at least one do not certify composition: the trimming
step can destroy hot saturation or a protected private reserve.

Theorem 2.1 is constructive at the terminal scale, but it does not create
new singleton positions and therefore cannot evade Corollary 1.3.

## 3. The deep-ideal capacity obstruction

Restrict now to odd dimension

\[
 k=2r-1,
 \qquad W=\binom{k}{r}.
\]

The lower-target count and optimal depth are

\[
 \Lambda=\sum_{s=1}^{r-1}\binom{k}{s}=2^{k-1}-1,
 \tag{3.1}
\]

\[
 d=\min\left\{j:\Lambda\le jW+\binom{j+1}{2}\right\}.
 \tag{3.2}
\]

After the natural flag rows through rank `r-d` and their spills are fixed,
the full deep residual ideal has size

\[
 D_k=\sum_{s=1}^{r-d-1}\binom{k}{s}.
 \tag{3.3}
\]

We use two classical uniform estimates.  Stirling's formula gives

\[
 W=2^k\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi k}}
      \left(1+O(k^{-1})\right).
 \tag{3.4}
\]

For `X` binomial with parameters `(k,1/2)`, the de Moivre--Laplace theorem
with the Berry--Esseen error gives, uniformly in real `x`,

\[
 \Pr\left(\frac{2X-k}{\sqrt k}\le x\right)
 =\Phi(x)+O(k^{-1/2}).
 \tag{3.5}
\]

Equation (3.5) is an imported classical probability estimate; it is not
reproved in this note.  Only the sharp constant below uses it.  The
divergence and impossibility conclusion also follow from the elementary
ratio proof after Theorem 3.1.

### Theorem 3.1 (deep ideal has positive density)

Put

\[
 p_*=\Phi(-\sqrt{\pi/2})>0.
\]

Then

\[
 d=\sqrt{\frac{\pi k}{8}}+O(1),
 \tag{3.6}
\]

\[
 D_k=2^k\left(p_*+O(k^{-1/2})\right),
 \tag{3.7}
\]

and

\[
 \boxed{
 \frac{D_k}{W+d}
 =\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2}}\,p_*\sqrt k+O(1)
 \longrightarrow\infty.}
 \tag{3.8}
\]

#### Proof

Put `a=Lambda/W`.  From (3.1) and (3.4),

\[
 a=\sqrt{\frac{\pi k}{8}}\left(1+O(k^{-1})\right).
 \tag{3.9}
\]

The choice `ceil(a)` is feasible in (3.2), so `d<=ceil(a)=O(sqrt(k))`.
Conversely, feasibility of `d` gives

\[
 a\le d+\frac{d(d+1)}{2W}=d+o(1),
\]

because `d=O(sqrt(k))` and `W` is exponential.  Hence `d=a+O(1)`, proving
(3.6).

Since `r=(k+1)/2`, the largest deep rank is

\[
 r-d-1=\frac{k-2d-1}{2}.
\]

Therefore, for `X` as in (3.5),

\[
 \frac{D_k}{2^k}
 =\Pr\left(
     \frac{2X-k}{\sqrt k}
       \le-\frac{2d+1}{\sqrt k}
   \right)-2^{-k}.
 \tag{3.10}
\]

By (3.6), the threshold in (3.10) is
`-sqrt(pi/2)+O(k^(-1/2))`.  Equation (3.5) and the bounded density of the
normal distribution give (3.7).  Finally `d/W=o(1)`, so divide (3.7) by
(3.4) to obtain (3.8).  \(\square\)

For completeness, the divergence in (3.8) has a direct coefficient proof.
Stirling's lower bound gives `W>=2^(k-1)/sqrt(k)` for all sufficiently large
`k`, whence `d<=sqrt(k)+1`.  For

\[
 0\le j\le2\sqrt k+2,
\]

the exact ratio is

\[
 \frac{\binom{k}{r-1-j}}{W}
 =\prod_{h=1}^{j}\frac{r-h}{r+h}.
 \tag{3.11}
\]

For large `k`, every `h/r<=1/2`, and

\[
 \log\frac{1-u}{1+u}\ge-3u\qquad(0\le u\le1/2).
\]

Thus (3.11) is at least `e^(-15)` throughout the
`floor(sqrt(k))` indices

\[
 j=d,d+1,\ldots,d+\lfloor\sqrt k\rfloor-1.
\]

All corresponding ranks lie in (3.3), so

\[
 D_k\ge e^{-15}\lfloor\sqrt k\rfloor W.
 \tag{3.12}
\]

Since `d=o(W)`, (3.12) proves `D_k/(W+d)->infinity` without (3.5).

### Corollary 3.2 (full singleton absorption is impossible)

For every sufficiently large odd `k`, no optimal Pascal flag package can
assign the full residual deep ideal to singleton cells, in one round or in
any number of monotone rounds.  More precisely, at least

\[
 D_k-(W+d)
 \tag{3.13}
\]

deep targets must be realized on intervals of length at least two, and the
singleton-assignable fraction is at most

\[
 \frac{W+d}{D_k}
 =\frac{\sqrt{2/\pi}}{p_*}\,k^{-1/2}+o(k^{-1/2}).
 \tag{3.14}
\]

#### Proof

A word of length `W+d` has exactly `W+d` physical singleton intervals.
Distinct targets require distinct cells.  Apply Corollary 1.3 and Theorem
3.1.  Every remaining target uses a non-singleton interval.  \(\square\)

This obstruction is asymptotic rather than a `k=15` no-go.  At `k=15`, one
has `r=8`, `d=3`, and

\[
 D_{15}=\binom{15}{1}+\binom{15}{2}
       +\binom{15}{3}+\binom{15}{4}=1940<6438=W+d.
\]

Thus a terminal singleton attack remains cardinality-feasible at that one
dimension, although the common-reserve and hot-position conditions still
have to be proved.

## 4. The exact replacement lemma

The private-reserve normalized-matching theorem remains useful, but only as
a terminal absorber.  A general proof now needs the following genuinely
different bulk statement.

### Open Lemma 4.1 (non-singleton bulk skeleton)

Given the natural flag pins and labelled spills of a Pascal flag package,
construct one trace-two controller word which

1. realizes a bulk subfamily of the deep ideal on pairwise distinct eligible
   intervals of length at least two, leaving a terminal family `R_term` and
   unused singleton set `U` with

   \[
    |R_{\rm term}|\le |U|\le W+d;
   \]
2. preserves every central and fixed protected union and every controller
   footprint;
3. leaves one common private reserve on all chronology positions, whose
   restriction to `U` is compatible with the terminal family; and
4. leaves a terminal residual family and hot set satisfying Theorem 2.1
   (or the cumulative normalized conditions (2.6)--(2.7)).

Corollary 3.2 shows that the first clause cannot be weakened to a
singleton-round construction: asymptotically the non-singleton bulk must
contain a `1-O(k^(-1/2))` fraction of the entire deep ideal.

Open Lemma 4.1 is not proved here.  It is the smallest replacement for the
failed full singleton-absorption programme.  It asks for a constructive
interval-union skeleton, not another Hall theorem.

## 5. Audited boundary

Unconditional results in this note are:

1. all persistent singleton rounds flatten exactly to Theorem 1.1;
2. independent per-round reserve certificates can fail to compose;
3. one common fractional certificate with exact target and hot-position
   degrees gives a terminal integral absorber;
4. the deep ideal has positive Boolean density while singleton positions
   have central-binomial scale; and
5. consequently almost every deep target requires a non-singleton interval.

No construction of the non-singleton bulk skeleton, general Pascal flag
package, `k=15` exact word, or proof of the contiguous-OR conjecture is
claimed.
