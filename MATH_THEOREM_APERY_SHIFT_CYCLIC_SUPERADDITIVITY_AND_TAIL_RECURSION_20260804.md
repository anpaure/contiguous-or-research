# Apéry shifts are cyclically superadditive Bellman tables

**Date:** 2026-08-04  
**Status:** unconditional pure-mathematical theorem.  It applies to every
maximum-density Apéry reduction and identifies its formal periodic tail as
an exact Bellman clock in its own right.  It does not prove Gaussian
positivity or `nu(k) <= B(k) + O(1)`.

## 1. Setup

Let

\[
 c_0=0,\qquad c_1,\ldots,c_n\ge0,
\]

and fix a maximum-density denomination `h`:

\[
 \lambda={c_h\over h}=\max_{1\le j\le n}{c_j\over j},
 \qquad P=h\lambda.
\tag{1.1}
\]

Assume `lambda>0`, as in every first-crossing Bellman application.  This
also ensures convergence of the Gaussian train below; the identically zero
table is not a finite-functional case.

Put

\[
                         d_j=c_j-j\lambda\le0.       \tag{1.2}
\]

On `Z/hZ`, put a directed edge from `u` to `u+j mod h`, labelled `j` and
having reduced weight `d_j`.  For
`r in Z/hZ`, let `beta_r` be the maximum reduced weight of a walk from
zero to `r`, with `beta_0=0`.  Zero-weight loops and cycles are allowed;
because every edge has nonpositive weight, an attaining walk can be chosen
simple after repeated vertices are removed.  In particular, the maximum
exists: the size-one edge reaches every residue, and after cycle removal
only finitely many labelled simple walks remain.

For `0<=r<h`, define the physical Apéry shifts

\[
                         s_r=r\lambda+\beta_r.       \tag{1.3}
\]

The standard size-one walk gives `s_r>=r c_1>=0`, while `beta_r<=0`
gives

\[
                         0\le s_r\le r\lambda<P.     \tag{1.4}
\]

## 2. Cyclic superadditivity

### Theorem 2.1

For `0<=r,t<h`, the shifts satisfy

\[
 \boxed{
 \begin{aligned}
  s_{r+t}&\ge s_r+s_t &&(r+t<h),\\
  P+s_{r+t-h}&\ge s_r+s_t &&(r+t\ge h).
 \end{aligned}}
\tag{2.1}
\]

Consequently

\[
                         (0,s_1,\ldots,s_{h-1},P)    \tag{2.2}
\]

is a nonnegative internally superadditive table, and `P/h` is one of its
maximum generator efficiencies.

#### Proof

Choose attaining walks `Q_r,Q_t` for `beta_r,beta_t`.  Translate `Q_t` by
the residue `r` in this Cayley digraph and concatenate it after `Q_r`.
The resulting walk ends at `r+t mod h` and has reduced weight
`beta_r+beta_t`.  Removing every repeated-residue segment cannot decrease
its weight, because every removed closed segment has nonpositive reduced
weight.  Therefore

\[
             \beta_{(r+t)\bmod h}\ge\beta_r+\beta_t. \tag{2.3}
\]

If `r+t<h`, adding `(r+t)lambda` gives the first inequality in (2.1).
If `r+t>=h`, add

\[
 (r+t)\lambda=P+(r+t-h)\lambda
\]

to obtain the second.  These are exactly all internal superadditivity
inequalities for (2.2).  Finally (1.4) gives `s_r/r<=lambda=P/h` for every
`r>0`. \(\square\)

## 3. The formal tail is an exact Bellman clock

### Theorem 3.1

Let `W` be the Bellman clock generated by the table (2.2).  Then, for every
`q>=0` and `0<=r<h`,

\[
                         \boxed{W_{qh+r}=qP+s_r.}     \tag{3.1}
\]

Thus the formal Apéry Gaussian tail

\[
 \mathcal L_h(P;s_1,\ldots,s_{h-1})
 =\sum_{q\ge0}\sum_{r=0}^{h-1}K(qP+s_r)              \tag{3.2}
\]

is the complete Bellman functional of one honest internally
superadditive `h`-slot table.

#### Proof

The configuration of `q` endpoint generators and one residue-`r`
generator attains `qP+s_r`.

Conversely, encode a part of size `j<h` and value `s_j` by the state
`0P+s_j`, and a part of size `h` and value `P` by `1P+s_0`.  When states
`bP+s_r` and `cP+s_t` are combined, (2.1) bounds their total value by

\[
(b+c)P+s_{r+t}
\]

if `r+t<h`, and by

\[
(b+c+1)P+s_{r+t-h}
\]

if `r+t>=h`.  Thus the state records the exact quotient and residue of the
combined capacity while upper-bounding its value.  Induction over all
parts of an arbitrary exact fill of capacity `qh+r` gives the upper bound
`qP+s_r`.  This proves (3.1).  Since `P>0`, the Gaussian trains converge
absolutely, and summation gives (3.2). \(\square\)

## 4. Consequences

1. The stabilized residue shifts in an Apéry reduction cannot be varied
   independently; all ordinary and carry superadditivity inequalities are
   automatic.
2. If `P>=A` and positivity has already been proved for grid size `h`, then
   the formal tail (3.2) is positive.  Only the availability-filtered finite
   head still needs to be priced.
3. In the five-slot `h=3` branch, the formulas

   \[
   s_1=\max\{a,2b-p\},\qquad s_2=\max\{b,2a\}
   \]

   automatically satisfy

   \[
                        s_2\ge2s_1,qquad p\ge s_1+s_2.
   \]

   Hence its eventual three-coset train is an honest three-slot Bellman
   clock even when its period `p` is below the Gaussian threshold.

The theorem is a recursion of structure, not of the first-crossing grid:
when `P<A`, the formal table reaches the threshold only after repeated
periods, so the existing `h`-slot threshold theorem cannot simply be cited.
