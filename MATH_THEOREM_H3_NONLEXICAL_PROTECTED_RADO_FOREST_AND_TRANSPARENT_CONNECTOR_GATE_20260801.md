## 1. The sharp SCD compatibility statement

Put \(n=2m+1\), \({\cal B}_r=\binom{[n]}r\), and let

\[
 f_i=(q_i=T^i_0\supset T^i_1\supset\cdots\supset T^i_{d-1}),
 \qquad 1\le d\le m+1,                                      \tag{1.1}
\]

be prescribed flags at distinct rank-\(m\) roots.

### Theorem 1.1 (occurrence-labelled selector compatibility)

Assume \(h\le m+1\) roots are prescribed.  Let
\[
                     {\cal R}\subseteq[h]\times\{1,\ldots,d-1\}
\]
be any set of forced mark labels, meaning \(y_{f_i,j}=1\).  An exact
integral marked selector using the prescribed flags and all those forced
variables exists if and only if

\[
                    (i,j)\longmapsto T^i_j
       \quad\hbox{is injective on }{\cal R}.                    \tag{1.2}
\]

If only fragments of a flag are specified, their data at each root must also
extend to one flag.

#### Proof

Two forced occurrences of one target violate its equality row.  Conversely,
apply the protected SCD selector with all prescribed flags quarantined and
unmarked.  It gives one flexible marked provider for every target.  For each
forced occurrence, transfer that target's unique mark from its flexible
provider to the prescribed variable.  Injectivity makes the transfers
disjoint, and the selector LP imposes no contiguity requirement on the marked
depths of one chosen flag. \(\square\)

Common unmarked path vertices and even branching unmarked edge values are
therefore harmless.  At \(m=3,d=3\), for example,

\[
                 123\to12\to1,\qquad124\to12\to2               \tag{1.3}
\]

may force the two bottom marks \(1,2\), although the displayed edge union
branches at \(12\).

### Corollary 1.2 (fully marked rooted prefixes)

Suppose every prescription is a prefix beginning at its rank-\(m\) root and
every nonroot vertex of every prefix is forced marked.  For \(h\le m+1\),
the following are equivalent:

1. the occurrence-labelled selector is feasible;
2. the prefixes are pairwise vertex-disjoint; and
3. a root-preserving chain forest contains all the prefixes.

Indeed, the first meeting of two distinct-root prefixes is a duplicate
target occurrence.  Conversely, disjoint prefixes give at most \(h\)
pairwise disjoint edges at every rank interface, and strict Boolean shadow
surplus extends them to full adjacent-level matchings.  Each resulting chain
has a unique rank-\(m\) root.  In particular, two distinct-root prefixes do
not concatenate.

### Theorem 1.3 (unlabelled edge fragments)

Forget root labels and occurrence multiplicity, and let \(E_r\) be the set
of required edges between \({\cal B}_r\) and \({\cal B}_{r-1}\), where
\[
                            m-d+2\le r\le m.
\]
Write \(U(E_r),D(E_r)\) for its upper and lower endpoints, and let \(N\)
denote neighbourhood in this rank-\((r-1,r)\) containment graph.  An
unlabelled required edge set extends to a high-slab chain factor if and only
if, at every such level,

\[
 E_r\text{ is a matching}                                    \tag{1.4}
\]

and

\[
 |N(X)\setminus U(E_r)|\ge |X|
 \quad\left(X\subseteq{\cal B}_{r-1}\setminus D(E_r)\right).  \tag{1.5}
\]

These are the exact residual Hall conditions after the prescribed matching
endpoints are deleted.  If the edge set is the union of at most
\(h\le m+1\) saturated fragments, then \(|E_r|\le h\) and, for every
nonempty \(X\),

\[
 |N(X)\setminus U(E_r)|
 \ge |X|+n-r-|E_r|\ge |X|.                                  \tag{1.6}
\]

Thus (1.4) alone is exact in the bounded regime: the unlabelled union must be
a descending linear forest.  Here \(12\to1\) and
\(1\to\varnothing\) may concatenate.  The resulting chain factor need not
retain a forgotten fragment owner or a duplicate occurrence label.

### Proposition 1.4 (sharpness of \(m+1\))

For \(m\ge2\) and \(d\ge2\), choose \(A\in{\cal B}_{m-1}\) and \(a\in A\).
For every
\(x\notin A\), prescribe and mark

\[
        A\cup\{x\}\longrightarrow(A\setminus\{a\})\cup\{x\}. \tag{1.7}
\]

There are \(m+2\) pairwise vertex-disjoint rooted edges and their marked
targets are distinct.  Nevertheless, their roots exhaust all rank-\(m\)
neighbours of \(A\), so \(A\) has no provider.  Hence neither target
injectivity nor vertex-disjointness suffices as a universal theorem at
\(h=m+2\).

This reconciles the two earlier wordings: vertex-disjointness is exact for
fully marked, occurrence-labelled, distinct-root prefixes; a descending
linear forest is exact for bounded unlabelled fragments.

## 2. The nonlexical physical ground

Work now on

\[
 \Omega=[2m-1],\qquad
 {\cal O}={\Omega\choose m},\qquad
 {\cal L}={\Omega\choose m-1},\qquad
 {\cal U}={\Omega\choose m+1}.                         \tag{2.1}
\]

Put

\[
 W=|{\cal O}|=|{\cal L}|,\qquad
 P=|{\cal U}|={m-1\over m+1}W,\qquad
 C=W-P=\operatorname {Cat}_m.                          \tag{2.2}
\]

For a directed Johnson edge \(e:X\to Y\), define

\[
                 \lambda(e)=X\cap Y,\qquad
                 \upsilon(e)=X\cup Y.                \tag{2.3}
\]

A directed linear forest has indegree and outdegree at most one and an
acyclic underlying undirected graph.  An **upper-exact lower-injective
Catalan forest** is such a forest \(Q_0\) satisfying

\[
 |Q_0\cap\upsilon^{-1}(R)|=1\quad(R\in{\cal U}),
 \qquad
 |Q_0\cap\lambda^{-1}(L)|\le1\quad(L\in{\cal L}).      \tag{2.4}
\]

It has \(P\) edges on \(W\) spanning owners and hence exactly \(C\)
components, isolates included.

A prepared edge or connector atom carries its literal immediate-palette,
residence-boundary and protected-OR-occurrence data.  A collection is called
**payload-compatible** only when these certificates have a simultaneous
realization.  Individual local safety is not enough.  The Rado theorems below
assume that payload compatibility, together with the indicated port rows, is
represented by one matroid.  This assumption is the precise transparency
gate, not shorthand for an unproved superposition principle.

## 3. First prescribed Rado theorem: build \(Q_0\)

Let \(P_0\) be a prescribed directed portal forest with distinct upper
colours.  For each remaining \(R\in{\cal U}\setminus\upsilon(P_0)\), let
\({\cal E}_R\) be a nonempty menu of prepared directed Johnson edges of
upper colour \(R\).

Assume the following nonlexical alignment.

1. Every edge in \({\cal E}_R\) has one fixed tail \(\tau(R)\), and the
   tails \(\tau(R)\) are distinct across upper colours.  On protected
   colours, \(\tau(R)\) is the prescribed portal tail.
2. There is a potential \(\phi:{\cal O}\to\mathbb R\) which strictly
   increases on every candidate and protected directed edge.
3. A matroid \(N_0\) on the complete edge ground represents, after adjoining
   \(P_0\), exactly the simultaneous conditions of head injectivity,
   lower-colour injectivity and payload compatibility.
4. The protected bank \(P_0\) is independent in \(N_0\).

The potential and tail choices are selected prospectively; they are not the
canonical lexical GMN choices.

### Theorem 3.1 (protected upper-forest Rado theorem)

Under the preceding assumptions, an upper-exact lower-injective directed
Catalan forest of the form
\[
             Q_0=P_0\cup\{e_R:e_R\in{\cal E}_R,\
                   R\in{\cal U}\setminus\upsilon(P_0)\}
\]
inside the represented \(N_0\)-face exists if and only if

\[
 r_{N_0/P_0}\!\left(\bigcup_{R\in S}{\cal E}_R\right)\ge |S|
 \quad
 \left(S\subseteq{\cal U}\setminus\upsilon(P_0)\right).        \tag{3.1}
\]

#### Proof

Rado's theorem applied to the residual upper-colour families in the
contracted matroid \(N_0/P_0\) says that (3.1) is necessary and sufficient
for an independent transversal, one candidate edge for each residual upper
colour.  Adjoin \(P_0\).

The fixed tails are distinct, and \(N_0\)-independence gives distinct heads
and lower colours.  Hence every owner has indegree and outdegree at most one.
An undirected cycle under those degree bounds would be coherently directed,
but \(\phi\) strictly increases on every selected arc.  No cycle exists.
The transversal uses every upper colour exactly once, contains \(P_0\), and
satisfies (2.4).  Its component count is (2.2).  The converse follows by
reading the residual edges of any such represented solution as an
\(N_0/P_0\)-independent transversal. \(\square\)

The exactness in the converse uses the word **represents** in Assumption 3.
If \(N_0\)-independence merely implies the physical conditions, then (3.1)
is still a sufficient theorem but need not be necessary.

### Corollary 3.2 (support-first occurrence Rado)

Suppose instead that one first supplies a directed linear-forest support
\({\cal R}\) containing \(P_0\), with \(\lambda\) injective on \(P_0\).
Assume every subset extending \(P_0\) is payload-transparent.  Tail, head
and graphic independence are then
hereditary, and only lower-colour injectivity remains.  The exact condition
for an upper-exact subset is ordinary Hall/Rado:

\[
 \left|\{\lambda(e):e\in{\cal R},\ \upsilon(e)\in S\}
           \setminus\lambda(P_0)\right|\ge |S|
 \quad(S\subseteq{\cal U}\setminus\upsilon(P_0)).               \tag{3.2}
\]

This is Rado for the lower-colour partition matroid contracted by
\(\lambda(P_0)\).  It is the exact positive content of selecting portals
before component merging: once the unmerged host itself is a transparent
linear forest, representative scattering has disappeared.

Concrete ways to realize \(N_0\) include a functionally aligned/private
head--lower provider partition, a genuinely laminar family of nested sites,
or one explicit unit-gated gammoid which has already been proved to compile
all the listed resources.  The intersection of arbitrary head, lower and
token matroids is not itself a matroid.

## 4. Second prescribed Rado theorem: choose \(M_0\) and \(J\) jointly

Let \(Q_0\) be supplied by Theorem 3.1 or Corollary 3.2.  Its directed path
components form a set \({\cal K}\) of order \(C\).  Write \(h_K,b_K\) for
the initial and terminal owners of component \(K\), and fix the required
root component \(K_*\).  The unused lower colours are

\[
 {\cal A}={\cal L}\setminus\{\lambda(e):e\in Q_0\},
 \qquad |{\cal A}|=C.                                      \tag{4.1}
\]

Introduce the destination set

\[
             {\cal D}=({\cal K}\setminus\{K_*\})\cup\{\bot\},
             \qquad |{\cal D}|=C.                           \tag{4.2}
\]

A **connector-terminal atom** is a prepared label

\[
                              t=(K,d,a)                       \tag{4.3}
\]

with \(K\in{\cal K}\), \(d\in{\cal D}\), \(a\in{\cal A}\), and

\[
                              a\subset b_K.                   \tag{4.4}
\]

If \(d=K'\) is real, also require

\[
                K'\ne K,\qquad a\subset h_{K'},               \tag{4.5}
\]

so \(b_K\to h_{K'}\) is a Johnson connector with lower colour \(a\).
The symbol \(\bot\) designates the final component and uses no outgoing
connector.  Let \({\cal E}_d\) be the menu of atoms with destination \(d\).

Assume that all real component arcs lie in one fixed DAG.  Let \(N_1\) be a
matroid on the atom ground which represents exactly

* distinct source components \(K\);
* distinct provider colours \(a\); and
* simultaneous packet, immediate-palette, residence-collar and OR-token
  compatibility.

Let \(P_1\) be a prescribed connector-path atom bank.  Require it to use
distinct destinations and to be independent in \(N_1\).  Write
\(d(P_1)\) for its used destinations.

### Theorem 4.1 (joint endpoint--connector Rado theorem)

Inside the declared atom atlas and represented \(N_1\)-face, the bank \(P_1\)
extends, through (4.7)--(4.8), to a prospective perfect matching \(M_0\) and
a set \(J\) of \(C-1\) connectors which join all components of \(Q_0\) into
one directed Hamilton owner path starting in \(K_*\) and satisfy the declared
payload if and only if

\[
 r_{N_1/P_1}\!\left(\bigcup_{d\in Y}{\cal E}_d\right)\ge |Y|
 \quad
 \left(Y\subseteq{\cal D}\setminus d(P_1)\right).              \tag{4.6}
\]

Every represented solution contains \(P_1\) and preserves the declared
payload, including the complete marked immediate-upper deck already present
in \(Q_0\) and every supplied higher OR-deck witness represented by its
protected tokens.

#### Proof

Rado's theorem in \(N_1/P_1\) says precisely that (4.6) is equivalent to an
independent transversal containing one atom for every destination not fixed
by \(P_1\).  Together with \(P_1\), there are \(C\) selected atoms, one per
destination.  Source and provider injectivity on two sets of order \(C\)
therefore uses every component source and every colour of \({\cal A}\)
exactly once.

For every oriented internal edge \(X\to Y\) of \(Q_0\), set

\[
                         M_0(X\cap Y)=X.                       \tag{4.7}
\]

For the selected atom \((K,d,a)\) from source \(K\), set

\[
                              M_0(a)=b_K.                       \tag{4.8}
\]

On a component with \(v\) owners, (4.7) maps its \(v-1\) internal lower
colours to all nonterminal owners, and (4.8) maps one unused lower colour to
its terminal owner.  Across components, the internal colours and
\({\cal A}\) partition \({\cal L}\), while nonterminals and terminals
partition \({\cal O}\).  Every displayed map is a containment incidence.
Thus \(M_0:{\cal L}\to{\cal O}\) is a perfect incidence matching.

Every real destination is a nonroot component and is used once, so every
nonroot component has connector indegree one while \(K_*\) has indegree
zero.  Every component is a source once, but the unique \(\bot\)-source has
no real outgoing arc; hence all other sources have outdegree one.  The fixed
DAG excludes a directed cycle.  Therefore the \(C-1\) real arcs form one
directed Hamilton path of the component quotient starting at \(K_*\).
Expanding the directed components gives the required Hamilton owner path.

For an internal edge put the successor incidence at \(Y\); for a real atom
put it at \(h_d\).  These are the \(W-1\) transitions of the alternating
Hamilton chronology.  The \(\bot\)-provider is its single unused successor
port.  If that provider also lies in \(h_{K_*}\), and the closing incidence
is separately declared allowed and payload-compatible, it may instead close
the chronology to a second perfect phase and an owner cycle.  Finally,
\(N_1\)-independence supplies exactly the represented payload compatibility.
\(\square\)

### Corollary 4.2 (private-provider rooted Hall)

Suppose each source component \(K\) has one fixed provider \(a_K\), the
\(a_K\) are distinct, and every remaining packet, palette, collar and
OR-token payload resource is globally private.  Then provider injectivity
is automatic from source injectivity, and \(N_1\) is just the source
partition matroid.  Condition (4.6) becomes
ordinary rooted Hall on the destination menus.  This is the clean exact
meaning of a private provider; merely having many providers is not enough.

Both Theorems 3.1 and 4.1 allow a prescribed bank of any compatible size.
For the intended bounded bank, contract the forced atoms in the resource
matroid and delete their already-used upper-colour or destination classes.
This removes every new ``extend through the portals'' issue.  What remains
is production of the two represented matroids and verification of their
contracted rank rows.

### Proposition 4.3 (the two Rado stages remain jointly quantified)

Let \(\mathfrak Q(P_0)\) be the set of first-stage \(N_0\)-independent
upper-colour transversals containing \(P_0\).  For each
\(Q\in\mathfrak Q(P_0)\), form its component set, unused lower bank,
induced terminal matroid \(N_1(Q)\), menus and prescribed atom bank
\(P_1(Q)\).  The exact combined condition is

\[
 \exists Q\in\mathfrak Q(P_0)\quad
 \forall Y\subseteq{\cal D}(Q)\setminus d(P_1(Q)):
 \quad
 r_{N_1(Q)/P_1(Q)}\!\left(\bigcup_{d\in Y}{\cal E}_d(Q)\right)
 \ge |Y|.                                                     \tag{4.9}
\]

Thus (3.1) and (4.6) are exact integral stages, but (3.1) alone does not say
that an arbitrary first-stage transversal has the required terminal atlas:
the components, endpoints and \(N_1\) depend on \(Q\).  Sequential arbitrary
selection is justified only if (4.6) is proved uniformly for every
\(Q\in\mathfrak Q(P_0)\), or if the first Rado choice is made with the
witness in (4.9) attached.  Equation (4.9) is the surviving joint
\((M_0,Q_0,J)\) quantifier.

## 5. Why the alignment hypotheses are sharp

### 5.1 Physical linear forests are not a matroid

On vertices \(\{c,1,2,3\}\), put

\[
 I=\{c1,c2\},\qquad J=\{c1,c3,12\}.                            \tag{5.1}
\]

Both are linear forests and \(|I|<|J|\).  Adding \(c3\) to \(I\) gives
degree three at \(c\), while adding \(12\) gives the triangle
\(c12c\).  Thus no element of \(J\setminus I\) augments \(I\).  Direct
physical forest selection cannot be fed to ordinary Rado without first
making tail/head/graphic safety automatic, as in Theorem 3.1, or restricting
to a prebuilt linear-forest support, as in Corollary 3.2.

### 5.2 The minimum source--provider Rado obstruction

Take three connector atoms \(a,b,c\) with resource table

\[
\begin{array}{c|ccc}
 &a&b&c\\ \hline
\text{destination}&d_1&d_1&d_2\\
\text{source}&s_1&s_2&s_2\\
\text{provider}&p_1&p_2&p_1
\end{array}                                                    \tag{5.2}
\]

Every two resource rows have a common size-two choice:

\[
 \{a,c\}\quad(\text{destination/source}),\qquad
 \{b,c\}\quad(\text{destination/provider}),\qquad
 \{a,b\}\quad(\text{source/provider}).                        \tag{5.3}
\]

No pair passes all three rows.  The component arcs can be placed from
\(s_1,s_2\) forward to \(d_1,d_2\), so the whole reservoir is a DAG.
Moreover, source--provider compatible sets are not a matroid: \(\{c\}\)
and \(\{a,b\}\) are independent, but neither \(\{a,c\}\) nor
\(\{b,c\}\) is.  Thus there is no safety matroid \(N_1\) obtained merely by
intersecting the source and provider partitions.

This is a sharp abstract/direct-summand obstruction; it is not asserted that
these three labels form a complete Boolean connector atlas.  It is minimum
under loopless local admissibility: with fewer
than three labels, either the target size is one or the full two-label set is
the only size-two candidate.  A bounded protected portal bank may be added
as a disjoint forced direct summand; (5.2) survives the contraction unchanged.
Replacing the provider row by an independent transparency gammoid gives the
same three-label Borromean obstruction.  Hence separate Rado or Hall checks
on destinations, sources, providers and tokens do not synchronize.

### 5.3 The natural Boolean relaxation is genuinely nonintegral

At \(m=3\), the eight oriented Boolean diamonds in the parity-circuit note
form a \(Q_3\) compatibility graph.  Weight \(1/2\) on every diamond covers
four upper colours exactly and saturates all used lower, tail and head rows,
but every integral compatible subset has size at most two.  After adding one
unit diamond and the residual matching, this is a fractional extreme point
of the complete prospective-\(M_0\) LP.

The same point remains feasible after every graphic-forest inequality.  Its
half-weight physical graph has eight edges on six vertices and total weight
four.  For at most four vertices,

\[
                  x(E(S))\le {\binom{|S|}{2}\over2}\le |S|-1; \tag{5.4}
\]

deleting any one of the six vertices removes at least two half-edges, so a
five-vertex set has weight at most three; the full set has weight four.  The
extra unit diamond is a disjoint edge.  Since the original point was extreme
and remains feasible after intersecting with these valid halfspaces, it is
still extreme.

This refutes a generic LP/TU or support-local rounding proof even when the
graphic row is present.  It is not a Boolean nonexistence theorem: integral
solutions outside this support exist.

There is also a direct SCD warning.  The standard GK-SCD upper selector is
upper-bijective, lower-injective and acyclic, yet the owner

\[
                              X_*=1(01)^{m-1}                    \tag{5.5}
\]

has degree exactly \(m-1\).  Thus, for \(m\ge4\), exact SCD marginals do not
supply the physical owner cap two required by (2.4).

## 6. Transparency is an independent global row

On \([7]\) at \(m=4\), consider

The exchanges are \(1\to3,4\to5,3\to7\), so every edge increases the

### Proposition 6.2 (upper tokens have nonzero mixed difference)

At the common lower core \(L=123\), put

 [12367]-[12356]-[12347]+[12345]\ne0.                          \tag{6.6}

## 7. Exact surviving theorem and scope

The strongest proved nonlexical **Rado-route** implication is the following.

> **Protected nonlexical Rado host theorem.**  Suppose a bounded prepared
> portal bank is included in a fixed-tail, common-potential candidate atlas
> whose remaining head/lower/payload conflicts are represented by one
> matroid \(N_0\).  Suppose the nested condition (4.9) holds, witnessed by a
> first-stage transversal \(Q_0\) whose prepared
> connector-terminal atlas lies in a DAG, all source/provider/payload
> conflicts are represented by one matroid \(N_1(Q_0)\), and (4.6) holds
> after contracting the prescribed connector bank.  Then there is a
> jointly selected nonlexical \((M_0,Q_0,J)\) containing both protected banks,
> with \(Q_0\) upper-exact and lower-injective, \(J\) a rooted Hamilton
> component connector, the complete marked immediate-upper deck retained,
> and every supplied immediate palette, residence collar and protected full
> OR-deck token retained.

Every word in the conclusion follows from an explicit hypothesis or from the
two Rado proofs.  What is not proved is that Boolean candidate atlases
\(N_0,N_1\) with these properties and rank inequalities exist for all \(m\).
The sharp obstructions show why independent marginal, Hall, graphic and token
checks cannot replace that construction.

The open mathematical target is therefore not another post-hoc SCD merge.
It is one of the following genuinely correlated objects:

1. a nonlexical fixed-tail/common-potential atlas with a private, laminar or
   gammoid representation of all residual upper-forest resources; or
2. a prebuilt hereditary transparent directed linear-forest support passing
   the occurrence Hall condition (3.2);

followed in either case by a private/aligned connector-terminal atlas passing
(4.6).

Section 11 gives the alternative cycle-factor route: replace the
connector-terminal atlas by a guarded regenerative socket forest.  That
route is exact conditionally but its literal \(q\)-port packet is presently
only a height-one node.

No construction of a complete arbitrary-width upper deck, lower-depth
compiler, common-cap, exterior regeneration, voltage, opening, or
additive-constant conclusion is asserted.  Those gates remain outside the H3
theorem unless separately proved.

## 8. The rolling-reset three-return bridge

The opened bidirectional rolling reset exports the exact boundary

\[
 \mathsf A:\text{ one head--owner attachment pair},\qquad
 \mathsf P_0,\mathsf P_1:\text{ two predecessor-parity pairs}. \tag{8.1}
\]

This signature is neither three independent forced edges nor three elements
which may simply be added to \(P_0\) or \(P_1\).

### 8.1 The reset phases live on opposite halves

For \(0\le a<N-1\), write

\[
 D^\rightarrow
   =\{T_a\to T_{a+1}\},\qquad
 D^\leftarrow
   =\{T_{a+1}\to T_a\},                                  \tag{8.2}
\]

where the braces in (8.2) are understood over all such \(a\).  Both
orientations of the \(a\)th physical edge have

\[
 I_a=T_a\cap T_{a+1},\qquad U_a=T_a\cup T_{a+1}.            \tag{8.3}
\]

Consequently,

* \(D^\rightarrow\cup D^\leftarrow\) repeats every upper colour \(U_a\)
  and lower colour \(I_a\);
* the fixed-tail rule would require the same \(U_a\) to have both
  \(T_a\) and \(T_{a+1}\) as its tail; and
* one common strict potential would require both

\[
             \phi(T_a)<\phi(T_{a+1})
       \quad\hbox{and}\quad
             \phi(T_{a+1})<\phi(T_a).                       \tag{8.4}
\]

Thus the union of the two reset phases is not independent in the upper
task, lower-colour or fixed-tail rows of Theorem 3.1.  It cannot be
contracted in one common-potential \(N_0\).  For every internal \(a\), the
pair

\[
                 T_a\to T_{a+1},\qquad T_{a+1}\to T_a          \tag{8.4a}
\]

is the closed doubleton and repeats \(U_a\).  Hence no edgewise phase mixing
is allowed.  For the omitted seam specifically, either one direction
recloses the isolated reset component, while both directions repeat the
unique owner \(U_{N-1}\).

Indeed, after forgetting the typed signs, all three pairs in (8.1) have the
same endpoint roots \(T_0,T_{N-1}\).  Their unique common rank-\((r+1)\)
owner is \(U_{N-1}=T_0\cup T_{N-1}\).  Three direct same-neighbour closures
would therefore put demand three on one capacity-one owner; the two
predecessor directions are precisely the forbidden opposite seam
orientations.  Every positive bridge needs nontrivial external returns.
Theorem 8.9 supplies them as one coupled long-rail packet rather than as
three independently selected corridors.

The same warning applies to each external return.  An alternating return
contains a selected half and an unselected replacement half.  Contraction
may force the selected half of one phase; it must never contract the union
of both halves.

There is also an atomic-lift requirement.  A head--owner attachment column
\((q,o)\) and a predecessor edge \((p,q)\) are only projections.  Neither is
an element of the present \(N_0\) or \(N_1\) until they are combined into a
literal legal turn \((p,q,o)\).  The bi-contractible module below must
declare a stage assignment for every such turn.  In its natural
\(Q_0\)-pure specialization:

1. every owner-return segment replacing a marked upper representative
   belongs to the \(N_0\) upper-edge atlas; the theorem below assumes the
   owner return is entirely \(Q_0\)-pure, unless every unmarked seam segment
   is lifted to a compound \(N_1\) atom carrying its owner/head-exactness
   row;
2. the internal halves of both predecessor parity paths are already the
   directed \(Q_0\) edges in (8.2); and
3. an external predecessor closure belongs to \(N_1\) only when it consists
   entirely of free component connector-terminal atoms.

Reversal changes \(M_0(I_a)=T_a\) into \(M_0(I_a)=T_{a+1}\), so \(M_0\)
is phase-dependent.  If one physical return segment consumes resources in
both stages, two independent contractions do not ensure that the same
segment was selected.  Such a segment needs one compound atom or an explicit
joint lift certificate.

To compare the phases without confusing their stage-indexed grounds, fix one
occurrence-labelled physical projection ground and maps

\[
 \alpha:\{\text{literal turn occurrences}\}\to
          \{\text{head--owner attachment occurrences}\},\qquad
 \pi:\{\text{literal turn occurrences}\}\to
          \{\text{predecessor occurrences}\}.               \tag{8.4b}
\]

A turn occurrence may have a nonempty image under both maps.  All symmetric
differences below are taken only after physical expansion and application of
\(\alpha\) or \(\pi\); a symmetric difference of raw \(N_0\)- and
\(N_1\)-atoms would be ill-typed.

### Definition 8.1 (bi-contractible reset module)

Let \(\varepsilon\in\{\rightarrow,\leftarrow\}\).  A bi-contractible reset
module consists of phase-indexed stage banks

\[
 B_0^\varepsilon\subseteq E(N_0^\varepsilon),\qquad
 B_1^\varepsilon(Q^\varepsilon)
       \subseteq E(N_1^\varepsilon(Q^\varepsilon))             \tag{8.5}
\]

with the following properties.

1. The banks are occurrence-labelled full-turn lifts.  Put

   \[
       L^\varepsilon=\operatorname {expand}
          (B_0^\varepsilon\sqcup B_1^\varepsilon).
   \]

   On the occurrence-labelled projection grounds they satisfy

   \[
   \begin{aligned}
     \alpha(L^\rightarrow)\mathbin\triangle
        \alpha(L^\leftarrow)&=C_{\mathsf A},\\
     \pi(L^\rightarrow)\mathbin\triangle
        \pi(L^\leftarrow)&=C_{\mathsf P_0}\sqcup C_{\mathsf P_1},
   \end{aligned}                                               \tag{8.4c}
   \]

   where the right sides are exactly the one attachment and two correctly
   paired predecessor alternating cycles, with no other projected change.
   Either the three external return paths are mutually resource-disjoint
   except at their typed endpoints, or they are the three projections of one
   coupled occurrence-labelled macro whose internal cancellations have been
   checked jointly.  In the coupled case no shared projection is counted as
   two selected physical occurrences, and the complete phase bank is simple
   on every literal resource.  Neither bank uses the direct reset seam, and
   no return bank contains the terminal \(\bot\)-atom.  The owner return is
   \(Q_0\)-pure, or every one of its \(N_1\) segments is a compound atom
   carrying the missing owner/head row.
2. Each \(B_s^\varepsilon\) is independent in its phase matroid, uses
   distinct task classes, is physical in its phase forest or DAG, and
   preserves every declared palette, residence and OR-token payload.  The
   compound payload also represents the complete functional
   owner-attachment row in both phases, including every unmarked connector
   turn.  At stage zero, the entire atlas
   \(B_0^\varepsilon\cup G_0\), not merely the forced bank, satisfies the
   phase-indexed fixed-tail, distinct-tail and strict-potential hypotheses of
   Theorem 3.1, with a potential \(\phi^\varepsilon\); alternatively it lies
   in one supplied hereditary directed-linear-forest support as in
   Corollary 3.2.
3. The two phase banks use the same sets of upper task classes at stage zero
   and, after identifying their component quotients, the same destination
   classes at stage one.  Each \(G_s\) is a common ground of
   occurrence-labelled **literal atoms**, not merely an isomorphic quotient
   ground.  Every common atom has the same physical expansion in both
   phases, preserving its tail, head, initial and terminal owner, source,
   destination, provider, literal connector turn and every payload token.
   All packet-only atoms, every atom meeting a reserved packet resource, and
   every atom whose physical expansion changes with the reset phase are put
   into a paired bank or deleted from \(G_s\).
4. On \(G_0\), put

\[
 \widehat N_0^\varepsilon
      =\left.(N_0^\varepsilon/B_0^\varepsilon)\right|G_0.      \tag{8.6}
\]

   The common independence system

\[
 I(\widehat N_0^\rightarrow)
       \cap I(\widehat N_0^\leftarrow)                         \tag{8.7}
\]

   is represented by one matroid \(\overline N_0\), and its task families
   are the same residual upper-colour menus in both phases.
5. For every common first-stage transversal \(S_0\), the two forests

\[
                Q^\varepsilon=B_0^\varepsilon\cup S_0          \tag{8.8}
\]

   have literally identified exterior components, unused lower providers,
   destination classes and connector grounds \(G_1(S_0)\), including the
   same root component \(K_*\), the same exterior initial/terminal owners,
   and one identical fixed \(\bot\)-atom with the same source and provider.
   Reset boundary atoms and reset-internal component states are phase-indexed
   and therefore lie outside the common exterior.  Each phase has a fixed
   DAG containing
   \(B_1^\varepsilon(Q^\varepsilon)\) and every residual menu used below.
   On that ground,

\[
 \widehat N_1^\varepsilon(S_0)
  =\left.
      \bigl(N_1^\varepsilon(Q^\varepsilon)/
            B_1^\varepsilon(Q^\varepsilon)\bigr)
    \right|G_1(S_0),                                         \tag{8.9}
\]

   and

\[
 I(\widehat N_1^\rightarrow(S_0))
       \cap I(\widehat N_1^\leftarrow(S_0))
       =I(\overline N_1(S_0))                                \tag{8.10}
\]

   for one represented matroid \(\overline N_1(S_0)\), with the same
   residual destination menus and payload in both phases.  Each phase
   connector atlas satisfies all hypotheses of Theorem 4.1 on this literal
   ground: the source, destination, provider, \(M_0^\varepsilon\)-incidence,
   owner and payload rows are exactly represented; the \(\bot\)-incidence is
   allowed and payload-compatible; and every full terminal transversal in
   the fixed DAG expands to the asserted rooted Hamilton component path.

Call the module **full-functional** when, in addition, the completed
\(\alpha\)- and \(\pi\)-projections in each phase are the prescribed perfect
functional attachment and perfect predecessor states of the rolling-reset
theorem.  Without this extra clause, “reset exchange” below means only the
exchange between the two declared H3 projection states.

Equality of the two phase contractions in (8.6) and (8.9) is the cleanest
sufficient condition for (8.7) and (8.10).  It also suffices that one
contracted independence system contain the other, or more generally that
their intersection have a proved matroid representation.  The equal
head/owner and predecessor tail/head footprints of the three alternating
cycles make the pure partition contractions agree.  They do not
automatically equalize \(N_0\)'s lower/payload rows, \(N_1\)'s
provider/payload rows, component topology, or the \(Q_0\)-versus-\(J\)
stage assignment.

### Theorem 8.2 (phase-paired nested Rado theorem)

Let a bi-contractible reset module be given.  Delete its used upper and
destination task classes and every atom conflicting with either packet.
Write

\[
 {\cal E}^{\rm com}_R={\cal E}_R\cap G_0,\qquad
 {\cal E}^{\rm com}_d(S_0)={\cal E}_d(S_0)\cap G_1(S_0)       \tag{8.10a}
\]

for the resulting common residual menus.  Within these two declared phase
atlases, a switch-ready protected H3 host with one common exterior exists if
and only if

\[
 r_{\overline N_0}
   \left(\bigcup_{R\in S}{\cal E}^{\rm com}_R\right)\ge |S|
 \qquad
 \left(S\subseteq{\cal U}_{\rm res}\right)                    \tag{8.11}
\]

and there is an \(\overline N_0\)-independent full residual upper
transversal \(S_0\) for which

\[
 r_{\overline N_1(S_0)}
   \left(\bigcup_{d\in Y}{\cal E}^{\rm com}_d(S_0)\right)\ge |Y|
 \qquad
 \left(Y\subseteq{\cal D}_{\rm res}(S_0)\right).              \tag{8.12}
\]

Equivalently, let \(\mathfrak T_0\) be the family of **all** full residual
upper transversals in the menus \({\cal E}^{\rm com}_R\) which are
\(\overline N_0\)-independent.  Equation (8.11) says precisely that this
family is nonempty, and the nested condition is

\[
 \exists S_0\in\mathfrak T_0\quad
 \forall Y\subseteq{\cal D}_{\rm res}(S_0):
 \quad
 r_{\overline N_1(S_0)}
   \left(\bigcup_{d\in Y}{\cal E}^{\rm com}_d(S_0)\right)
      \ge |Y|.                                                 \tag{8.13}
\]

#### Proof

Rado's theorem in \(\overline N_0\) makes (8.11) equivalent to a common
residual upper-colour transversal \(S_0\).  By (8.7), adjoining
\(B_0^\varepsilon\) gives a legal upper-exact forest in each phase.
The common exterior identification makes the same residual destination
families available at stage one.  Rado in \(\overline N_1(S_0)\) makes
(8.12) equivalent to one common terminal transversal \(S_1\).  Equations
(8.9)--(8.10) show that

\[
 Q^\varepsilon=B_0^\varepsilon\cup S_0,\qquad
 {\cal T}_1^\varepsilon
      =B_1^\varepsilon(Q^\varepsilon)\cup S_1                 \tag{8.14}
\]

are legal in both phase atlases.  Theorem 4.1 constructs the corresponding
perfect \(M_0^\varepsilon\); the real-destination atoms of
\({\cal T}_1^\varepsilon\) are \(J^\varepsilon\) and give the rooted
Hamilton chronology in each phase, starting at the identified \(K_*\) and
using the phase terminal atom at \(\bot\).  The compound payload makes the
functional owner attachment exact on both marked and connector turns.
The literal packet hypothesis says that the symmetric difference of the two
hosts has, after physical expansion, \(\alpha\)-difference exactly
\(C_{\mathsf A}\) and \(\pi\)-difference exactly
\(C_{\mathsf P_0}\sqcup C_{\mathsf P_1}\), with no other projected
difference.  Toggling all three alternating cycles therefore realizes the
declared H3 projection exchange while every exterior atom remains literally
fixed.  For a full-functional module this is the complete functional
attachment/predecessor reset exchange.

Conversely, two hosts in the declared atlases which are identical outside
the reset module yield common residual sets \(S_0,S_1\).  These are
independent in both phase contractions and hence in the represented
intersection matroids.  Rado necessity gives (8.11)--(8.12). \(\square\)

For one phase chosen permanently in advance, the common-minor hypotheses
are unnecessary: contract only \(B_s^\varepsilon\), delete the used task
classes, and apply Theorems 3.1 and 4.1 in that phase.  This produces one
phase host, not a deferred reset switch.  Under Definition 8.1's joint
full-turn lift, the phase-selected pieces of both parity returns are already
the fixed banks \(B_0^\varepsilon,B_1^\varepsilon\), so their number creates
no additional selection matroid.  Without that lift, they need not be
independent or even belong to one stage.

Theorem 8.2 cannot be applied inside one original fixed-tail,
common-potential atlas, by (8.4).  It requires two phase-indexed atlases or
support-first forests, or one separately proved stateful macro whose
contraction realizes the same common minors.

### Theorem 8.3 (sharp bi-contraction Borromean obstruction)

The common-minor hypothesis in Definition 8.1 is not a formality.  It can
fail on three residual labels even when both phase-specific Rado systems
separately pass and both phase matroids are contraction--restriction minors
of one graphic matroid.

Let \(G\) have vertices \(x,y,z,w\) and edges

\[
 c=xy,\qquad b=yz,\qquad u=zx,\qquad
 a=yw,\qquad v=wx.                                           \tag{8.15}
\]

Let \(M=M(G)\), and on the residual ground \(\{a,b,c\}\) put

\[
 N^\rightarrow=\left.(M/u)\right|\{a,b,c\},\qquad
 N^\leftarrow=\left.(M/v)\right|\{a,b,c\}.                   \tag{8.16}
\]

Contracting \(u\) makes \(b,c\) parallel, while contracting \(v\) makes
\(a,c\) parallel.  Take two Rado task families

\[
                         \{a,b\},\qquad\{c\}.                 \tag{8.17}
\]

The forward phase has the full transversal \(\{a,c\}\), and the reverse
phase has the full transversal \(\{b,c\}\).  The pair \(\{a,b\}\) is
independent in both phase matroids but violates the first task family.
Therefore no two-element set is simultaneously a task transversal and
independent in both \(N^\rightarrow,N^\leftarrow\).

#### Proof

The only forbidden residual pair of \(N^\rightarrow\) is \(\{b,c\}\);
the only forbidden residual pair of \(N^\leftarrow\) is \(\{a,c\}\); and
the task partition forbids \(\{a,b\}\).  These are the three two-subsets of
\(\{a,b,c\}\).  Moreover, \(\{c\}\) and \(\{a,b\}\) are independent in
both phase minors, but neither \(a\) nor \(b\) augments \(\{c\}\) inside
their common independence system: \(a,c\) fails in \(N^\leftarrow\), while
\(b,c\) fails in \(N^\rightarrow\).  Hence

\[
 I(N^\rightarrow)\cap I(N^\leftarrow)
\]

is not a matroid, directly violating Definition 8.1(4).  The assertions
follow. \(\square\)

This is the minimum loopless three-label obstruction.  Adding the
corresponding phase reset bank and private returns as separate forced direct
summands leaves it unchanged after contraction.  It is an abstract
matroid/minor no-go, not a Boolean nonexistence theorem.  It proves that
separate phasewise Rado certificates, even from one parent matroid, do not
reserve a common reset exterior.

Equivalently, without (8.7) or (8.10), the common exterior lies in

\[
 I(M_{\rm task})\cap
 I(\widehat N_s^\rightarrow)\cap
 I(\widehat N_s^\leftarrow),                                \tag{8.18}
\]

a genuine three-matroid common-set problem.  The earlier three-label
destination/source/provider obstruction is the same Borromean pattern.

### 8.4 The open Johnson square is the preferred return module

Let the two adjacent reset endpoints be

\[
                    E=L+x,\qquad F=L+y,\qquad
                    U=L+x+y,
\]

where \(x\ne y\).  Choose

\[
                    p\in L,\qquad z\notin U,
\]

and put

\[
             E'=L-p+x+z,\qquad F'=L-p+y+z.                  \tag{8.18a}
\]

The four roots \(E,E',F',F\) form a Johnson square whose fourth edge is the
forbidden direct seam \(EF\).  Open that edge and take the two phase paths

\[
\begin{aligned}
 {\cal S}^{\rightarrow}&:
       E\to E'\to F'\to F,\\
 {\cal S}^{\leftarrow}&:
       F\to F'\to E'\to E.                                 \tag{8.18b}
\end{aligned}
\]

Write the three lower resources as

\[
 D_x=L-p+x,\qquad D_0=L-p+z,\qquad D_y=L-p+y
\]

and the three owners as

\[
 O_x=L+x+z,\qquad
 O_0=L-p+x+y+z,\qquad
 O_y=L+y+z.                                                 \tag{8.18c}
\]

### Theorem 8.4 (support-four square three-return identity)

Each phase in (8.18b) is a matching of three literal ordered Boolean turns.
Both phases use exactly the same simple lower and owner sets

\[
                 \{D_x,D_0,D_y\},\qquad\{O_x,O_0,O_y\}.
\]

In the head--owner projection, their symmetric difference is the one
alternating path

\[
             E-O_x-E'-O_0-F'-O_y-F.                         \tag{8.18d}
\]

In the predecessor tail--head projection, their symmetric difference is the
disjoint union of the two alternating paths

\[
\begin{aligned}
 E^-&-(E')^+-(F')^- -F^+,\\
 F^-&-(F')^+-(E')^- -E^+.                                  \tag{8.18e}
\end{aligned}
\]

Thus, after identifying \(E=T_0,F=T_{N-1}\) with the reset boundary, the
open square has exactly one attachment return and the two signed predecessor
returns.  The two seam orientations \(E\to F\) and \(F\to E\) share the
single lower/owner pair \(L,U\), but neither is selected.

#### Proof

The three turns of the forward phase are

\[
 (D_x,O_x,E,E'),\quad(D_0,O_0,E',F'),\quad
 (D_y,O_y,F',F),
\]

and the reverse phase consists of their oppositely directed versions in
reverse order.  The displayed lower and owner sets are therefore common and
simple.  Projecting head to owner gives respectively

\[
\{(E',O_x),(F',O_0),(F,O_y)\},\qquad
\{(F',O_y),(E',O_0),(E,O_x)\},
\]

whose union is (8.18d).  Projecting tail to head gives the two matchings in
(8.18b); in the bipartite tail/head copies their union has exactly the two
components (8.18e). \(\square\)

This is support-minimal among simple open Johnson-cycle returns on the same
seam.  A smaller open cycle would use a two-edge detour \(E-C-F\).  Every
common neighbour \(C\) either repeats the lower colour \(L\) on both edges
or repeats the direct owner \(U\), so no such detour is simultaneously
lower- and owner-injective.  The minimality is scoped to this open
Johnson-path architecture; it is not a classification of every possible
nonlocal compound trade.

Call the square **centrally guarded and prospectively labelled** when:

1. its two phase turn sets have occurrence-labelled flags on the intended
   unmerged chains;
2. its only contact with the reset is at \(E,F\) and the shared omitted seam
   resources \(L,U\); all other lower, owner, tail and head occurrences are
   private to the central module;
3. adjoining it to the two opened-reset phases closes precisely the three
   projected paths of the reset theorem and equalizes the complete
   immediate lower, owner, typed-tail and typed-head ledgers; and
4. every further payload actually claimed for this central cell is carried
   by an explicit occurrence token.

This definition deliberately makes no forest/DAG, residence, exterior-OR
or compiler assertion.  The three square edges form one literal path and
are necessarily consecutive in its degree-at-most-one physical component.
Prospective occurrence labels cannot scatter them while retaining that
path.  Moreover, the square alone has different free tail/head endpoints in
its two orientations; a common residual contraction can be asserted only
for a larger combined phase module.

#### Proof

### Proposition 8.7 (sharp forest/DAG cycle lock)

Let

\[
 {\cal R}^+:E\longrightarrow\cdots\longrightarrow F
\]

be the opened forward reset path and let \({\cal R}^-\) be its reverse.
Tail/head capacity one forces \({\cal R}^+\) to pair with the square path
from \(F\) back to \(E\), and forces \({\cal R}^-\) to pair with the
opposite square path.  The two legal phase banks are therefore opposite
orientations of one isolated directed cycle.

No partition of either cycle into a first-stage forest \(Q_0\) and
second-stage connector set \(J\) satisfies the H3 architecture.  If
\(J=\varnothing\), then \(Q_0\) contains a cycle.  If \(J\ne\varnothing\),
the \(Q_0\)-components are directed intervals of the cycle; contracting
them turns \(J\) into a directed cycle, or into a loop when \(|J|=1\).
Thus the quotient is not a DAG.

The same conclusion holds after grouping consecutive turns into compound
atoms, because expansion recovers the same physical cycle.  It also holds
for the completed reset--rail cycle below.  Consequently every H3 use
requires a separate phase-paired topology opener which actually
removes/replaces a cycle edge and preserves the complete resource ledger;
compound naming or a post hoc stage assignment is insufficient.

#### Proof

The forward reset path already uses every internal reset root once as tail
and head.  Its only missing outgoing tail is \(F\), and its only missing
incoming head is \(E\).  Capacity one therefore propagates the unique
orientation \(F\leadsto E\) through the square.  The reverse case is
symmetric.  The quotient assertion is the cyclic ordering of the remaining
\(J\)-arcs after every intervening \(Q_0\)-interval is contracted.
\(\square\)

The obvious abstract opener would cut a cycle edge \(u\to v\) and an
exterior path edge \(a\to b\), then replace them by \(a\to v,u\to b\),
with the conjugate splice in the reversed phase.  This is topologically
correct but not a literal two-edge Boolean solution.  Two distinct
owner-exact Johnson edges cannot be cross-reconnected while preserving the
two tails, two heads and the two-owner multiset: after either assignment of
the old owners, two distinct rank-\((r+1)\) owners would have to share two
rank-\(r\) facets, whereas they share at most one.  Hence every nondegenerate
owner-exact opener has support at least three.  The ternary hex is the first
available topology type, but its guarded embedding with the packet cut
ledger remains unproved.

### Theorem 8.8 (resident local rail and bare-wreath cut price)

Put \(L_0=K+x\), so \(V_1=L_0+y\), \(V_0=L_0+z\), and

 p_1,\ldots,p_s\in L_0\setminus\{b\},\qquad

for auxiliary \(b\in L_0,c\notin U_0\).  With
\(P_j=\{p_1,\ldots,p_j\}\) and

\begin{aligned}
 A_j&=L_0-P_j+Q_j+y &&(0\le j\le s),\\
 B_0&=L_0-P_s+Q_s+z,\\
 B_j&=L_0-\{p_{j+1},\ldots,p_s\}
          +\{q_{j+1},\ldots,q_s\}+z &&(1\le j\le s).
                                                               \tag{8.18h}
\end{aligned}
\]

Then \(A_0=V_1,B_s=V_0\), and

\[
 {\cal R}_s=A_0A_1\cdots A_sB_0B_1\cdots B_s              \tag{8.18i}
\]

is a simple lower- and owner-rainbow Johnson path of length \(2s+1\).
Its two orientations have the same immediate palettes, one attachment
alternating path, the two signed predecessor alternating paths, and the
same complete internal interval-union deck.  Every internally bounded
positive coordinate run has length at least \(s+1\); taking \(s=d\) gives a
flat resident return whenever \(d\le m-2\).

For \(s=1,p_1=x,q_1=w\), (8.18i) is exactly the support-four path
(8.18g).  For \(s>1\) it is a replacement, not an exterior collar retaining
the three square edges.

The closed rail obtained by adjoining the omitted seam is a wreath.  At
interval depth \(q\), \(1\le q\le s\), exactly \(q\) cyclic blocks cross
the cut \(V_0V_1\); their union targets are distinct within and between
depths.  Hence the complete distinct local cut collateral is

\[
                           \sum_{q=1}^{s}q
                              ={s(s+1)\over2}.                \tag{8.18j}
\]

All larger internal intervals have the saturated full active union.  Thus
(8.18j) is the exact local occurrence bank lost when the **bare rail
wreath** is cut at \(V_0V_1\).  It is a classification, not a global hole

the complete-cycle cut ledger is the one in Proposition 8.10 below.

#### Proof

Successive edges in (8.18i) swap \(p_j\) for \(q_j\), then \(y\) for
\(z\), then swap the \(q_j\)'s back for the \(p_j\)'s in the same order.
The prefix indices and the presence of \(y\), neither seam coordinate, or
\(z\) make all lower and owner resources distinct.  Reversing a simple
path preserves those palettes.  For any owner-rainbow path, the attachment
projection of its two orientations is one alternating endpoint path, while
the bipartite predecessor projection separates by parity into the two
signed endpoint paths.

The cyclic active word is a window of length \(s+1\) in

\[
             (z,p_1,\ldots,p_s,y,q_1,\ldots,q_s),
\]

so every nonconstant cyclic run has length \(s+1\), and opening at the seam
only clips boundary runs.  Reversal bijects all internal intervals.
Finally, a block of \(q+1\) consecutive cyclic owners crosses a fixed cut
in exactly \(q\) starting positions.  The window formula makes their targets
distinct for \(q\le s\), proving (8.18j). \(\square\)

### Theorem 8.9 (exact complete-reversal reset--rail packet)

Put \(n=d+1\), \(N=2n\).  Let \(C\) be a permanent reset core of rank
\(r-n\), choose private coordinates \(X_0,\ldots,X_{N-1}\), and put

\[
 T_a=C\cup\{X_a,\ldots,X_{a+n-1}\}\quad(a\in{\mathbb Z}_N),
 \qquad E=T_0,\quad F=T_{N-1}.                             \tag{8.18l}
\]

Assume

\[
                         r\ge2d+2,\qquad k-r\ge2d+1.        \tag{8.18m}
\]

Write \(L=E\cap F\), \(\delta=E-L\), \(\alpha=F-L\).  Choose
distinct \(x_1,\ldots,x_d,b\in C\) and distinct
\(y_1,\ldots,y_d\) outside the complete reset support.  With
\(X[j]=\{x_1,\ldots,x_j\}\) and \(Y[j]=\{y_1,\ldots,y_j\}\), put

\[
\begin{aligned}
 P_j&=(L-X[j])+Y[j]+\alpha &&(0\le j\le d),\\
 Q_0&=(L-X[d])+Y[d]+\delta,\\
 Q_j&=L-\{x_{j+1},\ldots,x_d\}
          +\{y_{j+1},\ldots,y_d\}+\delta &&(1\le j\le d).
                                                               \tag{8.18n}
\end{aligned}
\]

Thus \(P_0=F,Q_d=E\).  The opened reset followed by the return rail is the
cyclic root word

\[
 Z^+=(T_0,\ldots,T_{N-1},P_1,\ldots,P_d,Q_0,\ldots,Q_{d-1})
                                                               \tag{8.18o}
\]

of length \(M=4d+2\); put \(Z_i^-=Z_{-i}^+\).

Then the following are unconditional local facts.

1. \(Z^+\) is a simple rank-\(r\) Johnson cycle and all of its immediate
   lower and upper colours are distinct.  \(Z^-\) is its complete directed
   reversal and has exactly the same two palettes.
2. Every nonconstant coordinate has one positive and one negative cyclic
   run, and both signs have length at least \(d+1\).  The positive-run
   census is

   \[
   \begin{array}{c|c}
   \text{coordinate}&\text{positive-run length}\\ \hline
   y_j&d+1\\
   \alpha,\delta&2d+1\\
   x_j&3d+1\\
   X_i\in(E\cap F)-C&3d+1\\
   X_i\notin E\cup F&d+1.
   \end{array}                                             \tag{8.18p}
   \]

   Coordinates in \(C-\{x_1,\ldots,x_d\}\) are constant one and all
   remaining coordinates are constant zero.
3. For every cyclic width \(1\le q\le M\),

   \[
    \bigcup_{t=0}^{q-1}Z^-_{i+t}
       =\bigcup_{t=0}^{q-1}Z^+_{-i-q+1+t}.                 \tag{8.18q}
   \]

   Hence the complete cyclic interval-OR decks agree width by width and
   with multiplicity.  Start labels are reflected, not fixed pointwise.
4. The maximal cyclic depth-\(d\) antecedents

   \[
                  A_i^\varepsilon
                    =\bigcap_{t=i-d}^{i}Z_t^\varepsilon
   \]

   are nonempty, satisfy \(D^dA^\varepsilon=Z^\varepsilon\), and obey

   \[
       A_i^-=A_{d-i}^+,\qquad
       (D^jA^-)_i=(D^jA^+)_{d-j-i}\quad(0\le j\le d).
                                                               \tag{8.18r}
   \]

   Thus every derivative-row inventory and every cyclic interval-OR deck
   at every derivative depth agree under one explicit reflection/rotation.
5. The two phase projections have one attachment cycle and two predecessor
   parity cycles.  Opening a common edge gives literally the one attachment
   path and two signed predecessor paths exported by the opened reset.
6. Reflection is an isomorphism of every target--cell incidence graph whose
   cells are wholly internal to this closed packet.  Internal compiler
   transport is therefore exact; no aggregate-count relaxation is being
   used.

#### Proof

Every internal rail root contains a fresh \(y_j\), so it is not a reset
root.  Every rail upper colour contains a fresh \(y_j\); every rail lower
colour either contains one or omits an \(x_j\in C\), whereas reset colours
contain all of \(C\) and no \(y_j\).  This proves global root and immediate
palette simplicity.

On the rail, \(y_j\) occupies
\(P_j,\ldots,P_d,Q_0,\ldots,Q_{j-1}\), exactly \(d+1\) roots.
The complementary rail pieces for \(x_j\) merge through the reset, where
\(x_j\) is permanent, giving length \(3d+1\).  The reset and rail halves of
\(\alpha,\delta\) merge at \(F,E\), giving \(2d+1\).  Seam-common reset
coordinates extend through all \(2d\) internal rail roots, and all other
private reset runs remain of length \(d+1\).  This proves (8.18p) and its
signed complement.

Equation (8.18q) is the interval reflection of one cyclic word.  The anchor
\(b\) makes every \(A_i^\varepsilon\) nonempty; the cyclic run criterion
gives \(D^dA^\varepsilon=Z^\varepsilon\).  Substituting
\(Z_i^-=Z_{-i}^+\) in the defining intersection gives (8.18r), and sliding
union commutes with the same reflection.  Finally, alternation advances one
root around the even \(M\)-cycle in the attachment projection and two roots
in the predecessor projection, which has
\(\gcd(M,2)=2\) components.  Reversing each internal cell interval proves
the incidence isomorphism. \(\square\)

This theorem supersedes every earlier conditional residence or internal
upper/compiler clause for the completed packet.  It proves a closed local
component, not its Catalan planting.

### Proposition 8.10 (exact exterior cut collateral)

Rotate the cycle so a proposed common cut is \(Z_{M-1}Z_0\), and write

\[
 P_t=\bigcup_{a=0}^{t-1}Z_a,\qquad
 S_t=\bigcup_{a=M-t}^{M-1}Z_a,\qquad
 H=P_M=S_M.                                               \tag{8.18s}
\]

If \(X\) is any suffix-union of a fixed left exterior and \(Y\) any
prefix-union of a fixed right exterior, the complete raw crossing ledger is

\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
\text{left cut}&X\cup P_t\longleftrightarrow X\cup S_t\\
\text{right cut}&S_t\cup Y\longleftrightarrow P_t\cup Y
\end{array}
\qquad(1\le t<M).                                         \tag{8.18t}
\]

Intervals spanning the whole packet contribute \(X\cup H\cup Y\) in both
phases.  A pair in (8.18t) collapses exactly when
\(P_t\mathbin{\triangle}S_t\) is contained in the relevant exterior union.
The longest zero run has length \(3d+1\), so every cyclic interval of length
at least \(3d+2\) has union \(H\); this uniform threshold is sharp over all
cuts.  For a fixed cut the two prefix/suffix saturation times can be
smaller.  Thus only \(1\le t\le3d+1\) can contribute a nontrivial raw
exterior pair, and actual casualties are the last-witness differences after
bulk duplicates are removed, not the number of displayed pairs.

At the immediate level the cut omits the same lower/upper pair

\[
 I_*=Z_{M-1}\cap Z_0,\qquad U_*=Z_{M-1}\cup Z_0
                                                               \tag{8.18u}
\]

in both phases.  An upper-exact host must realize \(U_*\) once elsewhere,
and \(I_*\) must be coherently free or reassigned under lower injectivity.
The two support-four pairs in (8.18aa) below are the analogous
prefix/suffix calculation for the bare four-root square; they are not a
claim about the full \(4d+2\)-root packet after its reset path is inserted.

#### Proof

A left-crossing interval meets the first \(t\) packet roots in one phase
and the last \(t\) roots after reversal; the right statement is symmetric.
An interval meeting both sides contains all packet roots.  The collapse
criterion is the elementary identity
\(X\cup P_t=X\cup S_t\) iff
\(P_t\triangle S_t\subseteq X\).  The saturation bound is equivalent to
meeting every positive run, whose complementary zero runs are listed by
(8.18p). \(\square\)

There is also an exact residence-collar test at the chosen cut.  If the cut
splits a nonconstant coordinate's positive run into boundary pieces of
lengths \(a_c,b_c\), reversal swaps those lengths.  If the fixed exterior
contributes one-ages \(\ell_L,\ell_R\) at its two sockets, simultaneous
depth-\(d\) safety in both phases is exactly

\[
 \ell_L+a_c,\ \ell_L+b_c,\ \ell_R+a_c,\ \ell_R+b_c
       \ge d+1,
\]

or equivalently

\[
 \ell_L,\ell_R\ge d+1-\min\{a_c,b_c\}.                    \tag{8.18v}
\]

This is boundary accounting for the topology opener; it does not reopen
the closed packet's residence theorem.

### Theorem 8.10A (unavoidable antecedent cut collateral)

The maximal antecedent \(A\) from Theorem 8.9 has a stronger cut
obstruction.  For every cyclic width \(1\le w\le2d\), the map

\[
 (s,w)\longmapsto\bigcup_{j=0}^{w-1}A_{s+j}               \tag{8.18w}
\]

is injective, and its value has rank

\[
                         r-d+w-1.                          \tag{8.18x}
\]

It has no witness at another width.  Consequently every linear cut deletes
exactly \(w-1\) globally unique internal witnesses at each width
\(2\le w\le2d\), and hence leaves at least

\[
 \sum_{w=2}^{2d}(w-1)=d(2d-1)                             \tag{8.18y}
\]

distinct antecedent-OR values unsupported **inside the packet**.  The
forced-loss rank histogram has \(t\) targets of rank \(r-d+t\) for every
\(1\le t\le2d-1\).

#### Proof

In the four-block normal form of \(A\), every active coordinate is inserted
exactly once and its preceding zero run has length at least \(2d+1\).
During an interval of width at most \(2d\), every extension therefore adds
one coordinate absent from all preceding cells, proving the rank formula.
If two starts differ by \(h\le(4d+2)/2\), the entering coordinate at the
later start, or at the first endpoint beyond the overlap, separates their
unions.  The rank separates different widths.  At any cut exactly \(w-1\)
width-\(w\) cyclic intervals cross, and injectivity makes all of their
values lose their only internal witness. \(\square\)

Thus there is no internally safe common cut and no \(O(1)\) internal
residual.  A global host must duplicate the explicit rank-graded family,
reverse a compatible exterior socket, use a compensating packet, or retain
the cyclic cells through a non-linear contraction.  The theorem does not
say those targets lack witnesses elsewhere in the spanning carrier.  The
debt is \(\Theta(d^2)=\Theta(m)\) at the intended
\(d=\Theta(\sqrt m)\), so it cannot be hidden in an \(O(d)\) prepared
collar; an ambient bulk duplicate/reflection mechanism is load-bearing.  The
stronger formulas

\[
 \min\operatorname {lost}={d(7d+1)\over2},\qquad
 \max\operatorname {lost}={d(7d+3)\over2}
\]

are audited only for \(d\le30\) and are not used as all-\(d\) theorems here.

   increasing first-stage potential/support;
2. one phase-paired opener joins the path to the ambient component without
   a cycle or branch, restores \(U_*\) and every removed lower/owner task,
   and exposes the same labelled global root, terminal and \(\bot\) role;
3. the \(d\)-overlap and raw Ferrers twin bank of Theorem 8.10B are
   installed on their reserved owner positions, all four clipped bank ends
   receive guarded collars or legal ambient continuations, and every
   exterior window not covered by that local bank is represented explicitly;
4. after the internal reflected compiler cells are identified, the
   boundary and exterior hazards admit one common residual compiler;
5. the opened phase banks give the common \(N_0/N_1\) component,
   provider and destination minors of Definition 8.1; and
6. the nested residual rank rows (8.11)--(8.13) hold.

Under these hypotheses Theorem 8.2 plants and extends both phases.  The
packet's internal immediate palettes, residence, all derivative rows,
all cyclic interval decks, three returns and internal compiler transport
come from Theorem 8.9 and are not additional assumptions.  The first-stage
rank rows remain nonautomatic: a residual colour can still lose all
providers.  Likewise, the bounded selector theorem cannot directly
quarantine \(4d+2\) roots under its \(m+1\) guarantee unless
\(4d+2\le m+1\).  At the central rank of
\({\cal B}_{2m+1}\), the local coordinate-supply condition (8.18m) itself
reduces to \(m\ge2d+2\).

This is the fixed-exterior local-switch theorem.  In a reversal-quotient
induction, items 2, 4 and 5 need hold for one oriented complete state only;
the opposite endpoint roles, witnesses and compiler are obtained by
reflecting that state.  The topology join, exterior witnesses and existence
of the one oriented compiler remain real conditions.

There is now an unconditional partial realization of item 1.  In the
middle-levels incidence host, the opened packet lifts to one common
undirected alternating path with \(4d+2\) roots, \(4d+1\) lower vertices
and \(8d+2\) incidence edges.  The small protected-factor theorem extends
it to a spanning \(q_1\) two-factor whenever

\[
                              m\ge8d+4.                    \tag{8.18z}
\]

The same residual undirected completion supports either orientation; the
packet's \(4d+1\) adjacent-upper colours are distinct and its labelled links
form one graphic-independent path.  Thus immediate root/lower-\(q_1\)
planting and the protected Catalan **seed** are closed in this range.
However, the arbitrary residual two-factor need not be upper-surjective,
need not have bounded component count, and reverses the whole containing
component rather than the packet against a fixed directed exterior.
Consequently (8.18z) does not supply the H3 \(N_0\) transversal, topology
opener, exterior ladders or common residual compiler required above.

For \(m\ge18d\), Theorem 8.10B strengthens this partial realization to a
single owner/lower-\(q_1\) two-factor containing the opened packet and both
raw duplicate banks.  Residence still requires all four bank ends to meet
guarded collars or legal ambient continuations.  The two-collar endpoint
layout has the conditional threshold \(m\ge22d\), while four standalone
collars have the conditional threshold \(m\ge26d\).  None of these marginal
factor statements imposes upper-\(q_1\) surjectivity, bounded component
count, exterior all-width coverage or the lower/root/head/graphic
correlation relative to one fixed \(M_0\).

In particular, the disjoint second-facet theorem closes only the endpoint
projection.  If \(\psi(R)\) is the chosen tail and \(\phi(R)\) a second
facet, rooted legality still requires

\[
                    M_0(\psi(R)\cap\phi(R))=\psi(R).       \tag{8.18ag}
\]

Arbitrary injective \(\phi\) can repeat the lower intersections
\(\psi(R)\cap\phi(R)\), so it need not satisfy (8.18ag) or the graphic
forest row.  No degree-only rainbow shortcut is used here; the
Boolean-specific fixed-\(M_0\) lower/root/head/graphic correlation remains
joint.

For completeness, the exact fixed-exterior collateral of the literal
support-four reversal can be written without asymptotics.  If \(X\) is the
union of the owners in the exterior interval immediately to the left of the
module, the only changed prefix targets are

\[
\begin{aligned}
 X\cup K\cup\{x,y\}
    &\longleftrightarrow X\cup K\cup\{x,z\},\\
 X\cup K\cup\{x,y,w\}
    &\longleftrightarrow X\cup K\cup\{x,z,w\}.               \tag{8.18aa}
\end{aligned}
\]

The right cut has the same two pairs with the arrows reversed; intervals
containing the whole module have common union
\(X\cup K\cup\{x,y,z,w\}\).  A pair in (8.18aa) collapses exactly when the
relevant exterior union already contains both \(y\) and \(z\).  Therefore
internal arbitrary-width equality does not imply context-free exterior OR
transparency.

Finally, the two rail orientations are alternative phase banks, not a
serial composition.  The reflected-reverse/forward \(q\)-gon composition
identifies intermediate edges with their opposite orientations and repeats
their owners.  The closed-doubleton theorem therefore forbids unioning or
serially feeding the two square/rail phases through the same ports; the
combined reset contraction must select exactly one orientation in each
host state.

### 8.5 Typed linkage and compiler scope

The endpoint pairs in (8.1) are named.  A generic strict gammoid links a
source set to an unpaired sink bank and may cross the two parity sinks.
Thus endpoint declarations are not yet a return packet.  One of the
following is necessary:

1. three already routed private paths;
2. three type-separated corridor networks whose internal physical resources
   are pairwise disjoint, miss the reset interior, and in which each source
   can reach only its prescribed sink type; or
3. a full pairing-resolved linkage certificate retained as packet state; or
4. one coupled multi-return macro in which shared projected resources cancel
   internally and the selected literal occurrence bank is jointly simple.

On that resource-disjoint type-separated face, the return-routing matroid is
the direct sum of three rank-one strict gammoids.  More generally one may
use one separately proved joint typed-linkage matroid.  A rank-three
independent set chooses one owner return and both parity returns.  Hence the
number three does not itself create three crossing matroids.  The separate
Lane-L theorem
MATH_THEOREM_L_ROLLING_RESET_THREE_RETURN_TYPED_GAMMOID_AND_COMPILER_GATE_20260801.md
gives the exact fixed-table typed-corridor theorem.

The coupled alternative is not a direct sum of three gammoids.  It is one
compound bank and may be contracted only after its full physical
tail/head/owner and payload lift has been proved.  The opposite-seam
Cartesian no-go in Proposition 10.3 shows why this larger alternative is
needed in the Boolean fan.

Compiler relocation remains a different network.  Under the deletion-only
fixed-cap interface, fix the flag table, functional attachment, background
matchings and cap \(\theta\); keep the target set and every nonhazard
incidence unchanged; and require the complete compiler effect of the three
literal returns to be exactly deletion of the occurrence-labelled hazard
cells \(D_{\mathsf A},D_{\mathsf P_0},D_{\mathsf P_1}\).  The exact proved
condition is

\[
 D_{\mathsf A}\cup D_{\mathsf P_0}\cup D_{\mathsf P_1}
       \in I(M_\theta^*),                                    \tag{8.19}
\]

where \(M_\theta^*\) is the dual compiler transversal strict gammoid.  A
single target with three occurrence cells gives the sharp \(U_{2,3}\)
hazard: every proper return subfamily is compiler-safe while the triple is
not.  This is proved and independently audited in
MATH_THEOREM_L_ROLLING_RESET_THREE_RETURN_STRICT_GAMMOID_OBSTRUCTION_AND_PRIVATE_SINK_GATE_20260801.md.

Condition (8.19) may be included in \(N_0\) or \(N_1\) only after its
pullback to the same atom ground has been proved matroidal and aligned with
the other safety rows.  Otherwise it is an additional constraint; for
multi-cell return packets its pullback need not be a matroid at all.
Separate compiler feasibility does not strengthen Theorem 8.2.  For the
closed packet of Theorem 8.9, reflection already identifies every wholly
internal compiler cell and incidence.  A local switch against one frozen
exterior still needs cut-halo, exterior and common-residual compiler
compatibility.  A reversal-quotient induction instead needs one oriented
compiler and its reflected copy, as Section 11 records.

### 8.6 Exact fixed-exterior bridge verdict

The opened reset now connects to the protected-host **gate** exactly as

\[
 \boxed{\text{take the exact resident }(4d+2)\text{-cycle of Theorem 8.9}}
 \longrightarrow
 \boxed{\text{plant its root bank and make one phase-common cut/opener}}
 \longrightarrow
 \boxed{\text{plant the }d\text{-overlap/twin bank and exterior compiler}}
 \longrightarrow
 \boxed{\text{identify the two contracted residual stages}}
 \longrightarrow
 \boxed{\text{apply nested Rado in the common minors}}.       \tag{8.20}
\]

The square of Theorem 8.4 remains the support-minimum central algebra, but
the complete-reversal rail is the proved all-depth physical packet.  Its two

closed.  Equation (8.18z) plants the common immediate-\(q_1\) seed for

phase-aligned residual minors, the exact common-exterior problem remains

No phase-common Boolean topology opener, upper-exact Catalan host

This entire common-minor chain is the exact theorem for a **relative local

## 9. Ternary Boolean-hex rebase of the topology layer

The authenticated ternary Boolean hex has old and new physical phases

\[
 O=\{A\to B,\ C\to D,\ E\to F\},\qquad
 N=\{A\to F,\ C\to B,\ E\to D\}.                              \tag{9.1}
\]

They use exactly the same lower, upper, tail and head resources.  If the
three edges of \(O\) lie on three distinct directed cycles, deleting them
opens

\[
             B\leadsto A,\qquad D\leadsto C,\qquad F\leadsto E,
\]

and \(N\) concatenates these paths into

\[
                  B\leadsto A\to F\leadsto E\to D
                       \leadsto C\to B.                       \tag{9.2}
\]

Thus three cycles become one with no path catalyst.

There is an interface issue before applying this identity to H3.  Sections
2--4 work on a \(2m-1\)-coordinate host and first produce a partial
upper-exact forest, whereas the authenticated hex theorem is stated on the
\(2m\)-coordinate central ordered-diamond host inside a four-resource
matching.  A coning/lift to such a factor, with the protected occurrence
labels retained, is not a consequence of \(N_0\) or \(N_1\).  The following
statement therefore starts with that factor as an explicit hypothesis.

### Definition 9.1 (guarded cycle-triple hypergraph)

Let \(F\) be an occurrence-labelled four-resource matching, and let
\({\cal C}(F)\) be the set of directed cycle components in its physical
projection.  The guarded cycle-triple hypergraph \({\cal H}^{\rm grd}(F)\) has
vertex set \({\cal C}(F)\).  A labelled hyperedge is an authenticated
Boolean hex for which

1. its old phase \(O\) is literally contained in \(F\), with its three old
   edges on the three named distinct cycles;
2. replacing \(O\) by \(N\) meets no protected reset atom and preserves
   every declared marked occurrence, palette, residence, all-width OR and
   compiler token; and
3. the toggled factor remains in the declared prospective factor class.

If additional guard tokens are not private, a **guarded matching** means a
component-disjoint hyperedge family whose complete token union is also
payload-compatible.  Ordinary matching in the underlying 3-graph is enough
only on the hereditary private-token face.

### Theorem 9.2 (exact guarded ternary contraction)

If \({\cal I}\) is a guarded matching of \(s\) hyperedges in
\({\cal H}^{\rm grd}(F)\), toggling all its packets produces another
guarded four-resource factor and

\[
                              c(F')=c(F)-2s.                  \tag{9.3}
\]

Consequently a matching covering all but \(r\) current cycles gives

\[
                      c(F')={c(F)+2r\over3}.                 \tag{9.4}
\]

If every reachable factor with \(c>2\) has a guarded matching of size

\[
                 \nu_{\rm grd}({\cal H}^{\rm grd}(F))
                        \ge\left\lfloor {c\over3}\right\rfloor, \tag{9.5}
\]

and the same hypothesis regenerates after the simultaneous toggle, then
\(O(\log c)\) rounds leave cycle debt one when \(c\) is odd and cycle debt
two when \(c\) is even.

#### Proof

Component-disjoint authenticated hexes have disjoint old typed supports,
and each new phase uses exactly its old four-resource support.  Their
toggles therefore commute.  Equation (9.2) changes three cycle components
to one on every chosen footprint, proving (9.3).  If \(3s=c-r\), (9.4)
follows.  Under (9.5), \(r\le2\), so
\(c_{t+1}\le(c_t+4)/3\).  This reaches at most two in logarithmically many
rounds.  Every round subtracts an even number, hence preserves cycle-count
parity. \(\square\)

For a safety matroid \(N_{\rm safe}\) on the occurrence-labelled packet
ground, the one-round condition (9.5) has the exact integer form

\[
\begin{aligned}
 \sum_{P:C\in\kappa(P)}x_P&\le1 &&(C\in{\cal C}(F)),\\
 \sum_{P\in A}x_P&\le r_{N_{\rm safe}}(A)
                                  &&(A\subseteq{\cal P}(F)),\\
 \sum_Px_P&\ge\lfloor c/3\rfloor,\qquad
 x_P\in\{0,1\}.                                             \tag{9.6}
\end{aligned}
\]

Here \(\kappa(P)\) is the packet's component triple.  The occurrence labels
cannot be collapsed: two hexes on the same triple may have different
residence, OR or compiler footprints.  If those footprints are merely
capacity-one tokens rather than one safety matroid, (9.6) is the
corresponding literal set-packing system.

For a switch-ready reset host, apply Definition 9.1 to the two phase factors
on their literally identified exterior.  A **phase-paired guarded
hyperedge** is a pair of hex packets whose contractions preserve that
identification and whose projected difference remains (8.4c).  A common
guarded matching satisfying (9.5) in the phase-paired hypergraph may be
toggled in both factors, and Theorem 9.2 then commutes with Theorem 8.2.
Separate near-perfect matchings in the two phase hypergraphs do not suffice.

Equation (9.5) is now the exact topology supply target.  It is not a Rado
rank row.  Matchings of a 3-uniform hypergraph do not form a matroid: for

\[
 e_a=\{1,2,a'\},\qquad e_b=\{2,3,b'\},\qquad
 e_c=\{3,4,c'\},
\]

\(\{e_b\}\) and \(\{e_a,e_c\}\) are matchings, but neither member of the
second augments the first.  Even a tripartite realization is a common
independent-set problem for three partition matroids.  Payload conflicts can
add further crossing systems.  Hence the two-stage protected Rado theorem
does not manufacture (9.5); the live route is a prospective
matching/absorption theorem in the literal guarded hypergraph.

A bare perfect component matching need not be protected.  On nine cycles,
let three component-disjoint packets partition the cycles and give them
three distinct compiler hazard cells adjacent to one protected target.  The
unprotected 3-graph has a perfect matching, while the safe-deletion matroid
is \(U_{2,3}\), so the protected matching number is two.  This is the same
triple hazard as (8.19).  The standard Aharoni--Haxell sufficient condition
also cannot certify a near-perfect one-shot bank at this density: for
\(k\approx c/3\) rank-three task families it demands matching number
greater than \(3(k-1)\), while the component shore caps it at
\(\lfloor c/3\rfloor=k\).

The phrase “only cycle parity at most two remains” is therefore conditional on
the regenerative quantifier in (9.5).  One near-perfect first-round matching
proves only (9.4).  Also, the even terminal pair still needs one bounded
opening/parity sidecar.  When that sidecar is the packet of Theorem 8.9,
its returns, closed residence, internal OR decks and internal compiler
incidences are already exact.  Phase-common contraction, the topology cut,
exterior cross-windows and the residual compiler remain separate unless
they were included in the guard.

Nor does bounded cycle debt mean that the standard Boolean factor is
connected.  If it has \(M\) middle vertices and \(N\) selected edges, every
degree-at-most-one component is a path or cycle and

\[
 \#\{\hbox{path components, isolates included}\}=M-N.         \tag{9.7}
\]

For the outer-exact \(2m\)-coordinate factor,
\(M=\binom{2m}{m}\), \(N=\binom{2m}{m-1}\), and (9.7) equals
\(\operatorname {Cat}_m\).  Closed hex toggles preserve this number.
Therefore Theorem 9.2 removes the large **cycle** obstruction, while the
Catalan path-connector row remains.

### Theorem 9.3 (opened-hex H3 contraction)

There is a distinct H3 use which acts directly on path components.  Suppose
three \(Q_0\)-components have endpoint data

\[
 K_A:(h,b)=(B,A),\qquad K_C:(D,C),\qquad K_E:(F,E),
\]

and their three virtual closures \(A\to B,C\to D,E\to F\) are the old phase
of an authenticated hex.  Its new atoms induce the quotient arcs

\[
 K_A\to K_E,\qquad K_C\to K_A,\qquad K_E\to K_C.              \tag{9.8}
\]

The full set (9.8) is a directed 3-cycle, so it is not an \(N_1\) bank:
it violates the DAG condition, has no \(\bot\), and consumes all three
providers.  Omit any one arc.  The remaining two arcs form a directed
length-two path, merge the three \(Q_0\)-components into one, and leave the
omitted old lower colour as the unused provider of the fused terminal.

More generally, let \({\cal I}\) be a matching of \(s\) path-component
triples with such opened hexes.  Choose one cut per triple so that

1. the \(2s\) retained atoms lie in their literal \(N_1\) destination
   menus, use distinct sources, destinations and unused lower providers,
   and lie in one fixed DAG;
2. their union is independent in the represented payload matroid and
   avoids, or has a phase-paired certificate through, the reset bank; and
3. each omitted lower colour is reserved as the terminal provider of the
   fused component.

Then the \(2s\) atoms are a legal prescribed \(P_1\) bank and make the exact
update

\[
        C\longmapsto C-2s,\qquad
        |{\cal A}|\longmapsto |{\cal A}|-2s.                  \tag{9.9}
\]

After contracting this bank, the residual rank inequalities (4.6) are
necessary and sufficient for completing the rooted Hamilton chronology
inside the declared \(N_1\) atlas.

#### Proof

Within each triple, deleting one arc from (9.8) leaves one directed path
through all three components, using two of their three unused providers.
Different triples are component-disjoint, and the hypotheses give all
remaining \(N_1\) and payload rows.  Hence the union is a DAG bank of
disjoint paths.  Each triple replaces three path components and three
unused providers by one of each, proving (9.9).  The last assertion is
Theorem 4.1 after contraction. \(\square\)

If the opened-hex triple supply regenerates with a matching covering all but
at most two current path components, Theorem 9.3 reaches one component from
odd \(C\) and two from even \(C\) in \(O(\log C)\) rounds.  In the even
case one ordinary guarded seam plus the single \(\bot\)-assignment finishes
the path topology.  This opened form does **not** inherit the full
four-resource equality \(O=N\), because one new atom is omitted.  Its
immediate-upper, residence, full-OR and compiler rows must therefore be
certified in \(N_1\); they do not follow from the closed hex theorem.

## 10. Coordinate covers and median-expander transfer

### 10.1 What the Chapter 9 coordinate cover actually supplies

For a palette parameter \(H\), put

\[
       q=\lceil2H\log H\rceil,\qquad s=q(q+1)+1.              \tag{10.1}
\]

The two-sided coordinate-cover lemma gives fixed maps

\[
                 f,g:[H]^s\longrightarrow[H]^s
\]

such that, for every \(x,y\in[H]^s\), some \(d\in[s]\) satisfies

\[
                 x_d=f(y)_d\qquad\hbox{or}\qquad
                 y_d=g(x)_d.                                \tag{10.2}
\]

The accompanying palette packing gives many equal-sized palettes \(P,Q\)
with at least \(s\) labels in each of \(P\setminus Q\) and
\(Q\setminus P\).  In its Ramsey application, the first alternative in
(10.2) chooses a distinct label \(a_d\in Q\setminus P\), and the second a
label \(b_d\in P\setminus Q\).  These are two choices of active endpoint
palette.  They are not, without a new lift, the two phases of a reset or two
orientations of a physical return path.

For the reset, encode the three endpoint states
\(\mathsf A,\mathsf P_0,\mathsf P_1\) into **one bundled alphabet symbol**
at every coordinate, relabel that finite product alphabet as \([H]\), and
then choose \(q,s,f,g\) for this bundled value of \(H\).
Applying (10.2) separately to three words can choose three different
coordinates and three incompatible sides.  It proves only three marginal
nonempty cells.

For bundled endpoint words \(X,Y\), define the raw coordinate cells

\[
 {\cal D}(X,Y)=
 \{(+,d):X_d=f(Y)_d\}\cup
 \{(-,d):Y_d=g(X)_d\}.                                      \tag{10.3}
\]

For \((\sigma,d)\in{\cal D}(X,Y)\), let
\({\cal R}_{\sigma,d}(X,Y)\) be the menu of **literal compound packets**
whose one common coordinate and side simultaneously supply the named
attachment return and both correctly signed predecessor returns.  The
preferred raw entries are the complete-reversal reset--rail packets of
Theorem 8.9; the square of Theorem 8.4 is their minimum central cell but is
not resident for \(d\ge2\).  A raw packet becomes eligible only after the
root planting, common cut/opener and exterior guards of Corollary 8.11 have
been supplied.  A more general coupled non-Cartesian module may be admitted,
but a triple of private direct corridors or a pair of independent Cartesian
ears is not an entry.  A packet is eligible only when it

1. is an occurrence-labelled full tail--head--owner lift of all three
   paths, with the correct pairing and one synchronized reset phase;
2. is full-functional and bi-contractible in the sense of Definition 8.1;
3. is simple on every literal lower, upper, owner and marked-target
   capacity after its declared alternating removals;
4. retains Theorem 8.9's internal palette, residence, derivative, OR and
   compiler-incidence isomorphisms through its topology opening;
5. explicitly covers the exterior ladders (8.18t), the cut erosion halo,
   and the \(d(2d-1)\) rank-graded loss family (8.18y) by ambient duplicate
   witnesses or a reflected/compensating payload; and
6. has one common residual compiler after the cut-halo hazards are added.

Within the coordinate-cell architecture, the exact universal lift condition
is therefore

\[
 \forall X,Y:\qquad
 \bigcup_{(\sigma,d)\in{\cal D}(X,Y)}
       {\cal R}_{\sigma,d}^{\rm elig}(X,Y)\ne\varnothing.      \tag{10.4}
\]

The coordinate-cover lemma proves only
\({\cal D}(X,Y)\ne\varnothing\).  A clean sufficient lift is to supply an
eligible compound packet for every raw cell.

### Theorem 10.1 (bundled coordinate-cover reset interface)

Assume (10.4), and assume the common residual phase matroids of every
eligible packet pass the nested rank conditions (8.11)--(8.13).  Then every
bundled reset endpoint-state pair has a switch-ready protected H3 host.
Conversely, any host obtained through this coordinate-cell architecture
selects an eligible member of the union in (10.4).

#### Proof

Equation (10.2) makes \({\cal D}(X,Y)\) nonempty, and (10.4) selects one
literal three-return packet on a single synchronized cell.  Contract its
selected phase banks, delete the used task classes, and apply Theorem 8.2.
The converse reads the coordinate and packet used by the asserted
architecture. \(\square\)

The phrase “separated palettes” in Theorem 10.1 must mean separation after
projection to physical resources.  Let \(B_i^\varepsilon(e_i)\) be the
selected full-turn half of return \(i\in
\{\mathsf A,\mathsf P_0,\mathsf P_1\}\), and let
\(\mu_{\rm ext}^\varepsilon\) count the exterior after the alternating old
halves have been removed.  If \(o(t)\) is the literal owner of turn
occurrence \(t\), the exact one-copy row includes

\[
 \mu_U^\varepsilon=
 \mu_{\rm ext}^\varepsilon(U)+
 \sum_i\bigl|\{t\in B_i^\varepsilon(e_i):o(t)=U\}\bigr|
 \le1                                                        \tag{10.5}
\]

for every physical owner \(U\), with equality in a completed owner factor.
The analogous multiplicity is exactly one for every protected marked-target
token.  Repeated upper values on explicitly unmarked connector turns do not
claim a marked-deck token, but they do not relax (10.5) or any other literal
capacity row.

### Proposition 10.2 (sharp palette-quotient owner obstruction)

Write the adjacent reset endpoints as

\[
             T_0=I\cup\{a\},\qquad T_{N-1}=I\cup\{b\}.
\]

Their only rank-\((r+1)\) common owner is

\[
                         U=T_0\cup T_{N-1}.                  \tag{10.6}
\]

Take three \(1\times1\) return matrices, one for each return type, and give
their cells three disjoint abstract palette labels.  If every cell is the
direct closure on these endpoints, all three cells project to the same
owner fibre.  In the physical owner partition matroid,

\[
 r_{\rm owner}
   (E_{\mathsf A}\cup E_{\mathsf P_0}\cup E_{\mathsf P_1})
                        =1<3.                                \tag{10.7}
\]

Thus every row and column is covered and the abstract palettes are disjoint,
but no physical triple exists.

#### Proof

Any rank-\((r+1)\) set containing both adjacent \(r\)-sets contains their
union, which already has rank \(r+1\), proving uniqueness in (10.6).  The
rank cut (10.7) follows.  Adding a fresh type coordinate would make the
putative owner rank \(r+2\), so it cannot split the owner while preserving
the same endpoints and immediate-owner rank. \(\square\)

The two signed predecessor closures additionally share the unique lower
colour \(T_0\cap T_{N-1}\), the unique upper colour (10.6), and form the
closed doubleton.  The authenticated Cartesian Boolean-hex fan cannot be
used to evade this obstruction with three private ears: its displayed
orientation has zero head--owner attachment current, while target-centred
ears for the two parity directions both consume those same lower and upper
resources.  The viable coordinate-cover target is therefore one coupled
owner-changing macro or longer nonlocal compound corridor, not three formal
copies of the direct seam or three private target-centred hexes.

### Proposition 10.3 (opposite-seam single-ear common-residual no-go)

Even using one Cartesian **gain ear** separately in each reset phase cannot
produce a common contracted exterior.  For the forward seam
\(e_\to=(L,U,E,F)\), a fan choice \(b\in L,c\notin U\) has old-pair tail
set

\[
             {\cal T}_\to(b,c)
                =\{U-b,\ L-b+c+d\}.                          \tag{10.7a}
\]

For the reverse seam \(e_\leftarrow=(L,U,F,E)\), a choice
\(b'\in L,c'\notin U\) has

\[
             {\cal T}_\leftarrow(b',c')
                =\{U-b',\ L-b'+c'+a\}.                       \tag{10.7b}
\]

These unordered tail sets are unequal for every pair of choices.
Consequently none of the \((m-1)^4\) products consisting of one standard
Cartesian ear per orientation is an eligible coordinate-matrix cell in
(10.4).

#### Proof

The first member of (10.7a) cannot equal the second member of (10.7b),
because equality of their parts outside \(L\) would force \(c'=d\in U\).
Similarly the second member of (10.7a) cannot equal the first member of
(10.7b), since that would force \(c=a\in U\).  Equality would therefore
match first members, forcing \(b=b'\); the second members would then require
\(\{c,d\}=\{c',a\}\), impossible because \(a\ne d\) are the respective
unique elements in \(U\setminus L\). \(\square\)

This obstruction occurs before owner, target, residence or compiler tests.
Accordingly every coordinate cell in Theorem 10.1 must reserve a coupled
multi-ear module, a non-Cartesian owner-changing open path, or a larger
alternating module whose phase-private tail differences cancel internally.
A pair of independently selected single ears is not a phase-paired packet.

### 10.2 What the Chapter 3 median normalization transfers

The Chapter 3 argument is a component-**injection** theorem, not a
Hamilton-fusion theorem.  The following finite form isolates exactly what
can be imported.

Let a finite token set \(V\) have an original partition \({\cal Q}\) and an
ambient partition \({\cal A}\).  For \(x\in V\), put
\(M(x)=|Q(x)|\).  Suppose a graph \(H[A]\) is a
\(\gamma\)-edge-expander on every \(A\in{\cal A}\).  Choose a
vertex-weighted median \(m_A\) of \(M\) on \(A\), and define

\[
 F(x)={M(x)\over M(x)+m_A},\qquad
 {\cal E}=\sum_{xy\in E(H)}|F(x)-F(y)|.                       \tag{10.8}
\]

For \(0<\delta<1/2\), all but at most
\({\cal E}/(\gamma\delta)\) vertices satisfy
\(|F(x)-1/2|\le\delta\).  Two such vertices in the same ambient block have
component-size ratio at most

\[
                  \rho_\delta=
             \left({1+2\delta\over1-2\delta}\right)^2.        \tag{10.9}
\]

Now let \(\tau\) be a permutation of \(V\), put
\({\cal P}=\tau{\cal Q}\), and suppose every \(P\in{\cal P}\) is an
\(\alpha\)-expander in a transported graph.  Let \(b\) be the number of
transported edges crossing between original \({\cal Q}\)-blocks.  For
\(P\), choose \(D(P)\in{\cal Q}\) maximizing \(|P\cap D(P)|\) and put
\(L(P)=|P\setminus D(P)|\).  Let

\[
 h=|\{x:A(x)\ne A(\tau x)\}|.
\]

### Theorem 10.4 (finite median-expander injection)

Fix \(0<\eta<1\) with
\(\rho_\delta<2(1-\eta)\).  Retain \(P=\tau Q\) when

1. \(L(P)\le\eta|P|\); and
2. some \(x\in Q\) has \(\tau x\in P\cap D(P)\), with
   \(x,\tau x\) outside the exceptional set from (10.9) and in the same
   ambient block.

Then \(P\mapsto D(P)\) is injective on the retained blocks, and the total
vertex mass of all unretained source blocks is at most

\[
 {\mathsf B}=
 {2b\over\alpha\eta}+
 {h+2{\cal E}/(\gamma\delta)\over1-\eta}.                    \tag{10.10}
\]

#### Proof

Expansion inside \(P\), summed over transported components, gives
\(\sum_P L(P)\le2b/\alpha\).  Hence high-loss blocks have total mass at
most \(2b/(\alpha\eta)\).  For a low-loss block with no witness,
\(\tau^{-1}(P\cap D(P))\) lies in the \(h\) ambient-crossing vertices or in
one of the two median-exceptional sets.  These preimages are disjoint as
\(P\) varies and have size at least \((1-\eta)|P|\), proving (10.10).
For a retained block, (10.9) and
\(|P\cap D(P)|\ge(1-\eta)|P|\) give
\(|P\cap D(P)|>|D(P)|/2\).  Two disjoint transported blocks cannot both
occupy a strict majority of one \(D(P)\), proving injectivity. \(\square\)

Permutation conservation is load-bearing here.  It converts a one-sided
bound on \(F(\tau x)-F(x)\) into a two-sided \(L^1\) energy bound because
\(\sum_x(F(\tau x)-F(x))=0\).  A partial H3 connector relation has no such
identity; completing it arbitrarily may add illegal turns.

### Corollary 10.5 (conditional partial Catalan fusion)

Let \(C=|{\cal Q}|\).  Suppose each retained nonfixed assignment
\(Q\mapsto D(\tau Q)\) is already a literal terminal-to-initial connector
atom with a distinct unused lower provider, and suppose the complete atom
bank is payload-compatible and avoids the protected reset components.  If
\(f_0\) retained assignments are fixed or quarantined, then one can select
a directed linear-forest connector bank with at least

\[
                         {C-{\mathsf B}-f_0\over2}            \tag{10.11}
\]

edges.

Indeed, the injection gives indegree and outdegree at most one.  Delete one
arc from each directed cycle; a nontrivial cycle has length at least two, so
at least half of the nonfixed arcs remain.  The resulting bank fuses that
many Catalan components and may be contracted in \(N_1\).

This corollary is only partial.  H3 currently supplies neither the two
uniform expander decompositions nor a literal socket-token permutation
\(\tau\).  An \(o(W)\) exceptional **vertex mass** can still meet
\(\Theta(C)\) small Catalan components; since
\(C=\operatorname {Cat}_m=2W/(m+1)\), a component-count result needs error
\(o(W/m)\), or a separate lower size/balanced-socket hypothesis.  Majority
overlap does not create terminal sockets, provider injectivity, a prescribed
root, an ordered Hamilton chronology, or transparent upper/collar/compiler
tokens.

Finally, Corollary 10.5 does not itself produce authenticated Boolean hex
triples.  To feed Theorem 9.2, the retained injection data must be grouped
into literal, guard-transparent three-cycle hexes and must satisfy the
regenerative near-perfect matching condition (9.5).  That is a new
three-uniform supply theorem; median normalization alone neither states nor
proves it.

## 11. Global-reversal quotient and monotone \(q\)-port fusion

The connector-terminal Rado theorem of Section 4 is one exact way to join
the Catalan components.  A second route is now visible: start from an
upper-exact directed **cycle factor** and repeatedly use only the
upper-support-monotone fusion direction of the resident \(q\)-port packet.
This route does not start from the path forest \(Q_0\); production of the
upper-exact cycle factor, including the fixed-\(M_0\)
lower/root/head/graphic correlation, is its first explicit hypothesis.

### 11.1 Global reversal removes the phase-common intersection

An oriented complete state consists of the source word, every derivative
row, the selected upper-witness bank, the occurrence-labelled lower
compiler, the ordered endpoint/socket data and all carried guards.  Let
\({\cal R}\) reverse every occurrence address, exchange left and right
endpoint roles and reflect every selected certificate.

### Theorem 11.1 (reversal-quotient gauge)

Suppose the admissible state class and the same-parity transition relation
are closed under \({\cal R}\), and suppose every reversal orbit has at least
one representative with a transition to an admissible child.  Then it
suffices to construct one oriented child state.  Reversing that complete
state gives the opposite certificate bijectively:

\[
 D^j({\cal R}A)_i=(D^jA)_{n-j-1-i},                       \tag{11.1}
\]

and the same interval reflection transports residence, erosion, every upper
witness and every compiler edge.  In particular,

\[
                         [H_0]=[H_3],\qquad[H_1]=[H_2].    \tag{11.2}
\]

No perfect matching in a fixed-address intersection
\(G^+\cap G^-\) is required; one terminal compiler and its reflection
suffice.

For a quantitative induction, the charged length and carried sidecar bound
must also be reversal-invariant.  This extra hypothesis is unnecessary for
the purely existential certificate transport above.

Global reversal changes neither component count nor any unsupported target.
It does not restore intervals deleted by opening a cycle, and with \(c\)
independently oriented components it removes only the common phase bit.
The \(c-1\) relative bits and every named one-sided socket remain real.

#### Proof

Reflection sends an interval \([i,j]\) to
\([n-1-j,n-1-i]\) without changing its member sets.  This proves (11.1)
and bijects every structure defined from intervals or occurrence incidence.
The orbit statement is then the standard choice-of-representative induction.
Component count and target deficiency are isomorphism invariants, while
the phase vector is quotiented only by addition of the all-one vector.
\(\square\)

Thus the monotone fusion may be carried out as \(H_2\to H_3\) in one
representative; its reflection is the equally monotone fusion
\(H_1\to H_0\).  The lossy split \(H_0\to H_1\) is never needed.

### 11.2 One guarded fusion round

Fix \(q\ge3,d\ge1\), and put

\[
                         \ell=2d+2,\qquad p=q\ell.          \tag{11.3}
\]

For an oriented resident \(q_1\)-simple cycle factor \(F\), form the
component hypergraph whose vertices are its directed cycles.  A hyperedge
is a **closed authenticated \(q\)-port packet** when its \(q\) components
are literally the \(H_2\) rail cycles and the replacement is the displayed
\(H_3\) state.  A family is guarded when its component footprints and
typed resources are disjoint and its complete one-oriented
upper/compiler/exterior payload is jointly feasible.

### Theorem 11.2 (guarded one-round fusion packing)

Let \(F\) have \(c\) components, and let a guarded matching contain \(s\)
closed packets.  Put

\[
                              u=c-qs.
\]

Switch all packets from \(H_2\) to \(H_3\).  The switches commute and give
a factor \(F'\) with

\[
 c'=s+u=c-(q-1)s={c+(q-1)u\over q}.                       \tag{11.4}
\]

The complete root, lower, immediate-upper, tail and head resource sets are
unchanged with coefficient one.  Every component remains depth-\(d\)
resident, and for componentwise interval support as a **set**,

\[
                              \operatorname {Deck}(F)
                                  \subseteq\operatorname {Deck}(F').
                                                               \tag{11.5}
\]

If a terminal lower compiler for \(F'\) and every declared exterior ticket
are part of the guard, the resulting oriented state is admissible.  Its
globally reversed certificate follows from Theorem 11.1.

#### Proof

Component-disjoint packets have disjoint old factor atoms.  Their old and
new phases have identical four-resource signatures, so the simultaneous
replacement is a factor.  Each packet replaces \(q\) cycles by one, giving
(11.4).  The resident-rail theorem proves the run assertion, and
\(\operatorname {Deck}(H_2)\subseteq\operatorname {Deck}(H_3)\) proves
(11.5) after adjoining the untouched components.  The compiler and exterior
claims are exactly the stated guard. \(\square\)

The inclusion (11.5) is ungraded.  Multiplicities already change at width
\(d+3\), and neither derivative/source cells nor a lower compiler are
transported by the nonlinear fusion unless separately certified.

For the reset arity \(q=2(d+1)=\ell\), one selected state uses

\[
                   p=q^2=4(d+1)^2                         \tag{11.6}
\]

root positions and the same number of lower, upper, tail and head
resources.  One oriented old/new catalogue has \(q(q-1)\) common rail atoms
and \(2q\) alternative direct atoms, hence

\[
                    q(q+1)=2(d+1)(2d+3)                   \tag{11.7}
\]

atom tickets.  The noncore coordinate supply is \(q+d+1=3d+3\), and the
known \(2m\)-ground realization requires \(m\ge3d+1\).  None of these counts
includes arbitrary exterior witnesses, compiler cells or the final-opening
twin bank.

### 11.3 The weakest exact hierarchy condition

A **guarded relative socket forest** is a rooted forest on the initial
components with the following occurrence-labelled data at every internal
node \(v\).

1. After all descendants are fused, the \(q_v\) child outputs are the
   current distinct cycle components named at \(v\).
2. Each child exports one oriented old atom
   \(e_{v,i}=A_{v,i}\to B_{v,i}\).  These atoms form a literal balanced
   \(q_v\)-gon, and the replacement permutation is one \(q_v\)-cycle.
3. The replacement is four-resource exact, and the resulting component has
   a certified depth-\(d\) boundary-run/antecedent state.
4. If \(v\) is not a forest root, its output contains the named parent
   socket in the required orientation, and no descendant or sibling switch
   deletes that occurrence.
5. Every required upper target whose current witness is destroyed has a
   new internal occurrence or an occurrence-labelled ambient ticket which
   survives all later scheduled fusions, or is explicitly recertified at
   the next fusion which modifies it.  Simultaneous tickets satisfy the
   actual one-copy matching/Rado conflicts.
6. Incomparable nodes have disjoint current typed resources.  An
   ancestor--descendant overlap is allowed only through the declared
   output-to-input socket and its complete guard state.
7. The final oriented state has the required lower compiler.  If every
   intermediate state must itself be admissible, the same condition is
   imposed after every node.

Condition 5 has an exact Hall form on a private ticket bank.  If \(r_v(T)\)
is the still-unpaid number of copies of target \(T\), and \(C_v(T)\) is its
surviving ticket menu, then

\[
 \left|N({\cal A})\right|
       \ge\sum_{T\in{\cal A}}r_v(T)
       \qquad(\text{every target family }{\cal A}).          \tag{11.8}
\]

When tickets have crossing physical resources, (11.8) must be replaced by
the represented Rado/conflict rank condition; marginal Hall is not exact.
For terminal-only upper exactness, temporary debt is permitted and the same
condition is imposed once on the final unpaid ledger.

### Theorem 11.3 (guarded postorder fusion theorem)

A guarded relative socket forest can be applied in postorder.  It produces
one cycle for each forest root, preserves the complete immediate palettes,
depth-\(d\) residence and every required upper target, and ends with the
declared one-oriented lower compiler.  If the forest has \(c\) leaves,
\(r\) roots and uniform arity \(q\), then the number of fusion nodes is

\[
                         I={c-r\over q-1},\qquad
                         c_{\rm final}=c-I(q-1)=r.          \tag{11.9}
\]

Conversely, every successful state-by-state protected no-split fusion
schedule induces such a laminar component forest, with its actually
consumed sockets and actual witness/compiler certificates.  Hence the
guarded relative socket forest is the weakest exact prospective condition
for state-by-state protected fusion.

#### Proof

At a node, postorder has already produced its named child components and
Condition 4 exposes the literal old sockets.  The \(q_v\)-gon topology
fuses them, Conditions 3 and 5 transport residence and upper support, and
Condition 6 prevents interference.  Induction proves the forward statement
and summing the \(q-1\) decrements proves (11.9).

Conversely, component partitions only coarsen under fusion.  The historical
blocks are therefore laminar and form a rooted forest.  Label each internal
node by the old sockets actually consumed, the output socket actually
created and the certificates actually used.  These data satisfy the seven
conditions. \(\square\)

Relative orientations are part of the socket data.  Writing
\(\epsilon_v\) for an output phase and \(\tau_{vi}\) for the required
child-to-parent offset gives

\[
                         \epsilon_{v_i}+\epsilon_v=\tau_{vi}
                         \quad\text{in }{\mathbb F}_2.       \tag{11.10}
\]

On a tree without absolute pins these equations are soluble uniquely up to
global complement.  Named one-sided sockets are feasible exactly when their
prescribed parities agree along every connecting path.  Global reversal
removes the one common complement bit, not an inconsistent relative phase.

### Corollary 11.4 (regenerating matching recurrence and support)

Suppose the admissible oriented class regenerates after every guarded batch,
and at round \(t\) its selected matching leaves \(u_t\le B\) components
uncovered, where \(B\ge1\).  Then

\[
 c_{t+1}={c_t+(q-1)u_t\over q},\qquad
 c_t\le B+(c_0-B)q^{-t}.                                  \tag{11.11}
\]

Thus \(O(\log_q c_0)\) regenerative rounds reduce to \(O(B)\) components.
Every hierarchy ending with \(r\) components uses exactly
\((c_0-r)/(q-1)\) packets.  At reset arity its cumulative selected-state
packet charge is

\[
 {4(d+1)^2\over2d+1}(c_0-r)
   =\left(2d+3+{1\over2d+1}\right)(c_0-r),                 \tag{11.12}
\]

and its two-phase catalogue charge is

\[
 {2(d+1)(2d+3)\over2d+1}(c_0-r).                          \tag{11.13}
\]

These are only the **formal** occurrence/ticket budgets obtained by charging
one full frozen packet at every abstract internal node.  They equal distinct
physical support only for a disjoint realization.  The frozen packets cannot
form such a nested hierarchy, while a generalized socket node has no proved
\(q^2\) footprint; any temporal reuse or replacement packet needs the
state-compatible socket and support proof in Theorem 11.3.

### 11.4 Mixed \(q=3,4\) loose combs at Catalan scale

The fixed reset arity is unnecessary for bulk component arithmetic.  Put

\[
 W_m=\binom{2m-1}{m},\qquad
 C_m=\operatorname {Cat}_m={2W_m\over m+1}.                \tag{11.14a}
\]

A **serial loose comb** is a sequence of component hyperedges
\(E_1,\ldots,E_N\), of sizes three or four, such that \(E_1\) uses only
fresh component labels and every later \(E_t\) meets the preceding union in
one relay label and introduces \(|E_t|-1\) new labels.  It is
**hereditarily sealed at depth \(d\)** when:

1. every component--hyperedge incidence carries its own oriented old socket,
   and toggling one authenticated \(q\)-gon deletes no later socket;
2. after every prefix is fused, the aggregate component exposes the next
   relay socket with its complete run-age state;
3. every required cyclic interval target whose witness crosses that socket
   has a surviving noncrossing witness or an occurrence-labelled repair
   ticket which survives, or is recertified at, the next modifying fusion;
4. all immediate resources and simultaneous repair tickets obey their
   literal one-copy/Rado conflicts, and the relative socket phases are
   consistent; and
5. the final orientation has one terminal compiler.

Condition 3 is the **hereditary cut signature**.  It is stronger than
regenerating a \(q_1\) edge: Corollary 6.3 of the rail theorem proves support
monotonicity only for the frozen \((2d+2)\)-root port cycles.  A larger
aggregate can have unique targets crossing its exported socket.

### Theorem 11.5 (guarded mixed loose-comb fusion)

A hereditarily sealed serial loose comb may be fused in its displayed
order.  Every step meets \(q\) distinct current components, preserves the
complete immediate palettes and its certified residence/upper payload, and
leaves the next relay socket available.  If it has \(c\) leaf components,
\(r\) output components, \(N_3\) ternary packets and \(N_4\) quaternary
packets, then

\[
                       c-r=2N_3+3N_4.                     \tag{11.14b}
\]

For \(c\ge3\), \(r=1\) is achieved with the \(q=4\)-dominant counts

\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c}
c\bmod3&N_4&N_3&N_3+N_4\\ \hline
1&(c-1)/3&0&(c-1)/3\\
0&(c-3)/3&1&c/3\\
2,\ c\ge5&(c-5)/3&2&(c+1)/3 .
\end{array}                                               \tag{11.14c}
\]

These counts minimize both the number of packets and the full-standard-core
owner census.  They have \(N_4\equiv c-1\pmod2\), so the permutation sign
has no further obstruction.  The only exceptional count is \(c=2\).

#### Proof

After the first toggle, suppose the preceding union is one current
component.  The next loose edge uses its one relay socket there and one
socket on each of \(q-1\) untouched components.  Conditions 1--4 make the
toggle legal and preserve the next interface.  Induction proves the
protected statement, while summing the decrements gives (11.14b).

Equation (11.14c) is checked in the three residue classes and attains
\(\lceil(c-1)/3\rceil\), the lower bound because one packet reduces the
component count by at most three.  Also

\[
 3N_3+4N_4=(c-1)+(N_3+N_4),
\]

so the same table minimizes a private standard-core census.  Reducing
(11.14b) modulo two gives the sign assertion. \(\square\)

The comb may be ordered with its at most two ternary nodes first.  Thus a
full branching regeneration theorem is not needed for this arithmetic: the
only nontrivial serial interface types are

\[
                         3\to3,\qquad3\to4,\qquad4\to4.    \tag{11.14d}
\]

This is a reduction of the missing theorem, not its proof.  In particular,
the frozen packet supplies none of these composable interfaces.

### Corollary 11.6 (exact formal census and asymptotic capacity)

Put \(\ell=2d+2\).  Charging one disjoint full-standard core to every comb
node gives the formal owner count

\[
 B_{\rm core}=\ell(3N_3+4N_4)
 =\ell\begin{cases}
 (4c-4)/3,&c\equiv1\pmod3,\\
 (4c-3)/3,&c\equiv0\pmod3,\\
 (4c-2)/3,&c\equiv2\pmod3,\ c\ge5 .
 \end{cases}                                               \tag{11.14e}
\]

The same census holds separately for every immediate typed palette.  If the
new cut/run/exterior guard at each node uses at most \(\gamma d\) additional
protected owners, and a guarded standalone four-collar realization of the
terminal reset opening uses its bound \(13d+2\), then

\[
 B_{\rm total}\le B_{\rm core}
       +\gamma d(N_3+N_4)+13d+2.                           \tag{11.14f}
\]

For \(c=C_m\),

\[
 B_{\rm core}< {8\over3}(d+1)C_m,\qquad
 {B_{\rm total}\over W_m}
 \le {16(d+1)+2\gamma d\over3(m+1)}
       +O_\gamma\!\left({d\over W_m}\right).               \tag{11.14g}
\]

Consequently a constant-\(\gamma\) protected comb has

\[
                    B_{\rm total}=O(C_md)=o(W_m)           \tag{11.14h}
\]

exactly in the regime \(d=o(m)\); at the intended
\(d=\Theta(\sqrt m)\) its protected fraction is \(O(m^{-1/2})\).

This proves scalar owner and immediate-palette capacity only.  It does not
construct the \(\gamma d\)-bounded hereditary cut signatures, distribute
sockets among possibly tiny components, solve their Rado conflicts, or
enforce the fixed-\(M_0\) lower/root/head/graphic row.  The current
small-protected-subgraph theorem also does not plant a
\(\Theta(C_md)\)-sized prescribed bank.

\(48{,}568/48{,}568\) residual demands.  A protected-preserving \(C_6\)

### 11.6 Exact cut--contract--rejoin interface

There is a second route from a nonresident cycle factor to resident atoms.
Its cut and seam choices are joint; minimizing the cut set before exposing
the endpoint atlas is not proof-safe.
On a directed owner cycle \(T_0,\ldots,T_{n-1}\), let gap \(i\) lie between
\(T_i\) and \(T_{i+1}\).  A maximal positive coordinate run
\(T_a,\ldots,T_{b-1}\) which is not constant on its whole component and has
cyclic length at most \(d\) defines the circular gap interval

\[
                         I=[a-1,b-1].                     \tag{11.14k}
\]

Let \({\cal I}_d(F)\) be all such intervals over the factor and let
\(\tau_d(F)\) be their minimum transversal number, componentwise.

### Theorem 11.7 (joint residence contraction and coloured endpoint gate)

Let \(D\) hit every interval of \({\cal I}_d(F)\).  Delete the corresponding
factor adjacencies.  Every resulting owner path is depth-\(d\) resident and
has a nonempty maximal antecedent whenever the owner rank exceeds \(d\).
If a cycle receives no cut, add one arbitrary opening cut; writing \(u(D)\)
for the number of such cycles, the number of contracted path atoms is

\[
                           a(D)=|D|+u(D).                   \tag{11.14l}
\]

For each oriented path atom retain its two endpoint owners, every protected
cut casualty, and its finite truncated coordinate-age transition map of
width \(d\).  The latter records, coordinatewise, ages
\(0,1,\ldots,d,d^+\), whether the whole atom is one, and whether a forbidden
maximal run has already closed.  Form the **state-expanded coloured endpoint
packet master** as follows.  A candidate
from the right end of atom \(P\) to the left end of atom \(Q\) is present
exactly when:

1. the two owners form a legal directed Johnson seam;
2. it carries an input/output age-state transition, and the selected
   transitions around every successor component are consistent and accept;
3. its lower and upper immediate colours respect their required capacities;
4. every old arbitrary-width witness destroyed by the cuts has a surviving
   or newly selected occurrence ticket; and
5. its lower/compiler cell and every named guard are literal.

Clone resources when a capacity exceeds one.  Then a protected resident
cycle cover of the path atoms in this construction class is exactly a
feasible solution of this master: its packet row is a rainbow matching which
chooses one packet for every right endpoint and uses every left endpoint
once, while its shared orientation and age-state rows link the incoming and
outgoing choices of each atom.  The state row is essential when an atom is
all-one in a coordinate, because a run may traverse several seams.  It
collapses to pairwise seam tests only under an explicit sealing hypothesis.
The cover is one chronology exactly when the induced successor permutation
is one cycle; alternatively, reserve one root opening and a guarded loose
fusion forest on the selected cycles.

Consequently the exact optimized cut parameter for this route is

\[
 \tau_d^{\rm join}(F)=
 \min\{|D|:D\text{ hits }{\cal I}_d(F)
       \text{ and the complete endpoint master for }D\text{ is feasible}\}.
                                                               \tag{11.14s}
\]

It may be strictly larger than \(\tau_d(F)\).  A theorem may choose among
minimum alternatives only after proving that at least one supports the
boundary-age/colour matching.

#### Proof

The interval-stabbing criterion says that an unhit interval is precisely an
old short run left internal to one piece, while every run internal after all
intervals are hit already had length at least \(d+1\).  This proves the
first statement and (11.14l).

In a rejoining, each atom has one successor and one predecessor.  Record
each selected seam together with all resources in Conditions 1--5; this is
the packet matching, and the actual coordinate ages give a feasible state
assignment.  Conversely, distinct incoming endpoints and one choice from
every outgoing list give a permutation of all atoms.  Propagating the
linked truncated states around each successor component proves that no
newly closed maximal run has length at most \(d\), including runs traversing
all-one atoms.  Conditions 3--5 give the declared palettes, witnesses and
compiler.  Connectivity is exactly the one-cycle condition on that
permutation. \(\square\)

This is an exact contraction, not a claim that ordinary bipartite Hall is
enough.  The cut-alternative variables, the shared orientation of each
atom's incoming and outgoing incidences, and the seam variables belong to
one master.  On any fixed globally consistent orientation/state sheet,
after padding packet footprints to size \(s\), the
Aharoni--Haxell expansion condition

\[
 \nu\!\left(\bigcup_{P\in{\cal X}}{\cal H}_P\right)
        >s(|{\cal X}|-1)
       \qquad(\varnothing\ne{\cal X}\subseteq{\cal P})     \tag{11.14m}
\]

is a sufficient Boolean theorem for the resource-disjoint cycle-cover row.
Here \({\cal H}_P\) is the menu
of complete resource footprints for the outgoing end of \(P\), and
\(\nu\) is physical matching number.  A simpler but weaker list criterion
is \(L>3e\Delta\), where every menu has size at least \(L\) and every packet
conflicts with at most \(\Delta\) packets in other menus.  Neither follows
from uncoloured endpoint Hall: three endpoint/lower/upper partition rows
already have the Borromean obstruction, and connectivity is an additional
graphic row.  Thus (11.14m) must either be imposed on menus whose successor
choices are already connected, or be followed by the one-cycle/fusion row
of Theorem 11.7.

There is, however, an exact deterministic route to a vanishing cut density.
Write each factor transition as

\[
              T_{i+1}=T_i-\{\alpha_i\}+\{\beta_i\},
\]

and for \(1\le t\le d\) put

\[
 M_t(F)=\#\{i:\beta_i=\alpha_{i+t}\},                    \tag{11.14n}
\]

with indices read on their own factor component.  Every short positive run
of length \(t\) contributes one of these lag-\(t\) coincidences.  Therefore,
if the initial factor is \(A\)-lag-spread,

\[
                         M_t(F)\le {AW\over k}
                         \qquad(1\le t\le d),             \tag{11.14o}
\]

then

\[
 {\,\tau_d(F)\over W}\le {Ad\over k},\qquad
 {a(D)\over W}\le {Ad\over k}+{c(F)\over W}.              \tag{11.14p}
\]

For a Catalan-scale factor \(c(F)=O(W/k)\), this is \(O(d/k)\), hence
vanishing at \(d=\Theta(\sqrt k)\).

The proof is only the injection of every short run into its entering edge
and its first leaving edge, followed by
\(\tau_d(F)\le|{\cal I}_d(F)|\).  The hypothesis is the substantive Boolean
mixing row.  Immediate-palette completeness alone does not imply it.  At the
opposite extreme, a packing of \(\beta W\) pairwise disjoint defect
intervals forces \(\tau_d(F)\ge\beta W\), so a bounded-away cut density is
possible unless lag-spread or another anti-clustering theorem is imposed.

Choosing one legal gap from every defect interval gives, whenever the

\[
 \tau_d(F)\le adC_m,\qquad

\]

### 11.7 Recuttable SCD packets and the false boundary-cube identity

The Catalan SCD forest has a structural advantage over the seven-cycle

\(C_m=W_m-U_m=\operatorname {Cat}_m\) paths.  If \(D\) cuts \(t\) of

\[
 P=C_m+t,\qquad
 \#\{\text{raw missing lower-}q_1\text{ colours}\}
       =W_m-(U_m-t)=P.                                  \tag{11.14v}
\]

This is the exact lower square.  It also has a functional interpretation.
After restricting the coherent root injection to the uncut edges, the
terminal-root and source-root banks both have size \(P\).  A forward
connector from terminal root \(a\) into source root \(s\) has lower colour
\(a\) exactly when \(a\subset M_0(s)\) and \(a\ne s\).  Thus lower colour
is source-private on this face.

There is no analogous upper identity.  Let \(A(F)\) be the total number of
holes of the uncut forest in ranks at least \(m+2\), and let \(B_F(D)\) be
the number of distinct previously present deep values whose every internal

containment give the disjoint formula

\[
 H_F(D)=t+A(F)+B_F(D),\qquad
 P-H_F(D)=C_m-A(F)-B_F(D).                            \tag{11.14w}
\]

Consequently \(H=P-2^d\) is equivalent to the additional cut-dependent

### Theorem 11.8 (closed functional-slot repair)

On a functional-site atlas, the minimum number of unrepaired targets is
exactly

\[
 \delta=\max_{{\cal X}\subseteq{\cal Z}}
             \bigl(|{\cal X}|-|N({\cal X})|\bigr).          \tag{11.14y}
\]

In particular, if an exceptional bank has size at most \(b\), every other
target has at least \(L\) sites, and every site supports at most \(L\)
targets, then all but at most \(b\) targets have simultaneous closed
repairs.  A protected bank deleting \(p\) sites is harmless if the first
degree lower bound is strengthened to \(L+p\).

#### Proof

Functional-site disjointness makes compatible packet families exactly the
matchings of the target--site graph, so defect Hall gives (11.14y).  For the
degree/load condition, double count edges from any nonexceptional target
set \({\cal X}\):

\[
                   L|{\cal X}|\le e({\cal X},N({\cal X}))
                      \le L|N({\cal X})|.                \tag{11.14z}
\]

Hall applies.  Removing \(p\) sites lowers each target degree by at most
\(p\). \(\square\)

Singleton support alone has no bounded-defect consequence: \(n\) targets
all adjacent to one site have one local repair each and matching defect
\(n-1\).  If different sites still conflict through several resource rows
or through the final graphic support, Theorem 11.8 is inapplicable; the
problem remains a coloured hypermatching plus a graphic row, not a generic
matroid intersection.

\(\min\{N,|X|+h\}-a\ge|X|\).  Conversely, for
\(|X|\le N-h\), delete \(h\) left vertices outside \(X\) and choose \(h\)
right vertices meeting \(N(X)\) as much as possible; robustness forces
\(|N(X)|\ge|X|+h\).  For
\(|X|>N-h\), delete all \(N-|X|\) left vertices outside \(X\); robustness
against the same number of right deletions forces \(N(X)=R\).

### Theorem 11.8A (protected small-bank absorption)

Let \({\cal Z}\) be an upper-zero bank and let \({\cal S}\) be a bank of
closed functional absorber sites.  Suppose:

1. the target--site graph satisfies Hall;
2. each absorber covers one credited target, closes every new palette/deck
   casualty internally, preserves the declared boundary state and protected
   bank, and after contraction removes at most \(a\) balanced functional
   pairs;
3. absorbers at distinct selected sites have disjoint complete site
   footprints and commute; and
4. every core atom avoiding the contracted pairs remains legal with the same
   orientation and labels in one fixed graph \(G\) satisfying (11.14ab) for
   \(h=a|{\cal Z}|<N\).

Then all targets in \({\cal Z}\) can be absorbed simultaneously and the
residual functional problem still has a perfect matching.  If one absorber
uses at most \(\sigma\) owner positions, the extra protected charge is at
most

\[
                            \sigma|{\cal Z}|.           \tag{11.14ac}
\]

Hall first selects distinct absorber sites.  Their contraction deletes at
most \(h\) balanced core pairs, and (11.14ab) supplies the residual matching.
This proves the theorem.  For constant-size packets, an \(o(N)\) zero bank
has \(o(N)\) cost.  For resident facet-scale packets
\(\sigma=O(d)\), the exact requirement is

\[
                            d|{\cal Z}|=o(N).           \tag{11.14ad}
\]

The hypotheses are sharp in scope.  Three perfect projections do not imply
one common matching: the four atoms

\[
 (s_1,h_1,a),\ (s_1,h_2,b),\
 (s_2,h_1,b),\ (s_2,h_2,a)
\]

have perfect matchings on every pair of shores, but the two source--head
perfect matchings repeat respectively \(a\) and \(b\).  Also a perfect
functional core need not be one-deletion robust.  The later zero-singleton
bank closes the 187/78 support layer but realizes the same three-resource
warning as a literal bow tie, so it still supplies no common oriented core.
Theorem 11.8A applies only after that coloured selector has been constructed,
and only to boundary-changing packets; canonical self-closing refinements use
the stronger zero-damage lift (11.14ae).

There is a precise conditional interpretation of the canonical gap eight.
If a protected path occupies \(b\) pieces and its \(b-1\) internal arcs are
contracted, there remain \(P-b+1\) component atoms and \(P-b\) connector
tasks.  When \(H(D)\le P-b\), inject the hole bank into those tasks and pad

### 11.8 Sharp obstructions to deriving the hierarchy from the packet

There are three independent no-go statements.

First, the frozen packet has height one.  Each \(H_2\) input component is
the complete rail-plus-seam cycle on \(\ell=2d+2\) roots.  Its vertices are
already saturated in a two-factor.  The \(H_3\) output has \(q\ell\) roots,
so it is neither a whole input component of a later fixed-\((q,d)\) packet
nor capable of containing a proper selected subcycle.  Global reversal
maps it to \(H_0\), still one component.  Returning to the \(q\)-component
orbit would require the forbidden split \(H_0\to H_1\).  Hence the literal
theorem supplies one round, not socket regeneration.

Second, component hypergraph supply is insufficient.  On \(2q-1\) cycles,
take two individually legal \(q\)-packets whose component footprints meet
in one component and whose old phases use the same literal atom there.
Their footprints form a loose hypertree.  Whichever packet fires first
deletes the shared old atom and does not restore it, so the other packet is
disabled.  Arithmetic predicts two merges and one final component, but the
process stops after one merge with \(q\) components.  Thus laminarity or
hypergraph connectivity without occurrence-level socket
regeneration/exclusivity is not sufficient.

Third, every fixed-\(q\) fusion preserves

\[
                         c\pmod {q-1}.                     \tag{11.14}
\]

The least positive abstract remainder is

\[
                 \rho_q(c)=1+((c-1)\bmod(q-1)).             \tag{11.15}

guarantee dimension-uniform \(O(1)\) components.  Conditional regenerative

additional \(q=3\) fusions, with formal full-standard-packet charge at most

\[
                              6d(d+1)=O(d^2).               \tag{11.16}
\]

It removes the arithmetic remainder but not the socket-regeneration,
exterior or compiler gates.

Finally, even a successful cyclic hierarchy must be opened into the final
chronology.  The \(d\)-overlap and Ferrers twin bank of Theorem 8.10B repay
