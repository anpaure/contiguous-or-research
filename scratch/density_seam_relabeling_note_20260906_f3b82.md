# Two-pass inventories and seam-inclusive independent-relabeling barriers

Date: 2026-09-06. Status: proved probabilistic obstructions and deterministic
cut bounds. **No solution to the coefficient-one conjecture is obtained,
and no globally correlated construction is excluded.**

The almost-cover route seeks words of length `(1+o(1))W(k)` missing only
`o(2^k)` nonempty targets, where `W(k)=binom(k,floor(k/2))`. Its equivalence
to the full conjecture after sublinear dimension extension is recorded in
`../MASTER_HANDOFF.md`, Section 3.9, and
`../MATH_THEOREM_SUBLINEAR_DIMENSION_CYLINDER_COMPLETION_20260905.md`.
That equivalence is context, not a premise of the proofs below.

The independent-wreath Gaussian coupon calculation already appears in
`../MWC_SUFFICIENT_REDUCTION_AND_RANDOM_BARRIER_AUDIT_20260725.md`, Section 3.
Here the two-pass inventory is exact, and the word-level obstruction counts
**all literal intervals, including intervals crossing block seams**.

## 1. Definitions and conclusions

Letters are nonempty subsets of `[k]`. A cyclic word of period `N` uses
intervals of lengths `1,...,N`. Write `U_s(A)` for its distinct rank-`s`
interval unions, `M_s=binom(k,s)`, and

\[
 h_I(A)=\sum_{s\in I}(M_s-|U_s(A)|),\qquad
 h(A)=\sum_{s=1}^k(M_s-|U_s(A)|).
\]

Put
\[
 \beta=\frac{1+\log2}{2},\qquad
 \Psi(t)=\frac1{\sqrt\pi}\int_{\mathbb R}
                 e^{-u^2-t e^{u^2}}\,du\quad(t>0).
 \tag{1}
\]

Two conclusions will be proved. All asymptotics are as `k -> infinity`.

* For `P` independent uniform permutation passes, concatenated cyclically,
  if `N=Pk=(c+o(1))W(k)`, `0<c<infinity`, then for every fixed `epsilon>0`,
  \[
   \Pr\{h(A)<(\Psi(c\beta)-\varepsilon)2^k\}
      \le e^{-\Omega_{c,\varepsilon}(W(k)/k)}.
   \tag{2}
  \]
* More generally, independently uniformly relabel deterministic set-word
  blocks, each having union `[k]`, and concatenate them in a prescribed
  cyclic order. If their lengths satisfy
  \[
   N=\sum_j\ell_j=(c+o(1))W(k),\qquad
   L=\max_j\ell_j=o(W(k)),
  \]
  then
  \[
   \Pr\{h(A)<(\Psi(c)-\varepsilon)2^k\}
      \le e^{-\Omega_{c,\varepsilon}(W(k)/L)}.
   \tag{3}
  \]

Numerically, `Psi(beta) approximately 0.290015662` and
`Psi(1) approximately 0.239268784`. These are lower bounds, not assertions
that the hole densities converge to those constants. Linear concatenations
have at least as many holes as their cyclic closures.

## 2. Exact two-pass inventory

Fix a permutation `pi` and take an independent uniform permutation `tau`.
For `1<=s<k`, let `G_s(pi,tau)` be all rank-`s` unions of intervals starting
in `pi`, with ends allowed in either pass. Set `H_j=sum_{i=1}^j 1/i` and
`H_0=0`. Then

\[
 \boxed{D_{k,s}:=\mathbb E|G_s(\pi,\tau)|
       =k-s+1+(k-s)(H_{k-1}-H_{k-s}).}
 \tag{4}
\]

**Proof.** At start `i` in the first pass, stop on seeing the `s`-th distinct
coordinate and call the union `T_i`. Every rank-`s` union at that start is
this same set, so the inventory is the support of `T_1,...,T_k`.

Repeated outputs form consecutive runs. Two internal length-`s` windows
of a permutation are distinct. An internal window equal to a later crossing
target must contain the last coordinate of `pi`, hence must end at that last
position. In this case, or when the earlier witness already crosses the
seam, suppose `T_i=T_j=T`, `i<j`, and let `e_j` be the later witness endpoint.
The suffix of `pi` from `i` and the prefix of `tau` through `e_j` lie in `T`.
Thus `[i,e_j]` has union `T`. For every `i<=a<=j`, `[a,e_j]` both contains
the later witness and lies inside `[i,e_j]`, so its union is `T` and its
first rank-`s` union is also `T`. Consequently
\[
 |G_s|=k-\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}{\bf1}_{\{T_i=T_{i+1}\}}. \tag{5}
\]

Let the first-pass suffix at `i` have size `a`, and let `x` be its first
coordinate. If `a>s`, the two outputs are distinct internal windows. If
`2<=a<=s`, remove `x` and call the remaining suffix `R`, of size `a-1`.
At the next start, the output is `R` together with the first `s-a+1`
coordinates of `tau` outside `R`. These form a uniform subset of that size
in a set of size `k-a+1`. The old and new outputs agree exactly when that
subset contains `x`: in that case its other coordinates are precisely the
first `s-a` coordinates of `tau` outside `R union {x}`. Therefore
\[
 \Pr(T_i=T_{i+1})=\frac{s-a+1}{k-a+1}\quad(2\le a\le s).
\]
Taking expectations in (5) gives
\[
 D_{k,s}=k-\sum_{a=2}^s\frac{s-a+1}{k-a+1},
\]
which is (4).

**One-pass qualification.** Applying (4) to a cyclic permutation-pass word
requires `P>=2`. For `P=1`, the next pass is the same permutation, not an
independent one: the cyclic rank-`s` inventory has size `k`. For example,
at `k=3,s=2` it is `3`, whereas (4) is `5/2`. The asymptotic hypothesis in
(2) automatically gives `P -> infinity`.

## 3. Avoidance by conditional induction

Let events `E_1,...,E_P` have an ordinary dependency graph: each `E_i` is
independent of the entire sigma-algebra generated by its other nonneighbors.
Suppose `0<=x_i<1` and
\[
 \Pr(E_i)\le x_i\prod_{j\in\Gamma(i)}(1-x_j). \tag{6}
\]
Then
\[
 \Pr\Bigl(\bigcap_i E_i^c\Bigr)\ge\prod_i(1-x_i). \tag{7}
\]

**Proof.** Induct jointly on the size of an avoidance set to show that all
such conditioning events have positive probability and that
\[
 \Pr(E_i\mid\bigcap_{j\in S}E_j^c)\le x_i\qquad(i\notin S).
\]
The empty-set case follows from (6). Positivity for sets of size `m` follows
by multiplying conditional avoidance probabilities with conditioning sets
of size less than `m`. For `|S|=m`, split `S=R dotcup T`, where `T` comprises
the neighbors of `i`. If `T` is empty, independence proves the bound.
Otherwise independence and the induction hypothesis give
\[
 \Pr(E_i\mid\text{avoid }S)
 \le\frac{\Pr(E_i\mid\text{avoid }R)}
           {\Pr(\text{avoid }T\mid\text{avoid }R)}
 \le\frac{\Pr(E_i)}{\prod_{j\in T}(1-x_j)}\le x_i.
\]
The denominator is expanded sequentially; every conditioning set used
there has size at most `m-1`. Multiplying the final conditional avoidance
bounds proves (7). This proof uses joint, not merely pairwise, independence
from nonneighbors.

## 4. All literal intervals of independently relabelled blocks

Let `B_1,...,B_P` be deterministic nonzero blocks, each with union `[k]` and
length `ell_j>=1`. Independently choose uniform coordinate permutations
`sigma_j` and form the cyclic word `A=sigma_1 B_1 ... sigma_P B_P`.
For a proper rank `s`, let `F_{j,s}` be the inventory of **all** rank-`s`
interval unions whose starts lie in block `j`, and put
\[
 d_{j,s}=\mathbb E|F_{j,s}|\le\ell_j.
\]
The inequality holds because unions from any one start are nested.

Fix a rank-`s` target `T` and set `E_j={T in F_{j,s}}`. For `P>=2`, a proper
target cannot include a complete intervening block. Thus `E_j` depends
only on `sigma_j,sigma_{j+1}`; **all** proper witnessing intervals are
accounted for. Simultaneous coordinate relabelling preserves the law, so
\[
 \Pr(E_j)=d_{j,s}/M_s. \tag{8}
\]
The dependency graph has maximum degree two. At `P=2` it is a single edge,
not a claim of independence between the two events. At `P=1`, there is one
event depending only on `sigma_1`; (8) and the general argument below still
hold with its actual cyclic inventory, not the two-independent-pass formula.

Write `L=max ell_j`, `N=sum ell_j`, and suppose `M_s>=8L`. Set
\[
 x_j=\frac{d_{j,s}}{M_s-4L},\qquad y=\frac{L}{M_s-4L}\le\frac14.
\]
Since every neighborhood has at most two distinct vertices,
\[
 x_j\prod_{i\in\Gamma(j)}(1-x_i)
 \ge\frac{d_{j,s}(M_s-6L)}{(M_s-4L)^2}
 \ge\frac{d_{j,s}}{M_s}.
\]
The last inequality is equivalent to `2L(M_s-8L)>=0`. Apply (7) and
`log(1-x)>=-x/(1-x)` to obtain the finite, nonuniform bound
\[
 \boxed{\Pr(T\notin U_s(A))
 \ge\exp\left(-\frac{\sum_jd_{j,s}}{M_s-5L}\right)
 \ge\exp\left(-\frac{N}{M_s-5L}\right).} \tag{9}
\]
For permutation passes with `P>=2`, `L=k` and `d_{j,s}=D_{k,s}`, hence
\[
 \Pr(T\notin U_s(A))
 \ge\exp\left(-\frac{P D_{k,s}}{M_s-5k}\right)
 \qquad(M_s\ge8k). \tag{10}
\]

## 5. Central-band asymptotics and concentration

Fix `A_0>0` and let `I={s: |s-k/2|<=A_0 sqrt(k)}`; for large `k` these
are proper nonzero ranks. Write `z_s=(s-k/2)/sqrt(k)` and `b=|I|`.
Stirling's estimate gives, uniformly in this fixed band,
\[
 \frac{M_s}{W(k)}=e^{-2z_s^2}(1+o(1)),\qquad
 \frac{\sqrt{k}W(k)}{2^k}=\sqrt{\frac2\pi}(1+o(1)). \tag{11}
\]
Also, using `q=k-s` in (4),
\[
 \frac{D_{k,s}}k
 =\frac qk\left(1+\log\frac kq\right)+O(k^{-1})
 =\beta+o(1) \tag{12}
\]
uniformly in this band. If `L=o(W(k))`, then `M_s>=8L` eventually throughout
the band, and `L/M_s=o(1)` uniformly. No growing-band uniformity is being
inferred from `L=o(W(k))` alone.

Suppose `N/W(k) -> c>0`. Summing (9), or (10) for passes, and using (11)
gives
\[
 \frac{\mathbb E h_I(A)}{2^k}
 \ge\sqrt{\frac2\pi}\int_{-A_0}^{A_0}
                e^{-2z^2-\theta e^{2z^2}}\,dz-o(1), \tag{13}
\]
where `theta=c` for general blocks and `theta=c beta` for passes.

Here is the bounded-difference detail. Replacing `sigma_j` affects starts
only in blocks `j-1,j`, at most `a_j=ell_{j-1}+ell_j` positions. For a fixed
rank let `C` be the common inventory of unaffected starts, and `V,V'` the
old and new affected inventories. Both have size at most `a_j`, so
\[
 \bigl||C\cup V|-|C\cup V'|\bigr|
 =\bigl||V\setminus C|-|V'\setminus C|\bigr|\le a_j. \tag{14}
\]
Their symmetric difference can have size `2a_j`; (14) bounds the **count**,
which is what the hole statistic needs. For `P=1`, counting its starts
twice only overestimates the bound. Thus the bounded-difference constants
for `h_I` are at most `b a_j`, and
\[
 \sum_j(b a_j)^2\le4b^2\sum_j\ell_j^2\le4b^2LN.
\]
The bounded-difference inequality for independent variables yields
\[
 \Pr\{h_I\le\mathbb E h_I-t\}
 \le\exp\left(-\frac{t^2}{2b^2LN}\right). \tag{15}
\]
For `t` a fixed positive multiple of `2^k`, (11) and `b=O(sqrt(k))` make
the exponent `-Omega(W(k)/L)`. For passes it is `-Omega(W(k)/k)`.

Given `epsilon>0`, first choose a fixed `A_0` large enough that the integral
in (13) is within `epsilon/3` of `Psi(theta)`; then let `k` grow. Combine
(13)--(15), with `h(A)>=h_I(A)`, to prove (2)--(3). No independence between
ranks is used. Also `N/L -> infinity` under these asymptotic assumptions,
so the exceptional one-block situation is automatically absent there.

## 6. Cyclic pools, cut capacity, and adaptive recombination

This section distinguishes a pool bound from a bound on a literal word.
Let the source blocks now be arbitrary nonzero **cycles**, not necessarily
of full support. Their independently relabelled full cyclic pool is
\[
 \mathcal F=\bigcup_j\bigcup_{s=1}^k U_s(\sigma_jB_j).
\]
For a family, define `h_I(F)=sum_{s in I}(M_s-|F intersect binom([k],s)|)`.
For `d^0_{j,s}=|U_s(B_j)|<=ell_j`, independence gives, when `M_s>L`,
\[
 \Pr(T\notin\mathcal F)
 =\prod_j(1-d^0_{j,s}/M_s)
 \ge\exp\left(-\frac{N}{M_s-L}\right). \tag{16}
\]
The count Lipschitz constant per source is `b ell_j`, by (14), so the sum
of squared constants is at most `b^2LN`. Thus the same fixed-band integral
and concentration hold for pool holes, with `theta=c`. Equation (16) alone
does **not** bound the holes of a concatenation: its cross-block intervals
may cover targets outside the pool.

**Cut capacity.** At any fixed cut, at most `s` distinct rank-`s` targets
have an interval witness crossing it. The distinct nonempty left suffix
unions form a chain with at most `s` members of size at most `s`. For each
one, unions with the right prefix chain supply at most one rank-`s` set.
This also proves the cyclic version: split a crossing interval at the cut
into a left suffix and right prefix. The total-length restriction only
removes pairs from these two chains.

Take `R` nonempty contiguous pieces, each from one source cycle and of
length at most that source's period, and concatenate unchanged copies of
their letters into a word `C`. Reversals, repetitions, overlaps, and all
choices made after observing the relabellings are allowed. Every witness
inside one piece belongs to the **full cyclic** pool, even if that piece
crosses the source cycle's presentation cut. Every target outside the pool
must cross a new join. There are `R` joins for a cyclic output, or `R-1`
for a linear output. Therefore, simultaneously for every such choice,
\[
 \boxed{h_I(C)\ge h_I(\mathcal F)-R\sum_{s\in I}s.} \tag{17}
\]
No union bound over adaptive choices is needed.

There are two important scope restrictions. A designated-window subfamily
cannot replace the full cyclic pool without proving that it contains all
within-piece witnesses. Also, if pieces instead cross different blocks of
an already concatenated cyclic source word with `Q` old block seams,
including its closing seam, those old seams must be charged too. Applying
cut capacity first to the old word, then to the new joins, gives the safe bound
\[
 h_I(C)\ge h_I(\mathcal F)-(Q+R)\sum_{s\in I}s. \tag{18}
\]

For completeness, (17) implies a quantitative fragmentation obstruction.
Here `N` is total sampled source length, not the eventual output length.
Assume `N=(c+o(1))W(k)` and `L=o(W(k))`. For fixed small `A_0>0`, (16) and
concentration give, with probability tending to one,
\[
 h_I(\mathcal F)
 \ge W(k)\sqrt{k}\{2A_0e^{-c}+O_c(A_0^3)-o(1)\},\qquad
 \sum_{s\in I}s=A_0k^{3/2}+O(k). \tag{19}
\]
For every fixed `0<delta<2e^{-c}`, choose `A_0` sufficiently small in (19).
Then some `gamma=gamma(c,delta)>0` satisfies: except with probability
`exp(-Omega_{c,delta}(W(k)/L))`, **every** output using
\[
 R\le(2e^{-c}-\delta)W(k)/k
\]
has at least `gamma 2^k` holes. To obtain the displayed exponential
probability, use the expectation version of (19), make the absolute cubic
error at most `delta A_0/4`, and apply pool concentration with the fixed
deviation `t=(delta A_0/4)W(k)sqrt(k)`. For large `k` the remaining finite
errors cost at most another `delta A_0/4`. Equation (17) then leaves at
least `(delta A_0/4)W(k)sqrt(k)` holes, a fixed positive fraction of `2^k`.

In particular, with probability `1-o(1)` in the fixed-margin sense just
proved, an `o(2^k)`-hole recombination at `c=1` requires
\[
 R\ge(2/e-o(1))W(k)/k. \tag{20}
\]
For precision, for every fixed `delta>0` and every deterministic `eta_k -> 0`,
the probability that some output has `h(C)<=eta_k 2^k` and
`R<=(2/e-delta)W(k)/k` is at most `exp(-a_delta W(k)/L)` for all sufficiently
large `k`, where `a_delta>0`. The vanishing margin in (20) follows by a
diagonal choice; no almost-sure eventual assertion is made.
This is a fragment-count bound, **not** a lower bound on extra word length.
It rules out reordering long independent blocks with `o(W(k)/k)` joins,
but does not exclude a correlated construction or sufficiently extensive
recombination. For the broader pieces in (18), the corresponding charge is
`Q+R`, not `R` alone.

## 7. Independent recheck and corroboration

The finite `P>=2` qualification for the permutation application is the
correction to the earlier presentation. At `P=2` the simple graph has one
edge; the degree-two estimates remain valid. The old/new inventory check
does not require doubling the concentration constants, because (14) is a
cardinality bound rather than a symmetric-difference bound. The fixed-band
order of limits and the old-seam charge in (18) make the other scope issues
explicit. Neither asymptotic constant changes.

not typically supply the almost-cover antecedent. They do not solve the
conjecture, rule out rare good realizations, or exclude globally correlated
permutation, chain-cover, or set-word constructions.
