# Fixed-H typed one-cell sockets: separated common-cap planting and the exact Pascal-host boundary

Date: 2026-08-01  
Lane: A, additive-constant common-cap planting  
Status: exact conditional planting theorem and sharp resource boundary.  The
theorem below does not construct the required prepared cuts in every Pascal
child and does not complete owner/q1 topology.

## 0. Result

For arbitrary disjoint ears, one global maximal-word test is necessary and
sufficient, even when an interval meets several ears and has a frozen
exterior OR contribution.  Its only obstructions are one-row/one-coordinate
positive-cover cores and empty screened positions; there is no higher
subset-Hall inequality in the downward-closed Boolean source alphabet.

When the affected collars are sufficiently separated, the literal
common-cap system is a direct product of the `H` one-ear systems.  Once the
destroyed assignments and complete collateral bank have their certified
returns, each ear costs one inserted source cell, consumes its typed
singleton fan address, and leaves exactly `d-1` other addresses.  Thus the
exact ledger is

\[
 \Delta |A|=H,\qquad
 \Delta|\mathcal B_d|=Hd,\qquad
 \Delta\operatorname{Addr}_{\rm post}=H(d-1).                \tag{0.1}
\]

The complete local semantic bank per ear contains `2d-1` fans, `d-1`
destroyed crossings, and, when every shorter transported crossing is
protected, the triangular collar of size `binom(d-1,2)`.  Hence its row count
is

\[
 R_d=3d-2+{d-1\choose2}={d^2+3d-2\over2}.                  \tag{0.2}
\]

Accordingly, the word-length price is \(O(H)\), the live source/cap
footprint is \(\Theta(Hd)\), and the complete local semantic replay has
\(\Theta(Hd^2)\) rows.  The common-cap theorem adds no further numerical
Hall tax, although one mixed row can still make the whole fixed assignment
infeasible.

There is a dimension-uniform geometric packing theorem: if every one of the
`H` typed tasks has more than

\[
                         (H-1)(6d-3)                         \tag{0.3}
\]

fully certified candidate centres, and centre-separated candidates are
pairwise private in their owner/q1/topology resources, then centres can be
chosen so that no source interval of length at most `d+1` meets two ears.
All local common-cap certificates then compose literally.  Without resource
privacy, an additional explicit conflict-degree term is necessary.

For the currently certified residence-safe endpoint-coatom implementation,
one private collar bank has `rho-1=Theta(d)` labels, where `rho` is the child
positive-run threshold.  `H` private collars therefore use

\[
                            H(\rho-1)                         \tag{0.4}
\]

auxiliary labels and `2H(rho-1)` collar owners.  This is a sufficient
`Theta(Hd)` implementation cost, not a lower bound for every conceivable
planting.  The one-cell word-length cost remains exactly `H`.

No unconditional Pascal-child planting follows from these facts.  The
formal quadratic coatom catalogue has fixed-incumbent fibre one, and the
prepared boundary-chain theorem supplies only two global endpoint banks.
The canonical complementary two-chain coatom glue also fails the one-ear
fan--crossing trace test already at `d=2`; hence the current catalogue does
not even provide one generic fully certified centre to iterate.
The missing theorem is the existence of the certified centre sets in (0.3),
or a serial safe-walk substitute, in one accepting child chronology.

## 1. Prepared occurrence-labelled ears

Fix `d>=2` and a baseline source chronology.  A candidate ear centred at an
insertion gap `c` has affected old-source support

\[
             J(c)=[c-d+1,c+d-1],\qquad |J(c)|=2d-1.          \tag{1.1}
\]

Its declared local rows consist of the fan/crossing rows of the frozen
star-hidden theorem, any selected shorter transported pins, and every owner,
residence, q1 or compiler row which the certificate claims to preserve.  A
row meeting fixed exterior source positions carries their literal frozen OR
contribution `E`; it is not replaced by a wholly local equality.

Call the candidate **cap-certified** for typed target `tau` when the maximal
letters

\[
 K_p=P_p\cap\bigcap_{J\ni p}T_J                            \tag{1.2}
\]

are all nonempty and reproduce every declared row,

\[
                 T_J=E_J\cup\bigcup_{p\in J}K_p.            \tag{1.3}
\]

In particular, (1.2)--(1.3) include the exact fan--crossing coupling

\[
                  Z\cup C_i=L_{i+1}\cup R_{d-i+1},          \tag{1.4}
\]

the typed singleton `A_*=Z=S_tau`, and all selected triangular pins.  Call
the candidate **fully certified** if, in addition, its physical owner and
q1 replay and all declared protected witnesses have been checked.  This
definition deliberately does not infer owner/q1 topology from common-Q.

A family of fully certified candidates is **jointly resource-compatible**
when their introduced owner occurrences, q1 colours, topology ports and
other capacity-one resources satisfy the global multiplicity/injectivity
rows and are compatible with the retained protected resources.  Individual
full certification does not imply joint resource compatibility.

### Theorem 1.1 (exact mixed-row multi-socket common-cap criterion)

Let

\[
                         V=\dot\bigcup_{s=1}^{H}V_s          \tag{1.5}
\]

be the live source positions of the disjoint ears.  Let \({\cal R}\) contain
every assigned local or mixed row.  For \(R\in{\cal R}\), write \(J_R\subseteq
V\) for its live positions, \(E_R\) for the OR of its frozen exterior
positions, and \(T_R\) for its required target.  Let \(P_p\) be the literal
cap at \(p\in V\), and define

\[
              K_p=P_p\cap\bigcap_{\substack{R\in{\cal R}\\p\in J_R}}T_R.
                                                                  \tag{1.6}
\]

There is a word of nonempty letters \(A_p\subseteq P_p\) satisfying

\[
                  E_R\cup\bigcup_{p\in J_R}A_p=T_R
                  \qquad(R\in{\cal R})                    \tag{1.7}
\]

if and only if

\[
 E_R\subseteq T_R\quad(R\in{\cal R}),\qquad
 K_p\ne\varnothing\quad(p\in V),                           \tag{1.8}
\]

and

\[
                  T_R=E_R\cup\bigcup_{p\in J_R}K_p
                  \qquad(R\in{\cal R}).                    \tag{1.9}
\]

When feasible, \(A_p=K_p\) is the unique componentwise maximal word.

Equivalently, for each coordinate \(x\), put

\[
 S_x=\{p\in V:x\in P_p\text{ and }x\in T_R
                    \text{ for every }R\text{ with }p\in J_R\}.          \tag{1.10}
\]

Then (1.8)--(1.9) say exactly:

1. no frozen exterior coordinate lies outside its target;
2. every source position belongs to at least one \(S_x\); and
3. every demanded pair \((R,x)\), \(x\in T_R\setminus E_R\), has
   \(J_R\cap S_x\ne\varnothing\).

Thus fixed-assignment common-cap has no higher subset-Hall obstruction in
the downward-closed Boolean source alphabet.  Every failure has one of three
certificate types: an exterior overflow, an empty screened position, or an
uncovered row-coordinate pair.  Several types may occur simultaneously.

#### Proof

If \(A\) is feasible and \(p\in J_R\), exactness of row \(R\) gives
\(A_p\subseteq T_R\).  Hence \(A_p\subseteq K_p\).  It follows that every
\(K_p\) is nonempty and

\[
 T_R=E_R\cup\bigcup_{p\in J_R}A_p
     \subseteq E_R\cup\bigcup_{p\in J_R}K_p.
\]

The reverse inclusion follows from \(E_R\subseteq T_R\) and (1.6), proving
necessity.  Conversely, (1.8)--(1.9) say directly that \(A_p=K_p\) is
nonzero, cap-legal and realizes every row.  The coordinate formulation is
the same equality read one coordinate at a time.  Since coordinates may
superpose in one source letter and one occurrence may serve several rows,
there is no capacity constraint involving a family of demanded pairs.
\(\square\)

This last conclusion is specific to fixed target-to-row assignments and
arbitrary nonempty bitmask letters.  Target-to-cell injection, fixed-rank
source alphabets, owner degree two and physical q1 resources can reintroduce
matching constraints; they are not part of Theorem 1.1.

### Corollary 1.2 (exact trimming and factorization)

Partition the rows into local rows and mixed rows, where a mixed row meets at
least two ears.  First form the local maximal letters

\[
 K^0_p=P_p\cap
       \bigcap_{\substack{R\text{ local}\\p\in J_R}}T_R,    \tag{1.11}
\]

and the mixed screens

\[
 M_p=\bigcap_{\substack{R\text{ mixed}\\p\in J_R}}T_R.
                                                                  \tag{1.12}
\]

Then the global maximal letter is exactly \(K_p=K^0_p\cap M_p\).
Consequently global feasibility is equivalent to:

1. every trimmed \(K_p\) is nonempty;
2. every one-ear fan, crossing, triangular and carrier reconstruction
   remains exact after replacing its local \(K^0_p\) by \(K_p\); and
3. every mixed row satisfies (1.9).

Assuming every one-ear local system is feasible, the untrimmed local maximal
words compose unchanged if and only if

\[
                 T_R=E_R\cup\bigcup_{p\in J_R}K^0_p
                 \qquad(R\text{ mixed}).                  \tag{1.13}
\]

If no row meets two ears, (1.13) is vacuous and the common-cap system is the
direct product of the \(H\) one-ear systems.

For a typed star \(A_{s,0}=S_s\), the singleton local row already makes its
local maximal letter equal to \(S_s\).  Every mixed row through that star
therefore imposes the necessary condition

\[
 S_s\subseteq P_{s,0}\cap
       \bigcap_{\substack{R:\,0_s\in J_R}}T_R.
\]

This containment is not sufficient by itself: the mixed positive demands
and every other trimmed local reconstruction must still pass Theorem 1.1.

#### Proof

Equation (1.6) splits into the local intersection (1.11) and mixed
intersection (1.12).  Theorem 1.1 then gives the three exact conditions.
If (1.13) holds, every \(K^0_p\) occurring in \(R\) is contained in \(T_R\),
so no mixed screen shrinks it; conversely the unchanged local word can
realize \(R\) only through (1.13).  \(\square\)

### Corollary 1.3 (ordered-interval signature compression)

Assume the ear blocks are ordered on the source line.  A mixed interval
meeting ears \(a,a+1,\ldots,b\) sees a suffix of ear \(a\), every whole
intermediate ear, and a prefix of ear \(b\).  For the local maximal words,
define the corresponding unions

\[
 \operatorname{Suf}_a(R),\qquad
 \operatorname{Tot}_s,\qquad
 \operatorname{Pre}_b(R).
\]

Then its unchanged-product test (1.13) is exactly

\[
 T_R=E_R\cup\operatorname{Suf}_a(R)
       \cup\bigcup_{a<s<b}\operatorname{Tot}_s
       \cup\operatorname{Pre}_b(R).                        \tag{1.14}
\]

Thus all cross-ear interval checks compress to literal prefix, suffix and
total OR signatures.  This is fixed-address equality, not merely inclusion
of OR decks.

## 2. Exact separated-product theorem

### Theorem 2.1 (fixed-H collar-disjoint planting)

Let `c_1,...,c_H` be fully certified centres for pairwise distinct typed
tasks, and suppose

\[
                         |c_i-c_j|\ge3d-1\quad(i\ne j).       \tag{2.1}
\]

For a cyclic source chronology, (2.1) means cyclic distance at least
`3d-1`; all intervals and the proof below are then read modulo the cycle.

Assume every row not protected by an unchanged exterior witness has source
length at most `d+1` and has been included in the appropriate local replay.
Assume also that the selected family is jointly resource-compatible.  Then
the `H` one-cell replacements may be performed simultaneously.  Every
declared cap equality and protected OR witness survives; the global
owner/q1/topology rows hold by the explicit compatibility hypothesis.
The source-length and address ledgers are (0.1), and the full triangular
semantic row count is `H R_d`.

#### Proof

Suppose `c_i<c_j`.  The right end of `J(c_i)` is `c_i+d-1` and the left end
of `J(c_j)` is `c_j-d+1`.  By (2.1), their difference is at least `d+1`.
Therefore the shortest source interval meeting both supports has length at
least `d+2`.  Every unprotected row of length at most `d+1` consequently
meets at most one support.

A literal OR row meeting no support is unchanged.  A row meeting one support is one of
the corresponding literal local equations, with all unchanged source
letters already represented by its frozen contribution `E_J`; its equality
therefore follows from that local certificate.  Nonemptiness is positionwise
and hence local.  The same trichotomy applies to protected OR witnesses.
Owner/q1 resource collisions are not interval equations and are instead
excluded by joint resource compatibility.  Thus the literal edits commute.

One equality insertion adds one source cell.  Its `2d-1` fan cells have one
surplus over the `2d-2` two-chain assignments; typing the common singleton
uses that surplus.  The one-cell exchange has gross credit `d`, so exactly
`d-1` other addresses remain.  Summing over disjoint ears gives (0.1).

Finally, the fans and destroyed crossings contribute `(2d-1)+(d-1)` rows.
The shorter crossing pairs form the strict triangle and number
`binom(d-1,2)`, proving (0.2).  \(\square\)

Long upper intervals are not silently covered by this theorem.  They must
have a witness avoiding all chosen supports or appear in a joint replay.
That is the exact reason for the protected-witness hypothesis.

### Corollary 2.2 (conditional additive-\(H\) Pascal child)

Assume additionally that the terminal middle chronology has length \(W+H\),
contains every middle owner, is depth-\(d\) resident and upper-complete, and
that the old lower compiler has exactly the \(H\) typed targets as its
defect.  If each fully certified ear augments the lower compiler by its
typed target and all other lower assignments are retained or returned, then
there is a universal word of length

\[
                         W+d+H=B(k)+H.                       \tag{2.2}
\]

#### Proof

Theorem 2.1 and Theorem 1.1 give one nonzero literal antecedent realizing
the complete lower assignment.  The terminal chronology supplies every
middle and upper target.  Its source length is
\((W+H)+d=B(k)+H\).  The owner, q1, residence and upper hypotheses are used
here as separate physical rows; common-cap alone does not imply them.
\(\square\)

For fixed \(H\), this is an additive-constant conclusion conditional on the
prepared child.  It is not an existence theorem for that child.

### Corollary 2.3 (uniform fixed-H centre packing)

For each task `tau`, let `G_tau` be a set of fully certified centres, with at
most one candidate per centre.  Assume first that candidates for different
tasks are automatically jointly resource-compatible whenever their centres
are separated as in (2.1).  If

\[
                       |G_\tau|>(H-1)(6d-3)                    \tag{2.3}
\]

for every task, then the centres required by Theorem 2.1 exist.

#### Proof

Choose tasks successively.  One already chosen centre forbids exactly the
integer centre positions at distance at most `3d-2`, at most

\[
                         2(3d-2)+1=6d-3
\]

positions of the next task's list.  Before the last choice, fewer than the
right side of (2.3) positions have been removed.  Greedy selection works.
\(\square\)

The cyclic case is identical: a radius-`3d-2` cyclic ball has at most
`6d-3` positions (and possibly fewer on a short cycle).

More generally, suppose one chosen candidate excludes, outside its geometric
ball, at most \(\Delta_{\rm phys}\) candidates from any later task list by
owner, q1, topology or other capacity-one conflicts.  The same greedy proof
works under

\[
 |G_\tau|>(H-1)(6d-3+\Delta_{\rm phys}).                   \tag{2.4}
\]

In particular, if a child of source length `N` supplies at least `eta N`
resource-private certified centres per task, fixed `H` is plantable whenever
`eta N>(H-1)(6d-3)`.  This is a genuine uniform planting theorem once the
positive-density private-centre hypothesis is proved; raw formal packet
counts do not imply that hypothesis.

## 3. Exact private-coatom resource ledger

The nested coatom collar theorem uses, for child residence threshold `rho`,
one bank

\[
                         U_j=\{u^j_0,\ldots,u^j_{\rho-2}\}   \tag{3.1}
\]

on both shores of packet `j`.  Assume first that the underlying packet owner
sets are pairwise disjoint.  If the `U_j` are also pairwise disjoint and avoid
every protected packet support, the collar proof preserves owner-disjoint
paths and independent boundary states.  Hence the certified private
implementation consumes (0.4) labels and `2H(rho-1)` collar owners.  No
factor two is paid in labels: the two shores of one packet use the same
bank.

The condition is a common-complement condition,

\[
 \left|\Omega\setminus\bigcup_{j=1}^H\operatorname{supp}(P_j)\right|
                         \ge H(\rho-1).                       \tag{3.2}
\]

Separate per-packet slack does not imply (3.2).  For example, if
`supp(P_1)=Omega-A` and `supp(P_2)=Omega-B`, where `A,B` are disjoint sets
of size `rho-1`, then each packet separately has the required slack while
the common complement is empty.

There is no asymptotic label-count obstruction to a deliberately aligned
formal bank once `r>=d+4`.  An endpoint-planted rank-`r` coatom packet uses a common
structural bank `K dotcup F` of size `r-2` and six active labels.  Give `H`
formal packets pairwise-disjoint active six-sets and private collar banks.
If

\[
                         r-2+6H+H(\rho-1)\le k,               \tag{3.3}
\]

then all labels fit in a `k`-coordinate host.  Their packet owners are
pairwise distinct: after deleting the common structural part, every block
or screen owner retains a nonempty active subset belonging to its own
six-set.  Private `u^j_0` labels distinguish all collar owners.  Thus (3.3)
constructs an owner-disjoint **formal protected path forest** for fixed `H`
whenever `rho=o(r)` and `k=2r+O(1)`.

This last statement is not a Pascal-child chronology theorem.  Extending
the forest to one accepting owner path/cycle, retaining q1 and upper decks,
and placing the typed common-Q ears at those occurrences are separate rows.

## 4. Two sharp obstructions

### 4.1 Mixed rows create genuine cross-socket coupling

Disjoint source positions alone do not make common-Q a product.  The
smallest pure positive-cover core has two depth-two ears on the alphabet
`{a,b}`.  On each ear take every source letter and hence every local target
equal to `{a}`.  Each ear is feasible in isolation.  Add one mixed row
meeting both ears, with frozen exterior contribution `E={a}` and target
`T={a,b}`.  Every global maximal letter remains the nonempty set `{a}`, but
the demanded coordinate `b` has no provider.  Thus the local tests pass and
there is no empty position, yet the joint system fails.

The dual empty-position core takes caps/typed values `{a}` at the two
positions and a mixed row with `E=T={b}`.  Intersecting with its target
empties every touched letter.  These examples are minimal in socket count,
depth, alphabet size and mixed-row count.

Thus arbitrary-width mixed rows must be included globally.  Geometric
separation removes this obstruction only for the bounded-span row bank;
protected exterior witnesses remove it for the declared long targets.

### 4.2 A formal menu is not a planted slot bank

After endpoints, core and filler are fixed, one incumbent coatom word
recovers its active labels and lies in exactly one formal endpoint-fixed
catalogue member.  Therefore the quadratic relabelling inventory supplies
neither the centre sets `G_tau` nor the Hall row needed for `H` tasks.
Likewise the two prepared global endpoint banks cannot be counted `H`
times.  These are quantifier obstructions, not shortages in (0.1).

## 5. Exact proved boundary

The fixed-H common-cap and geometric planting question is closed under the
explicit prepared-host hypothesis:

* Theorem 1.1 is the exact global common-Q test for arbitrary mixed rows;
* on the row-separated face it is the product of the local maximal-letter
  systems;
* the exact costs are `H` word cells, `H(d-1)` residual addresses and
  `H R_d` local semantic rows;
* a resource-private certified-centre density exceeding (2.3), or a
  catalogue satisfying the augmented conflict bound (2.4), yields disjoint
  planting greedily;
* the current private residence collar costs `Theta(Hd)` auxiliary labels,
  while no theorem proves this cost necessary outside that architecture.

The minimum remaining Pascal statement is: produce, for every fixed typed
task bank, centre sets satisfying (2.3) in one accepting child, or produce a
serial safe walk whose successive local cap states satisfy (1.2)--(1.4).
Owner degree two, q1 injection, final connected topology and upper-witness
protection are not consequences of the common-cap theorem and remain
explicitly separate.

## 6. Adversarial audit

The proof was checked against the following failure modes.

1. The separation constant is `3d-1`, not `3d-2`: at equality the nearest
   support positions differ by `d+1`, so the shortest interval meeting both
   has `d+2` cells.  A centre distance `3d-2` would permit a length-`d+1`
   row to meet both ears.
2. The forbidden-centre count is `6d-3`, the size of the integer ball
   `|c'-c|<=3d-2`.  The cyclic ball can only be smaller.
3. Formula (0.2) counts semantic target rows only.  It does not count extra
   owner, residence, q1, topology or witness guards; these must be included
   in “fully certified.”
4. The direct-product proof does not apply to an arbitrary long interval
   crossing two ears.  Such a target needs an unchanged external witness or
   one joint mixed-row equation.
5. `H(d-1)` counts residual compiler addresses, not fresh coordinates and
   not an already constructed matching.  Conversely, `H(rho-1)` is the
   auxiliary-label cost of the current private collar, not a word-length
   cost or a universal lower bound.
6. Inequality (3.3) proves only that the protected owner fragments coexist
   as disjoint Johnson paths.  It does not provide their order in one Pascal
   child or an accepting completion.

These checks leave Theorem 2.1 and Corollaries 2.2--2.3 conditional exactly
on their stated prepared-host data; no unconditional additive-constant
conclusion is inferred.
