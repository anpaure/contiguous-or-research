# Cross-slot pentagon couplings: diagonal normal form and finite-state lower bound

Date: 2026-07-26

Method: pure mathematics only.

## 0. Verdict

Let

\[
                              R=\mathbb F_5^k                         \tag{0.1}
\]

be the \(k\)-slot row-label group.  A genuinely coupled ownership overlay
can be described by a finite voltage graph \(B\): its base vertices are
controller states, and a directed base edge \(e:u\to v\) carries a voltage

\[
                              a(e)\in R.                              \tag{0.2}
\]

The lift has vertices \((u,x)\), \(x\in R\), and edges

\[
                              (u,x)\longrightarrow(v,x+a(e)).          \tag{0.3}
\]

If \(H_B\le R\) is the subgroup generated by voltages of closed walks in
\(B\), every connected lifted component has

\[
                              |V(B)|\,|H_B|                            \tag{0.4}
\]

vertices when the base and lift are connected in the evident sense.

There is an exact coefficient-one **macro-token** construction which
synchronizes the whole local D3 ownership catalogue.  If
\(\rho_\gamma\in\operatorname{Sym}(\mathbb F_5)\) is the relative owner
permutation at local \(X\)- or \(Y\)-phase \(\gamma\), assign its global
token at \(x=(x_0,\ldots,x_{k-1})\) from old owner \(x\) to new owner

\[
 \rho_\gamma^\Delta(x)
   =(\rho_\gamma(x_0),\ldots,\rho_\gamma(x_{k-1})).                 \tag{0.5}
\]

The ownership group is the diagonal copy of the finite local group
\[
 G_{\rm loc}=\langle\rho_\gamma\rangle\le S_5.
\]
Thus the catalogue uses exactly the original \(5^k\) row labels and every
overlay component has at most \(|G_{\rm loc}|\le120\) rows.  Since one
\(\rho_\gamma\) is the certified five-cycle, every component projects
uniformly onto all five rows in every slot.  A component \(O\) has tagged
carrier

\[
                    \frac{|O|}{5}\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\Delta_i,              \tag{0.6}
\]

of norm \(\Theta(|O|k)\).

The cyclic part is the one-state voltage loop

\[
                              d=e_0+\cdots+e_{k-1}.                     \tag{0.7}
\]

Its holonomy is \(\langle d\rangle\cong C_5\), so every lifted component
has five rows and the lift uses exactly the original \(5^k\) row labels.
Section 2 gives the literal correlated \(X/Y\) token sets and owner maps.

This is not yet a middle-levels inclusion-path factor.  Its natural token
is the ordered tuple, or disjoint union, of the \(k\) local tokens.
A synchronized local \(X\to Y\) transition adds one point in every slot,
so it has rank jump \(k\), not one.  Equivalently, its cyclic ownership
seam has width \(k\); for the original disjoint product ports it has
Johnson distance at least \(k\).

There is also an exact width-one rotor resolution of the same diagonal
voltage.  Take \(B=C_k\), with controller states \(0,\ldots,k-1\), and give
edge \(i\to i+1\) voltage \(e_i\).  The unique base-cycle voltage is

\[
                              d.                                       \tag{0.8}
\]

Hence

\[
                              H_B=\langle d\rangle\cong C_5,            \tag{0.9}
\]

and every lifted component has exactly \(5k\) vertices.  It traverses each
slot five times, so a slot-tagged local pentagon carrier
\(\Delta_i\) accumulates as

\[
                              5\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\Delta_i.                \tag{0.10}
\]

This second realization has only width-one seams, but has \(k5^k\) row
vertices: the controller is an additional row coordinate.  The two
constructions are the extremal ends of the state--width tradeoff.

There are two rigorous no-go statements.

1. **Slot-complete finite-state couplings still generate the full
   product.**  If, for every slot \(i\), the controller transition retains
   two parallel owner edges whose voltages differ by \(e_i\), then
   \(e_i\in H_B\).  Thus
   \[
                              H_B=R,                                  \tag{0.11}
   \]
   and components are exponential.  This includes every coupling which
   keeps the full local D3 pentagon fibre at a fixed controller transition.
2. **Bounded state and bounded coupling width still force exponential
   components.**  Suppose \(B\) has \(M\) states, every voltage involves
   at most \(w\) slots, and the carrier-active edge voltages collectively
   involve all \(k\) slots.  Then
   \[
       \boxed{\;
       \dim_{\mathbb F_5}H_B
          \ge
       \left\lceil
       \frac{(k-w(M-1))_+}{w(2M-1)}
       \right\rceil.
       \;}                                                        \tag{0.12}
   \]
   In particular, fixed \(M,w\) give component size
   \(5^{\Omega(k)}\).  Polynomial component size requires
   \[
                              Mw=\Omega(k/\log k),                     \tag{0.13}
   \]
   and bounded component size requires \(Mw=\Omega(k)\).

The proposed codimension-one relation
\(\sum_i e_i=0\) does not solve fragmentation: its subgroup has dimension
\(k-1\) and size \(5^{k-1}\).  Indeed it is realized by a one-state,
width-two voltage graph, so the blanket assertion that every finite-state
cross-slot coupling generates the full product is false.  A diagonal
subgroup has the right size, but requires either a voltage touching
\(\Theta(k)\) slots at once or a controller with \(\Theta(k)\) states.

Thus the minimal surviving coefficient-one object is sharply identified:
a new cross-slot \(X/Y\) path gadget must encode a growing controller
inside the existing \(5^k\) rows, or realize a nonlocal voltage of width
\Omega(k/\log k)\), without multiplying owners.  No bounded-state,
bounded-width replacement can do this.

## 1. Exact voltage components

Let \(B=(S,E)\) be a connected directed multigraph; reverse traversal of
an edge has voltage \(-a(e)\).  Fix a base state \(s_0\).  Define

\[
 H_B=\{a(W):W\text{ is a closed walk at }s_0\}\le R,             \tag{1.1}
\]

where \(a(W)\) is the signed sum of its edge voltages.

### Lemma 1.1 (component theorem)

Two lifted vertices \((s_0,x)\) and \((s_0,y)\) lie in the same component
if and only if

\[
                              y-x\in H_B.                          \tag{1.2}
\]

More generally, after one reference path from \(s_0\) to every \(s\) is
fixed, a component contains one translate of \(H_B\) over every base
state.  Hence it has \(M|H_B|\) vertices when \(M=|S|\).

#### Proof

Every lifted walk changes the fibre coordinate by its voltage.  A walk
from \((s_0,x)\) to \((s_0,y)\) therefore gives \(y-x\in H_B\).
Conversely every closed base walk of voltage \(y-x\) lifts from
\((s_0,x)\) to \((s_0,y)\).  Appending a fixed reference path to another
base state gives the final assertion. \(\square\)

This is the precise distinction between the number and size of
components.  A one-dimensional diagonal holonomy gives many components
but each component has only \(5M\) rows; a codimension-one holonomy gives
only five components but each is exponentially large.

## 2. Exact algebraic \(X/Y\) ownership ledgers

### 2.1 Synchronous whole-catalogue diagonalization

Let \(Z=\mathbb F_5\) be the five local rows.  At each local \(X\)- or
\(Y\)-phase \(\gamma\), index the five shared physical tokens by their old
owners:

\[
                         \mathcal T_\gamma
                            =\{t_{\gamma,z}:z\in Z\}.              \tag{2.1}
\]

Exact ownership on the new shore defines a permutation
\[
 \rho_\gamma(z)
    =\operatorname{own}_{+}(t_{\gamma,z})\in Z.                   \tag{2.2}
\]
At the rooted and complementary endpoint phases this is the identity.
At the certified first internal \(X\)-phase, a relabelling of \(Z\) makes
\(\rho_\gamma(z)=z+1\).

On the original global row set \(R=Z^k\), define the correlated phase
tokens

\[
 \mathbf t_{\gamma,x}
   =(t_{\gamma,x_0},\ldots,t_{\gamma,x_{k-1}}),
       \qquad x\in R.                                             \tag{2.3}
\]

The tuple notation is formal; equivalently use a disjoint union with the
slot tags retained.  Assign owners

\[
 \operatorname{own}_{-}(\mathbf t_{\gamma,x})=x,\qquad
 \operatorname{own}_{+}(\mathbf t_{\gamma,x})
      =\rho_\gamma^\Delta(x)
      :=(\rho_\gamma(x_0),\ldots,\rho_\gamma(x_{k-1})).            \tag{2.4}
\]

For every \(X\)-phase and every \(Y\)-phase both owner maps are
bijections.  Thus every correlated token is owned exactly once on each
shore, with exactly \(5^k\) rows and no ownership defect.  Endpoint tokens
give the identity matching.

Put
\[
                       G_{\rm loc}=\langle\rho_\gamma:\gamma\rangle
                           \le\operatorname{Sym}(Z).              \tag{2.5}
\]
After the endpoint matchings are contracted, the ownership components are
the orbits on \(Z^k\) of
\[
                       G_{\rm loc}^\Delta
                          =\{(g,\ldots,g):g\in G_{\rm loc}\}.      \tag{2.6}
\]
Consequently every component \(O\) has
\[
                              |O|\le |G_{\rm loc}|\le120.         \tag{2.7}
\]

Because \(G_{\rm loc}\) contains a five-cycle, it is transitive on \(Z\).
For every coordinate projection \(p_i:O\to Z\), all fibres have the same
size: if \(O=G_{\rm loc}/G_x\), then
\[
 |p_i^{-1}(z)|=\frac{|G_{x_i}|}{|G_x|}=\frac{|O|}{5}.             \tag{2.8}
\]
Let \(\delta_i(z)\) be the tagged slot-\(i\) signed carrier contribution
of local row \(z\), and put \(\Delta_i=\sum_{z\in Z}\delta_i(z)\).
Then
\[
 \sum_{x\in O}\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\delta_i(x_i)
     =\frac{|O|}{5}\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\Delta_i.                      \tag{2.9}
\]
With disjoint slot tags its norm is \(\Theta(|O|k)\) whenever the local
ledger is nonzero.

This is the exact bounded-component, coefficient-one ownership
construction requested.  It fails at precisely one axiom of a
middle-levels path factor.  If each local \(X\)-token has rank \(r\) and
its following \(Y\)-token rank \(r+1\), the disjoint-union realization of
(2.3) has ranks \(kr\) and \(k(r+1)\).  Its jump is \(k\), not one.
Thus (2.3) is a legal macro-incidence but not a legal \(X\subset Y\) edge
of the Boolean middle-levels graph.

The ordinary coordinatewise refinement does not repair this.  When an
intermediate tuple exposes the certified pentagon token in slot \(i\) and
keeps the other slots at their endpoint tokens, its relative owner
permutation is the coordinate five-cycle \(c_i\).  The endpoint token
supplies the identity matching with the same controller endpoints.
The resulting two-edge closed walk has voltage \(e_i\).  Exposing every
slot therefore generates
\[
                         \langle e_0,\ldots,e_{k-1}\rangle=R,
\]
by Theorem 4.1.  Hence a successful rank-one refinement must introduce
new mixed \(X/Y\) states whose owner maps never expose these independent
coordinate pairs; merely shuffling the tuple coordinates recovers the
full product overlay.

### 2.2 A width-one rotor resolution

Take \(S=\mathbb Z/k\mathbb Z\).  For every \(i\in S\) and \(x\in R\)
introduce distinct formal tokens

\[
                              \widehat X_{i,x},\qquad \widehat Y_{i,x}. \tag{2.10}
\]

Let the old catalogue have rows \((i,x)\), with

\[
 \operatorname{own}_{-}(\widehat X_{i,x})
   =\operatorname{own}_{-}(\widehat Y_{i,x})=(i,x).              \tag{2.11}
\]

Put

\[
                              T(i,x)=(i+1,x+e_i).                  \tag{2.12}
\]

Let the new catalogue own the same tokens by

\[
 \operatorname{own}_{+}(\widehat X_{i,x})
   =\operatorname{own}_{+}(\widehat Y_{i,x})=T(i,x).             \tag{2.13}
\]

Both owner maps are bijections.  Therefore every formal \(X\)-token and
every formal \(Y\)-token is owned exactly once on either shore.  The
contracted ownership overlay is the permutation graph of \(T\).  Each
symbol in (2.10) may be replaced by a disjoint copy of the complete finite
D3 \(X/Y\) token catalogue; applying the same owner bijections to every
symbol preserves exact token multiplicities.  What remains absent is the
physical inclusion adjacency between successive catalogues.

Now

\[
                              T^k(i,x)=(i,x+d),                    \tag{2.14}
\]

where \(d\) is (0.7).  Since \(d\) has order five, every \(T\)-cycle has
length \(5k\), proving (0.9)--(0.10) directly.  The number of components is

\[
                              \frac{k5^k}{5k}=5^{k-1}.             \tag{2.15}
\]

This is an exact ownership construction, but not an exact middle-levels
path factor.  It has two defects relative to coefficient one:

1. the row and token catalogues are enlarged by the controller factor
   \(k\);
2. (2.10) specifies ownership but supplies no physical containments
   \(X\subset Y\) or root-to-complement path adjacency.

The construction is nevertheless useful: it proves that diagonal holonomy
and \(\Theta(k)\) carrier are algebraically compatible.  The obstruction is
physical realization without controller multiplicity, not the voltage
ledger itself.

## 3. Why a codimension-one relation is insufficient

Put

\[
                              H_0=\left\{x\in R:
                                      \sum_{i=0}^{k-1}x_i=0\right\}. \tag{3.1}
\]

Then

\[
                              \dim H_0=k-1,\qquad |H_0|=5^{k-1}.  \tag{3.2}
\]

A voltage graph with holonomy \(H_0\) has five fibre cosets, but each
component contains \(5^{k-1}\) row labels over every base state.  Thus the
single relation \(\sum e_i=0\) reduces the number of components and not the
fragmentation parameter relevant to rounding.

More generally, polynomial component size requires

\[
                              \dim H_B=O(\log k),                  \tag{3.3}
\]

while bounded component size requires \(\dim H_B=O(1)\).

The full-product alternative is false even with one controller state and
width two.  Put one loop of voltage \(e_i-e_{i+1}\) for
\(0\le i<k-1\).  The loop voltages span exactly \(H_0\).  Thus this
finite-state coupling acts in all coordinates but has holonomy of
codimension one, not all of \(R\).  Its failure is quantitative rather
than algebraic: each component still has \(5^{k-1}\) rows.

## 4. Slot-complete couplings recover \(C_5^k\)

The D3 pentagon overlay has a common root-owner matching and an internal
owner matching differing from it by a five-cycle.  In a product slot this
is translation by \(e_i\).

### Theorem 4.1 (parallel-edge criterion)

Suppose that for every \(i\) the controller voltage graph contains two
directed edges

\[
                              e_i^0,e_i^1:u_i\longrightarrow v_i \tag{4.1}
\]

with the same endpoints and

\[
                              a(e_i^1)-a(e_i^0)=e_i.               \tag{4.2}
\]

Then \(H_B=R\).

#### Proof

Traverse \(e_i^1\) and return along the reverse of \(e_i^0\).  This is a
closed walk of voltage \(e_i\), so \(e_i\in H_B\).  The \(k\) unit vectors
generate \(R\). \(\square\)

The two parallel edges are exactly what remains when a common controller
transition is applied to both the root-token owner matching and the
five-cycle pentagon-token owner matching.  Therefore any coupling which
retains the complete five-row local pentagon fibre at a fixed controller
transition satisfies Theorem 4.1.

To evade it, the controller transition must depend on the local pentagon
row or token phase.  That is a genuinely cross-slot change, not a gauge of
the independent product.

## 5. A quantitative finite-state locality bound

Assume:

1. \(B\) has \(M\) vertices;
2. every edge voltage has support size at most \(w\);
3. every coordinate \(1,\ldots,k\) occurs in the support of at least one
   edge voltage.

The third assumption is the weakest tagged statement that the coupling can
act on all \(k\) carrier slots.

### Theorem 5.1 (state--width lower bound)

Under these assumptions, (0.12) holds.

#### Proof

Choose a spanning tree \(T\) of the underlying base graph.  Its \(M-1\)
edge voltages involve at most

\[
                              w(M-1)                               \tag{5.1}
\]

coordinates; call their union \(U\).

Gauge the voltage graph along \(T\): assign to each base vertex the voltage
of its tree path from a fixed root, and subtract the endpoint potentials
from every edge voltage.  All tree voltages become zero.  Every non-tree
edge \(e\) becomes the voltage of its fundamental closed walk, hence lies
in \(H_B\).  Its support has size at most

\[
                              w(2M-1),                             \tag{5.2}
\]

because that closed walk uses \(e\) and at most \(2(M-1)\) tree edges.

Every coordinate outside \(U\) which occurred in an original edge voltage
still occurs in at least one gauged non-tree voltage: the endpoint
potentials use only coordinates in \(U\).  Hence the union of supports of
the non-tree voltages contains at least

\[
                              k-w(M-1)                             \tag{5.3}
\]

coordinates.

Select non-tree voltage vectors greedily.  Whenever their current support
union does not contain all coordinates in (5.3), choose a vector containing
a new coordinate.  Such a vector is linearly independent of the previously
chosen ones, since every vector in their span vanishes outside their
support union.  Each chosen vector adds at most \(w(2M-1)\) coordinates.
Therefore at least

\[
             \left\lceil
             \frac{(k-w(M-1))_+}{w(2M-1)}
             \right\rceil                                      \tag{5.4}
\]

independent vectors are required.  They lie in \(H_B\), proving (0.12).
\(\square\)

### Corollary 5.2

If \(M,w=O(1)\), then every component has size \(5^{\Omega(k)}\).

If every component is polynomial in \(k\), then
\(\dim H_B=O(\log k)\).  Rearranging (0.12) gives

\[
                              Mw=\Omega(k/\log k).                 \tag{5.5}
\]

For bounded components the same argument gives \(Mw=\Omega(k)\).

Thus a constant-state controller can achieve polynomial fragmentation only
through transitions coupling \(\Omega(k/\log k)\) slots at once.  A
bounded-width coupling needs a controller whose size grows almost
linearly.

## 6. The exact remaining coefficient-one target

The synchronous diagonal ledger (2.1)--(2.9) shows the ideal algebraic
ownership profile:

\[
                    \text{component size at most }120,\qquad
                    \text{carrier action }\Theta(k).              \tag{6.1}
\]

Its missing part is rank-one physical incidence.  Its cyclic subaction has
five-row components.  The width-one rotor (2.12) shows
how to resolve the diagonal voltage into local moves:

\[
                    \text{component size }5k,\qquad
                    \text{carrier action }\Theta(k),               \tag{6.2}
\]

but its controller is an extra row coordinate.  A coefficient-one
realization must encode that controller without changing the number of
root owners.  There are only two ways left by Theorem 5.1.

There is already an exact divisibility obstruction to encoding this
particular uniform rotor.  If the \(5^k\) original owners were partitioned
into rotor cycles of length exactly \(5k\), then
\[
                              5k\mid5^k,
\]
so
\[
                              k\mid5^{k-1}.                         \tag{6.3}
\]
Hence \(k\) must be a power of five.  For all other \(k\), an exact
coefficient-one realization must use nonuniform cycles, discard a
residual, or abandon the literal width-one rotor.  Condition (6.3) is
necessary, not asserted sufficient.

1. **Encoded growing controller.**  Store \(\Theta(k)\) controller states
   inside \(O(\log k)\) of the existing product coordinates and construct
   a reversible cross-slot path factor which updates that code while
   applying the pentagon in the addressed data slot.
2. **Nonlocal seam.**  Build one legal \(X/Y\) splice whose ownership
   voltage touches \(\Omega(k/\log k)\) slots, preferably the full diagonal
   \(d\), in one bounded number of token phases.

Either option needs new physical token sets.  The original product tokens
cannot implement a diagonal seam in one incidence edge: if
\[
                         P_x=\bigsqcup_{i=0}^{k-1}P^{(i)}_{x_i}    \tag{6.4}
\]
is the union of the \(k\) disjoint D3 port blocks, then
\[
             \frac12|P_{x+d}\triangle P_x|
                =\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}
                   \frac12|P^{(i)}_{x_i+1}\triangle P^{(i)}_{x_i}|
                \ge k.                                           \tag{6.5}
\]

Thus \(P_x\) and \(P_{x+d}\) are at Johnson distance at least \(k\), not
one.  In particular, any physical path between them has at least \(k\)
Johnson swaps, hence at least \(2k\) edges in the two-rank inclusion
graph; no bounded-phase atom on the original disjoint port blocks can
realize the diagonal owner seam.  Resolving this distance through
one-slot intermediate states
reintroduces sparse voltages; Theorem 5.1 then applies unless a growing
controller keeps only their diagonal holonomy.

No exact inclusion-path factor realizing either option is presently
constructed.  What is proved is the sharp statewise boundary:

\[
\boxed{
\begin{array}{c}
\text{bounded controller}+\text{bounded cross-slot width}\\
\Longrightarrow\text{exponential overlay components};\\[2mm]
\text{polynomial components}+\Theta(k)\text{ carrier}\\
\Longrightarrow Mw=\Omega(k/\log k)
\text{ and new physical }X/Y\text{ tokens}.
\end{array}}                                                   \tag{6.6}
\]

This closes every genuinely finite-state local coupling while isolating
the minimal nonlocal or growing-controller replacement.
