# Promotion-ring local descent: private transverse orbits obstruct every fixed bounded paired-orbit transposition atlas

Date: 2026-07-26

Method: pure mathematics only.  Every state below selects one actual cyclic
frame in every top fibre.  No relaxed frame column is introduced.

## 0. Verdict

Fix a positive integer \(d\), choose \(d\) pairwise disjoint coordinate
transpositions, and let

\[
 \Gamma=\langle s_1,\ldots,s_d\rangle\cong(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^d.
\]

Start from an independently uniform selection \(F\) of one cyclic frame in
every top.  There is a choice of \(F\) for which a positive-density family
of full \(\Gamma\)-orbits of middle targets has total load exactly one.
The expected density, with \(d\) fixed and \(m\to\infty\), is

\[
 \boxed{2^{-d}e^{-2^d}.}
 \tag{0.1}
\]

The total load on each such orbit is invariant under every **paired
top-orbit switch** for the generators \(s_i\).  Hence it is invariant on
the finite state component generated by those paired switches.  A global
energy minimizer on that restricted component is a genuine local minimum
against every compound paired-orbit move for the \(d\) generators, yet its
floor-corrected middle collision energy is at least

\[
 \boxed{
 \left({(2^d-1)2^{-d-1}e^{-2^d}}\right)W
 }
 \tag{0.2}
\]

for all sufficiently large \(m\).  Any nonnegative entrance or exact-tag
floor energy may be added without weakening this bound.

This is an actual-column, integral, fibre-dense obstruction, but its exact
move scope is essential.  It obstructs local descent based on any *fixed
bounded family of paired top-orbit transposition moves*.  In the literal
one-frame-per-top product fibre, changing just one top is legal.  Such a
move need not preserve the transverse target-orbit total.  Therefore the
argument is not a bad local minimum for the full promotion-ring selection
fibre, and it is not a separating inequality for its integer hull.

The paired switch is still a legal compound move: it changes both tops in
a two-element orbit together, or transposes the frame on a fixed top.  It
is simply not the most general legal move.  Pairing is imposed to retain
the relabelling symmetry and the orbit-total invariant.

## 1. The tuned full-ring fibre and its exact floor

Put

\[
 n=2m,\qquad W=\binom{2m}{m},\qquad
 M=m+H,\qquad R=\binom{2m}{M}=\binom{2m}{m-H}.
 \tag{1.1}
\]

Assume throughout that

\[
 H=o(m),\qquad H\longrightarrow\infty,qquad
 \theta_m:={MR\over W}\longrightarrow1,qquad MR\ge W.
 \tag{1.2}
\]

The crossing-tuned height in the promotion-ring construction satisfies
these hypotheses.  Write

\[
 T=MR=W+s.
 \tag{1.3}
\]

Then \(s=o(W)\), and in particular \(W\le T<2W\) for all sufficiently
large \(m\).

For each top \(U\in\binom{[2m]}M\), select an oriented cyclic frame
\(\pi_U\), modulo rotation.  Its middle deck is

\[
 \mathcal D(\pi_U)
 =\{I_{\pi_U}(j,m):j\in\mathbb Z/M\mathbb Z\}.
 \tag{1.4}
\]

The \(M\) members of this deck are distinct.  For a selection
\(G=(\pi_U)_U\), define

\[
 \mu_G(X)=|\{U:X\in\mathcal D(\pi_U)\}|.
 \tag{1.5}
\]

Thus \(\sum_X\mu_G(X)=T\).  The exact integer pair-collision floor is
\(s=T-W\), and the floor-corrected middle energy is

\[
 \Phi_m(G)=\sum_{X\in\binom{[2m]}m}\binom{\mu_G(X)}2-s.
 \tag{1.6}
\]

The elementary pointwise identity

\[
 \boxed{
 \Phi_m(G)
 =|\{X:\mu_G(X)=0\}|
  +\sum_{\mu_G(X)\ge3}\binom{\mu_G(X)-1}{2}
 }
 \tag{1.7}
\]

will be decisive.  Indeed,

\[
 \sum_X\left(\binom{\mu_G(X)}2-\mu_G(X)+1\right)
 =\sum_X\binom{\mu_G(X)}2-T+W,
\]

and the summand is \(1,0,0,\binom{k-1}{2}\) at loads
\(k=0,1,2,k\ge3\), respectively.

If the objective also contains an entrance row with \(N\) targets and
total load \(T\), where \(N\le T<2N\), its floor-corrected pair energy is

\[
 \Phi_{\rm ent}=\sum_S\binom{b_S}{2}-(T-N)\ge0
 \tag{1.8}
\]

by the same identity.  If the second row consists of top tags, each top
tag has load exactly one, so its collision energy is identically zero.
Thus a lower bound for \(\Phi_m\) is automatically a lower bound for
either combined objective.

## 2. Paired top-orbit switches and the restricted atlas

Let \(\tau\) be a coordinate transposition.  It acts on a selection by

\[
 (\tau G)_U=\tau\pi_{\tau^{-1}U}.
 \tag{2.1}
\]

For the symmetry-preserving subatlas, group tops into the
\(\tau\)-orbits \(K\).  If
\(K=\{U,\tau U\}\), switching this component makes the replacement

\[
 (\pi_U,\pi_{\tau U})
 \longmapsto
 (\tau\pi_{\tau U},\tau\pi_U).
 \tag{2.2}
\]

If \(K=\{U\}\), it replaces \(\pi_U\) by \(\tau\pi_U\).  In both
cases the result still selects one actual cyclic frame in every affected
top.  Different top-orbit pairs have disjoint top supports, so an
arbitrary subset may be switched simultaneously.  Call this the
**paired \(\tau\)-orbit cube**.

It is not the full legal product cube.  Replacing only the frame at
\(U\) by \((\tau G)_U\), while leaving \(\tau U\) unchanged, still
selects one actual cyclic frame in every top and is therefore legal.
Pairing is an extra symmetry restriction, not an integrality condition.

Now fix the disjoint transpositions \(s_1,\ldots,s_d\).  Define
\(\mathfrak C_\Gamma^{\rm pair}(F)\) to be the finite set of states
reachable from \(F\) by any finite sequence of compound paired-orbit
switches for the \(s_i\).  This definition is stateful: after every
switch, the next paired orbit cube is formed from the current state.

Because the state space is finite, every energy has a minimizer
\(F_*\) on \(\mathfrak C_\Gamma^{\rm pair}(F)\).  Every compound paired
switch for any \(s_i\) at \(F_*\) remains in
\(\mathfrak C_\Gamma^{\rm pair}(F)\).  Consequently

\[
 \boxed{F_*\text{ is local against every compound paired-orbit move for
 each }s_i.}
 \tag{2.3}
\]

Statement (2.3) deliberately makes no claim about an independently
chosen one-top replacement.  Such a move can break the invariant below.

## 3. Transverse target orbits and their exact invariant

Write the support of \(s_i\) as the pair \(P_i=\{a_i,b_i\}\).  Let

\[
 \mathcal X_\pitchfork
 =\left\{X\in\binom{[2m]}m:|X\cap P_i|=1
                    \text{ for every }i\right\}.
 \tag{3.1}
\]

Every \(X\in\mathcal X_\pitchfork\) has trivial stabilizer in
\(\Gamma\), so its orbit \(Q=\Gamma X\) has size

\[
 k=2^d.
 \tag{3.2}
\]

There are exactly

\[
 \boxed{
 Q_d=\binom{2m-2d}{m-d}
 =\frac{(m)_d^2}{(2m)_{2d}}W
 =(4^{-d}+o_d(1))W
 }
 \tag{3.3}
\]

such full target orbits.  Here \((x)_j=x(x-1)\cdots(x-j+1)\).

For a target orbit \(Q\), put

\[
 Z_Q(G)=\sum_{X\in Q}\mu_G(X).
 \tag{3.4}
\]

### Lemma 3.1 (paired-orbit invariance)

For every \(i\), every current state \(G\), and every paired top orbit
\(K\), switching \(K\) preserves \(Z_Q(G)\) for every full transverse
orbit \(Q\).

#### Proof

Let \(w_K\) be the middle-load vector contributed by the selected frames
whose tops lie in \(K\).  Equations (2.1)--(2.2) say exactly that the
switched component contributes \(s_iw_K\).  Since
\(s_iQ=Q\),

\[
 \sum_{X\in Q}(s_iw_K)(X)
 =\sum_{X\in Q}w_K(s_i^{-1}X)
 =\sum_{X\in Q}w_K(X).
\]

The same proof covers a fixed top component.  \(\square\)

It follows inductively that

\[
 \boxed{Z_Q(G)=Z_Q(F)\quad
        (G\in\mathfrak C_\Gamma^{\rm pair}(F)).}
 \tag{3.5}
\]

This is an integral statewise invariant of actual cyclic frame columns.

## 4. Exact one- and two-target counts

Choose the base frame independently and uniformly at every top.  Fix a
middle target \(X\).  It is contained in exactly

\[
 R_0=\binom mH
 \tag{4.1}
\]

tops.  In a fixed containing top, a uniform cyclic frame contains \(X\)
in its middle deck with probability

\[
 p={M\over\binom Mm}
   ={H!m!\over(M-1)!}.
 \tag{4.2}
\]

Consequently

\[
 \boxed{R_0p={m!^2\over(m-H)!(m+H-1)!}
       ={MR\over W}=\theta_m.}
 \tag{4.3}
\]

The probability \(p\) tends to zero.

Now let \(X,Y\in Q\) be distinct and let their Johnson distance be
\(t\), so \(|X\setminus Y|=|Y\setminus X|=t\).  Necessarily
\(1\le t\le d\), and the number of common tops is exactly

\[
 R_t=\binom{m-t}{H-t},
 \qquad {R_t\over R_0}={(H)_t\over(m)_t}.
 \tag{4.4}
\]

For a common top \(U\), the two omitted \(H\)-sets
\(U\setminus X\) and \(U\setminus Y\) have intersection size
\(H-t\).  For all sufficiently large \(m\), \(d<H\).  The exact
number of oriented cyclic orders modulo rotation in which both omitted
sets are intervals is

\[
 2(H-t)!(t!)^2(m-t)!.
 \tag{4.5}
\]

Indeed, the two overlapping intervals have one of the two possible
oriented block orders, and the four blocks have respective sizes
\(t,H-t,t,m-t\).  Dividing by the total \((M-1)!\) frames and summing
over common tops gives the exact simultaneous-hit expectation

\[
\begin{aligned}
 \Lambda_t
 &=\binom{m-t}{H-t}
   {2(H-t)!(t!)^2(m-t)!\over(M-1)!}\\
 &=\boxed{\theta_m\,{2(t!)^2\over(m)_t^2}}.
\end{aligned}
 \tag{4.6}
\]

In particular, the total expected number of within-one-top simultaneous
hits among the members of \(Q\) is

\[
 \sum_{t=1}^d2^{d-1}\binom dt\Lambda_t
 =2^d\theta_m\sum_{t=1}^d
      \binom dt{(t!)^2\over(m)_t^2}
 =O_d(m^{-2}).
 \tag{4.7}
\]

## 5. Exclusive stars and the private-orbit probability

For \(X\in Q\), let \(\mathcal T_X\) be its set of containing tops and
remove every top which contains another member of \(Q\):

\[
 \mathcal E_X
 =\mathcal T_X\setminus
   \bigcup_{Y\in Q\setminus\{X\}}\mathcal T_Y.
 \tag{5.1}
\]

The families \(\mathcal E_X\), \(X\in Q\), are pairwise disjoint.
By (4.4) and the fact that \(Q\) has \(\binom dt\) members at distance
\(t\) from \(X\),

\[
 0\le1-{ |\mathcal E_X|\over R_0}
 \le\sum_{t=1}^d\binom dt{(H)_t\over(m)_t}
 =O_d(H/m)=o(1).
 \tag{5.2}
\]

On an exclusive top for \(X\), the selected frame hits \(Q\) precisely
when it hits \(X\), an event of probability \(p\).  Therefore the
exclusive-star loads are mutually independent binomial variables with
means

\[
 |\mathcal E_X|p=\theta_m(1+o(1)).
 \tag{5.3}
\]

Since \(p\to0\), they converge jointly to independent
\({\rm Poisson}(\theta_m)\) variables.  This statement can be read
elementarily: the sum of the exclusive loads is binomial with parameter
\(p\), total mean \(2^d\theta_m+o(1)\), so its probability of being one
is

\[
 (2^d\theta_m+o(1))
   \exp(-2^d\theta_m+o(1)).
 \tag{5.4}
\]

The nonexclusive tops contribute vanishing total expected load.  Indeed,
by (5.2),

\[
 \mathbb E[\hbox{nonexclusive load on }Q]
 \le\sum_{X\in Q}(R_0-|\mathcal E_X|)p
 =O_d(\theta_mH/m)=o(1).
 \tag{5.5}
\]

Thus Markov's inequality and (5.4) give

\[
 \boxed{
 \Pr[Z_Q(F)=1]
 =2^d\theta_m e^{-2^d\theta_m}+o_d(1)
 =2^de^{-2^d}+o_d(1).
 }
 \tag{5.6}
\]

Call \(Q\) **private** when \(Z_Q(F)=1\).  Linearity of expectation,
(3.3), and (5.6) yield

\[
\begin{aligned}
 \mathbb E\,|\mathcal P(F)|
 &=\binom{2m-2d}{m-d}
   \left(2^d\theta_me^{-2^d\theta_m}+o_d(1)\right)\\
 &=\boxed{(2^{-d}e^{-2^d}+o_d(1))W}.
\end{aligned}
 \tag{5.7}
\]

Consequently, for all sufficiently large \(m\), some deterministic
actual-frame selection \(F\) satisfies

\[
 \boxed{|\mathcal P(F)|
       \ge2^{-d-1}e^{-2^d}W.}
 \tag{5.8}
\]

No concentration assertion is needed.

## 6. The linear floor obstruction throughout the paired switch component

Fix an \(F\) satisfying (5.8).  By (3.5), every private orbit has total
load one in every state
\(G\in\mathfrak C_\Gamma^{\rm pair}(F)\).  Since all loads
are nonnegative integers, precisely one member of that orbit has load one
and the other \(2^d-1\) members have load zero.  Distinct target orbits
are disjoint.  Therefore every state in the switch closure has at least

\[
 (2^d-1)|\mathcal P(F)|
 \tag{6.1}
\]

middle holes.  Equations (1.7) and (5.8) prove

\[
 \boxed{
 \Phi_m(G)
 \ge(2^d-1)2^{-d-1}e^{-2^d}W
 \qquad(G\in\mathfrak C_\Gamma^{\rm pair}(F)).
 }
 \tag{6.2}
\]

In particular, the minimizer \(F_*\) from (2.3) is a local minimum for
every compound paired-orbit move and satisfies the same linear lower
bound.

There is an equivalent statewise dual description.  Let

\[
 Y=\bigcup_{Q\in\mathcal P(F)}Q,
 \qquad y=\mathbf1_Y.
 \tag{6.3}
\]

Then \(|Y|=2^d|\mathcal P(F)|=\Omega_d(W)\), while every state in the
closure obeys

\[
 \boxed{
 \langle y,\mu_G\rangle=|\mathcal P(F)|,
 \qquad
 \langle y,\mathbf1\rangle=2^d|\mathcal P(F)|.
 }
 \tag{6.4}
\]

Thus \(y\) gives an \(\Omega_d(W)\) integral separation between the
entire paired switch component and the unit middle load.  It is
fibre-dense:
asymptotically its expected support density is \(e^{-2^d}\).

In the language of the Gram method, every allowed paired-orbit effect
\(\Delta_K\) for one of the generators satisfies

\[
 \boxed{\langle y,\Delta_K\rangle=0.}
 \tag{6.5}
\]

Thus \(y\) is an exact left-kernel vector for the whole generated move
system, not merely a small-eigenvalue direction.  The corresponding
centred quadratic separation is also linear.  On every private orbit,
exactly one coordinate has load one and the other \(2^d-1\) coordinates
have load zero, so

\[
 \sum_{X\in Q}(\mu_G(X)-\theta_m)^2
 =(1-\theta_m)^2+(2^d-1)\theta_m^2.
 \tag{6.6}
\]

Summing (6.6) over the private orbits gives
\(\Omega_d(W)\) centred \(\ell^2\)-energy in every state of the paired
component.  Equation (1.7), rather than a real-valued relaxation of
(6.6), is what retains the exact integer floor in (6.2).

## 7. Exact implication boundary

The argument proves all of the following.

1. The obstruction uses actual cyclic frame columns and exact legal
   paired top-orbit switches.
2. It retains the integer floor \(T-W\); no signed or real relaxation is
   used.
3. It survives arbitrary compound switching in each prescribed
   paired-orbit cube.
4. It supplies a positive-density statewise dual weight and a linear
   collision lower bound at a local minimum of the restricted generated
   component.

It does **not** prove either of the following.

1. It does not construct a state which is local for the full product
   fibre, even for one prescribed transposition.  Changing a single top
   without its paired mate may change (6.4).
2. It does not separate the full convex hull of integral one-frame-per-top
   selections from the balanced fractional point.  The weight \(y\) is
   chosen from the random base state and is valid on one paired switch
   component, not on the whole integer fibre.

There is a sharp reason that the orbit invariant cannot be upgraded to all
coordinate transpositions.

### Lemma 7.1 (the unrestricted transposition move graph is connected)

Allow the legal one-top alternative supplied by every coordinate
transposition.  Then the move graph on all selections of one cyclic frame
per top is connected.

#### Proof

Fix a top \(U\) and two labels \(a,b\in U\).  The coordinate
transposition \(\tau=(a\ b)\) fixes \(U\).  Changing only that top
replaces \(\pi_U\) by \(\tau\pi_U\) and leaves every other top unchanged.
Transpositions of pairs in \(U\) generate the full symmetric group on
\(U\), and that group acts transitively on the oriented cyclic frames of
\(U\) modulo rotation.  Hence the frame at \(U\) can be changed
arbitrarily without changing any other top.  Repeating this independently
over all tops connects any two selections.  \(\square\)

Consequently every exact state invariant preserved by all legal one-top
transposition moves is constant on the entire selection fibre.  In
particular, no nonconstant union-of-target-orbits weight can furnish a
full-fibre invariant obstruction.  This does not rule out a bad local
minimum: connected graphs may have non-global local minima.  It rules out
precisely the statewise invariant route to one.

Accordingly, the theorem closes only the paired-orbit Gram subatlas for a
fixed bounded list of disjoint transpositions.  It does not close the
literal product cube, whose independent top moves can redistribute mass
between the private target orbits.  By Lemma 7.1 the full product fibre
has no exact state-invariant obstruction; the remaining gate is a
quantitative positive-cut or energy-barrier theorem for independent
top-column replacements.
