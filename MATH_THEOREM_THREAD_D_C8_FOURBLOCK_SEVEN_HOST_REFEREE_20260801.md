# The four-block C8 source relation is not a flat seven-host macro

Date: 2026-08-01  
Lane: Thread D referee, quaternary-octagon source physicalization  
Status: exact literal-model no-go.  A genuinely nonflat quotient weave is
not excluded.

## 0. Verdict

Index four octagon blocks by `00,01,10,11`.  Give them the Klein
relabelings

\[
 1,\quad \beta=(a_1a_3),\quad \alpha=(a_0a_2),\quad
 \alpha\beta,
\]

and compare phase vectors `0110` and `1001`.  The new four-block identity is
a real positive source-signature relation, and the positionwise caps

\[
 P_{uv,p}=\rho_{uv}(Q^0_p\cup Q^1_p)                 \tag{0.1}
\]

are nonempty and phase common.  Neither fact physicalizes the four blocks.

In the literal central owner bank, the four blocks have

\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c}
 &\text{occurrences}&\text{distinct values}&\text{multiplicities}\\ \hline
\text{owners}&32d+92&8d+24&2^2,4^{8d+22}\\
\text{lower }q1&32d+88&8d+24&2^4,4^{8d+20}\\
\text{upper }q1&32d+88&16&2^4,4^4,(4d+8)^8.
\end{array}                                             \tag{0.2}
\]

Thus it is neither owner simple nor lower-rainbow.  The old and new sides
have the same three counters in (0.2), but their literal physical-edge
counters differ.  A common source cap therefore coexists with a decisive
owner/palette obstruction.

The seven planted-ray menu does not repair this literal model at zero net
source length.  There are two independent reasons.

1.  Every menu split is `X -> X,B` with `empty != B subseteq X`.  Such an
    adjacent pair is impossible at an internal position of a flat simple
    depth-`d` Johnson source: one owner transition is monotone, and equal
    rank turns it into a stutter.  Reversing the pair gives the dual
    stutter.  Replacing `X` by two proper halves instead is covered by the
    already proved rank-valley theorem.
2.  If the seven hosts are hidden at the eight endpoints of the four
    blocks, they alter only endpoint collars.  Delete the first and last
    `d` owner rows of every block.  The untouched interior bank still has

    \[
       24d+92\text{ occurrences on }8d+24\text{ values},\qquad
       2^{4d-6},3^{16},4^{4d+14}.                       \tag{0.3}
    \]

    In particular **every** owner value is still repeated.  No endpoint
    ordering, endpoint orientation, or endpoint-only planted split can make
    this bank simple.

Consequently a four-block physicalization must be a genuinely different
object: it must change interior owner occurrences on a linear bank, not
just plant the seven source rays at block ends.  Such a nonflat quotient
weave remains possible in principle, but it must reprove owner simplicity,
both palettes, residence, the complete source deck, and the common-cap
matching.  It is not a corollary of the source-signature identity.

## 1. The internal overlapping-split obstruction

Let `A=(A_0,...,A_(N-1))` be a source and

\[
                 O_i=\bigcup_{j=i}^{i+d}A_j             \tag{1.1}
\]

its flat depth-`d` owner rows.  Suppose that for some internal `p`,

\[
                 A_p=X,\qquad A_{p+1}=B\subseteq X.      \tag{1.2}
\]

At the transition for which `B` enters and `X` remains in the window, the
incoming letter contributes no coordinate.  Hence

\[
                         O_{i+1}\subseteq O_i.           \tag{1.3}
\]

If both rows have rank `r`, then `O_{i+1}=O_i`; they are not a Johnson edge.
For the reversed order `B,X`, use the transition at which `B` exits while
`X` remains.  Again one row contains the other, and equal rank forces a
stutter.

The qualifier *internal* is essential.  One can hide the bad transition
beyond a linear endpoint.  This is exactly why four blocks appear to offer
eight sockets.  Formula (0.3) shows why that endpoint escape still does not
physicalize the literal Klein bank.

The neutral seventh phase-one split `X -> X,X` is included: it gives an
immediate repeated-owner transition whenever it is internal.

## 2. Endpoint collars leave a complete repeated core

Let `T^e` be the `8d+23`-row owner path of octagon phase `e`.  Form the four
rows

\[
 \rho_{00}T^{e_{00}},\ \rho_{01}T^{e_{01}},\
 \rho_{10}T^{e_{10}},\ \rho_{11}T^{e_{11}},             \tag{2.1}
\]

where `(e_00,e_01,e_10,e_11)=0110` (or its complement) and the `rho` are
the Klein relabelings above.  Remove indices `0,...,d-1` and
`7d+23,...,8d+22` from every row.  Directly expanding the eight coatom
blocks and seven screens gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
 m_2&=4d-6,\\
 m_3&=16,\\
 m_4&=4d+14.                                             \tag{2.2}
\end{aligned}
\]

These sum to `8d+24` distinct values, and

\[
           2m_2+3m_3+4m_4=24d+92.                       \tag{2.3}
\]

The same formulas hold for `1001`.  An endpoint edit whose support lies in
the first or last source collar can change only the discarded owner rows.
Therefore all values in (2.2) remain duplicated.

This is stronger than a scalar excess count: there is no small exceptional
owner set for the endpoint hosts to absorb.  The entire literal owner set is
repeated in the protected interior.

## 3. The uniform private-tag dichotomy

One might try to distinguish the four copies by adjoining a private block
tag `t_uv` to every source letter of block `uv`.  This does make coincident
owners different.  It destroys the source-signature cancellation.

Indeed, an interval internal to block `uv` then has tag set exactly
`{t_uv}`.  No interval internal to a different block, and no interval
crossing a block boundary, has that same tag set.  Equality of the tagged
four-block decks would therefore imply, block by block,

\[
       \operatorname{Deck}(\rho_{uv}Q^0)
       =\operatorname{Deck}(\rho_{uv}Q^1),               \tag{3.1}
\]

which is false: the sharp directed differences have sizes `4d+1` and
`6d-3`.  A common tag preserves the source identity but does not separate
owners.  Thus uniform per-block tags cannot solve both rows simultaneously.

This does **not** exclude a position-dependent shared-rail weave.  Such a
weave is not a tag decoration of the literal four blocks; it is a new
source construction.

## 4. Exact gate for an edge-shared K2,2 rail weave

For a proposed position-dependent rail word `H_{uv,p}`, put

\[
 h_{uv}(I)=\bigcup_{p\in I}H_{uv,p},\qquad
 q^e_{uv}(I)=\rho_{uv}\!\left(\bigcup_{p\in I}Q^e_p\right). \tag{4.1}
\]

The exact source-signature row is the family of multiset equalities

\[
 \bigl\{( |I|,q^{u\oplus v}_{uv}(I)\cup h_{uv}(I)):
              uv,I\bigr\}
 =
 \bigl\{( |I|,q^{1\oplus u\oplus v}_{uv}(I)\cup h_{uv}(I)):
              uv,I\bigr\}.                              \tag{4.2}
\]

Equation (4.2) is only the additive within-block signature row.  A literal
four-block concatenation must additionally match every interval crossing
one, two, or three block seams.  For full contextual transparency these
rows must be supplemented by pointwise graded prefix and suffix equalities.
At owner width `d+1`, the
same rails must instead separate every repeated owner occurrence:

\[
 \rho_{uv}(T^{u\oplus v}_i)\cup h_{uv}([i,i+d])
 \quad\text{are all distinct and form the required owner set}. \tag{4.3}
\]

They must also make adjacent rows Johnson, keep every coordinate run at the
required floor, and lie in one phase-common cap.  Equations (4.2)--(4.3) are
the precise short-deck/owner-separation tension.  A claimed `K_{2,2}`
edge-shared construction is valid only after giving an occurrence bijection
for (4.2), not merely untagged cancellation, and a literal owner bijection
for (4.3), not merely four distinct endpoint labels.

The present no-go proves that `H` cannot be constant on each block and
cannot be supported only in the endpoint `d`-collars.  It does not prove
that no position-dependent solution of (4.2)--(4.3) exists.

There is, however, a sharp no-go for the most natural edge-shared rail.  Put
a row-private marker at one end of each block and a column-private marker at
distance `d`.  Every interval of width at most `d` sees at most one marker,
whereas a width-`d+1` owner window can see their ordered pair.  The owner
idea is sound, but short-deck cancellation would require **both** two-block
marginals

\[
\begin{aligned}
 D_{u0}^{u}+D_{u1}^{1-u}&=D_{u0}^{1-u}+D_{u1}^{u},\\
 D_{0v}^{v}+D_{1v}^{1-v}&=D_{0v}^{1-v}+D_{1v}^{v}        \tag{4.4}
\end{aligned}
\]

at every residual width, where `D_uv^e` is the relabelled graded deck of
block `uv` in phase `e`.  The exact permutation census, independently at
every `2<=d<=8` and for both the sharp and guarded blocks, gives

\[
 \#(\text{row only},\text{column only},\text{both},\text{neither})
                         =(2,2,0,6).                     \tag{4.5}
\]

Here (4.5) refers to the restriction to widths at most `d`.  For the
particularly natural choice `alpha=(a0 a2)`, `beta=(a1 a3)`, the column
marginals hold and the row marginals fail.  Thus the uncorrected two-anchor
`K_{2,2}` rail is impossible.  The seven planted rays may be viewed as a
candidate correction of the one failed marginal; Sections 1--2 show why
realizing that correction by flat internal or endpoint-only hosts still
does not physicalize the owner bank.

The finite census is enough to refute a single claimed dimension-uniform
raw two-anchor construction.  It is not presented as an all-`d`
classification of every possible position-dependent rail.

## 5. Scope correction on d-letter overlaps

The scalar calculation

\[
 4(9d+25)-3d-d=4(8d+25)                                \tag{5.1}
\]

says only that three hypothetical `d`-letter overlaps preserve the total
number of width-`d+1` windows.  It does **not** exhibit literal common
overlap words and does not prove that no owner windows are identified.
Any use of such overlaps must list the shared letters and replay their
owner rows.  The no-go above does not rely on (5.1).
