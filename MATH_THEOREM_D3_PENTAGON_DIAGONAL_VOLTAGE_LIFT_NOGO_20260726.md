# Diagonal voltage lifts of the \(D_3\) pentagon: exact carrier gain and statewise no-go

Date: 2026-07-26

Method: pure mathematics only.

## 0. Verdict

Fix \(k\) disjoint product slots carrying the same rooted \(D_3\)
pentagon overlay.  Label the five local rows by

\[
                              \Omega=\mathbb F_5.                 \tag{0.1}
\]

After the common root-owner matching is contracted, the local ownership
overlay contains the five-cycle

\[
                              c:x\longmapsto x+1.                 \tag{0.2}
\]

The product row set is

\[
                              R=\Omega^k=\mathbb F_5^k.           \tag{0.3}
\]

The copy of (0.2) in slot \(i\) is the coordinate translation

\[
                              c_i:x\longmapsto x+e_i.             \tag{0.4}
\]

Consequently the ordinary product overlay contains the regular translation
group

\[
                    C=\langle c_1,\ldots,c_k\rangle
                       \cong C_5^k,                              \tag{0.5}
\]

and every fixed exterior product fibre is one ownership component of
size \(5^k\).

Thus the common shorthand “\(5^k\) components” needs care: within one
fixed product skeleton the canonical tensorization gives **one
\(5^k\)-row component**.  Exterior skeleton choices may provide many
copies of this component, but do not fragment it internally.

A diagonal or more general subdirect subgroup

\[
                         H\le\mathbb F_5^k,\qquad
                         \operatorname{pr}_iH=\mathbb F_5        \tag{0.6}
\]

would have exactly the desired *slot-projected tagged* carrier count.  On
one \(H\)-coset every local pentagon row occurs \(|H|/5\) times in every
slot, so this projected carrier change is

\[
                   \Delta_H
                     =\frac{|H|}{5}\sum_{i=1}^k\Delta_i.         \tag{0.7}
\]

When the slot tags are disjoint,

\[
                   \|\Delta_H\|_1
                     =\frac{k|H|}{5}\|\Delta_{\rm loc}\|_1.      \tag{0.8}
\]

Hence the diagonal \(H=\langle(1,\ldots,1)\rangle\), of size five, would
give \(\Theta(k)\) carrier action on a five-row packet.

It is not an exact \(X/Y\)-ownership component.  Statewise exactness forces
every \(c_i\) to preserve every selectable packet.  A coset of \(H\) is
\(c_i\)-invariant only if \(e_i\in H\).  Requiring this for all \(i\)
gives

\[
                              H=\mathbb F_5^k.                    \tag{0.9}
\]

There is a stronger voltage statement.  Allow a different arbitrary row
gauge \(g_i\in\operatorname{Sym}(R)\) in every slot.  Exact ownership at
the interface between slots \(i-1\) and \(i\) contributes the transition
voltage

\[
                              q_i=g_i g_{i-1}^{-1},               \tag{0.10}
\]

while the pentagon token in slot \(i\) contributes

\[
                              h_i=g_i c_i g_i^{-1}.               \tag{0.11}
\]

The holonomy group generated by all \(q_i,h_i\) contains a conjugate of
the full group \(C_5^k\), and is therefore transitive on \(R\).  Thus
phase-dependent diagonalization cannot reduce the component size.  If the
transition voltages are omitted, the shared boundary tokens have different
owners and the putative packet has a nonzero \(X/Y\) defect.

Accordingly:

\[
\boxed{\text{No statewise diagonal or poly-size subdirect lift exists
within the product/gauge category.}}                          \tag{0.12}
\]

The only exact nonempty switch in one product fibre is the entire
\(5^k\)-row component.  It has carrier action across all \(k\) slots, but
retains the exponential fragmentation cost.  Escaping (0.12) requires a
genuinely new cross-slot factor whose token owner maps are not gauge
transforms of the independent pentagon catalogues.

## 1. Ownership overlays and the local five-cycle

Let \(F^-\) and \(F^+\) be the two exact rooted D3 pentagon factors.  Each
has five rows and the same rooted ports.  Form the bipartite ownership
overlay: its left vertices are the five rows of \(F^-\), its right
vertices the five rows of \(F^+\), and every physical \(X\)-state or
\(Y\)-colour joins its two owners.

The common root tokens give the identity matching between the two shores.
Contract them.  The explicit pentagon owner routing contains a five-cycle;
after a cyclic choice of labels it is exactly (0.2).  Therefore the local
overlay is connected.

For completeness, at the first internal \(X\)-phase the old row owners
and new row owners are

\[
\begin{array}{c|ccccc}
\text{token}&136&126&145&235&234\\ \hline
\text{old row}&1&2&3&4&5\\
\text{new row}&4&1&5&3&2.
\end{array}                                                   \tag{1.1}
\]

Thus the relative owner permutation is

\[
                              (1\ 4\ 3\ 5\ 2),                    \tag{1.2}
\]

a five-cycle.  Relabel its cyclic order by \(\mathbb F_5\) to obtain
(0.2).

Only this one local fact is required below:

\[
 \boxed{\text{some owned-token phase induces the translation \(c\) on
 the contracted five-row set.}}                                \tag{1.3}
\]

Additional local owner permutations can only merge components further.

## 2. Independent product slots

Fix a product skeleton with \(k\) marked slots.  A product row is a tuple

\[
                              x=(x_1,\ldots,x_k)\in R.             \tag{2.1}
\]

For each slot \(i\), freeze every other local row label and use the token
from (1.1) in slot \(i\).  Its owner edge joins

\[
                (x_1,\ldots,x_i,\ldots,x_k)
                  \quad\text{to}\quad
                (x_1,\ldots,x_i+1,\ldots,x_k),                   \tag{2.2}
\]

which is \(c_i\).

### Theorem 2.1 (Cartesian component theorem)

The ownership overlay of the two independent \(k\)-slot tensor factors is
connected on \(R\).  Its component has \(5^k\) rows on each shore.

#### Proof

The coordinate translations \(c_i\) occur among the contracted overlay
edges by (2.2).  They generate the regular translation group \(C\) in
(0.5).  Given \(x,y\in R\), the word

\[
                    c_1^{\,y_1-x_1}\cdots
                    c_k^{\,y_k-x_k}                              \tag{2.3}
\]

sends \(x\) to \(y\).  Hence every two rows lie in one component.
\(\square\)

This is exactly why choosing old/new independently at the \(k\) slots is
not a \(k\)-bit component cube.  Ownership closure joins all \(5^k\)
fillings before any component may be switched.

## 3. The tempting subdirect diagonal packet

Let \(H\le R\).  Think of its cosets as proposed overlay packets.  It is
subdirect when every coordinate projection is onto, as in (0.6).

Let \(\Delta_{\rm loc}\) be any authoritative signed carrier ledger for
one pentagon switch after projecting to its local slot coordinates.
Examples are:

\[
             \delta_{\rm pent}=2e_3+e_5-e_4-2e_2,
             \qquad \|\delta_{\rm pent}\|_1=6,                   \tag{3.1}
\]

for the intrinsic singleton target, and the once-suspended complete
depth-one ledger, whose \(\ell^1\)-norm is eighteen.

Give the carrier in slot \(i\) a separate tag, writing its copy as
\(\Delta_i\).  In a coset \(a+H\), each value of coordinate \(i\) occurs
exactly \(|H|/5\) times because the projection is a surjective homomorphism
with equal fibres.  Summing the local pentagon direction proves (0.7).
Disjoint tags prevent cancellation between the \(k\) summands and prove
(0.8).

This calculation deliberately suppresses physical collars and the other
slot coordinates.  It is an exact marginal ledger, and therefore the
most favourable possible interpretation of the diagonal proposal; it is
not asserted to be an isometry of the final untagged target histogram.

For the diagonal line

\[
                              H_\Delta
                                =\{t(1,\ldots,1):t\in\mathbb F_5\}, \tag{3.2}
\]

one obtains a five-row formal packet carrying one complete local
pentagon action in every slot.  This is the desired action--fragmentation
ratio at the level of marginal ledgers.

## 4. Exact statewise obstruction

### Theorem 4.1 (subdirect invariance no-go)

Let \(H\le R\).  Suppose every coset of \(H\) is a union of ownership
components of the product overlay.  If the pentagon is active in every
slot, then

\[
                              H=R.                               \tag{4.1}
\]

#### Proof

The component containing \(0\) is invariant under every overlay generator.
In particular \(c_i(H)=H\).  Since \(c_i\) is translation by \(e_i\),
this is equivalent to \(e_i\in H\).  The vectors
\(e_1,\ldots,e_k\) generate \(R\), proving (4.1). \(\square\)

The subdirect hypothesis is not actually needed for the no-go.  It is
only the hypothesis which makes the carrier formula (0.7) attractive.

### Corollary 4.2 (no exact diagonal signing)

For \(k\ge2\), the diagonal coset (3.2) is not independently switchable.
Indeed,

\[
                         (H_\Delta+e_i)\cap H_\Delta=\varnothing, \tag{4.2}
\]

because \(e_i\) is not a scalar multiple of \((1,\ldots,1)\).
Thus the slot-\(i\) owner matching sends all five rows of the proposed
packet to a disjoint diagonal coset.

If one chooses the \(F^+\) shore only on \(H_\Delta\), the corresponding
owner matching in slot \(i\) has five edges from \(H_\Delta\) to
\(H_\Delta+e_i\), and five edges from \(H_\Delta-e_i\) into
\(H_\Delta\).  The first five tokens are omitted in the hybrid and the
second five are duplicated.  With the slot/phase tags retained,

\[
                          \|\varepsilon_X\|_1\ge10k.              \tag{4.3}
\]

This is a literal ownership defect, not merely a failure of a preferred
row labelling.

More generally, let \(S\subseteq R\) be the rows on which the plus shore is
chosen.  Exact ownership forces

\[
                              S+c_i=S\qquad(1\le i\le k).         \tag{4.4}
\]

Since \(C\) is transitive, (4.4) has only

\[
                              S=\varnothing\quad\text{or}\quad S=R. \tag{4.5}
\]

This is the statewise form of Theorem 2.1.

## 5. Phase gauges and the voltage-lift no-go

One might try to conjugate each coordinate translation separately so that
all of them become one common diagonal translation.  Exact boundary
ownership records the changes of gauge and restores the missing rank.

Let \(g_0,\ldots,g_k\in\operatorname{Sym}(R)\) be arbitrary gauges at the
successive slot interfaces.  The shared boundary-token owner matching
between interfaces \(i-1\) and \(i\) has voltage

\[
                              q_i=g_i g_{i-1}^{-1}.               \tag{5.1}
\]

The active pentagon token in slot \(i\), read in gauge \(g_i\), has voltage

\[
                              h_i=g_i c_i g_i^{-1}.               \tag{5.2}
\]

Let

\[
                              G=\langle q_i,h_i:1\le i\le k\rangle. \tag{5.3}
\]

### Theorem 5.1 (gauge-invariant full holonomy)

The group \(G\) contains

\[
                              g_0 C g_0^{-1}.                    \tag{5.4}
\]

In particular \(G\) is transitive on \(R\), and every voltage-lift
component contains all \(5^k\) rows.

#### Proof

Put

\[
                       Q_i=q_iq_{i-1}\cdots q_1
                            =g_i g_0^{-1}.                       \tag{5.5}
\]

Both \(Q_i\) and \(h_i\) lie in \(G\).  Therefore

\[
                       Q_i^{-1}h_iQ_i
                           =g_0c_i g_0^{-1}\in G.                \tag{5.6}
\]

As \(i\) varies, these conjugates generate \(g_0Cg_0^{-1}\), proving
(5.4).  Since \(C\) acts regularly on \(R\), its conjugate is transitive.
\(\square\)

The conclusion remains true even if the \(h_i\)'s have all been made
equal to one diagonal permutation: the transition voltages \(q_i\) retain
the independent-coordinate holonomy.

### Corollary 5.2 (omitting transitions is an ownership defect)

Suppose one deletes the boundary voltage \(q_i\) to keep only the desired
diagonal local voltages.  Then the two rows joined by the physical shared
boundary token are assigned to different proposed components.  Switching
one component duplicates that token on one shore and omits it on the
other.  Hence deletion of \(q_i\) is incompatible with exact \(X/Y\)
ownership or port closure.

## 6. Exact carrier ledger of the only legal product component

The only nonempty component switch in one product fibre replaces the old
pentagon factor by the new one in all \(k\) slots on all \(5^k\) rows.
For a fixed slot \(i\), every assignment of the other \(k-1\) row labels
supports one local five-row pentagon packet.  There are \(5^{k-1}\) such
assignments.  Therefore the complete tagged carrier direction is

\[
                         \Delta_{\rm full}
                            =5^{k-1}\sum_{i=1}^k\Delta_i.         \tag{6.1}
\]

With disjoint slot tags,

\[
                         \|\Delta_{\rm full}\|_1
                            =k5^{k-1}\|\Delta_{\rm loc}\|_1.      \tag{6.2}
\]

For the intrinsic D3 direction (3.1), this is

\[
                              6k5^{k-1},                          \tag{6.3}
\]

and for the once-suspended complete depth-one ledger it is

\[
                              18k5^{k-1}.                         \tag{6.4}
\]

These are exact tagged ledgers.  A physical untagged target quotient may
identify or cancel targets from different slots, so (6.2)--(6.4) are
upper/action identities before that quotient.

The component has \(5^k\) rows per shore.  Its tagged action per row is

\[
                         \frac{k}{5}\|\Delta_{\rm loc}\|_1,       \tag{6.5}
\]

which is linear in \(k\), exactly as desired.  The exponential component
size is the unavoidable cost within the product/gauge category.  On this
fixed fibre its size-biased component moment is exactly

\[
                              \chi_k=5^k.                          \tag{6.6}
\]

## 7. Scope of the no-go

The theorem closes the following proposed mechanisms.

1. Restricting the ordinary product overlay to diagonal or other
   poly-size subdirect cosets.
2. Reindexing the plus shore globally.
3. Using a different row gauge in every slot to conjugate all local
   pentagon translations into one diagonal translation.
4. Dropping the gauge-transition owner edges while still claiming exact
   \(X/Y\) ownership.

It does not rule out a genuinely new cross-slot factor.  Such a factor
would have to replace the independent token maps (2.2) by owner
permutations generating a proper subdirect group from the outset, while
still enumerating every product \(X\)-state and \(Y\)-colour exactly once
and preserving literal port closure.  It cannot be obtained by a voltage
gauge of the independent D3 catalogues.

Thus the diagonal idea succeeds at the marginal carrier calculation
(0.7)--(0.8) and fails exactly at statewise ownership closure.
