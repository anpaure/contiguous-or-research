# Raw lead report: wreath trades and Markov basis

# Verdict

This route does **not** prove the contiguous-OR conjecture or labelled synchronization. It does, however, settle the signed trade lattice, characterize the factor-feasible Markov moves, and reduce legal transposition descent to an exact weighted Max-Cut problem.

The obstruction is no longer algebraic: it is the failure to prove that high-energy exact factors admit enough positively correlated ownership components, simultaneously across all depths and owners.

## 1. Exact integral lower-shadow lattice

Let \(A_r\) be cyclic \(r\)-interval incidence on cyclic orders, and let \(U_r\) be point-versus-\(r\)-set incidence.

### Theorem 1 — integral saturation

For \(2\le r\le n-2\),

\[
\ker_{\mathbb Z}U_r
\]

is generated by the rectangles

\[
e_{Kac}-e_{Kbc}-e_{Kad}+e_{Kbd},
\qquad |K|=r-2,
\]

where \(a,b,c,d\notin K\) are distinct. Consequently,

\[
\boxed{
(A_2,\ldots,A_{m-1})(\ker_{\mathbb Z}A_m)
=
\bigoplus_{r=2}^{m-1}\ker_{\mathbb Z}U_r.
}
\tag{1}
\]

Thus every simultaneous integer lower-rank correction having zero point margins has an integer signed lift preserving every middle row.

### Proof

Let \(\mathscr R_r\) be the rectangle lattice. Clearly \(\mathscr R_r\subseteq\ker_{\mathbb Z}U_r\).

Work over an arbitrary field. If \(g\) annihilates every rectangle, then for fixed \(a,b\),

\[
g(T+a)-g(T+b)
\]

is independent of the \((r-1)\)-set \(T\) avoiding \(a,b\): rectangle identities equate this difference on adjacent vertices of

\[
J([n]\setminus\{a,b\},r-1),
\]

and this Johnson graph is connected for \(2\le r\le n-2\). Write the common difference as \(d_{a,b}\). The cocycle identity gives

\[
d_{a,b}+d_{b,c}=d_{a,c}.
\]

Hence there are scalars \(a_i\) with \(d_{i,j}=a_i-a_j\), and Johnson connectivity then gives

\[
g(S)=c+\sum_{i\in S}a_i.
\]

The parameter map \((c,a_1,\ldots,a_n)\mapsto g\) has one-dimensional kernel: all \(a_i=t\) and \(c=-rt\). This remains true when the characteristic divides \(r\). Therefore the annihilator has dimension \(n\), so the rectangle span has dimension \(\binom nr-n\) over every field.

Now \(\ker_{\mathbb Z}U_r\) is primitive and has rank \(\binom nr-n\). If \(\mathscr R_r\) had nontrivial finite index in it, some prime would lower the rank of its reduction, contradicting the field calculation. Thus

\[
\mathscr R_r=\ker_{\mathbb Z}U_r.
\]

Each rectangle has a coefficient-one Petr–Turek selector lift in \(\ker_{\mathbb Z}A_m\), vanishing at every other lower rank. Summing these lifts rank by rank proves (1). ∎

This completely removes integer-index and cross-rank divisibility obstructions. It does **not** give a nonnegative or support-feasible factor: the selector lifts have colliding wreaths on both signs.

## 2. Exact-factor Markov and Graver moves

### Theorem 2 — factor-fibre decomposition

Let \(F,F'\) be exact factors. Cancel their common wreaths and form the bipartite multigraph whose left vertices are \(F\setminus F'\), whose right vertices are \(F'\setminus F\), and whose edges are middle sets, joining their unique old and new owning wreaths.

Then:

1. the graph is \(n\)-regular;
2. each connected component \(K\) defines a squarefree support-feasible move
   \[
   z_K=\mathbf1_{K\cap F'}-\mathbf1_{K\cap F}\in\ker_{\mathbb Z}A_m;
   \]
3. \(z_K\) is conformally indecomposable, hence a Graver element;
4. switching the components one at a time connects \(F\) to \(F'\) through exact factors.

### Proof

Every selected wreath contains \(n\) middle sets, giving degree \(n\). Inside a component, the two sides cover exactly the same edge set, so their incidence difference lies in the kernel.

If \(0\ne u\sqsubseteq z_K\) is a conformal kernel submove, then for every ownership edge its two endpoints must either both be selected by \(u\) or both omitted. Thus the selected vertices form a union of graph components. Connectedness leaves only \(u=0\) or \(u=z_K\). ∎

Hence the connected support-feasible bitrades are exactly the squarefree packing-compatible Graver moves relevant to the exact \(A_mx=\mathbf1\) fibre.

This is an abstract exact-fibre Markov basis, but it gives no uniform support bound: components may have factor-scale size.

## 3. Bounded selectors fail; genuine bounded trades exist

If \(d_\circ(C,D)\) is adjacent-swap distance between cyclic orders, then

\[
|\mathcal W_m(C)\cap\mathcal W_m(D)|
\ge n-2d_\circ(C,D).
\tag{2}
\]

Indeed, one adjacent swap changes only two middle intervals. Therefore middle-disjoint wreaths satisfy

\[
d_\circ(C,D)\ge m+1.
\tag{3}
\]

A selector square has cyclic-order diameter at most two, so neither sign is a middle packing.

More generally:

\[
\boxed{
\text{every nonzero connected support-feasible selector composite uses at least }
\left\lceil\frac{m+1}{2}\right\rceil
\text{ cells}.}
\tag{4}
\]

For if \(s\) connected cells had \(2s\le m\), all surviving orders would be pairwise middle-overlapping. Each sign could contain at most one order. The kernel equation would then give \(e_C-e_D\) with identical middle interval families; those families reconstruct the unoriented cyclic order, so \(C=D\), a contradiction.

Thus selector generators are necessarily mesoscopic.

In contrast, the legal MSW four-letter \(2\)-for-\(2\) trades are genuinely bounded. Their depth actions satisfy

\[
\|\delta_1\|_2^2=4,\qquad
\|\delta_q\|_2^2=8\quad(q\ge2),
\tag{5}
\]

but their complete first-shadow projections are linearly independent and touch only \(O(1/m)\) of the first-shadow layer. They are sparse absorbers, not a bulk frame, and contain no nonzero combination that fixes depth one while changing a deeper rank.

## 4. Fixed-window plateaus

For fixed \(A>0\), let \(H_A=\lceil A\sqrt m\rceil\). Uniformly for \(q\le H_A\),

\[
\log\frac W{N_q}
=\frac{q^2}{m}+O_A(m^{-1/2}).
\tag{6}
\]

Therefore \(c_q\) is bounded by a constant depending only on \(A\). More precisely,

\[
\frac1{\sqrt m}\sum_{q\le H_A}\frac1{c_q}
\longrightarrow
\int_0^A\frac{dx}{\lfloor e^{x^2}\rfloor},
\tag{7}
\]

and

\[
\frac1m\sum_{q\le H_A}\frac q{c_q}
\longrightarrow
\int_0^A\frac{x\,dx}{\lfloor e^{x^2}\rfloor}.
\tag{8}
\]

These are ordinary Riemann sums; the integrands have only finitely many discontinuities on a fixed interval.

In particular, the \(c_q=1\) plateau ends at

\[
q_1=(\sqrt{\log2}+o(1))\sqrt m.
\tag{9}
\]

Hence bounded four-letter moves have weighted curvature \(\Theta_A(\sqrt m)\), while the universal balanced-\(C_8\) estimate permits \(O_A(m)\). The weights do not erase the plateau.

## 5. Exact mobile-quota and orbit obstructions

Write \(W=cN+r\). For a load vector \(\mu\), define

\[
D^-=\sum_S(c-\mu(S))_+,\qquad
D^+=\sum_S(\mu(S)-c-1)_+.
\]

Then the minimum floor/ceiling-balanced overload is exactly

\[
\boxed{O(\mu)=\max(D^-,D^+).}
\tag{10}
\]

Moreover, the pair-collision excess

\[
\Phi(\mu)=\frac12\sum_S(\mu(S)-c)(\mu(S)-c-1)
\]

satisfies

\[
\Phi\ge D^-+D^+\ge O.
\tag{11}
\]

If \(\mu=c\mathbf1+\mathbf1_{\mathcal H}\) is already balanced and

\[
\delta=\mathbf1_P-\mathbf1_M,
\qquad P\cap M=\varnothing,
\]

then

\[
\boxed{
O(\mu+\delta)
=
\max\{|P\cap\mathcal H|,\ |M\setminus\mathcal H|\}.
}
\tag{12}
\]

Thus a plateau-neutral move must simultaneously remove only high targets and add only low targets. For a multidepth trade this checkerboard condition must hold at every depth with the same move sign.

For a fixed transposition \(\tau\), every component move has the form

\[
A_{m-q}z_K=(\tau-I)a_{K,q}.
\tag{13}
\]

Hence every \(\tau\)-fixed target load and every two-point \(\tau\)-orbit total is invariant.

If \(O\) ranges over these one- and two-point orbits, put

\[
t_O=\sum_{S\in O}\mu_q(S),\qquad
p_O=(t_O-c_q|O|)_+,
\]

and write \(W=c_qN_q+r_q\). Even allowing arbitrary redistribution inside every orbit, every cube vertex obeys

\[
\boxed{
O_q\ge
\sum_Op_O-
\min\left\{
r_q,\,
\sum_O\min(|O|,p_O)
\right\}.
}
\tag{14}
\]

This follows by assigning \(h_O\) of the \(r_q\) high quotas to orbit \(O\): its overload is at least \((p_O-h_O)_+\), and optimizing the \(h_O\)'s gives (14).

Thus component fragmentation cannot repair bad orbit totals.

## 6. Exact Hilbert Max-Cut identity

Put

\[
\Psi_H(F)=\sum_{q\le H}\frac{\Phi_q(F)}{c_q},
\qquad
\lambda_q=\frac W{N_q},
\qquad
f_q=\mu_q-\lambda_q\mathbf1.
\]

Use the weighted Hilbert inner product

\[
\langle x,y\rangle_H
=\sum_{q\le H}\frac{\langle x_q,y_q\rangle_2}{c_q}.
\]

Fix a coordinate transposition \(\tau\). For every ownership component \(K\), let

\[
d_K=a_K-\tau a_K,\qquad d=\sum_Kd_K=f-\tau f.
\]

For a subset \(I\) of components, let \(F_I\) switch exactly the components in \(I\), and put \(d_I=\sum_{K\in I}d_K\).

### Theorem 3 — exact component-cut law

\[
\boxed{
\Psi_H(F_I)-\Psi_H(F)
=
-\frac12\langle d_I,d_{I^c}\rangle_H.
}
\tag{15}
\]

Equivalently, with

\[
A_\tau=\|d\|_H^2,\qquad
R_\tau(I)=\|d-2d_I\|_H^2,
\]

\[
\Psi_H(F_I)-\Psi_H(F)
=\frac18(R_\tau(I)-A_\tau).
\tag{16}
\]

### Proof

Since \(\tau^2=1\),

\[
f(F_I)
=\frac{f+\tau f}{2}+\frac{d-2d_I}{2}.
\]

The two summands are respectively \(\tau\)-invariant and anti-invariant, hence orthogonal. The integer-floor subtraction in \(\Phi_q\) is factor-independent, so it cancels. Expanding the squared norms gives (15)–(16). ∎

Consequently,

\[
\boxed{
\Psi_H(F)-\min_I\Psi_H(F_I)
=
\frac12\max_I\langle d_I,d_{I^c}\rangle_H.
}
\tag{17}
\]

This is a weighted Max-Cut problem on the Gram graph of component effects. Strict descent occurs exactly when some component cut has positive total correlation.

Fair independent signs give

\[
\mathbb E_I\Psi_H(F_I)
=
\Psi_H(F)-\frac18(A_\tau-V_\tau),
\qquad
V_\tau=\sum_K\|d_K\|_H^2.
\tag{18}
\]

The fair condition \(A_\tau>V_\tau\) is sufficient, but not necessary; correlated signs can succeed when fair resampling fails.

Two exact obstructions remain:

- If the ownership overlay is connected, it has one component, \(A_\tau=V_\tau\), every cut correlation is zero, and the only children are \(F,\tau F\).
- If the component effects are mutually orthogonal, again every cut correlation is zero.

## 7. The scale-correct heat target and why \(\gamma\) does not simplify

Let

\[
\mathcal Q_H=2\Psi_H.
\]

For the uniform-transposition, fair-component heat kernel \(\mathcal P\), define the audited \(\Gamma_H\) and higher-harmonic surplus \(\mathcal S_H\). Then exactly

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal P\mathcal Q_H
=
\left(1-\frac2n\right)\mathcal Q_H
+\Gamma_H-\mathcal S_H.
}
\tag{19}
\]

Moreover,

\[
\boxed{
\Gamma_H-\mathcal S_H
=
\mathcal P\mathcal Q_H-
\left(1-\frac2n\right)\mathcal Q_H.
}
\tag{20}
\]

Thus \(\Gamma-\mathcal S\) is already the discounted coboundary of \(\mathcal Q\). Its discounted sum telescopes exactly to terminal energy; a generic “coboundary estimate” is therefore tautologically as hard as controlling \(\mathcal Q\).

The master’s scale-correct sufficient statement is:

\[
\boxed{
\Gamma_H(F)-\mathcal S_H(F)
\le
\frac{2-\eta_A}{n}\mathcal Q_H(F)
+\frac{C_A}{n}H_A\operatorname{Cat}_m.
}
\tag{MHG\(_A\)}
\]

It is equivalent to

\[
\mathcal P\mathcal Q_H(F)
\le
\left(1-\frac{\eta_A}{n}\right)\mathcal Q_H(F)
+\frac{C_A}{n}H_A\operatorname{Cat}_m.
\tag{21}
\]

Iterating \(n^2\) steps gives

\[
\mathbb E\mathcal Q_H(F_{n^2})
\le
e^{-\eta_A n}\mathcal Q_H(F_0)
+\frac{C_A}{\eta_A}H_A\operatorname{Cat}_m.
\tag{22}
\]

The first term is \(o(W)\). Indeed,

\[
\mu_q(S)\le\binom{m+q+1}{q}
\le \exp(O_A(\sqrt m\log m)),
\]

because each occurrence has a distinct middle owner containing \(S\). Hence

\[
\mathcal Q_H(F_0)
\le
H_AW\exp(o(n)),
\]

which is killed by \(e^{-\eta_A n}\). Also,

\[
\frac{H_A\operatorname{Cat}_m}{W}
=\frac{H_A}{n}
=O_A(m^{-1/2}).
\]

Therefore \((MHG_A)\) proves the fixed-window weighted-overload target.

However, \((MHG_A)\) is strong: the fair heat kernel is doubly stochastic, so averaging (21) under the uniform distribution on all exact factors gives

\[
\mathbb E_{\rm unif}\mathcal Q_H
\le
\frac{C_A}{\eta_A}H_A\operatorname{Cat}_m.
\tag{23}
\]

Thus it proves that the **average** exact factor is good, not merely that one good factor exists. No supplied argument establishes this.

## 8. Smallest transposition-cube replacement lemma

The weakest natural lemma for this move class is the following.

> **Unproved local-minimum lemma \(LM_A\).**  
> If an exact factor \(F\) satisfies
> \[
> \langle d_I,d_{I^c}\rangle_H\le0
> \]
> for every coordinate transposition and every union \(I\) of ownership components, then
> \[
> \mathcal Q_{H_A}(F)=O_A(H_A\operatorname{Cat}_m).
> \]

This is enough: whenever a positive cut exists, (15) gives a strict exact-factor descent. The state space is finite, so descent reaches a cut-local minimum; \(LM_A\) then gives \(o(W)\).

A quantitative \(O(n)\)-step version would require the accumulated positive component-cut correlation to be a fixed fraction of the accumulated transposition displacement, up to \(O_A(H_A\operatorname{Cat}_m)\). This is strictly weaker than fair one-step \(MHG_A\) and allows neutral routing and correlated signs.

It remains unproved. A high-energy factor whose transposition overlays are all connected or correlation-cut nonpositive would be a counterexample.

## 9. Exact labelled-ownership obstruction

The heat argument controls histograms, not labelled synchronization.

Fix one balanced nested resolution \(P\). In one transposition cube, for component \(K\), classify every weighted owner-depth pair according to its old/new pointed labels:

- \(U_K\): neither candidate equals \(P_q(X)\);
- \(A_K\): only the old candidate equals \(P_q(X)\);
- \(B_K\): only the new candidate equals \(P_q(X)\).

Choosing the old side costs \(U_K+B_K\); choosing the new side costs \(U_K+A_K\). Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\min_{\text{cube vertices }F_\varepsilon}
\sum_{q\le H}\frac{e_q(F_\varepsilon,P)}{c_q}
=
\sum_K\bigl(U_K+\min(A_K,B_K)\bigr).
}
\tag{24}
\]

The same component bit controls every owner and every depth. This is the exact simultaneous compatibility condition; optimizing the depths separately is invalid.

Accordingly, to derive the literal fixed-window statement \((CA_A)\) from histogram descent, one still needs the following common-flow retraction theorem:

> **Unproved nested retraction lemma.**
> \[
> \min_{P\text{ balanced nested}}
> \sum_{q\le H_A}\frac{e_q(F,P)}{c_q}
> \le
> C_A'
> \sum_{q\le H_A}\frac{O_q(F)}{c_q}
> +o(W).
> \tag{25}
> \]

Independent floor/ceiling quota vectors do not prove (25); they can violate adjacent-layer Hall constraints. The proof must reconfigure one common nested flow.

Thus, absent an external theorem equating the histogram and labelled fixed-window formulations, \(MHG_A\) proves the final OR bound but not the stronger labelled \((CA_A)\).

## 10. Conditional final OR estimate

Since

\[
c_qM_q\le O_q\le\Phi_q,
\]

we have

\[
\sum_{q\le H}M_q
\le
\sum_{q\le H}\frac{\Phi_q}{c_q}
=\Psi_H.
\tag{26}
\]

If \(LM_A\), \(MHG_A\), or another legal macrotrade theorem gives \(\Psi_{H_A}=o(W)\) for every fixed \(A\), diagonalization supplies \(A=A(m)\to\infty\) slowly with

\[
H=A(m)\sqrt m=o(m),\qquad \Psi_H=o(W).
\]

Then

\[
W+O(HW/m)+2\sum_{q\le H}M_q=W+o(W),
\]

and the symmetric-chain product covers both outer tails in \(o(W)\). Hence

\[
\nu(2m+1)\le W+o(W),
\]

followed by the standard even-dimensional lift.

## Adversarial audit

The strongest claims were audited independently.

- The rectangle-lattice proof was checked in every characteristic, including primes dividing \(r\); the endpoints \(r=1,n-1\) are correctly excluded.
- Passing from oriented to unoriented cyclic orders introduces no factor: reversal columns are identical and selector lifts push forward integrally.
- The Graver theorem concerns only squarefree support-feasible factor differences; it does not identify the entire Graver basis of \(A_m\).
- Signed lattice saturation gives no nonnegative factor and no common nested resolution.
- The Hilbert cut identity uses one transposition and the same component subset at every depth; no rankwise sign choice is hidden.
- Connected overlays exactly cancel all continuous smoothing, even under repetition.
- The \(\gamma-\mathcal S\) criterion is an exact discounted coboundary, so it provides no independent structural estimate.
- Histogram descent alone does not establish labelled synchronization without the unproved common-flow retraction (25).

The stable conclusion is therefore:

\[
\boxed{\text{no lattice obstruction;\quad the unresolved theorem is a support-feasible,
multistep component-correlation/common-flow theorem.}}
\]

## Audit-correction addendum

This addendum records the localized scope and notation repairs from the
independent audit.  It overrides the corresponding broader wording above;
the accepted core theorems and the negative verdict are unchanged.

### A.1 Range of the bounded four-letter action

Equation (5) has the precise range

\[
 \|\delta_1\|_2^2=4,
 \qquad
 \|\delta_q\|_2^2=8
 \quad(2\le q\le m-2).
\]

At `q=m-1` the target rank is one and the four-letter trade has zero
action.  Thus the phrase `q>=2` in the body is not intended to include
`q=m-1`.

### A.2 Hypotheses in the overload and checkerboard formulas

The overload identities (10)--(12) assume throughout that the integer load
vector has the conserved total

\[
 \sum_S\mu(S)=W=cN+r.
\]

In (12) one must also assume

\[
 P\cap M=\varnothing,
 \qquad |P|=|M|,
\]

so that `delta=1_P-1_M` is a zero-sum squarefree update.  The resulting
checkerboard characterization applies only to this squarefree unit-update
scope.  For a general integral trade, including repeated additions or
removals at one target, the correct statement is the full mobile-quota
formula (10), not the squarefree checkerboard shorthand.

### A.3 Transposition stability of ownership components

The following lemma supplies the step used in (13).

**Lemma (component equivariance).**  Let `tau=(u v)` be a coordinate
transposition.  If `K` is a connected component of the ownership graph
between an exact factor `F` and `tau F`, with left side `L_K subset F` and
right side `R_K subset tau F`, then

\[
 \boxed{R_K=\tau L_K.}
\]

**Proof.**  For a cyclic order `C`, the total number of incidences of
`u,v` among its `n` cyclic middle intervals is `2m=n-1`.  Hence not every
middle interval contains exactly one of `u,v`; some interval contains both
or neither and is fixed setwise by `tau`.  That interval joins the left
vertex `C` to the right vertex `tau C`, so `tau L_K subseteq R_K`.
Every finite component of the `n`-regular bipartite ownership graph has the
same number of vertices on its two sides.  Therefore the inclusion is an
equality.  Consequently

\[
 z_K={\bf1}_{\tau L_K}-{\bf1}_{L_K},
 \qquad
 A_{m-q}z_K=(\tau-I)A_{m-q}{\bf1}_{L_K},
\]

which is exactly (13).  \(\square\)

### A.4 Self-contained definitions of `Gamma_H` and `S_H`

Write

\[
 \lambda_q=\frac W{N_q}=c_q+\alpha_q,
 \qquad 0\le\alpha_q<1,
 \qquad
 \beta_q=N_q\alpha_q(1-\alpha_q),
\]

and `f_q=mu_q-lambda_q 1`.  Given a uniformly random coordinate
transposition `tau`, let `Delta_(K,q)` be the depth-`q` load change of
ownership component `K`.  Define

\[
 \gamma_q(F)
 =\mathbb E_\tau\!\left[
    \frac14\sum_K\|\Delta_{K,q}\|_2^2
  \right]
  -\frac2n\beta_q,
\]

\[
 s_q(F)
 =\mathbb E_\tau\!\left[
    \frac14\|f_q-\tau f_q\|_2^2
  \right]
  -\frac2n\|f_q\|_2^2
 \ge0,
\]

where the last inequality is the Johnson spectral bound on the
zero-total, zero-point-margin subspace.  The quantities in (19)--(20) are

\[
 \boxed{
 \Gamma_H(F)=\sum_{q\le H}\frac{\gamma_q(F)}{c_q},
 \qquad
 \mathcal S_H(F)=\sum_{q\le H}\frac{s_q(F)}{c_q}.}
\]

Here `mathcal P` is specifically the kernel that first chooses `tau`
uniformly and then chooses every `F`-versus-`tau F` component side
independently and fairly.

### A.5 Range required by the proved diagonal estimates

The fixed-`A` window `H_A=ceil(A sqrt(m))` automatically lies in the
proved uniform range.  When diagonalizing to a growing `A=A(m)`, the
estimates actually established in this route require

\[
 \boxed{H=o(m^{2/3}),}
\]

not merely `H=o(m)`.  Equivalently, choose the diagonal slowly enough that
`A(m)=o(m^(1/6))`.  This restriction is compatible with
`A(m)->infinity`, and hence still gives `H/sqrt(m)->infinity`.  All uses of
the fixed-window expansion, the proved tail estimate, and the final
diagonal are to be read with this stronger range.

### A.6 Status of the proposed multistep version

The `O(n)`-step discussion in Section 8 is motivational, not a proved
theorem.  A valid quantitative multistep statement must include an explicit
accumulated-correlation hypothesis, for example a bound of the form

\[
 \sum_{t<T}
 \max_{I_t}\langle d_{t,I_t},d_{t,I_t^c}\rangle_{H_A}
 \ge
 \eta_A\mathcal Q_{H_A}(F_0)
 -C_AH_A\operatorname{Cat}_m,
\]

for a legal exact-factor trajectory and a stated value of `T=O_A(n)`.
Without such an estimate, repeated transpositions are only a proposed
mechanism for neutral routing, not a descent result.

### A.7 Scope of the connected-overlay obstruction

The statement that connected overlays cancel smoothing under repetition is
valid for repeated heat-bath use of one fixed transposition `tau`, or more
generally along a trajectory for which every selected ownership overlay is
connected.  It does not say that connectedness for one transposition
persists after switching to unrelated transpositions.  Later overlays may
be disconnected and may admit positive component-correlation cuts.

### A.8 Final scope correction

The proved lattice theorem removes only a **signed-lattice/index
obstruction** to simultaneous unlabelled rank corrections.  It does not
exclude other algebraic obstructions arising from nonnegativity,
squarefreeness, support feasibility, pointed ownership, or a common nested
flow.  Accordingly, the first verdict sentence and the final boxed slogan
should be read as

\[
 \boxed{\text{no signed-lattice/index obstruction is present; the
 support-feasible component-correlation and common-flow problems remain.}}
\]
