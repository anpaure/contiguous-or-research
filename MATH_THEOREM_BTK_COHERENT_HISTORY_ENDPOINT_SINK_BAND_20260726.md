# Coherent BTK histories create an endpoint-sink band

Date: 2026-07-26

Method: pure mathematics only.  No computation, search, solver, or web
input is used.

## 0. Outcome

Restricting the forbidden coordinate set to one actually generated by a
coherent two-sided-safe history does **not** rescue endpoint mixing for
the standard product BTK/Greene--Kleitman SCD.

Let \(P\) be a rank-\(m\) product-SCD diagonal of length \(h\), and
traverse it from its low endpoint to its high endpoint, starting with
empty memory.  Every physical direction of \(P\) is used exactly once.
Consequently, if \(h\le H-1\), this is a coherent two-sided
\(H\)-safe history and its terminal queue contains the complete active
alphabet

\[
                         \operatorname{Act}(P)
                         =I_A(P)\mathbin{\dot\cup}I_B(P).         \tag{0.1}
\]

For BTK paths, every Johnson-adjacent high endpoint \(Y\), with product
path \(Q\), satisfies

\[
                         |I_A(P)\cap I_A(Q)|\ge h-4.             \tag{0.2}
\]

Moreover \(|h(Q)-h|\le2\).  Thus, whenever

\[
                         h\ge5,\qquad 2h+3\le H,                 \tag{0.3}
\]

the history obtained by traversing \(P\) is an actual state-graph sink:
there is no Johnson seam followed by a whole product-SCD path \(Q\)
which remains even lower \(H\)-safe.  The shared \(A\)-coordinate is
inserted on \(P\) and removed on the reverse traversal of \(Q\), with
inclusive separation at most \(2h+3\).

The reverse orientation has the identical low-end obstruction, using the
\(B\)-active alphabets.  Hence every coherently oriented whole BTK
product block satisfying (0.3) terminates its route.

At

\[
                         H=\sqrt m\log\log m,                    \tag{0.4}
\]

every fixed Gaussian length band
\(a\sqrt m\le h\le b\sqrt m\), \(0<a<b<\infty\), satisfies
(0.3) for all large \(m\).  The exact number of product blocks in this
band is

\[
 N_{a,b}
 =\left[
     {2\over\sqrt\pi}\bigl(e^{-a^2}-e^{-b^2}\bigr)+o(1)
   \right]{W\over\sqrt m},
 \qquad W=\binom{2m}{m}.                                      \tag{0.5}
\]

Therefore a route system made from whole product-SCD blocks has at least
\(N_{a,b}\) components merely to contain this band.  Since

\[
                         {N_{a,b}\over W/H}
                         =\Theta(\log\log m)\longrightarrow\infty, \tag{0.6}
\]

it cannot have \(o(W/H)\) components.

There is also a literal six-coordinate version.  Take a product path of
length three whose two half chains both have minimum
\((m-3)/2\).  After its three edges, the live queue is exactly its three
\(A\)-directions and three \(B\)-directions.  This coherent queue has
zero outgoing whole-block degree for every \(H\ge9\).

Thus the earlier arbitrary-forbidden-set cut was not exploiting an
unrealizable state: the fatal set is generated automatically by traversing
the block itself.  The exact escape must split or internally switch the
product paths, change SCD frame at the seam, or allow enough elapsed
history before an active coordinate is reused.  Ordinary endpoint
splicing of whole fixed-frame BTK blocks is closed.

## 1. Product paths and the two-sided queue

Split the physical coordinates as

\[
                         A\mathbin{\dot\cup}B,
                         \qquad |A|=|B|=m.                       \tag{1.1}
\]

Fix the standard BTK SCD on each half.  For chains

\[
 C_a\subset\cdots\subset C_{m-a},
 \qquad
 D_b\subset\cdots\subset D_{m-b},                               \tag{1.2}
\]

put \(r=\max(a,b)\) and \(h=m-2r\).  Their rank-\(m\)
product diagonal is

\[
 P(C,D)=
 \bigl(C_{r+t}\cup D_{m-r-t}:0\le t\le h\bigr).                 \tag{1.3}
\]

In the low-to-high orientation, each of its \(h\) edges inserts one
previously unused \(A\)-coordinate and removes one previously unused
\(B\)-coordinate.  Write those two \(h\)-sets as \(I_A(P)\) and
\(I_B(P)\).

The two-sided \(H\)-memory state stores the coordinate pairs used on
the preceding \(H-1\) edges.  A new Johnson edge is legal precisely
when neither of its coordinate labels occurs in this queue.  Equivalently,
no physical coordinate may occur on two edges lying in one \(H\)-edge
window.

### Lemma 1.1 (the fatal queue is coherent and hereditary)

If \(h\le H-1\), traversing \(P\) from an empty queue is two-sided
\(H\)-safe.  At the high endpoint its queue support is exactly

\[
                         I_A(P)\mathbin{\dot\cup}I_B(P).          \tag{1.4}
\]

More generally, whenever \(P\) is traversed legally from an arbitrary
incoming queue, its terminal queue contains the set in (1.4).

#### Proof

Every chain increment occurs once, the \(A\)- and \(B\)-halves are
disjoint, and an edge pairs one unused increment from each half.  Hence
the \(2h\) labels on the \(h\) edges are pairwise distinct, proving
two-sided safety.  Since all \(h\) edges lie among the last \(H-1\)
edges, none has expired from the terminal queue.  This proves (1.4).
The same nonexpiration argument applies with an arbitrary older queue;
old labels may remain as well, but every label used by \(P\) is present.
\(\square\)

Thus an active-alphabet forbidden set is not an adversarial state imposed
from outside the dynamics.  It is the canonical terminal state of the
block.

## 2. The BTK record-tail overlap

We recall the exact deterministic fact needed for the continuation
argument and include its proof.

For a binary word \(w=w_1\cdots w_m\), let

\[
                         D_w(t)=\sum_{i=1}^t(2w_i-1),             \tag{2.1}
\]

and let \(\mathcal R(w)\) be the strict ascending record times of this
walk.  In the BTK parenthesis matching, the unpaired one-positions are
exactly \(\mathcal R(w)\): a one is unpaired precisely when no unmatched
zero precedes it, equivalently when its height is larger than every
previous prefix height.

Suppose \(|w|=k\ge m/2\), and put \(h=2k-m\).  If the maximum
of \(D_w\) is \(M\), the containing BTK chain has minimum rank
\(k-M\).  Its rank-\(k\) member uses the first \(M\) unpaired
positions, while its rank-\((m-k)\) member uses the first

\[
                         m-k-(k-M)=M-h.                          \tag{2.2}
\]

Therefore the active coordinates between those two symmetric ranks are
the last \(h\) elements of \(\mathcal R(w)\).

### Lemma 2.1 (record-tail stability)

Let \(X,Y\) be Johnson-adjacent middle owners, both lying at high
endpoints of their BTK product paths \(P,Q\).  If \(P\) has length
\(h\ge5\), then

\[
                         |I_A(P)\cap I_A(Q)|\ge h-4.             \tag{2.3}
\]

Also

\[
                         h(Q)\in\{h-2,h,h+2\}.                  \tag{2.4}
\]

#### Proof

Let \(w,w'\) be the \(A\)-restrictions of \(X,Y\).  An exchange
internal to \(A\) changes two opposite bits.  Between their positions
the walks \(D_w,D_{w'}\) differ by the constant \(2\) or \(-2\)
and agree afterwards.  The translated interval can create at most the
first records at two new height levels; when the walks reunite, the
changed previous maximum can suppress at most the first two later record
levels.  Thus

\[
                         |\mathcal R(w)\triangle\mathcal R(w')|
                         \le4.                                  \tag{2.5}
\]

An exchange internal to \(B\) leaves \(w\) unchanged.  A cross-half
exchange changes one bit of \(w\), translating one suffix by two.  It
adds or suppresses at most two initial record levels, so

\[
                         |\mathcal R(w)\triangle\mathcal R(w')|
                         \le2.                                  \tag{2.6}
\]

For ordered finite sets \(E,E'\), if
\(|E\triangle E'|\le d\), then their terminal tails satisfy

\[
 |T_s(E)\setminus T_t(E')|
 \le d+(s-t)_+.                                                  \tag{2.7}
\]

Indeed, first shorten the first tail if necessary and then perform the
at most \(d\) set edits; each edit removes at most one member of the
current terminal tail.

In the internal cases the \(A\)-rank, and hence \(h\), is unchanged,
so (2.5) and (2.7) lose at most four elements.  In a cross-half edge the
\(A\)-rank changes by one, so the product endpoint excess changes by
two; (2.6) and (2.7) again lose at most four.  Formula (2.2) identifies
these tails with \(I_A(P),I_A(Q)\), proving (2.3)--(2.4).
\(\square\)

For \(h\ge5\), a Johnson neighbor of a high endpoint is again a high
endpoint.  Indeed its \(A\)-rank is at least

\[
                         {m+h\over2}-1>{m\over2},                 \tag{2.8}
\]

whereas every low endpoint has \(A\)-rank at most \(m/2\).  Hence
Lemma 2.1 covers every endpoint neighbor.  Reverse-complement followed
by the half swap gives the identical low-end statement with
\(I_B\) in place of \(I_A\).

## 3. Coherent histories are sinks

### Theorem 3.1 (whole-block sink theorem)

Let \(P\) be a BTK product path of length \(h\) satisfying

\[
                         h\ge5,\qquad 2h+3\le H.                 \tag{3.1}
\]

Traverse \(P\) from low to high as part of any coherent history.  There
is no Johnson seam from its high endpoint to an endpoint of another
product path \(Q\), followed by a traversal of \(Q\), that is lower
\(H\)-safe.  In particular there is no two-sided \(H\)-safe
continuation.  The assertion includes the empty incoming history as a
special case.

The same statement holds after reversing every orientation.

#### Proof

Lemma 1.1 says that the terminal queue is coherent and contains
\(I_A(P)\).  Let \(Y\) be any Johnson-adjacent product endpoint and
let \(Q\) be its path.  By Lemma 2.1 there is

\[
                         z\in I_A(P)\cap I_A(Q),                  \tag{3.2}
\]

and \(h(Q)\le h+2\).

On the low-to-high traversal of \(P\), the coordinate \(z\) is
inserted.  Since the seam meets the high endpoint of \(Q\), a continuation
through \(Q\) must traverse it high-to-low, on which \(z\) is removed.
Thus \(z\) gives an insertion-then-removal positive residence.

There are \(h\) edges in \(P\), one seam edge, and at most \(h+2\)
edges in \(Q\).  The inclusive span from the first occurrence of \(z\)
to the second is therefore at most

\[
                         h+1+(h+2)=2h+3\le H.                    \tag{3.3}
\]

The lower intersection of the containing window consequently has rank
too large by at least one.  This violates lower \(H\)-safety.

For a high-to-low traversal ending at a low endpoint, use the low-end
version of Lemma 2.1.  The common \(B\)-coordinate is inserted on
\(P\) and removed on the following low-to-high traversal of \(Q\),
with the same span bound.  \(\square\)

This proves more than the conservative requirement that the next block's
whole active alphabet avoid the current queue.  Even if reuse were allowed
after expiration, the geometry forces the repeated label to occur before
expiration.

### Corollary 3.2 (no two whole short blocks concatenate)

In a route whose atoms are whole fixed-frame BTK product paths, every
atom satisfying (3.1) is the last atom of its route.  Therefore each route
contains at most one such atom.

#### Proof

Whichever orientation is chosen, Theorem 3.1 forbids a following atom.
\(\square\)

## 4. An exact six-coordinate coherent queue

The preceding theorem uses \(2h\) live directions.  The smaller
three-plus-three cut is also dynamically realizable.

Assume \(m\) is odd and put \(r=(m-3)/2\).  In each half take the
explicit BTK chain with bottom/top words

\[
                         (01)^r0^3,\qquad (01)^r1^3.             \tag{4.1}
\]

Its active alphabet is the final three coordinates.  Let \(P\) be the
product of two copies of this chain.  It has three edges.  By Lemma 1.1,
after a low-to-high traversal from empty memory the queue support is
exactly

\[
                         Z=U_A\mathbin{\dot\cup}U_B,
                         \qquad |U_A|=|U_B|=3.                   \tag{4.2}
\]

### Proposition 4.1 (literal six-coordinate sink)

For every \(H\ge9\), the coherent state (4.2) has no two-sided-safe
whole-product-block continuation.

#### Proof

Let \(X\) be the high endpoint.  An adjacent product endpoint has level
\(r-1,r\), or \(r+1\).

At level \(r\), the Johnson edge lies within one half.  The other half
chain is unchanged and its complete three-coordinate active alphabet is
contained in \(Z\).

At level \(r+1\), at least one component chain is a top or bottom child
of one of the displayed chains.  In BTK matching, deleting a bit from a
chain top can only remove unmatched-one record times; adding a bit to a
chain bottom can only remove unmatched-zero reverse-record times.  The
child active alphabet is therefore a subset of the old three-set and has
size one.  It meets \(Z\).

At level \(r-1\), at least one component is a top or bottom parent.  The
same matching monotonicity says its active alphabet contains the old
three-set, and again meets \(Z\).

Thus every adjacent endpoint block repeats a coordinate from the
three-edge history.  Its length is at most five.  Including the seam, the
two occurrences have inclusive span at most

\[
                         3+1+5=9\le H.
\]

Orienting the next block away from the common endpoint turns a repeated
\(A\)-coordinate into an insertion-then-removal residence and a repeated
\(B\)-coordinate into a removal-then-insertion residence.  Either one
violates two-sided safety.  \(\square\)

This answers the literal realizability question: the six-set is not only
contained in a coherent queue; it is exactly the queue support.

## 5. Gaussian-band census

Every SCD has

\[
                         \binom mr-\binom m{r-1}                 \tag{5.1}
\]

chains of minimum rank \(r\), because the rank-\(r\) sets are
partitioned between chains beginning at rank \(r\) and the unique
rank-\(r\) members of earlier chains.  Hence the number of ordered chain
pairs with maximum minimum rank \(r\), equivalently product paths of
length \(h=m-2r\), is exactly

\[
\begin{aligned}
 P_h
 &=\binom mr^2-\binom m{r-1}^2\\
 &=\binom mr^2
   {4(h+1)(m+1)\over(m+h+2)^2}.                                \tag{5.2}
\end{aligned}
\]

Uniformly for \(h=c\sqrt m+O(1)\), with \(c\) in a fixed compact
subset of \((0,\infty)\), Stirling's formula gives

\[
 P_h
 =\left({8c e^{-c^2}\over\sqrt\pi}+o(1)\right){W\over m}.       \tag{5.3}
\]

Only values of \(h\) having the parity of \(m\) occur.  Therefore the
mesh in the variable \(c=h/\sqrt m\) is \(2/\sqrt m\).  Summing
(5.3) over \(a\sqrt m\le h\le b\sqrt m\) gives the Riemann sum

\[
\begin{aligned}
 N_{a,b}
 &=
 {W\over m}{\sqrt m\over2}
 \int_a^b {8c e^{-c^2}\over\sqrt\pi}\,dc
 +o\!\left({W\over\sqrt m}\right)\\
 &=
 \left[
 {2\over\sqrt\pi}(e^{-a^2}-e^{-b^2})+o(1)
 \right]{W\over\sqrt m},                                      \tag{5.4}
\end{aligned}
\]

which is (0.5).

### Theorem 5.1 (component obstruction for whole BTK blocks)

Let \(H=\sqrt m\log\log m\).  Fix \(0<a<b<\infty\).
Any coherent route cover whose indivisible atoms include all BTK product
paths with lengths in
\([a\sqrt m,b\sqrt m]\) has at least \(N_{a,b}\) route
components.  In particular its component count is not \(o(W/H)\).

#### Proof

For large \(m\), every path in the band satisfies (3.1).  Corollary 3.2
says each route contains at most one of them.  Hence the number of routes
is at least their exact count (5.4).  Finally

\[
 {N_{a,b}\over W/H}
 =
 \left[
 {2\over\sqrt\pi}(e^{-a^2}-e^{-b^2})+o(1)
 \right]\log\log m\longrightarrow\infty.                       \tag{5.5}
\]

\(\square\)

## 6. Exact boundary

Proved:

1. the fatal active-alphabet queue is produced by an entirely coherent
   two-sided-safe traversal;
2. every whole BTK block of length \(5\le h\le(H-3)/2\) is a
   terminal state-graph atom in either orientation;
3. the literal three-plus-three fatal queue is exactly realizable;
4. a fixed Gaussian band contains the positive constant in (5.4) times
   \(W/\sqrt m\) such sinks; and
5. whole-block endpoint splicing therefore misses the required component
   scale by a factor \(\Theta(\log\log m)\).

Not proved:

* an obstruction after cutting a block internally;
* an obstruction to switching to a genuinely different SCD frame at the
  seam;
* an obstruction when an auxiliary route delays every repeated direction
  for at least \(H\) edges; or
* coefficient one.

The precise surviving constructive statement is consequently not a
restricted-degree theorem for whole product-SCD endpoints.  It must be an
internal path-surgery or moving-frame theorem which destroys the BTK
record-tail overlap before the terminal active alphabet becomes the live
queue.
