# A logarithmic nonaligned support defeats the whole suspended-seed obstruction

Date: 2026-07-25

Method: pure combinatorics and generating functions only.  No search,
computation, solver, or probabilistic model of exact factors is used.

## 0. Outcome

Let

\[
 n=2m+1,\qquad N=m-4,
\]

and retain the audited semilength-four collision roots

\[
 A=11110000,\qquad B=11101000,\qquad DD=11001100.
\]

For arbitrary Dyck words (U\in\mathcal D_u) and
(V\in\mathcal D_v), with (u+v=N), the three canonical rows

\[
 UAV,\qquad UBV,\qquad UDDV                                      \tag{0.1}
\]

own one common depth-one lower target.  Its finite-coordinate incidence
word is

\[
 \ell(U,V)=U\,S_0\,\overline V,
 \qquad S_0=01000010.                                           \tag{0.2}
\]

Thus (0.1) is the universe of *all* Dyck-prefix/Dyck-suffix suspensions of
this seed.  In particular it contains every triple used by the recursive
first-active-occurrence matching in
`FRACTIONAL_PACKET_GLOBAL_SEED_MATCHING_20260725.md`.

For an integer (s\) satisfying

\[
 1\le s\le \frac{m-4}{2},
\]

let

\[
 T_s=\{(i,2m+1-i):1\le i\le s\}.                               \tag{0.3}
\]

This is a matching of (s) coordinate transpositions, supported on the
two ends of the canonical finite-coordinate line.  It is deliberately
nonaligned with the one-sided prefix support used in the preceding
bounded-support theorem.

Let (K_{m,s}) be the number of pairs ((U,V)), (u+v=m-4), for which
the target (0.2) is invariant under the group generated by (T_s).
There is an absolute constant (C) such that

\[
 \boxed{
 K_{m,s}
 \le C\sqrt{s+1}\,2^{-s}\operatorname{Cat}_{m-3}.
 }                                                               \tag{0.4}
\]

Consequently the number of invariant packets in **any row-disjoint
matching chosen from all suspended copies of this seed** is at most the
right side of (0.4).

Since

\[
 \operatorname{Cat}_{m-3}/\operatorname{Cat}_m\longrightarrow 4^{-3},
\]

the choice

\[
 \boxed{
 \frac{2^s}{\sqrt{s+1}}\gg\sqrt m
 }                                                               \tag{0.5}
\]

makes the entire invariant suspended-seed certificate

\[
 o\!\left(\frac{\operatorname{Cat}_m}{\sqrt m}\right).
\]

For example one may take

\[
 s=\left\lceil
 \frac12\log_2m+\log_2\log(m+2)
 \right\rceil.                                                   \tag{0.6}
\]

The support then has only (2s=(1+o(1))\log_2m) coordinates and (s)
transpositions.

This is a sharp warning about the arbitrary-support gate.  It does **not**
construct an FSP factor: non-invariant seed triples may turn into other
packets, and unrelated collision resources remain.  It proves that no
uniform obstruction based only on targets invariant under the generated
coordinate group can continue to count this local seed above the FSP
scale once a suitably nonaligned logarithmic support is allowed.

## 1. The target word

The audited suspension identity gives the common upper-core word

\[
 \overline U\,T_0V,
 \qquad T_0=10111101.
\]

Every such lower target contains the distinguished coordinate
(\infty).  On the (2m) finite coordinates its incidence word is the
complement

\[
 U\,\overline{T_0}\,\overline V
 =U\,S_0\,\overline V,
\]

which proves (0.2).  Notice that

\[
 S_0=01000010,
 \qquad (S_0)_1=(S_0)_8=0.                                     \tag{1.1}
\]

Put

\[
 V^*=\operatorname{rev}(\overline V).
\]

Reverse-complementation maps Dyck words bijectively to Dyck words, so
(V^*\in\mathcal D_v).

The (T_s)-components are the pairs

\[
 \{i,2m+1-i\},\qquad1\le i\le s,
\]

and isolated vertices.  Therefore (0.2) is (T_s)-invariant exactly when

\[
 \operatorname{pref}_s(\ell(U,V))
 =\operatorname{rev}\bigl(\operatorname{suff}_s(\ell(U,V))\bigr).
                                                                    \tag{1.2}
\]

## 2. Reflection invariance forces a Dyck-prefix collision

### Lemma 2.1

Assume (s\le N/2).  If (1.2) holds, then

\[
 2u\ge s,\qquad2v\ge s,                                        \tag{2.1}
\]

and

\[
 \boxed{
 \operatorname{pref}_s(U)=\operatorname{pref}_s(V^*).
 }                                                               \tag{2.2}
\]

#### Proof

Suppose first that (2u<s).  Since (u+v=N\ge2s), one has (2v>s),
so the reversed suffix in (1.2) is the first (s) steps of the Dyck word
(V^*).  The left side first traverses all of (U), returns to height
zero, and then takes the first step of (S_0).  By (1.1) that step is a
down-step, so the left side has height (-1) at time (2u+1).  A Dyck
prefix cannot do this.  Hence (2u\ge s).

Suppose next that (2v<s).  Then (u=N-v>s/2), so the first (s) steps
of the left side lie inside the continuing Dyck word (U).  The first
(2v) steps of the reversed suffix form the complete Dyck word (V^*),
and hence return to height zero.  If (v>0), equality in (1.2) would make
(U) return to zero at time (2v).  Since (U) continues, its next step
must be an up-step.  On the reversed-suffix side the next step is the last
step of (S_0), which is a down-step by (1.1), a contradiction.  If
(v=0), the first reversed-suffix bit is already ((S_0)_8=0), whereas
the first bit of the nonempty Dyck word (U) is (1).  Thus (2v\ge s).

Under (2.1), the left side of (1.2) lies wholly in (U), while its right
side lies wholly in

\[
 \operatorname{rev}(\overline V)=V^*.
\]

This is exactly (2.2). \(\square\)

The two zeroes at the boundary of the fixed seed are doing real work:
they rule out the endpoint-heavy Catalan decompositions (u\ll s) and
(v\ll s).  What remains is a literal equality of two (s)-step Dyck
prefixes.

## 3. A uniform atom bound for a Dyck prefix

### Lemma 3.1

There is an absolute constant (C_0) with the following property.  Let
\(D\) be uniformly distributed over \(\mathcal D_a\), and let \(w\) be a
fixed binary word of length (k\le a).  Then

\[
 \boxed{
 \Pr(\operatorname{pref}_k(D)=w)
 \le C_0(k+1)2^{-k}.
 }                                                               \tag{3.1}
\]

#### Proof

If (w) is not a nonnegative walk prefix, the probability is zero.  If it
ends at height (h\), put (L=2a-k).  The number of nonnegative
completions from height (h) to zero in (L) steps is

\[
 B(L,h)
 =\frac{h+1}{(L+h)/2+1}
   \binom{L}{(L-h)/2}.                                          \tag{3.2}
\]

Here (0\le h\le k) and (L\ge a).  The elementary central-binomial
bounds

\[
 \binom{L}{\lfloor L/2\rfloor}
 \le C_1\frac{2^L}{\sqrt{L+1}},
 \qquad
 \operatorname{Cat}_a
 \ge c_1\frac{4^a}{(a+1)^{3/2}}
\]

inserted into (3.2) give

\[
 \frac{B(L,h)}{\operatorname{Cat}_a}
 \le C_2(h+1)
       \left(\frac{a+1}{L+1}\right)^{3/2}2^{-k}
 \le C_0(k+1)2^{-k}.
\]

This proves (3.1). \(\square\)

The factor (k+1) is harmless for the logarithmic application.  It also
has the correct ballot interpretation: a specified prefix ending at
height (h) has (h+1) times the central completion mass, up to absolute
constants.

## 4. Proof of the global upper bound

For fixed (u+v=N), let (K_{u,v,s}) be the number of pairs
((U,V)\in\mathcal D_u\times\mathcal D_v) satisfying (1.2).  By Lemma
2.1, only pairs satisfying (2.2) can contribute.

At least one of (u,v) is at least (N/2\ge s).

If (v\ge N/2), then for every fixed (U), Lemma 3.1 bounds the fraction
of (V^*\in\mathcal D_v) with the required prefix by
(C_0(s+1)2^{-s}).  Hence

\[
 K_{u,v,s}
 \le C_0(s+1)2^{-s}\operatorname{Cat}_u\operatorname{Cat}_v.
                                                                    \tag{4.1}
\]

If (u\ge N/2), apply the same lemma to (U) instead and obtain the same
bound.

Lemma 2.1 also says that both (u) and (v) are at least (s/2).  The
truncated Catalan convolution satisfies

\[
 \boxed{
 \sum_{s/2\le u\le N-s/2}
 \operatorname{Cat}_u\operatorname{Cat}_{N-u}
 \le \frac{C_3}{\sqrt{s+1}}\operatorname{Cat}_{N+1}.
 }                                                               \tag{4.2}
\]

Indeed, the standard two-sided Catalan estimates

\[
 c\frac{4^j}{(j+1)^{3/2}}
 \le\operatorname{Cat}_j
 \le C\frac{4^j}{(j+1)^{3/2}}
\]

show, after division by (\operatorname{Cat}_{N+1}), that the contribution
from (u\le N/2) is at most a constant times

\[
 \sum_{u\ge s/2}(u+1)^{-3/2}=O((s+1)^{-1/2}),
\]

and the other half is symmetric.

Summing (4.1) only over the allowable range and using (4.2) gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
 K_{m,s}
 &\le C_0(s+1)2^{-s}
       \sum_{s/2\le u\le N-s/2}
       \operatorname{Cat}_u\operatorname{Cat}_{N-u}\\
 &\le C_4\sqrt{s+1}\,2^{-s}\operatorname{Cat}_{N+1}\\
 &=C_4\sqrt{s+1}\,2^{-s}\operatorname{Cat}_{m-3}.
\end{aligned}
\]

This is (0.4).

Every recursive first-occurrence matching made from the seed
(\{A,B,DD\}) is a subfamily of the universe (0.1).  Therefore (0.4)
also bounds its invariant submatching.  No assumption about clean-prefix
regular languages, recursive denominators, or first-occurrence decoding is
needed for this upper bound.

## 5. Exact multistep interpretation

Consider an arbitrary adaptive sequence of exact component switches, all
using transpositions in (T_s).  The row-lineage theorem from
`BOUNDED_COORDINATE_SWITCH_PATH_OBSTRUCTION_20260725.md` says that every
initial canonical row (E) has a unique final descendant

\[
 g_EE,
 \qquad
 g_E\in\langle T_s\rangle
       \cong(S_2)^s.                                             \tag{5.1}
\]

If a seed target is (T_s)-invariant, its three row descendants still own
that same target and hence still form a packet under every balanced
depth-one quota.  Theorem (0.4) says, however, that among the **entire
suspended-seed universe**, only

\[
 O(\sqrt{s+1}\,2^{-s}\operatorname{Cat}_{m-3})
\]

targets even qualify for this invariant-lineage argument.

Thus the invariant-target method is genuinely sensitive to support
geometry.  The aligned prefix support gives a lower bound of order
((b+1)2^{-b}\operatorname{Cat}_m).  A nonaligned reflection matching
turns invariance into collision of two Dyck prefixes and makes the whole
available local-seed certificate exponentially small in the number of
transpositions.

The conclusion is deliberately one-sided:

* it does not show that a component-switch endpoint has small packet LP;
* it does not show that non-invariant old owners cease to collide after
  their row-dependent permutations;
* it does not control other local seeds or deeper ranks;
* it does show that a proposed **uniform arbitrary-support lower bound**
  cannot count more invariant copies of this seed than (0.4).

## 6. Travel-between-cubes implication

One-transposition native cubes retain a positive-density invariant seed
family.  Iterating cubes on a fixed aligned support retains the
logarithmic-support lower bound from the earlier report.  The construction
(T_s) supplies the first explicit travel geometry for which those
certificates can all fall below the target scale while the total support
is still logarithmic.

Accordingly, a valid next obstruction must use at least one of the
following:

1. non-invariant targets whose row-dependent images are forced to
   recoalesce;
2. simultaneous potentials from many nonconjugate seed types;
3. a component-switch invariant not expressible as a union-of-coordinate-
   components condition;
4. a quantitative cost for moving between the successive conjugate cubes,
   rather than a static invariant-packet count inside their common support.

The arbitrary-support gate is therefore not closed by relabelling the
aligned theorem.  The reflection matching (0.3) is a precise counterexample
at the FSP scale.
