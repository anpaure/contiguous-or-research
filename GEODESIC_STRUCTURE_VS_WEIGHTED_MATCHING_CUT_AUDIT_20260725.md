# Geodesic structure versus the weighted matching cut

Date: 2026-07-25

Method: pure mathematics only.

## 0. Verdict

The actual protected chunk catalogue has one structural feature not present
in the completely random linear counterexample: its \(g\) nested phase
columns are rooted on one Johnson geodesic and are coupled by common
departure and arrival orders.

However, none of the weaker consequences survives as a useful separator.
In particular, the random no-go can be strengthened so that every support
is a union of \(g\) saturated chains, every pairwise intersection has
poset width at most three, the incidence matrix has consecutive ones on
each chain, \(D\gg K^2\), and the uniform tag-saturating fractional point
has target loads \(1+o(1)\), while the largest integral matching is still
\(o(T)\).

The full unpruned geodesic fibre has rectangle/adjacent-swap exchanges, but
the Bernoulli isolated pruning does not preserve this exchange closure.
Thus no verified convex-order, consecutive-ones, balanced-matrix, or
exchange theorem currently improves the arbitrary-weight matching bound.
The strongest unconditional bound remains the weighted greedy estimate

\[
 \nu_w\ge {w(E)\over 1+(K+1)((1+\eta)D-1)},
\]

which is a factor \(K\) away from the required denominator \(D\).

The exact positive theorem still needed is a **geodesic weighted
augmenting theorem** proving

\[
 \nu_w\ge {w(E)\over(1+o(1))D}
\]

for every nonnegative weighting.  It must use the common Johnson-geodesic
root schedule in a way robust under pruning; individual strip shape is not
enough.

## 1. The hierarchy of structural properties

A retained protected support has the following properties.

1. It is the disjoint union, by phase, of \(g\) nested flag columns.
2. Every column has one target in each claimed consecutive rank, so its
   incidence vector has consecutive ones in its private rank order.
3. The \(g\) middle roots form a repetition-free Johnson geodesic.
4. All roots and flags are generated by two common ordered coordinate
   lists--the departure and arrival orders.
5. Before pruning, adjacent swaps of those orders generate rectangle
   exchanges between legal candidates.

Properties 1--2 are insufficient by Theorem 2.1 below.  Property 3 is the
first genuine separator from that counterexample.  Properties 4--5 are
the natural possible source of an integral theorem.

There are two reasons they are not yet a proof.

* The explicit three-strip non-Helly triangle gives an odd cycle submatrix,
  so the global protected incidence matrix is neither balanced nor
  consecutive-ones.
* Independent isolated pruning keeps only a polynomial number
  \(\mu\) of an enormous fibre.  For any fixed adjacent-swap rectangle, it
  may retain one corner and delete both exchange corners.  No theorem in
  the present catalogue guarantees exchange closure of the retained
  fibre.

Thus one cannot apply network-matrix, interval-matrix, or discrete-convex
integrality after the current pruning.

## 2. Chain-structured random counterexample

The random linear obstruction can be made vertically indistinguishable at
the level of chain decomposition.

### Theorem 2.1 (long-chain structure does not imply rounding)

Let \(g,L,D,T\to\infty\), put \(K=gL\), and assume

\[
 \log D=o(g),\qquad D\gg\log(gT),\qquad
 T\gg D^2g^4.
 \tag{2.1}
\]

There is a partitioned target hypergraph with \(T-o(T)\) retained tags
such that

1. every retained tag has exactly \(D\) candidates;
2. every target has degree at most \((1+o(1))D\);
3. every candidate is the union of exactly \(g\) disjoint saturated chains
   of length \(L\);
4. the intersection of any two candidates is the union of at most three
   such chains, and hence has poset width at most three;
5. the weights \(1/D\) saturate every tag and give target load at most
   \(1+o(1)\); but
6. every integral tag--target matching has size \(o(T)\).

The parameters may simultaneously satisfy \(D\gg K^2\).

#### Proof

Take \(N_0=gT\) pairwise-incomparable chain atoms.  Replace each atom \(a\)
by a private saturated chain

\[
 a_1<a_2<\cdots<a_L.
\]

Choose \(DT\) independent uniform \(g\)-subsets of the atom set, pre-grouped
into \(T\) tags of \(D\) candidates.  Replace every sampled atom subset by
the union of its \(L\)-chains.

An atom has binomial degree of mean \(D\).  All \(L\) targets in its chain
have that same degree.  Chernoff and \(D\gg\log(gT)\) show that the maximum
target degree is \((1+o(1))D\).

For two random \(g\)-subsets, the probability of sharing at least four
atoms is

\[
 O\left((g^2/N_0)^4\right)=O(g^4/T^4).
\]

Among \(DT\) candidates the expected number of such pairs is

\[
 O(D^2g^4/T^2)=o(1).
\]

More generally, deleting every tag incident with such a pair removes
\(o(T)\) tags under the weaker corresponding little-\(o\) assumption.
After deletion every support intersection has width at most three.

Target-disjointness of the expanded supports is exactly atom-disjointness
of the sampled \(g\)-sets.  For \(t\) specified candidates,

\[
 \Pr(\hbox{they are atom-disjoint})
 \le
 \exp\left(-{g^2t(t-1)\over2N_0}\right).
 \tag{2.2}
\]

Consequently the expected number of \(t\)-edge matchings is at most

\[
 \binom{DT}{t}
 \exp\left(-{g^2t(t-1)\over2N_0}\right).
 \tag{2.3}
\]

Set

\[
 t=C T\sqrt{{\log D+\log g\over g}}=o(T)
\]

with \(C\) sufficiently large.  The negative exponent in (2.3) dominates
the logarithm of the binomial factor.  Hence, with probability tending to
one, no matching has size \(t\).  Intersect this event with the degree and
intersection events above.  Uniform \(1/D\) weights give the asserted
fractional point. \(\square\)

This example even has a global rank order and consecutive ones within
every phase chain.  It violates the actual catalogue only at the next
level: its \(g\) roots are a random \(g\)-set, not one Johnson geodesic
driven by common coordinate exchanges.

## 3. Why the obvious convexity tests fail

### 3.1 Global consecutive ones

If the augmented incidence matrix had the consecutive-ones property, its
line/interval structure would give a substantially stronger integral
theory.  The literal three-strip configuration

\[
 P_1\cap P_2=\{A\},\qquad
 P_2\cap P_3=\{B\},\qquad
 P_3\cap P_1=\{C\}
\]

with \(A,B,C\) distinct is the \(3\times3\) odd-cycle submatrix.  It is a
Tucker/balancedness obstruction, so no global target ordering makes all
protected supports intervals.

### 3.2 Pointwise min/max closure

Inside one fixed product grid, coordinatewise meet and join of two cells
are legal cells.  But two candidate paths using crossing corners satisfy

\[
 \{(i,j),(i',j')\}\ne
 \{(\min(i,i'),\min(j,j')),
   (\max(i,i'),\max(j,j'))\}
\]

when the corners cross.  Thus uncrossing does not preserve the target
multiset.

For priority height vectors, the pointwise min and max preserve the sum of
column-prefix incidence vectors, but they need not be legal priority
permutations: for example, the two height permutations \((1,2)\) and
\((2,1)\) have pointwise min \((1,1)\) and max \((2,2)\).  Hence the legal
priority fibre is not a distributive sublattice.

### 3.3 Exchange closure after isolation

The full permutation fibre is connected by adjacent swaps.  The retained
fibre is obtained by independent marking followed by deletions.  The event
that a retained path has any one specified adjacent-swap neighbour has
probability at most \(O(gp)\), where

\[
 p={1\over L_0(\Delta+1)}
 ={m^{3-o(1)}\over L_0A}.
\]

Since the unpruned fibre size \(A\) is superpolynomial while \(g\) and the
retained degree are polynomial, this probability is \(o(1)\).  Thus local
exchange connectivity is typically destroyed rather than preserved.

## 4. Strongest unconditional arbitrary-weight lower bound

Let a multihypergraph have maximum vertex degree \(\Delta_1\) and edge
size at most \(R\).  For arbitrary nonnegative edge weights \(w_e\), run
the following greedy algorithm: choose a remaining edge of maximum weight,
put it into the matching, and delete every intersecting edge.

One chosen edge meets at most

\[
 1+R(\Delta_1-1)
\]

remaining edges, each of weight at most its own.  Charging all deleted
weight to the chosen edge proves

\[
 \boxed{
  \nu_w\ge {w(E)\over1+R(\Delta_1-1)}.}
 \tag{4.1}
\]

For the rational protected multicover,

\[
 R=K+1,\qquad \Delta_1\le(1+\eta)D,
\]

so

\[
 \boxed{
  \nu_w(\mathcal M_D)
  \ge {w(E)\over1+(K+1)((1+\eta)D-1)}.}
 \tag{4.2}
\]

Theorem 2.1 shows that no improvement from denominator \(KD\) to
\((1+o(1))D\) follows from chain decomposition, consecutive ranks,
width-three intersections, \(D\gg K^2\), and pointwise fractional
feasibility alone.

## 5. Exact geodesic exchange theorem still needed

Let \(\mathcal M_D\) now denote the actual bounded-displacement protected
multicover.  The required property can be stated without reference to an
algorithm.

> **Geodesic weighted augmenting theorem.**  For every \(w\ge0\), there is
> a tag--target matching \(M\) such that
> \[
>  w(M)\ge {w(E)\over(1+\epsilon_m)D},
>  \qquad \epsilon_m\to0.
> \]

Equivalently,

\[
 \chi_f'(\mathcal M_D)\le(1+o(1))D.
\]

To use the only surviving separator, a proof must construct alternating
augmentations from the common departure/arrival orders of Johnson-geodesic
roots.  It is not enough to count intersections.  A usable exchange form
would be:

*for every weighted matching \(M\) below the displayed bound, there is a
finite family of rectangle/geodesic exchanges which replaces a subset of
\(M\) by a heavier disjoint family, and every exchange corner used in the
replacement belongs to the retained catalogue.*

The last clause is precisely what independent isolated pruning does not
currently guarantee.  Thus the audit finds a concrete separator, but no
proved weighted gain from it.  The remaining theorem is exchange survival,
not another degree or pair-moment estimate.
