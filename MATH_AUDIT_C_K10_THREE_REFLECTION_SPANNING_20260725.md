## 1. The exact floor identity

At depth \(q\), write

\[
 N_q=\binom n{m-q},\qquad
 \lambda_q=\frac W{N_q}=c_q+\theta_q,qquad
 c_q=\lfloor\lambda_q\rfloor,
 \]

and let \(\rho_q=W-c_qN_q=N_q\theta_q\).  For an exact factor \(F\), put

\[
 f_q(S)=\mu_q^F(S)-\lambda_q.
\]

Since \(\sum_S f_q(S)=0\), direct expansion gives

\[
 \sum_S(\mu_q^F(S)-c_q)(\mu_q^F(S)-c_q-1)
 =\|f_q\|_2^2-\beta_q,
 \tag{1.1}
\]

where

\[
 \beta_q=N_q\theta_q(1-\theta_q)
 =\frac{\rho_q(N_q-\rho_q)}{N_q}.
 \tag{1.2}
\]

Consequently the full unhalved floor energy is exactly

\[
 \boxed{
 \mathcal Q_H(F)=\|f(F)\|_H^2-\Phi_H,\qquad
 \Phi_H=\sum_{q=1}^H\frac{\beta_q}{c_q},}
 \tag{1.3}
\]

with

\[
 \|f\|_H^2=\sum_{q=1}^H\frac{\|f_q\|_2^2}{c_q}.
\]

No reflection or chart choice changes \(\Phi_H\).  Moreover the exact
Gaussian-window asymptotic is

\[
 \Phi_H\sim\kappa_AW\sqrt m,
 \qquad
 \kappa_A=
 \int_0^A
 \frac{\{e^{x^2}\}(1-\{e^{x^2}\})}
 {e^{x^2}\lfloor e^{x^2}\rfloor}\,dx>0.
 \tag{1.4}
\]

Thus \(\Phi_H=\Theta_A(nHB)\).  Any iteration which contracts a linear
centered norm must replenish this floor to relative precision \(1/n\) in
order to leave only \(O_A(HB)\) floor-corrected error.  This is why the
variance must be charged together with the coherent signal in (0.6).

Every exact factor profile has zero point margins.  Hence

\[
 f_q\in\bigoplus_{j\ge2}U_j^{(m-q)}.
 \tag{1.5}
\]

The obstruction below lies in \(U_2\), so it does not use the forbidden
\(U_1\) direction.

## 2. An explicit radial slow vector in the pair module

Identify the coordinates with \(\mathbb Z_n\), let
\(d(i,j)\in\{1,\ldots,m\}\) be their cyclic distance, and put

\[
 \phi(a)=\cos\frac{2\pi a}{n}+\frac1{n-1}
 \qquad(1\le a\le m).
 \tag{2.1}
\]

For an unordered pair define

\[
 b_{ij}=\phi(d(i,j)).
 \tag{2.2}
\]

### Lemma 2.1

For every odd \(n\ge5\), \(b\) is a nonzero real vector in
\(U_2^{(2)}\), and

\[
 \boxed{
 \|b\|_2^2
 =\frac{n^2(n-3)}{4(n-1)}
 \ge\frac{n^2}{8}.}
 \tag{2.3}
\]

#### Proof

The elementary root-of-unity identities give

\[
 \sum_{a=1}^m\cos\frac{2\pi a}{n}=-\frac12.
 \tag{2.4}
\]

Since \(m=(n-1)/2\), equation (2.4) implies

\[
 \sum_{a=1}^m\phi(a)=0.
 \tag{2.5}
\]

For a fixed coordinate \(i\), exactly two coordinates lie at each cyclic
distance \(a\).  Thus

\[
 \sum_{j\ne i}b_{ij}=2\sum_{a=1}^m\phi(a)=0.
 \tag{2.6}
\]

The kernel of the point-pair incidence map is exactly \(U_2^{(2)}\).

There are exactly \(n\) unordered coordinate pairs at each distance
\(a\).  Also

\[
 \sum_{a=1}^m\cos^2\frac{2\pi a}{n}
 =\frac{n-2}{4}.
 \tag{2.7}
\]

Using (2.4), (2.7), and \(m=(n-1)/2\),

\[
\begin{aligned}
 \sum_{a=1}^m\phi(a)^2
 &=\frac{n-2}{4}
   -\frac1{n-1}+\frac1{2(n-1)}\\
 &=\frac{n(n-3)}{4(n-1)}.
\end{aligned}
 \tag{2.8}
\]

Multiplication by the \(n\) pairs at each distance proves the equality in
(2.3); its inequality is immediate for \(n\ge5\). \(\square\)

### Lemma 2.2

For the three involutions in (0.1),

\[
 sb=b,\qquad rb=b,
 \tag{2.9}
\]

and, for odd \(n\ge9\),

\[
 \boxed{
 \frac{\|b-tb\|_2^2}{\|b\|_2^2}
 \le\frac{1024\pi^2}{n^3}.}
 \tag{2.10}
\]

Consequently

\[
 \boxed{
 \sum_{g\in\{s,r,t\}}\|(I-P_g)b\|_2^2
 \le\frac{256\pi^2}{n^3}\|b\|_2^2.}
 \tag{2.11}
\]

#### Proof

Both \(s:x\mapsto-x\) and \(r:x\mapsto1-x\) preserve cyclic distance, so
(2.9) follows from (2.2).

The maps \(s\) and \(t=(0\ 1)s(0\ 1)\) agree outside

\[
 D=\{-1,0,1\}.
\]

For every \(x\in D\), the cyclic distance between \(sx\) and \(tx\) is at
most two.  Therefore, for a pair meeting \(D\), the two image-pair
distances differ by at most four.  Since the cosine is \(1\)-Lipschitz in
its angle,

\[
 |b_{t\{i,j\}}-b_{s\{i,j\}}|\le\frac{8\pi}{n}.
 \tag{2.12}
\]

The constant \(1/(n-1)\) cancels.  Fewer than

\[
 \binom n2-\binom{n-3}2=3n-6<3n
\]

pairs meet \(D\).  Since \(sb=b\), (2.12) gives

\[
 \|b-tb\|_2^2\le\frac{192\pi^2}{n}.
 \tag{2.13}
\]

Dividing (2.13) by the exact norm in (2.3) gives

\[
 \frac{\|b-tb\|_2^2}{\|b\|_2^2}
 \le
 \frac{768\pi^2(n-1)}{n^3(n-3)}
 \le\frac{1024\pi^2}{n^3}
\]

for \(n\ge9\).  Finally \(I-P_g=(I-g)/2\), and only the \(t\)-term is
nonzero, proving (2.11). \(\square\)

## 3. Exact lift to every Johnson layer

For \(2\le k\le n-2\), define the pair-inclusion lift

\[
 (T_kb)(S)=\sum_{\{i,j\}\subseteq S}b_{ij},
 \qquad |S|=k.
 \tag{3.1}
\]

### Lemma 3.1

If a pair vector \(z\) has zero point margins, then

\[
 \boxed{
 \|T_kz\|_2^2=\binom{n-4}{k-2}\|z\|_2^2.}
 \tag{3.2}
\]

Moreover \(T_kz\in U_2^{(k)}\), and for every coordinate permutation
\(g\),

\[
 T_k(gz)=gT_kz.
 \tag{3.3}
\]

#### Proof

The total sum of a zero-margin pair vector is zero.  Expanding
\(\|T_kz\|_2^2\), equal pairs occur in
\(\binom{n-2}{k-2}\) sets, pairs meeting in one point in
\(\binom{n-3}{k-3}\) sets, and disjoint pairs in
\(\binom{n-4}{k-4}\) sets.

Let \(S_0=\sum_e|z_e|^2\).  Squaring every zero point-margin identity and
summing over the point gives an ordered adjacent-pair cross sum
\(-2S_0\).  Squaring \(\sum_ez_e=0\) then shows that the ordered
disjoint-pair cross sum is \(S_0\).  Therefore

\[
\begin{aligned}
 \|T_kz\|_2^2
 &=\left[
 \binom{n-2}{k-2}
 -2\binom{n-3}{k-3}
 +\binom{n-4}{k-4}
 \right]S_0\\
 &=\binom{n-4}{k-2}S_0.
\end{aligned}
 \tag{3.4}
\]

The lift is equivariant by its definition.  It is the standard inclusion
copy of the pair-harmonic module, so its image is \(U_2^{(k)}\).  This can
also be checked directly: its total and every point margin vanish, and the
equivariant nonzero image of \(U_2^{(2)}\) has type \((n-2,2)\).
\(\square\)

Put \(v_k=T_kb\).  Applying (3.2) to both \(b\) and \(b-gb\), and using
(3.3), shows that every quotient in Lemma 2.2 is unchanged by the lift.
This proves (0.2) simultaneously for every \(2\le k\le n-2\), in
particular for every \(k=m-q\), \(q\le H\), once \(m\) is large.

The same construction may be placed in a single depth of the weighted
Gaussian direct sum.  The factor \(1/c_q\) cancels from both sides, so the
floor-compatible norm does not change (0.2).

## 4. What the slow mode does to iteration accounting

Suppose one uses three reflection kernels only through inequalities of the
form

\[
 \text{gain from kernel }g
 \ \ge\ a\|(I-P_g)v\|_H^2-R_g,
 \qquad a>0,
 \tag{4.1}
\]

and pays an averaged remainder

\[
 \frac13\sum_gR_g\le C_AHB.
 \tag{4.2}
\]

For the direction supplied by Section 3, the total signal in (4.1) is at
most

\[
 \frac{256\pi^2a}{n^3}\|v\|_H^2.
 \tag{4.3}
\]

Thus (4.1)--(4.2), as a uniform proof mechanism, cannot certify negative
drift until

\[
 \|v\|_H^2\gg_A n^3HB.
 \tag{4.4}
\]

Equation (1.3) shows exactly where the integer floor enters: replacing
\(\|v\|_H^2\) by \(\mathcal Q_H\) requires subtracting the same fixed
\(\Phi_H\) from both endpoints and charging the associated component
variance.  Merely appending three separate \(O_A(HB)\) variance estimates
does not produce a remainder of order the frame gap times \(HB\).

This is an obstruction to the proposed *deduction*, not a lower bound on
what a nonlinear state-adaptive circuit can achieve.  Such a circuit may
change its chart atom space after every endpoint.  It must, however, prove
the charged inequality (0.6) rather than infer it from group generation.

For the renewable matching-owner cells this distinction is exact.  Their
coherent quantities satisfy

\[
 \sum_g A_g(F)=4\sum_g\|(I-P_g)f(F)\|_H^2.
 \tag{4.5}
\]

The proved lower frame bound is \(1/(100n^6)\).  Combining it only with a
separate estimate \(\sum_gV_g=O_A(HB)\) yields a floor-corrected threshold
of order \(n^6HB\).  The vector of Sections 2--3 shows that no argument for
this triple can replace the frame scale by a positive constant: on the
ambient \(U_2\) direction, even the coherent sum in (4.5) is at most

\[
 \frac{1024\pi^2}{n^3}\|v\|_H^2.
 \tag{4.6}
\]

Hence a separate additive \(O_A(HB)\) variance payment cannot support a
uniform \(O_A(HB)\) stopping threshold.  This does **not** refute a charged
bound with \(\lambda_m=n^{-6}\): such a bound asks that the variance deficit
itself be controlled on the \(n^{-6}(\mathcal Q_H-C_AHB)\) scale, rather
than subtracting an unscaled \(O_A(HB)\) term.

## 5. Fixed packet charts have an exact dimension deficit

Fix one depth \(q\), and put \(k=m-q\).  The span of the constant function
and the \(n\) point-incidence functions on the \(k\)-subsets has dimension
exactly \(n\): their sole dependence is

\[
 \sum_{i=1}^n\mathbf1_{\{i\in S\}}=k.
\]

Therefore the admissible zero-total, zero-point-margin space

\[
 \mathcal Z_q=\bigoplus_{j\ge2}U_j^{(k)}
 \tag{5.1}
\]

has exact dimension

\[
 \dim\mathcal Z_q=N_q-n.
 \tag{5.2}
\]

Overlay an exact middle wreath factor with any second exact middle wreath
factor.  Its ownership graph has \(B\) row vertices on either shore, so it
has at most \(B\) connected components.  At a fixed depth, its complete
component-cut cube therefore lies in an affine space of dimension at most
\(B\).  It follows that any \(\ell\) *fixed common-base* overlays have
combined innovation span of dimension at most \(\ell B\).  In particular,
six fixed physical overlays have dimension at most \(6B\), whereas
(5.3)--(5.4) below give

\[
 N_q-n>6B
 \tag{5.2a}
\]

for all sufficiently large \(m\).  This generalizes the canonical packet
count below.  It is a tangent-space statement at one fixed collection of
overlays, not a bound on a decision tree which recomputes its owner
partitions at later endpoints.

Consider a reflected packet chart for an involution \(g\), with atom
effects \(d_R\), \(R\in\mathcal I_g\).  Its odd and even bundle atoms are

\[
 h_R=d_R-gd_R,\qquad k_R=d_R+gd_R.
 \]

Both parity spaces lie in

\[
 \operatorname{span}\{d_R,gd_R:R\in\mathcal I_g\},
\]

whose dimension is at most \(2|\mathcal I_g|\).  For a canonical
four-letter chart,

\[
 |\mathcal I_g|\le\operatorname{Cat}_{m-2}.
\]

Thus three such charts, including both parities, have combined dimension at
most \(6\operatorname{Cat}_{m-2}\).

Uniformly for \(q\le H\),

\[
 \lambda_q=\frac W{N_q}\le K_A
 \tag{5.3}
\]

for a constant \(K_A\) depending only on \(A\); this follows directly from

\[
 \lambda_q=\prod_{i=0}^{q-1}\frac{m+2+i}{m-i}
\]

and \(q=O_A(\sqrt m)\).  Hence

\[
 N_q-n\ge\frac{nB}{K_A}-n.
 \tag{5.4}
\]

Also

\[
 \frac{\operatorname{Cat}_{m-2}}B
 =\frac{m(m+1)}{4(2m-1)(2m-3)}\longrightarrow\frac1{16}.
 \tag{5.5}
\]

Equations (5.4)--(5.5) prove, for all sufficiently large \(m\),

\[
 \dim\mathcal Z_q>6\operatorname{Cat}_{m-2}.
 \tag{5.6}
\]

Consequently there is a nonzero \(z_q\in\mathcal Z_q\) orthogonal to every
even and odd atom of the three fixed charts.  Scaling \(z_q\) gives an
arbitrarily large quadratic direction in the ambient exact-margin space,
while all six fixed chart coherences remain zero.  This proves that the
known parity contractions are contractions of specified coherent
endpoints, not operator contractions of \(\mathcal Z_q\).

This dimension statement is deliberately an ambient linear statement.  It
does not assert that every vector of \(\mathcal Z_q\) is the histogram of a
literal exact wreath factor.  That extra nonlinear realizability is not
needed to refute a claim that the six fixed atom spaces linearly span all
exact-margin directions; it would be needed for a literal high-energy
counterexample factor.

In particular this paragraph does not contradict the renewable
matching-owner cell.  At the current factor \(F\), the sum of its current
component innovations is exactly \(gF-F\); after choosing a corner, a new
owner partition and a new tangent space are computed.  What the dimension
argument excludes is replacing that endpoint-dependent statement by one
fixed six-atom operator spanning theorem.

For the K10 half preparation the selected packet count is only

\[
 \frac12\operatorname{Cat}_{m-2}+O(1),
\]

so restricting all three charts to the prepared packets makes the dimension
deficit larger.

## 6. Exact interpretation of the K10 menu

Let \(P_m\) be the K10 Hamilton path and \(\mathcal R_m\) its productive
near-perfect matching.  Alternating the edges of the \(2m\)-edge path
splits it into two near-perfect matchings \(M_1,M_2\).  Put

\[
 g_i=\prod_{e\in M_i}e\quad(i=1,2),
 \qquad
 g_3=\prod_{e\in\mathcal R_m}e.
 \tag{6.1}
\]

The factors commute inside each product, and every \(g_i\) is an
involution with one fixed coordinate and \(m\) transpositions.  Thus K10
does canonically display three reflections of the right conjugacy class.

What K10 actually proves is

\[
 \left\langle e:e\in
 M_1\cup M_2\cup\mathcal R_m\right\rangle=S_n,
 \tag{6.2}
\]

because the union of the *individual transposition edges* is connected.
It does not follow that

\[
 \langle g_1,g_2,g_3\rangle\in\{A_n,S_n\},
 \tag{6.3}
\]

nor that the three-generator Schreier graphs have a uniform gap.  Even
generation does not imply such a gap: the explicit triple (0.1) generates
\(A_n\) or \(S_n\), yet Section 2 gives gap at most \(256\pi^2/n^3\) on
\(U_2\).

There is a second, physical distinction.  K10 by itself certifies a genuine
seam of colour \(e\) in the separate fresh overlay for each
\(e\in\mathcal R_m\).  It does not prove that those seams persist while the
other disjoint colours are applied or that they form components of the
single product overlay \(F/g_3F\).

The renewable matching-owner construction closes this physical point by
joining rows under the elementary abelian group generated by a matching.
Its invariant owner roots give a literal \(g_i\)-Haar cell at every exact
endpoint, and a nontrivial corner compiles in one pass of \(m\) fresh proper
transposition cuts.  This is not a consequence of the K10 seam count.  In
particular, that count still gives no upper bound on the joined-owner
variance \(V_{g_i}\).

The full matching-group extension strengthens this pointwise span.  Let
\(\Pi_s,\Pi_r\) average over the elementary abelian groups generated by the
two alternating matching classes of the Hamilton path.  For
\(v\perp U_0\oplus U_1\), put

\[
 d_i=\|(I-\Pi_i)v\|_2,\qquad i\in\{s,r\}.
\]

Every adjacent path transposition belongs to one of these two groups.  If
\(e\) is an edge in matching class \(i\), then

\[
 \|v-ev\|_2
 =\|(I-e)(I-\Pi_i)v\|_2\le2d_i.
 \tag{6.4}
\]

Order the path vertices as \(1,\ldots,n\), let \(\tau_p=(p\ p+1)\), and
write

\[
 E_p=\|v-\tau_pv\|_2^2.
\]

For \(a<b\), the endpoint transposition \((a\ b)\) has the adjacent word

\[
 \tau_a\tau_{a+1}\cdots\tau_{b-1}
 \tau_{b-2}\cdots\tau_a
\]

of length \(2(b-a)-1\).  Telescoping and Cauchy--Schwarz give

\[
 \|v-(a\ b)v\|_2^2
 \le4(b-a)\sum_{p=a}^{b-1}E_p.
 \tag{6.5}
\]

After summing over \(a<b\), the coefficient of \(E_p\) is at most

\[
 4n\,p(n-p)\le n^3.
 \]

The exact all-transposition Johnson identity on
\(\bigoplus_{j\ge2}U_j\) is

\[
 \sum_{a<b}\|v-(a\ b)v\|_2^2
 \ge4(n-1)\|v\|_2^2.
 \tag{6.5a}
\]

Consequently

\[
 \sum_{p=1}^{n-1}E_p
 \ge\frac{4(n-1)}{n^3}\|v\|_2^2.
 \tag{6.5b}
\]

Each alternating matching class has \(m=(n-1)/2\) edges.  Squaring (6.4)
and summing gives

\[
 \sum_{p=1}^{n-1}E_p
 \le4m(d_s^2+d_r^2)
 =2(n-1)(d_s^2+d_r^2).
 \tag{6.5c}
\]

Combining (6.5b)--(6.5c) yields

\[
 \boxed{
 \|(I-\Pi_s)v\|_2^2+\|(I-\Pi_r)v\|_2^2
 \ge\frac{2}{n^3}\|v\|_2^2.}
 \tag{6.6}
\]

This argument is layerwise and exact; weighted summation proves the same
\(2/n^3\) frame on the full Gaussian window.  Thus the renewable full
group cells do span every current centered direction quantitatively.  Their
exact fair-phase drift is

\[
 -\frac13\sum_{a\in\{s,r,t\}}
 \bigl(\mathscr A_a-\mathscr V_a\bigr),
 \qquad
 \mathscr A_a=\|(I-\Pi_a)f(F)\|_H^2.
 \tag{6.7}
\]

No estimate in K10 controls the joined-owner quantities
\(\mathscr V_a\).  Consequently (6.6) proves coherent coverage but not
contraction.

Finally, the K10 \(1/16\)-density preparation is a one-design.  This removes
its \(U_1\) indicator component and yields the seam boundary lower bound,
but it supplies no lower bound for the projection of an arbitrary current
load onto the packet-Haar atom span.  The dimension theorem in Section 5
shows that no such bound can hold for three fixed canonical packet spans.
The renewable owner cells evade this fixed-span statement pointwise, but
only by replacing the known two-row packet variance with the presently
uncontrolled joined-owner variance.

## 7. The exact positive replacement

A theorem that genuinely composes to \(\mathcal Q_H=o(W)\) may take either
of the following forms.

1. **Charged three-cell theorem.**  At every exact endpoint, let the three
   renewable matching-owner cells satisfy

   \[
   \sum_i(A_i-V_i)
   \ge12\lambda_m\bigl(\mathcal Q_H-C_AHB\bigr),
   \qquad \lambda_m>0.
   \tag{7.1}
   \]

   The sum must contain the actual joined-owner variance, including the
   exact adjacent-integer floor restitution.  An analogous larger family is
   possible with the constant \(12\) replaced by four times its size.

2. **Charged full-group theorem.**  For the uniform matching-group phase
   cells, prove

   \[
   \sum_i(\mathscr A_i-\mathscr V_i)
   \ge3\lambda_m\bigl(\mathcal Q_H-C_AHB\bigr),
   \qquad \lambda_m>0.
   \tag{7.2}
   \]

   The coherent frame (6.6) suggests the natural scale
   \(\lambda_m\asymp n^{-3}\), but any positive \(\lambda_m\) suffices for
   the finite descent.

3. **State-adaptive separation theorem.**  Whenever
   \(\mathcal Q_H>C_AHB\), construct one renewed chart, depending on the
   current endpoint, whose literal proper-cut cube satisfies

   \[
   A-V\ge4\lambda_m(\mathcal Q_H-C_AHB).
   \tag{7.3}
   \]

Any of these statements gives a geometric decrease until
\(O_A(HB)=o(W)\), because \(HB/W=H/n\to0\).  The renewable K10 completion
now establishes common-base persistence and the coherent span, but it
establishes none of the charged inequalities (7.1)--(7.3).  Joined-owner
variance is the sole remaining quantitative term.

The explicit radial vector in Sections 2--3 is likewise an exact Johnson
\(U_2\) direction, not a claim of literal wreath-factor realizability.  Its
role is to close the proposed quantitative inference from the particular
three-reflection frame.  The remaining nonlinear possibility is precisely
an endpoint-renewed charged theorem such as (7.1)--(7.3).

## 8. Exact seam-data insufficiency for joined-owner variance

The full-group variance has a simpler exact form than its definition first
suggests.  For a \(\Gamma_M\)-owner component \(K\), let \(x_K\) be its
stacked lower-load contribution in the reference phase and put

\[
 y_K=(I-\Pi_M)x_K.
 \tag{8.1}
\]

Uniform group averaging and unitarity give

\[
\begin{aligned}
 \mathbb E_{\gamma\in\Gamma_M}
 \|\gamma x_K-\Pi_Mx_K\|_H^2
 &=\|x_K\|_H^2-\|\Pi_Mx_K\|_H^2\\
 &=\|y_K\|_H^2.
\end{aligned}
 \tag{8.2}
\]

Consequently

\[
 \boxed{
 \mathscr V_M=\sum_K\|y_K\|_H^2,\qquad
 \mathscr A_M=\left\|\sum_Ky_K\right\|_H^2,}
 \tag{8.3}
\]

and therefore

\[
 \boxed{
 \mathscr A_M-\mathscr V_M
 =2\sum_{K<L}\langle y_K,y_L\rangle_H.}
 \tag{8.4}
\]

Thus the missing curvature is exactly a cross-component Gram sum.  A count
of middle-root seams, even with distinct colours and distinct root
transitions, does not appear in (8.4).

In particular, if the \(\Gamma_M\)-owner graph is connected, there is one
component and

\[
 \boxed{\mathscr A_M=\mathscr V_M.}
 \tag{8.4a}
\]

The full group cell then consists, before division by a global phase, only
of global coordinate translates and is energy-flat.  More generally,
pairwise orthogonal noninvariant component profiles also give equality in
(8.4).  Therefore a positive curvature theorem must prove both sufficient
owner fragmentation and positive Gram alignment; a large seam count proves
neither.

There is one exact route from single matching edges to the joined variance,
but it requires lower-profile norms rather than seam cardinalities.  Fourier
decomposition on \(\Gamma_M=(C_2)^m\) gives, for every profile vector \(x\),

\[
 \|(I-\Pi_M)x\|_H^2
 \le\frac14\sum_{e\in M}\|x-ex\|_H^2
 \le m\|(I-\Pi_M)x\|_H^2.
 \tag{8.5}
\]

Indeed a nontrivial character supported on \(J\subseteq M\) contributes
\(\|x_J\|^2\) to the outside-invariant norm and
\(4|J|\|x_J\|^2\) to the middle sum.  Applying (8.5) componentwise yields

\[
 \boxed{
 \mathscr V_M
 \le\frac14\sum_{e\in M}\sum_K\|x_K-ex_K\|_H^2.}
 \tag{8.6}
\]

Thus a useful K10-to-group theorem would have to control the right side of
(8.6), with the exact floor charge, for the **joined owner components**
\(K\).  Counting distinct commutator seams gives neither their lower-depth
effect norms nor their multiplicities inside the same \(K\), and therefore
does not bound (8.6).

The following countermodel makes the logical gap quantitative.

### Proposition 8.1 (arbitrary seam count with zero joined curvature)

Fix \(m\ge1\), an integer \(h\ge1\), a real number \(L>0\), and any
middle-root datum satisfying the exported K10 one-design, colour-congestion,
and distinct-seam assertions, with seam count \(S\).  There is an abstract
\(\Gamma=(C_2)^m\) owner-profile completion of that same datum with:

1. \(h\) nonzero owner components;
2. \(S\) distinct labelled seam certificates;
3. total coherent group distance
   \(\mathscr A=hL^2\);
4. joined-owner variance \(\mathscr V=hL^2\); and hence
5. \(\mathscr A-\mathscr V=0\).

The same system may be chosen so that the product reflection
\(g=e_1\cdots e_m\) is anti-invariant on every component, in which case
the binary quantities also satisfy \(A=V=4hL^2\).

#### Proof

Choose a nontrivial real character
\(\chi:\Gamma\to\{\pm1\}\) with \(\chi(g)=-1\); for example, prescribe
\(\chi(e_1)=-1\) and \(\chi(e_j)=1\) for \(j>1\).  Let
\(\mathcal H=\mathbb R^h\), with \(\Gamma\) acting by the scalar
\(\chi(\gamma)\) on all of \(\mathcal H\).  Its invariant projection is
\(\Pi=0\).  For the \(j\)-th owner component take

\[
 x_j=Le_j,
\]

where \(e_1,\ldots,e_h\) is the standard orthonormal basis.  Equations
(8.2)--(8.3) give

\[
 \mathscr A=\left\|\sum_{j=1}^hLe_j\right\|^2=hL^2,
 \qquad
 \mathscr V=\sum_{j=1}^h\|Le_j\|^2=hL^2.
\]

Since \(gx_j=-x_j\), the binary component innovation is
\(z_j=gx_j-x_j=-2Le_j\), and the same orthogonality gives
\(A=V=4hL^2\).

Finally retain the prescribed middle-root one-design and its \(S\) seam
labels unchanged.  The K10 seam conclusions specify their number, colours,
and distinctness as middle-root transitions; they impose no equation
coupling them to the lower-profile inner products in (8.4).  Hence this
owner-profile system is a completion of exactly the same exported data.
\(\square\)

Proposition 8.1 is a rigorous **data-insufficiency** theorem.  It is not a
literal wreath-factor counterexample: it deliberately models exactly the
information exported by the K10 seam theorem and omits unproved rowwise
cyclic-prefix constraints.  It proves the following exact boundary.

> No inequality
> \[
> \mathscr A_M-\mathscr V_M
> \ge\eta_m\mathscr A_M-R_m,
> \qquad \eta_m>0,\quad R_m=o(\mathscr A_M),
> \]
> can be deduced from the K10 seam cardinality, colour congestion, and
> one-design data alone.

Indeed choose \(L\) so that \(hL^2>R_m/\eta_m\) in Proposition 8.1.  A
successful variance theorem must use an additional physical statement
forcing positive Gram alignment among distinct joined owner components.
Equivalently, it must rule out connected or orthogonal group-owner
completions by exploiting common rowwise cyclic-prefix chronology; seam
abundance by itself does not do so.
