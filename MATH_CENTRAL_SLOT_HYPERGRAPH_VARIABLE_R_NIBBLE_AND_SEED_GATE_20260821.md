# The one-central slot hypergraph: exact profile, a variable-rank nibble lead, and the seed gate

**Status (2026-08-21).**  Sections 1--5, 7, and 8 below are proved.  They give the
exact weighted slot hypergraph obtained from the audited antipode-free
common-transformation fibers, show that its *local intersection kernel* is
small, and prove a first-order variable-rank nibble lemma.  They also show
why the tempting Alon--Kim--Spencer degree exponent cannot be imported by a
linearization and why the independently seeded regular hypergraph is not
the connector-free repeated-transformation palette.  Section 7 strengthens the latter point:
after deleting only the final checkpoint, a repeated common transformation
has a periodic, endpoint-forced suffix and cannot support a rank-`m` deck
matching on more than subexponentially many slots.  Section 8 then removes
this physical seed obstruction for the central gate: a proved linear
truncated-history seam serializes arbitrary independently seeded free cores
of length `Theta(n log n)` at `o(W)` central cost, while preserving the
same `o(1)` local-tree parameters.  Thus the live central gate is the
iterated growing-rank matching theorem, not seed compatibility.

Section 6 gives an alternative physically compatible
stationary product construction and reduces its missing regularity to an
explicit same-fiber deck-overlap estimate.  That estimate, and an iterated
variable-rank nibble, remain open.  The seam cost is not negligible after
blindly charging it at every rank in the full DCC band, so no coefficient-one
conclusion is claimed.

## 1. The relaxed one-central hypergraph

Put

\[
 n=2m+1,\qquad W={n\choose m}.
\]

Let `G` be one of the audited length-`ell` common-transformation families.
All its generator words induce the same position permutation, every
rank-`m` trace is simple and chordless, and, after the final common checkpoint
is deleted, no retained deck contains two disjoint rank-`m` targets.  Write

\[
 a=\ell-1,
 \qquad D_\pi(w)\subseteq { [n]\choose m}
\]

for the retained `a`-set generated by word `w` from seed state `pi`.

Take `b` abstract slots and put `A=ab`.  The **independently seeded relaxed
slot hypergraph** has vertex set

\[
 [b]\ \dot\cup\ { [n]\choose m}
\]

and, in slot `i`, the weighted edges

\[
 e(i,\pi,w)=\{i\}\cup D_\pi(w),
 \qquad x_{i,\pi,w}={1\over n!|\mathcal G|}.
 \tag{1.1}
\]

The word `weighted' avoids an irrelevant common-denominator blow-up.

### Theorem 1.1 (exact degrees and Johnson-distance codegrees)

Every slot has weighted degree one.  Every central target has weighted
degree

\[
 d_x(S)={A\over W}.                                   \tag{1.2}
\]

For `0<=j<=m`, let

\[
 B_j={m\choose j}{m+1\choose j}
\]

be the number of rank-`m` targets at Johnson distance `j` from a fixed
target.  If `c_j(w)` is the number of unordered distance-`j` pairs in the
retained deck of `w`, then for `d_J(S,T)=j>=1`, the target--target
codegree divided by (1.2) is exactly

\[
 \rho_j={2\,\mathbb E_w c_j(w)\over aB_j}.            \tag{1.3}
\]

In particular,

\[
 \rho_1={2(a-1)\over a m(m+1)}={8+o(1)\over n^2},
 \qquad \rho_m=0,                                    \tag{1.4}
\]

and, for `2<=j<=m-1`,

\[
 \rho_j\le {a-1\over B_j}.                           \tag{1.5}
\]

A slot--target pair has normalized codegree `a/W`, and two distinct slot
vertices have codegree zero.

#### Proof

For fixed `w`, changing a uniform seed merely applies a uniform relabeling
to its `a` distinct position targets.  Hence a fixed target occurs with
probability `a/W`, proving (1.2).  Relabeling is transitive on unordered
pairs at each Johnson distance, whose orbit has size `WB_j/2`.  This gives
(1.3).  Chordlessness says that the only distance-one pairs in a deck are
its `a-1` temporal edges.  Antipode removal gives `c_m=0`, and
`c_j<=binom(a,2)` gives (1.5).  The slot statements follow directly from
(1.1).  \(\square\)

For `a=Theta(n)`, (1.4)--(1.5) give a maximum normalized target pair
codegree `O(n^(-2))`.  More generally, for `a=o(n^(3/2))`,

\[
 \max_{S\ne T}\rho_{d_J(S,T)}
 =O\!\left({1\over n^2}+{a\over n^3}\right).         \tag{1.6}
\]

The near-antipodal distance `m-1`, rather than distance two, is the worst
term in (1.5).

## 2. The full profile gives an average local-tree parameter

Define

\[
 \Xi(S)=\sum_{T\ne S}\rho_{d_J(S,T)}^2.
 \tag{2.1}
\]

### Proposition 2.1 (squared-kernel bound)

Uniformly in `S`,

\[
 \Xi(S)=O\!\left({1\over n^2}+{a^2\over n^3}\right). \tag{2.2}
\]

Consequently `Xi(S)=O(1/n)` for `a=Theta(n)`, while for
`a=Theta(n log^2 n)` it is `O(log^4(n)/n)=o(1)`.

If two candidate decks are sampled independently from the fractional
incidence law conditional on both containing `S`, then the probability
that they have another target in common is at most `Xi(S)`.

#### Proof

There are `B_j` targets at distance `j`.  The `j=1` contribution in (2.1)
is `O(1/n^2)` by (1.4).  By (1.5), the remaining contribution is at most

\[
 a^2\sum_{j=2}^{m-1}{1\over B_j}=O(a^2/n^3).         \tag{2.3}
\]

Indeed the largest reciprocal is the `j=m-1` term, of order `n^(-3)`;
successive terms toward the middle decrease by at least a further
polynomial factor, and the distance-two end starts at order `n^(-4)`.
For the last assertion, sum over `T` the probabilities that both
conditionally independent decks contain `T` and use the union bound.
\(\square\)

Thus the maximum codegree does not tell the whole local story.  Two random
edges through one target have an additional intersection with probability
`o(1)`.  A projective-plane obstruction has this parameter of constant
order, not `o(1)`.

## 3. Why bounded-codegree reduction loses the exponential degree

The large common fiber produces exponentially many distinct decks.  It is
tempting to linearize the deck hypergraph and apply the
Alon--Kim--Spencer bound, whose displayed error contains
`D^(-1/(a-1))`.  The following elementary ceiling shows why that loses the
large `D`.

### Theorem 3.1 (Johnson-neighbor degree ceiling)

Let `L` be any multihypergraph whose edges are chordless paths with at
least two vertices in `J(n,m)`.  If every target pair belongs to at most
`C` edges of `L`, then

\[
 \Delta(\mathcal L)\le C\,m(m+1).                    \tag{3.1}
\]

In particular every linear extraction (`C=1`) has maximum degree
`O(n^2)`.

#### Proof

Fix a target `S`.  For every path edge containing `S`, choose one temporal
Johnson neighbor of `S` on that path.  A fixed pair `{S,T}` can be chosen
for at most its codegree, hence at most `C`, path edges.  There are exactly
`m(m+1)` Johnson neighbors of `S`, proving (3.1).  \(\square\)

Therefore every reduction whose conclusion depends only on `D/C` has

\[
 {D\over C}\le m(m+1)=\Theta(n^2).                   \tag{3.2}
\]

For `a>=n`, even the formally favorable simple-hypergraph factor satisfies

\[
 D^{-1/(a-1)}\ge \exp\{-O(\log n/n)\}=1-o(1).        \tag{3.3}
\]

The exponential raw deck degree cannot coexist with bounded pair
codegree because every path incidence consumes a temporal Johnson neighbor.
This disposes of the proposed direct Alon--Kim--Spencer/KKMO
linearization.

The higher-codegree theorem of Gould--Kelly has a different, genuinely
interesting formal scale: (1.4) permits `B=Theta(n)` before logarithmic
losses, suggesting a `W/n` leading leftover.  It nevertheless fixes the
edge rank through the hierarchy
`1/D << 1/A << gamma << 1/(a-1)`.  Its one-nibble concentration hypothesis
contains

\[
 \varepsilon^2\theta {D\over D_2}\ge \log^5D,
 \qquad \theta\ll1/a.                                \tag{3.4}
\]

At the desired `epsilon=Theta(1/n)` and `D/D_2=Theta(n^2)`, the left side
of (3.4) is `o(1)`, whereas the right side diverges.  Thus the published
proof cannot be diagonalized to this palette.  This is a failure of the
available theorem, not a counterexample to the desired matching.

## 4. One tiny bite does work when `r Delta_2/Delta=o(1)`

The first-order part of a variable-rank nibble can be proved directly.

### Lemma 4.1 (uniform isolated-edge bite)

Let `H` be an `r`-uniform `D`-regular multihypergraph with maximum pair
codegree at most `delta D`.  Mark every edge independently with probability

\[
 p={\alpha\over rD},\qquad 0<\alpha\le1.              \tag{4.1}
\]

and retain the marked edges which meet no other marked edge.  They form a
matching `M`.  If `q_v=Pr(v in V(M))`, then, uniformly in `v`,

\[
 q_v={\alpha\over r}e^{-\alpha}
 \left(1+O\left(\alpha r\delta+{\alpha\over D}
                         +{\alpha^2\over rD}\right)\right). \tag{4.2}
\]

In particular, if `r delta=o(1)` and `D->infinity`, one bite covers the
same `(1+o(1))alpha e^(-alpha)/r` fraction at every vertex.

#### Proof

For an edge `e`, let `N(e)` be the other edges meeting it.  The union of
the `r` incident edge-stars and the first two Bonferroni terms give

\[
 r(D-1)-{r\choose2}\delta D
 \le |N(e)|\le r(D-1).                               \tag{4.3}
\]

The probability that `e` is marked and isolated is
`p(1-p)^|N(e)|`.  Two isolated marked edges containing the same vertex are
mutually exclusive, so

\[
 q_v=\sum_{e\ni v}p(1-p)^{|N(e)|}.                   \tag{4.4}
\]

Insert (4.3), use `prD=alpha`, and expand the logarithm of `1-p` to obtain
(4.2).  \(\square\)

For the relaxed central palette, `r=a+1` and (1.6) give

\[
 r{\Delta_2\over\Delta}
 =O\!\left({a\over n^2}+{a^2\over n^3}\right).      \tag{4.5}
\]

This tends to zero both at `a=Theta(n)` and at
`a=Theta(n log^2 n)`.  Thus the obstruction is not the first bite.
What is not proved is the iteration: after conditioning on all preceding
bites, one must keep useful degrees and the distance-sensitive kernel
regular down to a residual density `rho=o(1)`.  The natural formal range is
`rho >> r Delta_2/Delta`; proving that range with only average waste, rather
than a union bound over `W` targets, would yield the required central
`o(W)` leave.  Lemma 4.1 alone does not justify this induction.

## 5. The independent-seed hypergraph is not the physical backbone

Let `sigma` be the common position transformation of `G`.  If connector-free
blocks from `G` are concatenated from an initial state `pi_1`, then their
checkpoint states obey

\[
 \pi_{i+1}=\sigma(\pi_i),\qquad
 \pi_i=\sigma^{i-1}(\pi_1).                           \tag{5.1}
\]

The branch word chosen in block `i` does not affect (5.1).

### Proposition 5.1 (exact seed compatibility gap)

The physical Cartesian set of `b` branch choices is

\[
 \bigcup_{\pi_1\in S_n}
 \prod_{i=1}^b
 \{D_{\sigma^{i-1}(\pi_1)}(w):w\in\mathcal G\}.      \tag{5.2}
\]

The relaxed hypergraph of Section 1 instead permits the larger product

\[
 \prod_{i=1}^b
 \bigcup_{\pi\in S_n}
 \{D_\pi(w):w\in\mathcal G\}.                        \tag{5.3}
\]

Consequently a matching in (5.3) does not, without a separate lifting
theorem, give one physical FIFO trajectory.  Independent seed choices can
be made physical by exact bridges, but one bridge per `Theta(n)` block has
superlinear total cost.  At polynomial macro scale bridges are affordable,
but all microblock seeds inside one macro remain coupled.

There is a stronger obstruction to using (5.2) itself when only the final
checkpoint is deleted: every exact endpoint fiber has an additional
branch-independent suffix.  Section 7 determines this suffix exactly and
shows that periodic repetition of `sigma` makes an almost-perfect central
deck matching impossible.  This is an obstruction to the exact matching
reduction, not by itself a lower bound against a more permissive union-cover
ledger, where an `o(W)` number of repeated central observations may be
charged.

#### Proof

Every word in `G` has the same complete-state endpoint transformation, so
(5.1) is forced inductively.  This proves (5.2); forgetting (5.1) gives
(5.3).  The bridge accounting is the already proved `R_br/ell` ratio.
\(\square\)

This also explains why the exact marginals in Theorem 1.1 are annealed:
they average a fresh uniform seed in every slot.  A bridge-free repeated
backbone has only one initial seed.

This seed obstruction is specific to the connector-free repeated full-state
backbone.  The later truncated-history connector theorem permits arbitrary
fresh histories at linear seam cost and makes the independently seeded
central palette physical once the core length is `n log n`; see Section 8.
It does not make the discarded seam incidences negligible simultaneously
over the whole DCC band.

## 6. A physical stationary product and its exact remaining statistic

There is a way to obtain a genuinely Cartesian physical palette without
independent bridges.  It does not yet prove regularity.  In this section
`d=n-f+1=m-H=Theta(n)` is the number of legal tail generators in the
underlying common-fibre palette.

Let independent uniform generator words `X_1,...,X_b`, each of length
`ell`, drive a trajectory whose initial state `Pi_1` is uniform on `S_n`.
Let `Sigma_i` be the position transformation induced by `X_i`, so

\[
 \Pi_{i+1}=\Sigma_i(\Pi_i).                           \tag{6.1}
\]

Freeze the transformation vector and the checkpoint states.

### Proposition 6.1 (stationary frozen-fiber rectangle)

Conditional on `(Sigma_1,...,Sigma_b)`, the generator blocks are independent
and each is uniform on its exact transformation fiber.  Replacing them
independently within those fibers preserves every checkpoint in (6.1), so
the resulting product is physically FIFO-compatible.  If `p_i(S)` is the
conditional probability that the retained central deck in slot `i`
contains `S`, then

\[
 \mathbb E p_i(S)={a\over W},\qquad
 \mathbb E\sum_i p_i(S)={A\over W}.                  \tag{6.2}
\]

The analogous annealed pair expectations are exactly (1.3).

#### Proof

The transformations are coordinatewise functions of independent blocks,
which proves conditional independence and uniformity on each fiber.  Every
replacement has the frozen endpoint, proving physical compatibility.
Uniform measure on states is stationary for the generator walk, and the
current block word is independent of its starting state.  Sampling a
size-biased fiber and then a uniform member reconstructs the original
uniform word.  Relabeling symmetry now proves (6.2) and (1.3).  \(\square\)

The usual band-simplicity, chordlessness, and antipode exclusions can be
imposed after this freezing.  For polynomial block and macro lengths their
total bad mass is `exp(-n/2+o(n))`.  Transformation cells whose conditional
bad mass is larger than its square root, and all observations in the
corresponding exceptional blocks, have `o(W)` total expected incidence.  On
every remaining cell, conditioning on the good event preserves the
checkpoint and Cartesian-product statements and perturbs the aggregate
annealed degree ledger by only `o(W)`.  Pointwise regularity still requires
the second-moment argument below.

Expectation is not enough: the desired weighted slot hypergraph needs
total degree defect `o(W)`.  The exact second-moment statistic is especially
simple.  For a transformation `tau`, let `F_tau` be its fiber and let
`D^pos(w)` denote the retained deck of position subsets.  Put

\[
 J(\tau)=\mathbb E_{w,w'\in F_\tau}
 |D^{pos}(w)\cap D^{pos}(w')|.                        \tag{6.3}
\]

Lemma 7.1 below shows that, when only the endpoint observation is deleted,
every two words in `F_tau` still share the last `f-m` retained central
targets.  Hence

\[
 J(\tau)\ge f-m=H+2.                                 \tag{6.3a}
\]

This lower bound is `o(a)` at all block lengths considered here, so it does
not contradict the desired condition below.  It does rule out any proposed
same-fiber estimate of order `O(1)` unless the whole forced suffix is first
truncated.

### Lemma 6.2 (fiber-overlap identity)

For fixed `tau` and a uniform seed `Pi`,

\[
 \mathbb E_\Pi p_{\Pi,\tau}(S)^2={J(\tau)\over W}.   \tag{6.4}
\]

Hence, for independent stationary slot environments, the condition

\[
 \mathbb E_\tau J(\tau)=o(a)                         \tag{6.5}
\]

would imply `Var(sum_i p_i(S))=o(1)` when `ab=(1+o(1))W`, and therefore

\[
 \mathbb E\sum_S\left|\sum_i p_i(S)-1\right|=o(W).  \tag{6.6}
\]

For the actual Markov sequence one additionally needs the corresponding
summable covariance bound between different slots.

#### Proof

Take independent `w,w'` uniformly from the same fiber and expand the square
of the conditional inclusion probability.  Since both decks are simple,
after averaging the seed a fixed label target `S` is common to the two
decks with probability

\[
 {|D^{pos}(w)\cap D^{pos}(w')|\over W}.
\]

This proves (6.4).  Summing independent-slot variances and using
`b=(1+o(1))W/a` gives `o(1)` from (6.5).  Equation (6.6) follows from
Cauchy--Schwarz and relabeling symmetry.  \(\square\)

The entropy estimate

\[
 H(X_i\mid\Sigma_i)\ge \ell\log d-\log(n!)           \tag{6.7}
\]

does **not** imply (6.5).  Abstractly, exponentially many `a`-sets can all
share a fixed positive fraction of their vertices while still having far
more than `exp(Omega(a log n))` choices for their remaining vertices.
Thus endpoint entropy can be negligible while `J(tau)=Theta(a)`.
A dynamical bridge-overlap theorem, not entropy alone, is required.

There is a relevant mixing warning.  The inverse tail-MTF generator walk is
the top-to-bottom-`d` shuffle.  For `d=Theta(n)`, its forward total-variation
mixing time is `Theta(n log n)`, but exact endpoint conditioning is a
two-sided bridge question.  Goel proved that the additive symmetrization of
the top-to-bottom-half shuffle has total-variation mixing time at least
`Omega(n^2)`.  Therefore the forward `Theta(n log n)` theorem does not
supply the `L^2`/ratio bridge estimate needed for (6.5), even when
`ell=n log^2 n`.

There is also a sharp scale tension.  Chordlessness forces the adjacent-pair
ratio in (1.4) at every block length, so

\[
 a{\Delta_2\over\Delta}\ge (8+o(1)){a\over n^2}.
 \tag{6.7a}
\]

Thus the natural variable-rank local-tree hypothesis requires `a=o(n^2)`.
The available two-sided full-state mixing warning occurs on the `n^2`
scale.  This does not rule out faster mixing of the particular central-deck
projection, but it shows that simply lengthening blocks until full bridge
mixing applies is incompatible with (6.7a).

At that longer scale the matching parameters themselves remain favorable:

\[
 a=\Theta(n\log^2n),\quad
 {\Delta_2\over\Delta}=O(\log^2n/n^2),\quad
 a{\Delta_2\over\Delta}=O(\log^4n/n)=o(1).           \tag{6.8}
\]

Thus a concrete positive route survives:

1. prove the same-fiber overlap and interslot covariance bounds
   (6.5)--(6.6) for stationary frozen tail-MTF bridges;
2. prove an average-waste variable-rank nibble that iterates Lemma 4.1 down
   to any density `rho` with `rho >> r Delta_2/Delta`;
3. apply it to the physically compatible product of Proposition 6.1.

Either of the first two bullets is a new theorem.  Neither is supplied by
the current entropy or fixed-rank matching results.

## 7. Exact endpoint-forced suffix and the repeated-transformation obstruction

This section uses the actual tail-MTF floor.  Write a state as an ordered
permutation

\[
 \pi=(\pi_1,\ldots,\pi_n),
\]

and let a legal move `T_q`, with `q>=f`, move `pi_q` to the front.  Put
`C_k(pi)={pi_1,...,pi_k}`.  In the DCC palette

\[
 K=\{m-H,\ldots,m+1+H\},\qquad f=m+H+2.              \tag{7.1}
\]

### Lemma 7.1 (forced reverse suffix)

Let a legal word of length `ell` have state sequence
`pi_0,...,pi_ell` and endpoint `eta=pi_ell`.  For every rank `k<f` and
every integer `s` with `0<=s<=min(ell,f-k)`,

\[
 C_k(\pi_{\ell-s})
   =\{\eta_{s+1},\eta_{s+2},\ldots,\eta_{s+k}\}.     \tag{7.2}
\]

Thus the final `min(ell,f-k)+1` rank-`k` observations, including the
endpoint, are functions of the endpoint alone, independently of the branch
word; this is `f-k+1` observations when `ell>=f-k`.

#### Proof

The inverse of `T_q` sends

\[
 (y_1,\ldots,y_n)
 \longmapsto
 (y_2,\ldots,y_q,y_1,y_{q+1},\ldots,y_n).            \tag{7.3}
\]

We prove by induction on `s` the stronger ordered-prefix identity

\[
 (\pi_{\ell-s,1},\ldots,\pi_{\ell-s,f-s})
   =(\eta_{s+1},\ldots,\eta_f).                      \tag{7.4}
\]

It is immediate at `s=0`.  Every reverse move has `q>=f`, so on applying
(7.3) its first `f-s-1` entries are entries `2,...,f-s` of the previous
state.  This proves the induction.  If `s<=f-k`, the first `k` entries in
(7.4) give (7.2).  \(\square\)

### Corollary 7.2 (one-checkpoint deletion does not give matchable decks)

Suppose every block uses a common position transformation `sigma`, and the
blocks are concatenated without intervening seed changes.  Let
`h=ord(sigma)`.  If only the final checkpoint is deleted, then the retained
rank-`m` deck in block `i` contains the branch-independent target

\[
 F_i=\{(\pi_{i+1})_2,\ldots,(\pi_{i+1})_{m+1}\},     \tag{7.5}
\]

where `pi_(i+1)=sigma(pi_i)`.  In particular `F_(i+h)=F_i`, so a family of
pairwise-disjoint retained rank-`m` decks uses at most `h` block slots.

Since the order of a permutation of `[n]` is at most Landau's function,

\[
 h\le \exp\{(1+o(1))\sqrt{n\log n}\}=e^{o(n)}.      \tag{7.6}
\]

For any polynomial block length and
`b=(1+o(1))W/ell`, this is `o(b)`.  In particular such a repeated-`sigma`
backbone, with only its endpoint observation deleted, cannot contain a
central matching covering `W-o(W)` targets.

#### Proof

Take `s=1,k=m` in Lemma 7.1.  The checkpoint sequence is periodic with
period dividing `h`, which gives (7.5) and the intersection between every
two slots congruent modulo `h`.  A deck matching can therefore use at most
one slot in each phase.  Equation (7.6) is the standard maximal-order bound
in `S_n`, while `W=exp(Theta(n))`.  \(\square\)

The corollary concerns the exact deck-matching gate.  At one middle rank
the total number of forced retained occurrences is only `b(f-m)=b(H+2)`,
which is `o(W)` for `ell=Omega(n)`.  Hence it would be incorrect to infer
from Corollary 7.2 alone that a union-cover formulation allowing `o(W)`
collisions is impossible.

Across the whole band the raw forced ledger is much larger.  At rank `k`,
the retained endpoint-forced observations are those with
`1<=s<=f-k`.  Across `b` repeated blocks they have at most `h(f-k)`
distinct values and therefore at least `(b-h)(f-k)` repeated occurrences.
Since the numbers `f-k`, for `k in K`, are `2H+2,2H+1,...,1`,

\[
 \sum_{k\in K}(f-k)={(2H+2)(2H+3)\over2}.            \tag{7.7}
\]

For `ell=Cn` and `H=ceil(sqrt(n log n))`, (7.7) is
`(2/C+o(1))W log n` raw repeated rank-incidences.  This is not automatically
a defect lower bound in a ledger which may assign outer-rank occurrences to
dummies, but any claim that one-checkpoint deletion makes the full-band
backbone collision-free must account for (7.7).

### Corollary 7.3 (minimal common truncation and its cost)

For a set of ranks `K_0 subseteq {1,...,f-1}`, deleting the last

\[
 q(K_0)=1+\max_{k\in K_0}(f-k)                       \tag{7.8}
\]

post-move observations of every block removes every target forced by
Lemma 7.1.  This is minimal for removing that entire endpoint-determined
suffix simultaneously at all ranks in `K_0`.  In particular,

\[
 q(\{m\})=H+3,
 \qquad q(K)=2H+3.                                   \tag{7.9}
\]

Central-only truncation has charged volume `b q({m})=o(W)` already when
`ell=Theta(n)`.  If the same discarded times are charged at every rank in
the full band, the raw charged rank-incidence ratio is

\[
 {|K|q(K)b\over W}
  =(1+o(1)){(2H+2)(2H+3)\over\ell}.                  \tag{7.10}
\]

Consequently (7.10) is `Theta(log n)` for `ell=Theta(n)`, `Theta(1)` for
`ell=Theta(n log n)`, and `Theta(1/log n)=o(1)` for
`ell=Theta(n log^2 n)`.  Deleting a merely unspecified `O(n)` suffix is
safe for the central ledger at the last scale, but need not be safe after
multiplication by all `|K|` ranks; the sharp `Theta(H)` truncation in (7.9)
is what gives the last conclusion in (7.10).

After this truncation, write `a'=ell-q(K_0)`.  Simplicity,
chordlessness, and antipode-freeness are inherited by the shortened deck,
and Sections 1--2 apply with `a'` in place of `a`.  At
`ell=Theta(n log^2 n)`,

\[
 {\Delta_2\over\Delta}=O(\log^2 n/n^2),\qquad
 (a'+1){\Delta_2\over\Delta}=O(\log^4 n/n)=o(1),
 \qquad
 \Xi=O(\log^4 n/n)=o(1).                             \tag{7.11}
\]

Thus sharp suffix deletion removes the explicit periodic obstruction and
preserves the favorable first-bite matching parameters.  It does not prove
the iterated nibble or the quenched regularity of the remaining fibers.

## 8. Free truncated histories remove the central seed gate

The fixed-transformation requirement is unnecessary if only the recency
history seen by the DCC ranks must be connected.  This uses the proved
linear-seam theorem in
`MATH_THEOREM_TWO_BLOCK_FIFO_PRODUCT_ATOMS_AND_LINEAR_SEAMS_20260821.md`.

Use the proved one-position narrowing of the free tail.  In the notation of
Section 7 the original floor is `f=m+H+2`; put

\[
 f^\sharp=f+1=m+H+3,\qquad
 k_0=f^\sharp-1=f=m+H+2,\qquad
 d^\sharp=n-k_0=m-H-1.                              \tag{8.1}
\]

An ordered `k_0`-history is an injective word on `[n]`, written from oldest
to newest.  A legal next letter is any of the `d^sharp` letters outside the
history, after which the oldest letter is dropped.  The history digraph is
`d^sharp`-in-regular and `d^sharp`-out-regular.  Since

\[
 d^\sharp\ge (k_0-d^\sharp)+2=2H+5                 \tag{8.2}
\]

for all sufficiently large `n`, its directed diameter is at most

\[
 L_{\rm seam}=3k_0+1=O(n).                           \tag{8.3}
\]

The constructive proof actually gives a seam of this fixed displayed
length.  It follows that, after any preceding block, one can reach an
arbitrary prescribed next history and then traverse an arbitrary legal
letter word from that history.  Complete bottom-order agreement is neither
asserted nor needed: every DCC rank is at most `k_0`, and its recency set and
future legality are determined by this truncated history.

Let `ell=Theta(n log n)`.  Define `Omega` to be all pairs `(u,w)`, where
`u` is an ordered `k_0`-history and `w` is a legal length-`ell` continuation.
Let `Omega_g` be the relabeling-invariant subfamily whose rank decks in the
DCC band are simple and chordless and whose two middle decks have the
audited extremal-distance exclusions.  The narrowed-tail Theorems 1.2--1.3
of `MATH_THEOREM_POSTPONED_GRAPHIC_COALESCENT_AND_DECOUPLING_GATE_20260821.md`
give

\[
 |\Omega_g|=(1-o(1))(n)_{k_0}(d^\sharp)^\ell.       \tag{8.4}
\]

Indeed uniform full states and generator words project with constant
multiplicity `d^sharp!` onto `(history,legal continuation)` pairs, and the
goodness event depends only on that projected path.

### Proposition 8.1 (exact invariant free-history palette)

Under the uniform law on `Omega_g`, every retained rank-`q` deck has size
`ell`, and for every `S in binom([n],q)`,

\[
 \Pr(S\text{ is in the deck})={\ell\over {n\choose q}}. \tag{8.5}
\]

At any fixed rank `q`, pair inclusion probabilities depend only on Johnson
distance.  At rank `m` the normalized profile is exactly (1.3), with
`a=ell`.  Uniformly at every rank `q in K`, the maximum normalized
same-rank pair codegree is

\[
 O\!\left({1\over n^2}+{\ell\over n^3}\right)
   =O(\log n/n^2).                                   \tag{8.6}
\]

Nested targets in different ranks have larger codegrees; (8.6) is only a
same-rank assertion, and the one-central hypergraph uses just `q=m`.

#### Proof

The uniform law on all starting histories is stationary because the
history digraph has equal in- and out-degree `d^sharp`.  Both `Omega_g` and the
deck event are invariant under every relabeling of `[n]`.  Since every good
deck has exactly `ell` targets, transitivity gives (8.5).  Transitivity on
Johnson-distance pair orbits gives (1.3).

For rank `q`, the number of distance-`j` targets from a fixed target is

\[
 B_{q,j}={q\choose j}{n-q\choose j}.                 \tag{8.7}
\]

Chordlessness gives the distance-one ratio
`2(ell-1)/(ell q(n-q))=O(n^(-2))`.  For `j>=2`, the
trivial pair count gives ratio at most `(ell-1)/B_(q,j)`.  Uniformly over
`q in K`, every allowed `B_(q,j)` with `j>=2` is `Omega(n^3)`; the only
smaller far-distance orbits are the excluded maximum-distance pairs at
`q=m,m+1`.  This proves (8.6).  The one-position narrowing is essential
here: `max K=f^sharp-2`, so the short-chord obstruction at one below the
old floor is absent.  \(\square\)

Take

\[
 b=\left\lfloor{W\over \ell}\right\rfloor,
 \qquad A=b\ell=W-O(\ell).                          \tag{8.8}
\]

The slot-plus-rank-`m` hypergraph formed from `Omega_g` is
`(1+o(1))`-almost regular with exponentially small relative error: a slot
has degree `|Omega_g|`, while a central target has degree
`b|Omega_g|ell/W=(A/W)|Omega_g|`.  Its
target-pair profile is (1.3), and

\[
 r={\ell+1}=\Theta(n\log n),\qquad
 {\Delta_2\over\Delta}=O(\log n/n^2),\qquad
 r{\Delta_2\over\Delta}=O(\log^2 n/n)=o(1),         \tag{8.9}
\]

while

\[
 \Xi=O(\log^2 n/n)=o(1).                            \tag{8.10}
\]

Every integral selection of the core edges can be serialized literally:
before core `i`, use (8.3) to reach its chosen history `u_i`.  The total
physical seam length is

\[
 bL_{\rm seam}=O(W/\log n)=o(W).                    \tag{8.11}
\]

At rank `m`, all seam observations may therefore be charged at `o(W)` cost.
The serialized physical length is `A+bL_seam=(1+o(1))W`; reserving the seam
inside an exact `W`-step budget is unnecessary for a coefficient-one
asymptotic statement and would introduce an avoidable `Theta(1/log n)`
degree imbalance.
Thus the independently seeded hypergraph is no longer an annealed physical
relaxation for the **one-central matching problem**.  The remaining central
gate is exactly the growing-rank iterated matching theorem from Section 4.

### Corollary 8.2 (exact physical lift of a central near-factor)

Suppose the free-history slot hypergraph has a matching of `t` core edges
with

\[
 W-t\ell=o(W).                                      \tag{8.11a}
\]

Then those chosen cores can be ordered arbitrarily and joined, including
the cyclic closing seam, to form a literal legal cyclic word of length
`(1+o(1))W`.  Its rank-`m` collision plus leave is `o(W)`.

#### Proof

Before each chosen core, apply the connector to reach that edge's prescribed
history.  This also joins the final core back to the first, so the cyclic
word is legal and all equal-letter gaps are at least `f^sharp`.  Its core
length is `t ell<=W` and its seam length is at most

\[
 tL_{\rm seam}=O((W/\ell)n)=O(W/\log n)=o(W).
\]

The matching makes all `t ell` core targets distinct.  If `S` is the number
of seam observations, adding the seams creates at most `S` repeated
occurrences, while the number of rank-`m` targets still unseen is at most
`W-t ell`.  Their sum is `o(W)` by (8.11a).  \(\square\)

There is an important full-band limitation.  Charging every seam
observation at every rank costs

\[
 O(|K|bL_{\rm seam})=O(|K|W/\log n),                \tag{8.12}
\]

which is not `o(W)` for the present
`|K|=Theta(sqrt(n log n))`.  Hence (8.11) removes the physical seed problem
for the central deck matching, but a full DCC conclusion still needs either
to use the seam observations rather than discard them or to amortize seams
over longer product atoms.  It would be an overclaim to insert (8.12) into
the all-rank checkpoint ledger as a negligible error.
