# One terminal folded-C8 host gives a replacement recurrence, conditional on prescribed triangular absorption

Date: 2026-08-01  
Lane: A, same-parity Pascal regeneration / terminal compiler  
Status: exact conditional induction, exact scalar triangular ledger, and
sharp physical-scope obstruction.  The current physical endpoint theorem
supplies only one ray, not the two cross matchings assumed by the induction.
No all-dimensional host embedding and no unconditional additive-constant
theorem are claimed.

## 0. Outcome

The zero-block theorem removes the need for a serial comparator network.  It
does **not**, by itself, reclaim the source position occupied by the previous
terminal host.

The correct induction state distinguishes ordinary background overhead from
one marked terminal split.  Write

\[
                    \chi_k=c_k+h_k,
       \qquad c_k\ge0,\qquad h_k\in\{0,1\},                 \tag{0.1}
\]

where `h_k=1` means that one of the charged positions is a certified binary
host split which can be contracted and replaced.  Under the exact hypotheses
in Theorem 4.1 below, a same-parity child satisfies

\[
               h_{k+2}=1,\qquad c_{k+2}\le c_k+b_{k+2},    \tag{0.2}
\]

and hence

\[
 \boxed{\quad
   \chi_{k+2}\le \chi_k+1-h_k+b_{k+2}.
 \quad}                                                     \tag{0.3}
\]

If the state is normalized so that

\[
                    h_k={\bf1}_{\{\chi_k>0\}},               \tag{0.4}
\]

then (0.3) is exactly

\[
 \boxed{\quad
       \chi_{k+2}\le \max\{\chi_k,1\}+b_{k+2}.
 \quad}                                                     \tag{0.5}
\]

Here `b_{k+2}` is the **net indispensable background charge after all
certified background contractions**, not the number of locally damaged
rows.  A positive fresh `b_{k+2}` at every step can still accumulate.  A
uniform additive bound follows from (0.5) only when the background charge
is eventually zero, is simultaneously reclaimed, or obeys a separate
contracting recurrence.

There are two load-bearing hypotheses behind (0.3).

1. The old marked split must become an ordinary contracted child block, with
   all contraction-dependent occurrences rehosted in one protected matching.
2. One fresh child split would have to give **literal addressed** versions
   of both zero-block cross matchings in one matching-closed cap state.  This
   is an unproved joint-host hypothesis, not a property of the currently
   authenticated endpoint rethread.

The triangular capacity projection is encouraging and sharp.  If
`d=d(k)` and `D=d(k+2)`, then `D` is `d` or `d+1`.  The pessimistic old-host
residual consists of two chains of length `d-1`; exactly one such host fits
the abstract child capacities `1,...,D`, while two hosts never do.  Thus the
zero-block collapse to one terminal host is quantitatively the largest bank
which those capacities could absorb.

But scalar capacity is not a physical absorption theorem.  On a no-jump
step `D=d>=3`, the two canonical opposite C8 rays cannot both be realized as
chains in the canonical one-sided boundary prefix.  The missing theorem is
an occurrence-labelled prescribed-block triangular completion, not another
capacity estimate.

## 1. One marked split and its exact contraction bank

Let

\[
                 \widetilde W=L\,Z\,T\,R,
       \qquad X=Z\cup T\ne\varnothing,                       \tag{1.1}
\]

and let

\[
                          \overline W=L\,X\,R                \tag{1.2}
\]

be its contraction.  The **full-block lift** sends an interval of
`overline W` avoiding `X` to its shifted copy and sends an interval containing
`X` to the interval containing both consecutive letters `Z,T`.

### Lemma 1.1 (exact full-block/side-cell partition)

The full-block lift is injective on physical intervals and preserves every
literal OR value.  Every interval of `widetilde W` outside its image is of
exactly one of the following two types:

* a left side cell ending at `Z` and not containing `T`; or
* a right side cell beginning at `T` and not containing `Z`.

Consequently, for any occurrence-labelled matching `M` in `widetilde W`,
write

\[
                         M=M_{\rm blk}\dot\cup M_{\rm side}  \tag{1.3}
\]

according as its cell is or is not a full-block lift.  If the targets of
`M_side` have an injection to legal cells of another bank, disjoint from the
images of `M_blk`, then the split may be contracted while preserving every
target of `M`.

#### Proof

An interval containing both `Z,T` has the same union after replacing them by
`X`; intervals avoiding both are unchanged up to index shift.  These images
are distinct.  An interval meeting exactly one member of the consecutive
pair must end at `Z` or begin at `T`, and conversely every such interval is
outside the image.  Reassign `M_side` by the displayed injection and retain
the contracted images of `M_blk`.  The two cell banks are disjoint, so their
union is a matching. \(\square\)

This is the exact sense in which an inherited host can become an ordinary
full block.  Merely carrying the two old letters intact into the child does
not save a charged position.  The saving occurs only after either literal
contraction (1.2), or a child baseline construction which counts the two
positions inside its uncharged triangular schedule and supplies the same
matching certificate.

For the canonical one-split two-ray word, the side cells in Lemma 1.1 are
precisely the two nested rays.  In a stronger reset state many of those
targets may already have contraction-safe duplicate occurrences; the true
residual is always the occurrence-labelled subbank actually used by
`M_side`, not automatically all `2(d-1)` ray values.

## 2. What one fresh split would have to supply

Put `m=D-1`.  In the canonical terminal birail state, each threshold shore
has multiset

\[
                         \{0^m,1,2,\ldots,m\}.                  \tag{2.1}
\]

For a physical pairing graph `H`, write its shores as

\[
 L=L_0\dot\cup L_+,qquad R=R_0\dot\cup R_+,
 \qquad |L_0|=|L_+|=|R_0|=|R_+|=m.                            \tag{2.2}
\]

The zero-block theorem gives the exact criterion

\[
 \lambda_{\rm ray}(H)=0
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 H[L_0,R_+]\text{ and }H[L_+,R_0]
 \text{ have perfect matchings}.                             \tag{2.3}
\]

No sorting of the positive thresholds and no sequence of adjacent
comparators remains in (2.3).

Suppose, conditionally, that one actual child letter `X_*` has a nonempty
decomposition

\[
                            X_*=Z_*\cup T_*                    \tag{2.4}
\]

and its two outward cumulative-union traces realize the two prescribed ray
chains **in one owner/cap/guard state**.  Splitting `X_*` into `Z_*,T_*`
would then create the two side-cell banks of Lemma 1.1.  If those
occurrence-labelled cells realize the two perfect matchings in (2.3), the
single split would supply the entire terminal ray matching.  None of these
sentences asserts that the authenticated folded endpoint has this joint
property.

An abstract edge in (2.3) is not automatically one physical compiler cell.
For the folded antidiagonal it is a paired ticket, and its two coordinates
must be realized by distinct legal interval addresses in compatible phase
states.  The phrase **fresh addressed C8 certificate** below always means
that this physical two-cell requirement has been checked.

### Lemma 2.1 (conditional one-host zero-ray-charge implication)

Assume:

1. the split (2.4) has the required two outward trace identities;
2. its physical occurrence graph has the two cross perfect matchings (2.3);
3. all selected ray cells are distinct and avoid the transported
   full-block matching; and
4. the complete selected row family lies in one legal cap state.

Then the fresh terminal ray bank has matching deficiency zero and adds
exactly one source position.  It creates no positive-threshold comparator
debt.

#### Proof

Take the disjoint union of the two cross matchings.  It saturates all four
shores and contains no zero--zero pair, hence has zero birail deficiency.
The addressed hypotheses turn its tickets into distinct physical cells.
Lemma 1.1 separates those cells from every full-block lift.  Splitting one
letter increases source length by one. \(\square\)

### 2.1 Current authenticated physical boundary

The independent common-endpoint replay gives, for every audited
`2<=D<=12`,

\[
                    \texttt{exact\_one\_ray\_comparators}=0.   \tag{2.5}
\]

The best unmodified common-endpoint cuts retain only fifteen of the sixteen
local upper support values.  A separate length-neutral three-edge seam does
restore all sixteen and yields an upper-support-safe resident two-path owner
forest.  On that forest the endpoint rethread proves, phasewise and for all
depths symbolically, one complete nested **suffix** ray with injective
target-labelled old-background transport.  The coordinate needed for the
complementary transported prefix ray may lie on the other path component.

Therefore the currently proved physical statement is only

\[
 \boxed{\text{one full ray + transported background, phasewise}.}       \tag{2.6}
\]

It is not either cross perfect matching in (2.3), much less both in one
cap/guard state.  Lemma 2.1 is the precise missing joint implication.  The
zero-block theorem says what to check once the two physical banks exist; it
does not manufacture the absent prefix bank or move it across components.

The same endpoint replay also identifies the non-ray debts which must not be
hidden in `b_(k+2)`.  The upper-safe owner surgery removes three old lower
colours and adds one new colour, so exact lower-palette repair needs three
named old-colour services, not merely two units by edge count.  Its planted
source shrinks the first `D` maximal-source positions.  Exactly

\[
                \{[i,j]:0\le i\le j<D\},
                \qquad \binom{D+1}{2}\text{ addresses},       \tag{2.7}
\]

change core.  Every old containment-graph edge remains individually legal
because the new core is smaller and its cap is unchanged, but simultaneous
source-row equality is not automatic.  These rows must pass Lemma 3.1
together with all fixed matching, owner, ray, and palette rows.  Thus the
current one-ray actuator supplies useful physical input to `PBTA_k`; it does
not satisfy its fresh-host clause.

## 3. The exact fixed-chronology cap and Hall tests

The cap and matching rows in Lemma 2.1 cannot be inferred from marginal ray
counts.  Fix a child source schedule.  For every equality row `R`, let

* `S_R` be its required target;
* `E_R` be its frozen exterior union;
* `J_R` be its live source positions; and
* `P_p` be the source cap at live position `p`.

Let `M_p` be any mandatory source lower bound at `p`, and put

\[
                  K_p=P_p\cap
                       \bigcap_{R:\,p\in J_R}S_R.             \tag{3.1}
\]

### Lemma 3.1 (maximal-word test)

There are nonempty source letters `Q_p` satisfying every equality row and
every mandatory lower bound if and only if

\[
\begin{aligned}
 &E_R\subseteq S_R &&\text{for every }R,\\
 &\varnothing\ne M_p\subseteq K_p &&\text{at every mandatory live }p,\\
 &S_R=E_R\cup\bigcup_{p\in J_R}K_p &&\text{for every }R.      \tag{3.2}
\end{aligned}
\]

At positions without a mandatory lower bound, only `K_p ne empty` is
required.  When (3.2) holds, `Q_p=K_p` is the componentwise maximal solution.

#### Proof

Every feasible `Q_p` lies in every target row using `p`, and hence in `K_p`.
This proves the necessary containments and one direction of the row equality.
Conversely, (3.2) makes the maximal choice `Q_p=K_p` nonempty, cap-legal,
compatible with `M_p`, and exactly reconstructive in every row. \(\square\)

Now reserve all addressed ray cells, palette sidecars, and any other forced
packet cells.  Delete their targets and cells from the residual
target--occurrence graph `G`.  A complete occurrence matching exists if and
only if

\[
                         |N_G(Y)|\ge |Y|
                         \qquad(Y\subseteq V_{\rm target}(G)). \tag{3.3}
\]

Equations (3.2) and (3.3) are separate.  Hall in a union of incompatible cap
states is not a common-`Q` certificate.

## 4. Exact one-terminal-host induction

Let

\[
 B(k)=W_k+d_k,qquad W_k=\binom{k}{\lceil k/2\rceil},          \tag{4.1}
\]

where `d_k` is the optimal triangular depth.  A **normalized terminal
state** at dimension `k` is a literal chronology of length

\[
                             B(k)+c_k+h_k                       \tag{4.2}
\]

with the decomposition (0.1), together with its owner, palette, upper,
residence, protected-occurrence, and common-cap certificates.  When
`h_k=1`, the state names one split (1.1).

### Theorem 4.1 (one-terminal Pascal replacement theorem)

Fix a same-parity transition `k -> k+2`.  Suppose the parent is a normalized
terminal state and the child construction has all of the following.

1. **Prescribed-block absorption.**  If `h_k=1`, contract the marked parent
   split.  The child presplit chronology has length at most

   \[
                    B(k+2)+c_k+b_{\rm base},                   \tag{4.3}
   \]

   and transports every inherited full-block cell injectively.  Every target
   formerly assigned to a side cell is included in the forced fresh-host or
   residual completion bank.  If `h_k=0`, (4.3) is required without a
   contraction.
2. **Fresh terminal host.**  One existing child letter admits the split
   (2.4), and Lemma 2.1 holds.
3. **Hard physical rows.**  The final owner word is simple and Johnson,
   both `q1` ledgers are correct, all required upper witnesses survive,
   residence/deadline and topology hold, and the entire fixed chronology
   passes Lemma 3.1.
4. **Residual completion.**  After the forced full-block and fresh-host
   assignments, (3.3) fails by at most `b_{\rm comp}` appendable targets and
   has no unmatched hard packet task.

Put

\[
                         b_{k+2}=b_{\rm base}+b_{\rm comp}.    \tag{4.4}
\]

Then the child has a literal terminal word with

\[
                 h_{k+2}=1,qquad c_{k+2}\le c_k+b_{k+2},     \tag{4.5}
\]

and hence satisfies (0.3).  Under the normalization (0.4), it satisfies
(0.5).

#### Proof

When `h_k=1`, prescribed-block absorption reclaims its one charged boundary
before the child is counted.  The other `c_k` positions are already included
in (4.3).  When `h_k=0`, there is no old host charge to reclaim.  In either
case, the one fresh split increases (4.3) by exactly one.  Appending the
`b_comp` residual soft targets gives length at most

\[
 B(k+2)+c_k+b_{\rm base}+1+b_{\rm comp}.
\]

This is (4.5).

For correctness, retain the full-block assignments, use the fresh addressed
cross matchings for the terminal bank, and complete the remaining targets by
(3.3).  The cell banks are disjoint by hypothesis.  Lemma 3.1 supplies one
literal source word for all of them, and the hard physical rows give the
claimed chronology. \(\square\)

### Corollary 4.2 (uniform one-host invariant)

If `b_{k+2}=0` at every sufficiently large same-parity step and one base
state has `chi_k<=1`, then every later state on that parity has

\[
                              \chi_k\le1.                       \tag{4.6}
\]

More generally, if the whole background bank is reset each time to a bound
`b_*`, rather than adding fresh debt, then `chi_k<=1+b_*`.  By contrast,
the estimate `b_{k+2}=O(1)` interpreted as a new positive birth at every
step gives only a linearly accumulating bound and is insufficient for
`B(k)+O(1)`.

The quantity in (0.1) is the physical source-length/essential-boundary
charge used by the CBC serial theorem.  It is not automatically the
route-inspection `chi` of an abstract selected trace multiset in a de Bruijn
graph.  Using the latter notation additionally requires that the child
trace selection containing the absorbed full block lies on the same
connected Euler trail.

## 5. Exact triangular capacity audit

Put

\[
 d=d_k,\qquad D=d_{k+2}.                                  \tag{5.1}
\]

### Lemma 5.1 (same-parity depth changes by at most one)

In the nontrivial range `d>=1`, one has

\[
                              D\in\{d,d+1\}.                    \tag{5.2}
\]

#### Proof

For `k=2m+1`,

\[
 \Lambda'=4\Lambda+3,qquad
 W'=\left(4-\frac2{m+2}\right)W;                       \tag{5.3}
\]

for `k=2m`,

\[
 \Lambda'=4\Lambda+\frac{W}{m+1}+3,qquad
 W'=\left(4-\frac2{m+1}\right)W.                       \tag{5.4}
\]

Here primes refer to `k+2`.  Parent infeasibility at depth `d-1`, multiplied
by four and combined with `W'<4W`, gives child infeasibility at `d-1`, so
`D>=d`.

For the other direction put

\[
                         \tau_q=\binom{q+1}{2}.
\]

Parent feasibility says `Lambda<=dW+tau_d`.  In the odd case it is enough
that

\[
 \left(4-\frac{2(d+1)}{m+2}\right)W
       +\tau_{d+1}-4\tau_d-3\ge0.                    \tag{5.4a}
\]

Since `d<=m`, the coefficient of `W` in (5.4a) is greater than two, while

\[
                   \tau_{d+1}-4\tau_d-3
                    =-\frac{3d^2+d+4}{2}.             \tag{5.4b}
\]

For `m=1`, (5.4a) is immediate.  For `m>=2`, unimodality gives

\[
 W=\binom{2m+1}{m}\ge\binom{2m+1}{2}=m(2m+1),
\]

and therefore `4W>=3d^2+d+4`; (5.4a) follows.

In the even case the sufficient coefficient is

\[
                4-\frac{2d+3}{m+1}>2,                \tag{5.4c}
\]

because `d<=m-1`; the negative polynomial is again (5.4b).  For `m>=2`,

\[
 W=\binom{2m}{m}\ge\binom{2m}{2}=m(2m-1),
\]

again gives `4W>=3d^2+d+4`.  The case `m=1` has `d=0` and lies outside the
stated nontrivial range.  Thus depth `d+1` is child-feasible and `D<=d+1`.
\(\square\)

The following elementary statement is the complete **abstract capacity**
test.

### Lemma 5.2 (Ferrers absorption criterion)

Let a residual bank be partitioned into chains of lengths

\[
                         a_1\ge a_2\ge\cdots\ge a_s.          \tag{5.5}
\]

It fits abstract boundary capacities `1,2,...,D` if and only if

\[
                         s\le D,qquad a_j\le D-j+1
                         \quad(1\le j\le s).                  \tag{5.6}
\]

#### Proof

Match the chains, in decreasing order, to the capacities
`D,D-1,...`.  This works exactly under (5.6).  Conversely the `j` largest
chains must occupy `j` distinct capacities, the smallest of whose largest
possible values is `D-j+1`. \(\square\)

One raw terminal C8 host has two ray chains of length `d-1`.  Thus `H`
uncontracted hosts have profile

\[
                    (d-1,d-1)^{H}.                            \tag{5.7}
\]

By (5.6), this profile fits if and only if

\[
                              2H\le D-d+2.                      \tag{5.8}
\]

Since `D-d<=1`, exactly one host fits and two hosts never fit.  With one
host followed by background-chain lengths `b_1>=...>=b_t` no larger than
`d-1`, the remaining conditions are

\[
                              b_j\le D-j-1.                     \tag{5.9}
\]

Any fixed number of bounded background chains therefore passes the scalar
test for large `d`.

The two unextended raw rays necessarily waste

\[
                (D+(D-1))-2(d-1)=2(D-d)+1                    \tag{5.10}
\]

capacity units.  If the exact child scalar slack

\[
           \sigma_D=DW_{k+2}+\binom{D+1}{2}-\Lambda_{k+2}     \tag{5.11}
\]

is smaller than (5.10), at least one ray must be extended by a comparable
target or a global chain exchange must move the waste.  Existence of a
set-theoretic one-step extension does not prove a target-disjoint triangular
factor containing it.

In a strong reset state the true contraction residual may be only two typed
endpoint tasks, or even empty, because the remaining ray targets already
have contraction-safe full-block occurrences.  Then (5.7) is a pessimistic
profile.  The exact profile is determined by `M_side` in (1.3).

## 6. Why scalar absorption is not physical absorption

The integral triangular **resource** model permits arbitrary boundary
chains.  A literal one-sided boundary word is much more rigid.  Fix a source
prefix `A_1,...,A_D`.  A target assigned to boundary endpoint `i` at suffix
length `s` must equal

\[
                         A_{i-s+1}\cup\cdots\cup A_i.          \tag{6.1}
\]

For a prescribed family of such rows, put

\[
 K_p=P_p\cap\bigcap_{(i,s):\ i-s+1\le p\le i}S_{i,s}.        \tag{6.2}
\]

The family has one common nonzero boundary word exactly when the maximal-word
equalities of Lemma 3.1 hold for these intervals.  Thus (5.6) is necessary
but not sufficient.

There is a sharp obstruction on the actual folded rays.  Suppress their
common core and write

\[
\begin{aligned}
 P_j&=B_P\cup\{f_1,\ldots,f_j\}, &&1\le j<d,\\
 S_j&=B_S\cup\{f_{d-j+1},\ldots,f_d\}, &&1\le j<d,
                                                               \tag{6.3}
\end{aligned}
\]

where `B_P` contains the private active label `a_3` and `B_S` contains the
private active label `a_1`.  No `P_j` contains `f_d` or `a_1`, and no `S_j`
contains `f_1` or `a_3`.

### Proposition 6.1 (canonical no-jump common-prefix obstruction)

For `D=d>=3`, the two chains in (6.3) cannot simultaneously occupy the two
largest capacities of one common boundary prefix.

#### Proof

The chain assigned to endpoint `d-1` has `d-1` distinct members and hence
uses every suffix length `1,...,d-1`.  If it is the `S` chain, then

\[
                              A_{d-1}=S_1                  \tag{6.4}
\]

contains `f_d` and `a_1`.  The other chain has `d-1` members at endpoint
`d`; at most one uses suffix length one, so at least one uses length at least
two and therefore contains `A_{d-1}`.  But no `P_j` contains `f_d` or `a_1`,
a contradiction.  Interchanging `P,S` gives the same contradiction using
`f_1,a_3`. \(\square\)

Even without the canonical labels, scalar sufficiency fails uniformly.  For
`d>=4`, two disjoint strict chains of length `d-1` satisfy (5.6).  Any source
prefix realizing them as suffix chains needs two disjoint nonempty support
intervals of length at least `d-1`, and hence length at least `2d-2>D` by
(5.2), impossible.

Proposition 6.1 does not obstruct placing the two rays at opposite global
ends, rehosting them in the fresh split itself, or using a global triangular
exchange.  It proves only that the canonical one-prefix fractional
realization cannot be invoked as an automatic old-host absorber.

## 7. Minimum remaining theorem

The exact missing assertion is the following.

> **Prescribed-block one-terminal absorption (`PBTA_k`).**  In one
> same-parity child, choose a contracted baseline chronology of length
> `B(k+2)+c_k+O(b_{k+2})` which contains the inherited host union as an
> ordinary source block.  It must transport every protected full-block
> occurrence, rehost the exact contraction side bank, and extend to an
> integral target-disjoint triangular owner/boundary factor in one legal
> common cap.  In particular, any monotone prefix triangle such as (2.7)
> must satisfy the simultaneous maximal-word rows, and every lost lower
> colour must have a named service.  In that same chronology, one actual
> letter admits the fresh
> addressed C8 split of Lemma 2.1, with the palette, upper, residence,
> deadline, and topology rows of Theorem 4.1.

`PBTA_k` implies the recurrence (0.3)--(0.5).  The zero-block theorem proves
the terminal matching algebra once the addressed cross graphs exist.  The
optimal triangular theorem proves the scalar fractional profile.  Neither
proves `PBTA_k`.

This is strictly weaker than regenerating a serial comparator network: the
child needs one terminal cross-matching host, not a physical orbit realizing
an antitone permutation.  It is strictly stronger than transporting an old
block or checking Ferrers capacities separately, because it demands one
literal owner/common-cap chronology and one residual occurrence matching.

## 8. Adversarial audit

1. **Marked versus undifferentiated overhead.**  If `chi_k>0` but none of
   its positions is the certified old terminal host, (0.5) is invalid; only
   `chi_(k+2)<=chi_k+1+b_(k+2)` follows.  The state variable `h_k` is
   essential.
2. **Transport versus reclamation.**  Full-block lifting preserves old
   intervals but does not reduce length.  The `-h_k` term requires literal
   contraction or uncharged prescribed-block absorption.
3. **Threshold tickets versus cells.**  A folded antidiagonal ticket has two
   physical coordinates, often in opposite phase words.  Lemma 2.1 assumes
   both addressed cell banks; (2.3) alone is insufficient.
4. **One ray versus two cross banks.**  The authenticated endpoint actuator
   gives only (2.6).  Upper-safe seam closure does not preserve the missing
   prefix coordinate on the same component, and the negative common-endpoint
   replay has (2.5).  It is invalid to cite that actuator as Lemma 2.1.
5. **Capacity versus chronology.**  Equations (5.6)--(5.9) are only the
   chain-capacity projection.  Proposition 6.1 and Lemma 3.1 show the missing
   common-word constraint.
6. **Support versus protected occurrences.**  Rehosting a target value does
   not preserve a named q1 owner, upper witness, deadline cell, or trace
   guard unless that type is included in the forced row family.
7. **Background birth.**  An `O(1)` positive `b_k` per generation is not a
   bounded recurrence.  It must reset, contract, or vanish.
8. **Serialization notation.**  The physical essential-boundary `chi` here
   is not automatically de Bruijn route-inspection `chi`; a connected-trace
   certificate is separately required if one works before materializing a
   literal word.

The proposed strategic simplification is therefore sound at the abstract
recurrence level: the comparator network disappears and, **if the joint
two-cross-bank host is built**, its contribution is replacement rather than
addition.  The exact unproved bridge is `PBTA_k`, whose fresh-host clause is
strictly stronger than the currently proved one-ray actuator.
