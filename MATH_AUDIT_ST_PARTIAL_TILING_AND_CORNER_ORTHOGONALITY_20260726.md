# PBBS reciprocal-trace partial tilings and corner-charge orthogonality

Date: 2026-07-26

Method: pure mathematics only. No computation, search, or external input.

## 0. Outcome

Put

\[
 N=2m+1,\qquad B_m=\operatorname {Cat}_m,
 \qquad H=\lceil A\sqrt m\rceil .
\]

This note audits whether the exact common-edge and all-corner theorems can
exclude a positive-density partial tiling of the long PBBS quotient cycles.
They cannot do so with their present quantifiers.

The obstruction is an exact orthogonality.  The reciprocal-height slack of
an integral packing is supported on

1. excess duration inside a selected return, and
2. eligible quotient edges not covered by any selected return.

Every common-edge or corner charge, on the other hand, is an edge *inside*
one selected support.  It therefore has zero uncovered-edge slack.  Thus an
injective internal-edge charge cannot be added to the reciprocal dual as a
new positive defect unless the possible charged roots themselves form an
\(o(B_m/\sqrt m)\) set, or every selected interval supplies a diverging
number of charges.

For zero-winding Gaussian returns the audited all-corner theorem supplies
neither alternative.  It proves instead that every critical-order packing
has asymptotically sparse corner atlases.  More precisely, fix
\(0<a<A\).  If

\[
 |\mathcal P_m|\ge c\frac{B_m}{\sqrt m}
 \tag{0.1}
\]

and every member is a positive-endpoint-overlap zero-winding return with

\[
 a\sqrt m\le s(I)\le A\sqrt m,
\]

then there is a deterministic \(\varepsilon_m\downarrow0\) such that,
after deleting \(o(B_m/\sqrt m)\) intervals,

\[
 |\mathcal C(I)|\le \varepsilon_m s(I).
 \tag{0.2}
\]

Here \(\mathcal C(I)\) is the complete set of minimal nonoverlap corners;
persistent-overlap returns have \(\mathcal C(I)=\varnothing\) and already
satisfy (0.2).

If in addition the packing is a near-saturator of the reciprocal trace on
this fixed Gaussian band, then its supports cover all but \(o(B_m)\) band
edges and its total height-gap slack is \(o(B_m)\).  Consequently the
literal residual left by the union of the two audited tools is:

> an approximate quotient-edge tiling by equality or bounded-slack
> Gaussian returns whose complete nonoverlap atlases are generated by
> \(o(s)\) internal corner roots, including the persistent-overlap case.

The special common-edge corner family is negligible because its charged
root has deterministic collars of length \(\Theta(\sqrt m)\).  No such
global rarity theorem is known for the general sparse-corner roots.  The
exact missing statement is therefore a *cross-interval sparse-corner
rarity/collision theorem*, not another single-return corner injection.

No little-oh packing theorem or coefficient-one conclusion is claimed.

## 1. Band-restricted complementary slackness

Fix \(0<a<A\), and retain only long quotient cycles and eligible return
supports whose invariant heights lie in

\[
 \mathcal H_m=[\lceil a\sqrt m\rceil,
                \lfloor A\sqrt m\rfloor].
\]

Let \(V_{a,A}\) be the corresponding quotient-edge set and put

\[
 \mathcal T_{a,A}
 =\sum_{D\in V_{a,A}}\frac1{h(D)+2}.
 \tag{1.1}
\]

For an integral edge-disjoint packing \(\mathcal P\) in this band, write
\(Q(I)\) for the full support, including insertion and removal edges.
Every edge of \(Q(I)\) has height \(h(I)\), and

\[
 |Q(I)|=s(I)+2.
\]

Double counting gives the exact identity

\[
\boxed{
 \mathcal T_{a,A}-|\mathcal P|
 =\sum_{I\in\mathcal P}
       \frac{s(I)-h(I)}{h(I)+2}
  +\sum_{D\in V_{a,A}\setminus\bigcup_IQ(I)}
       \frac1{h(D)+2}.}
 \tag{1.2}
\]

### Proof

Let \(\ell_D\in\{0,1\}\) be the load of edge \(D\).  Edge-disjointness
gives

\[
 \sum_{D\in Q(I)}\frac1{h(D)+2}
 =\frac{s(I)+2}{h(I)+2}
 =1+\frac{s(I)-h(I)}{h(I)+2}.
\]

Summing over \(I\), and then subtracting the covered reciprocal mass from
the total mass (1.1), proves (1.2).  Every term is nonnegative because the
height-gap theorem gives \(s(I)\ge h(I)\). \(\square\)

Suppose now that

\[
 |\mathcal P|=\mathcal T_{a,A}-o(B_m/\sqrt m).
 \tag{1.3}
\]

Since \(h(I)+2\le A\sqrt m+2\), the first term of (1.2) yields

\[
 \sum_{I\in\mathcal P}(s(I)-h(I))=o_A(B_m).
 \tag{1.4}
\]

Every uncovered band edge has weight at least
\((A\sqrt m+2)^{-1}\).  Hence the second term gives

\[
 \left|V_{a,A}\setminus\bigcup_IQ(I)\right|=o_A(B_m).
 \tag{1.5}
\]

Thus (1.3) really is an approximate edge tiling, not merely a large
collection of starts.

## 2. Internal charges are orthogonal to the trace defect

Let \(\Gamma(I)\) be any finite collection of certificates attached to
an interval \(I\), and suppose each certificate \(c\in\Gamma(I)\) is
charged to a quotient edge

\[
 \chi(I,c)\in Q(I).
 \tag{2.1}
\]

Assume that \(\chi\) is injective on all pairs \((I,c)\) in the packing.
This includes the audited all-corner charge and the special common-edge
charge.

### Theorem 2.1 (charge--slack orthogonality)

For every pair \((I,c)\), the charged edge contributes exactly zero to
the uncovered-edge term in (1.2).  Consequently the two universally valid
inequalities are only

\[
 \mathcal T_{a,A}-|\mathcal P|
 \ge\sum_{I\in\mathcal P}
       \frac{s(I)-h(I)}{h(I)+2},
 \tag{2.2}
\]

and, for every charged-root class \(\mathcal R\),

\[
 \sum_{I\in\mathcal P}|\Gamma(I)|\le|\mathcal R|.
 \tag{2.3}
\]

There is no additional positive term proportional to
\(\sum_I|\Gamma(I)|\) in (1.2).

### Proof

By (2.1), \(\chi(I,c)\) belongs to the union of the selected supports.
Its load is therefore one, so its summand

\[
 \frac{1-\ell_{\chi(I,c)}}{h(\chi(I,c))+2}
\]

in the fractional form of (1.2) is zero.  This proves the first assertion.
Equation (2.2) follows by dropping the uncovered-edge term.  Injectivity
of \(\chi\) gives (2.3). \(\square\)

This is not merely a failure of a particular estimate.  If a collection
of cycle intervals tiles its edge set, then the uncovered term is
identically zero while one may choose one distinct internal edge from each
tile.  Thus internal injectivity is perfectly compatible with equality in
the edge-capacity part of the reciprocal dual.  A corner theorem can
contradict a partial tiling only by proving that its possible charged roots
lie in a globally rare class, or by producing many charges per interval.

## 3. What the all-corner theorem actually gives

For a positive-overlap zero-winding return \(I\), let
\(\mathcal C(I)\) be its antichain of minimal nonoverlap corners.  If
there is no nonoverlap phase pair, set \(\mathcal C(I)=\varnothing\).

The audited corner-charge theorem says that for every fixed
\(0<\rho<1\),

\[
 \sum_{I\in\mathcal P}
 \#\{c\in\mathcal C(I):|c|_1\ge\rho s(I)\}
 \le C_{a,\rho}B_m m^{-1/4}.
 \tag{3.1}
\]

Indeed every such corner charges injectively, in one of two colours, to
an actual support root whose canonical terminal sector has length at least
\(2\rho a\sqrt m-1\).  The exact terminal-sector tail is

\[
 \#\{D:d(D)\ge L\}
 \le C B_m\bigl(L^{-1/2}+m^{-1/4}\bigr),
\]

which gives (3.1).

Corners with \(|c|_1<\rho s(I)\) form an antichain in the triangular
phase lattice.  Their first coordinates are distinct and lie in an
interval of length at most \(\rho s(I)+1\).  Hence

\[
 |\mathcal C(I)|
 \le \rho s(I)+1
 +\#\{c\in\mathcal C(I):|c|_1\ge\rho s(I)\}.
 \tag{3.2}
\]

Summing (3.2), using (3.1), and assuming (0.1), gives

\[
 \limsup_{m\to\infty}
 \frac{\sum_I|\mathcal C(I)|}{\sum_Is(I)}\le\rho.
\]

Here \(\sum_Is(I)\ge acB_m\), while the last two terms after division
are respectively \(O(m^{-1/2})\) and \(O_{a,\rho,c}(m^{-1/4})\).
Letting \(\rho\downarrow0\) proves

\[
 \frac{\sum_I|\mathcal C(I)|}{\sum_Is(I)}\longrightarrow0.
 \tag{3.3}
\]

Because all \(s(I)\) are comparable to \(\sqrt m\), (3.3) and Markov's
inequality give a deterministic \(\varepsilon_m\downarrow0\) for which
(0.2) fails on only \(o(B_m/\sqrt m)\) members.  Persistent-overlap
returns have zero corners and are included, not excluded.

Thus the all-corner theorem supplies asymptotically *fewer*, not more,
internal charges per interval in every possible critical-order packing.
The special common-edge family escapes this limitation only because one
of its internal roots has a globally rare, uniquely parseable collar.

## 4. Exact residual and the next sufficient statement

Combine (1.4)--(1.5) with (0.2).  A near-saturating positive-overlap
zero-winding packing in a fixed Gaussian band, if it exists, may be
reduced after negligible deletion to a family satisfying simultaneously

\[
 \left|V_{a,A}\setminus\bigcup_IQ(I)\right|=o(B_m),
 \tag{4.1}
\]

\[
 \sum_I(s(I)-h(I))=o(B_m),
 \tag{4.2}
\]

and

\[
 |\mathcal C(I)|\le\varepsilon_m s(I),
 \qquad\varepsilon_m\downarrow0.
 \tag{4.3}
\]

For zero winding, \(s(I)=h(I)\) identically, so (4.2) is automatic.
Equations (4.1) and (4.3) are therefore the genuine surviving pair:
almost all eligible edges are covered, but the complete same-return
nonoverlap atlas is generated by only \(o(s)\) irreducible corners.

A sufficient next theorem, stated with the quantifiers needed for
\((ST_A)\), is the following.

> **Sparse-corner cross-interval theorem (open).**  For every fixed
> \(0<a<A\) and every deterministic \(\varepsilon_m\downarrow0\), every
> quotient-edge-disjoint family of genuine positive-overlap zero-winding
> returns with
> \[
> a\sqrt m\le s(I)\le A\sqrt m,
> \qquad |\mathcal C(I)|\le\varepsilon_m s(I)
> \]
> has cardinality \(o_A(B_m/\sqrt m)\).

The statement must include \(\mathcal C(I)=\varnothing\), hence the
persistent-overlap class.  Proving it would exclude precisely the
positive-density partial tilings not reached by the existing corner
charges.  The special common-edge theorem proves it only for its explicit
parseable-collar subfamily.

Positive-winding equality/bounded-slack returns require their parallel
large-lag transported-cell statement and are not covered by the proposed
zero-winding theorem.

## 5. Proved boundary

Proved here:

1. the band-restricted exact slack identity (1.2) and its approximate
   tiling consequences (1.4)--(1.5);
2. the exact orthogonality of every internal common-edge/corner charge to
   the uncovered reciprocal defect, Theorem 2.1;
3. the sparse-corner conclusion (0.2), with all quantifiers derived from
   the audited corner tail;
4. the simultaneous residual (4.1)--(4.3).

Not proved:

1. the sparse-corner cross-interval theorem;
2. exclusion of persistent overlap;
3. the positive-winding large-lag analogue;
4. \((ST_A)\) or coefficient one.

Accordingly, positive-density partial tiling is **not** excluded by the
present common-edge/corner results.  Any assertion to the contrary would
add an internal injective charge to an uncovered-edge defect even though
their supports are disjoint in the exact sense of Theorem 2.1.
