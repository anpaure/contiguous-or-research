Date: 2026-07-24

Status: theorem-level report.  Pure mathematics only; no web search and no finite or
computational search.  The report proves an exact fresh-recomputation transition law,
a quantitative turnover-or-collapse theorem, a persistent heterogeneous-packet
fragmentation theorem, and a canonical MSW realization with a Catalan-sized packing

The following earlier audited inputs are used explicitly:

1. exact middle wreath factors exist;
2. for a transposition, direct ownership components are equivariant and their root

3. the Johnson Laplacian eigenvalue on degree \(j\) is \(j(n-j+1)\);
4. the canonical MSW factor has the shifted size-two components stated in
   `MSW_COMPONENT_HIERARCHY_REDUCTION.md`;
5. the owner-Schreier quotient and orbit-profile floor identities in
   `COMPONENT_NOISE_NEW_EXPLORATION_20260724.md` are valid with their audited scope.

## 0. Verdict

Put

\[
 n=2m+1,\qquad
 \mathcal X=\binom{[n]}m,\qquad
 W=|\mathcal X|,\qquad
 B=W/n=\operatorname{Cat}_m.
\]

The third wave proves both real adaptive fragmentation and a sharp reason why it does
not yet imply heat descent.

### 0.1 Positive theorem: fresh fragmentation really can persist

Suppose \((\tau_i,K_i)\) are ownership components of one exact factor, possibly for
different transpositions, and their owner sets are pairwise disjoint.  Their middle
root packets are then disjoint and \(\tau_i\)-invariant.  Switching any packets in
any order changes nothing on every unprocessed packet.  Therefore every remaining
\(K_i\) is still exactly a component of the freshly recomputed \(\tau_i\)-overlay.
All \(2^L\) heterogeneous packet choices are integral exact factors.

In the canonical MSW factor, form the coloured graph on Dyck rows with edges

\[
 \{P1100R,\ P1010R\},
 \qquad
 P\in\mathcal D_s,\qquad R\in\mathcal D_{m-s-2},\qquad0\le s\le m-2.
\]

It has exactly \(\operatorname{Cat}_{m-1}\) edges and maximum degree at most
\(m-1\).  Hence it contains a matching of at least

\[
 \boxed{
 L_m\ge
 \left\lceil\frac{\operatorname{Cat}_{m-1}}{2m-3}\right\rceil
 =\Theta(B/m)
 }
\]

pairwise disjoint, persistently switchable size-two packets.  Thus a fresh-recomputed
sequence of Catalan dimension exists, and each selected packet has target effect
magnitude at most one at every depth.

This answers the literal positive branch of the assigned problem.  Its limitation is
equally exact: the native MSW transpositions lie on a disconnected coordinate
matching, and the theorem does not show that a high orbit-profile release is carried
by one of these packets.

### 0.2 Universal transition theorem: bridge release becomes seams

Let \(I\) be a union of \(\tau\)-components, with invariant middle support \(U_I\),
and switch it.  Define the involution of the middle layer

\[
 \alpha_I(X)=
 \begin{cases}
 \tau X,&X\in U_I,\\
 X,&X\notin U_I.
 \end{cases}
\]

After pulling the child rows back to the original row labels, the freshly recomputed
\(\rho\)-partition is generated by

\[
 \boxed{
 o_F(Y)\sim o_F(\alpha_I\rho\alpha_IY).
 }
\]

The crossing \(\rho\)-orbits are precisely the short-permutation seams of type
\(\tau\rho\) or \(\rho\tau\).  Their number is

\[
 |U_I\setminus\rho U_I|=\tfrac12|U_I\triangle\rho U_I|.
\]

Modulo the old \(\tau\)-component partition, the fresh \(\rho\)-multigraph is
edge-for-edge identical to the old one.  Thus

\[
 \boxed{
 \mathcal P_\tau\vee\mathcal P_\rho(F^I)
 =\mathcal P_\tau\vee\mathcal P_\rho(F)
 }
\]

under row transport.  Fresh adaptation can change only the fine fragmentation inside
these fixed coarse join blocks.

### 0.3 Quantitative turnover-or-collapse

Let \(\mathcal P_\tau=\{K_i\}\), \(|K_i|=s_i\), and set

\[
 \chi(\mathcal P_\tau)
 =B-\frac1B\sum_i s_i^2.
\]

After arbitrary side choices in the entire \(\tau\)-cube, one transposition
\(\rho\ne\tau\), chosen independently of those side choices, has at least

\[
 \boxed{
 E_\rho\ge
 \frac{2n(n-1)}{n(n-1)-2}\,
 \chi(\mathcal P_\tau)
 }
\]

fresh edges joining different old cells.  If \(Z_\rho\) is the set of owners in
fresh \(\rho\)-components which meet at least two old cells, then

\[
 \boxed{
 |Z_\rho|
 \ge
 \frac{4n(n-1)}{(n-3)(n(n-1)-2)}\,
 \chi(\mathcal P_\tau).
 }
\]

For any threshold \(L\), either one mixed fresh component has more than \(L\)
owners—an immediate large collapse—or there are at least \(\lceil|Z_\rho|/L\rceil\)
mixed fresh components.  This is a genuine adaptive fragmentation-or-collapse
dichotomy.  It does not prevent the same \(O(B/n)\) owners from absorbing every
successive bridge.

### 0.4 Exact collapse mechanisms

There are two strong negative theorems.

First, fix two coordinates \(R=\{a,b\}\) and let
\(G_R=S_{[n]\setminus R}\).  Every wreath row has a middle window avoiding both
fixed coordinates.  Hence the owner-Schreier quotient for \(G_R\) has one block for
every exact factor.  Nevertheless, averaging over pairs \(R\) shows that some such
already-collapsed subgroup removes at least

\[
 1-\frac1{d_2},
 \qquad d_2=\dim E_2=\frac{n(n-3)}2,
\]

of the coherent squared discrepancy.  Its exact subgroup component noise restores
the full floor-corrected release, so its canonical heat gap is zero.

Second, at a global minimizer of the fixed-window energy, every freshly computed
transposition satisfies

\[
 \boxed{M_\tau+R_\tau\ge B_\tau.}
\]

Thus every corrected high-release bridge is immediately blocked by opposite-sign
mixing or by duplicate retention.  Every non-increasing adaptive path from a global
minimizer remains on the minimum plateau and the same obstruction reappears after
each fresh recomputation.  If the minima are high on an asymptotic subsequence, they
are the requested exact high-energy obstruction family; proving that branch would,
however, refute the desired fixed-window theorem and is not done here.

### 0.5 Genuine remaining theorem

The proved positive packets are not known to be high-release packets.  The proved
high-turnover bridge need not be the bridge with high orbit-profile release.  Even
when they coincide, middle-root seams are annihilated when roots are regrouped into
fresh invariant components; lower-rank heat is entirely a signed containment-leakage
correlation.

The precise missing statement is therefore a same-bridge, no-recycling inequality:
one transposition must simultaneously have high profile release, fresh microscopic
fragmentation, favourable signed leakage, and enough new owner mass not carried by
previous rounds.  No such theorem is proved.

## 1. Owner graphs and the all-colour Schreier expansion

For an exact factor \(F\), let

\[
 o_F:\mathcal X\to F
\]

be the middle-root owner map.  For an unordered coordinate transposition \(\tau\),
the loopless owner-orbit multigraph \(\Gamma_\tau(F)\) has one edge for each moved
root orbit \(\{X,\tau X\}\), joining \(o_F(X)\) and \(o_F(\tau X)\).  Parallel
edges are retained; loops are discarded.  Its component partition is
\(\mathcal P_\tau(F)\).

If the transposed coordinates have shorter cyclic distance \(d\in\{1,\ldots,m\}\)
in a row \(C\), the audited degree formula is

\[
 \deg_{\Gamma_\tau}(C)=2\min(d,m-1).
\tag{1.1}
\]

Thus all degrees are positive and even, and

\[
 2\le\deg_{\Gamma_\tau}(C)\le2(m-1)=n-3.
\tag{1.2}
\]

Let \(\Gamma_{\rm all}(F)\) be the edge-coloured multigraph obtained by taking the
union over all coordinate transpositions.

### Theorem 1.1 (regular all-colour owner-Schreier graph)

For every exact factor and every owner row,

\[
 \boxed{
 \deg_{\Gamma_{\rm all}}(C)
 =n(m-1)(m+2).
 }
\tag{1.3}
\]

If \(A\subseteq F\), \(|A|=s\), and

\[
 U_A=\bigsqcup_{C\in A}\mathcal W_m(C),
\]

then

\[
 \boxed{
 |\partial_{\Gamma_{\rm all}}A|
 =|\partial_{J(n,m)}U_A|
 \ge2n(n-1)s\left(1-\frac sB\right).
 }
\tag{1.4}
\]

Consequently \(\Gamma_{\rm all}(F)\) is connected, and its conductance satisfies

\[
 \boxed{
 \phi(\Gamma_{\rm all})
 \ge\frac{n-1}{(m-1)(m+2)}
 =\frac{2m}{(m-1)(m+2)}.
 }
\tag{1.5}
\]

Here conductance uses edge multiplicity and the regular degree in (1.3).

#### Proof

In one cyclic row there are exactly \(n\) unordered coordinate pairs at each shorter
distance \(d=1,\ldots,m\).  Summing (1.1) over all colours gives

\[
 n\left(2\sum_{d=1}^{m-1}d+2(m-1)\right)
 =n(m-1)(m+2),
\]

which proves (1.3).

A transposition-coloured owner edge crosses \(A\) exactly when its root orbit crosses
\(U_A\).  Every Johnson edge of the middle layer is generated by one unique
coordinate transposition, proving the equality in (1.4).

The centered indicator

\[
 g_A=\mathbf1_{U_A}-\frac sB\mathbf1_{\mathcal X}
\]

has zero total and zero point margins.  Indeed, every selected wreath contributes
exactly \(m\) middle windows through each coordinate.  Hence \(g_A\) lies in Johnson
degrees \(j\ge2\), where the least Laplacian eigenvalue is \(2(n-1)\).  Since

\[
 \|g_A\|_2^2=ns(1-s/B),
\]

Johnson Poincare gives (1.4).  For \(s\le B/2\), divide its lower bound
\(n(n-1)s\) by (1.3) times \(s\) to obtain (1.5).  Positive boundary for every
proper cut proves connectivity.  \(\square\)

The theorem is a real expansion theorem, but its expansion is spread over all
transposition colours.  It does not imply that any one colour has small or numerous
fresh components.

### Corollary 1.2 (a noncommuting turnover colour always exists)

Let \(\tau=(a\ b)\), and let \(U=U_A\subsetneq\mathcal X\) be the nonempty
root union of a proper owner subset \(A\subset F\), with \(\tau U=U\).
Some transposition \(\rho\) sharing exactly one coordinate with \(\tau\) satisfies

\[
 |U\setminus\rho U|>0.
\tag{1.6}
\]

The cut size is even and hence at least two.

#### Proof

If every \((a\ c)\), \(c\notin\{a,b\}\), preserved \(U\), then together with
\((a\ b)\) these transpositions would generate \(S_n\).  Since \(S_n\) is
transitive on \(\mathcal X\), \(U\) would be empty or all of \(\mathcal X\), a
contradiction.  Every cut in an even-degree owner graph has even size.  \(\square\)

This guarantees a genuine noncommuting seam, but not the strongest colour-pigeonhole
lower bound of Section 5.

## 2. One-switch fresh recomputation is a piecewise conjugation

Fix a transposition \(\tau\).  Let \(I\subseteq F\) be a union of cells of
\(\mathcal P_\tau(F)\), and put

\[
 U_I=\bigsqcup_{C\in I}\mathcal W_m(C).
\tag{2.1}
\]

Component equivariance gives \(\tau U_I=U_I\).  The component-side child is

\[
 F^I=(F\setminus I)\cup\tau I.
\tag{2.2}
\]

It is exact.  Let

\[
 \varphi_I:F\to F^I,
 \qquad
 \varphi_I(C)=
 \begin{cases}
 \tau C,&C\in I,\\
 C,&C\notin I,
 \end{cases}
\tag{2.3}
\]

be the natural row transport, and define the involution of \(\mathcal X\)

\[
 \alpha_I(X)=
 \begin{cases}
 \tau X,&X\in U_I,\\
 X,&X\notin U_I.
 \end{cases}
\tag{2.4}
\]

### Lemma 2.1 (owner-map gauge formula)

After transporting child rows back with \(\varphi_I^{-1}\),

\[
 \boxed{
 \varphi_I^{-1}o_{F^I}(X)=o_F(\alpha_I X).
 }
\tag{2.5}
\]

#### Proof

If \(X\notin U_I\), its owner row is untouched.  If \(X\in U_I\), then
\(X\in\mathcal W_m(\tau C)\) exactly when
\(\tau X\in\mathcal W_m(C)\), with \(C\in I\).  Pulling \(\tau C\) back to
\(C\) gives (2.5).  \(\square\)

### Theorem 2.2 (fresh piecewise-conjugation theorem)

For any next transposition \(\rho\), the freshly recomputed component partition
\(\mathcal P_\rho(F^I)\), pulled back to the row set \(F\), is generated by

\[
 \boxed{
 o_F(Y)\sim o_F(\alpha_I\rho\alpha_IY),
 \qquad Y\in\mathcal X.
 }
\tag{2.6}
\]

For a \(\rho\)-orbit \(\{X,\rho X\}\), the owner relation has the four forms

\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
(X,\rho X)\text{ relative to }U_I&\text{relation in old row labels}\\ \hline
(0,0)&o_F(X)\sim o_F(\rho X),\\
(1,1)&o_F(\tau X)\sim o_F(\tau\rho X),\\
(1,0)&o_F(\tau X)\sim o_F(\rho X),\\
(0,1)&o_F(X)\sim o_F(\tau\rho X).
\end{array}
\tag{2.7}
\]

The last two rows are the short-permutation seams.  Their number is exactly

\[
 \boxed{
 c_\rho(U_I)
 =|U_I\setminus\rho U_I|
 =\frac12|U_I\triangle\rho U_I|.
 }
\tag{2.8}
\]

#### Proof

The fresh \(\rho\)-relation is generated by the two child owners of \(X\) and
\(\rho X\).  By Lemma 2.1 these pull back to
\(o_F(\alpha_I X)\) and \(o_F(\alpha_I\rho X)\).  Substitute
\(Y=\alpha_I X\), using \(\alpha_I^2=1\), to obtain (2.6).  Expanding according
to membership in \(U_I\) gives (2.7).  Exactly one undirected seam arises from each
\(\rho\)-orbit crossing \(U_I\), proving (2.8).  \(\square\)

The involution \(\alpha_I\rho\alpha_I\) acts on the middle layer but is generally
not induced by a coordinate permutation.  This is the exact groupoid feature which
fixed owner-Schreier joins omit.

### Theorem 2.3 (coarse quotient invariance under fresh recomputation)

Let \(\mathcal P_\tau=\mathcal P_\tau(F)\).  Under the row transport (2.3),

\[
 \boxed{
 \Gamma_\rho(F^I)/\mathcal P_\tau
 \cong
 \Gamma_\rho(F)/\mathcal P_\tau
 }
\tag{2.9}
\]

edge-for-edge, including multiplicity.  Consequently,

\[
 \boxed{
 \mathcal P_\tau\vee\mathcal P_\rho(F^I)
 =\mathcal P_\tau\vee\mathcal P_\rho(F).
 }
\tag{2.10}
\]

#### Proof

For every root \(Z\), the owners of \(Z\) and \(\alpha_I Z\) lie in the same
\(\tau\)-component: outside \(U_I\) they are equal, and inside they are the owners
of \(Z\) and \(\tau Z\).  Therefore the two endpoints of the fresh edge in (2.6),
after taking \(\mathcal P_\tau\)-colours, equal the endpoint colours of the old
\(\rho\)-edge based at \(\alpha_IY\).  Since \(\alpha_I\) permutes
\(\mathcal X\), this is an edge-by-edge bijection.  Connected components of the
quotient graph are exactly the blocks of the displayed joins.  \(\square\)

### Proposition 2.4 (cut-collapse-cycle ledger)

For any owner partition \(\mathcal P\), contract its cells in \(\Gamma_\rho\),
discard loops, and let

\[
 E_\rho(\mathcal P)=\text{remaining edge multiplicity},
\]

\[
 r_\rho(\mathcal P)
 =|\mathcal P|-|\mathcal P\vee\mathcal P_\rho|,
\]

and let \(\gamma_\rho(\mathcal P)\) be the cycle rank of the quotient multigraph.
Then

\[
 \boxed{
 E_\rho(\mathcal P)=r_\rho(\mathcal P)+\gamma_\rho(\mathcal P).
 }
\tag{2.11}
\]

If every cell of \(\mathcal P\) has at most \(L\) owners, then

\[
 \boxed{
 r_\rho(\mathcal P)
 \ge
 \left\lceil\frac{E_\rho(\mathcal P)}{(n-3)L}\right\rceil.
 }
\tag{2.12}
\]

#### Proof

For a multigraph with \(v\) vertices, \(c\) connected components, and \(E\) edges,
the graphic rank is \(v-c\) and the cycle rank is \(E-v+c\), proving (2.11).

The weighted degree of a contracted cell is at most \((n-3)L\) by (1.2).  If the
quotient has graphic rank \(r\), the total number of vertices in its nontrivial
components is at most \(2r\).  Hence

\[
 E\le\frac{(n-3)L\cdot2r}{2}=(n-3)Lr.
\]

Rearrange integrally.  \(\square\)

Large owner release is therefore spent on actual coarse coalescence or on redundant
quotient cycles.  Neither term lower-bounds fine fresh fragmentation.

## 3. Adaptive rigidity inside a fixed subgroup

Let \(G\le S_n\) be generated by coordinate transpositions.  Let
\(\mathcal K_G(F)\) be the owner-Schreier partition: its cells are the row vertices
in the connected components of the incidence graph between rows and \(G\)-orbits on
the middle layer.

Consider any adaptive trajectory

\[
 F=F_0,F_1,\ldots,F_T
\]

in which, at every stage, one chooses a transposition \(\tau_t\in G\), freshly
computes its components in \(F_t\), and switches an arbitrary union of those
components.

### Theorem 3.1 (adaptive owner-Schreier coarse rigidity)

There is a row tracking under which every original row \(C\in F\) appears in
\(F_t\) as

\[
 g_{t,C}C,\qquad g_{t,C}\in G.
\tag{3.1}
\]

Under this tracking:

1. every lower-rank \(G\)-orbit total is invariant;
2. the row--\(G\)-orbit incidence graph and the partition \(\mathcal K_G\) are
   invariant;
3. for every candidate transposition \(\rho\),

   \[
   \boxed{
   \Gamma_\rho(F_t)/\mathcal K_G
   \cong
   \Gamma_\rho(F)/\mathcal K_G
   }
   \tag{3.2}
   \]

   edge-for-edge;
4. the enlarged owner partition

   \[
   \mathcal K_{\langle G,\rho\rangle}(F_t)
   \]

   has the same transported block structure, quotient cut multiplicities, collapse
   rank, and quotient cycle rank as at time zero;
5. every orbit-profile floor \(\Omega_{q,G}\), \(\mathfrak B_{q,G}\), and bridge
   release

   \[
   \Delta^Q_q(G,\rho)
   =\mathfrak B_{q,G}-\mathfrak B_{q,\langle G,\rho\rangle}
   \tag{3.3}
   \]

   is invariant under the preceding adaptive \(G\)-trajectory.

#### Proof

Induct on the switches.  Switching a current row \(gC\) by
\(\tau_t\in G\) replaces it with \(\tau_tgC\), so (3.1) persists.

A coordinate permutation in \(G\) preserves every \(G\)-orbit.  Therefore the
number of rank-\(r\) intervals of \(g_{t,C}C\) in a fixed \(G\)-orbit equals the
corresponding number for \(C\).  Summing over rows proves (1), and the same statement
at the middle rank proves that the row--orbit incidence graph is unchanged.

Equivalently, define a piecewise middle-layer permutation by

\[
 \alpha_t(X)=g_{t,C}^{-1}X
 \quad\text{when }X\in\mathcal W_m(g_{t,C}C).
\tag{3.4}
\]

The current row supports partition \(\mathcal X\), so \(\alpha_t\) is a
permutation; it preserves every \(G\)-orbit.  The coarse root unions owned by the
cells of \(\mathcal K_G\) are consequently unchanged.  A \(\rho\)-edge between
two such coarse cells depends only on which fixed root unions contain \(X\) and
\(\rho X\), proving (3.2).  Its quotient components give (4).  Finally
\(\langle G,\rho\rangle\)-orbit totals are sums of the invariant \(G\)-orbit totals,
so all profile floors and their differences are invariant.  \(\square\)

Adaptive side choices can alter only the fine fresh \(\rho\)-components inside the
fixed enlarged owner blocks and the signed leakage carried by those components.
They cannot improve or damage the coarse bridge geometry before \(\rho\) is added.

### Corollary 3.2 (bridge-colour expansion between coordinate components)

Assume the coordinate support of \(G\) is disconnected.  Let its coordinate
components have vertex sets \(V_1,\ldots,V_c\), \(c\ge2\), and put

\[
 M_G=\sum_{i<j}|V_i||V_j|.
\tag{3.5}
\]

For \(\mathcal P=\mathcal K_G=\{K\}\), some transposition \(\rho\) joining two
different coordinate components satisfies

\[
 \boxed{
 E_\rho(\mathcal K_G)
 \ge
 \frac{n(n-1)}{M_G}
 \left(B-\frac1B\sum_{K\in\mathcal K_G}|K|^2\right).
 }
\tag{3.6}
\]

For a union \(A\) of \(\mathcal K_G\)-cells, \(|A|=a\), some such bridge has

\[
 \boxed{
 \partial_\rho A
 \ge
 \frac{2n(n-1)}{M_G}a\left(1-\frac aB\right).
 }
\tag{3.7}
\]

These bounds remain unchanged after every preceding adaptive \(G\)-trajectory.

#### Proof

Every transposition internal to a coordinate component belongs to \(G\), so every
\(\mathcal K_G\)-root union is invariant under it and its quotient cut is zero.
Apply Theorem 1.1 or its partition-summed form and average the entire cut over the
\(M_G\) cross-component transpositions.  Adaptive invariance is Theorem 3.1.  \(\square\)

### Theorem 3.3 (adaptive coordinate-forest turnover sequence)

Start with any coordinate forest, let \(G_t\) be the product of the symmetric groups
on its coordinate components, and allow an arbitrary adaptive sequence of component
switches using transpositions in \(G_t\).  If the coordinate forest is disconnected,
there is a bridge transposition \(\rho_t\) joining two coordinate components for
which (3.6) holds at every state reached by those internal switches.

After freshly computing \(\Gamma_{\rho_t}\), let \(Z_t\) be the owners in
components meeting at least two \(\mathcal K_{G_t}\)-cells.  Then

\[
 \boxed{
 |Z_t|
 \ge
 \left\lceil
 \frac{2n(n-1)}{(n-3)M_{G_t}}
 \left(B-\frac1B
 \sum_{K\in\mathcal K_{G_t}}|K|^2\right)
 \right\rceil.
 }
\tag{3.8}
\]

For every \(L\ge1\), either a mixed fresh component has size larger than \(L\), or
there are at least \(\lceil|Z_t|/L\rceil\) mixed fresh components.

Adding \(\rho_t\) to the forest merges two coordinate components.  Repetition reaches
a connected coordinate tree in at most \(n-1\) rounds.  At every round the theorem
is uniform over all prior internal side choices.

#### Proof

Use Corollary 3.2.  Every crossing quotient edge has both endpoints in \(Z_t\), and
the fresh owner degree is at most \(n-3\), so \(2E_{\rho_t}\le(n-3)|Z_t|\).
The threshold dichotomy is the same covering argument as in Theorem 4.3 below.
Every added coordinate edge reduces the number of forest components by one.
Adaptive uniformity is Theorem 3.1.  \(\square\)

This is an unconditional adaptive sequence theorem at the owner-quotient level.  It
does not say that the chosen coordinate bridge has high lower-rank profile release or
positive heat.

## 4. Robust fresh turnover and the fragmentation-or-collapse dichotomy

Fix \(\tau\) and write

\[
 \mathcal P_\tau(F)=\{K_1,\ldots,K_k\},
 \qquad s_i=|K_i|.
\tag{4.1}
\]

Let

\[
 U_i=\bigsqcup_{C\in K_i}\mathcal W_m(C).
\tag{4.2}
\]

Every \(U_i\) is \(\tau\)-invariant.  Choose an arbitrary side vector
\(\varepsilon\in\{0,1\}^k\) and form

\[
 F^\varepsilon=\bigcup_i\tau^{\varepsilon_i}K_i.
\tag{4.3}
\]

Label \(\tau^{\varepsilon_i}C\) by \(C\).  The rows carrying labels in \(K_i\)
still partition exactly \(U_i\).

For a second transposition \(\rho\), let \(E_\rho^\varepsilon\) be the number of
fresh \(\Gamma_\rho(F^\varepsilon)\)-edges whose endpoints carry labels in
different old cells \(K_i\).

### Lemma 4.1 (orientation-independent old-cell turnover)

\[
 \boxed{
 E_\rho^\varepsilon
 =E_\rho^0
 =\frac12\sum_{i=1}^k|U_i\setminus\rho U_i|.
 }
\tag{4.4}
\]

Thus the entire quotient multigraph on the old cells, not only its total edge count,
is independent of \(\varepsilon\).

#### Proof

A fresh \(\rho\)-orbit edge joins old labels \(K_i,K_j\) precisely when one root
lies in \(U_i\) and the other in \(U_j\).  The root cells \(U_i\) do not depend on
the chosen row partitions in (4.3).  Summing the boundary of every \(U_i\) counts
each intercell root orbit twice.  The edgewise statement is Theorem 2.3.  \(\square\)

Put

\[
 \chi(\mathcal P_\tau)
 =B-\frac1B\sum_i s_i^2.
\tag{4.5}
\]

### Theorem 4.2 (uniform high-turnover colour after every side choice)

There exists a transposition \(\rho\ne\tau\), depending only on the old root
partition \(\{U_i\}\) and not on \(\varepsilon\), such that

\[
 \boxed{
 E_\rho^\varepsilon
 \ge
 \frac{2n(n-1)}{n(n-1)-2}\,
 \chi(\mathcal P_\tau)
 }
\tag{4.6}
\]

for every side vector \(\varepsilon\).

If all old components have size at most \(L_0\), then

\[
 \chi(\mathcal P_\tau)\ge B-L_0.
\tag{4.7}
\]

#### Proof

Apply (1.4) to every \(U_i\) and use (4.4):

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \sum_\rho E_\rho^\varepsilon
 &=\frac12\sum_i\sum_\rho|U_i\setminus\rho U_i|\\
 &\ge n(n-1)\sum_i s_i\left(1-\frac{s_i}{B}\right)\\
 &=n(n-1)\chi(\mathcal P_\tau).
 \end{aligned}
\tag{4.8}
\]

The \(\rho=\tau\) term is zero.  Divide by
\(\binom n2-1=(n(n-1)-2)/2\) and choose a maximizing remaining colour.
If \(s_i\le L_0\), then
\(\sum_i s_i^2\le L_0\sum_i s_i=L_0B\), proving (4.7).  \(\square\)

Let \(Z_\rho^\varepsilon\) be the union of owner vertices in fresh
\(\rho\)-components meeting at least two old cells.

### Theorem 4.3 (fresh fragmentation-or-collapse)

For the \(\rho\) supplied by Theorem 4.2,

\[
 \boxed{
 |Z_\rho^\varepsilon|
 \ge
 \left\lceil
 \frac{4n(n-1)}{(n-3)(n(n-1)-2)}
 \chi(\mathcal P_\tau)
 \right\rceil
 }
\tag{4.9}
\]

for every \(\varepsilon\).

For every integer threshold \(L\ge1\), at least one of the following holds:

1. a mixed fresh \(\rho\)-component has more than \(L\) owners;
2. the fresh overlay has at least

   \[
   \boxed{
   \left\lceil\frac{|Z_\rho^\varepsilon|}{L}\right\rceil
   }
   \tag{4.10}
   \]

   mixed components.

#### Proof

Every one of the \(E_\rho^\varepsilon\) crossing edges has both endpoints in
\(Z_\rho^\varepsilon\).  The loopless owner degree is at most \(n-3\), so

\[
 2E_\rho^\varepsilon\le(n-3)|Z_\rho^\varepsilon|.
\]

Insert (4.6) and take the integer ceiling.  If no mixed component is larger than
\(L\), at least \(\lceil|Z|/L\rceil\) such components are needed to cover \(Z\).
\(\square\)

The contracted transition multigraph on the old cells has degree
\(|U_i\setminus\rho U_i|\) at cell \(i\).  Every such degree is even, so this
quotient is Eulerian.  In particular, the coordinate word “bridge” is never a
graph-theoretic bridge: turnover arrives in parallel pairs or cycles.

By Proposition 2.4,

\[
 E_\rho=r_\rho+\gamma_\rho.
\tag{4.11}
\]

Thus Theorem 4.2 also gives the exact alternative

\[
 r_\rho\ge\frac{E_\rho}{2}
 \quad\text{or}\quad
 \gamma_\rho\ge\frac{E_\rho}{2}.
\tag{4.12}
\]

The first branch is genuine destruction of the old fragmentation in the layered
join; the second is redundant cyclic mixing.  Neither determines the sizes of the
newly recomputed components.

### Corollary 4.4 (adaptive turnover process and its exact gap)

Starting from any proper component switch, one may choose a next transposition by
Theorem 4.2.  If its fresh overlay is connected, the step has immediately collapsed.
If it is disconnected, a new proper component side is available and the procedure
can be continued after fresh recomputation.

At every round the quantitative bound (4.9) holds.  What does not telescope is the
identity of the carried owners.  The only unconditional multistep consequence is

\[
 \sum_t|Z_t|
 \ge
 \frac{4n(n-1)}{(n-3)(n(n-1)-2)}
 \sum_t\chi(\mathcal P_t),
\tag{4.13}
\]

with ceilings omitted.  The same \(O(B/n)\) owner core may occur in every \(Z_t\).
No exact-factor no-recycling bound is known.

This is the sharp scope of the universal turnover theorem.

## 5. Persistent disjoint packets: a genuine adaptive fragmentation theorem

The turnover theorem is anti-persistent.  A different exact mechanism gives genuine
persistence.

### Definition 5.1 (component packet)

A transposition component packet in an exact factor \(F\) is a pair
\((\tau,K)\), where \(K\) is one cell of \(\mathcal P_\tau(F)\).  Its root packet is

\[
 U_K=\bigsqcup_{C\in K}\mathcal W_m(C).
\]

It satisfies \(\tau U_K=U_K\), and both \(K\) and \(\tau K\) partition \(U_K\).

### Theorem 5.2 (heterogeneous packet cube and fresh persistence)

Let

\[
 (\tau_1,K_1),\ldots,(\tau_L,K_L)
\]

be component packets of the same exact factor, with pairwise disjoint owner sets
\(K_i\).  Then:

1. the root packets \(U_i=U_{K_i}\) are pairwise disjoint;
2. for every \(\varepsilon\in\{0,1\}^L\),

   \[
   \boxed{
   F_\varepsilon
   =\left(F\setminus\bigcup_iK_i\right)
    \cup\bigcup_i\tau_i^{\varepsilon_i}K_i
   }
   \tag{5.1}
   \]

   is an integral exact factor;
3. after toggling any subset of the packets in any order, every untoggled packet
   \(K_j\) remains exactly a component of the freshly recomputed
   \(\tau_j\)-overlay;
4. orientations and the order of the remaining toggles may therefore be selected
   adaptively without endangering their legality.

#### Proof

Distinct rows in an exact factor own disjoint middle-root blocks, so disjoint owner
sets give disjoint root packets.  On \(U_i\), either \(K_i\) or \(\tau_iK_i\)
is a row partition; outside their union the original rows remain.  This proves the
exactness of every (5.1).

Now toggle packets other than \(j\).  The factor restricted to \(U_j\) remains the
original row partition \(K_j\).  Since \(U_j\) is \(\tau_j\)-invariant, no
\(\tau_j\)-root orbit enters or leaves \(U_j\).  Hence no fresh owner edge joins
\(K_j\) to an outside row, while the internal owner graph is unchanged and connected.
Thus \(K_j\) remains exactly one fresh component.  \(\square\)

The theorem is stronger than a legal path: it gives a heterogeneous exact cube whose
coordinates may use different transpositions.  Its scope is disjoint-packet toggles.
An arbitrary switch whose root support meets an unprocessed packet can destroy that
packet.

### 5.1 Canonical MSW packet graph

Let \(F_m^{\rm MSW}\) be the canonical MSW factor, with rows indexed by Dyck words
\(\mathcal D_m\).  For

\[
 0\le s\le m-2,\qquad
 P\in\mathcal D_s,\qquad
 R\in\mathcal D_{m-s-2},
\]

the audited shifted-component theorem says that

\[
 P1100R,\qquad P1010R
\tag{5.2}
\]

form a size-two component for

\[
 \tau_s=(2s+2\ \,2s+3).
\tag{5.3}
\]

Define the simple coloured graph \(\mathfrak M_m\) on \(\mathcal D_m\) by taking
all edges (5.2), coloured by \(s\).

### Theorem 5.3 (Catalan matching of persistent size-two packets)

\[
 \boxed{
 |E(\mathfrak M_m)|=\operatorname{Cat}_{m-1},
 \qquad
 \Delta(\mathfrak M_m)\le m-1.
 }
\tag{5.4}
\]

Consequently \(\mathfrak M_m\) contains a matching of size

\[
 \boxed{
 \nu(\mathfrak M_m)
 \ge
 \left\lceil
 \frac{\operatorname{Cat}_{m-1}}{2m-3}
 \right\rceil.
 }
\tag{5.5}
\]

The matched edges are pairwise disjoint size-two packets satisfying Theorem 5.2.

#### Proof

The two endpoints of an edge with colour \(s\) differ exactly at bit positions
\(2s+2,2s+3\).  Hence the unordered endpoint pair determines \(s\), and then its
prefix \(P\) and suffix \(R\).  Thus the presentations are distinct and the graph is
simple.  Catalan convolution gives

\[
 |E|=\sum_{s=0}^{m-2}\operatorname{Cat}_s
       \operatorname{Cat}_{m-s-2}
 =\operatorname{Cat}_{m-1}.
\]

For a fixed word and fixed \(s\), there is at most one incident edge, so
\(\Delta\le m-1\).  A greedy matching choice deletes at most
\(2\Delta-1\le2m-3\) still-available edges.  It therefore selects at least the
ceiling in (5.5).  Theorem 5.2 applies to the resulting row-disjoint packets.
\(\square\)

Since

\[
 \frac{\operatorname{Cat}_{m-1}}{\operatorname{Cat}_m}\longrightarrow\frac14,
\]

the cube dimension is \(\Theta(B/m)\).  For \(m\ge8\), (5.5) is at least
\(2m=n-1\).  Indeed, at \(m=8\),

\[
 \operatorname{Cat}_7=429\ge 2m(2m-3)=208,
\]

and the Catalan ratio

\[
 \frac{\operatorname{Cat}_m}{\operatorname{Cat}_{m-1}}
 =\frac{2(2m-1)}{m+1}
\]

dominates the ratio of the succeeding quadratic bounds for every \(m\ge8\).
Indeed, for \(P_m=2m(2m-3)\),

\[
 \frac{P_{m+1}}{P_m}
 =\frac{(m+1)(2m-1)}{m(2m-3)},
\]

and

\[
 \frac{2(2m-1)}{m+1}
 \ge\frac{(m+1)(2m-1)}{m(2m-3)}
\]

is equivalent to \(3m^2-8m-1\ge0\).  Induction from \(m=8\) proves the claim.

### Corollary 5.4 (many distinct native transpositions)

For every \(m\ge4\), the MSW factor contains at least \(m-3\) pairwise disjoint
size-two packets belonging to distinct transpositions.

#### Proof

For \(s=2,\ldots,m-2\), choose

\[
 P_s=1^s0^s,\qquad R_s=(10)^{m-s-2}
\]

in (5.2).  Both rows in the resulting pair have initial run of ones exactly \(s\),
so pairs with different \(s\) are row-disjoint.  Their transpositions \(\tau_s\)
are distinct.  \(\square\)

Every one of these fresh size-two components satisfies, at every depth and target,

\[
 |d_K(S)|\le1
\tag{5.6}
\]

by the two-sided containment-leakage theorem.  Thus its within-component duplicate
residue \(\binom{|d_K(S)|}{2}\) vanishes exactly.

### 5.2 Relabelling scope

A global coordinate relabelling \(\sigma\) transports a surviving packet to

\[
 (\sigma\tau_i\sigma^{-1},\ \sigma K_i),
\]

and preserves disjointness and persistence.  Any desired current transposition label
can therefore be assigned to the next packet by a global rebasing.  Such a rebasing is
itself legal: decompose \(\sigma\) into transpositions and, for each letter, choose
the complete translated side of every current component.

This observation has a critical limitation.  Later global rebasings also move the
labels of earlier packets.  In a fixed pulled-back frame,

\[
 \sigma^{-1}(\sigma\tau_i\sigma^{-1})\sigma=\tau_i.
\]

Hence rebasing does not turn the native disjoint MSW colours into the edges of a
single owner-Schreier spanning tree.  It only permits arbitrary successive-frame
labels.  The whole-side preparation letters are additional layers and may themselves
coalesce the layered join.

Theorem 5.3 therefore proves genuine fresh fragmentation, but not connected-support
profile release.

## 6. Two-fixed-point collapse with near-total coherent release

The positive packet theorem coexists with a universal fixed-aggregate collapse much
stronger than full-coordinate connectivity.

Fix a pair

\[
 R=\{a,b\},\qquad V=[n]\setminus R,
 \qquad G_R=S_V,
\tag{6.1}
\]

where \(a,b\) are fixed pointwise.

### Theorem 6.1 (two-fixed-point owner-Schreier collapse)

For every exact factor,

\[
 \boxed{\mathcal K_{G_R}(F)=\{F\}.}
\tag{6.2}
\]

Thus any spanning tree on the \(n-2\) moving coordinates, using only \(n-3\)
transpositions, already has a one-block joined owner quotient.

#### Proof

Removing \(a,b\) from a cyclic order leaves two open arcs containing a total of
\(n-2=2m-1\) coordinates.  One arc contains at least \(m\) coordinates, and hence
contains a cyclic \(m\)-window avoiding both \(a\) and \(b\).  Therefore every row
owns a root in the single \(G_R\)-orbit

\[
 \binom Vm.
\]

The corresponding orbit vertex in the row--orbit incidence graph is adjacent to
every row, so the graph and its owner quotient are connected.  \(\square\)

Fix a controlled lower rank \(2\le r\le n-2\).  Let \(P_R\) be uniform averaging
over \(G_R\), and let \(E_j\) denote Johnson harmonic degree \(j\).

### Theorem 6.2 (rank-one \(E_2\) residue and tight frame)

The \(G_R\)-fixed subspace has dimensions

\[
 1\text{ in }E_0,\qquad2\text{ in }E_1,
 \qquad1\text{ in }E_2,\qquad0\text{ in }E_j\ (j\ge3).
\tag{6.3}
\]

Consequently, on the zero-total, zero-point-margin space, \(P_R\) is a rank-one
projection onto an \(E_2\) line.  As \(R\) ranges over coordinate pairs, these lines
form a tight frame.  With

\[
 d_2=\dim E_2=\binom n2-n=\frac{n(n-3)}2,
\tag{6.4}
\]

one has

\[
 \boxed{
 \frac1{\binom n2}\sum_R\|P_Rf\|_2^2
 =\frac1{d_2}\|f^{(2)}\|_2^2
 }
\tag{6.5}
\]

for every zero-margin vector \(f\).

#### Proof

A \(G_R\)-invariant function is constant on the four target classes determined by
membership in \(a,b\).  The slice degree filtration of this four-dimensional space
has dimensions \(1,2,1\) in degrees \(0,1,2\), and no higher part.  Removing total
and point margins leaves the one-dimensional degree-two line.

The family of rank-one projections is one \(S_n\)-orbit.  Its average commutes with
the irreducible \(S_n\)-action on \(E_2\), so it is scalar there.  Its trace is one,
and therefore the scalar is \(1/d_2\).  It annihilates all higher degrees.  This
proves (6.5).  \(\square\)

At ranks outside \(2\le r\le n-2\), degree two is absent and the corresponding
zero-margin invariant projection is zero.

For positive window weights \(1/c_q\), some pair \(R\) therefore satisfies

\[
 \boxed{
 \sum_{q\le H}\frac{\|P_Rf_q\|_2^2}{c_q}
 \le\frac1{d_2}
 \sum_{q\le H}\frac{\|f_q\|_2^2}{c_q}.
 }
\tag{6.6}
\]

Let \(\Theta_{H,G_R}\) be the exact orbitwise integer-restoration floor.  The
floor-corrected subgroup release is

\[
 \mathcal R_{H,G_R}
 =\sum_q\frac{\|f_q\|_2^2-\|P_Rf_q\|_2^2}{c_q}
 -\Theta_{H,G_R},
\tag{6.7}
\]

with

\[
 0\le\Theta_{H,G_R}\le\frac14\sum_q\frac{N_q}{c_q}.
\tag{6.8}
\]

Because Theorem 6.1 leaves one owner block, every canonical subgroup child is one
whole relabelling \(gF\).  Its energy equals that of \(F\).  The exact subgroup heat
identity therefore forces

\[
 \boxed{
 \mathcal C_{H,G_R}(F)=\mathcal R_{H,G_R}(F),
 }
\tag{6.9}
\]

so the fair heat gap is zero, however large the release.

### Corollary 6.3 (a literal late bridge after collapse)

Choose \(v\in V\) and let \(\rho=(b\ v)\).  Then

\[
 \langle G_R,\rho\rangle=S_{[n]\setminus\{a\}}.
\]

The latter point-stabilizer has zero orbit-profile floor by exact point margins.
Thus this single subgroup bridge releases exactly the residual \(G_R\)-profile floor,
while both cumulative owner quotients are already one block.  If the marginal is
large, it is a high-release bridge occurring after complete cumulative collapse and
its canonical aggregated heat is zero.

This corollary concerns cumulative subgroup partitions.  It does not assert that the
fresh direct \(\rho\)-overlay of the current exact factor is connected.

## 7. Middle-root release collapses to lower-rank leakage

The turnover theorems live on the middle layer.  We now identify exactly what remains
after passing to a lower rank.

Fix a union \(A\) of row blocks whose common middle support is \(U\), and let
\(r=m-q\).  Define the containment shadow

\[
 H_{U,q}(S)=|\{X\in U:S\subseteq X\}|,
 \qquad S\in\binom{[n]}r,
\tag{7.1}
\]

the cyclic occurrence count

\[
 x_{A,q}(S)=|\{C\in A:S\in\mathcal W_r(C)\}|,
\tag{7.2}
\]

and the noncyclic leakage

\[
 e_{A,q}=H_{U,q}-(q+1)x_{A,q}.
\tag{7.3}
\]

The cyclic containment lemma gives \(e_{A,q}\ge0\) pointwise.

For a next transposition \(\rho\), put

\[
 b_{U,\rho,q}=H_{U,q}-\rho H_{U,q}.
\tag{7.4}
\]

### Theorem 7.1 (containment release minus leakage cancellation)

\[
 \boxed{
 (q+1)(x_{A,q}-\rho x_{A,q})
 =b_{U,\rho,q}-(e_{A,q}-\rho e_{A,q}).
 }
\tag{7.5}
\]

At the middle rank \(q=0\), leakage vanishes and

\[
 \boxed{
 \|b_{U,\rho,0}\|_2^2
 =|U\triangle\rho U|
 =2c_\rho(U).
 }
\tag{7.6}
\]

#### Proof

Subtract the \(\rho\)-translate of (7.3) from (7.3).  At \(q=0\),
\(H_{U,0}=x_{A,0}=\mathbf1_U\) and \(e=0\).  \(\square\)

Thus the geometric seam count is an exact middle-rank release.  Below the middle,
the leakage derivative can cancel it completely.

### Theorem 7.2 (multi-depth containment-shadow selection)

Suppose \(U=\tau U\).  For arbitrary nonnegative weights \(w_q\), some
\(\rho\ne\tau\) satisfies

\[
 \boxed{
 \sum_qw_q\|H_{U,q}-\rho H_{U,q}\|_2^2
 \ge
 \frac{2n}{\binom n2-1}
 \sum_qw_q\|H_{U,q}-\overline H_{U,q}\|_2^2,
 }
\tag{7.7}
\]

where \(\overline H_{U,q}\) is the constant mean on the rank-\(r\) slice.

Writing

\[
 \lambda_{U,q}
 =\frac{|U|\binom mq}{\binom n{m-q}},
 \qquad \theta_{U,q}=\{\lambda_{U,q}\},
\tag{7.8}
\]

one has the integral lower bound

\[
 \boxed{
 \|H_{U,q}-\overline H_{U,q}\|_2^2
 \ge
 \binom n{m-q}\theta_{U,q}(1-\theta_{U,q}).
 }
\tag{7.9}
\]

#### Proof

On an arbitrary rank slice, the least nonconstant Johnson Laplacian eigenvalue is
\(n\).  Therefore

\[
 \sum_\rho\|H_{U,q}-\rho H_{U,q}\|_2^2
 \ge2n\|H_{U,q}-\overline H_{U,q}\|_2^2.
\]

The \(\rho=\tau\) term is zero because \(U=\tau U\), and hence every containment
shadow is \(\tau\)-invariant.  Sum in \(q\), divide by the remaining number of
transpositions, and choose one \(\rho\).

The mean in (7.8) follows by counting pairs \((S,X)\) with
\(S\subseteq X\in U\).  An integer vector of mean \(a+\theta\) has squared variance
at least \(N\theta(1-\theta)\), proving (7.9).  \(\square\)

### Corollary 7.3 (conditional occurrence release)

For the \(\rho\) selected in Theorem 7.2, define the weighted norm

\[
 \|v\|_w^2=\sum_qw_q\|v_q\|_2^2,
 \qquad
 \|v\|_{w,*}^2
 =\sum_q\frac{w_q}{(q+1)^2}\|v_q\|_2^2.
\tag{7.10}
\]

If, for some \(0\le\kappa<1\),

\[
 \|e_A-\rho e_A\|_{w,*}
 \le\kappa\|b_{U,\rho}\|_{w,*},
\tag{7.11}
\]

then

\[
 \boxed{
 \|x_A-\rho x_A\|_w^2
 \ge(1-\kappa)^2\|b_{U,\rho}\|_{w,*}^2.
 }
\tag{7.12}
\]

#### Proof

Apply the reverse triangle inequality to (7.5) in the weighted direct-sum Hilbert
space.  \(\square\)

Condition (7.11) is not proved.  It is one exact formulation of the missing
seam-to-lower-rank transfer theorem.

### Theorem 7.4 (fresh regrouping annihilates raw bridge release)

Let \(J\) be a component of a freshly computed \(\rho\)-overlay.  Its root union
\(V_J\) is \(\rho\)-invariant.  If \(x_{J,q}\) and \(e_{J,q}\) are its occurrence
and leakage vectors, then

\[
 \boxed{
 (q+1)(x_{J,q}-\rho x_{J,q})
 =-(e_{J,q}-\rho e_{J,q}),
 }
\tag{7.13}
\]

and therefore

\[
 \boxed{
 \|x_{J,q}-\rho x_{J,q}\|_2^2
 =\frac1{(q+1)^2}
 \|e_{J,q}-\rho e_{J,q}\|_2^2.
 }
\tag{7.14}
\]

#### Proof

Apply (7.5) with \(U=V_J\).  Its containment shadow is \(\rho\)-invariant, so
the \(b\)-term is zero.  \(\square\)

This is a universal algebraic immediate collapse of the raw middle bridge signal.
It holds whether the fresh overlay is connected or fragmented.  All lower-rank
component displacement is leakage.

### Corollary 7.5 (what an actual fresh size cap would buy)

If every fresh \(\rho\)-component has at most \(L\) owners, then at depth \(q\)

\[
 \boxed{
 \sum_J\|x_{J,q}-\rho x_{J,q}\|_2^2
 \le2W\left\lfloor\frac{qL}{q+1}\right\rfloor.
 }
\tag{7.15}
\]

In particular, size-two fragmentation gives an upper bound \(2W\) per depth and
transposition.

#### Proof

The two-sided leakage bound gives

\[
 \|x_{J,q}-\rho x_{J,q}\|_\infty
 \le\left\lfloor\frac{q|J|}{q+1}\right\rfloor.
\]

Also its \(\ell_1\)-norm is at most \(2n|J|\), since each of the two side
histograms has mass \(n|J|\).  Use \(\|v\|_2^2\le\|v\|_\infty\|v\|_1\), sum
over \(J\), and use \(\sum_J|J|=B\).  \(\square\)

The turnover theorem gives no such \(L\); its other branch is precisely a large
fresh component.

## 8. Exact fresh heat, two-scale fragmentation, and adaptive telescoping

Fix one freshly computed transposition \(\rho\).  For an unordered moved target pair
\(p=\{S,\rho S\}\) at depth \(q\), orient \(S\) once and put

\[
 d_{J,p}=x_{J,q}(S)-x_{J,q}(\rho S),
 \qquad z_p=\sum_Jd_{J,p},
 \qquad\epsilon_p=\mathbf1_{\{z_p\text{ odd}\}}.
\tag{8.1}
\]

### Proposition 8.1 (profile release, restitution, and fair heat)

In the full \(Q\)-normalization,

\[
 \boxed{
 \mathcal R_{\rho,H}
 =\frac12\sum_{q,p}\frac{z_p^2-\epsilon_p}{c_q},
 }
\tag{8.2}
\]

\[
 \boxed{
 \mathcal C_{\rho,H}
 =\frac12\sum_{q,p}
 \frac{\sum_Jd_{J,p}^2-\epsilon_p}{c_q}.
 }
\tag{8.3}
\]

Fair independent component resampling decreases the expected floor energy by exactly

\[
 \boxed{
 \mathcal R_{\rho,H}-\mathcal C_{\rho,H}
 =\frac12\sum_{q,p}
 \frac{z_p^2-\sum_Jd_{J,p}^2}{c_q}.
 }
\tag{8.4}
\]

Writing

\[
 B_p=\binom{|z_p|}{2},
 \quad
 M_p=\frac{\sum_J|d_{J,p}|-|z_p|}{2},
 \quad
 R_p^{\rm in}=\sum_J\binom{|d_{J,p}|}{2},
\tag{8.5}
\]

the targetwise contribution is

\[
 \boxed{
 \frac12\left(z_p^2-\sum_Jd_{J,p}^2\right)
 =B_p-M_p-R_p^{\rm in}.
 }
\tag{8.6}
\]

#### Proof

The optimal integer balancing of a two-target orbit with fixed total has parity
remainder \(\epsilon_p\).  Coherent midpoint balancing gives (8.2), while independent
component signs give (8.3).  Subtraction proves (8.4).  The algebra in (8.6) follows
from

\[
 2M=\sum|d|-|z|,
 \qquad2R^{\rm in}=\sum d^2-\sum|d|,
 \qquad2B=z^2-|z|.
\]

\(\square\)

For a deterministic component cut \(I\), put

\[
 a_{I,p}=\sum_{J\in I}d_{J,p}.
\tag{8.7}
\]

### Proposition 8.2 (exact deterministic cut and adaptive telescope)

Switching precisely the components in \(I\) decreases the full weighted energy by

\[
 \boxed{
 \mathcal Q_H(F)-\mathcal Q_H(F^I)
 =2\sum_{q,p}\frac{a_{I,p}(z_p-a_{I,p})}{c_q}.
 }
\tag{8.8}
\]

Consequently, for an arbitrary deterministic adaptive sequence,

\[
 \boxed{
 \mathcal Q_H(F_0)-\mathcal Q_H(F_T)
 =\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}
 2\sum_{q,p}\frac{a_{t,p}(z_{t,p}-a_{t,p})}{c_q}.
 }
\tag{8.9}
\]

#### Proof

On the pair \(p\), switching \(I\) changes the two loads by \((-a,+a)\).
Thus

\[
 x^2+y^2-(x-a)^2-(y+a)^2=2a(x-y-a)=2a(z-a).
\]

The linear part of the floor polynomial cancels because total rank mass is fixed.
Sum over pairs, depths, and then time.  \(\square\)

Fair positivity in (8.4) is sufficient, not necessary; the best correlated cut is
the signed quadratic maximization in (8.8).

### Theorem 8.3 (two-scale fresh-fragmentation identity)

Let \(\mathcal P_{\rm old}\) be either the old transposition partition in
Theorem 2.3 or the subgroup partition \(\mathcal K_G\) in Theorem 3.1, and put

\[
 \mathcal J
 =\mathcal P_{\rm old}\vee\mathcal P_\rho(F_{\rm fresh}).
\]

Every fresh component \(J\) lies inside one coarse join cell \(C\in\mathcal J\).
Define

\[
 d_{C,p}=\sum_{J\subseteq C}d_{J,p}.
\]

Then

\[
 \boxed{
 \begin{aligned}
 \frac12\left(z_p^2-\sum_Jd_{J,p}^2\right)
 ={}&\frac12\left(z_p^2-\sum_Cd_{C,p}^2\right)\\
 &+\frac12\sum_C
 \left(d_{C,p}^2-\sum_{J\subseteq C}d_{J,p}^2\right).
 \end{aligned}
 }
\tag{8.10}
\]

#### Proof

Add and subtract \(\frac12\sum_Cd_{C,p}^2\).  \(\square\)

The first line is correlation between the orientation-independent coarse join blocks.
The second is the genuinely fresh fragmentation recreated inside them.  If the
coarse join is one block, the first line vanishes; all adaptive heat must come from
fine re-fragmentation.  Owner-Schreier expansion controls neither sign.

### Proposition 8.4 (signed update of the next release)

Let a preceding \(\tau\)-switch have leakage vector \(e_{I,q}\).  Its load increment
is

\[
 b_{I,q}=(\tau-\mathrm{Id})x_{I,q}
 =\frac{(\mathrm{Id}-\tau)e_{I,q}}{q+1}.
\tag{8.11}
\]

For a prospective next \(\rho\)-pair, if its old difference is \(z_p\), its new
difference is

\[
 \boxed{
 z'_p=z_p+h_p,
 \qquad
 h_p=\frac{[(\mathrm{Id}-\rho)(\mathrm{Id}-\tau)e_{I,q}](S)}{q+1}.
 }
\tag{8.12}
\]

The parity-corrected release changes by

\[
 \boxed{
 z_ph_p+\frac{h_p^2}{2}
 -\frac{
 \mathbf1_{\{z_p+h_p\text{ odd}\}}
 -\mathbf1_{\{z_p\text{ odd}\}}
 }2.
 }
\tag{8.13}
\]

#### Proof

Equation (8.11) is (7.13) summed over the switched components, with signs arranged
for the increment.  Apply \(I-\rho\) to obtain (8.12).  Expand
\(((z+h)^2-\epsilon')/2-(z^2-\epsilon)/2\) to obtain (8.13).  \(\square\)

The mixed term \(z_ph_p\) has no sign.  This is the exact algebraic reason that a
large middle-root cut cannot be promoted to a positive next-step heat estimate.

## 9. Global-minimum locking: an exact high-release obstruction

Fix a height \(H\) and let \(F_*\) be a global minimizer of \(\mathcal Q_H\) over
the finite nonempty set of exact factors.

For every transposition \(\tau\), freshly compute its components and define the
weighted unordered-pair quantities

\[
 B_\tau=\sum_{q,p}\frac1{c_q}\binom{|z_p|}{2},
\tag{9.1}
\]

\[
 M_\tau
 =\sum_{q,p}\frac1{2c_q}
 \left(\sum_K|d_{K,p}|-|z_p|\right),
\tag{9.2}
\]

\[
 R_\tau
 =\sum_{q,p,K}\frac1{c_q}\binom{|d_{K,p}|}{2}.
\tag{9.3}
\]

### Theorem 9.1 (every corrected release is freshly locked)

At a global minimizer, for every transposition,

\[
 \boxed{M_\tau+R_\tau\ge B_\tau.}
\tag{9.4}
\]

Consequently, if \(B_\tau\ge L\), then

\[
 \boxed{
 M_\tau\ge L/2
 \quad\text{or}\quad
 R_\tau\ge L/2.
 }
\tag{9.5}
\]

If every fresh component has size at most two, then \(R_\tau=0\) and

\[
 M_\tau\ge B_\tau.
\tag{9.6}
\]

If the residual component effects are also targetwise sign-aligned, then
\(M_\tau=0\), and necessarily \(B_\tau=0\).

#### Proof

The exact fair expected decrease is

\[
 B_\tau-M_\tau-R_\tau
\]

by Proposition 8.1.  Every fair child is exact, while no average of children can
have energy below the global minimum.  This proves (9.4), and (9.5) follows by
pigeonhole.  Size-two leakage gives \(|d_{K,p}|\le1\), so every binomial term in
\(R_\tau\) vanishes.  Sign alignment makes \(M_\tau=0\).  \(\square\)

Define the nonnegative parity-restoration term

\[
 F_\tau
 =\sum_{q,p}\frac1{c_q}
 \left\lfloor\frac{|z_p|}{2}\right\rfloor.
\tag{9.7}
\]

Then the raw ideal orbit release and component restitution are exactly

\[
 G_\tau=B_\tau+F_\tau,
 \qquad
 C_\tau=M_\tau+R_\tau+F_\tau.
\tag{9.8}
\]

### Corollary 9.2 (three-way blocking of raw release)

At \(F_*\), \(C_\tau\ge G_\tau\).  If \(G_\tau\ge L\), then at least one of

\[
 F_\tau,\qquad M_\tau,\qquad R_\tau
\]

is at least \(L/3\).

Thus a raw-high bridge is blocked by integer parity restitution, opposite-target
mixing, or same-target duplicate retention.

### Theorem 9.3 (correlated cuts and adaptive paths are also locked)

For every deterministic union \(I\) of freshly computed \(\tau\)-components,

\[
 \boxed{
 \mathcal Q_H(F_*)-\mathcal Q_H(F_*^I)
 =2\sum_{q,p}\frac{a_{I,p}(z_p-a_{I,p})}{c_q}
 \le0.
 }
\tag{9.9}
\]

Every non-increasing adaptive component-switch trajectory beginning at \(F_*\)
remains on the global-minimum plateau.  At every new state, Theorems 9.1--9.3 apply
again to all freshly recomputed overlays.

#### Proof

Equation (9.9) is Proposition 8.2 and global minimality.  A non-increasing step from
a global minimum must have equal energy; hence every reached state is another global
minimizer.  Induct.  \(\square\)

This gives an exact asymptotic alternative for every fixed window:

1. either global minima already have \(\mathcal Q_H=o(W)\), which is the desired
   quadratic fixed-window conclusion;
2. or on a subsequence where the minima are not \(o(W)\), the minimizers form an
   exact high-energy family in which every corrected high-release transposition is
   immediately noise-locked and no adaptive non-increasing component path improves
   the objective.

The second branch is conditional on failure of the desired theorem.  Constructing it
unconditionally would be a major negative result and is not claimed.

The separate corrected global component-noise near-baseline gate from the second wave
has already been refuted by the audited Boolean-\(E_2\) stability theorem.  Nothing in
this section attempts to revive that gate; the statements here concern exact local
or adaptive component moves.

## 10. Quotient collapse alone cannot decide fresh fragmentation

Even an indiscrete owner-Schreier join is combinatorially compatible with microscopic
constituent partitions.  On the abstract owner set \(\mathbf F_2^k\), let the
\(i\)-th partition consist of pairs

\[
 \{x,x+e_i\}.
\tag{10.1}
\]

Every cell has size two, while the join of all \(k\) partitions is one block.  Since
\(\log_2B\sim n\), the number of coordinate colours is on the correct scale for this
phenomenon.

This is an abstract quotient model, not an exact wreath factor.  It proves that the
owner-Schreier join theorem alone cannot force a large component in any one fresh
overlay.

At the other extreme, for \(m=2\) the exact factor consisting of the step-one and
step-two cycles on \(\mathbb Z_5\) has two owners.  Every coordinate-transposition
overlay is connected because every transposition component has at least two owners.
The permutation \(x\mapsto2x\) nevertheless yields two singleton direct endpoint
components.  Its rank-one loads are uniform, so this is only a geometry audit, not a
high-release counterexample.

## 11. The exact remaining adaptive theorem

There are now four distinct quantities which must not be conflated.

1. **Coarse owner turnover**

   \[
   E_\rho(\mathcal K_G),
   \]

   controlled by Sections 1--4.

2. **Orbit-profile release**

   \[
   \Delta_H^Q(G,\rho)
   =\mathfrak B_{H,G}-\mathfrak B_{H,\langle G,\rho\rangle},
   \]

   controlled by the spanning-tree profile telescope.

3. **Fresh microscopic fragmentation**, encoded by the fine components inside the
   fixed coarse join blocks.

4. **Signed lower-rank heat**, encoded by (8.8) or, fairly, by (8.4).

The high-turnover colour and the high-profile-release colour may be different.  The
MSW persistent packets prove fresh microscopic fragmentation, but not high profile
release.  Middle seams can be large while (7.13) leaves only leakage, and the same
owners may be recycled through every round.

### Open gate 11.1 (same-bridge fresh fragmentation; unproved)

For fixed Gaussian height \(H_A\), one would need constants
\(\eta_A>0,C_A<\infty\) such that, above the Catalan residue, some bridge
\(\rho\) and some cut \(I\) of its freshly recomputed components satisfy

\[
 \boxed{
 2\sum_{q,p}\frac{a_{I,p}(z_p-a_{I,p})}{c_q}
 \ge
 \eta_A\Delta_H^Q(G,\rho)
 -C_A\frac{H_A B}{n}.
 }
\tag{11.1}
\]

The stronger fair version would replace the left side by

\[
 \frac12\sum_{q,p}
 \frac{z_p^2-\sum_Jd_{J,p}^2}{c_q}.
\tag{11.2}
\]

Neither is proved.

### Open gate 11.2 (cumulative novelty; unproved)

Along an adaptive coordinate-forest sequence, the mixed owner sets \(Z_t\) from
Theorem 3.3 or 4.3 must satisfy a revisit bound strong enough that a positive fraction
of

\[
 \sum_t|Z_t|
\]

is genuinely new before the subgroup profile floor vanishes.

No such no-recycling theorem is known.  Without it, the same \(O(B/n)\) carried core
may absorb all coordinate bridges.

Either open gate, together with the audited profile telescope and exact energy
telescope, would give a meaningful adaptive descent theorem.  Neither follows from
unsigned owner expansion, component count, or the owner-Schreier quotient.

## 12. Independent audit and final scope

The decisive results were derived in three independent attacks and then audited a
fourth time.  The following checks fix the conventions and rule out the most tempting
overclaims.

### 12.1 Edge and quotient normalization

Every \(\Gamma_\tau\)-edge is one unordered moved middle-root orbit.  A raw bipartite
quotient would count both orientations and double the edge constants.  With the
unordered convention:

\[
 \deg_{\Gamma_\tau}(C)=2\min(d_C,m-1)\le n-3,
\]

and every cut has even multiplicity.

For the old cells \(U_i\), each crossing root orbit contributes two to
\(\sum_i|U_i\setminus\rho U_i|\), which audits the factor \(1/2\) in (4.4).
The zero-point-margin Johnson gap contributes

\[
 2n(n-1)s_i(1-s_i/B)
\]

to the all-colour boundary of one cell.  Summing cells, halving, deleting the zero
\(\tau\)-term, and dividing by \(\binom n2-1\) gives exactly

\[
 \frac{2n(n-1)}{n(n-1)-2}\chi,
\]

not a generic mean-zero constant and not twice this value.

### 12.2 Fresh recomputation audit

The map \(\alpha_I\) is an involution because \(U_I\) is \(\tau\)-invariant.
It need not be a coordinate permutation.  Mapping a fresh effective orbit by
\(Y\mapsto\alpha_IY\) gives an old \(\rho\)-orbit with the same pair of
\(\mathcal P_\tau\)-colours.  Hence Theorems 2.2--2.3 preserve the contracted
multigraph including multiplicity, but do **not** preserve the uncontracted fresh
components.

For a general subgroup \(G\), the invariant object is the connected-component
partition of the row--\(G\)-orbit incidence graph.  The adaptive piecewise gauge
stays within every \(G\)-orbit, so this exact partition and every candidate bridge
quotient are unchanged.  The gauge \(\alpha\rho\alpha\) must not be identified with
an element of \(G\) or of the coordinate symmetric group.

### 12.3 Packet audit

The persistence theorem requires disjoint root packets and switches confined to those
packets.  An arbitrary overlapping switch may destroy the conclusion.

For the MSW graph, the endpoints differ exactly at positions \(2s+2,2s+3\), so the
colour \(s\), prefix \(P\), and suffix \(R\) are recovered from the edge.  This
proves simplicity.  Catalan convolution gives \(\operatorname{Cat}_{m-1}\) edges;
one edge per split gives degree at most \(m-1\); greedy deletion gives the exact
denominator \(2m-3\).  The imported MSW theorem says these pairs are entire size-two
components, not merely two rows joined inside a larger component.

Global rebasing permits one packet at a time to be displayed on any desired actual
coordinate edge.  It does not turn the intrinsic commuting packet colours into a
fixed-frame spanning tree: later rebasings move earlier labels, pulled-back conjugation
cancels, and the whole-side preparation transpositions must themselves be counted.
No spanning-tree high-release theorem is claimed from the packet construction.

### 12.4 Two-fixed-point audit

Every row meets the common middle orbit avoiding the two fixed coordinates, so one
owner-Schreier block is unconditional.  The harmonic statement is restricted to
\(2\le r\le n-2\).  On that range, the invariant dimensions are exactly
\(1,2,1\) in degrees \(0,1,2\), and Schur averaging of the rank-one degree-two
lines gives the factor

\[
 d_2^{-1}=\frac{2}{n(n-3)}.
\]

This is coherent Hilbert release plus cumulative owner collapse.  It does not assert
connectivity of a fresh direct transposition overlay.

### 12.5 Heat normalization audit

For one unordered moved target pair,

\[
 G_p=\frac{z_p^2-\epsilon_p}{2}
 =B_p+\left\lfloor\frac{|z_p|}{2}\right\rfloor,
\]

\[
 C_p=\frac{\sum_Kd_{K,p}^2-\epsilon_p}{2}
 =M_p+R_p^{\rm in}
  +\left\lfloor\frac{|z_p|}{2}\right\rfloor.
\]

Thus \(G_p-C_p=B_p-M_p-R_p^{\rm in}\).  Also

\[
 A_\tau-V_\tau=4\sum_p(B_p-M_p-R_p^{\rm in}),
\]

and the expected decrease of the full floor energy \(Q\) is the undoubled sum
\(\sum_p(B_p-M_p-R_p^{\rm in})\).  The collision energy \(Q/2\) would have half
this decrease.  Theorems 8--9 consistently use the full \(Q\)-normalization.

Global minimality gives \(M_\tau+R_\tau\ge B_\tau\) only after summing the
controlled depths and target pairs with the printed weights.  It is not a pairwise or
rankwise assertion.

### 12.6 Proved theorems

This report proves:

   fragmentation-or-large-component alternative at every round;

### 12.7 Not proved

The report does not prove:

### 12.8 Exhausted conclusion

The assigned dichotomy is resolved at the structural level.

There is a genuine fresh-recomputed fragmentation theorem: disjoint invariant packets
persist, and the MSW exact factor contains a \(\Theta(B/m)\)-dimensional family of
pairwise disjoint size-two packets.  There is also a universal adaptive
turnover-or-collapse theorem: every fragmented owner quotient admits a next bridge
which either creates many mixed fresh components or a large component, uniformly over
all preceding side choices.

At the same time, the owner-Schreier quotient is rigid under all internal adaptive
switches, a subgroup fixing two coordinates already collapses to one owner block while
removing almost all coherent discrepancy, and global minimizers lock every corrected
release through parity, mixing, or duplicate retention.

The remaining obstruction is no longer “find expansion.”  Expansion has been proved.
It is the simultaneous signed theorem

\[
 \boxed{
 \text{high profile release}
 +\text{ fresh microscopic components}
 +\text{ favourable leakage signs}
 +\text{ nonrecycled owners}.
 }
\]

No present exact-factor argument supplies all four terms for the same adaptive bridge.
