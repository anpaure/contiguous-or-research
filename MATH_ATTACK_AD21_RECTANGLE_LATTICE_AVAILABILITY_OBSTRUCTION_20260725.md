# AD21: exact rectangle lattice and owner-availability obstruction

Date: 2026-07-25

Pure mathematics only.  No computation, search, solver, or web input is
used.

## 0. Outcome

Let \(U\) be a top of size \(M\), and let a packet be the family of the
\(M\) cyclic intervals of each proper length in one cyclic order of \(U\).
The four-order adjacent-swap square gives an elementary rectangle at one
interval length and its complementary length.

This note determines the exact integral algebra of those rectangles and
then audits their nonnegative availability from an owner near-transversal.
The conclusions are sharp.

1. For \(2\le r\le M-2\), the integer lattice generated by all elementary
   rank-\(r\) rectangles on one top is exactly

   \[
    \ker_{\mathbb Z} A_r,                                  \tag{0.1}
   \]

   where \(A_r\) is the point-versus-\(r\)-set incidence matrix.  There is
   no parity index and no further integral conservation law.  The
   nonnegative cone generated by both orientations of the rectangles is
   the full real subspace \(\ker_{\mathbb R}A_r\).

2. Across all tops on \([2m]\), provided \(r+2\le M\), the same local
   rectangles generate the full global lattice

   \[
    \ker_{\mathbb Z} A_r^{[2m]}.                           \tag{0.2}
   \]

   Every generator in (0.2) is supported in one top and preserves the
   packet count of that top.

3. Lattice generation is not nonnegative connectivity.  For every
   \(3\le r\le M-3\), there are two nonnegative simple rank-\(r\) load
   vectors with identical point margins such that one is isolated under
   applicable elementary rectangles.

4. The two packets on either diagonal of one four-order square differ by
   two adjacent swaps.  Their middle owner union has size at most

   \[
    \boxed{M+4},                                           \tag{0.3}
   \]

   so they create at least \(M-4\) owner-collision units.  Consequently,
   an order-disjoint batch of \(t\) applicable local squares inside a
   packet family of owner-collision excess \(E\) obeys

   \[
    \boxed{t(M-4)\le E.}                                   \tag{0.4}
   \]

5. More generally, any connected support-feasible nonzero combination of
   local square cells in one top uses at least

   \[
    \boxed{\lceil M/4\rceil}                               \tag{0.5}
   \]

   cells.  Higher-order cancellation can avoid the direct diagonal
   overlap only at this mesoscopic scale.

6. A sum of nonmiddle rank-selector squares decomposes by top and preserves
   the complete middle-owner vector in every top separately.  If its
   positive and negative packet supports are owner packings and use at most
   one packet per top, then its interval effect is zero.  Thus no nontrivial
   multi-top sum of these fixed-top rectangles is a legal trade on an exact
   top transversal.

7. Allowing owner loads up to two makes a close negative pair coexist, but
   every selector preserves that doubled-owner vector exactly; it cannot
   return to an owner transversal.  If packet variables are restricted to
   \(\{0,2\}\), all rank corrections are even and the correction lattice is
   only \(2\ker_{\mathbb Z}A_r\).  Unit holes give a sharp parity
   obstruction.

Hence the four-order identity is algebraically complete but locally
unavailable at the calibrated near-transversal.  A positive absorber must
use owner-changing, genuinely nonlocal cross-top trades; fixed-top
rank-isolated rectangles cannot supply it.

## 1. The exact four-order identity

Fix a cyclic order \(C\) of \(U\).  Choose two disjoint adjacent pairs
\(P=\{p_0,p_1\}\) and \(Q=\{q_0,q_1\}\).  Let \(\tau\) interchange the
two entries of \(P\), let \(\sigma\) interchange the two entries of
\(Q\), and put

\[
 z=e_C-e_{\tau C}-e_{\sigma C}+e_{\tau\sigma C}.             \tag{1.1}
\]

Suppose one of the two arcs between the cuts through \(P,Q\) has length
\(a\).  Its internal core \(K\) has size \(a-2\).

### Proposition 1.1 (four-order rectangle identity)

For every proper interval length \(s\),

\[
 B_s z=0\qquad(s\notin\{a,M-a\}).                            \tag{1.2}
\]

Up to an overall sign and interchanging the names inside \(P,Q\),

\[
 B_a z=
 e_{Kp_0q_0}+e_{Kp_1q_1}
 -e_{Kp_0q_1}-e_{Kp_1q_0}.                                  \tag{1.3}
\]

At length \(M-a\), the effect is the complementary copy of (1.3).  If
\(2a=M\), both arcs contribute in the same rank, so the effect there is
the sum of the rectangle (1.3) and its complementary rectangle.

#### Proof

An interval unchanged by at least one of the two swaps cancels in the
second difference (1.1).  A survivor must therefore contain exactly one
entry of each adjacent pair.  Its two endpoints cut both pairs, so it is
one of the two arcs between those cuts.  Their lengths are \(a\) and
\(M-a\).  At the first length, the four choices of one member of \(P\)
and one member of \(Q\) give the four sets in (1.3), with the product
sign from (1.1).  Complementation gives the other arc. \(\square\)

In the calibrated central band, write \(r=M-a\).  Except at \(r=M-1\),
one has \(2\le a\le2H<M/2\).  The complementary copy of (1.3) is an
elementary rank-\(r\) rectangle: its four sets share the
\((r-2)\)-core \(U\setminus(K\cup P\cup Q)\).  Thus there is no half-rank
exception in the central application.  At \(r=M-1\), every cyclic order
has the same \(M\) co-singleton intervals, so order changes have zero
effect and no correction is needed.

## 2. The exact integer lattice

Let \(V\) be a set of size \(v\), and let

\[
 A_r:\mathbb Z^{\binom Vr}\longrightarrow\mathbb Z^V,
 \qquad
 (A_rx)_u=\sum_{S\ni u}x_S.                                \tag{2.1}
\]

For \(|K|=r-2\) and distinct \(a,b,c,d\notin K\), define

\[
 R(K;a,b,c,d)=
 e_{Kac}+e_{Kbd}-e_{Kad}-e_{Kbc}.                           \tag{2.2}
\]

Let \(L_r(V)\) be the integer lattice generated by all vectors (2.2).

### Theorem 2.1 (integral saturation)

For every \(2\le r\le v-2\),

\[
 \boxed{L_r(V)=\ker_{\mathbb Z}A_r.}                        \tag{2.3}
\]

#### Proof

Every rectangle has zero point margins, so the left side is contained in
the right side.

For the reverse inclusion, induct on \(v\), with \(r\) fixed.  Choose a
distinguished point \(p\).  For
\(x\in\ker_{\mathbb Z}A_r\), let \(x^{(p)}\) be its coefficient vector on
the \((r-1)\)-sets \(S\subset V-p\), where the coefficient is that of
\(S+p\) in \(x\).  The margin equation at \(p\) gives

\[
 \sum_S x^{(p)}_S=0.                                       \tag{2.4}
\]

The Johnson graph on \(\binom{V-p}{r-1}\) is connected, so its adjacent
differences generate the complete zero-sum integer lattice.  Write an
adjacent pair as

\[
 S=K+b,\qquad S'=K+c,qquad |K|=r-2.
\]

Because \(v\ge r+2\), there is
\(a\notin K\cup\{p,b,c\}\).  The rectangle

\[
 R(K;p,a,b,c)
 =e_{Kpb}+e_{Kac}-e_{Kpc}-e_{Kab}                          \tag{2.5}
\]

has \(p\)-containing projection \(e_S-e_{S'}\).  An integer combination
of such rectangles therefore has projection \(x^{(p)}\).  Subtract it
from \(x\).  The remainder is still point-balanced and has no set
containing \(p\).

If \(r\le v-3\), induction on \(V-p\) finishes.  If \(r=v-2\), the
remainder is supported on the \((v-2)\)-sets of the \((v-1)\)-set
\(V-p\), hence on its co-singletons.  Their point-incidence matrix is
\(J-I\), which is nonsingular over \(\mathbb Q\); the remainder is zero.
This is also the base case \(v=r+2\). \(\square\)

### Corollary 2.2 (cone and invariants)

The nonnegative cone generated by all oriented rectangles is

\[
 \operatorname{cone}_{\mathbb R}\{R(K;a,b,c,d)}
 =\ker_{\mathbb R}A_r.                                      \tag{2.6}
\]

Indeed, interchanging \(c,d\) negates a generator, so this cone is a
linear subspace; Theorem 2.1 gives its span.  Consequently every additive
integer or real invariant of rank-\(r\) loads under all rectangle moves
factors through the point margins \(A_r x\).  There is no extra parity or
total-mass invariant.  Total mass is already forced because

\[
 r\sum_Sx_S=\sum_{u\in V}(A_rx)_u.                         \tag{2.7}
\]

### Corollary 2.3 (one top and all tops)

On one top \(U\), the four-order identities generate (2.3) for every
\(2\le r\le M-2\), subject at half rank to the paired-complement form in
Proposition 1.1 if one insists on using a single rank-selector square.
In the calibrated central band there is no half-rank issue.

On the global ground set \([2m]\), suppose \(r+2\le M\) and
\(2r\ne M\).  Every elementary rectangle (2.2) uses only \(r+2\)
coordinates, so its support extends to some top \(U\) of size \(M\).
Proposition 1.1 realizes it there.  Hence

\[
 L_r^{\rm all\ tops}=\ker_{\mathbb Z}A_r^{[2m]}.             \tag{2.8}
\]

Every realizing square has two positive and two negative orders in the
same top.  Thus the packet-count vector indexed by tops is conserved even
though (2.8) has no further target-rank invariant.

At the exceptional half rank \(M=2r\), every physical square in a fixed
top has effect
\(R+\overline R\), where complementation is inside its top.  The exact
physical lattice there is therefore

\[
 \{x+\overline x:x\in\ker_{\mathbb Z}A_r},                 \tag{2.9}
\]

which lies in the complement-invariant part of that top's point-margin
kernel.  Sums over different tops have different complement involutions;
no global equality is asserted here.
In particular it cannot generate an anti-complementary correction.  The
calibrated central ranks satisfy \(r>M/2\), so (2.9) never enters the
application.

## 3. Lattice feasibility is not nonnegative connectivity

Theorem 2.1 permits negative intermediate coefficients.  This cannot be
removed formally.

### Proposition 3.1 (an isolated nonnegative fibre point)

Assume \(3\le r\le v-3\).  There are distinct simple nonnegative vectors
\(x,y\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{\binom Vr}\) such that

\[
 A_rx=A_ry,                                                  \tag{3.1}
\]

but no elementary rectangle is applicable to \(x\).

#### Proof

Choose disjoint sets \(K,P,Q\) with

\[
 |K|=r-3,qquad |P|=|Q|=3.
\]

This uses \(r+3\le v\) points.  Put

\[
 A=K\cup P,qquad B=K\cup Q,qquad x=e_A+e_B.               \tag{3.2}
\]

Repartition the six points of \(P\cup Q\) into a different pair of
three-sets \(P',Q'\), and put

\[
 y=e_{K\cup P'}+e_{K\cup Q'}.                               \tag{3.3}
\]

Every point of \(K\) has degree two in both vectors and every point of
\(P\cup Q\) has degree one, proving (3.1).  The only two present edges in
\(x\) intersect in \(r-3\) points.  The negative pair of an applicable
elementary rectangle must intersect in exactly \(r-2\) points.  Therefore
no move is applicable. \(\square\)

Thus the rectangles are a saturated lattice basis but not a Markov basis
for nonnegative fibres in this range.  At the calibrated ranks
\(r\le M-3\), this supplies a literal local counterexample.  The boundary
rank \(M-2\) has different small-complement geometry and is not covered by
this obstruction.

## 4. Exact owner overlap of one local square

For a cyclic order \(C\) of \(U\), let \(\mathcal P_m(C)\) be its
\(M\) middle \(m\)-interval owners.

### Lemma 4.1 (adjacent-swap distance ledger)

If \(C,D\) have cyclic adjacent-swap distance \(d\), then

\[
 |\mathcal P_m(C)\cap\mathcal P_m(D)|\ge M-2d.              \tag{4.1}
\]

#### Proof

One adjacent swap changes exactly the two length-\(m\) intervals whose
boundary separates the swapped entries.  Hence it changes the incidence
family by symmetric difference four.  Along \(d\) swaps,

\[
 |\mathcal P_m(C)\triangle\mathcal P_m(D)|\le4d.
\]

Both packets have size \(M\), giving (4.1). \(\square\)

The two orders on either sign diagonal of (1.1) differ by the two swaps
\(\tau\sigma\).  Therefore

\[
 |\mathcal P_m(C)\cap\mathcal P_m(\tau\sigma C)|\ge M-4,    \tag{4.2}
\]

and

\[
 \boxed{
 |\mathcal P_m(C)\cup\mathcal P_m(\tau\sigma C)|\le M+4.}  \tag{4.3}
\]

This proves (0.3).  In particular, neither diagonal is a middle partial
packing once \(M>4\).

Define the owner-collision excess of a packet multiset \(\mathcal F\) by

\[
 \operatorname{col}(\mathcal F)
 =M|\mathcal F|-\left|\bigcup_{C\in\mathcal F}
                         \mathcal P_m(C)\right|,             \tag{4.4}
\]

where packet occurrences are counted in the first term.  This quantity is
monotone when packets are added.

For the calibrated one-packet-per-top count \(|\mathcal F|=N_H\), put
\(S=MN_H\) and \(D=W-S\).  The exact number of missing middle owners is

\[
 W-\left|\bigcup_{C\in\mathcal F}\mathcal P_m(C)\right|
 =D+\operatorname{col}(\mathcal F).                       \tag{4.5}
\]

Thus an \(o(W)\)-hole middle near-transversal has
\(E=\operatorname{col}(\mathcal F)=o(W)\); this is not an additional
assumption.

### Theorem 4.2 (batch availability bound)

Suppose \(t\) local rectangle moves have order-disjoint negative packet
occurrences, and all \(2t\) negative occurrences lie in \(\mathcal F\).
Then

\[
 \boxed{\operatorname{col}(\mathcal F)\ge t(M-4).}           \tag{4.6}
\]

#### Proof

Group the \(2t\) negative occurrences into their \(t\) diagonal pairs.
By (4.3), the union of the owner supports of pair \(i\) has size at most
\(M+4\).  Hence the union for the full negative submultiset has size at
most \(t(M+4)\), while it has \(2Mt\) owner occurrences.  Its collision
excess is at least \(t(M-4)\).  Monotonicity gives (4.6) for
\(\mathcal F\). \(\square\)

Every non-half-rank rectangle has rank-effect \(\ell_1\)-norm four.  Thus
an order-disjoint batch available in an owner near-transversal with
collision excess \(E=o(W)\) contains only

\[
 t\le\frac{E}{M-4}=o(W/m)                                  \tag{4.7}
\]

moves and has total possible rank-effect \(\ell_1\)-norm at most
\(4E/(M-4)=o(W/m)\).  This statement concerns a simultaneous absorber
batch; it does not claim that a collision reservoir cannot be reused in a
long sequential walk.

The two supports in this ledger must not be conflated.  At the target rank
\(r=m\pm q\), one square changes exactly four **rank-\(r\) flag masks** by
(1.3) or its complement.  Its applicability, however, is decided by the
two full packets on its negative diagonal, hence by their **middle-owner**
families \(\mathcal P_m(C)\).  Bound (4.3) is a middle-owner bound and is
independent of \(q\).  Even if the four changed rank-\(r\) flags are all
new and perfectly useful, the move consumes at least \(M-4\) units of the
owner-collision budget before it can be applied.

## 5. Higher-order local trades are necessarily mesoscopic

Call a signed combination of square cells **support-feasible** if it lies
in \(\ker B_m\) and, after collecting cancellations, its positive packet
supports are pairwise middle-owner-disjoint and its negative packet
supports are pairwise middle-owner-disjoint.  Quotient cyclic orders by
reversal, which has the same interval packet at every rank.

### Theorem 5.1 (connected-cell lower bound)

Every connected component of square cells whose reduced sum is nonzero
and support-feasible contains at least

\[
 \boxed{\lceil M/4\rceil}                                  \tag{5.1}
\]

cells.

#### Proof

Join two cells when their four-order supports share an order coordinate.
One cell has adjacent-swap diameter at most two.  A spanning-tree walk in
a connected component of \(s\) cells shows that the union of its order
supports has diameter at most \(2s\).

If \(s<\lceil M/4\rceil\), then

\[
 2s\le\left\lfloor\frac{M-1}{2}\right\rfloor.
\]

Lemma 4.1 says that every two surviving packets in the component share a
middle owner.  Support-feasibility therefore leaves at most one positive
and at most one negative surviving order.  Every square has coefficient
sum zero, so the two sign counts are equal.  A nonzero component would be
\(e_C-e_D\) with

\[
 B_m(e_C-e_D)=0.                                            \tag{5.2}
\]

For calibrated \(H=M-m\ge2\) with \(2H<M\), the complements of the middle
owners are all cyclic \(H\)-intervals.  Their pair-codegree graph recovers
the adjacency cycle: two coordinates have maximal off-diagonal codegree
\(H-1\) exactly when they are adjacent.  Hence the middle packet determines
the cyclic order up to reversal.  Thus \(C=D\) in our quotient, contrary
to a nonzero reduced component. \(\square\)

Theorem 5.1 does not say that a support-feasible mesoscopic circuit exists.
It says that every fixed-size local absorber, including all six- and
eight-order candidates, is impossible.  Cancellation can remove the
direct diagonal overlap only after propagation through \(\Omega(M)\)
local cells.

## 6. Exact top-transversal rigidity

Consider only selector squares whose exceptional ranks are not the middle
rank.  By Proposition 1.1, each such square in top \(U\) satisfies

\[
 B_m z_U=0                                                  \tag{6.1}
\]

and has coefficient sum zero inside that same top.

### Theorem 6.1 (no local-rectangle trade on a top transversal)

Let \(z\) be any integer sum of such squares over arbitrarily many tops.
Suppose, after cancellation, that:

1. the positive packet occurrences form a middle-owner packing;
2. the negative packet occurrences form a middle-owner packing; and
3. each sign uses at most one packet in each top.

Then \(z\) has zero interval effect at every rank.

#### Proof

Decompose \(z=\sum_Uz_U\) by top.  Coefficient-sum conservation and
condition 3 say that, for every top, either both signs are absent or

\[
 z_U=e_{C_U^+}-e_{C_U^-}.                                  \tag{6.2}
\]

Equation (6.1) says the two middle packets in (6.2) are equal.  The
pair-codegree reconstruction in Theorem 5.1 says their cyclic orders agree
up to reversal.  Reversal preserves the complete interval family at every
rank.  Thus \(B_rz_U=0\) for every \(r\), and summing over tops proves the
claim. \(\square\)

In particular, summing fixed-top rectangles across many tops does not
create a nonlocal escape: their top-count and middle-owner conservation
hold separately in every summand.  A useful cross-top absorber must include
owner-changing moves whose middle effects cancel only after different tops
are combined.  Rank-isolated local selectors cannot do this.

For the calibrated augmented matching the availability failure is even
more immediate: two packets in the negative diagonal share the same top
resource vertex, so no matching can contain that pair.  Theorem 6.1 shows
that batching fixed-top squares cannot cure this by cancellation while
retaining one packet per top.

There is, however, an exact characterization of the smallest genuinely
nonlocal alternative.

### Theorem 6.2 (two-top owner-transfer criterion)

Let \(U,V\) be distinct tops at Johnson distance

\[
 t=|U\setminus V|=|V\setminus U|,
\]

and put \(C=U\cap V\), \(|C|=m+(H-t)\).  Suppose a legal two-top trade
replaces one packet \(P_U\) in \(U\) and one packet \(P_V\) in \(V\) by
one packet \(Q_U\) in \(U\) and one packet \(Q_V\) in \(V\), with the two
packets on each sign owner-disjoint.  Then

\[
 w={\bf1}_{P_U}-{f1}_{Q_U}
   ={f1}_{Q_V}-{f1}_{P_V}                              \tag{6.3}
\]

is supported on \(\binom Cm\), satisfies

\[
 A_m^Cw=0,                                                  \tag{6.4}
\]

and has

\[
 |\operatorname{supp}w^+|,
 |\operatorname{supp}w^-|le H-t+1.                        \tag{6.5}
\]

In particular, a nonzero two-top trade is possible only if

\[
 \boxed{t\le H-2.}                                         \tag{6.6}
\]

Conversely, four packets with the stated signwise disjointness form a
legal two-top owner trade exactly when their differences have the form
(6.3); conditions (6.4)--(6.5) are the resulting necessary local
constraints.

#### Proof

Equality of the total negative and positive owner vectors gives (6.3).
An owner which is not contained in both tops can occur on only one side of
the top decomposition, so its coefficient in that top difference must be
zero.  Hence \(w\) is supported on \(C\).

Every cyclic middle packet in a fixed top contains each coordinate in
exactly \(m\) owners.  The difference of two such packets has zero point
margins, proving (6.4).

An owner of \(P_U\) lying in \(C\) has complement in \(U\) equal to an
\(H\)-interval containing the fixed \(t\)-set \(U\setminus V\).  There
are at most \(H-t+1\) such intervals.  The positive and negative support
of \(w\) is contained in the corresponding common-owner portions of its
packets, proving (6.5).

If \(H-t=0\), there is only one \(m\)-set in \(C\).  If \(H-t=1\), the
\(m\)-sets of \(C\) are its co-singletons and their point-incidence matrix
is \(J-I\), hence nonsingular.  Equation (6.4) then forces \(w=0\).
Therefore a nonzero trade requires \(H-t\ge2\), which is (6.6).  The
converse assertion is just the owner-vector equality (6.3), together with
the assumed signwise packing legality. \(\square\)

For \(t\le H-2\), Theorem 2.1 says that every algebraically possible
transfer vector \(w\) in (6.4) is an integer sum of owner rectangles on
the common ground \(C\).  What remains nontrivial is **packet
realizability**: the two differences in (6.3) must come from whole cyclic
packets while both sign pairs stay disjoint.  Fixed-top rank selectors
have \(w=0\) separately and therefore never reach this nonlocal transfer
class.

The same argument has a useful many-top form.

### Proposition 6.3 (exact owner-transfer circulation)

Consider any legal trade with one old and one new packet in every affected
top, and with both the old packets and the new packets owner-disjoint.
After cancelling owners retained in the same top, its owner transfer is a
directed labelled multigraph on the affected tops with these properties.

1. An arc \(U\to V\) is labelled by an owner \(X\subseteq U\cap V\) which
   leaves the old packet in \(U\) and enters the new packet in \(V\).
2. Every arc joins tops at distance at most \(H\).
3. If \(d(U,V)=t\), there are at most \(H-t+1\) arcs from \(U\) to \(V\).
4. At every top, indegree equals outdegree, and the stronger coordinatewise
   identity

   \[
    \sum_{U\to V,\ X}{\bf1}_X
    =\sum_{V\to U,\ Y}{\bf1}_Y                            \tag{6.7}
   \]

   holds in \(\mathbb Z^U\).
5. A top with nonzero packet difference has common in/out degree at least
   two.

#### Proof

Owner equality pairs every owner removed at one top with its unique
occurrence added at another; signwise owner-disjointness makes this pairing
unique.  The label belongs to both tops, proving 1 and 2.  Property 3 is
the interval-containment bound used in (6.5).  Old and new packets both
have \(M\) owners, so after their common owners are cancelled, the two
remainders have equal size.  Their point-incidence vectors are also equal
because every packet in one top has constant point degree \(m\).  This
proves 4.  If that common degree were one, (6.7) would equate the one
outgoing owner with the one incoming owner, and the owner would have been
cancelled inside the top.  Hence a nonzero top has degree at least two.
\(\square\)

Thus every viable nonlocal absorber is an Eulerian circulation in the
distance-\(H\) graph of tops, with a point-balanced owner trade at every
vertex and capacity \(H-d+1\) on a directed top pair.  This condition is
strictly stronger than ordinary top-graph connectivity and is directly
testable without choosing flag corrections first.

## 7. The bounded-multiplicity tests

There are two distinct interpretations of ``0/2 bounded'', and neither
solves the owner gate.

### Proposition 7.1 (owner cap two preserves the defect)

Allow an integral packet multiset whose middle-owner loads lie in
\(\{0,1,2\}\).  A close diagonal pair from (1.1) can now coexist: its
common owners merely have load two.  Nevertheless every nonmiddle
selector move preserves the complete owner-load vector exactly by (6.1).
Consequently the sets of owner holes and owner doubles cannot change along
any sequence of these moves.

Thus cap two removes the immediate packing veto but cannot return a
defective multicover to an exact owner transversal.  Any such return needs
an owner-changing trade.

### Proposition 7.2 (the \(\{0,2\}\) parity obstruction)

If every packet variable is restricted to \(\{0,2\}\), the difference of
two packet configurations is coordinatewise even.  Therefore every
rank-\(r\) load correction is even.  Algebraically, the unrestricted even
rectangle lattice is exactly

\[
 2L_r(V)=2\ker_{\mathbb Z}A_r.                            \tag{7.1}
\]

It cannot correct a single unit hole or surplus.  Dividing such a
configuration by two produces only a fractional selector and does not give
one literal integral packet family.

If instead packet variables may take all values \(0,1,2\), this parity
invariant disappears, but Proposition 7.1 and the availability bounds of
Sections 4--6 remain.

## 8. Proved boundary

The exact four-order rectangle identity is as strong as possible in the
signed target lattice:

\[
 \text{rectangle lattice}=\text{all point-balanced integer corrections}.
\]

The failure is not spectral, rational, or a hidden Smith-normal-form
index.  It is the conjunction of three integral support facts:

* an elementary negative pair covers only \(M+4\) distinct owners out of
  \(2M\) occurrences;
* fixed-top selectors preserve both the top count and the entire owner
  vector in that top; and
* eliminating the overlap inside the local-cell calculus requires at least
  \(\lceil M/4\rceil\) cells.

No theorem here excludes a genuinely owner-changing cross-top circuit.
It proves that such a circuit cannot be obtained merely by batching or
doubling the rank-isolated four-order rectangles while retaining a
near-transversal owner support.
