# Fixed complete collars: recursion, capacity loss, and forced bypass

**Date:** 2026-08-02  
**Status:** unconditional collar lemmas and exact finite arithmetic.  This
note proves that a complete top collar cannot repair a bad lower time block,
quantifies the scalar cost of a dedicated collar, and proves that the K121
fresh-collar factor necessarily carries a macroscopic family of chains past
the bottom of its collar.  It does **not** prove an all-\(k\) adaptive Hall
chronology.

## 0. Verdict

For odd \(k=2m+1\), let

\[
 W={k\choose m},\qquad
 \Lambda=\sum_{s=1}^{m}{k\choose s}=2^{k-1}-1.
\]

Put the complete target ranks

\[
             t=m-L+1,t+1,\ldots,m                 \tag{0.1}
\]

in \(L\) consecutive one-rank blocks after every lower block.  There are
three separate facts.

1. **A collar does not repair lower expansion.**  Every family \(F\) in
   one lower time block gives a chronological-upset cut
   \[
        |\mathop{\rm Up}_{P_i}(F)|
        \le |\Gamma_{m+1}(F)|.                     \tag{0.2}
   \]
   This necessary inequality is independent of \(L\).  For every fixed
   \(L\), capacity-legal invariant lower blocks violating (0.2) exist for
   all sufficiently large odd \(k\).

2. **A wide dedicated collar is not additive.**  If the lower inventory is
   packed with perfect scalar efficiency, the exact minimum number of
   blocks is
   \[
      Q_L=L+\left\lceil {\Lambda-\sum_{j=0}^{L-1}{k\choose m-j}\over W}
                    \right\rceil
          =\left\lceil {\Lambda\over W}+E_L\right\rceil,      \tag{0.3}
   \]
   where
   \[
                    E_L=\sum_{j=0}^{L-1}
                    \left(1-{{k\choose m-j}\over W}\right). \tag{0.4}
   \]
   For \(L\to\infty\) with \(L=o(\sqrt k)\),
   \[
                    E_L=\left({2\over3}+o(1)\right){L^3\over k}. \tag{0.5}
   \]
   Thus a \(d(k)+O(1)\) proof using dedicated complete ranks must have
   \(L=O(k^{1/3})\).  In particular, the K121 choice \(L=d-1\) cannot
   scale: for \(L=d(k)-1\),
   \[
        Q_L-d(k)=\bigl(0.1316552642\ldots+o(1)\bigr)\sqrt k.  \tag{0.6}
   \]

3. **Resetting at the collar base is substantially stronger than global
   skipping.**  A lower factor anchored at rank \(t\) extends through the
   collar, but its unavoidable scalar depth is
   \[
       R_L=L+\left\lceil
          {\Lambda-\sum_{j=0}^{L-1}{k\choose m-j}\over {k\choose t}}
                        \right\rceil.                \tag{0.7}
   \]
   For \(L\to\infty\) with \(L=o(\sqrt k)\), its continuous version exceeds
   \(\Lambda/W\) by
   \[
             \left(\sqrt{\pi/2}+o(1)\right){L^2\over\sqrt k}. \tag{0.8}
   \]
   Hence a full base reset requires \(L=O(k^{1/4})\) even at the scalar
   level.  K121 has \(Q_6=8\), but \(R_6=10\); its certified depth-eight
   factor therefore genuinely uses global skipping.

The exact missing all-\(k\) statement is consequently a lower-bank
chronological owner-expansion theorem.  A bounded number of complete upper
ranks supplies no such theorem by itself.

## 1. The collar-independent same-time cut

Let \(P_0,\ldots,P_{b-1}\) be arbitrary lower time blocks, all using ranks
below \(t\), and append the complete ranks in (0.1) at times
\(b,\ldots,b+L-1\).  Owners are the rank-\(m+1\) sets.

### Theorem 1.1 (same-time owner-expansion is necessary)

For \(F\subseteq P_i\), define

\[
 \mathop{\rm Up}_{P_i}(F)
 =\{y\in P_i:\text{some }x\in F\text{ satisfies }x\subseteq y\}. \tag{1.1}
\]

If the full successor graph has a left-saturating matching, then

\[
              |\mathop{\rm Up}_{P_i}(F)|
              \le |\Gamma_{m+1}(F)|                 \tag{1.2}
\]

for every \(i,F\).  In particular,

\[
                         |F|\le|\Gamma_{m+1}(F)|.   \tag{1.3}
\]

This conclusion is unchanged if any number of complete later ranks is
appended.

#### Proof

Take the upset generated by \(F\) in the chronological containment order.
Its members at time \(i\) are exactly
\(\mathop{\rm Up}_{P_i}(F)\), and all of them are exposed roots: a
same-time subset is not a successor predecessor, and the generated upset
contains no earlier-time set.  Every generated member at a later time
contains an element of \(F\), so it is not exposed.  The owner shadow of
the generated upset is exactly \(\Gamma_{m+1}(F)\).  The exposed-root Hall
inequality therefore gives (1.2).  The inclusion
\(F\subseteq\mathop{\rm Up}_{P_i}(F)\) gives (1.3). \(\square\)

### Corollary 1.2 (every pre-collar block is individually owner-matchable)

For every pre-collar time block \(P_i\), the containment graph from \(P_i\)
to the rank-\(m+1\) owners has a matching saturating \(P_i\).

#### Proof

Inequality (1.3) for all \(F\subseteq P_i\) is exactly Hall's theorem for
that bipartite graph. \(\square\)

Theorem 1.1 is stronger than terminal aperture and is local to every time
block.  It explains the logical role of the K121 computation: the lower
two-block order itself passes all owner-expansion cuts; the six complete
ranks do not erase a failed lower cut.

## 2. Explicit invariant blocks which no collar repairs

Fix a core \(A\subseteq[k]\) of size \(a\), and put the truncated principal
star

\[
 {\cal F}_{A,L}
 =\{S:A\subseteq S,\ a\le |S|\le m-L\}.             \tag{2.1}
\]

It is invariant under the pointwise stabilizer of \(A\).  Its mass and
owner shadow are

\[
 S_{k,L,a}=\sum_{j=0}^{m-L-a}{k-a\choose j},
 \qquad
 N_{k,a}={k-a\choose m+1-a}.                         \tag{2.2}
\]

### Proposition 2.1 (principal-star collar obstruction)

If

\[
                   N_{k,a}<S_{k,L,a}\le W,          \tag{2.3}
\]

then \({\cal F}_{A,L}\) is a capacity-legal one-block lower bank which
violates global Hall after **every** complete \(L\)-rank collar.

For every fixed \(L\), (2.3) holds for all sufficiently large odd \(k\)
with some \(a=O(\log k)\).

#### Proof

Place (2.1) in one time block.  It is upward closed inside itself, so
Theorem 1.1 demands \(S_{k,L,a}\le N_{k,a}\), contradicting (2.3).

For the asymptotic assertion take

\[
                       a=\lceil\log_2(4\sqrt k)\rceil. \tag{2.4}
\]

Writing \(n=k-a\), the elementary central-binomial bound
\(W\ge2^k/\sqrt{2k}\) gives

\[
 S_{k,L,a}\le2^{k-a}\le {2^k\over4\sqrt k}<W.       \tag{2.5}
\]

The cutoff in (2.2) is only \(O(a+L)=O(\log k)\) below the median of a
\({\rm Bin}(n,1/2)\) variable.  Each atom has probability at most
\(1/\sqrt{n+1}\).  Symmetry therefore gives

\[
                  S_{k,L,a}\ge2^{n-2}               \tag{2.6}

for all sufficiently large \(k\).  On the other hand
\(N_{k,a}\le {n\choose\lfloor n/2\rfloor}\le
2^n/\sqrt{n+1}<2^{n-2}\).  Thus (2.3) holds. \(\square\)

Exact enumeration gives the following first odd dimensions inside this
principal-star family.  “First” here does **not** mean the first Hall
obstruction of any form.

every dangerous star among its time blocks.

## 3. Exact scalar cost of a dedicated collar

Put

\[
 q_k={\Lambda\over W},\qquad
 a_j={{k\choose m-j}\over W},\qquad
 A_L=\sum_{j=0}^{L-1}a_j,\qquad E_L=L-A_L.          \tag{3.1}
\]

### Theorem 3.1 (dedicated-collar capacity identity)

Every chronology which gives the \(L\) complete ranks separate blocks and
puts all lower targets in \(b\) blocks of capacity \(W\) satisfies

\[
             b+L\ge L+\lceil q_k-A_L\rceil
                  =\lceil q_k+E_L\rceil.             \tag{3.2}
\]

The right side is attainable in the scalar divisible-item relaxation.

If \(L=o(\sqrt k)\), then

\[
 E_L={2L(L^2-1)\over3k}
      +O\left({L^5\over k^2}+{L^2\over k^2}\right). \tag{3.3}
\]

If \(L/\sqrt k\to\alpha\ge0\), then

\[
 {E_L\over\sqrt k}\longrightarrow
 I(\alpha):=\int_0^\alpha(1-e^{-2x^2})\,dx.          \tag{3.4}
\]

#### Proof

The lower inventory is \(W(q_k-A_L)\), proving the first expression in
(3.2).  Since \(L\) is integral,

\[
 L+\lceil q_k-A_L\rceil=\lceil q_k+L-A_L\rceil.
\]

For \(j=o(\sqrt k)\), the exact product

\[
 a_j=\prod_{u=0}^{j-1}{m-u\over m+2+u}              \tag{3.5}
\]

gives

\[
 1-a_j={2j(j+1)\over k}
       +O\left({j^4+j^2\over k^2}\right).           \tag{3.6}
\]

Summing and using
\(\sum_{j=0}^{L-1}j(j+1)=L(L^2-1)/3\) proves (3.3).
For \(j/\sqrt k\to x\), the same product gives
\(a_j\to e^{-2x^2}\), uniformly on compact \(x\)-intervals.  The Riemann
sum is (3.4). \(\square\)

The deadline satisfies

\[
 d(k)=q_k+O(1),\qquad {d(k)\over\sqrt k}\longrightarrow
 c:=\sqrt{\pi/8}.                                   \tag{3.7}
\]

Consequently (3.3) forces \(L=O(k^{1/3})\) in every dedicated-collar
architecture of depth \(d+O(1)\).  With \(L=d-1\), (3.4) gives

\[
 I(c)=c-\sqrt{\pi/8}\,\operatorname {erf}(\sqrt2c)
     =0.13165526428568464\ldots,                    \tag{3.8}
\]

which proves (0.6).

### Exact K121 and K201 arithmetic

For K121, \(d=7,L=6,t=55\).  The lower inventory is

\[
365\,874\,438\,020\,805\,743\,712\,639\,854\,188\,713\,741,
\]

so two lower blocks and six collar blocks give the scalar minimum
\(Q_6=8=d+1\).

For K201, \(d=9,L=8,t=93\).  The lower inventory is

\[
416\,250\,861\,762\,741\,955\,493\,248\,412\,775\,369\,854\,323\,619\,084\,168\,762\,251\,720\,255,
\]

and it needs three \(W\)-capacity blocks.  Therefore

\[
                         Q_8=11=d+2.                 \tag{3.9}
\]

This is an unconditional capacity obstruction to copying the K121
\(L=d-1\), depth-\(d+1\) layout at K201.  It is not a Hall obstruction to
another K201 chronology.  In fact the first odd dimension where this
specific \(L=d-1\) scalar layout needs at least \(d+2\) blocks is K153;
K201 is a later instance.

## 4. Complete-base recursion and its stronger bottleneck

Let \({\cal B}_{<t}^+\) be the nonempty sets of rank below \(t\).

### Theorem 4.1 (complete-base extension)

Suppose \({\cal B}_{<t}^+\) has a factor into
\({k\choose t}\) chains, each of target length at most \(b\), together
with distinct formal rank-\(t\) anchors containing their chains.  Then the
full strict-lower ideal has a rank-\(m+1\)-owner-anchored factor of depth at
most

\[
                              b+L.                  \tag{4.1}
\]

#### Proof

Regard every formal rank-\(t\) anchor as the actual first target of its
chain.  For each \(s=t,\ldots,m-1\), normalized matching between the two
complete ranks gives an injection from every rank-\(s\) set to a containing
rank-\(s+1\) set.  Unmatched upper sets start new chains.  A perfect
containment matching sends rank \(m\) to the equally large owner rank
\(m+1\).  These matchings cover every collar target and every owner, and
attaching the lower chains gives (4.1). \(\square\)

The antecedent is an actual lower anchored-factor hypothesis, not a
capacity statement.  It remains **unproved** at near-average depth for all
\(k,t\).

Even if the antecedent were available, counting forces

\[
 b\ge\left\lceil{W(q_k-A_L)\over W_t}\right\rceil,
 \qquad W_t={k\choose t}=Wa_{L-1}.                  \tag{4.2}
\]

Thus the reset architecture has the exact scalar lower bound

\[
 R_L=L+\left\lceil{q_k-A_L\over a_{L-1}}\right\rceil. \tag{4.3}
\]

Using (3.3), \(q_k=\sqrt{\pi k/8}+O(k^{-1/2})\), and
\(1-a_{L-1}\sim2L^2/k\), one obtains, for
\(L\to\infty\) and \(L=o(\sqrt k)\),

\[
 L+{q_k-A_L\over a_{L-1}}-q_k
 =\left(\sqrt{\pi/2}+o(1)\right){L^2\over\sqrt k}.  \tag{4.4}
\]

Hence complete-base reset is compatible with \(d+O(1)\) only when
\(L=O(k^{1/4})\).  If \(L/\sqrt k\to\alpha\), its continuous normalized
depth has limiting coefficient

\[
 \alpha+\sqrt{\pi/8}\,e^{2\alpha^2}
                 \operatorname {erfc}(\sqrt2\alpha).          \tag{4.5}
\]

At \(\alpha=c=\sqrt{\pi/8}\), this is
\(0.9154139333901834\ldots\), against the deadline coefficient
\(c=0.6266570686577501\ldots\).

## 5. A macroscopic bypass is forced

The difference between K121's proved depth eight and the reset lower bound
ten can be made structural.

### Proposition 5.1 (forced base-bypass count)

Consider any chain factor following \(b\) lower times and then the complete
collar (0.1).  Put

\[
                 M_< =\sum_{s=1}^{t-1}{k\choose s}.             \tag{5.1}
\]

At least

\[
                  (M_<-bW_t)_+                                 \tag{5.2}
\]

lower targets lie on chains which skip rank \(t\), and at least

\[
              \left\lceil{(M_<-bW_t)_+\over b}\right\rceil     \tag{5.3}
\]

distinct chains skip rank \(t\) while carrying lower targets.

#### Proof

Only \(W_t\) chains can meet rank \(t\), one for each rank-\(t\) target.
Every such chain contains at most one target at each of the \(b\) lower
times, hence at most \(bW_t\) lower targets lie on those chains.  This is
(5.2).  A bypass chain also contains at most \(b\) lower targets, which
gives (5.3). \(\square\)

For the certified K121 layout, \(b=2,L=6,t=55\).  Proposition 5.1 forces

\[
131\,638\,076\,941\,976\,988\,069\,650\,913\,323\,730\,213
                                                                  \tag{5.4}
\]

lower targets onto at least

\[
65\,819\,038\,470\,988\,494\,034\,825\,456\,661\,865\,107       \tag{5.5}
\]

rank-55-bypassing chains.  The latter is
\(0.3434407704\ldots W\).  Thus the K121 global-skipping factor cannot be
recast as a complete rank-55 reset plus \(O(1)\) exceptional chains.

At K201, any hypothetical scalar-minimum \(L=8,b=3\) collar factor would
similarly require at least

\[
35\,298\,433\,437\,619\,724\,635\,995\,286\,023\,214\,414\,764\,732\,849\,302\,282\,440\,681\,419
                                                                  \tag{5.6}
\]

rank-93-bypassing chains.  No such K201 factor is asserted here.

## 6. Exact remaining hypothesis

The strongest proof-safe conclusion is negative but sharp:

* a complete collar propagates a lower anchored factor (Theorem 4.1);
* it does not create lower owner expansion (Theorem 1.1);
* a collar of width comparable with \(d\) already loses
  \(\Theta(\sqrt k)\) scalar blocks (Theorem 3.1); and
* forcing all lower chains through the collar base is incompatible with
  the known K121 factor and requires a macroscopic bypass (Proposition 5.1).

Therefore the remaining all-\(k\) clause cannot be replaced by “append a
fixed complete collar” or by “reset at its bottom rank with a bounded
sidecar.”  What remains **UNPROVED** is a chronology of the lower orbit bank
with \(d(k)+O(1)-L\) times such that every chronological root family—not
only every principal star slice—has owner shadow at least its mass, while a
macroscopic but structured collection of chains may skip the collar base.

Nothing in this note proves literal serialization, a protected host,
endpoint aperture, address/history propagation, residence, compiler
closure, or regenerative pull-cell closure.
