# Gate C: a multiscale variable-fragment compiler and the coordinate-star obstruction

**Status (2026-08-22).**  This note proves a new conditional compiler.
It replaces one near-spanning extended-track matching by
\(O(\log b)\) core-fragment matchings, each of which need cover only a fixed
fraction of the current middle residual.  The fragment length decreases with
the residual density.  Exact quota splitting introduces no rounding loss,
and the total seam cost is \(o(W)\).

This is a genuine weakening of the demanded matching fraction, but it does
not close Gate C.  There is an exact obstruction to any cardinality-only
stage theorem: the family of middle sets containing one fixed coordinate has
density \(1/2\), yet it contains no phase-refined fragment core of length
\(b+1\).  Thus the missing positive input is a product-specific
anti-junta/track-expansion statement for the residuals actually generated by
the construction, together with colored quota control.

## 1. Phase-refined atoms and fragment tokens

Let
\[
 b=2h+1,\qquad |\Omega|=2b,\qquad
 {\cal V}={\Omega\choose b},\qquad W=|{\cal V}|={2b\choose b}.     \tag{1.1}
\]
A word is a finite sequence of subsets of \(\Omega\).  It realizes a
nonempty target when that target is the union of a nonempty consecutive
interval of letters.  Write \(\nu(2b)\) for the minimum length of a word
realizing every nonempty subset of \(\Omega\).

Split \(\Omega=A\mathbin{\dot\cup}B\), with both shores of size \(b\),
choose a directed cyclic order on each shore, and repeatedly emit the type
period
\[
 B,A,B,A,\ldots,B,A,B.                                            \tag{1.2}
\]
Successive events of one type emit successive coordinates of its shore
cycle.  More formally, choose a pair of stream origins in
\(\mathbb Z_b^2\), and identify two origin pairs when they differ by an
element of \(\langle(h,h+1)\rangle\): advancing the time origin by one
whole type period makes exactly that change.  Thus there are \(b\) root-pair
cosets.  The resulting phase-refined atom is a cyclic word
\((w_t)_{t\in\mathbb Z_{b^2}}\), and its middle windows are
\[
 C_t=\{w_t,\ldots,w_{t+b-1}\}.                                   \tag{1.3}
\]

Every atom has \(b^2\) distinct middle windows.  Indeed, each length-\(b\)
type window consists of cyclic intervals of lengths \(h\) and \(h+1\) on
the two shores.  If \(r\in\mathbb Z_b\) is its time residue, the sum of the
two interval-start indices is the origin sum plus \(r\): before time \(r\),
the two streams have emitted a total of exactly \(r\) symbols.  Advancing
one type period changes their starts by
\((h,h+1)=(h,-h)\).  Since \((h,b)=1\), the \(b\) period advances at fixed
\(r\) visit all \(b\) start pairs with the prescribed sum.  The \(b\)
different residues have different sums, so together they visit every pair
in \(\mathbb Z_b^2\) exactly once.

A coordinate recurs only after \(b\) events of its own type.  The same-type
event gaps in (1.2) are \(2,\ldots,2,3\) on one shore and
\(2,\ldots,2,1\) on the other.  Among \(b\) consecutive gaps of the latter
kind at most two equal one.  Hence every coordinate recurrence gap is at
least \(2b-2\), and every block of at most \(2b-2\) emissions is injective.

The number of labelled atoms and the number through one middle target are
\[
 \widehat N=bW((b-1)!)^2,\qquad
 \widehat D={\widehat N b^2\over W}=b(b!)^2.                       \tag{1.4}
\]
The first count chooses the split, two directed cyclic orders, and one of
the \(b\) root-pair cosets.  The labelled family is invariant under
\(S_{2b}\), so double counting atom--window incidences gives the second.
In particular,
\[
 W\widehat D=b(2b)!.                                               \tag{1.5}
\]

Fix
\[
 H=\left\lceil\sqrt{2b\log(2b)}\right\rceil,\qquad g=b+H.          \tag{1.6}
\]
For all sufficiently large \(b\), \(g\le2b-2\).  A labelled fragment of
core length \(1\le L\le b^2/2\) is an atom and a cyclic start \(a\), with
core
\[
 F_L(a)=\{C_a,\ldots,C_{a+L-1}\}.                                 \tag{1.7}
\]
The labelled core-fragment hypergraph \({\cal H}_{b,L}\) has vertex set
\({\cal V}\) and one labelled edge \(F_L(a)\) for every such fragment;
parallel labels are retained.  A core matching means a collection whose
underlying vertex sets \(F_L(a)\) are pairwise disjoint.
Each labelled fragment has, at every \(1\le s\le g\), the \(L\)
designated occurrence tokens
\[
 T_s(a,j)=\{w_{a+j},\ldots,w_{a+j+s-1}\},
 \qquad 0\le j<L.                                                  \tag{1.8}
\]
They are genuine \(s\)-sets by the recurrence gap.  There are exactly
\(W\widehat D\) labelled fragments at each length \(L\).

## 2. Exact simultaneous quota splitting

The following elementary rounding fact is what makes different fragment
scales compatible.

### Lemma 2.1 (balanced row and column quotas)

Let \(N\ge1\) and let \(M_0,\ldots,M_{R-1}\) be nonnegative integers, with
\(M=\sum_jM_j\).  There are integers \(q_j(T)\), \(T\in[N]\), such that
\[
 \sum_Tq_j(T)=M_j,\qquad
 q_j(T)\in\{\lfloor M_j/N\rfloor,\lceil M_j/N\rceil\},             \tag{2.1}
\]
and their column sums \(Q(T)=\sum_jq_j(T)\) satisfy
\[
 Q(T)\in\{\lfloor M/N\rfloor,\lceil M/N\rceil\}.                  \tag{2.2}
\]

#### Proof

Write \(M_j=Na_j+r_j\), \(0\le r_j<N\).  List the \(N\) targets cyclically.
Starting where the preceding row stopped, give row \(j\) an extra unit on
the next \(r_j\) distinct targets, and then advance the pointer by \(r_j\).
This proves (2.1).  Across all rows the extra units are the first
\(\sum_jr_j\) entries of one infinite periodic target list, so every target
receives either the floor or the ceiling of \(\sum_jr_j/N\) extras.
Adding the common base \(\sum_ja_j\) proves (2.2). \(\square\)

Let \(a_j(T)\) be arbitrary nonnegative loads of total \(M_j\), and define
the stage overflow
\[
 V_j=\sum_T(a_j(T)-q_j(T))_+.                                    \tag{2.3}
\]
For \(A(T)=\sum_ja_j(T)\), the global overflow above \(Q\) obeys
\[
 \sum_T(A(T)-Q(T))_+
 \le\sum_jV_j,                                                     \tag{2.4}
\]
by subadditivity of the positive part.  If
\(h=|\{T:A(T)=0\}|\), then
\[
 \boxed{h\le(N-M)_++\sum_jV_j.}                                  \tag{2.5}
\]
Indeed global positive excess equals global positive deficit.  A hole at a
positive-quota target costs at least one unit of deficit, while a balanced
quota vector has exactly \((N-M)_+\) zero entries.

## 3. The variable-length multiscale compiler

For stages \(j=0,\ldots,R-1\), choose a length \(L_j\) and \(t_j\) labelled
fragments of that length.  Assume that all their middle cores (1.7) are
pairwise disjoint, both within and between stages.  Put
\[
 M_j=L_jt_j,\qquad M=\sum_jM_j=(1-\delta)W.                       \tag{3.1}
\]
For \(s\in\{b-H,\ldots,b+H\}\), let \(a_{j,s}(T)\) count the designated
rank-\(s\) tokens of stage \(j\).  Its total is \(M_j\).

Apply Lemma 2.1 separately at each rank, and write
\[
 V_{j,s}=\sum_T(a_{j,s}(T)-q_{j,s}(T))_+.                         \tag{3.2}
\]
At rank \(b\), take \(q_{j,b}\) to be the indicator of the stage-\(j\)
core.  Core disjointness makes these stage quotas and their sum balanced,
and \(V_{j,b}=0\).

### Theorem 3.1 (multiscale fragment compiler)

Under the definitions above,
\[
\begin{split}
 \nu(2b)\le\;&M+(g-1)\sum_j{M_j\over L_j}\\
 &+\sum_{s=b-H}^{b+H}\left((N_s-M)_++\sum_jV_{j,s}\right)
 +\sum_{|s-b|>H}{2b\choose s},
 \qquad N_s={2b\choose s}.                                      \tag{3.3}
\end{split}
\]
Consequently,
\[
 \delta=o(b^{-1/3}),\qquad
 {g\over W}\sum_j{M_j\over L_j}=o(1),\qquad
 \sum_{j,s}V_{j,s}=o(W)                                         \tag{3.4}
\]
imply
\[
 \boxed{\nu(2b)=(1+o(1)){2b\choose b}.}                          \tag{3.5}
\]

#### Proof

Linearize every selected fragment separately as the singleton block
\[
 \{w_a\},\{w_{a+1}\},\ldots,\{w_{a+L_j+g-2}\}.                   \tag{3.6}
\]
Every designated token (1.8) is an interval wholly inside its own block.
Overlaps between atoms, guards, or off-middle targets create no physical
conflict.  The total block length is
\[
 \sum_jt_j(L_j+g-1)=M+(g-1)\sum_j{M_j\over L_j}.                  \tag{3.7}
\]

At each band rank, (2.5) bounds the number of missing targets by the
corresponding term in (3.3).  Append each missing band target as one
set-valued letter.

It remains to control the quota-zero term.  For \(q\ge1\),
\[
 {N_{b\pm q}\over W}
 =\prod_{i=0}^{q-1}{b-i\over b+i+1}
 \le\exp\!\left(-{q^2\over2b}\right).                              \tag{3.8}
\]
The inequality follows by applying \(\log(1-u)\le-u\) termwise and using
\(\sum_{i<q}(2i+1)=q^2\) and \(b+i+1\le2b\).
If \(N_{b\pm q}>(1-\delta)W\), then, for \(\delta\le1/2\),
\[
 q<2\sqrt{b\delta}.
\]
Every positive deficit is at most \(\delta W\), whence
\[
 \sum_{s=b-H}^{b+H}(N_s-M)_+
 =O\!\left(W\delta(1+\sqrt{b\delta})\right)=o(W)                  \tag{3.9}
\]
under the first condition in (3.4).

For \(X\sim{\rm Bin}(2b,1/2)\), the exponential-moment estimate
\(\Pr(|X-b|\ge H)\le2e^{-H^2/b}\), together with
\(W\ge2^{2b}/(2b+1)\), makes the final sum in (3.3) \(o(W)\).
Equations (3.4), (3.7), and (3.9) prove the upper bound.

For the lower bound, all interval unions ending at one word position form
an inclusion chain.  At most one distinct middle target can be assigned to
that endpoint, so realizing all \(W\) middle targets needs at least \(W\)
positions. \(\square\)

The theorem requires only disjoint **cores**.  The \(H\)-step neighboring
windows used to express offset colors, and the extra letters in (3.6), may
overlap arbitrarily across fragments.

## 4. A density-adapted schedule has negligible total seams

Fix constants
\[
 {1\over3}<\gamma<c<2,\qquad 0<\theta<1,                           \tag{4.1}
\]
and, for a current middle residual fraction \(x\ge b^{-\gamma}\), put
\[
 d(x)=\max\{\log\log b,\log(1/x)\},\qquad
 L(x)=\left\lfloor{cb\log b\over d(x)}\right\rfloor.              \tag{4.2}
\]
For large \(b\), every such length satisfies
\[
 g\le L(x)\le {b^2\over2}.                                        \tag{4.3}
\]
The lower bound uses \(d(x)\le\gamma\log b\) and \(c>\gamma\);
the upper bound uses \(d(x)\ge\log\log b\).

Start with \(x_0=1\).  While \(x_j>b^{-\gamma}\), suppose that a core
matching inside the current middle residual, of length
\(L_j=L(x_j)\), is selected and covers
\[
 M_j=(x_j-x_{j+1})W\ge\theta x_jW.                               \tag{4.4}
\]
Let \(R_*\) be the first index with \(x_{R_*}\le b^{-\gamma}\).  Then
\[
 R_*\le R_{\max}:=
 \left\lceil{\gamma\log b\over-\log(1-\theta)}\right\rceil.     \tag{4.5}
\]
Indeed \(x_{j+1}\le(1-\theta)x_j\).  The remaining fraction
\(x_{R_*}\) is therefore at most \(b^{-\gamma}=o(b^{-1/3})\).

More importantly, the total seam charge is small.  For all sufficiently
large \(b\), flooring in (4.2) gives
\[
 {g\over L_j}\le {4d(x_j)\over c\log b}.                          \tag{4.6}
\]
The function \(d(x)\) decreases with \(x\).  Therefore
\[
\begin{split}
 {g\over W}\sum_j{M_j\over L_j}
 &\le {4\over c\log b}\sum_j(x_j-x_{j+1})d(x_j)\\
 &\le {4\over c\log b}\int_0^1d(x)\,dx
 =O\!\left({\log\log b\over\log b}\right)=o(1).                  \tag{4.7}
\end{split}
\]
Here
\[
 \int_0^1d(x)\,dx
 =(1-1/\log b)\log\log b
 +{\,\log\log b+1\over\log b}.                                   \tag{4.8}
\]

Combining Theorem 3.1 with (4.4)--(4.7) proves the following exact
reduction.

### Corollary 4.1 (constant-fraction stage gate)

It is sufficient to choose recursively, inside each actually generated
residual, a length-\(L(x_j)\) core matching covering a fixed fraction
\(\theta\) of that residual, and to choose the simultaneous stage quotas of
Lemma 2.1 so that
\[
 \sum_{j=0}^{R_*-1}\sum_{s=b-H}^{b+H}V_{j,s}=o(W).                \tag{4.9}
\]
No individual stage must be near-spanning.  This removes the one-shot
\(o(Wb^{-1/3})\) matching leave from the local matching request.  It does
not remove the colored-overflow request or prove that arbitrary residuals
have the required constant-fraction matching.

## 5. The support ledger stays open at every scale

Let a middle residual retain each target independently with probability
\(x\).  A length-\(L\) fragment survives precisely when its \(L\) distinct
core targets survive, so (1.5) gives
\[
 {\mathbb E}Z_x=W\widehat D x^L.                                  \tag{5.1}
\]
At \(L=L(x)\), (4.2) gives
\[
 L\log(1/x)\le cb\log b.                                         \tag{5.2}
\]
Stirling's formula and (1.5) give
\[
 \log(W\widehat D)
 =2b\log b+(2\log2-2)b+O(\log b).
\]
Consequently
\[
 \boxed{\log{\mathbb E}Z_x\ge(2-c+o(1))b\log b}                  \tag{5.3}
\]
uniformly for \(b^{-\gamma}\le x\le1\).

The same conclusion holds for a uniformly random exact residual of size
\(m=xW\).  Its exact survival probability is
\[
 {(m)_L\over(W)_L}
 =x^L\exp\!\left(O(L^2/m)\right)=x^L(1+o(1)),                     \tag{5.4}
\]
because \(m\ge b^{-\gamma}W\) is exponential while \(L\) is polynomial.

Thus the density-adapted schedule never crosses the labelled-support
first-moment extinction threshold when \(c<2\).  Equations (5.1)--(5.4)
are only an average over random residuals; they do not imply expansion or
even one surviving fragment for every residual of that size.

## 6. Exact coordinate-star obstruction

For a coordinate \(z\in\Omega\), put
\[
 {\cal S}_z=\{C\in{\cal V}:z\in C\},\qquad
 |{\cal S}_z|={W\over2}.                                          \tag{6.1}
\]

### Theorem 6.1 (stars contain no long phase fragments)

For every phase-refined atom, the start indices \(t\) for which
\(C_t\in{\cal S}_z\) form disjoint cyclic runs, each of length exactly
\(b\).  Hence
\[
 {\cal H}_{b,L}[U]\ \hbox{has no edge whenever}\quad
 U\subseteq{\cal S}_z,\quad L\ge b+1,                             \tag{6.2}
\]
where \({\cal H}_{b,L}\) is the labelled length-\(L\) core-fragment
hypergraph.

#### Proof

If \(z\) is emitted at position \(p\), then \(C_t\) contains that occurrence
exactly for
\[
 t=p-b+1,p-b+2,\ldots,p,                                         \tag{6.3}
\]
a run of \(b\) cyclic start indices.  Consecutive emissions of \(z\) are
separated by at least \(2b-2\).  Thus consecutive runs (6.3) are separated
by at least \(b-2>0\) other starts and cannot merge, including across the
cyclic boundary.  This proves the first assertion.  A fragment core of
length \(L\ge b+1\) cannot lie inside one of these runs, proving (6.2).
\(\square\)

For every \(x\le1/2\), a residual of any prescribed integer size at most
\(xW\) can therefore be chosen inside one coordinate star and be completely
edge-free at every \(L>b\).  Since \(c>\gamma\), all schedule lengths (4.2)
are \(>b\) for large \(b\).  Thus neither residual cardinality, the huge
average support (5.3), nor the full hypergraph's degree/codegree profile can
yield Corollary 4.1 for arbitrary residuals.

## 7. Exact remaining Gate-C statement

For any fixed constants (1/3<\gamma<c<2) and (0<\theta<1), the
multiscale compiler proves the implication
\[
\boxed{
\begin{array}{c}
\text{there is a recursively residual-disjoint sequence of
\(O(\log b)\) density-adapted core matchings,}\\
\text{each covers a fixed fraction until the leave is at most
\(b^{-\gamma}W\), where \(1/3<\gamma<c<2\),}\\
\text{and its aggregate band-quota overflow is \(o(W)\)}
\end{array}
\Longrightarrow
\nu(2b)=(1+o(1))W.}                                               \tag{7.1}
\]

Here ``residual-disjoint'' means precisely that stage \(j\) is matched in
the middle vertices left by stages \(0,\ldots,j-1\); this is the
core-disjointness hypothesis of Theorem 3.1.  No undefined expansion
property is an additional hypothesis in (7.1).  Rather, the coordinate-star
theorem shows why cardinality alone cannot prove the matching premise: a
stopped residual may have the right size and zero usable long tracks.  A
positive proof of that premise must therefore do at least one of the
following:

1. preserve a quantitative anti-junta/track-supersaturation invariant under
   the chosen matchings;
2. pre-couple the stages so their future residuals are certified
   track-expanding; or
3. prove a colored path-selection theorem directly for the reachable
   residual law.

This note proves none of those three alternatives.  Its advance is exact:
the near-perfect one-shot central matching is no longer necessary, quota
rounding and seams are closed, and the surviving obstruction is localized
to product-specific residual shape plus aggregate colored overflow.
