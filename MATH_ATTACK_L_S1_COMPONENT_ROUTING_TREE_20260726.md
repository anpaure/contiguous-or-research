# The \(s_1\)-component routing tree: exact phase owners, packet arithmetic, composition, and source reindexing

Date: 2026-07-26

Method: pure mathematics only.  No computation, search, solver, or web input
is used.

## 0. Outcome

Write

\[
 C_a=\operatorname {Cat}_a,qquad n=2r+1,qquad
 \tau=s_1=(2\ 3),qquad B=C_r.
\]

There are four separate conclusions.

1.  The full interaction components of (F_r) and (s_iF_r) are indeed
    described by normalized phase-owner maps, but not quite by a permutation
    group.  If (\kappa_\ell(w)) is the canonical owner of the
    (s_i)-image of the phase-(\ell) target of row (w), then after
    contracting the phase-zero port matching the edges are

    \[
                         u\sim f_\ell(u),qquad
             f_\ell=\kappa_\ell\kappa_0^{-1}.          \tag{0.1}
    \]

    The components are the equivalence classes generated by all relations
    in (0.1).  The even (X)-phase maps are permutations.  The odd
    (Y)-phase owner maps need not be injective, even for (r=3) and
    (s_1=(2\ 3)).  Thus the phrase "orbits of the phase-owner
    permutations" is correct only after bijectivity of every phase map has
    separately been proved.  Odd phases cannot be discarded.

2.  For (s_1), the imported exact component theorem gives shore sizes

    \[
                    k_j=C_j+C_{j+1},qquad
                    \text{multiplicity }C_{r-j-2}.      \tag{0.2}
    \]

    At the calibrated scale (B/p=\theta<16), all but at most the two
    largest component instances are (p)-bounded for all sufficiently
    large (r).  Hence the maximal (p)-bounded menu covers at least

    \[
                           \left(\frac{39}{64}+o(1)\right)B, \tag{0.3}
    \]

    not merely ( (3/8+o(1))B).  The (3/8) quantity is the mass of a
    deliberately microscopic end of the hierarchy and is useful because
    its packet sizes may be bounded independently of (r).

3.  A marked occurrence and the complete shadow column have different
    actions.  If every row of a component carries one equivariant marked
    occurrence at (T), switching that component gives

    \[
                        k_j(e_{\tau T}-e_T).             \tag{0.4}
    \]

    But its complete rank-((r-1)) action is

    \[
                    \Delta_{j,R}^{(r-1)}
                      =(\tau-1)H_{j,R}^{(r-1)},          \tag{0.5}
    \]

    and has forced reverse collateral.  In an orientation in which every
    representative (Q) contains (2) and not (3),

    \[
       \Delta_{j,R}^{(r-1)}
          =\sum_Q a_{j,R}(Q)(e_Q-e_{\tau Q}),qquad
       \boxed{\sum_Qa_{j,R}(Q)=0}.                     \tag{0.6}
    \]

    The proved colex pivot has coefficient (+1), so the remaining arms
    have total oriented coefficient (-1).  A component never consists
    only of its distinguished pivot arm.

4.  The numerical six-level recurrence is correct conditionally.  A fixed
    microscopic menu may be chosen so that one ideal split leaves at most

    \[
                              \rho=\frac{161}{256}       \tag{0.7}
    \]

    of either parent branch.  Since

    \[
                         \rho^6<\frac1{16},              \tag{0.8}
    \]

    six genuinely recursive legal splits would make every final marked
    fibre smaller than (p) whenever (B<16p).

    The required recursive factor theorem is not presently available.
    There is also a decisive source-reindex correction.  At fixed port
    (u), the new row is (s_iC_{s_i u}), not (s_iC_u).  Therefore a
    marked root subset such as (10D_r), which is not (s_1)-stable, does
    not move inside a two-point target orbit.  The size-two parent
    rectangle explicitly moves its upper aligned target even though that
    target is fixed as a coordinate set by (s_1).  Thus neither the
    proposed six-level tree nor the tempting aligned-orbit no-go follows
    from coordinate symmetry.  The exact general component action on the
    aligned child profile remains uncomputed beyond the first rectangle.

The proved positive composition statement is narrower and useful: rooted
packets in chronology-disjoint phase slabs compose exactly, even when their
row supports cross.  If two slabs share a (Y)-cut, an explicit mixed
colour defect is the additional obstruction.

## 1. The full phase-owner component theorem

Let the canonical rooted Chung--Feller factor (F_r) have rows indexed by
(w\in D_r).  Write its alternating two-ledger targets as

\[
 Z_{2a}(w)=X_a(w)\quad(0\le a\le r),qquad
 Z_{2a+1}(w)=Y_a(w)\quad(0\le a<r),                    \tag{1.1}
\]

where (Y_a(w)=X_a(w)\cup X_{a+1}(w)).  Let
(o_F(T)\in D_r) be the root of the unique canonical row owning target
(T), in the appropriate (X)- or (Y)-ledger.

Fix (s=s_i=(2i\ 2i+1)).  Define the phase-owner maps

\[
                   \kappa_\ell(w)=o_F(sZ_\ell(w)),
                   \qquad0\le\ell\le2r.              \tag{1.2}
\]

Because (s) preserves the Dyck family,

\[
                 \kappa_0=s|_{D_r},qquad
                 \kappa_{2r}=\kappa_0.                \tag{1.3}
\]

The second equality uses (X_r(w)=\overline w) and commutation of (s)
with complementation.

### Theorem 1.1 (normalized owner-equivalence theorem)

In the bipartite ownership overlay of (F_r) and (sF_r), label a right
row by its source (w).  The phase-(\ell) target gives the edge

\[
                         \kappa_\ell(w)_{\rm L}-w_{\rm R}. \tag{1.4}
\]

Contract the phase-zero perfect matching and label the contracted vertex
by its common port

\[
                              u=\kappa_0(w).             \tag{1.5}
\]

Then the phase-(\ell) edge is

\[
                         \{u,f_\ell(u)\},qquad
                         f_\ell=\kappa_\ell\kappa_0^{-1}. \tag{1.6}
\]

Consequently the full interaction components are exactly the classes of
the smallest equivalence relation on (D_r) containing

\[
                          u\sim f_\ell(u)
              \quad(u\in D_r, 0\le\ell\le2r).          \tag{1.7}
\]

If every (f_\ell) is bijective, these classes are the orbits of

\[
                     \langle f_\ell^{\pm1}:0\le\ell\le2r\rangle. \tag{1.8}
\]

Without that extra hypothesis, (1.7), not (1.8), is the exact statement.

#### Proof

The right source row (w) is the coordinate image of the canonical row
(w).  Its phase-(\ell) target is (sZ_\ell(w)), whose left canonical
owner is (\kappa_\ell(w)), proving (1.4).  At phase zero this is the
perfect matching (\kappa_0(w)_{\rm L}-w_{\rm R}).

Put (u=\kappa_0(w)), so (w=\kappa_0^{-1}(u)).  The other endpoint of
the contracted phase-(\ell) edge is labelled by its left root

\[
              \kappa_\ell(w)
                =\kappa_\ell\kappa_0^{-1}(u)=f_\ell(u).
\]

Connectivity of the contracted graph is therefore precisely the
equivalence closure (1.7).  If the maps are bijections, undirected paths
are exactly words in the maps and their inverses, giving (1.8). \(\square\)

The normalization order in (1.6) matters.  In source labels the conjugate
maps are

\[
                         h_\ell=\kappa_0^{-1}\kappa_\ell. \tag{1.9}
\]

Using (\kappa_\ell) itself after identifying the two shores is wrong
unless (\kappa_0=1).

### Proposition 1.2 (the odd owner maps need not be permutations)

For a flaw-preserving (s_i), every even map is a permutation:

\[
                         \kappa_{2a}=X_a^{-1}s_iX_a.     \tag{1.10}
\]

The odd maps need not be injective.  Already for (r=3) and
(s_1=(2\ 3)),

\[
                         \kappa_1(123)=\kappa_1(124)=123. \tag{1.11}
\]

#### Proof

The Chung--Feller column map (w\mapsto X_a(w)) is a bijection onto its
flaw layer, and (s_i) preserves that layer.  This proves (1.10).

For (1.11), the canonical phase-zero upper targets of rows (123) and
(124) are respectively

\[
                             1236,qquad1246.
\]

Their (s_1)-images are (1236) and (1346).  Both are upper targets of
the canonical row rooted at (123).  Thus the two source roots have the
same odd-phase owner. \(\square\)

This example proves that the odd maps cannot be called permutations.  It
does not by itself prove that odd edges enlarge the equivalence relation
generated by the even phases, because the two collided roots might already
be even-connected.  Omitting the odd edges would require a separate
redundancy theorem.  For general (i), Theorem 1.1 is the exact available
characterization.  The complete closed component law below is currently
proved only for (i=1).

## 2. Exact (s_1) packets and the (p)-cutoff

For (0\le j\le r-2), put

\[
 \mathcal A_j=
 \{1u0:u\in D_{j+1}\}\ \dot\cup\
 \{10\,1v0:v\in D_j\}.                               \tag{2.1}
\]

For (R\in D_{r-j-2}), let

\[
                          K_{j,R}=\{AR:A\in\mathcal A_j\}. \tag{2.2}
\]

The exact imported (s_1)-component theorem states that the sets
(K_{j,R}) are precisely the shore root sets of the interaction
components.  Hence

\[
 |K_{j,R}|=k_j=C_j+C_{j+1},qquad
 \#\{R\}=C_{r-j-2}.                                  \tag{2.3}
\]

They partition (D_r), equivalently

\[
              \sum_{j=0}^{r-2}C_{r-j-2}(C_j+C_{j+1})=C_r. \tag{2.4}
\]

### Lemma 2.1 (monotonicity and exact largest packet)

One has

\[
                         k_j=\frac{5j+4}{j+2}C_j,       \tag{2.5}
\]

and (k_j) is strictly increasing.  The largest packet is

\[
                         k_{r-2}=C_{r-2}+C_{r-1}.       \tag{2.6}
\]

It is (p)-bounded exactly when, writing (\theta=C_r/p),

\[
 \boxed{
 \theta\le\kappa_r
  :=\frac{4(2r-1)(2r-3)}{(5r-6)(r+1)}
  \longrightarrow\frac{16}{5}.}                      \tag{2.7}
\]

Moreover

\[
 \frac{k_{r-2}}{C_r}
  =\frac{(5r-6)(r+1)}{4(2r-1)(2r-3)}
  >\frac5{16}.                                        \tag{2.8}
\]

#### Proof

The Catalan recurrence

\[
                    C_{j+1}=\frac{2(2j+1)}{j+2}C_j
\]

gives (2.5), and either the same formula or direct comparison gives strict
monotonicity.  Also

\[
 \frac{C_{r-1}}{C_r}=\frac{r+1}{2(2r-1)},\qquad
 \frac{C_{r-2}}{C_r}
   =\frac{r(r+1)}{4(2r-1)(2r-3)}.
\]

Adding proves (2.7)--(2.8); the strict inequality in (2.8) reduces to a
positive linear polynomial in (r). \(\square\)

Thus the usual critical choice (C_r\ge4p) necessarily has a component
larger than (p).  In fact its largest component is larger than (5p/4).

### Theorem 2.2 (exact (p)-bounded menu)

Assume (p\ge2) and put

\[
 J(p)=\max\{j\ge0:k_j\le p\}.
\]

Then the number and shore mass of all (p)-bounded components are exactly

\[
 D_{r,p}=\sum_{j=0}^{\min(J(p),r-2)}C_{r-j-2},         \tag{2.9}
\]

\[
 V_{r,p}=\sum_{j=0}^{\min(J(p),r-2)}
                  (C_j+C_{j+1})C_{r-j-2}.             \tag{2.10}
\]

Reindexing the top strata by (d=r-j-2), a component has size

\[
 t_{r,d}=C_{r-d-2}+C_{r-d-1},qquad
 \text{multiplicity }C_d,                              \tag{2.11}
\]

and is selectable exactly when

\[
                 \theta\le\Theta_{r,d}:=\frac{C_r}{t_{r,d}},
                 \qquad
                 \Theta_{r,d}\longrightarrow\frac{4^{d+2}}5. \tag{2.12}
\]

For (r\ge6),

\[
                          C_{r-4}+C_{r-3}<\frac{C_r}{16}. \tag{2.13}
\]

Consequently (\theta<16) makes every component with (j\le r-4)
(p)-bounded.  Only the single (j=r-2) component and the single
(j=r-3) component can fail.  Uniformly in (\theta<16),

\[
 V_{r,p}\ge
 C_r-(C_{r-2}+C_{r-1})-(C_{r-3}+C_{r-2})
   =\left(\frac{39}{64}+o(1)\right)C_r.               \tag{2.14}
\]

#### Proof

Only (2.13) needs checking.  Since the consecutive Catalan quotient is
decreasing,

\[
 \frac{C_{r-3}}{C_r}<\frac5{126},qquad
 \frac{C_{r-4}}{C_{r-3}}\le\frac25
 \qquad(r\ge6).
\]

Hence

\[
 \frac{C_{r-4}+C_{r-3}}{C_r}
   <\frac5{126}\left(1+\frac25\right)
   =\frac1{18}<\frac1{16}.
\]

Monotonicity of (k_j) proves the first conclusion.  Subtracting the two
possibly unselectable instances from (2.4) gives (2.14), and the Catalan
quotients tend to powers of (1/4). \(\square\)

The limiting retained fractions, away from the two moving equality
thresholds, are

\[
 \begin{array}{c|c}
 \theta\text{ range}&V_{r,p}/C_r\\ \hline
 \theta<16/5&1+o(1)\\
 16/5\le\theta<64/5&11/16+o(1)\\
 64/5\le\theta<16&39/64+o(1).
 \end{array}                                           \tag{2.15}
\]

The exact finite thresholds are (\Theta_{r,0},\Theta_{r,1}), not their
limits.

There is also a trivial but sometimes useful general-(i) packet bound.
Every interaction component has at most (C_r) rows on a shore.  Thus the
choice

\[
                          r\le\lfloor\log_4p\rfloor     \tag{2.16}
\]

makes every component (p)-bounded, because

\[
                  C_r=\frac1{r+1}\binom{2r}{r}
                         \le\frac{4^r}{r+1}\le p.       \tag{2.17}
\]

This safe scale is below the overloaded scale and therefore does not by
itself split a (C_r>p) plateau.

### Proposition 2.3 (parent-child incidence coverage, not routed supply)

Embed the (C_r)-row aligned child fibre (10D_r) in the parent factor
(F_{r+1}).  Reindex parent components by (h=r-j).  A type-(h)
component has

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 &\text{packet size }C_{r-h}+C_{r-h+1},\\
 &\text{multiplicity }C_{h-1},\\
 &\text{child ports incident with the component }C_{r-h}.
 \end{aligned}                                         \tag{2.18}
\]

For (\theta=C_r/p<16), every type (h\ge4) is (p)-bounded for all
sufficiently large (r).  The number of child ports met by these components
is exactly

\[
       C_r-C_{r-1}-C_{r-2}-2C_{r-3}
          =\left(\frac{21}{32}+o(1)\right)C_r.          \tag{2.19}
\]

Their complete parent-row shore mass is

\[
       C_{r+1}-\bigl[(C_{r-1}+C_r)
          +(C_{r-2}+C_{r-1})
          +2(C_{r-3}+C_{r-2})\bigr]
          =\left(\frac{73}{128}+o(1)\right)C_{r+1}.     \tag{2.20}
\]

#### Proof

In (\mathcal A_j), the child ports are exactly the second summand
(10\,1v0), of cardinality (C_j=C_{r-h}).  The first summand is the
necessary companion shore material but is not in the child port set.
This proves (2.18).  The type-(4) packet size is
(C_{r-4}+C_{r-3}<C_r/16<p) by (2.13), and later types are smaller.
Subtracting the (h=1,2,3) child chunks, with multiplicities
(1,1,2), gives (2.19).  Subtracting their entire packet shores gives
(2.20). \(\square\)

Equations (2.19)--(2.20) are incidence counts only.  They do not say that
all covered child targets move, that they move to a common destination, or
that their full profiles have a favorable sign.  The source-reindex issue
in Section 7 is exactly why such a routing conclusion is invalid.

There is a second normalization correction.  If only the microscopic
parent components (j\le J) are used, their **parent-row** shore mass is
asymptotically (3/8) of (C_{r+1}), while the number of child ports they
meet is

\[
              M^{\rm child}_{r,J}
                =\sum_{j=0}^{J}C_jC_{r-j-1}.           \tag{2.21}
\]

Consequently, if first (r\to\infty) and then (J\to\infty),

\[
              \frac{M^{\rm child}_{r,J}}{C_r}
                 \longrightarrow
              \sum_{j\ge0}\frac{C_j}{4^{j+1}}
                 =\frac12.                             \tag{2.22}
\]

Thus a statement that the selectable menu moves (3/8) of an aligned
child plateau confuses parent packet-shore mass with child-port incidence.
The limiting microscopic menu meets (1/2) of the child ports, but no
(1/2)--(1/2) target split follows.  The companion rows in the first
summand of \(\mathcal A_j\) are indispensable for exactness, and port
reindexing couples their chronology back into the changed child rows.

## 3. Distinguished action versus complete target action

### 3.1 Marked occurrence projection

Let (K=K_{j,R}), and suppose every row of (K) carries one marked
occurrence whose old target is the same set (T), in a coordinate-equivariant
position.  Replacing the shore by its (\tau)-image sends every such target
to (\tau T).  Therefore

\[
 \boxed{
       \delta^{\rm mark}_{j,R}=k_j(e_{\tau T}-e_T).}    \tag{3.1}
\]

This formula is a marked projection.  It is zero when (\tau T=T).
It also changes if only a subset of the component rows belongs to the
marked fibre; Proposition 2.3 is the relevant parent example.

For a microscopic cutoff (J), switching every component with (j\le J)
moves exactly

\[
                 S_{r,J}=\sum_{j=0}^{J}
                     (C_j+C_{j+1})C_{r-j-2}            \tag{3.2}
\]

marked rows.  If (J\to\infty) and (J=o(r)), then

\[
                  \frac{S_{r,J}}{C_r}\longrightarrow\frac38. \tag{3.3}
\]

Hence one level produces the genuine marked two-bin split

\[
 C_re_T\longmapsto
       (C_r-S_{r,J})e_T+S_{r,J}e_{\tau T}
  =\left(\frac58+o(1)\right)C_re_T
   +\left(\frac38+o(1)\right)C_re_{\tau T}.           \tag{3.4}
\]

It switches whole components; no proper part of one (K_{j,R}) can be
chosen inside the support (F_r\cup\tau F_r).

### 3.2 Complete cyclic-window columns

Let (H_{j,R}^{(q)}) be the complete rank-(q) cyclic-window histogram of
the old component shore.  The new shore is its coordinate image, so at
every rank

\[
 \boxed{
             \Delta_{j,R}^{(q)}=(\tau-1)H_{j,R}^{(q)}.} \tag{3.5}
\]

At the two exact local ownership ranks this specializes to

\[
                    \Delta_{j,R}^{(r)}=0,qquad
                    \Delta_{j,R}^{(r+1)}=0,             \tag{3.6}
\]

because an interaction component is closed in both the (X)- and
(Y)-ledgers.

At the first lower rank, orient every nonfixed (\tau)-orbit by its member
(Q) containing (2) and not (3).  Then uniquely

\[
             \Delta_{j,R}^{(r-1)}
              =\sum_Qa_{j,R}(Q)(e_Q-e_{\tau Q}).        \tag{3.7}
\]

### Theorem 3.1 (forced reverse collateral)

For every component,

\[
                         \boxed{\sum_Qa_{j,R}(Q)=0}.     \tag{3.8}
\]

The proved colex-smallest target (P_{j,R}) has coefficient

\[
                         a_{j,R}(P_{j,R})=1.             \tag{3.9}
\]

Therefore all other arms have total oriented coefficient (-1).

#### Proof

Each component shore has (k_j) rows.  In the complete rank-((r-1))
cyclic histogram of one row, every physical coordinate belongs to exactly
the same number of windows, namely (r-1).  Hence the point-margin vector
of either shore is the constant vector (k_j(r-1)\mathbf1), and their
difference has zero point margins.

In (3.7), the point margin at coordinate (2) is exactly
(\sum_Qa_{j,R}(Q)), since (2\in Q\) and (2\notin\tau Q) for every
oriented representative.  It must vanish, proving (3.8).  Equation (3.9)
is the established colex-pivot theorem, and subtraction gives the last
claim. \(\square\)

For (j=0), the complete column is known exactly.  With (n=2r+1) and
the component-dependent ((r-3))-set (K_R),

\[
\begin{aligned}
 \Delta_{0,R}^{(r-1)}={}&
 e_{K_R\cup\{2,2r\}}-e_{K_R\cup\{3,2r\}}\\
 &-e_{K_R\cup\{2,n\}}+e_{K_R\cup\{3,n\}}.          \tag{3.10}
\end{aligned}
\]

Thus the smallest components already have one distinguished arm and one
oppositely oriented collateral arm.  Their four-target supports are
disjoint as (R) varies.  For (j>0), the complete collateral supports
and signs are not classified.

The full colex-pivot theorem is triangular across all pairs ((j,R)): the
pivots are distinct and every other target in a column is later than its
own pivot.  Consequently

\[
             \sum_{j,R}c_{j,R}\Delta_{j,R}^{(r-1)}=0
       \quad\Longrightarrow\quad c_{j,R}=0
       \text{ for every }(j,R).                       \tag{3.10a}
\]

In particular, no nonempty selection of the fixed (s_1)-components has
zero complete first-shadow action.  Componentwise collateral may collide,
but it cannot cancel the whole selected column sum inside this one
hierarchy.

Complementation gives the first upper column:

\[
                 \Delta_{j,R}^{(r+2)}(S^c)
                    =\Delta_{j,R}^{(r-1)}(S).           \tag{3.11}
\]

If the lower pivot is (e_P-e_{\tau P}) with (2\in P,3\notin P), the
upper pivot is (e_{P^c}-e_{\tau P^c}), where (3\in P^c,2\notin P^c).
The paired ranks are therefore counter-routed across the same coordinate
cut.  A calculation which sends their two distinguished loads to the same
child has the wrong sign.

Equations (3.5)--(3.11) prove that (3.1) cannot be substituted for the
complete PCap change.

## 4. The exact six-level arithmetic, conditionally

This section audits the proposed recurrence for a marked fibre containing
all (C_m) roots of the same local factor, as in (3.1)--(3.4).  It is not
the parent-child incidence count of (2.21): that menu meets asymptotically
one half of the child ports, but its target split is not determined by the
count.

The microscopic (3/8) mass admits an exact finite tail calculation.

### Lemma 4.1 (Catalan tail identity)

Put

\[
 p_J=\sum_{j=0}^{J}\frac{C_j+C_{j+1}}{4^{j+2}}.
\]

Then

\[
 \boxed{
 p_J=\frac38-\delta_J,qquad
 \delta_J=\frac{5J+8}{8}\frac{C_{J+1}}{4^{J+1}}.}    \tag{4.1}
\]

Moreover, for (m>J+1),

\[
             \frac{S_{m,J}}{C_m}>p_J.                 \tag{4.2}
\]

#### Proof

The Catalan generating function gives

\[
 \sum_{j\ge0}\frac{C_j+C_{j+1}}{4^{j+2}}
 =\frac1{16}\left(2+4(2-1)\right)=\frac38.          \tag{4.3}
\]

The displayed expression for the tail follows by induction on (J),
using (C_{J+2}=2(2J+3)C_{J+1}/(J+3)); it is correct at (J=0), and the
difference of consecutive tails is exactly
((C_{J+1}+C_{J+2})/4^{J+3}).

Finally

\[
                    \frac{C_{a-1}}{C_a}
                       =\frac{a+1}{2(2a-1)}>\frac14.
\]

Multiplying these strict inequalities gives
(C_{m-j-2}/C_m>4^{-j-2}), and summing proves (4.2). \(\square\)

The elementary central-binomial estimate

\[
                    \frac1{4^a}\binom{2a}{a}
                         \le\frac1{\sqrt{a+1}}          \tag{4.4}
\]

follows by induction, because the quotient of consecutive left sides is
((2a+1)/(2a+2)).  Applying (4.4) to (4.1) gives

\[
                         \delta_J\le\frac5{8\sqrt{J+2}}. \tag{4.5}
\]

Thus the explicit choice

\[
                              J\ge25598                \tag{4.6}
\]

ensures (\delta_J\le1/256).  For this fixed (J), and all sufficiently
large local dimensions (m), one also has

\[
        \frac38-\frac1{256}
          \le\frac{S_{m,J}}{C_m}
          \le\frac38+\frac1{256}.                     \tag{4.7}
\]

The upper bound is ordinary convergence for a fixed finite sum.

### Conditional Lemma 4.2 (six recursive canonical peels)

Suppose a marked fibre of initial mass (M_0=C_m<16p) admits a binary
routing tree of depth six with the following properties at every active
node.

1.  The node is a canonical Catalan port fibre of sufficiently large local
    dimension.
2.  Its (j\le J) (s_1)-components are legal packets in the current
    factor, each of size at most (p).
3.  Switching them divides the marked node mass according to (4.7), and
    sends the two parts to distinct target labels.
4.  The target labels of all depth-six leaves are globally distinct; more
    generally, any labels which reconverge have a separately proved total
    load at most (p).
5.  All node packets compose exactly, and the complete collateral profiles
    satisfy their capacity constraints.

Then every depth-six marked leaf, and hence every resulting marked target
load, has mass less than (p).

#### Proof

Both children have mass at most

\[
              \rho M,qquad
              \rho=\frac58+\frac1{256}=\frac{161}{256}. \tag{4.8}
\]

Now

\[
       161^3=4\,173\,281<4\,194\,304=\frac{256^3}{4}.
\]

Therefore (\rho^3<1/4), and hence (\rho^6<1/16).  Every final mass is
strictly smaller than

\[
                           \frac{C_m}{16}<p.            \tag{4.9}
\]

All masses are integers, so there is no adverse floor term. \(\square\)

This proves the numerical recurrence with exact constants.  It does not
prove any of hypotheses 1--5 after the first level.  Sibling separation
alone would be insufficient, because labels from different branches could
reconverge and add their formal leaf masses.  In particular,
(C_m/p<16) says only that at most sixteen ideal capacity bins would be
enough if arbitrary routing were available.  It does not produce sixteen
reachable target labels or a partition into at most sixteen legal packets.

## 5. Exact composition of chronology-disjoint rooted packets

There is a positive integral composition theorem, but it is a theorem
about phase support, not merely coordinate support.

For a rooted row (C), let (\mathbf a(C)) be its complete incidence
column in the (X\)- and (Y\)-ownership ledgers.  Let (A) and (B) be
two rooted slab transformations, each fixing its two boundary (X)-states
rowwise and having zero aggregate signed (X/Y) incidence.  On a row on
which both are defined, denote the four variants by

\[
                         C^{00},C^{10},C^{01},C^{11}.    \tag{5.1}
\]

### Theorem 5.1 (phase-disjoint cubical composition)

Assume the state positions changed by (A) and (B) are disjoint on every
common row, and that they share no changed (Y)-cut; their slabs may meet
at a boundary state fixed by both.  Then

\[
 \boxed{
 \mathbf a(C^{11})+\mathbf a(C^{00})
   =\mathbf a(C^{10})+\mathbf a(C^{01})}              \tag{5.2}
\]

row by row.  Consequently each transformation remains legal after the
other, they commute, and any finite family satisfying the same condition
composes to an exact rooted port factor.

#### Proof

Outside the two slabs all four rows agree.  Inside the (A)-slab the
(B)-bit is invisible, and inside the (B)-slab the (A)-bit is
invisible.  Boundary fixation ensures the same statement at the two gluing
edges.  This proves (5.2) target by target.

Rearranging (5.2) shows that the signed incidence effect of (B) after
(A) is exactly its effect before (A).  Summed over the rows of a legal
(B)-packet, this effect is zero.  The same holds with (A,B) reversed.
All Johnson adjacencies are local to one legal slab or one fixed boundary,
and the ports never change.  Induction proves the finite-family claim.
\(\square\)

Crossing row supports are therefore harmless when the chronology supports
are disjoint.  For example, the two contextual root pairs

\[
 \{11001100,10101100\},qquad
 \{11001100,11001010\}                                \tag{5.3}
\]

share one row.  One must not add their two original whole-column trade
vectors, which would give coefficient (-2) on that row.  Sequential
composition instead replaces it by the single row carrying both disjoint
slab modifications, and (5.2) supplies the higher-order correction.

### Lemma 5.1A (incomparable tree holes are chronology-disjoint)

In a canonical MSW trace, the states belonging to one rooted subtree form
one contiguous phase slab, possibly read in reverse.  Hence slabs belonging
to incomparable tree nodes are disjoint.

#### Proof

For Dyck words (P,Q), the exact concatenation identity is

\[
 P(PQ)=P(P)Q\ \Vert\
       \overline P\bigl(P(Q)\setminus\{Q\}\bigr).     \tag{5.3a}
\]

Thus every flip in (P) precedes every flip in (Q), and both sets of
flips occupy contiguous intervals.  Wrapping a word by (1(\cdot)0)
adds the two outer flips and sends the inner flip word to one shifted
reversed interval.  Induction down the ordered recursion tree proves the
first assertion.  Two incomparable nodes have disjoint coordinate sets;
their contiguous flip intervals therefore cannot overlap. \(\square\)

It follows that packets in incomparable rooted tree holes fall under
Theorem 5.1.  With a full Cartesian family of spectator port fillings, the
local choices form an exact rooted cube.  This proves port legality and
both ownership ledgers for a genuine tensor of finitely many disjoint
holes.

It does not prove that one (D_r)-fibre has a dense tiling by six such
Cartesian cylinders, nor that their marked targets expose six active
coordinate pairs.

### Theorem 5.2 (the mixed-cut obstruction)

Suppose (A) changes a phase-(t) state (L_u\to L'_u), while (B)
changes the adjacent phase-((t+1)) state (R_u\to R'_u).  Individual
legality tests only the cross pairs

\[
                           L'_u\sim_JR_u,qquad
                           L_u\sim_JR'_u.               \tag{5.4}
\]

Their composition additionally requires

\[
                           L'_u\sim_JR'_u               \tag{5.5}
\]

for every affected row and the vanishing of the mixed colour defect

\[
 \boxed{
 \Theta_{AB}=\sum_u\bigl(
 e_{L'_u\cup R'_u}-e_{L'_u\cup R_u}
 -e_{L_u\cup R'_u}+e_{L_u\cup R_u}\bigr)=0.}          \tag{5.6}
\]

#### Proof

Equation (5.5) is the new Johnson edge.  Subtract the four possible
ownership columns at their common (Y)-cut.  Every target away from that
cut cancels cubically; the uncancelled second difference is exactly
(5.6).  Thus (5.5)--(5.6) are necessary, and together with the two
individual ledgers they are sufficient at the shared cut. \(\square\)

Distinct coordinate pairs do not imply (5.5)--(5.6).  Overlapping or
nested chronology therefore needs a synchronized higher packet, not six
independently imported one-hole theorems.

## 6. Why the recursive component premise is still missing

The exact hierarchy (2.1)--(2.3) is a theorem for the canonical endpoint
pair

\[
                              F_r,\qquad s_1F_r.         \tag{6.1}
\]

After switching a proper collection of its components, the current factor
is a hybrid.  Its overlay with a second transposition must be recomputed;
there is no theorem that its components retain the Catalan law or that a
(3/8) shore survives in every conditional branch.

For a shifted transposition (s_i), the established contextual theorem
does give sealed size-two components

\[
                     P1100R\longleftrightarrow P1010R,  \tag{6.2}
\]

in number (C_{i-1}C_{r-i-1}).  It does not give a complete analogue of
(2.3).  Conjugating the whole canonical factor transfers (2.3) to a whole
conjugate factor, but a partial hybrid is not such a conjugate.

Theorem 5.1 removes this objection only when later packets occupy unchanged,
chronology-disjoint canonical slabs.  To obtain the proposed routing tree
one would still need a theorem which partitions every active row branch
into such slabs with the canonical Catalan proportions.  No such atlas is
currently proved.

## 7. Source reindexing and the exact scope of orbit arguments

The port normalization in Theorem 1.1 is load-bearing.  For an involution
(s=s_i), the row on the right shore having port (u) is

\[
                              \widetilde C_u=sC_{su},   \tag{7.0}
\]

not (sC_u).  Its source is (w=s^{-1}u=su).  This distinction disappears
when one sums a complete profile over an (s)-stable root block, but it
does not disappear after projecting to a marked subset of ports.

For one canonical (\tau)-overlay, let (u_K(S)) be the complete old profile
of a component whose source/root block satisfies (\tau K=K), and choose a shore bit
(\varepsilon_K\in\{0,1\}).  The resulting profile is exactly

\[
 \mu_\varepsilon(S)=\sum_K
 \begin{cases}
 u_K(S),&\varepsilon_K=0,\\
 u_K(\tau S),&\varepsilon_K=1.
 \end{cases}                                          \tag{7.0a}
\]

Therefore

\[
 \mu_\varepsilon(S)+\mu_\varepsilon(\tau S)
   =\mu_F(S)+\mu_F(\tau S),                            \tag{7.0b}
\]

and the componentwise common core is immovable:

\[
 \boxed{
 \mu_\varepsilon(S)\ge
       \sum_K\min\{u_K(S),u_K(\tau S)\}.}             \tag{7.0c}
\]

Thus even a two-point orbit with enough aggregate capacity may have a
one-sided floor caused by profile mass present on both shores of every
component.

More generally, let (\Gamma) be generated by (k) disjoint coordinate
transpositions and let (O) be a target orbit.  Suppose, as an additional
hypothesis, that every component source block used in the proposed sequence
is stable under the relevant generator, so that its **complete** profile
has invariant orbit total.  Then

\[
                             M_O=\sum_{S\in O}\mu(S)    \tag{7.1}
\]

is invariant.  This hypothesis holds for the fixed canonical (s_1) cube.
It is not automatic after passing to a hybrid factor or projecting to a
marked root subset.

### Lemma 7.1 (conditional exact orbit-capacity floor)

For an orbit (O),

\[
 \boxed{
   \sum_{S\in O}(\mu(S)-p)_+
       \ge (M_O-p|O|)_+.}                              \tag{7.2}
\]

If (O) splits exactly (c) of the (k) coordinate pairs, then

\[
                              |O|=2^c.                  \tag{7.3}
\]

#### Proof

For each (S), (\mu(S)\le p+(\mu(S)-p)_+).  Summing over (O) and
rearranging proves (7.2).  A transposition acts nontrivially on (S)
exactly when (S) contains one endpoint of its pair.  The (c) active
involutions act freely and independently, proving (7.3). \(\square\)

This is a serious condition for complete stable-block profiles.  A marked
projection cannot be inserted into it until stability of its marked port
set and equivariance of its occurrence map have separately been proved.

For later use, let \(\Phi_u(C)\) denote the target of the designated
aligned window when column \(C\) is installed at port \(u\).  The exact
marked action of a component \(K\) on a marked port set \(\mathcal B\) is

\[
 \boxed{
 \Delta^{\rm mark}_{K,\mathcal B}
   =\sum_{u\in K\cap\mathcal B}
       \left(e_{\Phi_u(sC_{su})}-e_{\Phi_u(C_u)}\right).} \tag{7.3a}
\]

Formula (3.1) is the special case in which every source row is marked and
\(\Phi_u(sC_{su})=sT\) is independent of \(u\).  Equation (7.3a) is the
formula required for the aligned child projection.

### Theorem 7.2 (the aligned marked-orbit claim is false)

Let the aligned child port set in the parent be

\[
                              \mathcal B=10D_r.         \tag{7.4}
\]

It is not (s_1)-stable.  For every (R\in D_{r-1}),

\[
                   1010R\in\mathcal B,\qquad
                   s_1(1010R)=1100R\notin\mathcal B.   \tag{7.5}
\]

The corresponding size-two component is the certified parent rectangle.
On its child row, the aligned targets change as

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 K_{\rm ext}\cup\{2\}
     &\longrightarrow K_{\rm ext}\cup\{4\},\\
 K_{\rm ext}\cup(J_{\rm loc}\setminus\{1\})
     &\longrightarrow
       K_{\rm ext}\cup(J_{\rm loc}\setminus\{3\}).
 \end{aligned}                                         \tag{7.6}
\]

Direct coordinate action by (s_1=(2\ 3)) would instead send the first
target to (K_{\rm ext}\cup\{3\}) and fix the second.  Hence the aligned
marked action is not a coordinate-orbit action.

#### Proof

Equation (7.5) is the literal swap of bits two and three.  The roots
(1100R,1010R) form the certified (j=0) interaction component.  The exact
parent rectangle calculation gives (7.6): the child intersection changes
from local coordinate (2) to (4), while the child union changes from the
complement of (1) to the complement of (3).  These are not the direct
coordinate images described above. \(\square\)

The mechanism is exact.  A component packet cannot switch the child arm
(10\,1v0R) without its companion primitive arm (1u0R).  Port reindexing
sends source chronology from one arm into the row occupying a port in the
other arm.  Thus the child-port counts (2.19) and (2.21) are not target-flow
formulas.

For (j>0), the complete aligned lower/upper marked action of (K_{j,R}) has
not been computed.  The current record therefore proves neither that six
different local target coordinates give a recursive capacity split nor
that a small coordinate orbit obstructs such a split.  The exact remaining
task is to compute these reindexed component actions, including their
companion-row and full-profile collateral, and then test their chronology
composition by Section 5.

## 8. Final proved/conditional boundary

The following statements are proved.

1.  The full overlay components are the equivalence classes generated by
    the normalized owner maps (1.6), with all (X)- and (Y)-phases
    included.
2.  The exact (s_1) component sizes, multiplicities, maximal packet, and
    (p)-cutoff are (2.3), (2.7), and (2.9)--(2.15).
3.  A common marked occurrence is split componentwise by (3.1), while the
    complete shadow column is (3.5) and has the forced collateral (3.8).
4.  Under an explicit recursive-canonical hypothesis, six splits suffice
    with the exact finite coefficient (161/256).
5.  Chronology-disjoint rooted packets compose integrally by Theorem 5.1;
    a shared (Y)-cut has the exact obstruction (5.5)--(5.6).
6.  Port reindexing has the exact form (7.0).  The aligned child port set
    is not (s_1)-stable, and the certified size-two rectangle has the
    non-orbit marked action (7.6).

The following statements remain unproved and must not be inferred.

1.  A (3/8) component hierarchy for general (s_i).
2.  Persistence of the hierarchy after a partial hybrid switch.
3.  A dense six-hole chronology-disjoint atlas covering every active
    Catalan branch.
4.  Capacity-safe routing of all collateral arms in (3.7), even in a
    hypothetical six-pair target embedding.
5.  The complete aligned marked action of (K_{j,R}) for (j>0), and global
    separation of the leaf target labels in the six-level recurrence.
6.  Any coefficient-one consequence.

The sharp verdict is therefore:

> One canonical (s_1) overlay genuinely splits an all-row, source-equivariant
> marked plateau by whole component packets.  That formula does not project
> to the aligned child subset: source reindexing imports the companion arm,
> and already the size-two rectangle moves both aligned targets outside
> their naive coordinate images.  The six-level numerical recurrence and
> the chronology-disjoint composition theorem are correct, but the proposed
> routing tree remains conditional on the uncomputed reindexed component
> actions and their full-profile collateral.  No aligned orbit no-go is
> proved.
