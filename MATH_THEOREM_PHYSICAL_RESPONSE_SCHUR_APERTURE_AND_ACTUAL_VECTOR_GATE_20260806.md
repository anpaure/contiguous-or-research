\tag{1.1}

\[
 q_b=g_A-g_{\tau A},
 \qquad

\tag{1.2}

where \(w_b\) is the literal hypothetical-next first-entry coefficient.
Put

\[
 W=\operatorname {diag}(w_b),
 \qquad

\tag{1.3}
\]

The complete-row theorem controls

\[
                         \|q\|_W^2=\sum_bw_bq_b^2.
\tag{1.4}
\]

The direct chi-square theorem needs to control

\[
                         \|Rq\|_{(D')^{-1}}^2.
\tag{1.5}
\]

The strongest convenient condition is the physical operator aperture

\[
 R^*(D')^{-1}R
 \preceq \Lambda W,
 \qquad
 \Lambda=C(1-a_i){\beta_T(i)d\over X_i}.
\tag{1.6}
\]

For `GDIR`, however, it is enough to prove (1.6) only on the one vector
\(q=q_i\) generated by the current stopped state:

\[
 \boxed{
 \mathbb E_i\|R_i^\omega q_i\|_{(D_i'^\omega)^{-1}}^2
 \le C(1-a_i){\beta_T(i)d\over X_i}\|q_i\|_{W_i}^2.}
\tag{AVA}

This **actual-vector aperture** is strictly weaker than operator
domination and is the weakest quadratic statement needed after the
decomposition (1.1).

## 2. Weighted Schur aperture

Let \(R=(r_{yb})\) be any real response matrix.  Choose positive numbers
\(s_{yb}\) whenever \(r_{yb}\ne0\), and nonnegative majorants
\(u_{yb}\ge|r_{yb}|\).  Define

\[
 A=\max_y{1\over c'_y}\sum_bu_{yb}s_{yb},
 \qquad
 B=\max_b{1\over w_b}\sum_y{u_{yb}\over s_{yb}},
\tag{2.1}
\]

with the convention that a zero-weight column must have zero response.

### Theorem 2.1 (coefficient-faithful Schur test)

One has

\[
 \boxed{R^*(D')^{-1}R\preceq ABW.}
\tag{2.2}
\]

#### Proof

For every output coordinate \(y\), weighted Cauchy--Schwarz gives

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \left(\sum_br_{yb}q_b\right)^2
 &\le
 \left(\sum_bu_{yb}s_{yb}\right)
 \left(\sum_b{u_{yb}\over s_{yb}}q_b^2\right)\\
 &\le A c'_y
 \sum_b{u_{yb}\over s_{yb}}q_b^2.
 \end{aligned}
\]

Divide by \(c'_y\), sum over \(y\), and use the second inequality in
(2.1):

\[
 \|Rq\|_{(D')^{-1}}^2
 \le A\sum_bq_b^2\sum_y{u_{yb}\over s_{yb}}
 \le AB\sum_bw_bq_b^2.
\]

This proves (2.2). \(\square\)

Thus the physical response aperture follows from

\[
 \boxed{
 \left(\max_y{\sum_bu_{yb}s_{yb}\over c'_y}\right)
 \left(\max_b{\sum_yu_{yb}/s_{yb}\over w_b}\right)
 \le C(1-a_i){\beta_T(i)d\over X_i}.}
\tag{SCHA}

The factors have literal meanings:

* the first is output-resource congestion relative to the actual next
  reference mass;
* the second is the total response exported by one first-entry boundary
  relative to its exact boundary coefficient.

There is no orbit scalar and no rescaling freedom.

### Corollary 2.2 (unweighted claim test)

If a nonnegative claim matrix \(u\) satisfies

\[
 \sum_bu_{yb}\le A c'_y,
 \qquad
 \sum_yu_{yb}\le B w_b,
\tag{2.3}
\]

then (2.2) holds with coefficient \(AB\).  This is Theorem 2.1 with
\(s_{yb}=1\).

The weighted form is important.  It permits a large output claim to be
balanced by a small input claim, or conversely, instead of requiring two
separate pointwise uniform bounds.

## 3. Actual-vector completion theorem

Let \(h_i=1-a_i\), and suppose the literal decomposition (1.1) satisfies
`(AVA)` and

\[
 \mathbb E_i\|r_i^\omega\|_{(D_i'^\omega)^{-1}}^2
 \le\eta_0h_i^2\Psi_i+h_iJ_i^{\rm root/two},
 \qquad \eta_0<\frac18.
\tag{3.1}
\]

Then, by \(\|u+v\|^2\le2\|u\|^2+2\|v\|^2\),

\[
 H_i\le
 2C h_i{\beta_Td\over X_i}\mathcal E_i^{\rm row}
 +2\eta_0h_i^2\Psi_i
 +2h_iJ_i^{\rm root/two}.
\tag{3.2}
\]

The service coefficient is \(\eta=2\eta_0<1/4\).  Therefore the direct
chi-square theorem and complete-row blocker conjugation give

\[
 \boxed{
 \mathbb E\sum_{i<\tau}{\mathfrak G_T(i)\over
                              \beta_T(i)X_i}
 \le C\left(\Psi_0+\mathsf A+
       \mathbb E\sum_{i<\tau}J_i^{\rm root/two}\right).}
\tag{3.3}

This theorem requires neither a normalized auxiliary generator nor an
all-vector Johnson-sector inequality.

   \tag{4.1}

   with the uniform transposition factor included when averaging over
   coordinate switches.
2. The complete-row polarization retains the full changed-resource set

   \mathbb E\sum_i{\beta_Td\over X_i}\sum_bw_bq_b^2.
   \tag{4.2}

## 5. Why the available aggregate bounds do not imply Schur aperture

The small potential cap gives

\[
 \beta_T\le C\varepsilon_P/d^2,
 \qquad
 \sum_xg_x^\circ=\beta_T\sum_xY_x.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Good loads give \(c_0\le Y_x\le C_0\).  Neither statement bounds the
output-resource congestion in (2.1).  All selected-relation mass may, at
the level of these two scalar hypotheses, be concentrated at one output
resource.

Likewise, an \(O(d)\) support bound for one switch controls only the number
of nonzero entries in one column of \(R\).  It does not control their
coefficient relative to \(w_b\), nor the number of different boundary
columns which feed the same output.

### Proposition 5.1 (support-one obstruction)

For every \(0<\lambda<1\), there is a response matrix with one nonzero
entry, \(c'=w=1\), for which

\[
                         R^*(D')^{-1}R\npreceq\lambda W.
\tag{5.2}
\]

#### Proof

Take \(R=(1)\).  Then both quadratic forms on the left and \(W\) equal
one, so (5.2) fails whenever \(\lambda<1\). \(\square\)

Thus sparse switch support alone cannot produce the small coefficient

\[
 (1-a_i){\beta_Td\over X_i}asymp{\beta_Td\over X_i^2}.
\tag{5.3}
\]

The smallness must occur in the literal response coefficient itself.

### Proposition 5.2 (trace does not imply aperture)

For every \(n\), there is an \(n\)-input response with

\[
 {1\over n}\operatorname {tr}igl(R^*R\bigr)={1\over n},
 \qquad
 \|R^*R\|_{2\to2}=1.
\tag{5.4}
\]

#### Proof

Take one output and put \(r_{1b}=n^{-1/2}\) for all \(b\).  Then
\(R^*R=n^{-1}{\bf1}{\bf1}^*\), which has trace one and operator norm one.
\(\square\)

This is the response-level version of concentration on one Johnson star.
It shows why orbit-average or trace normalization cannot replace either
`(AVA)` or `(SCHA)`.

## 6. The weakest proof-safe FIFO target

**Correction after literal expansion.**  The subsequent authoritative
identity is
`MATH_THEOREM_LITERAL_FIFO_DIAGONAL_CHISQUARE_UPDATE_AND_ADJOINT_FOURTH_ORDER_GATE_20260806.md`.
It shows that the full normalized-incidence residual need not factor
through the current complete-row score vector: even at `q=0`, a killed
row can create next-state incidence mismatch.  Thus the `Rq+r`
factorization below is only a sufficient decomposition for the portion
which genuinely depends on current switch scores; it is not the native
FIFO identity.  The native residual is the adjoint carré (5.2) of that
later theorem.

The all-vector Schur test is convenient but not necessary.  For any
response portion admitting such a factorization, one may prove one of the
following, in increasing order of strength:

1. the actual-vector inequality `(AVA)` for the one stopped profile;
2. a weighted claim ledger `(SCHA)` for its literal response matrix; or
3. the full operator aperture (1.6).

The first is the weakest and is fully sufficient.  It allows cancellations
specific to the actual selected-relation profile and does not impose a
pointwise likelihood cap.

One sufficient expansion has the form

\[
 \boxed{
 e'-(J^{\rm physical})^*f
 =\sum_{b=(A,\tau,G)}R_{\cdot b}
       (g_A-g_{\tau A})+r^{\rm root/two},}
\tag{6.1}

with all of the following fixed literally:

* the accepted transition probability occurs once;
* \(\mu_i^+(A;G)\) is the same hypothetical-next coefficient as in
  complete-row blocker conjugation;
* repeated occurrences retain their multiplicities;
* common numerator/reference motion is put in \(J^{\rm physical}\); and
* the residual rooted/two-entry terms keep their existing `ROc`/`FE3`
  coefficients.

The literal update theorem shows that (6.1) cannot cover the full response
without a separate innovation residual.  Theorem 2.1 reduces any genuine
`Rq` subface to two explicit sums of absolute literal coefficients; the
remaining innovation must be bounded in its native adjoint form.

## 7. Proof boundary

The weighted Schur theorem proves a coefficient-faithful route from a
literal response expansion to `GDIR`.  It also proves that the presently
known facts—exact boundary weights, full-row transport, small total
\(\beta_T\), good root loads, and \(O(d)\) switch support—do not by
themselves establish the required small aperture.

For the switch-dependent subface, the live row is (6.1), followed by
either `(AVA)` or `(SCHA)`.  For the full FIFO response, the later literal
diagonal theorem supersedes this frontier by displaying the additional
adjoint survivor carré.  Both formulations are immune to arbitrary
conductance rescaling.
