# Pair-radius de Bruijn states, the exact radius-drift identity, and a dense-reset obstruction

Date: 2026-07-26

Method: pure mathematics only. No finite search, computation, solver, or web
input is used.

## 0. Outcome

Fix a perfect matching \(\Pi\) of the \(2m\) physical coordinates. The
natural bounded-state attempt to realize the pair-radius bonus menus is the
safe de Bruijn graph whose physical transitions are pair flips, augmented by
quota-copy labels which are allowed to reset at every floor crossing. This
note proves that this attempt cannot realize the prescribed columns, even
approximately at the aggregate scale needed in the Gaussian window.

A Johnson transition deleting \(a\) and inserting \(b\) is a
\(\Pi\)-pair flip when \(b\) is the mate of \(a\), and is
\(\Pi\)-breaking otherwise. Let \(E_\Pi(P)\) be the number of pair-breaking
transitions in an \(H\)-safe owner permutation \(P\). At depth \(q\), let
\(\mu_q^-\) be its lower trace histogram and let \(\nu_q^-\) be any exactly
rounded pair-radius table: the floor \(c_q\) on every target, plus the extra
unit on the \(\rho_q\) targets of smallest pair radius. Then

\[
 qE_\Pi(P)+{m-q\over4}\|\mu_q^--\nu_q^-\|_1
 \ \ge\ 
 {Wq(2m-q-1)\over2(2m-1)}.                         \tag{0.1}
\]

The identical inequality holds for upper traces after complementation. It
is robust under an arbitrary corrected common-run table \(\tau_q^-\): one
replaces the norm in (0.1) by

\[
 \|\mu_q^--\tau_q^-\|_1+\|\tau_q^--\nu_q^-\|_1.   \tag{0.2}
\]

Consequently, putting

\[
 S_H=\sum_{q=1}^H{q\over m-q},\qquad
 a_H={2m-H-1\over2(2m-1)},                         \tag{0.3}
\]

and summing all lower-depth discrepancies and common-run corrections into
\(D_{\le H}\), one has the exact bound

\[
 \boxed{
 E_\Pi(P)\ge Wa_H-{D_{\le H}\over4S_H}.}           \tag{0.4}
\]

In particular, if \(H=o(m)\), \(H/\sqrt m\) is bounded away from zero,
and \(D_{\le H}=o(W)\), then

\[
                         E_\Pi(P)\ge(1/2-o(1))W.    \tag{0.5}
\]

If the physical transitions are all pair flips, then

\[
 D_{\le H}\ge4Wa_HS_H
              =(1+o(1)){WH^2\over m}.             \tag{0.6}
\]

Thus quota-level reset colours do not repair the pair-flip de Bruijn
construction: labels do not change \(E_\Pi(P)\). If a reset colour is
instead interpreted as a genuinely pair-breaking physical transition, at
least half of all physical transitions must carry such a colour in the
Gaussian window. The pair-radius construction then ceases to be a sparse
reset perturbation and has returned to the unrestricted Johnson chronology
problem.

This is a stable obstruction beyond the finite \(m=H=2\) permutation-square
example. It is a second-order pair statistic invisible to the common point
run vector. It does not rule out a construction using a positive density of
pair-breaking transitions, and it does not by itself settle the required
cycle count.

The conclusion is unchanged for a partial owner circulation. If its owner
leave is \(L=o(W/H)\) and its aggregate column discrepancy is \(o(W)\),
then (0.5) still holds for every \(H=\Omega(\sqrt m)\), \(H=o(m)\); see
Corollary 4.2.

## 1. The exact labelled de Bruijn attempt

Write \(\bar v\) for the mate of \(v\) under \(\Pi\), and put

\[
 \Omega=\binom{[2m]}m,\qquad W=|\Omega|.
\]

A physical \((H-1)\)-memory state is a safe Johnson path

\[
             \sigma=(X_0,X_1,\ldots,X_{H-1}).       \tag{1.1}
\]

If the transition \(X_{i-1}\to X_i\) deletes \(a_i\) and inserts \(b_i\),
safety means that the deletion letters are distinct, the insertion letters
are distinct, and no deletion letter is an insertion letter in the same
window. Equivalently, to append

\[
 X_H=X_{H-1}-a_H+b_H,                               \tag{1.2}
\]

one must choose

\[
 a_H\in X_{H-1}\setminus\{b_1,\ldots,b_{H-1}\},
 \qquad
 b_H\in X_{H-1}^c\setminus\{a_1,\ldots,a_{H-1}\}.  \tag{1.3}
\]

The physical safe de Bruijn graph \(\mathfrak D_H\) has the states (1.1)
as vertices and the safe length-\(H\) paths as shift edges

\[
 (X_0,\ldots,X_{H-1})\longrightarrow
 (X_1,\ldots,X_H).                                  \tag{1.4}
\]

The proposed reset augmentation replaces a physical state \(\sigma\) by
\((\sigma,\ell)\), where \(\ell\) may record the quota level, the top-copy
label, the current floor-reset epoch, the pair-radius cell, and any finite
additional controller state. A labelled transition must still project to
(1.4). An integral root-transversal Eulerian selection is a zero-one
selection of labelled edges such that:

1. exactly one selected edge starts at each physical root \(X_0\);
2. selected indegree equals selected outdegree at every labelled memory;
3. after forgetting labels, the signed target-column counts have their
   prescribed values.

The projection theorem for memory circulations says that such a selection
is generated by one physical permutation \(P\) of \(\Omega\): its edge at
root \(X\) is

\[
                    (X,PX,\ldots,P^HX).             \tag{1.5}
\]

In particular, Eulerizing the full labelled graph only gives a multicover;
it is not a root-transversal realization. Reset labels do not alter the
physical suffix equations in (1.5).

Call the physical edge \(X\to PX=X-a+b\) a **pair flip** if

\[
                              b=\bar a,              \tag{1.6}
\]

and a **pair-breaking reset** otherwise. Let

\[
 E_\Pi(P)=\#\{X:b(X)\ne\overline{a(X)}\}.           \tag{1.7}
\]

This physical use of the word reset must be distinguished from a quota-copy
reset in the label \(\ell\). A label reset may occur on a pair flip and then
does not contribute to (1.7).

## 2. Exact suffix radius identities

For any set \(T\), let

\[
                         d_\Pi(T)=\#\{M\in\Pi:M\subseteq T\}.  \tag{2.1}
\]

For a root \(X\), write

\[
 A_q(X)=\{a_1(X),\ldots,a_q(X)\},\qquad
 B_q(X)=\{b_1(X),\ldots,b_q(X)\}.                 \tag{2.2}
\]

Then the signed traces are

\[
 L_q(X)=X\setminus A_q(X),\qquad
 U_q(X)=X\cup B_q(X).                              \tag{2.3}
\]

### Lemma 2.1 (lower radius loss is charged to physical resets)

For every \(q\le H\),

\[
 d_\Pi(L_q(X))=d_\Pi(X)-F_q(X),                    \tag{2.4}
\]

where

\[
 F_q(X)=\#\{M\in\Pi:M\subseteq X,\ M\cap A_q(X)\ne\varnothing\}.
                                                               \tag{2.5}
\]

Moreover, every pair counted by \(F_q(X)\) has a distinct first deletion
occurring on a pair-breaking reset edge. Hence

\[
 F_q(X)\le\#\{1\le i\le q:P^{i-1}X\to P^iX
                       \text{ is pair-breaking}\}.             \tag{2.6}
\]

#### Proof

The lower trace is a subset of \(X\). A pair full in \(X\) remains full in
\(L_q(X)\) exactly when neither endpoint is deleted, which gives (2.4).
For a pair hit by \(A_q(X)\), consider its first deleted endpoint \(a_i\).
Its mate is still present in \(P^{i-1}X\). Since an inserted coordinate is
outside the current owner, \(b_i\) cannot be that mate. Thus the edge is
pair-breaking. Distinct hit pairs have distinct first-hit edges, proving
(2.6). \(\square\)

Because \(P\) is a permutation, every physical edge occurs in exactly
\(q\) rooted forward \(q\)-windows. Summing Lemma 2.1 gives

\[
 \boxed{
 \sum_{X\in\Omega}d_\Pi(L_q(X))
 \ge \sum_{X\in\Omega}d_\Pi(X)-qE_\Pi(P).}         \tag{2.7}
\]

There is an exact complementary statement. Put

\[
                         K_q(X)=[2m]\setminus U_q(X)
                                =X^c\setminus B_q(X).           \tag{2.8}
\]

At the middle rank, \(d_\Pi(X^c)=d_\Pi(X)\), because the number of full
pairs equals the number of empty pairs. Applying the first-insertion version
of the proof above yields

\[
 \boxed{
 \sum_Xd_\Pi(K_q(X))
 \ge \sum_Xd_\Pi(X)-qE_\Pi(P).}                  \tag{2.9}
\]

Indeed, the first insertion into a pair empty in \(X\) cannot be a pair
flip: immediately before that insertion its mate is absent. Equations
(2.7) and (2.9) are the exact suffix constraints used below.

## 3. The pair-radius column moment

Put

\[
 k=m-q,\qquad N_q=\binom{2m}k,\qquad
 W=c_qN_q+\rho_q,quad0\le\rho_q<N_q.              \tag{3.1}
\]

Let \(\mathcal H_q\) consist of the \(\rho_q\) rank-\(k\) targets of
smallest pair radius. More formally, it contains every target of radius
strictly below one boundary value and an arbitrary number of targets at
the boundary value. Define

\[
 \nu_q^-(T)=c_q+\mathbf1_{\{T\in\mathcal H_q\}}.    \tag{3.2}
\]

The table has total mass \(W\). Its exact point margins may need the
polynomial-size correction from the common-run theorem; that correction is
kept explicit below.

For a uniform rank-\(k\) set \(T\),

\[
 \overline d_q:=\mathbb E d_\Pi(T)
 ={k(k-1)\over2(2m-1)}.                             \tag{3.3}
\]

For a uniform middle owner,

\[
 \overline d_0={m(m-1)\over2(2m-1)},               \tag{3.4}
\]

and therefore

\[
 \overline d_0-\overline d_q
 ={q(2m-q-1)\over2(2m-1)}.                         \tag{3.5}
\]

Since \(\mathcal H_q\) selects the smallest \(\rho_q\) values of
\(d_\Pi\), its average radius is at most the whole-layer average. Hence

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \sum_T\nu_q^-(T)d_\Pi(T)
 &=c_qN_q\overline d_q+
   \sum_{T\in\mathcal H_q}d_\Pi(T)\\
 &\le(c_qN_q+\rho_q)\overline d_q
 =W\overline d_q.                                  \tag{3.6}
 \end{aligned}
\]

This is the only property of the quantile threshold used in the
obstruction. It remains true after a floor crossing and after arbitrary
selection inside the boundary radius layer.

### Lemma 3.1 (zero-mass \(\ell^1\)-to-radius estimate)

If two nonnegative tables \(\mu,\nu\) on the rank-\(k\) layer have the
same total mass, then

\[
 \sum_T\bigl(\mu(T)-\nu(T)\bigr)d_\Pi(T)
 \le {k\over4}\|\mu-\nu\|_1.                     \tag{3.7}
\]

#### Proof

The signed difference has total zero, so its total positive mass is half
its \(\ell^1\)-norm. Since \(0\le d_\Pi(T)\le k/2\), its positive radius
moment is at most \((k/2)(\|\mu-\nu\|_1/2)\). \(\square\)

## 4. Dense-reset theorem

Let

\[
 \mu_q^-(T)=\#\{X:L_q(X)=T\}.                     \tag{4.1}
\]

It has total mass \(W\). Equations (2.7), (3.4), (3.6), and Lemma 3.1
give

\[
 W\overline d_0-qE_\Pi(P)
 \le\sum_T\mu_q^-(T)d_\Pi(T)
 \le W\overline d_q+{m-q\over4}\|\mu_q^--\nu_q^-\|_1.
                                                               \tag{4.2}
\]

Using (3.5) proves (0.1).

Now let \(\tau_q^-\) be any corrected integral target table of mass \(W\),
for example one satisfying the exact point margins of a common integer run
vector. By the triangle inequality,

\[
 \|\mu_q^--\nu_q^-\|_1
 \le\|\mu_q^--\tau_q^-\|_1+
     \|\tau_q^--\nu_q^-\|_1.                     \tag{4.3}
\]

Thus (0.1) is robust in precisely the form (0.2). No assumption on the
point margins enters the proof.

For the upper sign define

\[
 \nu_q^+(U)=\nu_q^-([2m]\setminus U).              \tag{4.4}
\]

Then (2.9) gives the same argument on the complements of upper targets.
Hence (0.1)--(0.2) hold verbatim above.

### Corollary 4.1 (all-depth reset density)

Let

\[
 D_{\le H}=\sum_{q=1}^H
 \left(\|\mu_q^--\tau_q^-\|_1+
       \|\tau_q^--\nu_q^-\|_1\right).             \tag{4.5}
\]

Then (0.4) holds.

#### Proof

Write

\[
 a_q={2m-q-1\over2(2m-1)}.                         \tag{4.6}
\]

Equation (0.1) and (4.3) imply

\[
 \|\mu_q^--\tau_q^-\|_1+
 \|\tau_q^--\nu_q^-\|_1
 \ge {4q\over m-q}\bigl(Wa_q-E_\Pi(P)\bigr)_+.   \tag{4.7}
\]

If \(E_\Pi(P)\ge Wa_H\), (0.4) is immediate. Otherwise
\(a_q\ge a_H\) for every \(q\le H\), so summing (4.7) gives

\[
 D_{\le H}\ge4\bigl(Wa_H-E_\Pi(P)\bigr)S_H,       \tag{4.8}
\]

which rearranges to (0.4). \(\square\)

For \(H=o(m)\),

\[
 a_H={1\over2}+O(H/m),\qquad
 S_H=(1+O(H/m)){H(H+1)\over2m}.                    \tag{4.9}
\]

This proves (0.5). Setting \(E_\Pi(P)=0\) in (4.8) proves (0.6).

### Corollary 4.2 (robustness under an owner leave)

Let \(\Omega'\subseteq\Omega\) be the owner set of a partial circulation,
assume its selected edges are a union of \(H\)-safe physical cycles, and
put

\[
 L=W-|\Omega'|.                                      \tag{4.10}
\]

Let \(E_\Pi\) count pair-breaking selected edges. The actual trace table
\(\mu_q^-\) now has mass \(W-L\), while \(\tau_q^-\) and \(\nu_q^-\)
retain mass \(W\). Then

\[
 qE_\Pi+{m\over2}L+{m-q\over4}
 \left(\|\mu_q^--\tau_q^-\|_1+
       \|\tau_q^--\nu_q^-\|_1\right)
 \ge Wqa_q.                                         \tag{4.11}
\]

Put

\[
 Q_H=\{\lceil H/2\rceil,\ldots,H\},\quad
 S_H^*=\sum_{q\in Q_H}{q\over m-q},\quad
 T_H^*=\sum_{q\in Q_H}{1\over m-q},                \tag{4.12}
\]

and let \(D_H^*\) be the sum of the two norms in (4.11) over
\(q\in Q_H\). Then

\[
 \boxed{
 E_\Pi\ge Wa_H-
 {D_H^*+2mLT_H^*\over4S_H^*}.}                     \tag{4.13}
\]

Consequently, if

\[
 H=o(m),\qquad H=\Omega(\sqrt m),\qquad
 D_H^*=o(W),\qquad L=o(W/H),                        \tag{4.14}
\]

then \(E_\Pi\ge(1/2-o(1))W\).

#### Proof

The selected roots have total initial radius at least

\[
 \sum_{X\in\Omega'}d_\Pi(X)
 \ge W\overline d_0-{m\over2}L.                    \tag{4.15}
\]

Lemma 2.1 remains valid on every selected cycle. The signed table
\(\mu_q^--\nu_q^-\) has total mass \(-L\). If its positive and negative
masses are \(p,n\), then \(n-p=L\), so
\(p=(\|\mu_q^--\nu_q^-\|_1-L)/2\le
\|\mu_q^--\nu_q^-\|_1/2\). Thus Lemma 3.1's upper bound remains valid
with the same right side. Combining this observation with (4.15) proves
(4.11).

If \(E_\Pi\ge Wa_H\), (4.13) is immediate. Otherwise sum (4.11), after
division by \((m-q)/4\), over \(Q_H\) and use \(a_q\ge a_H\). This gives

\[
 D_H^*\ge4(Wa_H-E_\Pi)S_H^*-2mLT_H^*,             \tag{4.16}
\]

which is (4.13). Finally, under \(H=o(m)\),

\[
 S_H^*=\Theta(H^2/m),\qquad T_H^*=\Theta(H/m).     \tag{4.17}
\]

The first quantity is bounded away from zero under
\(H=\Omega(\sqrt m)\), while the leave contribution to the quotient in
(4.13) is \(O(mL/H)=o(W)\) under (4.14). \(\square\)

## 5. Exact point margins and why they do not remove the obstruction

Every physical owner permutation has one integer run vector
\(R=(R_v)\), \(\sum_vR_v=W\), and therefore

\[
 \sum_{T\ni v}\mu_q^-(T)={W\over2}-qR_v,
 \qquad
 \sum_{U\ni v}\mu_q^+(U)={W\over2}+qR_v.          \tag{5.1}
\]

Complete pair-radius layers are point-regular. If an exactly sized
pair-radius quantile were point-regular, it would lie on the rational line

\[
                              R_v={W\over2m}.        \tag{5.2}
\]

The next proposition verifies directly that the point correction may be
made polynomially small without losing the radius-moment conclusion.

### Proposition 5.1 (exact common-run correction near a radius quantile)

Assume \(q=o(m)\). One may choose the boundary portion of
\(\mathcal H_q\) and then modify the table \(\nu_q^-\) to a nonnegative
integral table \(\tau_q^-\) of mass \(W\) such that, for one balanced
integer vector \(R\), common to all depths,

\[
 R_v\in\left\{\left\lfloor{W\over2m}\right\rfloor,
                 \left\lceil{W\over2m}\right\rceil\right\},
 \qquad \sum_vR_v=W,                              \tag{5.3}
\]

one has

\[
 \sum_{T\ni v}\tau_q^-(T)={W\over2}-qR_v,         \tag{5.4}
\]

and

\[
                         \|\tau_q^--\nu_q^-\|_1=O(m^2).       \tag{5.5}
\]

Taking literal complements gives \(\tau_q^+\) with the upper identity in
(5.1) and the same correction bound. The choices may be made for every
\(q\le H=o(m)\) using the same \(R\), with aggregate two-sign correction
\(O(m^2H)=o(W)\).

#### Proof

Index the matched pairs by \(M_i=\{x_i,y_i\}\). The permutation

\[
 x_1\mapsto x_2\mapsto\cdots\mapsto x_m\mapsto y_1
 \mapsto\cdots\mapsto y_m\mapsto x_1             \tag{5.6}
\]

is a \(2m\)-cycle on the coordinates and preserves \(\Pi\), hence preserves
every pair-radius layer. Partition the boundary radius layer into orbits of
this cyclic group. Every complete orbit is point-regular. Take complete
orbits and then, if necessary, a portion of one last orbit to obtain exactly
\(\rho_q\) high targets. Only the last partial orbit is nonregular, so, if
\(d_v\) is the degree of \(v\) in \(\mathcal H_q\),

\[
                         \left|d_v-{(m-q)\rho_q\over2m}\right|
                         \le2m.                    \tag{5.7}
\]

Fix the balanced vector (5.3). Put \(k=m-q\) and define the residual
degree required above the constant floor by

\[
 a_v={W\over2}-qR_v-c_q\binom{2m-1}{k-1}.
                                                               \tag{5.8}
\]

It is integral, has sum \(k\rho_q\), and satisfies

\[
 a_v={k\rho_q\over2m}
       -q\left(R_v-{W\over2m}\right).             \tag{5.9}
\]

Hence \(|d_v-a_v|\le2m+q\), and the total number of surplus coordinate
units is at most

\[
 {1\over2}\sum_v|d_v-a_v|\le m(2m+q).             \tag{5.10}
\]

Pair surplus and deficit units. For a pair \((u,v)\), transfer one unit of
table load from a \(k\)-set \(S\) containing \(u\) and not \(v\) to
\(S-u+v\). There are \(\binom{2m-2}{k-1}\) possible sources. Since the
number of transfers is polynomial and this binomial coefficient is
exponential uniformly for \(q=o(m)\), the sources and recipients may be
chosen all distinct. Every source initially has load at least \(c_q\ge1\),
so the resulting table is nonnegative. Each transfer changes the
\(\ell^1\)-distance from \(\nu_q^-\) by two. Equations (5.8)--(5.10) prove
(5.4)--(5.5). Complementation proves the upper assertion. Summing through
\(H=o(m)\) proves the final claim. \(\square\)

Thus the correction in (4.5) can indeed be \(o(W)\). Corollary 4.1 shows
that even after exact point margins are restored, a pair-flip chronology
still has the independent radius-moment defect (4.8).

There is a sharper constant when the corrected columns are required
exactly at one Gaussian depth.

### Proposition 5.2 (sharp exact-column reset density)

Let \(q=(A+o(1))\sqrt m\), with fixed \(A>0\), and suppose the actual
lower trace table equals a common-run correction \(\tau_q^-\) from
Proposition 5.1. Put

\[
 \vartheta_A=\{e^{A^2}\},
 \qquad z_A=\Phi^{-1}(\vartheta_A),                \tag{5.11}
\]

with the convention \(\phi(z_A)=0\) when \(e^{A^2}\) is an integer.
Then

\[
 \boxed{
 {E_\Pi(P)\over W}\ge
 \beta(A)-o(1),\qquad
 \beta(A)={1\over2}+{e^{-A^2}\phi(z_A)\over4A}.}  \tag{5.12}
\]

Thus away from an exact floor crossing the prescribed low-radius bonus
columns force a reset density strictly larger than one half.

#### Proof

For a uniform rank-\((m-q)\) target, write \(D=d_\Pi(T)\). Its mean is
\(\overline d_q\) from (3.3), and

\[
 {D-\overline d_q\over\sqrt m/4}\Longrightarrow N(0,1).       \tag{5.13}
\]

Also \(N_q/W\to e^{-A^2}\) and
\(\rho_q/N_q\to\vartheta_A\) when \(e^{A^2}\) is not an integer. The
lowest-quantile first-moment identity following from (5.13) and uniform
integrability is

\[
 \sum_{T\in\mathcal H_q}d_\Pi(T)
 =\rho_q\overline d_q
  -N_q{\sqrt m\over4}\phi(z_A)+o(W\sqrt m).        \tag{5.14}
\]

At an integer crossing the bonus fraction tends to zero or one and the
second term in (5.14) is \(o(W\sqrt m)\), agreeing with the convention in
(5.11). Therefore

\[
 \sum_T\nu_q^-(T)d_\Pi(T)
 =W\overline d_q
  -N_q{\sqrt m\over4}\phi(z_A)+o(W\sqrt m).        \tag{5.15}
\]

The \(O(m^2)\) correction in Proposition 5.1 changes this moment by only
\(O(m^3)=o(W\sqrt m)\). Lemma 2.1 and exact equality of the actual and
corrected columns now give

\[
 qE_\Pi(P)\ge
 W(\overline d_0-\overline d_q)
 +N_q{\sqrt m\over4}\phi(z_A)+o(W\sqrt m).         \tag{5.16}
\]

Divide by \(qW\), use (3.5), and take the limit. \(\square\)

The new constraint is second-order with respect to the fixed matching
\(\Pi\): it counts full coordinate pairs, not individual coordinate
incidences. Thus satisfying every identity in (5.1) does not imply it.

## 6. Cycle economy and the exact boundary

The labelled de Bruijn graph is Eulerian before the root rows are imposed,
but an Euler tour of that graph repeats each physical root once for every
selected fibre state. After the root rows are imposed, memory balance
projects to one physical permutation, and its number of cycles is not
controlled by Eulericity of the labelled multigraph.

The dense-reset theorem is independent of cycle count. It proves:

1. a pair-flip de Bruijn realization, even with arbitrary quota-copy reset
   labels, has aggregate all-depth column discrepancy at least (0.6);
2. at \(H=\Theta(\sqrt m)\), any realization within \(o(W)\) of the
   rounded pair-radius tables uses at least \((1/2-o(1))W\) genuinely
   pair-breaking physical transitions;
3. augmenting the controller by finitely or boundedly many reset states
   cannot change either assertion, because both are statements about the
   projected physical permutation.

What remains open is a construction with a positive density of
pair-breaking transitions which simultaneously has the rounded all-depth
columns, exact common-run margins, and \(o(W/H)\) physical cycles. Such a
construction is no longer a sparse reset of the pair-cube/pair-flip
grammar; it is the unrestricted integral Johnson memory-circulation gate.

## 7. A stable literal-hole bound requiring only \(\Theta(W/H)\) resets

The \(\ell^1\)-table obstruction above is deliberately stronger than a
literal support-hole requirement. There is also an exact support statement,
with a weaker but sharp reset scale.

Let

\[
 A_d={m!\,2^{m-2d}\over d!^2(m-2d)!}              \tag{7.1}
\]

be the number of middle owners of pair radius \(d\), and let

\[
 B_{q,d}={m!\,2^{m-q-2d}\over
              d!(d+q)!(m-q-2d)!}                 \tag{7.2}
\]

be the number of rank-\((m-q)\) targets of radius \(d\). Invalid
factorials mean zero. Write \(M_q^-\) for the number of lower targets not
hit by a partial owner circulation.

### Proposition 7.1 (stable radial support cut)

For every partial \(H\)-safe owner circulation and every \(q\le H\),

\[
 \boxed{
 M_q^-\ge\sum_d(B_{q,d}-A_d)_+-qE_\Pi.}            \tag{7.3}
\]

The same inequality holds for the upper sign.

#### Proof

A rooted \(q\)-window containing no pair-breaking edge consists entirely
of flips of distinct singleton pairs. Its lower trace therefore has the
same pair radius as its root. There are at most \(qE_\Pi\) windows
containing a pair-breaking edge, since every such edge lies in exactly
\(q\) rooted \(q\)-windows.

Let \(C_d\) be the number of actual lower occurrences of radius \(d\).
Starting from the radius census of the retained roots, the vector \(C\) is
obtained by relabelling at most \(qE_\Pi\) occurrence classes. One relabelled
occurrence can reduce

\[
                         \sum_d(B_{q,d}-C_d)_+       \tag{7.4}
\]

by at most one. Omitting roots only decreases the starting census
coordinatewise from \((A_d)\) and cannot reduce (7.4). Finally, at most
\(\min\{B_{q,d},C_d\}\) distinct targets can be hit in radius class \(d\).
These observations prove (7.3). Upper targets give the same statement after
complementation. \(\square\)

For \(q=(A+o(1))\sqrt m\), \(A>0\) fixed, the exact Gaussian evaluation of
the unperturbed radial cut is

\[
 {1\over W}\sum_d(B_{q,d}-A_d)_+
 \longrightarrow
 \gamma(A):=e^{-A^2}\Phi(A/2)-\Phi(-3A/2)>0.       \tag{7.5}
\]

Indeed, under the rank-\((m-q)\) target law, the likelihood ratio

\[
 {A_d\over B_{q,d}}
 =2^q{(d+1)\cdots(d+q)\over
          (m-2d-q+1)\cdots(m-2d)}                 \tag{7.6}
\]

has logarithm converging to \(N(-A^2,4A^2)\); integrating
\((1-e^z)_+\) and using \(N_q/W\to e^{-A^2}\) gives (7.5).

Combining (7.3)--(7.5), any construction with \(M_q^-=o(W)\) must satisfy

\[
                         E_\Pi\ge
 \left({\gamma(A)\over A}-o(1)\right){W\over\sqrt m}.          \tag{7.7}
\]

Thus even the weaker literal-support objective needs
\(\Omega_A(W/H)\) physical pair-breaking seams at a Gaussian depth
\(q\asymp H\). Quota-copy reset labels, for which \(E_\Pi=0\), leave
\(\Omega_A(W)\) literal holes. The stronger table-equality objective needs
the positive-density reset bound (0.5).
