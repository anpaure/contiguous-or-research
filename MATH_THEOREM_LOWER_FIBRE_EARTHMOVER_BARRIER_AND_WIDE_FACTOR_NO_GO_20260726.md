# The lower-fibre earthmover barrier: no direct wide factor at Gaussian distance

Date: 2026-07-26

Method: pure mathematics only.  This note continues from
`MATH_THEOREM_JOINT_PARENT_WEDGE_TRADE_AND_GAUSSIAN_OCCURRENCE_NO_GO_20260726.md`.

## 0. Outcome

The requested direct factor does not exist as a Gaussian-distance correcting
factor in the stated parameter regime.  A factor of large global diameter
whose useful positive and negative ports are nevertheless locally paired is
not excluded, but it does not evade the Gaussian defect cut.
The obstruction is stronger than the affine-parent invariants and does not
depend on serial versus joint parent neutrality.

Let \(z\) be any integral Johnson-edge trade which preserves every lower
colour exactly,

\[
                         B_-z=0,                    \tag{0.1}
\]

and let \(t=\|z^-\|_1\) be its number of deleted edge-units.  If
\(\delta=B_+z\) is its upper-load change, then

\[
 \boxed{
                         \|\delta\|_{\rm EM}\le2t.} \tag{0.2}
\]

Here \(\|\cdot\|_{\rm EM}\) is Johnson earthmover distance on the upper
layer.  The reason is exact: within each fixed lower fibre \(S\), old and new
upper targets all contain \(S\), and hence are at Johnson distance at most
two.

If the current repeated and missing upper targets are separated by distance
\(\rho\), and the trade decreases repeat excess by \(h\), then

\[
 \boxed{
                         h\rho\le2t.}                \tag{0.3}
\]

Therefore a factor with

\[
 t=\Theta(\sqrt m),\qquad
 h=\Theta(\sqrt m),\qquad
 \rho=\Theta(\sqrt m)                               \tag{0.4}
\]

is impossible: the left side of (0.3) is \(\Theta(m)\), while the right
side is only \(\Theta(\sqrt m)\).  A factor carrying
\(h=\Theta(\sqrt m)\) independently useful Gaussian-distance ports must
delete

\[
                         t=\Omega(m),                \tag{0.5}
\]

not \(\Theta(\sqrt m)\) edges.

The obstruction is global.  A legal switch between two Hamilton cycles has
\(t\le W\), so every exact-lower-colour-preserving switch satisfies

\[
                         h\le {2W\over\rho}.         \tag{0.6}
\]

At \(\rho=\Theta(\sqrt m)\), it can repair only \(O(W/\sqrt m)=o(W)\)
defects, even if it is one enormous jointly neutral factor using the entire
cycle.

There is also a robust form.  Suppose the initial and final Hamilton cycles
are both lower-complete, but their lower multiplicities may differ.  The
total lower excess is

\[
 E=W-\binom n{m-1}=O(W/m).                           \tag{0.7}
\]

Then

\[
 \boxed{
                         \|u'-u\|_{\rm EM}
        \le2W+(m-2)E<4W,}                           \tag{0.8}
\]

and hence \(h<4W/\rho\).  Thus redistributing the small hereditary lower
excess does not evade the Gaussian barrier.

Consequently the moving-frame polishing strategy cannot start from a state
with a positive-density, Gaussian-separated upper defect while preserving
lower completeness.  Constant one now requires one of two qualitatively
different inputs:

1. choose the original lower-complete Hamilton cycle so that all but
   \(o(W)\) upper defect has bounded-distance repeat--hole partners; or
2. temporarily create a macroscopic lower-hole budget, leaving the
   lower-complete class during the correction.

The second option is outside the requested exact-lower-colour factor model.

## 1. Edge fibres and upper transportation

Let

\[
 \mathcal L=\binom{[n]}{m-1},\qquad
 \mathcal U=\binom{[n]}{m+1}.                       \tag{1.1}
\]

For a Johnson edge \(e=XY\), write

\[
                         L(e)=X\cap Y,
 \qquad
                         U(e)=X\cup Y.               \tag{1.2}
\]

For a signed integral edge vector \(z\), define

\[
                         \delta=B_+z,
 \qquad
                         \delta(U)=\sum_{e:U(e)=U}z(e).         \tag{1.3}
\]

Assume \(z\) has equally many positive and negative edge-units.  This holds
for every owner-neutral trade and for the difference of two Hamilton cycles.
Put

\[
                         t=\sum_ez^+(e)=\sum_ez^-(e).           \tag{1.4}
\]

For a zero-mass signed measure \(\eta\) on \(\mathcal U\), let

\[
 \|\eta\|_{\rm EM}
   =\min_\pi\sum_{U,V}d_J(U,V)\pi(U,V),              \tag{1.5}
\]

where \(\pi\) ranges over couplings from \(\eta^-\) to \(\eta^+\).
Equivalently, this is the transportation norm generated by atoms
\(e_V-e_U\) of cost \(d_J(U,V)\).

## 2. Exact lower preservation has transportation radius two

### Theorem 2.1 (lower-fibre earthmover bound)

If \(B_-z=0\), then (0.2) holds.

#### Proof

For each \(S\in\mathcal L\), let

\[
 t_S^- =\sum_{e:L(e)=S}z^-(e),
 \qquad
 t_S^+ =\sum_{e:L(e)=S}z^+(e).                      \tag{2.1}
\]

The equation \(B_-z=0\) says

\[
                         t_S^-=t_S^+=:t_S            \tag{2.2}
\]

for every \(S\).  Pair the \(t_S\) deleted edge-units of lower colour
\(S\) arbitrarily with the \(t_S\) added edge-units of the same colour.

If a paired deleted edge has upper target \(U\) and its added mate has upper
target \(V\), then

\[
                         S\subset U\cap V,
 \qquad |U|=|V|=m+1.                                \tag{2.3}
\]

Since \(|S|=m-1\), each of \(U,V\) is obtained from \(S\) by adding two
coordinates.  Therefore

\[
                         d_J(U,V)\le2.               \tag{2.4}
\]

The paired raw edge-units give a decomposition of \(\delta\) into
\(\sum_St_S=t\) transportation atoms, each of cost at most two.  Any
cancellation of equal upper targets can only lower the minimum transport
cost.  Hence \(\|\delta\|_{\rm EM}\le2t\). \(\square\)

No middle-owner condition was used.  In particular, joint cancellation of
owner ledgers across arbitrarily many affine parents cannot evade (0.2).

## 3. Actual upper-hole descent costs separated transport

Let \(u\) be the current upper load, and put

\[
 \mathcal R=\{U:u(U)\ge2\},
 \qquad
 \mathcal H=\{U:u(U)=0\},
 \qquad
 \rho=d_J(\mathcal R,\mathcal H).                   \tag{3.1}
\]

### Theorem 3.1 (descent lower bound)

If the feasible load change \(\delta\) lowers repeat excess by \(h>0\), then

\[
                         \|\delta\|_{\rm EM}\ge h\rho.         \tag{3.2}
\]

#### Proof

Put \(s=\|\delta^+\|_1=\|\delta^-\|_1\).  Let \(a\) be the positive mass
placed on current holes, and let

\[
                         b=\sum_U
                 \min\{\delta^-(U),(u(U)-1)_+\}     \tag{3.3}
\]

be the negative mass which actually removes current repeat excess.  The
exact nonlinear repeat formula gives

\[
                         a+b\ge s+h.                 \tag{3.4}
\]

In any coupling from \(\delta^-\) to \(\delta^+\), at least
\(a+b-s\ge h\) mass must travel from \(\mathcal R\) to \(\mathcal H\).
Every such unit travels distance at least \(\rho\), proving (3.2).
\(\square\)

Combining Theorems 2.1 and 3.1 proves (0.3).

## 4. The requested wide factor is impossible

Let \(z_m\) be a proposed direct factor with \(t_m\) deleted edges and
useful descent \(h_m\) on a state with defect separation \(\rho_m\).  Then

\[
                         t_m\ge {h_m\rho_m\over2}.   \tag{4.1}
\]

In the requested regime

\[
                         h_m\ge c\sqrt m,
 \qquad
                         \rho_m\ge c'\sqrt m,        \tag{4.2}
\]

for fixed \(c,c'>0\).  Equation (4.1) gives

\[
                         t_m\ge {cc'\over2}m.        \tag{4.3}
\]

This contradicts \(t_m=\Theta(\sqrt m)\).  Notice that global support
diameter is irrelevant: the upper change may have diameter \(\Theta(m)\),
but its exact lower-colour decomposition still has transportation radius
two.

Equivalently, let \(h_r\) be any lower bound, valid for every coupling from
\(\delta^-\) to \(\delta^+\), on the mass sent from a current repeated
target to a current hole at distance at least \(r\).  Then

\[
                         h_r\le {2t_m\over r}.       \tag{4.4}
\]

Thus a \(t_m=\Theta(\sqrt m)\) factor may have large geometric diameter, but
only \(O(1)\) of its useful ports can cross a \(\Theta(\sqrt m)\) gap.

For an owner-disjoint bank of factors with aggregate deleted-edge mass
\(T\le W\), the same proof applied after summing all trades gives

\[
                         h\rho\le2T\le2W.            \tag{4.5}
\]

Thus replacing a narrow chain by one direct algebraic factor does not change
the coefficient-one ledger.

## 5. Robustness under redistribution of lower excess

Exact preservation of each lower multiplicity is stronger than final lower
completeness.  We now remove that extra assumption.

Let \(C,C'\) be Hamilton cycles on all \(W\) middle owners.  Let their lower
loads be \(\ell,\ell'\), their upper loads be \(u,u'\), and put

\[
                         z=x_{C'}-x_C,
 \qquad
                         r=B_-z=\ell'-\ell.          \tag{5.1}
\]

Let \(t\) be the number of deleted edges, so \(t\le W\), and put

\[
                         q={1\over2}\|r\|_1.         \tag{5.2}
\]

### Theorem 5.1 (approximate lower-fibre transport)

The upper change satisfies

\[
                         \|u'-u\|_{\rm EM}
            \le2(t-q)+m q
            \le2W+(m-2)q.                           \tag{5.3}
\]

#### Proof

For every lower target \(S\), pair as many deleted and added edges of colour
\(S\) as possible.  Exactly \(t-q\) pairs are formed, and each costs at most
two by (2.4).  There remain \(q\) unpaired deleted and \(q\) unpaired added
edge-units.  Pair these arbitrarily.  The diameter of the upper Johnson
layer is at most \(m\), so these pairs cost at most \(mq\). \(\square\)

If both cycles are lower-complete, write

\[
                         \ell=\mathbf1+h,
 \qquad
                         \ell'=\mathbf1+h',
 \qquad
                         \|h\|_1=\|h'\|_1=E.        \tag{5.4}
\]

Then

\[
                         q={1\over2}\|h'-h\|_1\le E.            \tag{5.5}
\]

For \(n=2m\),

\[
                         E={W\over m+1},             \tag{5.6}
\]

and for \(n=2m+1\),

\[
                         E={2W\over m+2}.            \tag{5.7}
\]

Equations (5.3), (5.6), and (5.7) prove the uniform bound (0.8).  Combining
it with Theorem 3.1 gives

\[
                         h<{4W\over\rho}             \tag{5.8}
\]

for every switch between lower-complete Hamilton cycles.

## 6. Consequence for constant one

The Gaussian-correcting direct-wide-factor specification

\[
 (\text{diameter},\text{deleted mass},\text{useful ports})
   =(\Theta(\sqrt m),\Theta(\sqrt m),\Theta(\sqrt m))           \tag{6.1}
\]

violates the lower-fibre earthmover inequality and is therefore ruled out.
The minimum deleted mass for one such useful factor is \(\Omega(m)\), and a
global lower-complete transformation can repair only \(O(W/\sqrt m)\)
Gaussian-separated defects.

This does not prove that actual lower-complete Hamilton cycles necessarily
have a Gaussian-separated upper defect.  It proves the exact conditional
obstruction: if such separation occurs, no choice of exact-lower-colour
joint factor, affine moving frame, growing diameter, or serial algebraic
compiler can polish it to coefficient one.

Accordingly the moving-frame lane can survive only as a **base-cycle
selection theorem**, not as a universal polishing theorem.  One must build
the lower-complete Hamilton cycle with an upper defect admitting
\(O(1)\)-distance transport for all but \(o(W)\) units.  That is now the
minimal remaining geometric condition.
