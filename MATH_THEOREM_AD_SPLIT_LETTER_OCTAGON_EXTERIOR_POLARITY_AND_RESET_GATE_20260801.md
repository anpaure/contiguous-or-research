## 0. Outcome

Split-letter contraction gives the expected positive statement in its exact
scope.  If the old-only exterior targets of a phase switch are literal side
cells of `H=O(1)` actual source-letter splits, then `H` added positions retain
the entire new-phase OR deck and restore all those targets.  Complete rays and
rectangles are allowed, but their occurrence-labelled side-cell equality graph
must pass Hall; a bounded number of relation types is not enough.

For the resident quaternary coatom/screen tensor, this antecedent is realized
by **two prepared exterior source letters**

\[
                 X_L=\{a_0,a_1\},\qquad
                 X_R=\{a_1,a_2\}.                     \tag{0.1a}
\]

They are not tensor-internal inverse letters.  Split them in the two phase
orientations

\[
\begin{array}{c|cc}
 &X_L\text{ (far,near)}&X_R\text{ (near,far)}\\ \hline
 \epsilon=0&(a_0,a_1)&(a_2,a_1)\\
 \epsilon=1&(a_1,a_0)&(a_1,a_2).
\end{array}                                             \tag{0.1b}
\]

Two phase-common source corridors make the packet-near side cells exactly
the opposite-phase missing prefix and suffix masks.  Thus the four typed
debts, two at a time, cost exactly two added positions once the two exterior
hosts exist, while every old interval OR survives by full-block lift.

The tensor's **internal** source does not satisfy the same host hypothesis.
Its four exceptional
exterior profiles are

\[
 \Pi_0=C_L\cup\{a_0\},\quad \Pi_1=C_L\cup\{a_1\},
 \qquad
 \Sigma_0=C_R\cup\{a_1\},\quad \Sigma_1=C_R\cup\{a_2\}. \tag{0.1}
\]

In phase one, `a_1` is forced into every exact source prefix strictly before
`a_0` can occur; in phase zero the order is reversed.  At the other end,
phase one forces `a_2` into every relevant suffix before `a_1`, and phase zero
reverses that order.  The owner-occurrence separation is exactly `d+4`, while
an inverse letter reaches only `d` owner rows.  Consequently no exact inverse,
not only the maximal inverse, contains any one of the four direct
partial-endpoint ray identities.  Simultaneously splitting more fixed
**internal** source letters does not help, because only the last block of a
prefix (or first block of a suffix) can be partial.  This internal no-go is
compatible with the positive exterior construction (0.1a)--(0.1b).

There is no local two-sided rectangle debt: both tensor phases have the same
total union, so every interval spanning the entire tensor is literally
unchanged.  The additional donor-swap rectangle from the endpoint absorber is
different.  The frozen rolling-fan counterexample has an interior poison
letter `a_1`; any contraction-preserving refinement leaves that letter in
every interval spanning the two private banks.  Thus source splitting cannot
repair that rectangle without changing the order or replacing the interior
block.

Finally, a split used by a genuinely new side-cell witness is not
self-resetting.  Contracting it adds the omitted complementary half and
changes the target.  In the two-host octagon, however, the side targets of one
phase are native packet-boundary targets in the opposite phase.  Hence the
same two boundaries can be contracted, reversed and re-split without
accumulation **provided** one simultaneous matching transports the entire
protected occurrence bank through contraction **and phase replacement**, and
all ambient owner/q1/residence/deadline/common-cap rows pass.  Under these
hypotheses `Phi<=2`; without them only `Phi'<=Phi+2` is justified.  This
cross-phase clause is essential: the two tensor source decks are not equal.

## 1. Exact conditional split-host theorem

Let `A=(A_0,...,A_(N-1))` be a word of nonempty sets.  At selected positions
`p` choose nonempty `Z_p,T_p` with

\[
                         A_p=Z_p\cup T_p,                 \tag{1.1}
\]

and replace `A_p` by the ordered block `(Z_p,T_p)`.  Every old interval is
transported by taking both halves of every split block it meets.  Its union is
unchanged.

The genuinely new intervals are exactly the side cells.  If their first and
last old block indices are `p<q`, their values are

\[
 L_p\ \cup\!\bigcup_{p<i<q}A_i\ \cup R_q,              \tag{1.2}
\]

where `L_p` is either `A_p` or the right half `T_p`, and `R_q` is either
`A_q` or the left half `Z_q`, with at least one partial endpoint.  Inside one
split block the two singleton halves are the only new cells.

### Theorem 1.1 (bounded source-support restoration)

Let `A^+` be the source word of the retained phase and let `D` be the bank of
old-phase targets not already in its OR deck.  Suppose there are `H` actual
positions of `A^+`, decompositions (1.1), and a matching from every
occurrence-labelled member of `D` to a distinct side cell of value equal to
that member.  Then the expanded word has length `|A^+|+H`, retains every
interval value of `A^+`, and realizes all of `D`.

For ordinary, unlabelled coverage the matching condition reduces to existence
of one equal side cell for each distinct target.  For a single right-facing
ray the exact identity is

\[
 R_j=T_p\cup A_{p+1}\cup\cdots\cup A_{e_j},
 \qquad e_0<e_1<\cdots,                                 \tag{1.3}
\]

and the left-facing identity is its reversal.  A two-ended rectangle must
literally have form (1.2) for the relevant pairs of endpoints; merely being a
set-theoretic product of two nested chains is insufficient.

#### Proof

The full-block lift injects old intervals into the expanded word and preserves
their unions by (1.1).  Formula (1.2) is the exhaustive side-cell normal form:
every strictly interior old block is met completely, and only the two endpoint
blocks can be partial.  The assumed matching therefore supplies disjoint new
physical cells for `D`, proving the theorem.  Formula (1.3) is the one-partial-
endpoint specialization.  \(\square\)

This is the exact positive implication suggested by split-letter contraction.
The rest of the note tests its antecedent on the actual tensor.

## 3. Arbitrary-inverse polarity obstruction

Let `T^epsilon` denote the expanded owner word in phase `epsilon`.  Let
`X^epsilon` be **any** exact depth-`d` source inverse:

\[
 T_i^\epsilon=\bigcup_{p=i}^{i+d}X_p^\epsilon.           \tag{3.1}
\]

### Lemma 3.0 (no singleton domination inside the tensor)

If the tensor owners have rank `r`, then each of the four masks in (0.1)
has rank `r+3`, whereas every exact inverse letter has rank at most `r`.
Consequently none of the four debts is contained in an actual tensor source
letter, so the bounded dominating-letter reset cannot apply internally.

#### Proof

The tensor has `|K|=r-d-3` and `|Phi|=d+2`.  Each mask in (0.1) contains
`K`, all of `Phi`, and four active labels, giving rank `r+3`.  Every source
letter in (3.1) is contained in each rank-`r` owner window which uses it, so
it has rank at most `r`.  \(\square\)

If a coordinate first occurs in an interior owner row `ell>0`, then its
first source occurrence is exactly `ell+d`: owner `ell` needs it in
`[ell,ell+d]`, while owner `ell-1` forbids all earlier positions in that
window.  Dually, if its last owner occurrence is an interior row `r`, then
its last source occurrence is exactly `r`.

### Theorem 3.1 (the four direct split rays are impossible)

For every `d>=1` and every exact inverse (3.1), no contraction-preserving
split of the fixed tensor source realizes any of the following missing
profiles as a partial-endpoint prefix or suffix:

\[
 \Pi_0\text{ from phase 1},\quad \Pi_1\text{ from phase 0},
 \quad \Sigma_0\text{ from phase 1},\quad
 \Sigma_1\text{ from phase 0}.                           \tag{3.2}
\]

The conclusion remains true after simultaneously splitting arbitrarily many
fixed source positions.

#### Proof

In phase one the first owner occurrences are

\[
 \ell_1(a_1)=2d+5,qquad \ell_1(a_0)=3d+9.              \tag{3.3}
\]

Their exact first source positions are therefore `3d+5` and `4d+9`.
Every source prefix containing `a_0` consequently already contains `a_1`.
A partial final block can omit only part of its own source letter; it cannot
remove the earlier forced `a_1`.  Hence it cannot equal `Pi_0`, which
contains `a_0` and omits `a_1`.  Phase zero has the exchanged formula

\[
 \ell_0(a_0)=2d+5,qquad \ell_0(a_1)=3d+9,              \tag{3.4}
\]

and rules out `Pi_1` in the same way.

At the right end, phase one has the exact last owner, and hence exact last
source, positions

\[
 r_1(a_1)=5d+13,qquad r_1(a_2)=6d+17.                 \tag{3.5}
\]

Thus every source suffix containing `a_1` already contains the later
`a_2`, excluding `Sigma_0`.  Phase zero has

\[
 r_0(a_2)=5d+13,qquad r_0(a_1)=6d+17,                 \tag{3.6}
\]

and excludes `Sigma_1`.

Under simultaneous splitting, every block strictly inside a source prefix
or suffix is included in full.  Only its terminal block can be partial.
The preceding forced coordinate therefore remains, so additional splits do
not alter the argument.  \(\square\)

This is stronger than a failure of the maximal erosion.  Every exact inverse
is covered.  It is also exactly source-level: a common owner envelope or a
set-theoretic nesting identity cannot reverse the forced occurrence order.

### Corollary 3.2 (conditional external-host boundary)

Theorem 3.1 does not forbid a different ambient source word from carrying a
debt ray.  It says precisely what that word must change: before the excluded
polarity label is forced, an actual source host must expose the desired label
and satisfy (1.3), or an unaffected duplicate witness must exist.  Such a
host is extra structure; it is not exported by the resident tensor.

## 4. Rectangle relations

### Proposition 4.1 (the local tensor rectangle is already exact)

Every interval meeting both exterior shores contains the whole tensor, whose
union is `Theta` in both phases.  Hence the full-span relation is identically

\[
 \{H_L\cup\Theta\cup H_R: H_L\text{ a left suffix union},
                              H_R\text{ a right prefix union}\}. \tag{4.1}
\]

It requires no split-letter repair.

The nonlocal endpoint absorber additionally exchanges two donor fragments.
That operation replaces `suffix(P_1) x prefix(P_2)` by the reversed donor
grid.  It is not the local rectangle (4.1).

### Proposition 4.2 (the donor poison survives every refinement)

In the private-bank construction of Theorem 4.1 of
`MATH_THEOREM_AD_QUATERNARY_OCTAGON_EXTERIOR_RAY_AND_RESIDENCE_INTERFACE_20260801.md`,
each selected old donor-grid target contains one private label from each bank
and omits `a_1`.  In the new chronology every interval containing both banks
has `B_1` strictly inside it, and `a_1 in B_1`.

Replace any collection of letters by consecutive nonempty blocks having the
same block unions.  Every such two-bank interval still contains the whole
refinement block of `B_1`, because that block is strictly interior.  It still
contains `a_1`, and therefore cannot equal an old selected target.  Thus no
number of contraction-preserving split letters repairs this donor rectangle
while the order and the poison block remain fixed.

This proposition is a literal interval-word obstruction.  As in the earlier
donor audit, it is not claimed that the private-bank fixture extends to a
global four-resource exact factor.

There is also a catalogue-independent quantitative obstruction.

### Proposition 4.3 (new rectangle antichain bound)

After `H` binary source-letter splits, let `U` be an inclusion antichain of
targets which were absent from the contracted old deck and which are
realized only by new side cells.  Then

\[
                         |U|\le 2H+\binom H2
                              ={H(H+3)\over2}.           \tag{4.2}
\]

Hence a donor rectangle containing a product `[0,r] x [0,r]` cannot be born
from `O(1)` splits: its antidiagonal gives the necessary inequality

\[
                         {H(H+3)\over2}\ge r+1.          \tag{4.3}
\]

This does not apply when the rectangle was already in the contracted old
deck; every such old cell is preserved automatically by its full-block
lift.

#### Proof

A side cell with one partial endpoint belongs to one of the two nested rays
of its split block.  The `2H` rays meet an antichain at most once each.  A
side cell with two partial endpoints is determined by a pair of split
blocks, giving at most `binom(H,2)` further cells.  Singleton halves already
belong to the one-ended rays.  This proves (4.2); the product antidiagonal
proves (4.3).  \(\square\)

## 5. Exact reset gate

Let a protected target `S` be witnessed after a split by a side cell `J`.
Contract a used split block and close `J` to the smallest interval containing
the contracted image.  This adds precisely the complementary half omitted at
that partial endpoint.

### Lemma 5.1 (a genuinely new side witness is not self-resetting)

If the added complementary half is not contained in `S`, contraction changes
the value of `J`, so that witness is lost.  In particular, if `J` is a
genuinely new side-cell value—its full-block closure has a different union—
the boundary which creates `J` cannot be reset using that same witness.

For a family of protected occurrences, a simultaneous reset exists exactly
when the targets have a saturating matching to contracted cells whose
full-block expansions still have the required values.

#### Proof

Closing a partial endpoint inserts the omitted half and changes no other
letter.  The union remains `S` exactly when the inserted half is a subset of
`S`.  The occurrence statement is Hall's theorem applied to all safe
contracted cells.  \(\square\)

For the hypothetical cap split (2.8), the four reset deficits are exactly

\[
\begin{array}{c|cc}
 \epsilon&H_*\setminus\Pi_\epsilon&H_*\setminus\Sigma_\epsilon\\ \hline
 0&\{a_1\}&\{a_2\}\\
 1&\{a_0\}&\{a_1\}.
\end{array}                                               \tag{5.1}
\]

On the genuine directed debt rays of Proposition 2.1, the relevant exterior
union omits both labels in the polarity pair.  Hence it omits precisely the
reset label displayed in (5.1).  Closing the left or right side cell adds
that label and strictly enlarges its target.  Even the idealized one-letter
`H_*` repair would therefore leave one essential split boundary until an
alternate occurrence of every protected ray target is installed.

Consequently the conditional `+H` theorem of Section 1 is a one-transition
result.  If `Phi` split boundaries were essential before the transition,
one simultaneous contraction-safe Hall matching jointly with all protected
occurrences and new tasks reclaims `g` of them, and `r<=H` new boundaries
are born, then

\[
                            \Phi'\le\Phi-g+r.             \tag{5.2}
\]

Without a positive lower bound on `g`, split debt can accumulate.  Neither
the four typed profile count nor the split identities alone supplies such a
reset.  The present octagon has an additional native-return structure which
does so conditionally.

### Theorem 5.2 (conditional two-host phase reset)

Assume that both refinements (0.1b) satisfy the ambient boundary-owner, q1,
residence, deadline and prescribed common-cap rows.  Assume further that:

1. all inherited target occurrences use admissible full-block lifts;
2. simultaneous contraction of **both** marked host blocks has a saturating
   contraction-safe matching jointly with every protected occurrence;
3. after replacing the contracted packet `Q^epsilon` by
   `Q^(1-epsilon)`, the entire protected occurrence bank together with the
   two old side tasks has one simultaneous matching to opposite-phase
   native/full-block cells, with native
   `Pi_(1-epsilon),Sigma_(1-epsilon)` available; and
4. the two reverse-split cells are free for `Pi_epsilon,Sigma_epsilon`.

Then both old splits may be contracted, the packet phase changed, and the
same two exterior hosts re-split in the opposite orientations.  All inherited
targets survive, the old side targets move to native cells, and Theorem 2.3
supplies the newly missing pair.  If these are the only carried marked
boundaries, then

\[
                              \Phi'\le2.                 \tag{5.3}
\]

Hence repeated phase changes do not accumulate this typed-boundary debt.
Without the three assumptions, the unconditional statement is only
`Phi'<=Phi+2`.

#### Proof

Item 2 permits simultaneous contraction without coalescing any protected
matching cells.  Item 3 is the separate cross-phase transport: it retains the
whole protected bank and moves the former side tasks to their native pair.
Item 4 and reverse orientation of the same hosts invoke Theorem 2.3 for the
opposite pair.  Exactly two marked host boundaries remain.  \(\square\)

The cross-phase hypothesis cannot be deleted.  For the sharp inverse at
`d=1`, the mask

\[
                              S=\{z,a_3,f_0\}            \tag{5.4}
\]

is the phase-zero singleton source letter `Q^0_26` (position 27 in the
contracted extended word).  Its occurrence is unaffected by both exterior
splits and is contraction-safe, but `S` is absent from the complete interval
deck of both the contracted and expanded phase-one words.  Thus within-phase
full-block transport plus contraction Hall does not transport an arbitrary
protected bank through phase replacement.

### Corollary 5.2a (exact fixed-transition reset Hall criterion)

Fix the contract--phase-change--reverse-split chronology of Theorem 5.2.
Let `P` be the complete occurrence-labelled protected bank, including the old
side tasks, and let `C` be the physical cells of the final opposite-phase
expanded word which pass the owner, q1, residence, deadline and prescribed-cap
guards.  Join `p in P` to `c in C` exactly when `OR(c)` equals the target value
of `p`.  Then the fixed transition resets all protected occurrences if and
only if

\[
                         |N(U)|\ge |U|
             \qquad\text{for every }U\subseteq P.      \tag{5.5}
\]

#### Proof

Any literal final word assigns distinct protected occurrences to distinct
guarded cells of equal value, hence gives a matching saturating `P` and
implies (5.5).  Conversely Hall's theorem gives exactly such an assignment;
the fixed chronology already supplies the source word and Theorem 2.3 its two
new typed cells.  \(\square\)

If the target (5.4) belongs to `P`, its vertex has degree zero in the `d=1`
phase-zero to phase-one graph, so the fixed transition fails Hall.  This is a
smallest explicit obstruction to automatic whole-bank reset, not an
obstruction when an ambient duplicate cell removes that isolated vertex.

### Theorem 5.3 (exact ray-copy reset)

Suppose an expanded word has an old split bank whose complete set of
contraction-dependent protected targets is `U`.  Before contracting that
bank, split fresh actual source letters.  If `U` has a saturating matching
to fresh side cells and unaffected cells, disjoint from the transported old
matching, then all old split boundaries may be contracted.  Every target in
the contracted reference deck and every protected member of `U` survives,
so the number of live boundaries is at most the size of the fresh split
bank.

In particular, a fresh literal copy of each old endpoint ray, with distinct
cells, transports the whole ray bank without accumulation.

#### Proof

Perform the fresh splits first; full-block lift preserves the current old
deck.  Move `U` to the matching supplied by the fresh and unaffected cells.
By definition, every remaining protected witness is contraction-stable.
Contract the old bank and retain the displayed matching.  \(\square\)

The first, central and last **internal** returned source banks of the
canonical tensor do not meet this theorem's antecedent for the four hard
rays: Proposition 2.2 rules out domination and Theorem 3.1 rules out the
required ordered endpoint profiles.  Theorem 5.2 instead uses two exterior
hosts and native opposite-phase occurrences.  It is not an internal
ray-copy reset.

## 6. Residence and scope

Block contraction transports every named old interval value and its order.
If the carrier is represented by those named intervals, its owner trace and
the two exterior residence signatures are unchanged; the residence conditions
from the octagon interface remain the same.  Intervals crossing `s` split
positions become longer by `s` source letters.

Therefore this statement is an unrestricted literal OR-word preservation
theorem, not a proof that one fixed flat `D^d` row survives.  A fixed-width
compiler needs deadline slack or a separate reflattening argument.  No
common-cap conclusion is made.

The exact verdict is

\[
\boxed{
 \begin{array}{l}
 \text{O(1) actual side-cell hosts}\Rightarrow\text{O(1) zero-loss birth};\\
 \text{two prepared exterior hosts close the octagon typed bank at +2};\\
 \text{the tensor-internal inverse exports none of those four hosts};\\
 \text{its local rectangle is already exact, while the donor rectangle is}\\
