# Haxell and local-lemma criteria for the q2 occurrence conflict graph

**Date:** 2026-08-05  
**Method:** pure graph/probability bounds applied to the exact occurrence
conflict graph; no computation or search  
**Status:** unconditional sufficient criteria and exact conflict ledgers.
They do not prove the q2 section for an arbitrary Middle Levels cycle.  The
near-unit average q2 list size shows why black-box thick-part theorems cannot
close the gate.

## 1. Residual occurrence conflict graph

Perform the exact singleton propagation of the sparse-kernel theorem.  For
every surviving q2 colour \(D\), let \(V_D\) be its surviving
occurrence-labelled witness terms.  A term assigns one or two repeated q1
labels to specified occurrences.

Make a graph \(G\) on the union of the \(V_D\).  Two vertices in different
parts are adjacent exactly when their equality assignments disagree on
some repeated q1 label.  Pairwise consistency of these partial equality
assignments is global consistency.  Hence

\[
 \boxed{\text{the residual positive q2 CSP is feasible iff }G
 \text{ has an independent transversal of the parts }V_D.} \tag{1.1}
\]

Write \(\ell_D=|V_D|\) and \(\Delta=\Delta(G)\).

## 2. Exact multiplicity degree bounds

Let \(m(Y)\) be the original occurrence multiplicity of a repeated q1
label.  One literal occurrence position is incident with at most two q2
witness indices.  Thus, for a term \(t\) assigning \(Y\) to occurrence
\(o\), the number of terms incompatible through \(Y\) is at most

\[
                         2(m(Y)-1).                 \tag{2.1}
\]

A term assigns at most two labels, so

\[
 \boxed{d_G(t)\le
 2\sum_{Y\in\operatorname{var}(t)}(m(Y)-1).}         \tag{2.2}
\]

If \(\mu=\max_Ym(Y)\), then

\[
                         \boxed{\Delta\le4(\mu-1).}  \tag{2.3}
\]

There is also an exact global formula before residual deletions.  For an
occurrence \(o\) of \(Y\), let \(a_Y(o)\in\{0,1,2\}\) be the number of
retained unequal-adjacency q2 witnesses incident with that occurrence.
Conflicts created by \(Y\) number

\[
 E_Y=\sum_{o<o'}a_Y(o)a_Y(o').                       \tag{2.4}
\]

No conflict pair is generated by two different q1 labels: two q2 witness
terms with the same two endpoint labels would have the same rank-\((r-1)\)
middle owner, which occurs only once in a spanning factor.  Hence

\[
 |E(G_{\rm full})|=\sum_YE_Y.                        \tag{2.5}
\]

In particular, if \(\kappa=\sum_Y(m(Y)-1)\), then

\[
 E_Y\le4{m(Y)\choose2},\qquad
 |E(G_{\rm full})|\le2\mu\kappa.                     \tag{2.6}
\]

## 3. Haxell criteria

For a graph without a finite total dominating set, put
\(\gamma_t=\infty\).  Haxell's domination criterion says that an
independent transversal exists if, for every subfamily \(\mathcal B\) of
parts,

\[
 \boxed{\gamma_t\!\left(
 G\left[\bigcup_{D\in\mathcal B}V_D\right]\right)
 \ge2|\mathcal B|.}                                  \tag{3.1}
\]

This allows unit parts and unequal lists when their induced conflicts
cannot be totally dominated too cheaply.

### Corollary 3.1 (thick-part criterion)

If \(\ell_D\ge2\Delta\) for every residual part, then the q2 section
exists.  By (2.3), the explicit multiplicity condition

\[
 \boxed{\ell_D\ge8(\mu-1)\quad\text{for every }D}     \tag{3.2}
\]

is sufficient.  Indeed, a total dominating set in a graph of maximum
degree \(\Delta\) has order at least \(|V|/\Delta\); (3.1) follows.

## 4. Class-pair local lemma

For distinct residual parts let

\[
 c_{DE}=e_G(V_D,V_E),\qquad
 \rho_D=|\{E:c_{DE}>0\}|.                            \tag{4.1}
\]

Choose one vertex uniformly and independently from every part.  For an
interacting pair \(D,E\), let \(A_{DE}\) be the event that the two choices
conflict.  Then

\[
 \Pr(A_{DE})={c_{DE}\over\ell_D\ell_E}.              \tag{4.2}
\]

The event depends only on the variables \(D,E\).  The asymmetric Lovasz
local lemma gives:

### Theorem 4.1 (asymmetric class-pair criterion)

If there are \(x_{DE}\in(0,1)\) such that for every interacting pair

\[
 {c_{DE}\over\ell_D\ell_E}
 \le x_{DE}
 \prod_{\substack{FG:\{F,G\}\cap\{D,E\}\ne\varnothing\\FG\ne DE}}
 (1-x_{FG}),                                         \tag{4.3}
\]

then the residual conflict graph has an independent transversal.

If uniformly

\[
 \ell_D\ge L,\qquad c_{DE}\le c,\qquad\rho_D\le\rho,
\]

the symmetric local lemma yields the convenient sufficient condition

\[
                         \boxed{L^2\ge e\,c(2\rho-1).} \tag{4.4}
\]

## 5. Boolean-lattice geometry of class interactions

A conflict between distinct q2 classes \(D,E\) must be caused by a common
repeated q1 label \(Y\).  Necessarily

\[
 D,E\subset Y,\quad |D|=|E|=r-3,\quad |Y|=r-2.
\]

For \(D\ne E\), this forces

\[
 Y=D\cup E,\qquad |D\cap E|=r-4.                    \tag{5.1}
\]

Thus the class-interaction graph is a subgraph of
\(J(2r-1,r-3)\), and

\[
                         \boxed{\rho_D\le(r-3)(r+2).} \tag{5.2}
\]

For fixed \(D,E\), the common label \(Y=D\cup E\) is unique.  A term of
class \(D\) incident with \(Y\) is indexed by a middle owner
\(Y+\{a\}\), with \(a\notin Y\); every owner occurs once.  Hence there
are at most \(r+1\) such terms, and

\[
                         \boxed{c_{DE}\le(r+1)^2.}   \tag{5.3}
\]

Equations (4.4), (5.2), and (5.3) give a fully explicit, though very
strong, dimension-only local-lemma certificate.

## 6. Why black-box spread cannot close the gate

Let \(M={2r-1\choose r-3}\) be the number of q2 classes.  There are at
most \(P={2r-1\choose r-1}\) unequal adjacent turn positions, so even
before residual deletions their average list size is at most

\[
 {P\over M}
 =\frac{(r+2)(r+1)}{(r-1)(r-2)}
 =1+\frac{6r}{(r-1)(r-2)}.                           \tag{6.1}
\]

The total list surplus is

\[
 P-M={3r\over r+2}\,\kappa<3\kappa.                 \tag{6.2}
\]

Thus all genuine choice lives in fewer than \(3\kappa\) nonsingleton
classes.  For \(r\ge9\), the average in (6.1) is below two, so some
original q2 class is a singleton.  If two singleton classes force
different occurrences of one repeated q1 label, the instance is
immediately infeasible.  No Haxell thickness or local-lemma estimate can
repair such a deterministic conflict.

The proof-safe order is therefore:

1. propagate the forced singleton core exactly;
2. apply (3.1), (3.2), or (4.3) only to the surviving flexible kernel; or
3. construct a chronology whose representatives are coherent by design.

The ordinary Middle Levels Theorem and the GMM/MSW factor theorems do not
state the singleton compatibility in item 1.  The PBBS max-height theorem
closes the positive q2 section by an explicit symbolic choice, not by
large-list spread.

## 7. Source and scope

The independent-transversal input is P. E. Haxell, *A Note on Vertex List
Colouring*, Combinatorics, Probability and Computing 10 (2001), 345--347;
the total-domination form is the standard Haxell ISR criterion from the
same hypergraph-matching framework.  Section 4 uses the ordinary
asymmetric Lovasz local lemma.

This note supplies sufficient certificates and exact conflict counts.  It
does not claim that an arbitrary Mutzе/GMM/MSW Hamilton cycle or factor
satisfies them, and it does not replace the separate graphic cycle-hit
clauses needed by the two-factor Pascal lift.
