## 0. Verdict

The new four-block relation closes an additive, width-graded source-signature
row.  It also has a nonempty pointwise cap common to its two endpoints.  These
facts do **not** make the literal four-block concatenation a physical `C8`
move.  The new owner-value quotient audit nevertheless gives a genuine
positive replacement: after selecting one physical edge per distinct lower
colour, every degree-two selection is Hamiltonian, and two selections in
each phase are upper-complete.  The central owner, both `q1` palettes,
topology, residence and nonzero maximal inverse are therefore simultaneously
realizable.  The remaining source-support difference is exactly two nested
rays on each directed side.

The exact conclusions are as follows.

1. The chordless `C8` margins leave only two global states.  With
   `eta=0110`, block `i` is in phase `eta_i xor sigma`, where
   `sigma in {0,1}`.  Four block phases cannot be chosen independently.
2. For either fixed global state, simultaneous common-`Q` feasibility is an
   exact maximal-set test.  Its full solution set is a union of products of
   Boolean set intervals.  Mixed rows crossing two or more blocks are handled
   by the same test and cannot in general be checked block by block.
3. The direct-sum signature-surplus graph is a connected `K_(2,2)`.  Hence
   attaching distinct block-private coordinates destroys the cancellation;
   all such tags would have to agree.  Private tagging cannot repair physical
   owner multiplicity.
4. In the literal Klein lift the `32d+92` central owner occurrences have only
   `8d+24` distinct values, almost all of multiplicity four.  The lower
   `q1` row has the same scale of repetition, and the deduplicated physical
   support has maximum middle degree four.  Exact `d`-letter source overlaps
   do not identify the owner windows.  Thus this literal model is neither a
   simple owner bank, a strict lower-palette bank, nor a cap-two factor.
5. Quotienting by owner value and choosing the physical edges jointly *does*
   supply the missing central weave.  There are sixteen lower-rainbow
   degree-two selections per phase, all Hamilton cycles, and exactly two per
   phase cover all sixteen upper colours.  A common-edge cut gives
   same-endpoint resident Hamilton paths on `8d+24` owners.
6. On every ray-clean cut the only directed support differences are the
   prefix and suffix rays in (5.1)--(5.2), of total size `2(d-1)` in either
   direction.  The three useful scalar faces must not be conflated: the
   minimum graded distance is `6d+4` but its old/new ray bases occur at
   different source addresses; aligning both bases costs `10d+12`; retaining
   all upper `q1` support costs `10d+20`.  On the aligned face two explicit
   common hosts are forced by the ray bases.  Their local common-`Q` set
   intervals are singletons, so local cap feasibility is proved.  These hosts
   are not native owner-preserving source letters.  Planting them
   owner-legally, closing the cut palette casualty, and passing the residual
   whole-word common-`Q` Hall rows remain open.

No owner-legal two-host planting or terminal compiler is constructed here,
and no CBC or `B+O(1)` conclusion is claimed.

## 1. The source-signature relation and its quantifiers

Let `G^0,G^1` be the two guarded sharp-octagon source blocks and let `Sigma`
denote their additive width-graded source signature.  The signature may
include every internal interval-OR occurrence and any guarded boundary row
which is part of the authenticated relation.  It does not silently include
cross-block rows that were not declared.

On the four active labels put

\[
 \alpha=(a_0\ a_2),\qquad \beta=(a_1\ a_3),
 \qquad \pi=(1,\beta,\alpha,\alpha\beta).                 \tag{1.1}
\]

The involutions commute.  Put

\[
                 \eta=(0,1,1,0),\qquad
                 \epsilon_i(\sigma)=\eta_i\mathbin\oplus\sigma.
                                                                  \tag{1.2}
\]

Thus the two endpoint phase patterns are `0110` and `1001`.  If

\[
                       \Delta=\Sigma(G^0)-\Sigma(G^1),             \tag{1.3}
\]

then their signed difference is

\[
 \sum_{i=0}^3 \pi_i\bigl(\Sigma(G^{\epsilon_i(0)})
                    -\Sigma(G^{\epsilon_i(1)})\bigr)
        =(1-\alpha)(1-\beta)\Delta.                              \tag{1.4}
\]

Consequently the new four-block identity is exactly the mixed-difference
relation

\[
                         (1-\alpha)(1-\beta)\Delta=0.              \tag{1.5}
\]

Equation (1.4) is formal and dimension independent: it is just expansion of
the two phase vectors.  Thus (1.5), rather than four independently selectable
phase equalities, is the precise source-signature content of the relation.

For the authenticated guarded block order, the three literal
suffix--prefix seam unions also agree pointwise at every pair of relative
lengths; intervals spanning a complete block see the common total union, and
the complete macro prefixes and suffixes agree pointwise.  Hence the actual
source theorem is the stronger contextual identity

\[
 \operatorname{Deck}_q(X\mathcal W^-Z)
   =\operatorname{Deck}_q(X\mathcal W^+Z)
       \qquad(q\ge1)                                            \tag{1.6}
\]

for arbitrary fixed exterior words `X,Z`.  Its octagon action is `4g_8`, not
zero.  Thus the relation is a genuine source-level `C8` move; the physical
failure below occurs only after owner dilation.

The whole-chain `C8` table independently forces

\[
 s_0=s_1,\qquad s_0=s_2,\qquad s_1=s_3,
 \qquad s_2=s_3.                                               \tag{1.7}
\]

After the fixed offsets `eta_i` are restored, (1.7) gives exactly (1.2).
There are only two global states.

At every corresponding sharp-source position `p`, the set

\[
 C_{i,p}=\pi_i\bigl(Q_p^0\cup Q_p^1\bigr)                       \tag{1.8}
\]

is nonempty and contains the source letter at both endpoints.  Therefore
the relation has a positive pointwise **containment cap**.  Equality of
required interval unions is a stronger condition and is treated next.

### Scope warning

Equation (1.6) applies to the fixed authenticated guarded macro.  A new
host, contraction, quotient chronology, or lower pin changes its row family;
every resulting mixed interval must then be added to Section 2.  Likewise,
(1.8) proves containment only; it does not prove positive cover after lower
pins and protected rows are intersected into the cap.

## 2. Exact maximal-screen theorem

Fix one global state `sigma`.  Let `P` be the set of all live screen/source
positions in the four blocks.  For `p in P`, let `C_p^sigma` be its current
cap, including the relabelled pointwise cap (1.8), every owner envelope and
every already fixed restriction.

Let `mathcal R` be the complete family of exact OR rows that this state must
realize.  A row `R` has:

* a target set `S_R`;
* a frozen exterior contribution `B_R^sigma`; and
* a set `J_R subseteq P` of live screens met by the row.

The desired screen letters `X_p` must be nonempty and satisfy

\[
 \varnothing\ne X_p\subseteq C_p^\sigma,
 \qquad
 B_R^\sigma\cup\bigcup_{p\in J_R}X_p=S_R.                     \tag{2.1}
\]

Define the maximal screen at `p` by

\[
 U_p^\sigma=C_p^\sigma\cap
             \bigcap_{R:\,p\in J_R}S_R,                        \tag{2.2}
\]

where an empty intersection of row targets means the whole ground set.

### Theorem 2.1 (four-block maximal-screen equivalence)

The system (2.1) is feasible if and only if

\[
\begin{aligned}
 &B_R^\sigma\subseteq S_R &&(R\in\mathcal R),\tag{2.3a}\\
 &U_p^\sigma\ne\varnothing &&(p\in P),\tag{2.3b}\\
 &S_R=B_R^\sigma\cup\bigcup_{p\in J_R}U_p^\sigma
                                      &&(R\in\mathcal R).\tag{2.3c}
\end{aligned}
\]

When (2.3) holds, the componentwise maximal word

\[
                              X_p=U_p^\sigma                     \tag{2.4}
\]

is a witness.

#### Proof

Every feasible `X_p` is contained in every row target through `p`; hence
`X_p subseteq U_p^sigma`.  Equation (2.1) therefore implies (2.3a),
(2.3b), and the inclusion

\[
 S_R\subseteq B_R^\sigma\cup\bigcup_{p\in J_R}U_p^\sigma.
\]

The reverse inclusion follows from (2.3a) and the definition (2.2), proving
(2.3c).  Conversely, (2.3b) makes (2.4) a nonzero word, and (2.3c) verifies
every row.  \(\square\)

The theorem includes every cross-block row.  Checking its restrictions to
the four blocks separately is sufficient only when no row meets more than
one block and no further equality couples their screen choices.

### Theorem 2.2 (exact set-interval decomposition)

Put

\[
                           D_R=S_R\setminus B_R^\sigma.          \tag{2.5}
\]

Assume (2.3).  For every demanded pair `(R,x)` with `x in D_R`, choose one
screen

\[
                 \omega(R,x)\in J_R
                 \quad\hbox{with}\quad x\in U_{\omega(R,x)}^\sigma.
                                                                    \tag{2.6}
\]

For each `p`, put

\[
 L_p(\omega)=\{x:\omega(R,x)=p\text{ for at least one }R\}.      \tag{2.7}
\]

If `L_p(omega)` is empty, choose an arbitrary `e_p in U_p^sigma` and replace
`L_p(omega)` by `{e_p}`.  Then every choice in the product of Boolean set
intervals

\[
               L_p(\omega)\subseteq X_p\subseteq U_p^\sigma
                         \qquad(p\in P)                           \tag{2.8}
\]

satisfies (2.1).  Conversely, every solution of (2.1) lies in at least one
product (2.8).

#### Proof

Condition (2.3c) guarantees a choice in (2.6).  The lower inclusions in
(2.8) cover every element of every `D_R`; the upper inclusions introduce no
element outside a row target through `p`.  Together with (2.3a), this gives
(2.1).  Nonemptiness is supplied by the chosen `e_p` when necessary.

Conversely, from a solution choose, for every `x in D_R`, one
`p in J_R` with `x in X_p`.  Use these choices as `omega`; at any unused
screen choose `e_p in X_p`.  Then that solution satisfies (2.8).  \(\square\)

This is the requested exact set-interval normal form.  A mixed row causes a
choice of which screen carries a demanded coordinate, but no hidden
nonlinear condition remains once the maximal sets (2.2) reconstruct all
rows.

### Corollary 2.3 (one-screen rows)

Suppose all rows under consideration meet one live screen `p` and no other
live screen.  Put

\[
 L_p^\sigma=\bigcup_R(S_R\setminus B_R^\sigma),
 \qquad
 U_p^\sigma=C_p^\sigma\cap\bigcap_R S_R.                       \tag{2.9}
\]

They are feasible exactly when

\[
 B_R^\sigma\subseteq S_R\quad\hbox{for every }R,qquad
 L_p^\sigma\subseteq U_p^\sigma,qquad U_p^\sigma\ne\varnothing.
                                                                    \tag{2.10}
\]

Every nonempty `X_p` in the interval `[L_p^sigma,U_p^sigma]` works.  If a
carrier row forces a coordinate `c_p^sigma`, adjoin it to `L_p^sigma`.  If
a literal screen `H_p^sigma` is pinned, replace (2.10) by the exact condition

\[
       \varnothing\ne H_p^\sigma,\qquad
                      L_p^\sigma\subseteq H_p^\sigma
                                      \subseteq U_p^\sigma.       \tag{2.11}
\]

## 3. The global `C8` phase gate and protected rows

For block-private rows, let `F_i subseteq {0,1}` be the set of **raw block
phases** passing Corollary 2.3 (or the full within-block form of Theorem
2.1).  Define normalized feasible global states by

\[
                         \widehat F_i
          =\{\sigma:\eta_i\mathbin\oplus\sigma\in F_i\}.         \tag{3.1}
\]

Then one endpoint common-`Q` state exists exactly when

\[
                          \bigcap_{i=0}^3\widehat F_i\ne\varnothing.
                                                                    \tag{3.2}
\]

A closed switch needs both global states.  On the block-private face this is
equivalent to `F_i={0,1}` for every `i`.  With cross-block rows, (3.2) must be
replaced by the two complete tests (2.3), one for each `sigma`.

The normalization in (3.1) is essential.  For example, pin block `0` to a
row legal only in raw phase `0`, and pin block `2` to a distinct row also
legal only in raw phase `0`.  Each local system is feasible, but
`eta_0=0` and `eta_2=1`, so the two rows force opposite values of `sigma`.
No global `C8` state exists.  This is the smallest two-block correlation
obstruction.

There is also an exact transparency test for a protected row retained at
the same target in both endpoint states.  Suppose its frozen contribution is
the same set `E` in both states and its phase-forced live contributions are
`A^0` and `A^1`.  Then the row can have the same value at both endpoints if
and only if

\[
                           A^0\mathbin\triangle A^1\subseteq E.   \tag{3.3}
\]

Indeed, (3.3) is equivalent to `E union A^0=E union A^1`.  In the general
case of different exterior contributions or shrinkable screens, the exact
criterion is simply Theorem 2.1 at both endpoints; (3.3) must not be applied
after discarding those distinctions.

## 4. Private-tag obstruction and the literal physical no-go

### Lemma 4.1 (`K_(2,2)` surplus connectivity forbids private tags)

Form the block-transfer graph of the direct-sum relation: join old block `i`
to new block `j` whenever a noncommon graded-signature occurrence is
cancelled between those blocks.  For the authenticated four-block relation
this graph is the connected graph `K_(2,2)`.

Attach a tag set `tau_i`, disjoint from every old coordinate, uniformly to
all source letters of block `i`.  The tagged signature relation can hold
only if

\[
                              \tau_i=\tau_j                         \tag{4.1}
\]

on every transfer edge.  Hence all four tags must agree.  In particular,
pairwise-private tags destroy the cancellation.

#### Proof

Every nonempty interval lying in block `i` gains exactly `tau_i`.  A
noncommon occurrence transferred from block `i` to block `j` therefore has
tagged values `S union tau_i` and `S union tau_j`.  Since all tags are fresh,
equality forces (4.1).  Connectivity propagates equality to every block.
\(\square\)

Thus the most immediate attempt to distinguish the repeated physical owner
copies is incompatible with the source relation.

### Theorem 4.2 (literal Klein four-block model is not a carrier)

For every depth in the authenticated range `2<=d<=12`, the literal
four-block Klein model has the
following exact occurrence ledgers at either endpoint:

\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c}
\text{row}&\text{occurrences}&\text{distinct values}
 &\text{multiplicity histogram}\\ \hline
\text{central owners}&32d+92&8d+24&2^2,\ 4^{8d+22}\\
\text{lower }q1&32d+88&8d+24&2^4,\ 4^{8d+20}\\
\text{upper }q1&32d+88&16&2^4,\ 4^4,\ (4d+8)^8.

After deduplicating physical edges, the graph has

\[
             8d+40\text{ edges},\qquad
             \deg\text{-histogram }2^{8d},3^{16},4^8.           \tag{4.3}
\]

In particular it has maximum middle degree four.  The largest literal
phase-common `d`-letter collars at the three joins still miss six required

not, without a separate exact screen calculation, rule out every smaller

Consequently the literal model is not a simple exact owner bank, not a
strict lower-`q1` bank, and not a cap-two physical factor.  Neither the

#### Proof

The closed formulas (4.2)--(4.3) are the exact orbit counts of the four
relabelled tensor paths.  Their two endpoint owner/lower/upper counters
agree, but almost every owner and lower colour occurs four times.  A simple
owner factor requires one physical occurrence per owner and degree at most
two, while a strict lower palette requires load one.  Thus either of the
first two rows and independently (4.3) is fatal.

For the overlap statement, a source block has length `9d+25` after its two
guards.  Overlapping each neighboring pair in `d` source positions does not
identify a width-`d+1` window.  Direct maximal-collar reconstruction gives
the stated six losses and fourteen births.  The multiplicity and degree
obstructions already prove the theorem, independently of how a smaller
collar behaves.  \(\square\)

The theorem is scoped to the literal four complete paths and their stated
overlaps.  It does not exclude a nonliteral quotient weave which identifies
or reroutes interior owner occurrences while preserving the source
signature.

### Theorem 4.3 (exact folded Hamilton lift, `2<=d<=12`)

Quotient the literal support by owner value and, for each distinct lower
`q1` colour, retain exactly one incident physical edge.  In either phase:

1. `8d+8` lower colours have one provider and sixteen have two providers;
2. exactly sixteen provider selections give degree two at all `8d+24`
   owners;
3. all sixteen selections are Hamilton cycles; and
4. exactly two Hamilton cycles cover all sixteen upper `q1` colours.

Every old/new pair among these two upper-complete cycles has the same upper
multiplicity counter.  Cutting a common edge produces same-endpoint
Hamilton paths, and every audited common cut is depth-`d` resident and has a
nonzero maximal inverse.  The cut deletes exactly one lower `q1` colour.

There are three useful cut faces.

* A **minimum-signature cut** has graded counter distance `6d+4` and loses
  one upper `q1` support value in addition to the lower cut colour.  Its
  old/new prefix bases occur at `(3d+6,7d+18)` and its old/new suffix bases
  at `(2d+6,6d+18)`.  Thus it does not by itself supply fixed common host
  slots.
* An **aligned-base cut** has the same prefix and suffix ray-base addresses in
  both phases, namely `4d+12` and `5d+12`.  Its source-support differences
  are exactly (5.1)--(5.2), its graded counter distance is `10d+12`, and its
  cut upper counter has support fifteen.  Its one missing upper value is the
  same in both phases:

  \[
             U_*=K\cup\{z,a_0,a_2,f_0,f_1,\ldots,f_d\}.         \tag{4.4}
  \]
* An **upper-safe cut** retains all sixteen upper values and has the same
  two-chain support difference, but its minimum graded counter distance is
  `10d+20`.  No fixed common two-host alignment is supplied by the audited
  upper-safe statement.

#### Proof

After owner-value quotient, sort the providers by lower colour.  The provider
histogram is `1^(8d+8)2^16`.  The degree-two equations on the sixteen binary
choices have exactly sixteen solutions; direct traversal of each selected
support gives one cycle on all `8d+24` owners.  Computing the upper counters
leaves exactly two solutions per phase with support sixteen and gives the
common counter assertion.  Deleting a common cycle edge gives a Hamilton
path with common endpoints.  Direct coordinate-run and maximal-erosion
tests give residence and nonzero inversion.

Minimizing over all ray-clean cuts gives graded distance `6d+4`.  Restricting
to cuts with both base addresses aligned gives (5.1)--(5.2), the addresses
in (5.5), and graded distance `10d+12`; its cut upper counter has support
fifteen.  The aligned common cut has endpoints

\[
 K\cup\{z,a_0,a_2\}\cup\{f_1,\ldots,f_d\},\qquad
 K\cup\{z,a_0,f_0\}\cup\{f_1,\ldots,f_d\},
\]

so their union is (4.4), independently of phase.  The upper-safe subfamily
has support sixteen and minimum graded distance `10d+20`, with the displayed
address displacement.  These are exhaustive finite statements for
`2<=d<=12` in the authenticated audit.  \(\square\)

Thus the quotient fold supplies the nonliteral central weave left open by
Theorem 4.2.  It must be selected jointly; the previously frozen `m=9`
forest contains no such folded path as a post-hoc subobject.

## 5. Exact two-ray host and common-`Q` gate

Suppress the fixed core `K` only in the notation.  Let
`F[i,j]={f_i,...,f_j}`.  For the aligned cut, the old-only source values are

\[
\begin{aligned}
 \mathcal P^-&=\{K\cup\{z,a_3\}\cup F[1,t]:1\le t\le d-1\},\\
 \mathcal S^-&=\{K\cup\{z,a_1\}\cup F[t,d]:2\le t\le d\},       \tag{5.1}
\end{aligned}
\]

and the new-only values are

\[
\begin{aligned}
 \mathcal P^+&=\{K\cup\{z,a_1\}\cup F[1,t]:1\le t\le d-1\},\\
 \mathcal S^+&=\{K\cup\{z,a_3\}\cup F[t,d]:2\le t\le d\}.       \tag{5.2}
\end{aligned}
\]

These are four nested chains, two in either directed difference.  Their
bases are

\[
\begin{array}{c|cc}
 &-&+\\ \hline
 b_L&K\cup\{z,a_3,f_1\}&K\cup\{z,a_1,f_1\}\\
 b_R&K\cup\{z,a_1,f_d\}&K\cup\{z,a_3,f_d\},
\end{array}                                                     \tag{5.3}
\]

and the two minimal phase-common host unions are

\[
 X_L=K\cup\{z,a_1,a_3,f_1\},\qquad
 X_R=K\cup\{z,a_1,a_3,f_d\}.                                  \tag{5.4}
\]

In the audited aligned source the four bases occur as singleton source
letters at the same two addresses in both phases, and the invariant address
statement is

\[
                              p_R-p_L=d.                         \tag{5.5}
\]

(Absolute offsets depend on the chosen guarded serialization.)  Neither
`X_L` nor `X_R` is a legal native maximal-erosion letter at those internal
addresses.

### Theorem 5.1 (two-host local common-`Q` equivalence)

Assume an owner-legal construction supplies two old host letters `X_L,X_R`
adjacent to the corresponding prefix and suffix filler rails.  Split and
orient them as follows:

\[
\begin{array}{c|cc}
\text{native state}&\text{left host, far-to-near}
                    &\text{right host, near-to-far}\\ \hline
-&(X_L,b_L^+)&(b_R^+,X_R)\\
+&(X_L,b_L^-)&(b_R^-,X_R).
\end{array}                                                     \tag{5.6}
\]

Use `X_L` as the common cap on both left cells and `X_R` as the common cap
on both right cells.  Then:

1. all source letters in (5.6) are nonempty and cap-legal in both phases;
2. the near left cell and its filler fan realize the complete missing prefix
   chain, and the near right cell and its filler fan realize the complete
   missing suffix chain;
3. if the two old host letters were refined rather than freshly inserted,
   every old interval transports by full-block contraction; and
4. on the local ray rows, the maximal common-`Q` equations force the four
   displayed letters in (5.6) exactly.  There is no residual local cap
   choice;
5. the `2d-2` ray targets occupy pairwise-distinct physical cells and have
   a literal diagonal perfect matching; and
6. the unique cell trimming both host blocks is phase-common, so no
   additional cross-host support debt is born.

#### Proof

The two bases in each row of (5.3) are subsets of the corresponding union
in (5.4).  Reading from `b_L^+` (respectively `b_L^-`) into the prefix filler
rail successively adds `f_2,...,f_(d-1)` and gives `mathcal P^+`
(respectively `mathcal P^-`).  The suffix statement is the reverse reading
from `b_R^+` or `b_R^-`.  The far full host is outside these one-sided fan
intervals.  This proves items 1--2.  Replacing one old letter `X` by two
consecutive letters with union `X` gives the canonical full-block lift of
every old interval, proving item 3.

For common `Q`, pin the far cell to the singleton row `X_L` or `X_R`.  Its
lower and upper screen bounds are both `X`.  At the near left cell, the
intersection of every target in the required ray chain is exactly the base
`b_L`; the singleton base row makes the lower bound the same set.  Hence its
set interval is `[b_L,b_L]`.  The suffix calculation is identical.  Theorem
2.1 now proves item 4.  The prefix and suffix fan cells lie on opposite
sides of the rail, so they are distinct, and their displayed values are
distinct; matching each ray target to its displayed cell proves item 5.
Finally

\[
 b_L^-\cup b_R^-=b_L^+\cup b_R^+
   =K\cup\{z,a_1,a_3,f_1,f_d\}.                              \tag{5.7}
\]

Every interval trimming both host blocks contains the intermediate filler
rail, and (5.7) makes its value phase-common.  This proves item 6.  \(\square\)

### Exact boundary of Theorem 5.1

The theorem proves **distinct-value support closure** of the two ray banks
and exact local common-`Q` feasibility.  It does not prove equality of the
complete width-graded occurrence counters.  Any protected addressed
occurrence bank must be included explicitly in Theorem 2.1.

Equivalently, if `A^-\setminus A^+=mathcal P^- dotcup mathcal S^-` and the
reverse difference is `mathcal P^+ dotcup mathcal S^+` at the level of
distinct support, while `H^-` and `H^+` are the two local host words from
(5.6) in their same-phase orientations, then

\[
 \operatorname{Supp}(A^-)\cup\operatorname{Supp}(H^+)
   =\operatorname{Supp}(A^+)\cup\operatorname{Supp}(H^-).       \tag{5.8}
\]

The opposite orientation in (5.8) is load-bearing.  Using `H^-` with
`A^-` and `H^+` with `A^+` doubles rather than cancels the support residue.
Equation (5.8) is a support identity, not a contextual concatenation theorem;
prefix/suffix mixed rows with the ambient word remain in Theorem 2.1.
It is also not a graded-occurrence identity.  On the authenticated
`2<=d<=12` range, the aligned folded pair has graded `L1=10d+12`, while the
two-host local pair has `L1=4d-4`; exact opposite-phase composition leaves
graded residue `6d+16`.  The corresponding upper-safe residue is `6d+24`.
Thus a compiler protecting addressed multiplicity needs an additional
linear occurrence transport even after distinct support closes.

 U_{p_L}^\epsilon=b_L^\epsilon,qquad
 U_{p_R}^\epsilon=b_R^\epsilon.                                \tag{5.9}
\]

Hence a singleton host row requiring `X_L` or `X_R` fails (2.3c).  Neither
host is common-`Q` feasible on the fixed folded owner path.

#### Proof

Maximal erosion is the intersection of all owner targets using a source
position.  At the two anchors the audit identifies those intersections with
the corresponding bases in (5.3).  Intersecting the free phase-union cap
with the owner targets therefore gives (5.9).  Since each base is a strict
subset of its host union, the singleton reconstruction target `X` cannot be
obtained.  This is exactly Theorem 2.1, not merely a rank objection.
\(\square\)

More importantly, the host hypothesis is currently false in the native
folded source.  Maximal erosion is the componentwise largest source word
which dilates to the fixed owner path, and (5.4) strictly enlarges its letters
at (5.5).  The two hosts must therefore be planted at clipped endpoints,
paid by a simultaneous owner rethread/contraction, or implemented by a
nonflat compiler.  The upper-safe cut additionally needs a common-slot
alignment theorem (or a moving-host state) before (5.6) applies literally.
On the aligned-base face the alternative is one
phase-common external witness for `U_*` in (4.4).
If the two host letters already exist, their binary refinements cost two
source positions in total.  If both two-cell blocks must be inserted from
scratch, the raw charge is four positions.  A zero-charge use therefore
needs two or four certified contractions respectively; no such contraction
ledger follows from local cap feasibility.

For any additional owner, upper, protected or lower row meeting a host
cell, local feasibility is still exactly (2.3).  In particular, a target
omitting one compulsory element of the relevant `b_L^epsilon`,
`b_R^epsilon`, or `X` is an immediate set-interval obstruction.

### Theorem 5.3 (canonical birail Hall deficiency is zero)

Encode a two-prefix terminal task by left and right thresholds `x_i,y_i`.
For the canonical folded two-ray bank the marginal multisets are

\[
 \{x_i\}=\{y_i\}=\{0^{d-1},1,2,\ldots,d-1\},
 \qquad N=2d-2.                                               \tag{5.10}
\]

Put `L(a)=#\{i:x_i<=a\}`, `R(c)=#\{i:y_i<=c\}` and

\[
 E_L=\max_a(L(a)-a),\qquad E_R=\max_c(R(c)-c).                 \tag{5.11}
\]

Then `E_L=E_R=d-1`, and therefore the minimum corner/Hall deficiency is
exactly zero.  Pairing increasing left thresholds with decreasing right
thresholds attains it.

#### Proof

For `0<=a<=d-1`, (5.10) gives `L(a)=d-1+a`, and the same formula holds on
the right.  Hence `E_L=E_R=d-1` and `E_L+E_R=N`.  The antitone-birail
optimum theorem gives

\[
 \min_\pi\max_{a,c}
 \bigl(\#\{i:x_i\le a,\ y_{\pi(i)}\le c\}-a-c\bigr)_+
       =(E_L+E_R-N)_+=0.                                    \tag{5.12}
\]

\(\square\)

Removing a strict concordance by one `2x2` endpoint swap decreases every
southwest corner count pointwise.  Hence the abstract birail selector has no
nonglobal local minimum.  The folded `C8` ray exchange has exactly this
comparator shape: it swaps `a_1,a_3` between the prefix and suffix chains.

This closes the **abstract ray Hall row**.  A physical compiler still needs
the owner-legal hosts of Theorem 5.1, occurrence-labelled transport of the
actual adjacent obligations into (5.10), and one matching-closed cap state.
A fresh split charge per comparator is unusable; the two-host state must be
recycled, so its total length charge `chi` is bounded globally rather than
per swap.

## 6. Exact lower completion after the folded lift

The folded Hamilton theorem supplies the central owner and both `q1` rows,
but opening its cycle creates one lower-colour casualty.  On the aligned cut
there is also one upper-colour casualty.  The two host fans, if planted,
reserve `2(d-1)` phase-opposite ray target-cell incidences.  These mandatory
incidences are physical only after their source slots and fan cells are
owner-legal and pairwise distinct.

Fix `sigma`.  Let `psi` be a proposed occurrence injection of all mandatory
ray, cut-repair and guarded tokens into pairwise-distinct physical cells of
the folded carrier.  Let `phi` inject the remaining lower targets into cells
unused by `psi`.  Include in `mathcal R(sigma,psi,phi)`:

* every mandatory channel target-cell equality;
* every equality selected by `phi`;
* all owner, upper, protected, cross-block and frozen-exterior rows; and
* every source cap of the proposed physical carrier.

For a live source position `p`, define

\[
 Q_p(\sigma,\psi,\phi)=C_p^\sigma\cap
       \bigcap_{R:\,p\in J_R}S_R.                              \tag{6.1}
\]

### Theorem 6.1 (nonlinear physical compiler equivalence)

The pair `(psi,phi)` gives a literal common-`Q` lower compiler in state
`sigma` if and only if:

1. `psi union phi` is injective on physical cells and respects the declared
   target multiplicities;
2. every set in (6.1) is nonempty;
3. `B_R^sigma subseteq S_R` for every row; and
4. every row reconstructs:

   \[
        S_R=B_R^\sigma\cup\bigcup_{p\in J_R}
                                      Q_p(\sigma,\psi,\phi).     \tag{6.2}
   \]

The separate Johnson adjacency, owner simplicity, degree-two topology,
residence and connectivity conditions must also hold in the proposed
carrier; they are not OR equations.

#### Proof

After `psi,phi` are fixed, Theorem 2.1 applies verbatim to their complete
row family.  Conditions 2--4 are exactly its necessary and sufficient
conditions; condition 1 is the lower occurrence-capacity condition.
\(\square\)

This existential nonlinear system is the exact remaining statement before
a guarded atlas is proved.  Individual incidence legality and separate
per-block common-`Q` feasibility do not imply it.

For the canonical two-ray subbank itself, Theorems 5.1 and 5.3 make its
local contribution to the Hall deficiency zero.  Thus any positive residual
cut comes from the cut-colour repair, addressed/protected occurrences,
ambient targets, or cap-state incompatibility—not from the marginal
two-ray threshold counts.

### Definition 6.2 (matching-closed guarded atlas)

A physical guarded state `theta` consists of one global phase, one simple
physical carrier, its mandatory occurrence matching `M_0^theta`, and an
occurrence-labelled residual target-cell graph `G_theta`.  It is
**matching closed** if every matching in `G_theta` is jointly co-selectable
with `M_0^theta` and passes the complete maximal-`Q` equations in that same
state.

This is exactly the trace-guarded property needed to remove cap conflicts;
individual edge legality is weaker.

### Theorem 6.3 (conditional residual Hall)

Let `theta` be a matching-closed guarded state.  Contract the mandatory
matching and write

\[
 D_\theta=\mathcal L\setminus V_L(M_0^\theta),\qquad
 C_\theta=\mathcal C\setminus V_C(M_0^\theta).                 \tag{6.3}
\]

Then its exact residual deletion deficiency is

\[
 \delta_\theta=
 \max_{X\subseteq D_\theta}
 \left(|X|-|N_{G_\theta[D_\theta,C_\theta]}(X)|\right)_+ .     \tag{6.4}
\]

In particular the residual lower row is complete if and only if all Hall
cuts in (6.4) are nonpositive.

If the free cells are genuinely partitioned into a packet bank and a
background bank, with corresponding transversal matroids `M_P^theta` and
`M_B^theta`, (6.4) is equivalently

\[
 r_{M_P^\theta}(X)+r_{M_B^\theta}(X)\ge |X|
                    \qquad(X\subseteq D_\theta).                \tag{6.5}
\]

A further sum over four block matroids is valid only if the four physical
cell banks are disjoint, every cross-block cell has been assigned to one
bank, and their joint atlas is matching closed.

#### Proof

Matching closure makes every graph matching physically common-`Q` exact.
Hall's theorem therefore gives (6.4).  Conversely every physical residual
assignment is a graph matching, so no weaker cut suffices.  Formula (6.5)
is the matroid-union rank criterion for the disjoint packet/background cell
partition.  \(\square\)

Let `Theta_phys(sigma)` be all such physical states in global phase `sigma`,
and value an empty fibre at `+infinity`.  The exact terminal lower deficiency
on this atlas is

\[
 \boxed{
 \delta=
   \min_{\sigma\in\{0,1\}}
   \min_{\theta\in\Theta_{\rm phys}(\sigma)}
   \max_{X\subseteq D_\theta}
   \left(|X|-|N_{G_\theta}(X)|\right)_+.}                      \tag{6.6}
\]

One may not union the two phase graphs, union individually legal block
incidences, or exchange the two minima with the maximum.  A closed physical
`C8` generator requires a compatible endpoint pair and regeneration, which
is stronger than separately minimizing (6.6).

## 7. Smallest remaining theorem

The common-`Q` algebra itself is now exact.  The smallest missing positive
statement is an **owner-legal two-ray host and compiler theorem** supplying:

   than inside a pre-existing forest;
2. two fixed owner-legal sockets carrying (5.6), or a moving-host analogue
   on an upper-safe cut;
3. repair of the one cut lower colour and, on the aligned face, the one cut
   upper colour;
4. preservation or explicit rematching of every addressed occurrence not
   covered by distinct-value ray support; and
5. a matching-closed residual occurrence atlas (or a direct solution of
   Theorem 6.1); and
6. a physical realization of the concordance-removing comparator on arbitrary
   adjacent terminal obligations while recycling the same two-host/cut state,
   so the total retained length charge `chi` is `O(1)` rather than one charge
   per comparator.

The central quotient weave is no longer missing.  The exact obstruction has
moved to planting the two nonnative hosts without changing the folded owner
path, and then passing the global—not merely local—common-`Q` rows.  The
connected `K_(2,2)` tag lemma still rules out implementing those sockets by
uniform block-private coordinates.  Once items 1--6 hold, Theorem 5.3 says
the canonical ray margins contribute **zero** Hall defect; there is no
remaining scalar birail loss to absorb.
