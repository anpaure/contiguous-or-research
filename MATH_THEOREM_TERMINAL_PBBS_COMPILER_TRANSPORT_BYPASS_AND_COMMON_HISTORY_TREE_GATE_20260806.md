# Terminal PBBS compiler transport bypasses the two-coordinate cap

**Date:** 2026-08-06  
**Method:** composition of literal occurrence injections and common-history
Euler splicing; no computation or search  
**Status:** unconditional reduction theorem.  On a terminal, nonrecursive
branch, one already materialized strict-lower compiler matching transports
through every source-level PBBS rethread currently used.  Hence the
two-coordinate typed cap is not a terminal necessity.  The missing theorem is
earlier: construct one resident ordinary antecedent realizing the initial
gap-section compiler and extend/fuse it through the owner-factor completion.

## 1. Literal compiler transport is functorial

For a source word `A`, let `C_<r(A)` be its occurrence-labelled contiguous
intervals whose OR has rank below the middle rank `r`.  A **literal lower
transport** from `A` to `A'` on a cell family `D subseteq C_<r(A)` is an
injective map

\[
                 \Phi:D\longrightarrow C_{<r}(A')
\tag{1.1}
\]

such that

\[
                 \operatorname {OR}_{A'}(\Phi(J))
                 =\operatorname {OR}_{A}(J)
                 \qquad(J\in D).
\tag{1.2}
\]

### Theorem 1.1 (terminal compiler functoriality)

Let `mu` be a matching of a strict-lower target family `L` to distinct cells
of `A^(0)`.  Suppose a sequence of source rewrites

\[
 A^{(0)}\longrightarrow A^{(1)}\longrightarrow\cdots
 \longrightarrow A^{(t)}
\tag{1.3}
\]

has literal lower transports `Phi_j`, and the domain of each `Phi_j` contains
the image of `mu` under the preceding transports.  Then

\[
 \Phi_t\circ\cdots\circ\Phi_1\circ\mu
\tag{1.4}
\]

is a matching of the same targets to distinct literal cells of `A^(t)`.

No common-cap matching, second occurrence coordinate, or terminal socket is
needed to certify those targets in the final word.

#### Proof

Equation (1.2) preserves the target value at every step.  Injectivity
preserves distinct physical addresses.  A composition of injective,
value-preserving maps is injective and value-preserving.  Hence (1.4) is a
literal compiler matching.  \(\square\)

The theorem is deliberately terminal.  An active child continuation may
still require two occurrence coordinates or a typed state even though the
present target is already covered.

## 2. Audit of the current PBBS moves

The current source-level moves divide cleanly.

1. A prospectively common-history-planted clean `C6` has a value-, width-,
   and occurrence-bijection on every strict-lower source interval.
2. The rank-three-screen pentagon has the same complete strict-lower
   occurrence bijection, provided its common history and complete moved

3. Serial clean ears and pentagons compose their occurrence bijections; no

4. The split role-zero macro has an occurrence-**injective** exact-value map

5. The budgeted deadline collar copies every cyclic interval of width at

Thus none of these local moves creates a new lower Hall problem after one
literal matching has been materialized.

### 2.2 Steps which do **not** yet have such a transport

1. The gap-potential section gives one selected lower occurrence at every
   depth in the canonical **owner** factor, but its own scope explicitly
   excludes an ordinary-word realization.  For depths larger than `d`, the
   maximal-antecedent row identity does not turn that owner intersection

2. The tagged-port geodesic chain, the rank-stratified upper-backup paths,

3. The unprotected cycles added by factor completion are not presently

This is the exact break.  All local source rewrites are compiler-functorial;
the global owner completion has not yet been lifted to the source category.

## 3. Common-history tree fusion preserves the whole short deck

The source-fusion part has an exact sufficient form.

Let `V` index cyclic `d`-resident owner components.  On component `v`, let
`T^v` be its Johnson trace, `P_j^v` its maximal antecedent envelopes, and

\[
                         F_j^v=\{D_j^v,I_{j-d-1}^v\}
\tag{3.1}
\]

its forced source letters.  Suppose each component already has an antecedent
carrying its assigned compiler cells.

Choose a tree `K` on `V`.  For every incidence `(v,e)` with `e in E(K)`,
choose a block of `d` consecutive source positions on component `v`, with
the chosen blocks on one component separated by at least one unaltered
maximal position.  For `e=uv`, align the two blocks and assume

\[
 F_{e,j}^{u}\cup F_{e,j}^{v}
       \subseteq P_{e,j}^{u}\cap P_{e,j}^{v}
       \qquad(0\le j<d).
\tag{3.2}
\]

Put

\[
                         H_{e,j}=F_{e,j}^{u}\cup F_{e,j}^{v}.
\tag{3.3}
\]

### Theorem 3.1 (short-deck-preserving history-tree fusion)

Under (3.2), the component antecedents may be chosen to contain the common
history `(H_(e,0),...,H_(e,d-1))` at both ends of every tree edge.  Their
order-`d` de Bruijn circuits then splice to one Euler circuit which uses
every labelled owner window once and preserves occurrence-injectively every
source interval of width at most `d+1` from every component.

Consequently the union of the component compiler matchings remains a
literal compiler matching in the fused source word, with no added source
position.

#### Proof

Condition (3.2) is exactly the common-history criterion for one pair of
blocks.  The separated-short-block freedom theorem allows all blocks on one
component to be altered simultaneously: each block has length `d`, retains
every forced endpoint, and the intervening maximal position separates the
support gaps made by distinct blocks.  This proves the common histories.

Regard every `(d+1)`-letter owner window as a labelled edge of the order-`d`
de Bruijn graph.  Each component antecedent is an Euler circuit.  The two
circuits joined by a tree edge share the vertex (3.3), so splice one circuit
into the other there.  Induction over the tree gives one Euler circuit of
their union and adds no edge or source position.

Every starting address and width at most `d+1` is determined by one labelled
edge together with an offset inside its retained `d`-history.  The splice
changes only the traversal order at an equal history vertex; it neither
changes the labelled edge nor its internal subwords.  Hence the complete
short-cell multiset, with occurrence labels, is transported.  Apply Theorem
1.1 to the selected compiler cells.  \(\square\)

### Guarded-seam warning

Theorem 3.1 is a source theorem, not automatically an owner-palette theorem.
The two new cross-component adjacencies at a de Bruijn splice need not be
Johnson edges.  A PBBS application must additionally require that each tree
splice is made at a prepared graph connector, or that its two replaced
immediate colours and upper casualties already belong to the protected
backup bank.  This seam condition is separate from lower compiler transport.

## 4. The fixed-`M_0` residual Hall gate is an alternative, not a PBBS gate

The rank-stratified upper backups are deliberately added as **unoriented**
owner paths to the tagged protected forest.  The protected-factor theorem
extends the whole bank to a simple two-factor; orienting a factor cycle may
reverse a backup path, but reversal does not change its owner set or owner
union.  Hence every upper target remains witnessed in either orientation.

Therefore it is unnecessary, on this route, to seed every upper path in one
preselected matching `M_0` and then prove residual Hall in `G-M_0`.  That is
a valid alternative coloured construction, not a load-bearing missing row
of the current tagged-port construction.

## 5. Terminal cap bypass

Suppose the canonical bottom PBBS source has one literal matching `mu` for
all strict-lower targets, and suppose the subsequent construction consists
of:

1. common-history `C6`/pentagon transports and split transports from
   Section 2.1;
2. a resident component completion satisfying Theorem 3.1 and the guarded
   seam condition; and
3. the ordinary deadline opening collar.

Then Theorem 1.1 gives a zero-defect lower compiler in the final word.
Upper targets are supplied by the protected upper paths.  On this terminal
branch, the two-coordinate typed common cap has no remaining target-coverage
job and may be deleted from the proof.

It remains relevant in either of two situations:

* it is the mechanism used to manufacture the **initial** literal bottom
  matching; or
* a recursive child continuation needs two active occurrence coordinates or
  another nonpassive typed service.

## 6. Weakest exact remaining lemma

The direct terminal route needs the following statement, strictly weaker
than a two-coordinate antecedent atlas.

> **Protected one-copy PBBS antecedent completion.**  Materialize the
> canonical gap-section SDR as one literal strict-lower matching in a
> `d`-resident ordinary antecedent.  Carry the prescribed common-history
> pentagon and split blocks in that same word.  Complete the owner/`q1`
> forest and choose resident antecedents for its residual components so that
> the forced-envelope containments (3.2) hold on a guarded common-history
> spanning tree.  Preserve the already named arbitrary-upper backup paths.

This lemma gives one source occurrence per lower target, not two.  By the
theorems above, one is sufficient for a final universal word.  Its genuinely
new content is concentrated in two places:

1. ordinary-word realization of the all-depth canonical gap-section cells,
   especially depths greater than `d`; and
2. resident, palette-safe source fusion of the unprotected factor-completion
   components.

No later target-to-cell Hall theorem or typed terminal router is needed on
the terminal branch once this lemma holds.
