# Coloured spectral escape for the second insertion mark

**Date:** 2026-08-06  
**Method:** the exact Johnson least eigenvalue and greedy fresh-label
extension; no computation or search  
**Status:** unconditional marked strengthening of the dense-set fresh-path
lemma.  A residual family of density `alpha` contains a fresh `d`-path whose
second insertion avoids every forbidden mark set of size
`O(alpha k-d)`.  In particular, above separator density `C/d`, one can
avoid any prescribed `O(k/d)` heavy marks.  This is the exact local escape
inequality needed by a balanced greedy or augmenting construction.  By
itself it does not bound the number of heavy marks generated by that
construction.

## 1. Setup

Let

\[
                         \mathcal L={ [k]\choose t},
 \qquad                         M=|\mathcal L|,             \tag{1.1}
\]

with

\[
 {k\over3}\le t\le{k\over2},\qquad d^2\le k.             \tag{1.2}
\]

Let `X subseteq L`, put `alpha=|X|/M`, and let `H subseteq [k]` be a set of
forbidden insertion marks.  Write

\[
                         D_0=t(k-t)                          \tag{1.3}
\]

for the degree of `J(k,t)`.

## 2. Exact escape theorem

### Theorem 2.1

If

\[
 \boxed{
 \alpha>
 \frac{2t|H|+2dk+t}{D_0+t}.}                              \tag{2.1}
\]

then `X` contains an oriented fresh `d`-path

\[
                         S_0,S_1,\ldots,S_{d-1}             \tag{2.2}
\]

whose second insertion label satisfies

\[
                         b_2\notin H.                       \tag{2.3}
\]

#### Proof

The least Johnson eigenvalue is `-t`.  Hence the induced average degree on
`X` is at least

\[
                         D_0\alpha-t(1-\alpha).             \tag{2.4}
\]

Repeatedly delete a vertex below half this average.  A nonempty subgraph
`G subseteq J(k,t)[X]` remains with minimum degree

\[
 q\ge {D_0\alpha-t(1-\alpha)\over2}.                       \tag{2.5}
\]

By (2.1),

\[
                         q>t|H|+dk.                         \tag{2.6}
\]

Choose any first edge

\[
                         S_0\xrightarrow{-a_1,+b_1}S_1     \tag{2.7}
\]

of `G`.  Among the neighbours of `S_1`, at most `t|H|` insert a coordinate
from `H`: for every proposed insertion there are at most `t` deletion
choices.  At most another

\[
                         (k-t)+t=k                          \tag{2.8}
\]

reuse one of the first two event labels, by deleting `b_1` or inserting
`a_1`.  Since (2.6) is stronger than `q>t|H|+k`, there is a second edge

\[
                         S_1\xrightarrow{-a_2,+b_2}S_2     \tag{2.9}
\]

with `b_2 notin H` and with no repeated event label.

Suppose `j<d-1` transitions have been selected freshly.  A neighbour is
forbidden only if it deletes one of the `j` used insertion labels or inserts
one of the `j` used deletion labels.  There are at most

\[
                         j(k-t)+jt=jk<dk                    \tag{2.10}
\]

such neighbours.  Equation (2.6) therefore permits another edge of `G`.
Iteration gives all `d` vertices in (2.2), and no event label repeats.
`square`

### Theorem 2.2 (whole insertion-bank escape)

Under the same hypothesis (2.1), the path may be chosen so that

\[
                         \{b_1,\ldots,b_{d-1}\}\cap H=\varnothing.
\tag{2.11}
\]

More generally, for two forbidden banks `H_+,H_-`, all insertion labels
may avoid `H_+` and all deletion labels may avoid `H_-` provided

\[
 \alpha>
 {2t|H_+|+2(k-t)|H_-|+2dk+t\over D_0+t}.
\tag{2.12}
\]

#### Proof

Under (2.1), the minimum-degree core in (2.5) satisfies

\[
                         q>t|H|+dk.
\]

At every current vertex, at most `t|H|` neighbours insert a member of
`H`.  Choose the first edge outside this bank.  After `j` fresh events
have been chosen, at most another `jk<dk` neighbours reuse an old event
label.  Thus at every step there remains a neighbour which both inserts
outside `H` and keeps all events fresh.  Iteration proves (2.11).

For the two-sided form, at most `t|H_+|` neighbours insert a forbidden
label and at most `(k-t)|H_-|` neighbours delete one.  Repeating the same
minimum-degree calculation with (2.12), and retaining the `jk` reuse
budget, proves the final assertion. \(\square\)

## 3. Separator-scale form

Since `D_0+t=t(k-t+1)`, failure of a marked escape implies the exact bound

\[
 { |X|\over M}
 \le
 \frac{2|H|+2dk/t+1}{k-t+1}.                             \tag{3.1}
\]

Under (1.2), `k/t<=3` and `k-t+1>=k/2`, so

\[
 \boxed{
 { |X|\over M}
 \le {4|H|\over k}+{12d+2\over k}.}                        \tag{3.2}
\]

### Corollary 3.1

Fix `A>=0`.  If

\[
                         |H|\le {Ak\over d}                \tag{3.3}
\]

and

\[
                         |X|>{(4A+14)M\over d},             \tag{3.4}
\]

then `X` contains a fresh `d`-path with `b_2 notin H`.

#### Proof

Equations (3.2), (3.3), and `d^2<=k` bound every counterexample by

\[
 { |X|\over M}
 \le {4A\over d}+{12\over d}+{2\over k}
 <{4A+14\over d}
\]

for all sufficiently large `d`.  `square`

### Corollary 3.2 (erase a fixed event hotspot)

Let `mathcal P` be a matching of oriented fresh paths, let `U` be its
lower leave, and fix

\[
                         |H|\le {Ak\over d}.
\]

If

\[
                         |U|\ge {(4A+14)M\over d},
\tag{3.5}
\]

then one-for-one replacements transform `mathcal P` into a matching of
the same size and leave size for which no selected path has any insertion
event in `H`.

#### Proof

As long as a selected path `P` uses a member of `H`, apply Theorem 2.2 to
`X=U union V(P)`.  Corollary 3.1's density calculation supplies a fresh
replacement path `Q subseteq X` whose complete insertion bank avoids `H`.
The replacement meets no other selected path and preserves the leave
size.  The total number of selected insertion events in `H` strictly
decreases, so the process terminates. \(\square\)

This is stronger than erasing only a fixed bank of second marks.  It is
still a fixed-hotspot theorem: after `H` is changed, later replacements
may reuse coordinates erased in an earlier round, so it does not by itself
give the simultaneous sharp event-load bound required by the macro
selector.

## 4. Two-sided version

Apply the theorem to the reverse orientation.  Since the reverse second
insertion is the forward deletion `a_(d-2)`, the same bound proves:

* above the threshold (2.1), there is a fresh path with forward `b_2`
  outside any prescribed `H`; and
* independently, there is a fresh path with forward `a_(d-2)` outside any
  prescribed set of the same size.

Obtaining both restrictions on one path requires a two-checkpoint version
of the greedy construction.  The same forward construction works if one
reserves the corresponding forbidden-neighbour budget at the second and
penultimate steps; a sufficient condition is

\[
 q>t|H_+|+(k-t)|H_-|+dk,                                  \tag{4.1}
\]

where `H_+` forbids forward `b_2` and `H_-` forbids forward
`a_(d-2)`.  Indeed, choose the second transition as in Theorem 2.1, extend
greedily until `S_(d-3)`, choose transition `d-2` while excluding the at
most `(k-t)|H_-|` neighbours whose deletion label lies in `H_-`, and then
choose the final fresh transition.  At every checkpoint the reused-label
budget is below `dk`; (4.1) dominates both checkpoint bounds.  This
two-checkpoint form is useful for balancing the orientation pair
`(b_2,a_(d-2))`.

## 5. What this proves and what it does not

The theorem supplies exactly the local statement requested by a heavy-mark
avoidance algorithm:

\[
 |X|\gg M/d,\qquad |H|=O(k/d)
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \text{an admissible next block avoiding }H.               \tag{5.1}
\]

It also explains the natural scaling.  At residual density `alpha`, the
minimum-degree proof exposes `Omega(alpha k)` possible insertion labels;
at the terminal density `1/d`, this is only `Omega(k/d)`.

The result alone is not a complete mark-balanced factor theorem.  A naive
maximal packing with the final target capacity `Theta(M/(kd))` can have far
more than `k/d` saturated coordinates, so Corollary 3.1 no longer applies.
This failure is exact.  Put `N=Theta(M/d)` for the required number of paths
and let

\[
                         L_*={C N\over k}                  \tag{5.2}
\]

be any fixed-factor admissible coordinate cap.  Total-load accounting gives
only

\[
                         |H|\le {N\over L_*}={k\over C}.   \tag{5.3}
\]

To force `|H|<=A k/d` by this argument one would need

\[
                         L\ge {dN\over Ak}
                           ={d\over AC}L_*,                \tag{5.4}
\]

a dimension-dependent factor `Theta(d)` which the companion cylinder cannot
absorb.  Thus the escape theorem plus a scalar saturated-set count cannot
prove the desired selector.

One still needs either:

1. a weighted potential showing that the near-saturated mark set remains
   `O(k/d)` until separator time;
2. alternating path switches which rebalance marks; or
3. a coloured matching theorem using (3.1) as its cut inequality.

Even a successful coordinate-balancing theorem would close only the first
marked row.  The full companion ratio also requires, within each fixed
second label, spread over the owner/kernel occurrences `(T,b_2)`.  That is
the finer marked-ratio condition in the stopped-hazard synthesis.

The orientation-robust obstruction in
`MATH_OBSTRUCTION_FIXED_MARK_FRESH_PATH_PACKING_DEFEATS_SEPARATOR_PRUNING_20260806.md`
shows why this balance must be enforced during selection, rather than by
post-hoc deletion.
