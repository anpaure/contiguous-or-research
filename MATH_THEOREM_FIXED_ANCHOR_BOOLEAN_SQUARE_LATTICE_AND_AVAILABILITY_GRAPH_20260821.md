# Fixed-anchor Boolean squares generate the full point-margin lattice

**Date:** 2026-08-21  
**Status:** unconditional integral-lattice theorem and exact availability-graph
reduction; physical reachability inside a wreath-factor orbit is not claimed

## 1. The fixed-anchor theorem

Let `n>=4`, let `2<=k<=n-2`, and distinguish a point `infinity` of
`[n]`.  Put `V=[n]-{infinity}` and

\[
 X_{n,k}=\mathbb Z^{\binom{[n]}k}.
\]

Write `[S]` for the basis vector indexed by the `k`-set `S`, and let

\[
 U_{n,k}:X_{n,k}\longrightarrow\mathbb Z^n,
 \qquad U_{n,k}[S]={\bf1}_S                                  \tag{1.1}
\]

be the point-incidence map.  If `K` is a `(k-2)`-set in `V` and
`a,b,c` are three distinct points of `V-K`, define the anchored square

\[
 s(K;a;b,c)
 =[Kab]-[Kac]-[K\,\mathord\infty\,b]
                 +[K\,\mathord\infty\,c].                  \tag{1.2}
\]

Let `L_infinity` be the integer lattice generated by all currents (1.2).

### Theorem 1.1 (integral fixed-anchor saturation)

\[
 \boxed{\qquad L_\infty=\ker_{\mathbb Z}U_{n,k}.\qquad}      \tag{1.3}
\]

Thus even the Boolean squares whose four-point pivot contains one fixed
point generate the entire point-margin kernel.  In particular,

\[
 X_{n,k}/L_\infty
 \cong \operatorname {im}U_{n,k}
 =\left\{z\in\mathbb Z^n:\ \sum_i z_i\equiv0\pmod k\right\},\tag{1.4}
\]

so `L_infinity` is saturated and has rank `binom(n,k)-n`.

#### Proof

For a `(k-1)`-set `B subset V` and `a in V-B`, put

\[
 v_{a,B}=[B+a]-[\mathord\infty+B].                          \tag{1.5}
\]

If `B=K+b` and `B'=K+c` are adjacent in the Johnson graph on the
`(k-1)`-sets of `V-{a}`, then

\[
 v_{a,B}-v_{a,B'}=s(K;a;b,c).                               \tag{1.6}
\]

That Johnson graph is connected.  Hence, modulo `L_infinity`, all the
vectors `v_{a,B}` with fixed `a` have one common class; call it `t_a`.

Let `e_B=[infinity+B]` for `B in binom(V,k-1)`.  A non-anchor target
`A in binom(V,k)` has, for every `a in A`, the representation

\[
 [A]=e_{A-a}+t_a\pmod {L_\infty}.                            \tag{1.7}
\]

If adjacent `(k-1)`-sets are written `B=K+b` and `B'=K+a`, then applying
(1.7) to `A=K+a+b` gives

\[
 e_B+t_a=e_{B'}+t_b.                                        \tag{1.8}
\]

Consequently

\[
 e_B-\sum_{i\in B}t_i
 =e_{B'}-\sum_{i\in B'}t_i.                                \tag{1.9}
\]

The Johnson graph on `binom(V,k-1)` is connected, so the common class in
(1.9) is a single element `s`.  Equations (1.7)--(1.9) show that the
quotient `X_{n,k}/L_infinity` is generated by

\[
                         s,\quad (t_a)_{a\in V}.             \tag{1.10}
\]

Every generator (1.2) has zero point incidence, so (1.1) factors through
this quotient.  On the generators (1.10) the factor map is

\[
 U(t_a)={\bf e}_a-{\bf e}_\infty,
 \qquad U(s)=k{\bf e}_\infty.                               \tag{1.11}
\]

Indeed, the first identity follows from (1.5), while for any
`B in binom(V,k-1)` the definition in (1.9) gives

\[
 U(s)=U(e_B)-\sum_{i\in B}U(t_i)=k{\bf e}_\infty.
\]

The vectors in (1.11) are integer-linearly independent: a relation has
coefficient zero at every non-anchor coordinate, and then also at the
anchor coordinate.  Therefore the factored incidence map is injective.
Its kernel is zero, which proves (1.3).  Finally, its image is generated
by the differences `e_a-e_infinity` and `k e_infinity`, giving exactly
the congruence lattice in (1.4).  This also proves saturation and the rank
claim. `square`

## 2. An exact graph criterion for a restricted current bank

For each `a in V`, let `G_a` be any spanning subgraph of the Johnson graph

\[
 J_a=J\!\left(V-\{a\},k-1\right).                           \tag{2.1}
\]

Let `L(G)` be generated only by the anchored squares (1.6) corresponding
to the edges of the graphs `G_a`.

### Corollary 2.1 (connectivity suffices)

If every `G_a` is connected, then

\[
                         L(G)=\ker_{\mathbb Z}U_{n,k}.       \tag{2.2}
\]

More generally, if `c_a` is the number of connected components of `G_a`,
then the abelian group

\[
                 \ker_{\mathbb Z}U_{n,k}/L(G)               \tag{2.3}
\]

is generated by at most

\[
                         \sum_{a\in V}(c_a-1)               \tag{2.4}
\]

elements.  In particular its rational rank is at most (2.4).

#### Proof

The proof of Theorem 1.1 used, for fixed `a`, only edge differences along
the graph `J_a`.  Thus connected `G_a` give the same proof verbatim.

In general, add `c_a-1` abstract Johnson edges to connect `G_a`.  Adding
the corresponding anchored square generators for every `a` produces a
lattice satisfying (2.2).  Hence the cosets of those at most (2.4)
additional squares generate (2.3). `square`

This is an exact algebraic reduction of the availability problem.  A
physical switch theorem would be sufficient if, after fixing one label
as anchor, its currently usable `C_8` currents connected all the graphs
`G_a`; a quantitative version may instead bound their total component
excess.  Connectivity of the abstract Johnson graphs alone does **not**
show that those switches are simultaneously present in one evolving
wreath factor.

## 3. Relation to the dimension-free wreath `C_8`

At first shadow the dimension-free two-row wreath switch has current

\[
 -[Kbp]-[Kcq]+[Kcp]+[Kbq],                                  \tag{3.1}
\]

where `|K|=k-2` and the four pivot labels are distinct.  If one pivot is
chosen to be `infinity`, (3.1) is (1.2), up to sign and relabelling.
Conversely every generator (1.2) is realized by the genuine two-row
**partial-factor** switch template: put its common core in the interior of
the long block `P`, use the two remaining non-anchor pivot points as the
ends of `P`, use `infinity` and the last pivot point as the two exchanged
boundary labels, and fill the unused labels into the remaining slots.
This local realization does not assert that the old row pair extends to
one global exact wreath factor.

Therefore Theorem 1.1 strengthens the unrestricted-square lattice result:
a single anchor-star of the correct physical current type already has no
linear or congruence deficit.  What remains is not an abstract span issue
but a **legal, conformal, nonnegative path** through exact wreath factors.
The 3,014-state `b=9` physical orbit, whose minimum first-shadow hole count
is one, is a finite warning that lattice equality by itself does not imply
such a path to the relaxed optimum.

## 4. The two asymptotic compiler thresholds

Let `A=binom(2r+1,r)` be the number of middle targets in an exact factor,
and let

\[
 Q=\sum_T(\mu(T)-1)_+=A-|\{T:\mu(T)>0\}|                    \tag{4.1}
\]

be its first-shadow duplicate mass.  These two targets must be kept
separate.

1. **First-shadow only.**  The capacity-`c` row-Hall compiler loses a
   fraction

   \[
                  O\!\left({c\over b}+{bQ/A\over c}\right).\tag{4.2}
   \]

   Optimizing gives `c/b asymp sqrt(Q/A)`.  Thus the q1 compiler needs
   only

   \[
                              Q=o(A).                         \tag{4.3}
   \]

2. **A collar through depth `H`.**  Purging the repeated first-shadow
   occurrences charges their deletion through every retained depth, for
   aggregate loss `O(HQ)` in addition to the independent deeper-hole
   ledger.  Its first-shadow input therefore needs

   \[
                              HQ/A=o(1).                      \tag{4.4}
   \]

   For the intended `H=Theta(sqrt(r log r))`, a bound
   `Q=O(A/r^{1/2+epsilon})` for any fixed `epsilon>0` is already
   band-sufficient.  When `0<epsilon<1/2`, this target is strictly weaker
   than `Q=O(A/r)`.

The fixed-anchor lattice theorem proves neither (4.3) nor (4.4).  It
removes a possible algebraic obstruction and identifies the required next
step: prove enough physical Johnson-edge availability, with a conformal
augmentation argument, to reach a factor satisfying one of these bounds.
