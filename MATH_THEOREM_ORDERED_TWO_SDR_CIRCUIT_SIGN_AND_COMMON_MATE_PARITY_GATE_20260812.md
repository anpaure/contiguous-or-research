# The common-mate two-phase module has an even successor action

**Date:** 2026-08-12
**Method:** exact permutation action of alternating matching circuits
**Status:** unconditional parity certificate.  It identifies a missing

## 1. Successor action of one matching circuit

Let `G=(L,U;E)` be balanced bipartite and let

\[
                         F=M_0\mathbin{\dot\cup}M_1
\]

be an ordered two-SDR.  Regard `M_i:L -> U` as bijections and put

\[
                         \rho=M_1^{-1}M_0.           \tag{1.1}
\]

The cycles of `rho` are the oriented components of `F`.

Suppose an alternating circuit of length `2 ell` switches phase zero.
List its lower vertices cyclically as `x_1,...,x_ell`, with old edges
`x_i M_0(x_i)` and new edges joining `M_0(x_i)` to `x_(i+1)` (indices
cyclic).  If `M'_0` is the switched matching, then

\[
                         \alpha=M_0^{-1}M'_0          \tag{1.2}
\]

is one `ell`-cycle on these lower vertices.  Hence

\[
                         \operatorname {sgn}\alpha
                         =(-1)^{\ell-1}.             \tag{1.3}
\]

The analogous phase-one switch gives

\[
                         \beta=M_1^{-1}M'_1,         \tag{1.4}
\]

again an `ell`-cycle.

### Theorem 1.1 (two-sided successor formula)

For simultaneous disjoint circuit switches in the two phases, let
`alpha` and `beta` be the products of their induced lower-shore cycles.
Then

\[
                         \boxed{\rho'=\beta^{-1}\rho\alpha}       \tag{1.5}
\]

and

\[
 \boxed{
 {\operatorname {sgn}\rho'\over\operatorname {sgn}\rho}
 =(-1)^{
   \sum_{C\text{ in phase }0}(|C|/2-1)
  +\sum_{C\text{ in phase }1}(|C|/2-1)}.}           \tag{1.6}
\]

#### Proof

By definition `M'_0=M_0 alpha` and `M'_1=M_1 beta`.  Therefore

\[
 (M'_1)^{-1}M'_0
 =\beta^{-1}M_1^{-1}M_0\alpha,
\]

which is (1.5).  Equation (1.6) follows from (1.3), multiplicativity of
the sign, and `sgn(beta^{-1})=sgn(beta)`. \(\square\)

There are odd abstract circuits--either `C8` alone is odd--but no

### Proposition 2.2 (exact index-two action lattice)

Record a two-phase matching transformation by the pair `(beta,alpha)` in
(1.5), and define

\[
 \chi(\beta,\alpha)
   =\operatorname {sgn}(\beta)\operatorname {sgn}(\alpha)
   \in\{+1,-1\}.                                    \tag{2.3}
\]

Then `chi` is multiplicative under composition, and

\[
 {\operatorname {sgn}\rho'\over\operatorname {sgn}\rho}
                         =\chi(\beta,\alpha).       \tag{2.4}
\]

Consequently the exact algebraic envelope of every generator system made
from independent even phase moves and paired odd--odd moves is the
index-two subgroup

\[
 \boxed{
 H=\{(\beta,\alpha):
       \operatorname {sgn}\beta=
       \operatorname {sgn}\alpha\}.}               \tag{2.5}
\]

If the available even circuits generate the alternating groups on the two
active socket banks and one paired odd--odd circuit is available, then the
generated action group is exactly `H`.  Without those connectivity
hypotheses, it is a possibly smaller subgroup of `H`; the parity exclusion
remains unconditional.

An unpaired `C8` has `chi=-1` and crosses the other coset.  Thus the
smallest possible parity repair is exactly one terminally unpaired odd
matching circuit (or an equivalent odd exported socket action).

#### Proof

Formula (2.4) is (1.6).  Under composition, the left and right permutation
factors multiply, so their signs multiply; hence `chi` is a character and
its kernel is (2.5).  Independent even moves generate `A times A`.  If
`(beta_*,alpha_*)` is odd--odd, its coset together with `A times A` is all
of `H`.  A one-phase `C8` has one odd and one identity factor, so it lies
in the other coset. \(\square\)

### Theorem 2.3 (a private q2-transparent odd actuator exists)

For every sufficiently large `m`, `ML_m` has an ordered two-SDR containing
a one-phase alternating `C8` and four untouched opposite-phase common
mates such that
switching the `C8`

1. preserves the spanning two-factor condition;
2. has exactly zero signed `q2` turn current; and
3. has `chi=-1`.

Moreover, the actuator can be planted vertex-disjointly beside any fixed
proper two-coloured bank supported over a fixed core of codimension
`t>=2` (with `t=O(1)`).

#### Proof

Choose a set `K` of size `m-2`, distinct labels

\[
                         a,b,x\notin K,
 \qquad s\in K,
 \qquad c\notin K\cup\{a,b,x\}.
\]

Use the incidence cycle

\[
\begin{aligned}
 X&=K+a,&R&=K+a+b,\\
 Y_1&=K-s+a+b,&S&=K-s+a+b+x,\\
 Y_2&=K-s+b+x,&M&=K+b+x,\\
 X_x&=K+x,&Q&=K+a+x,
\end{aligned}
\]

in the order

\[
                         X-R-Y_1-S-Y_2-M-X_x-Q-X.    \tag{2.6}
\]

Declare alternate edges old in one phase, and retain in the opposite
phase the common mates

\[
 X(X+c),\quad Y_1(Y_1+c),\quad
 Y_2(Y_2+c),\quad X_x(X_x+c).                       \tag{2.7}
\]

The eight selected edges in (2.6)--(2.7) form a proper two-coloured
2-bounded linear forest.  Declare the other four edges of (2.6)
forbidden.  All displayed vertices contain the fixed core `K-s`, so this
is a bounded face (`t=2`).  The bounded phased-bank extension theorem
first joins its coloured components through private alternating paths and
then extends the resulting path, while avoiding the four new edges, to an
ordered two-SDR for every sufficiently large `m`.

For the relative statement, let `C` be the fixed core of the pre-existing
bank.  Choose `K-s` to contain `C`, and choose every remaining label in
(2.6)--(2.7) outside its finite noncommon support.  There are linearly many
available labels and only constantly many exclusions.  The actuator and
the old bank are then vertex-disjoint and lie in one bounded face over
`C`; apply the same phased-bank extension theorem to their union.

At the four changed lower owners, the old-to-new turns telescope as

\[
\begin{aligned}
 K+a+b+c&\longmapsto K+a+x+c,\\
 K-s+a+b+x+c&\longmapsto K+a+b+c,\\
 K+b+x+c&\longmapsto K-s+a+b+x+c,\\
 K+a+x+c&\longmapsto K+b+x+c.
\end{aligned}                                       \tag{2.8}
\]

Hence the complete signed `q2` current is zero.  Finally the switched
circuit is a one-phase `C8`, so (2.1) gives `chi=-1`. \(\square\)

Thus neither abstract ordered-two-SDR extension nor `q2` transparency
prevents payment of the parity tax.  What remains is to give this odd
actuator the required occurrence-level socket action while keeping the
`q>=3`, residence, and recursive-interface ledgers.  An arbitrary private
copy supplied by Theorem 2.3 has none of those guarantees.

#### Proof

## 3. Component parity

Let `c(F)` be the number of components of the ordered two-factor.  Since
these are exactly the cycles of `rho`,

\[
                         \operatorname {sgn}\rho
                         =(-1)^{|L|-c(F)}.            \tag{3.1}
\]

Hence

\[
 \boxed{
 {\operatorname {sgn}\rho'\over\operatorname {sgn}\rho}
 =(-1)^{c(F')-c(F)}.}                               \tag{3.2}
\]

### Corollary 3.1 (odd-component-change obstruction)

Every terminal transformation made only from the paired common-mate
module and `C6` packets changes the number of factor components by an even
integer.

The audited B8 base packet is required to join six lifted cycles into one,
so its advertised component change is

\[
                         -5,                         \tag{3.3}
\]

which is odd.  Therefore the persistent transformation

\[
 \text{source `C8` + creator `C8` + only `C6` transport faces}    \tag{3.4}
\]

cannot simultaneously retain the canonical B8 `-5` component action.

At least one of the following must occur:

1. an additional unpaired odd circuit is present;
2. the creator is undone before the terminal state, leaving an odd net
   number of `C8` matching switches;
3. the augmented module has an even, rather than `-5`, terminal component
   action and its target socket signature is recomputed accordingly; or
4. an exported endpoint relocation contributes the missing odd socket
   action.

This is an exact parity tax, not a heuristic topology warning.

## 4. First-return socket sign

Let `B subseteq L` be a set of occurrence-labelled lower sockets.  Assume
every component meeting the module meets `B`, and define the first-return
permutation

\[
 \tau(b)=\rho^{j(b)}(b),
 \qquad j(b)=\min\{j>0:\rho^j(b)\in B\}.             \tag{4.1}
\]

Let `c_I` be the number of cycles of `rho` disjoint from `B`.  Then

\[
 c(\rho)=c(\tau)+c_I.                                \tag{4.2}
\]

For two states on the same lower and socket sets,

\[
 \boxed{
 {\operatorname {sgn}\tau'\over\operatorname {sgn}\tau}
 =
 {\operatorname {sgn}\rho'\over\operatorname {sgn}\rho}
 (-1)^{c'_I-c_I}.}                                  \tag{4.3}
\]

#### Proof

Equation (4.2) follows because first return contracts every socket-bearing
cycle but neither joins nor splits it.  Apply
`sgn(pi)=(-1)^(|domain(pi)|-c(pi))` to `rho,rho',tau,tau'`; the fixed
domain sizes cancel and give (4.3). \(\square\)

### Corollary 4.1 (socket parity gate)

If a protected local replacement creates or destroys no socket-free
component, then every paired common-mate/`C6` transformation induces an
even change of the exposed socket permutation.

Therefore an odd prescribed socket reconnection cannot be hidden inside
the internal paths.  It requires an odd circuit or an odd change in the
number of socket-free components.  In particular, the `c=5` two-endpoint
relocation is not automatically harmless: its occurrence-level first-return
permutation must be computed, and an odd transposition there is a possible
source of the missing parity in Corollary 3.1.

### Corollary 4.2 (the two relocated endpoints must cross)

Assume the persistent-creator module is required to retain the canonical
odd B8 component action, and assume it creates no socket-free component.
Label the two old endpoints promoted by the `c=5` common mates and the two
exported replacement endpoints in their inherited exterior order.  Then
the relocation bijection between these two two-element banks must be the
odd (crossed) bijection, not the even (parallel) one.

#### Proof

The internal source-plus-creator/`C6` action is even by Corollary 2.1,
whereas the required B8 action is odd by (3.3).  Formula (4.3) forces the
exterior socket relocation to have sign `-1`.  On two labelled endpoints,
the only odd bijection is the transposition. \(\square\)

Thus the parity audit does more than request an unspecified socket check:
it fixes the required orientation of the two-endpoint relocation in the
persistent-creator branch.

## 5. Consequence for the finite MNW target

Abstract ordered-two-SDR extendability of the old/forbidden edge banks is
separate from this parity issue.  Even when an ambient ordered two-SDR
exists, the currently specified persistent source-plus-creator module
cannot be assigned the old canonical `-5` socket/component signature.

The finite connector-faithful module must therefore declare one of two
different targets:

* **creator returned:** terminal creator state is restored, leaving an odd
  source action and allowing the canonical `-5` signature; or
* **creator retained:** the total matching action is even, so the terminal
  component and socket signatures must also be even relative to the
  initial state.

No proof may mix the edge bank of the second target with the topology
ledger of the first.  This is the exact remaining `Z_2` row before any
finer socket-group calculation.
