# Independent audit: complement socket, dual aperture, and shadow-clock compilation

**Date:** 2026-08-06  
**Primary theorem:**
`MATH_THEOREM_ODD_COMPLEMENT_ONE_SOCKET_AND_DUAL_APERTURE_REDUCTION_20260806.md`  
**Method:** literal endpoint and scan-matching algebra; no search or
computation  
**Verdict:** the one-socket reduction and endpoint algebra pass.  One
scope qualification is necessary: reflected phase-one rewrites are not
directed in the fixed phase-zero contraction merely by conjugacy.  The
shadow-clock lemma below removes that macroscopic phase obstruction once
one earlier nonquiet delimiter clock has been installed.  The remaining
unproved object is the installation/retirement and terminal restoration
collar.

## 1. The complement matching and last-intersection splice pass

On

\[
 Q=\{u\in\{0,1,2\}^m:\sum_i u_i=m\},
\]

the map \(\mathsf c u=2-u\) is an involution.  Its fixed-point equation
forces \(u_i=1\) for every \(i\).  Hence it has one fixed point and all
other orbits are two-cycles.

Choose one orientation \(u\) of every nonfixed complement pair.  Retain
the source path \(P_u\), reroute the distinct source path
\(P_{\mathsf c u}\) to \(b(u)=\tau A(u)\), and retain
\(P_{\mathbf 1}\).  If each reroute last meets the old linkage on
\(P_{\mathsf c u}\), avoids the old linkage thereafter, and the reroutes
are mutually disjoint, then the terminals are exactly

\[
 \{A(u),\tau A(u):u\in I\}\ \dot\cup\ \{A(\mathbf1)\}.
\]

Thus all sources are used once and the terminal boundary edges are
complete except for one socket.  No route in the opposite orientation is
needed.  This validates Theorems 2.1--2.2 of the primary theorem.

## 2. The direct aperture branches preserve the claimed boundary label

The physical boundary pair is

\[
                    p_m=(2m,0).
\]

At the quiet source it is \(q_0(u_m)\), whose first coordinate is

\[
 \beta(u_m),\qquad
 \beta(0)=0,\quad\beta(1)=\beta(2)=2.
\]

Every internal activation and zipper move is supported away from
\(p_m\).  In the generic aperture branch the only boundary change is the
transfer that sets coordinate zero to one.  In the two direct branches

\[
 (02,t_0=2)\to(12,t_0=1)\to(21,t_0=1),
\]

and

\[
 (20,t_0=0)\to(10,t_0=1)\to(01,t_0=1),
\]

the displayed \(02,12,21\) or \(20,10,01\) is the first internal pair,
not the coordinate \(2m\) entry of \(p_m\).  Therefore coordinate
\(2m\) remains \(\beta(u_m)\) in both direct branches as well.  Hence

\[
                    A(u)_{(2m,0)}=(\beta(u_m),1)
\]

holds without a hidden generic-branch qualification.

Applying

\[
 \Gamma=\tau\overline{\phantom{x}},\qquad
 (x,y)\mapsto(2-y,2-x)
\]

on the ordered boundary pair gives

\[
 \begin{array}{c|c|c}
 u_m&A_*(u)=\Gamma A(\mathsf cu)&b(u)=\tau A(u)\\ \hline
 0&10&10\\
 1&10&12\\
 2&12&12.
 \end{array}
\]

Thus the advertised boundary-one residue is exact.  This table says
nothing about the internal coordinates, as the primary theorem correctly
notes.

## 3. The exact scope of the \(\Gamma\)-conjugacy

On an internal ordered pair, complement maps the phase-zero rows by

\[
 01\leftrightarrow10\ \longleftrightarrow\
 12\leftrightarrow21,
 \qquad
 02\leftrightarrow11\ \longmapsto\
 20\leftrightarrow11.
\]

On \(p_m=(2m,0)\), reflected complement interchanges the mass-one and
mass-three rows and fixes \(02\leftrightarrow11\) setwise.  It also maps
the internal phase-zero quiet alphabet

\[
                   \{00,20,22\}
\]

to the phase-one quiet alphabet

\[
                   \{00,02,22\},
\]

while preserving the boundary quiet alphabet.  Therefore

\[
                   \Gamma M_0\Gamma^{-1}=M_*
\]

is a correct identity of first-nonquiet **matching edge sets**.

It is not a directed-path conjugacy in the fixed digraph.  The physical
swap \(\tau=(0\ 2m)\) is not an automorphism of the nonwrap coordinate
path, and generally

\[
                   \Gamma(P_0)=P_*\ne P_0.
\]

Consequently an image under \(\Gamma\) is not automatically a path in
\(D(M_0)-V(P_0)\).  This limitation is stated correctly in the primary
theorem and is load-bearing.

## 4. A fixed shadow clock compiles every reflected phase

The remaining phase issue admits a useful exact reduction which does not
require adaptive installation of phase-zero and phase-one matching rows.

### Theorem 4.1 (shadow-clock phase compilation)

Fix a scan pair \(p_h\) and a selected nonquiet row

\[
                    \alpha\leftrightarrow\bar\alpha
\]

on \(p_h\).  Suppose all scan pairs before \(p_h\) are quiet.  Let
\(G_{\rm work}\) be any token graph generated by physical transfers whose
support is disjoint from the scan prefix through \(p_h\).

Then every walk

\[
                 z_0,z_1,\ldots,z_t
\]

in \(G_{\rm work}\) has a directed lift in the fixed phase-zero digraph
\(D(M_0)-V(P_0)\).  During the lift \(p_h\) remains the first nonquiet
scan pair.  If a family of work walks is vertex-disjoint, their directed
lifts are vertex-disjoint.

In particular, every local edge which a reflected construction would
have used as a phase-one matching edge may instead be regarded as an
ordinary physical work edge and lifted through the one fixed phase-zero
shadow clock.

#### Proof

For one work edge \(z_i z_{i+1}\), perform that physical transfer at the
majority state above \(z_i\).  It reverses the cut-open shore and leaves
the scan prefix and \(p_h\) unchanged.  Hence \(p_h\) is still first
nonquiet, and the unique next matching edge toggles
\(\alpha\leftrightarrow\bar\alpha\), returning to the majority state
above \(z_{i+1}\).  Concatenation gives the directed lift.

The minority vertex of the lifted edge entering \(z_{i+1}\) is the
unique \(M_0\)-mate of the majority vertex above \(z_{i+1}\).  Therefore
two lifted paths can meet at a minority vertex only if their work walks
meet at the corresponding work state.  This proves disjointness.

Finally \(p_h\) precedes the physical boundary row and is nonquiet at
every lifted state, so the installed matching never reaches the boundary
row.  No lifted state lies in \(V(P_0)\).  This is precisely the
clocked-tail-minor argument, now applied to the reflected work alphabet.
\(\square\)

### Corollary 4.2 (the adaptive phase profile is not a macroscopic gate)

Suppose the delimiter-equipped counterflow has a nonquiet delimiter pair
\(p_h\) which

1. is earlier than every pair modified during the reflected return;
2. has a quiet scan prefix; and
3. is kept fixed until the terminal collar.

Write each reflected two-arc rewrite as its two underlying physical token
transfers.  Theorem 4.1 lifts those work transfers through \(p_h\) in the
original phase-zero contraction.  Thus the clock-dependent phase table of
the reflected zipper does not need to be installed in the matching.

The still-open part is to install such a delimiter clock at the handoff,
place all return work after it, and retire it while restoring the root and
the exact terminal.  Those are collar/ordering conditions, rather than a
global dual-phase matching theorem.

## 5. The counterflow can leave directly from the quiet source

The terminal-to-terminal formulation in the primary theorem is much
stronger than the last-intersection theorem requires.  In fact no
aperture egress is needed, including in either direct \(p_1\) branch.

Assume \(m\ge3\).  Let \(x(u)\) be the original quiet source, let
\(r_0=2m-1\) be its unpaired root coordinate, and put
\(x_0=2m-2\).  Thus \(x_0\) is the second coordinate of the last internal
scan pair \(p_{m-1}\) and is adjacent to the root.  At every quiet source
the root equals one and

\[
 p_{m-1}=q(u_{m-1})\in\{00,20,22\}.
\]

### Theorem 5.1 (universal quiet-source root departure)

For every quiet source \(x(u)\), there is a directed two-arc path which
changes the root from one to a flag in \(\{0,2\}\) and creates a selected
clock on \(p_{m-1}\).  Apart from its initial source, the path is disjoint
from the entire old promotion/aperture linkage.  These departures are
pairwise vertex-disjoint over all \(u\).

More explicitly, the three local rows are

\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
u_{m-1}&\text{physical root transfer}&
       \text{following selected edge on }p_{m-1}\\ \hline
0&(00\mid1)\to(01\mid0)&(01\mid0)\to(10\mid0),\\
1&(20\mid1)\to(21\mid0)&(21\mid0)\to(12\mid0),\\
2&(22\mid1)\to(21\mid2)&(21\mid2)\to(12\mid2).
\end{array}
\tag{5.1}
\]

#### Proof

For digits zero and one, the \(x_0\)-coordinate is zero, so transfer the
root unit to \(x_0\).  For digit two, transfer one unit from \(x_0\) to
the root.  This gives the first arrow in every row of (5.1), changes the
root to zero or two, and makes \(p_{m-1}\) nonquiet.  Every earlier scan
pair is still quiet.  Hence \(p_{m-1}\) is the first nonquiet pair and
the second arrow in (5.1) is the unique following matching edge.

The majority endpoint recovers the source digit: \((10,0)\), \((12,0)\),
and \((12,2)\) are distinct.  Every other quiet pair is unchanged, so it
recovers the whole source \(u\).  A minority intermediate is the unique
matching mate of its following majority endpoint.  Thus the departures
are pairwise disjoint.

All states of every old promotion/aperture path have root value one.
Both new states in (5.1) have root value zero or two, so the departure
meets the old linkage only at its source.  Since \(p_{m-1}\) is nonquiet,
neither new state belongs to the installed boundary current
\(V(P_0)\).  \(\square\)

### Corollary 5.2 (the root flag installs the shadow clock)

Starting from (5.1), apply the ternary zipper from right to left across

\[
                    p_{m-2},p_{m-3},\ldots,p_1.
\]

Its starting clock is exactly \(10\) or \(12\), as required by the
ternary rewrite.  It ends with a selected clock on \(p_1\), leaves a
reverse-decodable record chain on \(p_2,\ldots,p_{m-1}\), and never
touches the root.  The fixed starting position \(p_{m-1}\), together
with the adjacent non-one root flag, is an unambiguous end delimiter for
the record chain.  Thus all these zipper paths are pairwise disjoint and
avoid the old linkage after their quiet sources.

Consequently the counterflow enters the exact hypothesis of the
shadow-clock compilation theorem without reversing the coordinate-zero
aperture and without a special case for the direct \(p_1\) branches.

For a complement pair, retain \(P_u\) through \(A(u)\), but reroute
\(P_{\mathsf cu}\) immediately at its quiet source \(x(\mathsf cu)\).
The last-intersection permutation is complement, and the desired sink is
still \(\tau A(u)\).

## 6. Root restoration and the boundary-one row have a one-edge exit

The non-one root flag can also be retained through the whole target-writing
stage.  It need not be restored by a phase-changing matching collar.

Let

\[
                         b(u)=\tau A(u).
\]

Its physical boundary pair is \(10\) or \(12\), so coordinate \(2m\)
equals one.  For a fixed route flag \(f\in\{0,2\}\), define
\(E_f(u)\) by keeping every coordinate of \(b(u)\) except

\[
 (E_f(u)_{2m-1},E_f(u)_{2m})=
 \begin{cases}
 (0,2),&f=0,\\
 (2,0),&f=2.
 \end{cases}
\tag{6.1}
\]

### Theorem 6.1 (universal flagged exit)

There is one directed physical edge

\[
                         E_f(u)\longrightarrow b(u)
\tag{6.2}
\]

which restores the root to one.  It is a nonmatching edge, preserves the
target's internal decoder until the terminal, and handles both target
boundary rows, including \(12\).

#### Proof

For \(f=0\), transfer one unit from coordinate \(2m\) to the root:

\[
                         (0,2)\longrightarrow(1,1).
\]

For \(f=2\), transfer one unit from the root to coordinate \(2m\):

\[
                         (2,0)\longrightarrow(1,1).
\]

The edge \(\{2m-1,2m\}\) is physical and is not one of the scan-pair
matching edges, because coordinate \(2m-1\) is unpaired.  The aperture
terminal \(A(u)\) lies on the majority shore, and the boundary partner
\(b(u)=\tau A(u)\) lies on the minority shore.  Hence its other physical
neighbour \(E_f(u)\) lies on the majority shore and (6.2) has the required
nonmatching orientation.

All internal coordinates of \(E_f(u)\) equal those of \(b(u)\), including
the first nonquiet aperture marker.  Thus \(E_f(u)\notin V(P_0)\) and the
target decoder is untouched.  If \(b(u)\) has boundary \(10\), the two
possible preboundary rows are \(20\) and \(00\).  If it has boundary
\(12\), the two possible preboundary rows are \(22\) and \(02\).  The
last case explicitly includes the former \(10\to12\) boundary-one
residue: the work stage writes \(22\) or \(02\), and the one root edge
writes \(12\).  \(\square\)

Consequently no terminal phase switch is required after the state
\(E_f(u)\) has been written.  The next lemmas give the finite clock
retirement and remove the component-mass obstruction.  Only labelled
serialization of the macroscopic writer remains afterward.

### Lemma 6.2 (safe alignment of the root flag)

While the \(p_1\) shadow clock is active, the root flag may be changed
from zero to two or from two to zero by the two logical tail transfers

\[
                 02\longrightarrow11\longrightarrow20
 \qquad\hbox{or}\qquad
                 20\longrightarrow11\longrightarrow02
\tag{6.3}
\]

on the ordered pair \((2m-1,2m)\).  Each logical transfer has its directed
two-arc lift through \(p_1\).  No state of this flag-changing path lies on
the old linkage.

#### Proof

The two displayed moves are legal transfers across the physical edge
\(\{2m-1,2m\}\), disjoint from \(p_1\).  Apply Theorem 4.1 to lift them.
At the endpoints the root is zero or two.  At the only intermediate root
value one, coordinate \(2m\) also equals one.  But coordinate \(2m\) is
the first entry of the quiet physical boundary code on every old
promotion/aperture path, and is always

\[
                         \beta(u_m)\in\{0,2\};
\]

the aperture never changes it.  Hence the intermediate `11' root-boundary
signature is absent from the old linkage.  The retained zipper records
recover the source, so different counterflow paths remain disjoint.
\(\square\)

Thus before retirement we may, without loss, align the route flag to

\[
                         f=\beta(u_m).
\tag{6.4}
\]

The values of the other tail coordinates, including coordinate \(2m\),
can then be written under the same shadow clock.

### Theorem 6.3 (finite aperture-retirement collar)

Assume the marked work stage has aligned the flag as in (6.4).

If the target \(A(u)\) has nonquiet \(p_1\), write \(E_f(u)\) directly
with that same selected \(p_1\) row and apply Theorem 6.1.

Otherwise the target \(p_1\) is one of

\[
                         00,\qquad20,\qquad22.
\tag{6.5}
\]

Let \(j>1\) be the first nonquiet target scan pair.  Write a majority
state \(S(u)\) with root \(f=\beta(u_m)\), boundary coordinate
\(2m=1\), the target marker value on \(p_j\), and the following local
\((p_1,0)\) row:

\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
\text{target }p_1& S(u)_{p_1,0}&
       \text{physical aperture intermediate}\\ \hline
00&(10,0)&(00,1),\\
20&(10,2)&(20,1),\\
22&(12,2)&(22,1).
\end{array}
\tag{6.6}
\]

All other internal coordinates of \(S(u)\) equal their target values.
Then the following five arcs are directed and end at \(b(u)=\tau A(u)\):

1. use the physical \(\{0,1\}\) edge in (6.6);
2. toggle the now-first nonquiet target marker \(p_j\);
3. if \(f=0\), move the coordinate-zero unit to coordinate \(2m\);
   if \(f=2\), move the coordinate-\(2m\) unit to coordinate zero;
4. toggle \(p_j\) back to its target value; and
5. use the root edge in Theorem 6.1.

Throughout the collar, every nonterminal state retains either the
non-one root flag or the target decoder.  The collar avoids \(V(P_0)\)
and the old linkage.

#### Proof

The first arrow in (6.6) is exactly the generic aperture transfer.  It
changes the active \(p_1\) clock to its quiet target value and sets
coordinate zero to one.  Therefore \(p_j\) becomes first nonquiet, and
its selected edge is the unique second arc.  Denote the resulting
majority state by \(F(u)\).  Its internal marker is the matching mate of
the target marker, its root is \(f\), and its local triple
\((2m-1,2m,0)\) is

\[
                 (0,1,1)\quad(f=0),
       \qquad    (2,1,1)\quad(f=2).
\tag{6.7}
\]

For \(f=0\), the third arc is

\[
                 (0,1,1)\longrightarrow(0,2,0),
\]

and for \(f=2\) it is

\[
                 (2,1,1)\longrightarrow(2,0,2).
\]

It reverses the shore and changes no internal scan pair, so \(p_j\) is
still first nonquiet.  The fourth arc is therefore its selected matching
edge, returning the marker to the target value.  The resulting majority
state is exactly \(E_f(u)\).  The fifth arc is Theorem 6.1 and ends at
\(b(u)\).

The root remains zero or two through the first four arcs, so those states
cannot meet the old linkage.  The marker remains internal and nonquiet,
so no state belongs to the boundary-current endpoint set.  Reverse
decoding reads the target marker, the row of (6.6), and the root flag;
thus collars for distinct \(u\) are disjoint.  \(\square\)

The direct \(p_1\) branches \(01,21\) and the exceptional generic branch
\(11\) are included in the first sentence of Theorem 6.3: their target
\(p_1\) is already a selected nonquiet row, so no aperture-retirement
arcs are needed.

### Theorem 6.4 (the shadow clock routes the component-mass defect)

Delete the \(p_1\) clock coordinates and hold the root fixed.  The
remaining physical work graph has two components,

\[
 L=\{0,2m\},
 \qquad
 R=\{3,4,\ldots,2m-2\}.
\tag{6.8}
\]

For \(m\ge5\), while \(p_1\) remains in one of its six selected
nonquiet states, one can

1. move an arbitrary feasible number of units from \(L\) to \(R\) or
   from \(R\) to \(L\);
2. change the clock mass among one, two, and three; and
3. realize any prescribed configurations inside \(L\) and \(R\) with
   the resulting component masses.

All moves have directed lifts in the fixed phase-zero contraction and
the root flag is untouched.

#### Proof

An ordinary work transfer strictly inside \(L\) or \(R\) leaves the
literal \(p_1\) row unchanged at the physical intermediate and therefore
toggles the clock to its selected mate on the following matching edge.
It changes no component mass.  Call such an edge a **neutral reset**.

To move one unit from \(L\) to \(R\), arrange a donor at coordinate zero
and a hole at coordinate three and use

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 01&\xrightarrow{,0\to1,}11\xrightarrow{M_0}02,\\
 02&\xrightarrow{,2\to3,}01\xrightarrow{M_0}10.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{6.9}
\]

The net effect is \(|L|_{\rm mass}-1\),
\(|R|_{\rm mass}+1\).  A neutral reset changes the terminal clock
\(10\) back to the required starting endpoint \(01\), at the cost of one
reversible internal work edge but with no component-mass change.  Thus
(6.9) may be repeated.

For the reverse transfer, arrange a hole at coordinate zero and a donor
at coordinate three and use

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 12&\xrightarrow{,1\to0,}02\xrightarrow{M_0}11,\\
 11&\xrightarrow{,3\to2,}12\xrightarrow{M_0}21.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{6.10}
\]

This moves one unit from \(R\) to \(L\); a neutral reset changes
\(21\) back to \(12\).

The same local table gives the clock-mass ladder

\[
 \begin{array}{rcll}
 10&\xrightarrow{,3\to2,}11\xrightarrow{M_0}02,
     &\quad&1\to2,\\
 02&\xrightarrow{,2\to3,}01\xrightarrow{M_0}10,
     &&2\to1,\\
 02&\xrightarrow{,0\to1,}12\xrightarrow{M_0}21,
     &&2\to3,\\
 21&\xrightarrow{,1\to0,}11\xrightarrow{M_0}02,
     &&3\to2.
 \end{array}
\tag{6.11}
\]

Here the missing or extra clock unit is supplied by the indicated work
component.  Equations (6.9)--(6.11), together with neutral resets, route
any feasible mass triple

\[
       (\operatorname{mass}L,operatorname{mass}p_1,
                         \operatorname{mass}R)
\]

to any other triple with the same total and clock mass in
\(\{1,2,3\}\).

It remains only to arrange donors and holes and then write the internal
configurations.  The capacity-two token graph on a path is connected at
every fixed nonextreme mass: sweep from left to right and move the prefix
excess across each successive edge.  For \(m\ge5\), the central total,
the root bound, the two-site bound on \(L\), and the three-unit clock
bound keep \(R\) strictly between zero and full capacity throughout any
needed reset; hence it contains a legal neutral work edge.  The two-site
component \(L\) is connected at each fixed mass as well.  Lift every such
internal work edge through the active clock by Theorem 4.1.  \(\square\)

Theorem 6.4 removes the only scalar obstruction to the work writer.  The
remaining issue is labelled serialization: the component-routing schedule
must overwrite the source record chain by the target record chain while
retaining a reversible source/stage decoder at every intermediate state.
The tagged time-expansion theorem gives exactly this conclusion once an
explicit digit-by-digit target transducer is specified; ordinary
unlabelled token-graph connectivity alone does not prove pairwise
disjointness of the whole bank.

### Lemma 6.5 (the complement record transducer has two-bit state)

For a ternary digit \(a\), write

\[
                   B(a)=\beta(a),\qquad G(a)=\gamma(a).
\]

The pair \((B(a),G(a))\in\{0,2\}^2\) takes the three values

\[
                         (0,0),\quad(2,0),\quad(2,2)
\]

and determines \(a\).  Complement acts by the exact reflected rule

\[
 \boxed{
 B(2-a)=2-G(a),
 \qquad
 G(2-a)=2-B(a).}
\tag{6.12}
\]

Consequently the source zipper code

\[
 c(B_1),\ (G_1,B_2),\ (G_2,B_3),\ldots
\]

and the complemented target code

\[
 c(2-G_1),\ (2-B_1,2-G_2),\
        (2-B_2,2-G_3),\ldots
\tag{6.13}
\]

are related by a one-record-lookahead finite-state transducer.  At a cut
between processed and unprocessed records, the two interface bits
\((B_i,G_i)\), equivalently their reflected pair, recover the digit at
the cut.  Hence a word consisting of a target-code prefix, this interface,
and a source-code suffix still recovers the entire ternary source.

#### Proof

Substitute \(a=0,1,2\) in the definitions of \(\beta,\gamma\).  The
three displayed pairs are distinct, and reflected complement sends them
respectively to

\[
                         (2,2),\quad(2,0),\quad(0,0).
\]

This proves (6.12).  In a zipper record, the first entry is \(G_i\) and
the second is the next clock label \(B_{i+1}\).  Applying (6.12) gives
(6.13).  Sequential decoding on either side of the interface then proves
the last assertion.  \(\square\)

Thus the outstanding writer is finite-state rather than a growing-memory
problem.  A proof-safe completion must still give a literal local token
path implementing (6.12) while keeping the two interface bits until their
target copies have been installed.  The component router proves that all
required mass compensation is available, but does not by itself provide
this occurrence-labelled local path family.

### Lemma 6.6 (literal extreme-code swap)

In a full zipper code, the two code bits \((B_i,G_i)\) for the internal
ternary digits occupy disjoint two-coordinate connector blocks; the final
digit is held by the terminal delimiter/collar.  On two adjacent connector
blocks there is the literal path

\[
\begin{aligned}
0022&\to0112\to1012\to1102\to2002\\
    &\to2011\to2101\to2110\to2200.
\end{aligned}
\tag{6.14}
\]

Every arrow moves one unit across one adjacent physical edge.  Hence, if
the four coordinates lie after the \(p_1\) shadow clock, (6.14) has its
directed lift in the fixed contraction.  The reverse path swaps the
blocks in the opposite direction.

Moreover, after including the terminal delimiter block, every central
ternary word has the same number of code blocks \(00\) and \(22\): it is
just the identity

\[
                       \#\{i:u_i=0\}=\#\{i:u_i=2\},
\tag{6.15}
\]

which follows from \(\sum_i u_i=m\).  Thus, at the unlabelled level,
pairing the \(00\) and \(22\) blocks and composing adjacent block swaps,
with Theorem 6.4 handling a block paired to the terminal collar,
implements the complement rule (6.12); the \(20\) blocks stay fixed.

#### Proof

Reading (6.14) from left to right, the moved units cross positions
\(3\to2,2\to1,3\to2,2\to1,4\to3,3\to2,4\to3,3\to2\).
Every coordinate stays between zero and two, so all arrows are legal.
The endpoint exchanges \(00\) and \(22\).  Equation (6.15) follows by
subtracting \(m\) from the digit sum.  \(\square\)

This gives a concrete physical atom for the finite transducer.  It does
not by itself choose the extreme-block pairing in a way that makes all
source paths simultaneously disjoint.  A moving-delimiter/FIFO schedule
must serialize these swaps so that a target-prefix/source-suffix decoder
survives; that is the exact labelled residue.

### Proposition 6.7 (the unmarked FIFO claim is false)

The fixed branch tag and canonical monotone interval transport do not by
themselves give a pairwise-disjoint path family.  Put

\[
                    A=00,\qquad B=20,\qquad C=22.
\]

The two connector words

\[
                         ABCB,qquad ACBB
\tag{6.16}
\]

are both balanced: they encode the ternary words \((0,1,2,1)\) and
\((0,2,1,1)\), each with digit sum four.  In the first word the selected
opposite pair is the displayed \(A,C\).  Moving \(C\) left once through
the intervening \(B\) gives the second word literally.  The second word
starts with the same selected ordered pair already adjacent.  Thus a
delimiter which records only the pass and ordered block types gives the
same global state on the two routes.  The interchange endpoint cannot be
deleted from a graph path.

The circular step in the rejected proof was the assertion that the
prefix discrepancies recover their own goal interval: the goal and its
selected endpoints are not determined by the current unmarked tape.

There is, however, an exact corrected reduction.  Mark every crossed
stationary block by

\[
                    20\mapsto11,qquad
                    01\mapsto10,qquad
                    21\mapsto12.
\tag{6.17}
\]

These marks are injective, mass-preserving, and disjoint from the macro
