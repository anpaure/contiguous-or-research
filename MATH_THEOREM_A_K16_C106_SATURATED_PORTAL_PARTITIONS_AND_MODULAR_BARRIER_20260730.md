## 1. Scope

Let $E$ be the authenticated 211,604 direction-coherent seams of the
length-eight K16 source.  For $e:u\to v$, let
$h_e\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{93}$ be its defect-incidence vector and let
$s_e\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}$ be its exact scale-two direct-dual slack.  The five lock triples are
the triples in the frozen five-lock theorem.

This note proves two complementary facts.

1. Once seams of individual slack one are admitted, the signed cycle lattice
   in the 93 service coordinates plus seam count is all of
   \(\mathbb Z^{94}\).  Hence no nontrivial finite-abelian congruence on only
   those global coordinates can exclude an $R=1,2,3,$ or $4$ C106
   signature.  Positivity, total-slack chronology, or port capacity is
   indispensable.
2. Those nonlinear restrictions nevertheless have a very small exact
   equality face.  Every integral C106 circulation is a packing of
   *saturated portal cycles*.  Their lock supports form a set partition of
   the $R$ `S` locks.  For $R=2,3,4$, the complete abstract portal master
   has respectively $20,50,75$ lock-set/partition states before repeated-
   target labels, or $540,450,225$ states after those labels are included.

The first assertion is a no-go for a proof method, not a feasibility result.
The second is an exact necessary extended formulation, not a construction.

## 2. Exact saturation of the signed cycle lattice

For a balanced signed seam vector $z\in\ker_{\mathbb Z}B$, write

\[
        \Phi(z)=\left(\sum_e h_ez_e,\ \sum_ez_e\right)\in\mathbb Z^{94}.
\]

Let $L_1$ be the subgroup generated by balanced signed cycles in the graph
formed by seams of individual direct slack at most one, with the usual SCC
cycle eligibility.

### Theorem 2.1 (no global modular quotient beyond accounting)

For the frozen catalogue,

\[
                         \boxed{\Phi(L_1)=\mathbb Z^{94}}.       \tag{2.1}
\]

Consequently, if $G$ is any finite abelian group and

\[
               \chi:\mathbb Z^{94}\longrightarrow G
\]

is a homomorphism which vanishes on every signed cycle column in the
slack-at-most-one graph, then \(\chi=0\).  Since that graph is contained in
every individual-slack layer $R\ge1$, the same conclusion holds for the
full catalogue used at C106 $R=1,2,3,4$.

If slack is retained as a ninety-fifth coordinate, the only universal
integer relation is the primitive direct-accounting row

\[
       \sum_t b_tm_t-2C+R=0.                            \tag{2.2}
\]

Thus an additive modular certificate depending only on global target
multiplicities, seam count, and total slack cannot sharpen (2.2) on these
layers.

#### Proof

The authenticated exact-saturation audit constructs fundamental signed
cycle columns in each individual-slack layer.  In layer one it exhibits a
$94\times94$ minor of absolute determinant one in the 93 service
coordinates plus count.  Equivalently, its Smith invariant factors are

\[
                              1^{94}.                   \tag{2.3}
\]

Therefore the subgroup generated by those columns has index one in
\(\mathbb Z^{94}\), proving (2.1).  A homomorphism which vanishes on a
generating subgroup equal to the whole domain is zero.  Adding more seams
cannot create a nonzero annihilator, proving the assertion for every
$R\ge1$ layer and the full graph.

Although the audited basis is written using signed fundamental cycles, this
does not hide a directedness assumption.  In each active SCC every eligible
arc lies on a directed cycle.  The sum of one such cycle through every arc is
a strictly positive integral circulation $w$.  Given any signed integral
circulation $z$, choose $N$ so large that $z+Nw\ge0$.  Both $z+Nw$
and $Nw$ decompose into nonnegative directed cycles, and their difference
is $z$.  Hence the group generated by directed-cycle columns is the same
signed cycle lattice used by the determinant audit.

Finally, exact replay gives (2.2) on every seam, and its coefficient on $R$
is one.  Hence the slack coordinate is recovered integrally from the other
94 coordinates; (2.1) leaves no torsion or further congruence in that
primitive accounting hyperplane.  \(\square\)

### Boundary of Theorem 2.1

The theorem concerns the **signed group completion** of the cycle semigroup.
It does not say that a demanded C106 vector has a nonnegative cycle
decomposition, a decomposition of total slack at most four, or a
capacity-one decomposition.  Fundamental signed generators may use negative
coefficients, and a cycle in the individual-slack-one graph may have total
slack larger than one.  Therefore the theorem does not contradict the
five-lock Hamming inequality, which is a nonnegative bounded-slack theorem.
Nor does it contradict congruences on a restricted support such as the
662-seam `SSSSS` support.

It does prove that a new R1--R4 obstruction cannot be merely another Smith-
normal-form character of the global 94-coordinate cycle lattice.  It must
use at least one of:

* nonnegative cycle decomposition;
* total slack distributed cycle by cycle;
* port capacity/disjointness;
* a proper support restriction; or
* additional physical rows.

## 3. Saturation of every selected cycle

For a directed simple cycle $C$, put

\[
 r(C)=\sum_{e\in C}s_e,
 \qquad
 \sigma_i(C)=\sum_{e\in C}\sum_{t\in T_i}h_e(t)\pmod2,
\]

and let $A(C)=\operatorname{supp}\sigma(C)\subseteq[5]$.  The frozen
all-slack five-lock theorem gives

\[
                            |A(C)|\le r(C).              \tag{3.1}
\]

Fix a C106 normal-form signature.  Let $S\subseteq[5]$ be its `S`
positions, so $|S|=R$; at every $i\notin S$, exactly one member of
$T_i$ is repeated.

### Theorem 3.1 (saturated portal-cycle partition)

In every integral balanced capacity-one C106 realization:

1. every selected cycle $C$ satisfies
   \[
                           r(C)=|A(C)|;                 \tag{3.2}
   \]
2. the nonempty sets $A(C)$ are pairwise disjoint;
3. their union is exactly $S$;
4. every tight selected cycle has $A(C)=\varnothing$.

Thus the positive cycles canonically define a set partition of $S$.  In
particular, no selected cycle can have total slack above $R$, and a cycle
whose slack exceeds the Hamming weight of its lock syndrome is forbidden.

#### Proof

Capacity one decomposes the selected circulation into vertex-disjoint simple
directed cycles.  The global lock syndrome is the indicator of $S$, and
the total direct slack is $R=|S|$.  Hence

\[
 R=\left|\mathop{\bigoplus}_C A(C)\right|
   \le \sum_C|A(C)|
   \le \sum_Cr(C)=R.                                  \tag{3.3}
\]

Every inequality is equality.  Equality in the second inequality gives
(3.2) cycle by cycle.  Equality in the Hamming triangle inequality says that
no lock coordinate occurs in two cycle syndromes.  Their XOR is $S$, so
the nonempty supports partition $S$.  A tight cycle has $r(C)=0$, hence
empty support by (3.2).  \(\square\)

This is the correct way to exclude wasteful high-slack fractional arcs from
an integer-hull relaxation.  One must lift the circulation to cycles and use
(3.2); simply deleting every seam with $s_e>R$ from the unrestricted
continuous arc LP is not an exact statement about that broader LP.

## 4. Exact Bell-partition extended master

Let $\Pi(S)$ denote the set of set partitions of $S$.  For fixed $R$,
define

\[
 \Omega_R=\{(S,\pi,\beta): |S|=R,\ \pi\in\Pi(S),\
             \beta_i\in T_i\ (i\notin S)\}.            \tag{4.1}
\]

Here $\beta_i$ names the repeated target in the non-`S` lock $i$.
Introduce nonnegative master variables \(\Lambda_{S,\pi,\beta}\) with

\[
                       \sum_{\Omega_R}\Lambda=1.       \tag{4.2}
\]

For every nonempty $A\subseteq[5]$, put

\[
 w_A=\sum_{(S,\pi,\beta):A\in\pi}\Lambda_{S,\pi,\beta},             \tag{4.3}
\]

and for every lock target $t\in T_i$, put

\[
 y_t=\sum_{(S,\pi,\beta):i\notin S,\ \beta_i=t}
                           \Lambda_{S,\pi,\beta}.       \tag{4.4}
\]

Let $Y_C\ge0$ be cycle-column variables.  The exact portal coupling rows
are

\[
 \sum_{C:r(C)=|A(C)|,\ A(C)=A}Y_C=w_A
 \qquad(\varnothing\ne A\subseteq[5]).                \tag{4.5}
\]

The target rows use baseline one plus $y_t$, while nonlock targets remain
exactly one.  Tight zero-syndrome columns are completed separately, subject
to the same service, count, and port-capacity rows.

### Theorem 4.1 (exact abstract portal convex hull)

Equations (4.2)--(4.5) give exactly the convex hull of the C106 normal-form
lock ledgers together with their allowed portal-cycle support partitions.
They are necessary for every physical C106 circulation and remain valid
after taking arbitrary convex combinations of physical circulations.

They are not sufficient for physical feasibility: cycle service vectors,
port disjointness, count 106, target co-location, and the tight completion
must still be imposed.

#### Proof

An integral realization has one normal-form pair $(S,\beta)$.  Theorem 3.1
supplies one partition $\pi$ of $S$, so its data are obtained by setting
the corresponding $\Lambda$ to one.  Convex combinations give
(4.2)--(4.5).

Conversely, the simplex (4.2) is by definition the convex hull of its unit
vectors.  At a unit vector, (4.3) is exactly the incidence vector of the
blocks of $\pi$, and (4.4) is exactly the repeated-target choice
\(\beta\).  Therefore its projection is precisely the stated abstract
convex hull.  \(\square\)

The state counts are small:

\[
\begin{array}{c|ccc}
R&\binom5R B_R&|\Omega_R|=\binom5R B_R3^{5-R}
  &\text{portal types for fixed }S\\ \hline
2&20&540&2\\
3&50&450&5\\
4&75&225&15
\end{array}                                             \tag{4.6}
\]

Thus an R2 disjunction has only the types `2` and `1+1`; R3 has
`3`, `2+1`, and `1+1+1` with their labelled blocks; and R4 has the fifteen
set partitions of four locks.  These are the proof-guided branches that the
raw group-convex arc LP omits.

### Corollary 4.2 (odd-intersection cuts)

For a fixed $S$ and every odd nonempty $J\subseteq S$, every portal
partition vector satisfies

\[
       \sum_{A\subseteq S:\ |A\cap J|\text{ odd}}w_A\ge1.            \tag{4.7}
\]

Indeed, in a partition of $S$, the number of blocks meeting $J$ oddly
has the same parity as $|J|$, and hence is a positive odd integer.

For $R=3$, the degree rows

\[
              \sum_{A\ni i}w_A=1\quad(i\in S)          \tag{4.8}
\]

together with (4.7) for $J=S$ give the complete portal-partition convex
hull.  Writing $p_{ij}=w_{\{i,j\}}$ and $t=w_S$, the nontrivial row is

\[
                         p_{12}+p_{13}+p_{23}+t\le1,    \tag{4.9}
\]

and the remaining mass is the all-singleton partition.  Explicitly, assign
weights $p_{ij}$ to the three pair-plus-singleton partitions, weight $t$ to
the one-block partition, and weight
$1-p_{12}-p_{13}-p_{23}-t$ to the all-singleton partition.  The three degree
rows recover exactly the singleton coordinates, proving sufficiency.

For $R=4$, the
four triple choices $J\subset S$ give immediate valid cuts; the exact
15-state simplex (4.2) is preferable to an incomplete hand list of facets.

## 5. Exact lock-triple host patterns

The partition support also forces a small co-location catalogue.

* If $i\in S$, its target demand on $T_i$ is $(1,1,1)$.  Exactly one
  portal cycle has odd $T_i$-load.  Therefore either one cycle carries all
  three targets, or that portal cycle carries one target and one even-
  syndrome cycle carries the other two.
* If $i\notin S$ and $t\in T_i$ is repeated, its demand is $(2,1,1)$,
  and every selected cycle has even $T_i$-load.  Therefore either one cycle
  carries $(2,1,1)$, or two cycles carry respectively
  \((2,0,0)\mid(0,1,1)), or they split as
  \((1,1,0)\mid(1,0,1)), with coordinates ordered so that the first is the
  repeated target.

In particular every lock triple is hosted on at most two selected cycles.
This gives a finite physical filter after portal columns are generated: a
signature fails if its required one/two-cycle host pattern has no
port-disjoint realization.  Marginal group balance alone does not see this
condition.
