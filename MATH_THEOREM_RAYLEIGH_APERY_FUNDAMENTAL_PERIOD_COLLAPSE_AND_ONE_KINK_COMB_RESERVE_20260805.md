# Rayleigh Apéry clocks: exact fundamental-period collapse and a positive one-kink comb reserve

**Date:** 2026-08-05  
**Method:** pure mathematics; no computation, search, or solver  
**Status:** unconditional exact reduction and positive family.  A zero
proper residue defect is not a new boundary case: it collapses the formal
clock exactly to a smaller fundamental period.  Every primitive one-kink
Apéry table decomposes into arithmetic Rayleigh combs and has a uniform
strict reserve.  Consequently any nonpositive formal Apéry clock must be
primitive and genuinely multi-kink.  This does not sign that remaining
cone and does not treat the finite availability shoulder.

## 1. Formal Apéry clocks

Fix `g>=1`, `lambda>0`, and put

\[
 P=g\lambda.
\tag{1.1}
\]

Let

\[
 d:\mathbb Z/g\mathbb Z\longrightarrow[0,\infty),
 \qquad d_0=0,
\tag{1.2}
\]

be cyclically subadditive:

\[
 d_{r+t}\le d_r+d_t.
\tag{1.3}
\]

Assume the physical shifts

\[
 s_r=r\lambda-d_r\qquad(0\le r<g)
\tag{1.4}

are nonnegative.  The formal clock is

\[
 U_m=\lambda m-d_{m\bmod g},
\tag{1.5}

and its Rayleigh phase functional is

\[
 \Phi_g(d)
 =\sum_{r=0}^{g-1}F_P(s_r),
 \qquad
 F_P(x)=\sum_{q\ge0}K(qP+x).
\tag{1.6}

The equivalent shift table

\[
 0=s_0,s_1,\ldots,s_{g-1},s_g=P
\tag{1.7}

is internally superadditive, including the carry inequalities.

## 2. A zero defect collapses the fundamental period

### Theorem 2.1 (exact critical-residue collapse)

Suppose

\[
 d_a=0
\tag{2.1}

for some proper residue `1<=a<g`, and put

\[
 g_0=\gcd(a,g),\qquad P_0=g_0\lambda.
\tag{2.2}

Then `d` is constant on every coset of the subgroup generated by `a`:

\[
 \boxed{d_{r+a}=d_r\qquad(r\in\mathbb Z/g\mathbb Z).}
\tag{2.3}

Hence there is a cyclically subadditive defect

\[
 \widetilde d:\mathbb Z/g_0\mathbb Z\longrightarrow[0,\infty)
\tag{2.4}

such that

\[
 d_r=\widetilde d_{r\bmod g_0}.
\tag{2.5}

The apparent `g`-period clock is literally the `g_0`-period clock:

\[
 \boxed{
 U_m=\lambda m-\widetilde d_{m\bmod g_0}
 \quad(m\ge0).}
\tag{2.6}

In particular,

\[
 \boxed{
 \Phi_g(d)=\Phi_{g_0}(\widetilde d).}
\tag{2.7}

There is no multiplicity factor.

#### Proof

Let `L=g/g_0` be the order of `a` in the cyclic group.  Repeated
subadditivity gives

\[
 d_{na}\le n d_a=0
\]

for every integer `n>=0`; hence every element of the subgroup generated by
`a`, including `-a=(L-1)a`, has defect zero.  Therefore

\[
 d_{r+a}\le d_r+d_a=d_r,
\]

while

\[
 d_r=d_{r+a-a}\le d_{r+a}+d_{-a}=d_{r+a}.
\]

This proves (2.3).  The subgroup generated by `a` is precisely the set of
multiples of `g_0`, so (2.4)--(2.5) follow.  Subadditivity of
`widetilde d` is inherited by choosing representatives.

Substitution of (2.5) into (1.5) gives (2.6), so the two infinite clock
sequences agree term by term.  Their absolutely convergent Rayleigh sums
are therefore equal, proving (2.7). \(\square\)

### Direct phase check

Write `g=L g_0`.  For `0<=u<g_0` and `0<=k<L`,

\[
 s_{u+kg_0}
 =u\lambda-\widetilde d_u+kP_0.
\tag{2.8}

Thus the points

\[
 qP+s_{u+kg_0}
 =(qL+k)P_0+u\lambda-\widetilde d_u
\]

range exactly once over every nonnegative `P_0`-translate.  This independently
replays the no-multiplicity identity (2.7).

If repeated collapses reach `g_0=1`, the clock is arithmetic and

\[
 \Phi=C(\lambda)>{377\over108000}.
\tag{2.9}

## 3. The primitive one-kink family

Fix integers

\[
 1\le q\le g,
 \qquad h=g-q,
\tag{3.1}

and a number `eta>0`.  Put

\[
 P=q\eta,
 \qquad
 s_r=\eta(r-h)_+quad(0\le r\le g).
\tag{3.2}

Thus the shift increments are zero through the initial block of length
`h`, then are constantly `eta` to the endpoint.  The corresponding maximum
density is

\[
 \lambda={P\over g}={q\eta\over g}.
\tag{3.3}

### Lemma 3.1 (physical validity)

The table (3.2) is nonnegative and internally superadditive.  Moreover

\[
 {s_r\over r}\le {P\over g}=\lambda
 \qquad(1\le r<g).
\tag{3.4}

If `h>0`, every inequality in (3.4) is strict; hence the endpoint is the
unique maximum-density denomination and (3.2) is an honest primitive
Apéry table.

#### Proof

Put `f(r)=(r-h)_+`.  If `i+j<=g`, then

\[
 f(i+j)\ge f(i)+f(j).
\tag{3.5}

Indeed the claim is immediate if at most one of `i,j` exceeds `h`; if both
do, its two sides are `i+j-h` and `i+j-2h`.

If `i+j>=g`, then

\[
 f(g)+f(i+j-g)\ge f(i)+f(j).
\tag{3.6}

When at most one of `i,j` exceeds `h`, the right side is at most
`g-h=f(g)`.  When both exceed `h`, put `x=i+j`.  If `x<=g+h`, (3.6) is
`g-h>=x-2h`; if `x>=g+h`, both sides equal `x-2h`.
Multiplication by `eta` proves every ordinary and carry-superadditivity
inequality.

For `r<=h`, (3.4) is trivial.  For `h<r<=g`, it is equivalent to

\[
 {r-h\over r}\le {g-h\over g},
\]

which follows from `r<=g` and is strict for `r<g` when `h>0`. \(\square\)

### Theorem 3.2 (exact arithmetic-comb decomposition)

The complete formal functional of (3.2) is

\[
 \boxed{
 \Phi_g
 =C(\eta)+hC(P).}
\tag{3.7}

Consequently

\[
 \boxed{
 \Phi_g>(h+1){377\over108000}>0.}
\tag{3.8}

#### Proof

Among the `g` phases, the values are

\[
 \underbrace{0,\ldots,0}_{h+1\text{ copies}},
 \eta,2\eta,\ldots,(q-1)\eta.
\tag{3.9}

One zero together with the `q-1` positive phases is the complete residue
system modulo `P=q eta`.  Hence

\[
 \sum_{j=0}^{q-1}F_{q\eta}(j\eta)
 =\sum_{n\ge0}K(n\eta)
 =C(\eta).
\tag{3.10}

The remaining `h` zero phases each contribute `F_P(0)=C(P)`, proving
(3.7).  Apply the uniform all-mesh arithmetic-comb bound separately to
`eta` and `P` to obtain (3.8). \(\square\)

### Calibrations

* `q=1`, `h=g-1` is exactly the endpoint-only family.  Formula (3.7)
  becomes `g C(P)`.
* `q=g`, `h=0` is the arithmetic clock.  Formula (3.7) becomes
  `C(P/g)`; its apparent period `g` collapses to the fundamental period
  one by Theorem 2.1.
* Intermediate `q` are primitive whenever `h>0`, despite their complete
  decomposition into two positive arithmetic comb contributions.

## 4. Exact surviving formal obstruction

Let a **primitive** Apéry table mean one satisfying

\[
 d_r>0\qquad(1\le r<g).
\tag{4.1}

### Corollary 4.1

If a nonpositive formal Apéry clock exists, then one exists whose true
fundamental period satisfies all of the following:

1. it is primitive in the sense of (4.1);
2. its shift-increment profile is not one-kink;
3. its phase functional cannot be reduced by a proper critical residue or
   by an arithmetic-comb identity of the form (3.7).

#### Proof

Choose a nonpositive formal clock with minimum true residue period.  A
proper zero defect contradicts minimality by Theorem 2.1.  A one-kink table
is strictly positive by Theorem 3.2. \(\square\)

Thus the endpoint-only covariance obstruction does not threaten the
combined phase functional: it is the `q=1` member of an entirely positive
comb-decomposable family.  The unresolved analytic gate begins only with
primitive multi-kink Apéry profiles.

This is an exact reduction, not a proof that those profiles are positive.
In particular, nonlinear phase interactions between two or more kinks are
not controlled by summing their separate zeroth modes.
