# Root-prefix suspension of the \(\mathcal D_4\) port factor and the first-insertion visibility obstruction

Date: 2026-07-26

Method: pure mathematics only.

## 0. Result

Write \(C_r=\operatorname {Cat}_r\), and let \(F_r\) be the canonical
MSW \(\mathcal D_r\)-port complement-path factor.  Let \(G_4\) be the
explicit fourteen-row factor from
MATH_THEOREM_D4_PAIR23_PORT_FACTOR_20260726.md.  Thus \(G_4\) and
\(F_4\) have the same ports and the same complete \(X/Y\) ownership
ledgers, but their first-insertion pair totals differ by

\[
                             (0,0,-1,+1).                 \tag{0.1}
\]

There is an exact root-aligned suspension to every \(s\ge4\).  Replace
\(F_4\) by \(G_4\) in every prefix cylinder

\[
       \mathcal C_s=\{PB:P\in\mathcal D_4,\ 
                         B\in\mathcal D_{s-4}\}.          \tag{0.2}
\]

The result is a complete \(\mathcal D_s\)-port factor
\(G_s^{\rm pre}\), with exact \(X/Y\) ownership and the same port at
both ends of every row.  Its global first-insertion histogram differs
from that of \(F_s\) by

\[
 C_{s-4}\bigl(4(e_3-e_2)-e_4-e_6+2e_7\bigr),           \tag{0.3}
\]

and its four pair totals differ by

\[
                       C_{s-4}(0,0,-1,+1).               \tag{0.4}
\]

More generally, every one of the eight \(H_4\)-conjugates of \(G_4\)
suspends, and its state may be chosen independently for each suffix
\(B\).  This gives \(8^{C_{s-4}}\) complete integral factors.  Among all
\(C_s\) roots, exactly \(8C_{s-4}\) have a four-label menu under this
atlas, \(2C_{s-4}\) have a three-label menu, \(2C_{s-4}\) have a
two-label menu, and \(2C_{s-4}\) have a one-label menu within the active
cylinder; roots outside the cylinder are untouched.

Exactly \(11C_{s-4}\) of the \(C_s\) roots change their
first-insertion target.  Hence the changed fraction is

\[
 {11C_{s-4}\over C_s}>{11\over256},\qquad
 {11C_{s-4}\over C_s}\longrightarrow {11\over256}.     \tag{0.5}
\]

This is a genuine root-boundary action, not a strict-interior fringe
substitution: the active \(\mathcal D_4\) word is the initial Dyck
prefix, and the MSW path processes it before the suffix.  Nevertheless
there is a sharp limitation.  In the unary rooted-context operad
generated by

\[
             x\mapsto Ax,\qquad x\mapsto xB,\qquad
             x\mapsto1x0,                               \tag{0.6}
\]

the local first insertion remains the global first insertion if and only
if every nontrivial operation on the route from the hole to the root is a
right concatenation.  Thus every globally visible unary suspension of
the \(\mathcal D_4\) seed is supported on (0.2).  Even allowing an
arbitrary multistate alphabet on each such fourteen-row block, at most

\[
                       14C_{s-4}=(7/128+o(1))C_s         \tag{0.7}
\]

root targets can leave a previously common cell.  A fibre of size \(C_s\)
cannot thereby be reduced to cap \(p=C_s/\theta\) unless

\[
 \theta\le {1\over1-14C_{s-4}/C_s}
                 ={128\over121}+o(1).                   \tag{0.8}
\]

For the single binary switch \(F_4\leftrightarrow G_4\), the sharper
necessary bound is

\[
 \theta\le {1\over1-11C_{s-4}/C_s}
                 ={256\over245}+o(1).                   \tag{0.9}
\]

Consequently the root-prefix suspension proves positive-density boundary
mobility, but unary context suspension of this finite seed cannot supply
the boundary capacity required uniformly for all \(\theta<4\).  Such a
theorem needs a synchronized multirow root operation which changes the
first edge across a wrapping or left-concatenation boundary; that
operation is not generated by the existing one-hole context functor.

## 1. Path and ledger notation

For \(R\in\mathcal D_r\), write the canonical path as

\[
       \Gamma_r(R)=(X_0(R),X_1(R),\ldots,X_r(R)),
       \qquad X_0(R)=R,\quad X_r(R)=\overline R.          \tag{1.1}
\]

Its \(X\) resources are its vertices and its \(Y\) resources are the
adjacent unions \(X_i(R)\cup X_{i+1}(R)\).  A rooted packet trade is
exact when its negative and positive sides have identical aggregate
multisets of both types of resources, with the two paths indexed by a
root \(R\) having the same endpoints \(R,\overline R\).

The canonical concatenation identity is

\[
 \Gamma_{a+b}(AB)=
     \Gamma_a(A)B\ \Vert\
     \overline A\bigl(\Gamma_b(B)\setminus\{B\}\bigr).  \tag{1.2}
\]

Here juxtaposition means concatenation on disjoint consecutive coordinate
blocks, and \(\Vert\) identifies the common seam state
\(\overline A B\).  Identity (1.2) is the only recursive fact needed
below.

## 2. Exact root-prefix suspension

### Theorem 2.1 (complete \(\mathcal D_s\) suspension)

For every \(s\ge4\), the prescription in (0.2) produces a complete
\(\mathcal D_s\)-port complement-path factor \(G_s^{\rm pre}\) with
exactly the same aggregate \(X\) and \(Y\) ledgers as \(F_s\).

#### Proof

Fix \(B\in\mathcal D_{s-4}\).  The fourteen roots \(PB\),
\(P\in\mathcal D_4\), form one packet.  On its row indexed by \(P\),
replace the first slab in (1.2) by the corresponding path

\[
                              \Gamma_4^G(P)B
\]

and keep the tail

\[
 \overline P\bigl(\Gamma_{s-4}(B)\setminus\{B\}\bigr)   \tag{2.1}
\]

unchanged.  The new first slab ends at \(\overline P B\), because the
explicit \(\mathcal D_4\) factor has the same terminal port
\(\overline P\) row by row.  Therefore (2.1) joins legally, and the whole
row still runs from \(PB\) to

\[
                 \overline P\,\overline B=\overline{PB}.
\]

The aggregate \(X\) ledger of the new first slabs equals that of the old
first slabs: it is precisely the equality of the two \(\mathcal D_4\)
\(X\) ledgers, with the fixed spectator \(B\) appended.  The same
statement holds for all \(Y\) resources internal to the first slab.  The
seam \(Y\) resource and every tail resource are identical row by row,
since both depend only on the common endpoint \(\overline P\) and the
fixed path of \(B\).  Thus the positive and negative fourteen-row
packets have exactly the same complete resource multiset.

The root sets

\[
                 \{PB:P\in\mathcal D_4\}
                 \qquad(B\in\mathcal D_{s-4})            \tag{2.2}
\]

are disjoint.  In the canonical factor their resource sets are also
disjoint, because \(F_s\) is an exact factor.  Each replacement has
exactly the same resource set as its own negative packet, so positive
packets for distinct \(B\)'s cannot collide with one another or with an
unchanged row.  Keeping all roots outside \(\mathcal C_s\) canonical
therefore gives a complete factor.  Ports remain fixed row by row.
\(\square\)

The proof is integral throughout.  There is no fractional point,
rounding, or divisibility condition: it is a disjoint union of literal
fourteen-for-fourteen exact trades.

## 3. Exact first-insertion census

The path in (1.2) starts by traversing \(\Gamma_4(P)B\).  Hence its first
inserted global coordinate is exactly the first inserted local coordinate
of the row \(P\); the suffix \(B\) is a spectator.

For the explicit factor, the new and old local first-insertion histograms
are respectively

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 h(G_4)&=5e_8+e_2+4e_3+e_4+e_6+2e_7,\\
 h(F_4)&=5e_8+5e_2+2e_4+2e_6.
 \end{aligned}                                         \tag{3.1}
\]

Their difference is the bracket in (0.3), and summing the same action
over the \(C_{s-4}\) suffixes proves (0.3)--(0.4).

For comparison, the complete canonical first-insertion census follows
from the first-return decomposition.  A root whose first primitive
component has semilength \(j\) has canonical first insertion \(2j\), and
there are \(C_{j-1}C_{s-j}\) such roots.  Therefore

\[
 h(F_s)=\sum_{j=1}^{s}C_{j-1}C_{s-j}e_{2j},            \tag{3.2}
\]

and the full new census, not merely its signed difference, is

\[
 h(G_s^{\rm pre})
 =\sum_{j=1}^{s}C_{j-1}C_{s-j}e_{2j}
  +C_{s-4}\bigl(4(e_3-e_2)-e_4-e_6+2e_7\bigr).        \tag{3.3}
\]

Comparing the fourteen rows, the first-insertion coordinate is unchanged
only at the ports

\[
                         1234,\qquad1235,\qquad1356.     \tag{3.4}
\]

It changes at the other eleven.  Thus exactly \(11C_{s-4}\) global roots
change target.

The Catalan ratio is

\[
 {C_{s-4}\over C_s}
   =\prod_{n=s-3}^{s}{n+1\over2(2n-1)}.                 \tag{3.5}
\]

Every factor in (3.5) is strictly larger than \(1/4\), and each tends to
\(1/4\).  This proves (0.5).  It also gives

\[
 {14C_{s-4}\over C_s}\longrightarrow{14\over256}
                                      ={7\over128}.     \tag{3.6}
\]

### Corollary 3.1 (integral multistate prefix atlas)

Let

\[
 H_4=\langle(2\ 3),(4\ 5),(6\ 7)\rangle.
\]

For each \(B\in\mathcal D_{s-4}\), choose an arbitrary
\(h_B\in H_4\), and lift the conjugate factor \(h_BG_4\) through the
context \(x\mapsto xB\).  Make these choices independently over all
suffixes and leave every root outside \(\mathcal C_s\) canonical.  Every
one of the resulting \(8^{C_{s-4}}\) objects is a complete integral
\(\mathcal D_s\)-port factor.

Under this atlas, the exact local \(H_4\)-orbit census lifts unchanged:
per suffix, eight roots have four possible first-insertion labels, two
have three, two have two, and two have one.  Thus a fraction

\[
                    {8C_{s-4}\over C_s}\longrightarrow {1\over32}
                                                               \tag{3.7}
\]

of all roots has a literal four-state boundary menu.

#### Proof

Every element of \(H_4\) preserves \(\mathcal D_4\), ports, and both
ownership ledgers.  Theorem 2.1 applies to each conjugate.  Its
disjoint-resource argument also proves that the choices for different
suffixes are independent.  The rootwise menu census is the exact
\(8,2,2,2\) census of the finite \(H_4\)-orbit, repeated once for every
suffix.  \(\square\)

The first-insertion action survives at the boundary of the size-\(s\)
root path.  As always, the intersection of the full path
\(X_0,\ldots,X_s\) is empty because \(X_s=X_0^c\).  Thus (0.3) is a
boundary-child action, not a claim that the mark survives a later
full-hole ancestor window.

## 4. First-insertion visibility in the rooted context operad

### Lemma 4.1 (visibility lemma)

Let \(C[x]\) be a common one-hole Dyck context constructed from the three
operations (0.6), deleting vacuous concatenations by the empty word.  Let
two local rooted paths have the same ports but different first inserted
coordinates.  Their local first insertion is visible as the first
insertion of the lifted paths \(C[x]\) if and only if

\[
                              C[x]=xB                    \tag{4.1}
\]

for a fixed Dyck suffix \(B\).

#### Proof

For \(x\mapsto xB\), identity (1.2) traverses the path of \(x\) first,
so its first edge and inserted coordinate are unchanged.

For a nonempty left concatenation \(x\mapsto Ax\), identity (1.2)
traverses the fixed path of \(A\) first.  The first inserted coordinate is
therefore fixed by \(A\) and is independent of the path in the hole.

For \(x\mapsto J(x)=1x0\), if the local flip word is \(\rho\), the
lifted flip word is

\[
                       (2r+2,\ 1+\operatorname {rev}\rho,\ 1). \tag{4.2}
\]

Its first inserted coordinate is the fixed new outer coordinate \(2r+2\),
again independent of the local path.

Once a left concatenation or a wrapping occurs on the route from the hole
to the global root, this exterior first edge remains earlier than every
edge generated inside the hole; later context operations cannot restore
the local edge to first position.  Conversely, a composition of right
concatenations is a single right concatenation \(x\mapsto xB\), and the
first edge remains local.  This proves (4.1).  \(\square\)

### Corollary 4.2 (sharp support of unary \(\mathcal D_4\) visibility)

Among all common unary rooted-context lifts of the \(\mathcal D_4\)
packet to semilength \(s\), the roots on which its first-insertion choice
can be globally visible are exactly the prefix cylinder \(\mathcal C_s\).
Its size is \(14C_{s-4}\).

#### Proof

By Lemma 4.1 a visible lift has the form \(P\mapsto PB\).  Varying the
fourteen local ports \(P\in\mathcal D_4\) and all possible suffixes
\(B\in\mathcal D_{s-4}\) gives exactly (0.2).  Conversely Theorem 2.1
shows that every such block is legally and independently switchable.
\(\square\)

This is stronger than the old fringe warning.  A strict interior fringe
copy may have positive density as a subtree pattern while having zero
global first-edge visibility.  Corollary 4.2 classifies the copies whose
first edge genuinely reaches the root boundary.

## 5. Capacity consequence

Suppose a principal root fibre contributes \(C_s\) occurrences to one
physical boundary target, whose allowed cap is

\[
                              p={C_s\over\theta}.         \tag{5.1}
\]

Any repair must move at least

\[
                         C_s-p=(1-\theta^{-1})C_s        \tag{5.2}
\]

occurrences out of that target.

An arbitrary collection of states obtained by changing only the unary
\(\mathcal D_4\) prefix packets leaves every root outside
\(\mathcal C_s\) untouched.  Even if every root inside \(\mathcal C_s\)
can be sent to a fresh target, it moves at most \(14C_{s-4}\)
occurrences.  Comparing with (5.2) gives

\[
          1-\theta^{-1}\le {14C_{s-4}\over C_s},         \tag{5.3}
\]

which is exactly (0.8).  This bound is independent of how many local
\(H_4\)-conjugates or other states of the same prefix packet are offered:
they all act on the same prefix support.

For the literal binary choice \(F_4\leftrightarrow G_4\), only the eleven
rows outside (3.4) leave their old target.  Replacing \(14\) by \(11\) in
(5.3) proves (0.9).

When \(\theta\uparrow4\), (5.2) demands movement of nearly \(3C_s/4\)
roots.  Unary \(\mathcal D_4\) suspension exposes only
\((7/128+o(1))C_s\) roots, and the displayed binary state actually moves
only \((11/256+o(1))C_s\).  Therefore no rounding or discrepancy theorem
can close this gap: it is an integral support deficit before selection.

## 6. Exact remaining gate

The positive theorem and the obstruction separate the issue cleanly.

1. The explicit \(\mathcal D_4\) factor does suspend to every scale as a
   complete, integral, root-boundary-active port factor.  Complete \(X/Y\)
   ownership, fixed ports, and a positive-density first-insertion change
   are all proved.

2. Associating or iterating right concatenations does not enlarge its
   visible support: \((xB_1)B_2=x(B_1B_2)\).  Applying the seed after a
   fixed left prefix or below a \(J\)-wrapper hides its first insertion.

3. Hence the known one-hole rooted context functor cannot yield the
   multistate capacity needed for all \(\theta<4\).  The missing primitive
   must be a genuinely synchronized root packet whose collective routing
   changes the first global edge through at least one \(J\) or
   left-product interface.  Equivalently, it must be more than a common
   unary context applied row by row to the finite seed.

The theorem does not rule out such a multi-hole or row-dependent root
associator.  It gives the quantitative test it must beat: in the worst
concentrated fibre its exposed support must be at least
\((1-1/\theta)C_s\), and therefore at least \((3/4-o(1))C_s\)
uniformly up to \(\theta<4\).

Nor is the support bound asserted for a conjugation of the entire
\(\mathcal D_s\) factor followed by a nontrivial ownership-overlay
completion.  That is not a common unary context lift: its negative shore
is a conjugate of \(F_s\), rather than the literal canonical prefix
packet.  Combining such root-scale coordinate components with the
noncoordinate seed would require a new common-completion theorem.  The
present obstruction applies exactly to the operadic suspension requested
here and not to that larger, still-unproved construction.
