# Adversarial audit: fifth-wave Graver covariance routing

## Audit verdict

The central theorem in the fifth-wave lead report is correct: on the exact
factor face generated by two factors, every midpoint-preserving covariance
optimization for the weighted quadratic pair-collision objective reduces
exactly to one antipodal Graver signing.  The pair-sum floor, parity
restitution term, diamond constant, orbit average, and
\(o(W/\sqrt m)\) sufficient slack scale all survive independent
derivation.

The report does **not** prove the missing asymptotic wreath theorem.  Its
actual advance is:

1. a complete mixed-sign covariance theorem on each fixed physical Graver
   face;
2. an exact invariant obstruction on that face;
3. an exact parity-descent versus Graver-realizability identity; and
4. a scale-correct unproved sufficient lemma \(\mathrm{PGR}_A\).

The following qualifications are essential and are respected in the lead
report:

- graph-theoretic alternating cycles are not automatically exact trades;
- a common signing must be used at every depth;
- midpoint-face optimality is not a global lower bound over unrelated
  factors;
- a useful asymptotic physical cube has not been constructed;
- the result is unlabelled and proves neither synchronization nor the OR
  conjecture.

## 1. Normalization and the floor identity

The report uses

\[
R_q(F)=\frac12\sum_S
(\mu_q(S)-a_q)(\mu_q(S)-a_q-1),
\]

the undoubled pair excess.  Let
\(\lambda_q=a_q+\theta_q\).  The scalar identity is

\[
\frac12(z-a_q)(z-a_q-1)
=\frac12\left[
(z-\lambda_q)^2
+(2\theta_q-1)(z-\lambda_q)
-\theta_q(1-\theta_q)
\right].
\tag{A1}
\]

The linear term in (A1) is generally nonzero.  It disappears only after
summing a complete load vector and using

\[
\sum_S(\mu_q(S)-\lambda_q)=0.
\]

Consequently

\[
R_q(F)
=\frac12\left[
\|\mu_q-\lambda_q\mathbf1\|^2
-N_q\theta_q(1-\theta_q)
\right]
\]

is correct.  This independently verifies (1.5).  Any coordinatewise proof
that silently deletes the linear term would be false; the lead report
explicitly uses the total-load cancellation.

All diamond gains in the report use this undoubled convention.  Passing to
the earlier full energy \(Q_q=2R_q\) doubles objectives and gains.

## 2. Exactness of the ownership cube

After common wreaths of \(F,G\) are cancelled, each middle set is incident
to exactly one left and one right owner.  A supported hybrid indicator
satisfies, on every overlap edge,

\[
x_{\rm left}+x_{\rm right}=1.
\]

On a connected bipartite component, these equations force all left
coordinates to one common bit and all right coordinates to its complement.
This proves both directions:

- every whole-component choice is exact;
- there are no other exact factors supported on \(F\cup G\).

For a conformal submove \(h\sqsubseteq g_K\), the homogeneous version of the
same equations propagates inclusion through the component.  Therefore
\(h=0\) or \(h=g_K\).  The squarefree Graver assertion and uniqueness of the
conformal decomposition are valid.

An \(n\)-regular bipartite overlap graph can be decomposed into ordinary
alternating cycles, but flipping one such edge-cycle would generally select
only part of an incident wreath.  Exact ownership propagates through all
other incidences.  Thus the connected components, not arbitrary cycles,
are the valid primitive moves.  This is a decisive scope correction.

## 3. Diamond constant

For a component cut \(I\mid I^c\), put

\[
A=\sum_{i\in I}V_i,\qquad B=\sum_{i\notin I}V_i.
\]

The endpoint pair has deviations
\(\pm(A+B)/2\), while the complementary hybrid pair has deviations
\(\pm(A-B)/2\).  Since \(R\) is one half of the squared norm minus a fixed
floor,

\[
\begin{aligned}
&\Phi(F)+\Phi(G)-\Phi(H_I)-\Phi(H_{I^c})\\
&\qquad
=\frac14\bigl(\|A+B\|^2-\|A-B\|^2\bigr)
=\langle A,B\rangle.
\end{aligned}
\]

Hence replacing the endpoint coin by the hybrid coin gains
\(\langle A,B\rangle/2\) in expected \(R\).  The constant in (3.3) is
correct.

The identity preserves the full wreath first moment because

\[
x_F+x_G=x_{H_I}+x_{H_{I^c}}.
\]

It therefore preserves middle ownership and every wreath marginal of the
two-point law.

## 4. Antipodal optimality

For a sign law with \(\mathbb Es=0\),

\[
\mathbb E\Phi
=\frac12(\|v\|^2-J_H)
+\frac18\operatorname{tr}(G\mathcal R),
\qquad
\mathcal R=\mathbb E ss^{\mathsf T}.
\]

Every such \(\mathcal R\) is a convex combination of sign outer products.
Conversely, for every convex combination of
\(ss^{\mathsf T}\), replacing each atom \(s\) by the equiprobable pair
\(s,-s\) realizes the same second moment and zero sign means.  Thus the
feasible second-moment set is exactly

\[
\operatorname{conv}\{ss^{\mathsf T}:s\in\{\pm1\}^r\}.
\]

The objective is linear on this polytope, so its minimum is attained by one
outer product, realized by one antipodal exact-factor pair.  No
negative-dependence assertion is used.

For the antipodal law,

\[
\operatorname{Cov}(x)=\frac14hh^{\mathsf T},
\qquad h=\sum_i s_i g_i.
\]

The law is positively correlated on equal-sign wreaths and negatively
correlated on opposite-sign wreaths.  This independently confirms that the
construction is mixed-sign, as required by the earlier no-Rayleigh theorem.

Orbit symmetrization is also sound: every sample remains an exact factor,
the objective is invariant, and transitivity gives global wreath marginal
\(B/|\mathscr W_m|=1/D\).

## 5. Pair-sum floor and parity constants

Put

\[
e_a(z)=\frac12(z-a)(z-a-1).
\]

For fixed \(t=r+s\), convex exchange gives the minimizing split
\(\{\lfloor t/2\rfloor,\lceil t/2\rceil\}\).  Direct substitution yields

\[
b_a(t)
=e_a(\lfloor t/2\rfloor)+e_a(\lceil t/2\rceil)
=\left\lfloor\frac{(t-2a-1)^2}{4}\right\rfloor.
\]

If \(d=r-s\), then \(d\equiv t\pmod2\), and

\[
e_a\!\left(\frac{t+d}{2}\right)
+e_a\!\left(\frac{t-d}{2}\right)
=b_a(t)+\frac14
\left[d^2-\mathbf1_{\{t\ {\rm odd}\}}\right].
\tag{A2}
\]

Dividing (A2) by two gives the average energy of the antipodal pair.  This
verifies the coefficients

\[
\mathfrak B_H=\frac12\sum_{q,S}\omega_qb_{a_q}(t_{q,S})
\]

and

\[
\mathfrak C_H=\frac18\sum_{q,S}\omega_q
\left[d_{q,S}^2-\mathbf1_{\{t_{q,S}\ {\rm odd}\}}\right].
\]

Both are nonnegative.  The first vanishes exactly for

\[
t\in\{2a,2a+1,2a+2\}.
\]

The second vanishes exactly when \(d=0\) for even \(t\) and
\(|d|=1\) for odd \(t\).  Therefore simultaneous vanishing is equivalent to
both antipodal loads lying in \(\{a,a+1\}\) at every target.

For an arbitrary midpoint-preserving law, each target load is an integer
random variable of mean \(t/2\).  Minimum integer variance proves expected
energy at least \(b_a(t)/2\).  Thus the pair-sum floor is genuinely
covariance-invariant on the face, not merely an artifact of antipodal laws.

## 6. Orbit-average formula

Fix \(q\) and let \(X=\mu_q^F(S)\) for a uniformly random rank target.
For uniform \(\pi\), the averaged pair

\[
(S,\pi^{-1}S)
\]

is uniform on all ordered target pairs.  Hence the two empirical loads in
the orbit average behave as independent copies \(X,Y\).

Let

\[
\rho=\Pr\{X\text{ is odd}\}.
\]

Then

\[
\Pr\{X+Y\text{ is odd}\}=2\rho(1-\rho),
\]

and

\[
\mathbb E_\pi
\|\mu_q^F+\mu_q^{\pi F}-2\lambda_q\mathbf1\|^2
=2\|\mu_q^F-\lambda_q\mathbf1\|^2.
\]

Substitution in the global pair-floor formula gives exactly

\[
\mathbb E_\pi\mathfrak B_q(F,\pi F)
=\frac12R_q(F)
+\frac{N_q}{4}
\left[\rho_q(1-\rho_q)-\theta_q(1-\theta_q)\right].
\]

There is no asymptotic error and no independence assumption on wreaths.
Only transitivity on rank targets is used.

## 7. Quantitative parity gap

Let \(k_S=\mu_q(S)-a_q\), and let \(o_q\) count the odd \(k_S\).  The
pointwise inequality

\[
\left|\mathbf1_{\{k\ {\rm odd}\}}-k\right|
\le k(k-1)
\qquad(k\in\mathbb Z)
\]

gives

\[
|o_q-N_q\theta_q|\le2R_q.
\]

Writing \(e=R_q/N_q\) and \(f(t)=t(1-t)\), the largest possible increase of
\(f\) when its argument moves by at most \(2e\) is

\[
\begin{cases}
2e(1-2e),&e\le1/4,\\
1/4,&e\ge1/4.
\end{cases}
\]

Therefore

\[
\frac{L_q}{N_q}\ge
\begin{cases}
4e^2,&e\le1/4,\\
2e-1/4,&e\ge1/4.
\end{cases}
\]

This proves \(L_q\ge0\), with equality only at \(R_q=0\).

For each \(q\),

\[
R_q\le L_q+\frac12\sqrt{N_qL_q}.
\]

After multiplying by \(\omega_q\), summing, and applying Cauchy--Schwarz,

\[
\Phi_H
\le L_H+\frac12
\sqrt{\left(\sum_q\omega_qN_q\right)L_H}.
\]

On a fixed Gaussian window,

\[
\sum_{q\le H}\omega_qN_q=\Theta_A(WH).
\]

Thus \(L_H=o(W/H)\) implies \(\Phi_H=o(W)\).  This verifies the scale in
\(\mathrm{PGR}_A\).  An \(o(W)\) Graver slack alone is insufficient for
this deduction.

For the sharper target \(\Phi_H=O(W/H)\), this inequality by itself would
require \(L_H=O(W/H^3)\); the lead report correctly avoids claiming the
factor-scale result from an \(O(W/H)\) slack.

## 8. Descent and minimizer quantifiers

The exact identity

\[
\mathbb E_\pi\Theta_H(F,\pi F)
=\Phi_H(F)-\frac14L_H(F)
+\frac18\mathbb E_\pi(\Delta_H-O_H)
\]

implies strict descent if

\[
\mathbb E_\pi(\Delta_H-O_H)<2L_H(F).
\]

Indeed, some permutation has an improving antipodal pair, and one of its
two exact corners improves \(F\).

A factor stable under every such cube, including a global minimizer, must
therefore satisfy the reverse inequality.  No stationarity, mixing, or
interchange of adaptive choices is being assumed.

For a global minimizer, the all-plus signing gives
\(\Delta_H\le\ell_H\).  Strict inequality would lower the antipodal-pair
average, contradicting minimality, so

\[
\Delta_H(F,\pi F)=\ell_H(F,\pi F)
\]

for every \(\pi\).  Uniform orbit averaging gives

\[
\mathbb E_\pi\ell_H
=2\|Tx_F-u\|^2
=4\Phi_H(F)+2J_H.
\]

Therefore the stronger bound

\[
\mathbb E_\pi\Delta_H\le2J_H+\eta
\]

implies \(\Phi_H\le\eta/4\), with the stated exact constant.

At a global minimizer this stronger gate is algebraically equivalent to a
bound on the desired energy after rigidity is invoked.  It should not be
advertised as an independently easier theorem.  The parity-slack gate is the
more informative fifth-wave formulation.

## 9. Invariant obstructions

The report's obstruction statements are all exact.

1. **Connected overlay.** One component gives only the endpoint pair and
   \(\Delta_H=\ell_H\).
2. **Shadow orthogonality.** If the \(V_i\) are pairwise orthogonal, every
   signing has discrepancy \(\sum_i\|V_i\|^2\).
3. **Diagonal dominance.**
   \[
   \Delta_H\ge
   \max\left\{0,\sum_i\|V_i\|^2
   -2\sum_{i<j}|\langle V_i,V_j\rangle|\right\}.
   \]
4. **Restricted trade span.** Projection of \(Tx_F-u\) onto the orthogonal
   complement of the allowed trade-shadow span is invariant throughout the
   reachability class.
5. **Whole orbit.** Coordinate relabeling preserves every \(R_q\)
   pointwise; orbit randomization converts the same norm into covariance and
   supplies no drift.

These are route-specific obstructions.  They do not lower-bound the minimum
over all exact factors.

## 10. Final scope audit

The exact implications are:

- A physical Graver face satisfying
  \[
  \mathfrak B_H=o(W),\qquad \Delta_H-O_H=o(W)
  \]
  contains a factor of weighted pair excess \(o(W)\).
- If \(\mathrm{PGR}_A\) holds at a global minimizer, fixed-window unlabelled
  MWB follows.
- Proving the statement for every fixed \(A\), followed by the already
  audited diagonalization, would give MWB.

The report does not prove:

- existence of a useful multi-component \(F/G\) overlay;
- small invariant pair-sum floor for any asymptotic physical class;
- a parity-restoring common signing;
- the unproved \(\mathrm{PGR}_A\) estimate;
- labelled common-owner synchronization;
- a literal OR word directly.

The claimed “nontrivial class” theorem is conditional on a physical Graver
cube having the displayed floor and signing properties.  No such asymptotic
wreath class is constructed.  This is a characterization and an exact
obstruction, not closure of the conjecture.

With these qualifications, the fifth-wave lead report passes the adversarial
audit.
