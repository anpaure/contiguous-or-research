# Audit of the finite `Q_6` symmetric-splice neighborhood

## Verdict

`Q6_SCD_SPLICE_NEIGHBORHOOD_NO_GO.md` passes audit within its explicitly
defined move class.

The search includes every cyclic rotation of an existing bounded rank
segment and every cyclic upper-tail rotation among equal-radius chains.
For all `24` seeds, there are exactly four nontrivial generators.  Every
one/two-generator state fails almost orthogonality; the stronger enumeration
of all `15` nonempty subsets also fails.  Ninety-six persistent private
intersection witnesses certify the failure.

The result is not a no-go theorem for arbitrary symmetric path-cover
replacement or for a coupled move that changes both decompositions
atomically.

## 1. Validity of the splice operations

For a bounded segment rotation, chain `i` keeps its own vertices through
rank `ell`, takes ranks `ell+1,...,h` from the next chain on the directed
cycle, and resumes its own tail at rank `h+1`.

The two tested cross-cover relations are exactly the two new boundary
edges.  Internal segment edges are inherited from one old chain.  Original
bottom and top vertices remain in place, so each new chain has the same
symmetric rank interval.  Rotating segments is a permutation of the old
vertices, hence the chains remain a partition.

For an upper-tail rotation, all participating chains have the same bottom
and top ranks.  The one cross-cover relation supplies the only new edge, and
permuting equal-rank tails preserves symmetry and the partition.

The verifier calls `validate_scd` on every generated state, independently
checking saturation, endpoint-rank symmetry, and exact coverage of all 64
cube masks.

## 2. Completeness in the stated class

At fixed bounded-segment boundaries, define an arc `i->j` precisely when
chain `i` can take chain `j`'s segment: the left and right cross-cover
conditions both hold.  Any legal segment permutation decomposes into simple
directed cycles of this graph.  The cycle DFS fixes the least chain index,
so it enumerates each simple cycle once up to cyclic rotation and retains
both orientations.

The tail case uses the analogous one-boundary digraph inside each
equal-radius group.  Two-chain swaps are the directed cycles of length two
and are also independently generated by the direct two-chain routine; exact
SCD deduplication removes duplicates.

Thus radius one is exhaustive for one cyclic component in this class, and
radius two is exhaustive for a composition of two components, even if the
intermediate pair is not almost orthogonal.

## 4. Audit of persistent witnesses

For each constructed pair, the verifier enumerates every cross-pair of one
`D`-chain and one `E`-chain.  Whenever their intersection contains more than
one nonextreme mask, it records every two-mask subset of that illegal
intersection.

Fix one base generator.  Exactly eight of the sixteen Boolean states contain
it.  The verifier intersects the eight violation catalogs and requires a
nonempty result.  This gives a private witness that survives every subset of
the other three generators.

The check succeeds for four generators in each of 24 seeds.  For seed zero
the witnesses are

```text
D cut 2: {45,345}
D cut 3: {456,3456}
E cut 2: {12,123}
E cut 3: {126,1236}
```

These agree with the explicit chains in Section 3 of the main note.  Any
nonempty state contains at least one generator, hence retains that
generator's private illegal intersection.  This proves the entire Boolean
closure is nonorthogonal.

## 5. Guardrails

The enumeration does not include:

* replacing a path packet by a newly solved path cover unrelated to old
  rank segments;
* an atomic coupled operation whose validity depends simultaneously on
  changes in `D` and `E`;
* changing chain endpoints through a larger balanced re-pairing not
  decomposable into the stated rotations.

Accordingly, the main note correctly recommends a coupled or
non-rotational move rather than claiming global impossibility.
