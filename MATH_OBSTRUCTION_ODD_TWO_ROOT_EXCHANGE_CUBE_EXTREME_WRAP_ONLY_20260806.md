# Odd current: the two canonical root bases have only extreme wrap edges

**Date:** 2026-08-06  
**Method:** an explicit oppositely ordered scan zipper and a two-bit quiet-code
inequality; no computation  
**Status:** unconditional no-go theorem on every labelled odd
capacity-two sector, hence in particular on every ordinary sector with
trivial background stabilizer.  The Boolean exchange cube generated by
the two canonical root phases is far too small: at nonextreme compressed
mass, its union contains no physical wrap edge at all.

## 1. The two root phases

Put

\[
                         n=2m+1
\tag{1.1}
\]

and cut the coordinate cycle between \(2m\) and zero.  On an ordered pair
use

\[
 01-10,\qquad02-11,\qquad12-21,
\tag{1.2}
\]

with quiet states

\[
 q(0)=00,\qquad q(1)=20,\qquad q(2)=22.
\tag{1.3}
\]

Let \(M^-\) pair

\[
 (0,1),(2,3),\ldots,(2m-2,2m-1)
\tag{1.4}
\]

and leave coordinate \(2m\) unpaired.  Let \(M^+\) pair

\[
 (1,2),(3,4),\ldots,(2m-1,2m)
\tag{1.5}
\]

and leave coordinate zero unpaired.  Inspect the \(M^-\)-pairs from left
to right in (1.4), and inspect the \(M^+\)-pairs from right to left in
(1.5).

At odd total mass, the unmatched sets are

\[
\begin{aligned}
 Q^-&=\{x(u)=q(u_1)q(u_2)\cdots q(u_m)1\},\\
 Q^+&=\{y(v)=1q(v_1)q(v_2)\cdots q(v_m)\},
\end{aligned}
\tag{1.6}
\]

where the compressed mass \(\sum_j u_j=\sum_j v_j\) is fixed.

## 2. The transport pairing is literal rotation

### Lemma 2.1 (quiet-block zipper)

In the alternating union \(M^-\cup M^+\), the path starting at \(x(u)\)
ends at

\[
                         \rho x(u)=1q(u_1)\cdots q(u_m).
\tag{2.1}
\]

Thus the strong exchange bijection between the two surplus bases is

\[
                         \phi(x(u))=\rho x(u).
\tag{2.2}
\]

#### Proof

For one quiet block at the current interface, the first available
\(M^+\)-edge in the right-to-left order followed by the first available
\(M^-\)-edge in the left-to-right order gives, respectively,

\[
\begin{array}{rcl}
001&\longrightarrow&010\longrightarrow100,\\
201&\longrightarrow&210\longrightarrow120,\\
221&\longrightarrow&212\longrightarrow122.
\end{array}
\tag{2.3}
\]

In every row, the singleton one moves from the right of \(q(a)\) to its
left and the same quiet block remains behind it.

Starting from (1.6), the rightmost shifted pair is necessarily nonquiet,
so (2.3) applies to \(q(u_m)\).  Inductively, suppose the suffix blocks
already lie in the shifted \(M^+\)-alignment, with the singleton
immediately to their left.  The right-to-left \(M^+\)-scan sees that
entire suffix as quiet and acts at the interface with the preceding
block.  After that edge, the left-to-right \(M^-\)-scan sees the untouched
prefix as quiet and acts on that preceding block before it can enter the
shifted suffix.  The two edges are exactly one row of (2.3).  Thus the
singleton moves one block left and the shifted quiet suffix grows by one.
After \(m\) repetitions it reaches coordinate zero, leaving the blocks in
their original order.  The endpoint is (2.1). \(\square\)

This proof uses only nearest-neighbour transfers.  It is not the
complete-transfer Gray path.

## 3. Exact classification of physical wrap edges

Write

\[
 q(a)=\bigl(2A(a),2B(a)\bigr),
\qquad
 A(a)={\bf1}_{a\ge1},\quad B(a)={\bf1}_{a=2}.
\tag{3.1}
\]

Thus

\[
                         0\le B(a)\le A(a)\le1.
\tag{3.2}
\]

### Theorem 3.1 (extreme-wrap rigidity)

There is a physical boundary-transfer edge between

\[
 x(u)=q(u_1)\cdots q(u_m)1\in Q^-
\]

and

\[
 y(v)=1q(v_1)\cdots q(v_m)\in Q^+
\]

if and only if either

\[
 u=v=(0,\ldots,0)
\qquad\text{or}\qquad
 u=v=(2,\ldots,2).
\tag{3.3}
\]

#### Proof

A boundary transfer changes only coordinates zero and \(2m\), so all
interior coordinates of \(x(u)\) and \(y(v)\) agree.  Reading alternating
interior positions gives

\[
 B(u_j)=A(v_j),\qquad
 B(v_j)=A(u_{j+1})
\tag{3.4}
\]

for \(1\le j<m\), together with

\[
 B(u_m)=A(v_m).
\tag{3.5}
\]

At the two boundary coordinates, the pairs are

\[
 \bigl(2A(u_1),1\bigr)
 \quad\text{and}\quad
 \bigl(1,2B(v_m)\bigr).
\]

They differ by one legal unit transfer exactly when

\[
                         B(v_m)=A(u_1).
\tag{3.6}
\]

Equations (3.4)--(3.6), with indices read cyclically, give

\[
 A(u_j)\ge B(u_j)=A(v_j)\ge B(v_j)=A(u_{j+1})
\tag{3.7}
\]

for every \(j\in\mathbb Z_m\).  Following (3.7) around the cycle forces
equality everywhere.  Hence

\[
 A(u_j)=B(u_j)\quad\text{for every }j,
\]

so no \(u_j\) equals one, and all \(A(u_j)\) have the same value.  The
word \(u\) is therefore all zero or all two.  Equations (3.4) then force
the same conclusion for \(v\).

Conversely, the two extreme words give the literal boundary edges

\[
 00\cdots001\longleftrightarrow100\cdots00
\]

and

\[
 22\cdots221\longleftrightarrow122\cdots22.
\]

This proves (3.3). \(\square\)

### Corollary 3.2 (central two-base no-go)

If the compressed mass \(s\) satisfies

\[
                         0<s<2m,
\tag{3.8}
\]

then

\[
                         W[Q^-\cup Q^+]=\varnothing.
\tag{3.9}
\]

In particular (3.9) holds at the central compressed mass \(s=m\).
Therefore no surplus basis in the Boolean exchange cube generated by
\(M^-,M^+\) can be paired by physical boundary edges, even before hub
colours are imposed.

#### Proof

The only boundary edges from Theorem 3.1 have compressed masses zero and
\(2m\). \(\square\)

The obstruction is not repaired by adding the other canonical root scans.
For \(r\in\mathbb Z_n\), let \(Q_r\) be the quiet residue which has
singleton value one at \(r\) and quiet pairs

\[
 (r+1,r+2),(r+3,r+4),\ldots
\tag{3.10}
\]

cyclically.

### Theorem 3.3 (all-canonical-root rigidity)

If one physical transfer across the fixed boundary \(\{2m,0\}\) joins a
state of \(Q_r\) to a state of \(Q_s\), then

\[
                         \{r,s\}=\{0,2m\}.
\tag{3.11}
\]

The edge is consequently one of the two extreme edges in Theorem 3.1.
Thus, at nonextreme mass,

\[
 W\left[\bigcup_{r\in\mathbb Z_n}Q_r\right]
 =\varnothing.
\tag{3.12}
\]

#### Proof

Every nonroot coordinate of a state in \(Q_r\) has value zero or two;
the root \(r\) is its unique coordinate of value one.  A boundary transfer
changes only coordinates zero and \(2m\).

If \(r\notin\{0,2m\}\), the value one at \(r\) is unchanged by the
transfer.  The endpoint in \(Q_s\) must therefore have the same root
\(s=r\).  But then both boundary coordinates are nonroots at both ends,
so they must have values zero or two at both ends.  A unit transfer changes
each of them to one, a contradiction.

If \(r\) is a boundary coordinate and \(s=r\), the transfer changes the
root value one, again impossible.  Hence distinct endpoints require the
two different boundary roots, proving (3.11).  After orienting them as
\(Q^-,Q^+\), Theorem 3.1 gives the two extreme edges and no others.
\(\square\)

## 4. Consequence for the odd blossom programme

The stabilizer-root surplus theorem remains useful: the whole cut-open
Hall problem is one cotransversal matroid, and a pairable surplus basis
would close the uncoloured odd current.  The present theorem locates the
failure of its smallest explicit face:

\[
\boxed{
\text{two canonical root bases + arbitrary component flips do not expose
even one physical wrap edge at nonextreme mass}.}
\tag{4.1}
\]

Thus a positive proof must use at least one of:

1. a genuinely noncanonical minority-saturating matching whose surplus
   basis is not contained in the union of **any** canonical quiet root
   bases;
2. general cotransversal basis exchange beyond the two-base cube;
3. a cross-fibre odd ear which changes the quiet code before returning to
   the boundary; or
4. a fan/receiver augmentation which realizes the current outside the
   literal two-base union.

This no-go occurs before rotation quotienting and before hub colouring.
Passing to an aperiodic ordinary background (trivial stabilizer) therefore
does not remove it.  Periodic quotients may coalesce additional states,
but cannot validate the proposed universal two-root construction because
that construction already fails on every trivial-stabilizer sector.

## 5. Scope

Proved:

1. an explicit nearest-neighbour zipper for the adjacent-root transport;
2. exact identification of its exchange bijection with rotation;
3. exact classification of every boundary edge in the union of the two
   quiet surplus bases; and
4. emptiness of the boundary graph on the union of all canonical quiet
   root bases at every nonextreme compressed mass.

Not proved:

1. nonexistence of another surplus basis with a boundary matching;
2. factor-criticality or non-factor-criticality of the full physical
   sector;
3. any hub-rainbow or receiver-extension theorem; or
4. the all-\(k\) upper bound.
