## 0. Verdict

The positive `m=4` attachment

\[
 [18]-g_{(19,84)}-[20]-g_{(21,26)}-[24]
\tag{0.1}
\]

does identify the correct tractable face, but it does not extend through the

That obstruction is now repaired exactly at `m=5`.  From the transparent

carry the exact augmented deficiencies

\[
 (3,3,3)\to(2,2,2)\to(1,1,1)\to(0,0,0).
\tag{0.2a}
\]

The final forced-port decoration works on the entire two-standard-glue cube.
After removing those two glues, the repaired factor has components of orders
`120,132`; either standard glue alone joins them.  Relative to one common
prepared gap core, the two changed atom pairs are disjoint and give exactly

\[
 [82]-g-[84]-g-[88],\qquad
 [50]-g-[52]-g-[56].
\tag{0.2b}
\]

Thus the prepared private/aligned face is not merely conditional at the
first failed dimension: it has an explicit repaired `m=5` member.  What
remains open is a uniform repair-packet theorem and the deeper/literal
interfaces, not the `m=5` central palette--gap--component state.

The strongest dimension-uniform theorem that survives is the following.
First apply a possibly nonstandard fixed repair prefix and obtain a factor
`F*` carrying **one common guarded decoration** `D`.  Prepare every later
collar on one choice-independent, occurrence-labelled gap core.  If the
collars have globally private tree attachments, or one aligned effective
gap edge per component edge, and each collar exposes one source in one fixed
unpaired-sink alternating network, then the remaining simultaneous
component/gap/linkage choice is ordinary graphic--gammoid intersection.  For
`q` factor components it has a Hamiltonizing solution exactly when

\[
 \boxed{
 r_C(X)+r_L(T\setminus X)\ge q-1
 \quad\text{for every }X\subseteq T .}
\tag{0.2}
\]

If all linkage routes are globally private as well, every component
spanning tree works.

The qualifiers are load-bearing.  The decoration is one global `D`, not one
decoration per edge.  Privacy is one global allocation on the prepared core,
not one private rooting per edge.  Each label contributes one linkage atom;
multiple sources per label restore a grouped problem.  Named source--sink
pairings are not tested by the gammoid rank.  The general three-row
one-atom product is matroid `3`-parity and contains `3DM`; arbitrary grouped
bundles need not form a matroid at all.

This theorem closes the selection problem **after** a suitable repaired
catalogue is supplied.  It neither constructs that catalogue for every `m`
nor closes the final physical socket, voltage, deeper-shadow, residence or
literal compiler gates.

There are therefore two logically different move classes.  A hexagon
transparent for one fixed decoration preserves its selected local turn
multisets on both shores and its retained-fragment boundary alternation; it
is a zero-debt private-gluing move.  A palette-repair `C10` or ordinary
hexagon may change the decoration and carry matching debt, and enters this
theorem only after one packet-end common-core linkage has paid that debt.
The authoritative `ML(7)` census makes the distinction literal: among `31`
alternating hexagons, `16` give Hamilton outputs, `10` outputs are decorable,
but only `6` share a fixed decoration with the source.  The counterexample's
repairing hexagon belongs to the ordinary repair class, not automatically to
the fixed-decoration transparent class.  Thus neither an arbitrary frozen
SDR nor an arbitrary published gluing tree supplies the entrance state.

## 1. The exact entrance state

Let `F*` be a middle-levels two-factor.  A guarded decoration

\[
 D=(D_-,D_+)
\tag{1.1}
\]

means:

1. the two selected turn-colour maps are bijections onto their full lower
   and upper alphabets;
2. selected shore types alternate on every component of `F*`;
3. every component contains a literal trace breaker protected from all
   collars to be used later.

For a collar `t`, write `F_t^0,F_t^1` for its old/new local choices and
`B_t^0,B_t^1` for their occurrence-labelled gap atoms.  Let
`C_*` be the declared future colour interface.

### Definition 1.1 (prepared common `D`-core)

A collar family `T` is prepared under `D` if there is one fixed forest
`Gamma^o` and fixed old/new boundaried atoms such that, for every permitted
choice vector `sigma`,

\[
 \Gamma(F_\sigma,D_-)
   =\Gamma^\circ\cup\bigcup_{t\in T}B_t^{\sigma_t},
\tag{1.2}
\]

where distinct atoms meet only on their declared part of `C_*`.  The exact
turn-owner banks, residual matching atoms, fragment endpoint types and trace
guards factor over the same choice vector.  In particular, every permitted
choice is simultaneously palette- and boundary-transparent for the **same**
`D`.

Equivalently, if `Decor(F*)` is the global decoration space and `T_t` is the
transparent face of collar `t`, the palette quantifier is

\[
 D\in \operatorname{Decor}(F^*)\cap\bigcap_{t\in T}{\cal T}_t,
\tag{1.3}
\]

not merely

\[
 \operatorname{Decor}(F^*)\cap{cal T}_t\ne\varnothing
 \quad(t\in T).
\tag{1.4}
\]

All prospective old bridge gaps must be deleted at the outset, or retained
as the explicit old alternatives in (1.2).  Physical edge-disjointness of
the collars does not imply (1.2).  For example, if the current core contains
the path `x-h-y`, inserting a new gap `g` adjacent to `x,y` creates the
four-cycle `x-h-y-g-x`; it becomes safe only after another choice deletes
`h`.  The local status of the insertion is therefore order-dependent until
the old socket is prepared.

## 2. A dimension-uniform private-root theorem

The next lemma is the strongest fully automatic private-tree statement.

### Theorem 2.1 (prepared globally root-private bank)

Assume Definition 1.1 and the following hypotheses.

1. The residual colours have a disjoint decomposition
   \[
     R=R^\circ\mathbin{\dot\cup}\bigdotcup_{t\in T}R_t,
   \tag{2.1}
   \]
   the core matching covers `R^o`, and each selected atom `B_t^epsilon`
   carries the prescribed bijection from its bridge gaps to `R_t`.
2. There are pairwise disjoint private vertex sets `P_t`.  Every connected
   component of either atom `B_t^0` or `B_t^1` is a tree contained in `P_t`
   together with at most one vertex of the nonprivate core.  Atoms belonging
   to distinct labels meet only at nonprivate core vertices.
3. Every active collar is merge-only.  Its component effect is one edge
   `c_t`, and the chosen all-new collar edges form a spanning tree on the
   `q` initial components.
4. Every initial component's protected trace breaker is disjoint from all
   collars.

Then every old/new choice has a leaf-peelable gap graph with its prescribed
matching, and `D` remains a componentwise guarded decoration.  Every rooted
merge prefix retains a protected breaker on every current component.  The
all-new state has one factor component.

#### Proof

Start with the forest `Gamma^o`.  Attach the components of the selected
atoms one at a time.  Each new component is a tree and meets the graph built
so far in at most one vertex.  A first cycle would therefore have to enter
and leave one new private tree through two old vertices, contrary to the
hypothesis.  Thus every choice in (1.2) is a forest.

The disjoint bijections in (2.1) extend the core matching to every new gap
exactly once.  In a forest this perfect matching is unique and is recovered
by leaf peeling.  Simultaneous `D`-transparency in Definition 1.1 preserves
both exact palettes and boundary alternation.  A merge unions two guarded
components and destroys no protected breaker, so every output component
retains one.  Finally, a spanning tree of merge-only component edges reduces
`q` components to one.  \(\square\)

The `m=4` path (0.1) is one literal atom of this form: take `[24]` as its
nonprivate root block and the preceding two colour blocks and two gap
vertices as private.  Since there is only one glue, that fixture tests no
cross-collar compatibility.

### Proposition 2.2 (exact global-private signature test)

Fix `D`, a prepared core and a rooted component tree.  For every tree edge
`e`, let `L_e(D)` be its finite list of complete signatures: physical
toggle, residual matching, endpoint/guard transfer, attachment forest
`B_e`, declared root block `r_e`, and proposed private blocks

\[
 P_e=V(B_e)\setminus\{r_e\}.
\tag{2.2}
\]

A root-private bank exists exactly when one can choose
`ell_e in L_e(D)` so that

\[
 P_e\cap V(B_f)=\varnothing\quad(e\ne f)
\tag{2.3}
\]

and, in the rooted order,

\[
 B_e\cap\left(\Gamma^\circ\cup
              \bigcup_{f\prec e}B_f\right)
 \subseteq\{r_e\}.
\tag{2.4}
\]

#### Proof

Conditions (2.3)--(2.4) are precisely global private allocation and
one-root attachment.  They imply Theorem 2.1 by leaf-ear induction.
Conversely, within the declared root-private signature class, any violation
of (2.3) reuses a private block and any violation of (2.4) gives a bundle two
old attachment blocks, so the selected signatures are not a member of that
class.  \(\square\)

This is a finite exact CSP.  If its owner/port/interface scopes have a
running-intersection decomposition, it is solved by the usual tree messages

\[
 M_{v\to p}=\pi_{B_v\cap B_p}
 \left(R_v\Join\bigJoin_{u\in\operatorname{child}(v)}M_{u\to v}\right).
\tag{2.5}
\]

The plane-tree gluing tree alone does **not** bound this width: a colour owner
shared by distant collars must occur in every intervening bag.

## 3. The maximal graphic--gammoid face

The private theorem is stronger than necessary.  A weaker aligned face has
an exact min--max criterion.

Let `K_C` be the component graph on `q` vertices and let every label `t`
have one nonloop component edge `c_t`.  Let `M_C` be the pullback graphic
matroid on `T`.  After suppressing globally private leaves and series paths,
assume either:

* **private gap:** every subset of labels is gap-safe; or
* **aligned gap:** every label has one effective gap edge `g_t`, and there is
  an injective map `iota` of component vertices to gap-core blocks with
  \[
     c_t=uv\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
     g_t=\iota(u)\iota(v).
  \tag{3.1}
  \]

Then component independence implies gap independence.

Fix one directed vertex-capacitated alternating network `N`, one fixed
**unpaired** sink bank `Z`, and one distinct source `s_t` per label.  If a
fixed mandatory source set `B_0` is present, assume it is linkable and define

\[
 M_L=\left\{S\subseteq T:
 B_0\cup\{s_t:t\in S\}
 \text{ links vertex-disjointly to distinct sinks in }Z\right\}.
\tag{3.2}
\]

This is the restriction after contracting `B_0` of a gammoid on the label
ground.

### Theorem 3.1 (exact Edmonds criterion)

Under the common-`D`, prepared-core, one-component-edge, private/aligned-gap
and one-source hypotheses above, a jointly safe Hamiltonizing label set
exists if and only if

\[
 r_C(X)+r_L(T\setminus X)\ge q-1
 \quad\text{for every }X\subseteq T.
\tag{3.3}
\]

A set or a violated rank partition is found using a graphic rank oracle and
vertex-capacitated max flow.

#### Proof

All palette, boundary, residual and guard rows were frozen into the common
prepared catalogue.  The private/aligned hypothesis makes the gap row
redundant on every `M_C`-independent set.  Thus the remaining selectable
family is `I(M_C) intersection I(M_L)`.  Edmonds' matroid-intersection theorem gives

\[
 \max\{|S|:S\in I(M_C)\cap I(M_L)\}
 =\min_{X\subseteq T}\bigl(r_C(X)+r_L(T\setminus X)\bigr).
\tag{3.4}
\]

The component rank is at most `q-1`.  An independent set of size `q-1` is a
spanning tree on the `q` component vertices, hence is Hamiltonizing under the
merge-only hypothesis.  Equations (3.3)--(3.4) are therefore equivalent to
existence.  \(\square\)

If every label owns a fixed path to a distinct private sink and all these
paths are mutually vertex-disjoint, `M_L` is free and any component spanning
tree works.  Otherwise (3.3) is the exact checkable sufficient/existence
criterion on this face.

The theorem does not cover named source--sink pairings.  It also fails to
atomize a collar which contributes two or more inseparable exposed sources;
that pullback is grouped gammoid independence and need not be a matroid.
The exact oriented linkage relation of item 2169 must then be retained.

## 4. Why arbitrary grouped transparent banks are the wrong target

Contract the retained gap forest and suppose each bundle is a tree with
private interior.  Make a bipartite multigraph `Q`: one side consists of
bundle nodes, the other of contracted core blocks, and one incidence edge is
inserted for each attachment occurrence.

### Lemma 4.1 (bundle--core incidence criterion)

The union of a selected family of private-interior tree bundles is a forest
if and only if its bundle--core incidence multigraph `Q` is a forest.

#### Proof

Contract every retained-core tree and every maximal private path inside a
bundle.  Contraction preserves the cycle space and gives exactly `Q`, with
parallel incidences retained as a two-cycle.  Expanding the unique paths
reverses the contraction.  \(\square\)

Individual privacy is not enough.  On core blocks `u,v`, the two bundles

\[
 u-g_1-v,qquad u-g_2-v
\tag{4.1}
\]

are individually trees, but their union is the four-cycle
`u-g_1-v-g_2-u`.  Cyclic families of such paths show that every proper
subfamily may be safe while the full family is not; there is no fixed Helly
number.

After private suppression, the exact taxonomy is:

* at most one public attachment block: the gap row is free;
* two public blocks: one ordinary effective graphic edge;
* three public blocks: two inseparable effective edges, hence graphic
  matroid parity;
* larger bundles: a grouped graphic system, not generally a matroid.

Separately, when every label has one atom in each of the component graphic,
gap graphic and linkage rows, three copied grounds give matroid `3`-parity.
That one-atom three-row product already contains three-dimensional matching.
This must not be conflated with the arbitrary grouped case, which can be
worse and need not have a matroid representation.

Thus Theorems 2.1 and 3.1 identify the structurally justified recursive
target: prepare and align/privatize the collars, rather than solve a generic
grouped transparent family.

## 8. Exact remaining all-`m` theorem

The standard private-triple induction is false.  The corrected recursive
entrance statement needed at every semilength `m` is:

1. construct a fixed prefix `P_m` and
   \(F_m^*=F_m\mathbin\triangle P_m\);
2. construct one guarded alternating decoration `D_m` of `F_m^*`;
3. construct a later collar catalogue simultaneously compatible with
   `D_m` on one prepared common core;
4. prove that its gap row is globally private or aligned and that its
   one-source linkage ranks satisfy (3.3); and
5. separately discharge the occurrence-labelled physical socket/voltage,
   residence, deeper-shadow and common compiler interfaces.

Steps 1--4 are sufficient for the central guarded Hamiltonizing gluing
choice by Theorems 2.1 or 3.1.  They are not known for every `m`.  Theorem
7.1 proves all four central steps at the first failed dimension; it does not
give a uniform construction.

The smallest genuinely new existence lemma is therefore:

> For every `m`, there is a repaired factor `F_m^*` with one guarded
> decoration and a common prepared private/aligned collar catalogue whose
> component--gammoid rank partitions all satisfy (3.3).

This is checkable and avoids the generic grouped/parity face.  It is still
an unproved lemma, not an all-`k` theorem.
