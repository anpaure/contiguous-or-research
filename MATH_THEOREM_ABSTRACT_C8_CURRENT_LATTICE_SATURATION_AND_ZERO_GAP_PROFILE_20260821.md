# The abstract first-shadow `C_8` current lattice is saturated

**Date:** 2026-08-21  
**Status:** unconditional abstract-lattice and zero-gap-profile theorem;
physical reachability inside the canonical `C_8` factor orbit is not claimed

## 1. The lattice theorem

Let `n>=4` and `2<=k<=n-2`.  Write

\[
 V_{n,k}=\mathbb Z^{\binom{[n]}k}
\]

with basis vectors `[S]`, and let

\[
 I_{n,k}:V_{n,k}\longrightarrow\mathbb Z^n,
 \qquad I_{n,k}[S]={\bf1}_S                                    \tag{1.1}
\]

be the coordinate-incidence map.  For a `(k-2)`-set `K` and four
distinct points `a,b,c,d` outside `K`, put

\[
 q(K;a,b,c,d)
 =[Kac]+[Kbd]-[Kad]-[Kbc].                                  \tag{1.2}
\]

Let `Q_{n,k}` be the integer lattice generated by all currents (1.2).

### Theorem 1.1 (integral saturation)

\[
 \boxed{\qquad Q_{n,k}=\ker_{\mathbb Z} I_{n,k}.\qquad}       \tag{1.3}
\]

Consequently `Q_{n,k}` is saturated in `V_{n,k}` and

\[
 V_{n,k}/Q_{n,k}\cong\operatorname{im}I_{n,k}
 =\left\{z\in\mathbb Z^n:\ \sum_i z_i\equiv0\pmod k\right\}. \tag{1.4}
\]

In particular the quotient is torsion-free of rank `n`, while the current
lattice has rank

\[
                         \binom nk-n.                         \tag{1.5}
\]

Thus over characteristic zero the only linear invariants of all abstract
first-shadow `C_8` square currents are the `n` coordinate marginals.

#### Proof

Every generator (1.2) has zero coordinate incidence, so
`Q_{n,k} subseteq ker I_{n,k}`.  We prove the reverse inclusion by
induction on `n`; for `k=1` and `k=n-1` the incidence map has zero kernel,
which supplies the boundary cases used by the induction.

Fix `x in ker I_{n,k}` and split it according to whether a set contains
the last point `n`:

\[
 x=u+[n]v,
 \qquad
 u\in V_{n-1,k},\quad v\in V_{n-1,k-1}.                     \tag{1.6}
\]

The `n`-th coordinate of `I_{n,k}x=0` says that the sum of the
coefficients of `v` is zero.  The Johnson graph on the `(k-1)`-subsets of
`[n-1]` is connected.  Hence `v` is an integer sum of adjacent
differences

\[
              [K\cup\{b\}]-[K\cup\{a\}],\qquad |K|=k-2.     \tag{1.7}
\]

For each such difference choose

\[
 c\in[n-1]\setminus(K\cup\{a,b\});                          \tag{1.8}
\]

this is possible because `k<=n-2`.  The square relation

\[
 q(K;a,b,c,n)
 =[Kac]+[Kbn]-[Kan]-[Kbc]                                   \tag{1.9}
\]

has `n`-containing part exactly (1.7).  Subtracting the corresponding
integer combination of (1.9) from `x` therefore kills `v` completely.
The remaining vector `u'` excludes `n`, is still in the incidence kernel,
and hence lies in `ker I_{n-1,k}`.  If `k<=n-3`, the induction hypothesis
puts `u'` in `Q_{n-1,k} subseteq Q_{n,k}`.  If `k=n-2`, the map on the
`(n-2)`-subsets of `[n-1]` is the nonsingular matrix `J-I`, so `u'=0`.
This proves (1.3).

The first isomorphism theorem gives the first part of (1.4).  Every
incidence column has coordinate sum `k`.  Conversely, differences
`e_i-e_j` lie in the image, by subtracting two `k`-sets with a common
`(k-1)`-set, and one incidence column has sum `k`.  These vectors generate
every integer vector whose coordinate sum is divisible by `k`, proving
the second part of (1.4).  The remaining conclusions follow. `square`

## 2. Exact application to wreath first shadows

Assume `r>=3` and put

\[
 n=2r+1,\qquad k=r-1,\qquad m=\operatorname {Cat}_r,
\]

and write

\[
 A=nm=\binom{2r+1}r,
 \qquad N=\binom{2r+1}{r-1}.                                \tag{2.1}
\]

For every exact middle wreath factor `F`, its first-shadow multiplicity
vector `mu_F in V_{n,k}` satisfies

\[
 \sum_T\mu_F(T)=A,
 \qquad
 I_{n,k}\mu_F=km\,{\bf1}.                                  \tag{2.2}
\]

Indeed, one cyclic order supplies `n` cyclic `k`-windows and each point
lies in exactly `k` of them.

### Theorem 2.1 (a zero-gap integral point in the same fiber)

There is a simple family `D subseteq binom([n],k)` such that

\[
 |D|=A-N,
 \qquad
 \deg_D(i)=km-\binom{n-1}{k-1}\quad(i\in[n]).                \tag{2.3}
\]

Consequently

\[
 \mu^*(T)=1+1_D(T)                                          \tag{2.4}
\]

has exactly the same total and coordinate marginals as every `mu_F`, and

\[
 \Phi(\mu^*)=\sum_T\binom{\mu^*(T)}2=A-N.                   \tag{2.5}
\]

Thus the relaxed integral fiber has energy gap exactly zero.  Moreover,
for every exact factor `F`,

\[
 \boxed{\quad
 \mu^*-\mu_F\in Q_{n,k};
 \quad}                                                      \tag{2.6}
\]

that is, the difference is an integer combination of abstract first-shadow
`C_8` currents.

#### Proof

The required edge count and degree are

\[
 E:=A-N={2nm\over r+2},
 \qquad
 d:=km-\binom{n-1}{k-1}={2km\over r+2}={kE\over n}.          \tag{2.7}
\]

Both are integers by their middle expressions.  The general form of
Baranyai's partition theorem partitions the complete `k`-uniform
hypergraph into parts of prescribed sizes, each part having vertex
degrees differing by at most one.  Apply it with part sizes `E` and
`N-E`.  Since `kE/n=d` is an integer, the `E`-edge part is exactly
`d`-regular; call it `D`.  This proves (2.3).

Equations (2.4)--(2.5) are immediate: `N-E` targets have load one and
`E=A-N` targets have load two.  Equations (2.2)--(2.3) give
`I_{n,k}(mu^*-mu_F)=0`, so (2.6) follows from Theorem 1.1. `square`

For `r=2`, define `Q_{5,1}:={0}`.  Every singleton has load two in every
factor, so `mu^*=mu_F=2\,{\bf1}` and (2.3)--(2.6) hold directly.

## 3. Relation to the dimension-free physical switch

At first shadow, the dimension-free two-row `C_8` switch has current

\[
 -[Kbp]-[Kcq]+[Kcp]+[Kbq],                                  \tag{3.1}
\]

where `|K|=k-2` and `b,c,p,q` are distinct outside `K`.  After relabelling,
these are exactly the generators (1.2).  Conversely, every generator
(1.2) is realized by a genuine two-row **partial-factor** `C_8` toggle:
order the core inside the block `P`, use two of the four outside labels as
the endpoints of `P`, use the other two as the exchanged labels, and fill
the unused labels into the remaining slots.  This local realization does
not assert that the old row pair can be completed into an exact global
wreath factor.

Therefore Theorems 1.1 and 2.1 identify the exact abstract lattice of the
two-row partial-factor current type.  They prove all of the following:

1. no additional linear or congruence invariant obstructs perfect
   first-shadow balancing;
2. the abstract quotient dimension is only `2r+1`, much smaller than
   `A/r`;
3. an integral zero-gap target profile exists in the correct marginal
   fiber; and
4. every factor profile differs from such a target by an integral sum of
   the correct four-term currents.

They do **not** prove that this sum can be ordered so that every required
two-row switch is present in the evolving factor, nor that all intermediate
multiplicity vectors stay nonnegative.  The exact remaining gate is thus
an availability/conformal-reachability theorem for the canonical physical
`C_8` orbit, not a current-lattice quotient or fractional-rounding
obstruction.
