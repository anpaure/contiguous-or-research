# All-\(m\) suspension of the first nonlocal exact-factor circuit

## 1. Verdict

Let \(B_r\) denote cyclic \(r\)-interval incidence on cyclic orders of
\([2m+1]\).  The certified \(m=4\) exact-factor circuit \(w_4\) satisfies

\[
B_4w_4=B_3w_4=0,\qquad B_2w_4\ne0.
\tag{1.1}
\]

This report does not produce an infinite family of exact-factor circuits.
It does completely solve the natural constant-gap suspension algebra and
gives a sharp positive obstruction.

The main conclusions are:

1. There are exactly two unoriented constant-gap insertion types which
   suspend a depth-two-active signed relation from \(m\) to \(m+1\):
   the antipodal and near-antipodal types.  In directed notation their
   four distances are

   \[
   d\in\{m-1,m,m+1,m+2\}.
   \tag{1.2}
   \]

2. Every allowed distance has the same exact induced second-shadow map,

   \[
   B_{m-1}P_dw
   =(m-1)(\iota_x+\iota_y)B_{m-2}w.
   \tag{1.3}
   \]

   Consequently the \(m=4\) seed has a squarefree signed suspension to
   every dimension, with an explicit nonzero lower image.

3. Among nonnegative \(S_{2m+1}\)-equivariant, order-preserving insertion
   operators, the complete top-two-neutral cone is generated by the two
   dihedral orbit sums corresponding to (1.2).  Every nonzero operator in
   this cone repeats a new middle target already over one old wreath.
   Hence the cone contains no packing-preserving operator except zero.

4. Iterating the signed suspension cannot be repaired while retaining its
   literal lower effect.  After two steps, in dimension \(6\), that effect
   violates support feasibility by a sharp saturation argument.  In every
   dimension \(M\ge7\), one coefficient exceeds the universal depth-two
   packing capacity.

5. At the first step \(m=4\to5\), none of the four fixed directed
   constant-gap suspensions has a nonzero conformal, support-feasible
   top-two-neutral piece.  More strongly, allow each of the eight signed
   seed rows either no child or one child, with every active row choosing
   independently any distance in

   \[
   \{3,4,5,6\}
   \]

   Then the only selection preserving \(B_5\) and \(B_4\) is the empty
   one.  This is proved symbolically from the exact local signature words
   and the ownership-transfer table.

Thus the obstruction is not representation branching or signed incidence.
It is positivity in one exact-factor fibre.  A successful recursion must
break equivariance and use a genuinely nonuniform section—possibly sparse,
multi-child, auxiliary-row, or ownership-rerouting—while renormalizing the
lower signal and proving one common exact-factor completion.

The exact smallest surviving direct condition is stated in Section 10.
It remains **UNPROVED**.

No web search, finite search, solver, or computational experiment is used.

## 2. The seed and the four levels of positivity

Let \(\Omega_m\) be the set of unoriented cyclic orders on
\([2m+1]\), modulo rotation and reversal.  For \(C\in\Omega_m\),

\[
B_re_C=\sum_{I\in\mathcal W_r(C)}e_I,
\]

where \(\mathcal W_r(C)\) is the family of cyclic \(r\)-intervals of
\(C\).

An exact middle factor is a vector

\[
x\in\{0,1\}^{\Omega_m},
\qquad
B_mx=\mathbf1.
\tag{2.1}
\]

A reduced signed vector

\[
w=\mathbf1_P-\mathbf1_N
\tag{2.2}
\]

is support feasible when \(P,N\) are middle-wreath packings and

\[
B_m\mathbf1_P=B_m\mathbf1_N.
\tag{2.3}
\]

It is exact-factor realizable when one common residual packing \(H\)
makes both

\[
H\mathbin{\dot\cup}N,\qquad
H\mathbin{\dot\cup}P
\tag{2.4}
\]

exact factors.  These distinctions are essential:

* an integral signed kernel vector need not be support feasible;
* a support-feasible partial trade need not admit the completion (2.4);
* an exact-factor move need not be a connected circuit; and
* connectedness is the ordinary middle-Graver condition, whereas a
  disconnected packet can still be primitive after lower rows are stacked.

### The certified \(m=4\) circuit

In ordinary cyclic-interval order, take the four negative rows

\[
\begin{aligned}
A&=(1,6,4,3,2,8,7,5,9),\\
B&=(1,8,7,5,3,9,6,4,2),\\
C&=(1,3,8,7,4,5,9,6,2),\\
D&=(1,3,4,8,6,7,9,5,2).
\end{aligned}
\tag{2.5}
\]

Let \(\tau=(1\,2)\).  The positive side consists of the four
unoriented rows \(\tau A,\tau B,\tau C,\tau D\).  The two signs are
middle packings, they have a common ten-row completion to exact
fourteen-row factors, and their middle ownership overlay is connected.

Writing \(w_4=\mathbf1_P-\mathbf1_N\), the exact lower effect is

\[
\begin{aligned}
B_2w_4={}&
(e_{23}+e_{14}-e_{13}-e_{24})\\
&+(e_{15}+e_{29}-e_{25}-e_{19}).
\end{aligned}
\tag{2.6}
\]

Therefore (1.1) holds, and connectedness gives

\[
w_4\in\operatorname{Gr}(B_4)
\cap\operatorname{Gr}\binom{B_4}{B_3}.
\tag{2.7}
\]

The point margins of (2.6) vanish.  Hence its nonzero signal is already
in the degree-two Johnson isotype.  There is no degree-one obstruction
to suspension.

## 3. The exact gap-sector identity

Fix \(n=2m+1\), add two new labels \(x,y\), and orient an old cyclic
order

\[
C=(c_0,\ldots,c_{n-1}).
\]

Number its gaps by \(\mathbb Z_n\).  For
\(1\le d\le n-1\), let \(E_{a,d}C\) be obtained by inserting \(x\)
in gap \(a\) and \(y\) in gap \(a+d\).  Define the translate-sum
operator

\[
P_de_C=\sum_{a\in\mathbb Z_n}e_{E_{a,d}C}.
\tag{3.1}
\]

Put

\[
h_d(\ell)=(\ell-d)_++(\ell-(n-d))_+.
\tag{3.2}
\]

This is the number of directed gap pairs \(a,a+d\) contained in an
interval of \(\ell\) consecutive old gaps.

### Lemma 3.1 — exact sector formula

Let \(T\) be a new \(r\)-set, let

\[
t=|T\cap\{x,y\}|,
\qquad
U=T\setminus\{x,y\}.
\]

For \(r\ge3\),

\[
(B_rP_de_C)_T=
\begin{cases}
h_d(n-r+1)\,
\mathbf1_{\{U\text{ is an old }r\text{-interval}\}},
&t=0,\\[1mm]
\bigl(r-h_d(r-1)\bigr)\,
\mathbf1_{\{U\text{ is an old }(r-1)\text{-interval}\}},
&t=1,\\[1mm]
h_d(r-1)\,
\mathbf1_{\{U\text{ is an old }(r-2)\text{-interval}\}},
&t=2.
\end{cases}
\tag{3.3}
\]

#### Proof

If \(t=0\), both insertion gaps must avoid the \(r-1\) internal gaps
of \(U\).  The permitted gap set is one interval of length
\(n-r+1\), giving the first line.

If \(t=2\), both insertion gaps must lie in the \(r-1\) consecutive
internal-or-boundary gaps around the old \((r-2)\)-interval \(U\).
This gives the third line.

If \(t=1\), there are \(r\) possible placements of the selected new
label around the old \((r-1)\)-interval.  Exactly \(h_d(r-1)\) of
them force the other new label into the same \(r\)-window.  Removing
those placements gives the middle line.  Every count is targetwise, so
(3.3) follows. \(\square\)

### Theorem 3.2 — complete constant-gap classification

Suppose

\[
B_mz=B_{m-1}z=0,
\qquad
B_{m-2}z\ne0.
\tag{3.4}
\]

Then

\[
B_{m+1}P_dz=0
\tag{3.5}
\]

for every \(d\), while

\[
\boxed{
B_mP_dz
=h_d(m-1)\,\iota_{xy}B_{m-2}z}
\tag{3.6}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
B_{m-1}P_dz={}&
\bigl(m-1-h_d(m-2)\bigr)
(\iota_x+\iota_y)B_{m-2}z\\
&+h_d(m-2)\,\iota_{xy}B_{m-3}z.
\end{aligned}}
\tag{3.7}
\]

Consequently

\[
\boxed{
B_{m+1}P_dz=B_mP_dz=0
\iff
d\in\{m-1,m,m+1,m+2\}.}
\tag{3.8}
\]

For all four distances in (3.8),

\[
\boxed{
B_{m-1}P_dz
=(m-1)(\iota_x+\iota_y)B_{m-2}z\ne0.}
\tag{3.9}
\]

#### Proof

At new rank \(m+1\), the zero-, one-, and two-new-label sectors in
(3.3) use old rows \(B_{m+1}z,B_mz,B_{m-1}z\).  Complementation
identifies \(B_{m+1}z\) with \(B_mz\), so all three vanish.

At new rank \(m\), the first two sectors use \(B_mz,B_{m-1}z\), while
the two-new sector is exactly (3.6).

At new rank \(m-1\), the sectors give (3.7).

Because the two-new sector in (3.6) is disjoint from the other sectors
and \(B_{m-2}z\ne0\), top-two neutrality is equivalent to

\[
h_d(m-1)=0.
\]

By (3.2), this means

\[
d\ge m-1,\qquad n-d\ge m-1,
\]

which is exactly (3.8).  For those four values,
\(h_d(m-2)=0\), and (3.7) becomes (3.9).
\(\square\)

The directed distances \(m,m+1\) are the two orientations of the
antipodal type.  The directed distances \(m-1,m+2\) are the two
orientations of the near-antipodal type.  Inserting both new labels in
one old gap is not a missing third type: its two-new contribution to
\(B_m\) is a positive multiple, in fact \(m-1\), of
\(\iota_{xy}B_{m-2}z\), so it cannot preserve the second displayed
kernel in (3.4).

### Minimal algebraic property

Define the shallow quotient

\[
\mathcal H_m=
\frac{\ker B_m\cap\ker B_{m-1}}
{\ker B_m\cap\ker B_{m-1}\cap\ker B_{m-2}}.
\tag{3.10}
\]

The smallest linear suspension property is:

\[
L_m(\ker B_m\cap\ker B_{m-1})
\subseteq
\ker B_{m+1}\cap\ker B_m
\tag{3.11}
\]

together with denominator preservation

\[
\begin{aligned}
L_m(&\ker B_m\cap\ker B_{m-1}\cap\ker B_{m-2})\\
&\subseteq
\ker B_{m+1}\cap\ker B_m\cap\ker B_{m-1}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3.11A}
\]

Then \(L_m\) induces a map

\[
\overline L_m:\mathcal H_m\longrightarrow\mathcal H_{m+1}
\tag{3.12}
\]

which must be nonzero on the seed class.  Theorem 3.2 proves that the
individual natural constant-gap operators \(P_d\) satisfying
(3.11)--(3.12) on a depth-two-active class are exactly those in (3.8).

## 4. Classification of the positive equivariant cone

The preceding operators used an oriented representative.  At the level
of unoriented wreath columns,

\[
\Omega_m\simeq S_n/D_{2n}.
\]

Consider a nonnegative integral operator \(T\) with the following
properties:

1. \(T\) commutes with the action of \(S_n\) on the old labels and fixes
   \(x,y\);
2. every child preserves the cyclic order of the old labels; and
3. \(T\) is top-two neutral on every vector satisfying (3.4).

### Theorem 4.1 — natural-cone classification

Among distinct-gap insertions, every such operator is a nonnegative
combination of the two dihedral orbit sums

\[
Q_{m-1}=P_{m-1}+P_{m+2},
\qquad
Q_m=P_m+P_{m+1}.
\tag{4.1}
\]

Same-gap orbits have coefficient zero.  Moreover no nonzero operator in
this cone maps even one old wreath to a new middle packing.

#### Proof

The stabilizer of one old unoriented cycle is \(D_{2n}\).  Its rotation
subgroup is transitive on the gaps, so equivariance makes the coefficient
constant over every pointing orbit.  A reflection interchanges directed
distances \(d\) and \(n-d\).  Thus every distinct-gap orbit is a
nonnegative multiple of \(P_d+P_{n-d}\).

Equation (3.6) shows that every orbit with \(h_d(m-1)>0\) contributes
the same nonzero vector

\[
\iota_{xy}B_{m-2}z
\]

with a positive scalar.  Nonnegative orbit coefficients cannot cancel
one another.  Theorem 3.2 therefore leaves only the two orbits in
(4.1).  The same-gap orbit is excluded by the positive two-new
coefficient described after Theorem 3.2.

For a fixed old middle interval \(S\), one directed gap orbit contains
the new middle target \(S\cup\{x\}\)

\[
m+1-h_d(m)
=
\begin{cases}
m,&d=m-1,m+2,\\
m+1,&d=m,m+1
\end{cases}
\tag{4.2}
\]

times.  Every number in (4.2) exceeds one.  Hence even one nonzero orbit
repeats a middle target, and its image is not a packing. \(\square\)

This theorem is the exact symmetry obstruction.  A positive suspension
must break the old cycle stabilizer and choose phases state-dependently.
There is also a factor-size obstruction to a pure uniform product.  If
every old factor row produced exactly \(k\) children and no auxiliary
rows were used, then

\[
k\operatorname{Cat}_m=\operatorname{Cat}_{m+1},
\]

so

\[
k=\frac{\operatorname{Cat}_{m+1}}{\operatorname{Cat}_m}
=4-\frac6{m+2}.
\tag{4.3}
\]

This is the integer \(3\) at \(m=4\) and is nonintegral for every
\(m\ge5\).  A recursive factor lift must therefore have nonuniform
fibres or a macroscopic auxiliary completion even after equivariance is
broken.

## 5. The genuine all-dimensional signed suspension

For each supported unoriented row, fix one of its two orientations before
applying \(P_d\); orientations at later stages may be chosen recursively
and independently.  The sector identities are unchanged if an
orientation is reversed and \(d\) is replaced by \(2j+1-d\), and both
directed choices lie in the allowed set below.

For \(M\ge4\), choose at each step

\[
d_j\in\{j-1,j,j+1,j+2\},
\qquad 4\le j<M,
\]

use a fresh labelled pair \(\{x_j,y_j\}\), and define

\[
z_4=w_4,\qquad
z_{j+1}=P_{d_j}z_j.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Let

\[
D_M=\prod_{j=4}^{M-1}(2j+1),
\qquad
J_j=\iota_{x_j}+\iota_{y_j}.
\tag{5.2}
\]

### Theorem 5.1 — exact signed infinite family

Every \(z_M\) is squarefree, and

\[
|z_M^+|=|z_M^-|=4D_M,
\tag{5.3}
\]

\[
B_Mz_M=B_{M-1}z_M=0,
\tag{5.4}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
B_{M-2}z_M
=\frac{(M-2)!}{2}\,
J_{M-1}\cdots J_4(B_2w_4)\ne0.}
\tag{5.5}
\]

#### Proof

Deleting the newest labelled pair from a descendant recovers its unique
old parent and the two marked gaps.  If two descendants represented the
same unoriented cyclic order, an orientation-preserving identification
would give the same pointing, while an orientation-reversing
identification would change \(d_j\) to
\((2j+1)-d_j\).  Within one fixed directed operator these cannot be equal
because \(2j+1\) is odd.  Recursive deletion therefore recovers the
entire insertion history, so coefficients stay in
\(\{-1,0,1\}\).  Each parent has \(2j+1\) descendants, proving (5.3).

Equations (5.4)--(5.5) follow inductively from (3.8)--(3.9).  The scalar
is

\[
\prod_{j=4}^{M-1}(j-1)
=3\cdot4\cdots(M-2)
=\frac{(M-2)!}{2}.
\]

The operator \(J_{M-1}\cdots J_4\) has
\(2^{M-4}\) disjoint sectors, distinguished by choosing exactly one
label from each fresh pair.  It is injective, and (2.6) is nonzero.
\(\square\)

Conformally decomposing \(z_M\) in the integer kernel of

\[
\binom{B_M}{B_{M-1}}
\]

shows that every dimension contains a squarefree stacked-Graver vector
whose \(B_{M-2}\)-image is nonzero.  This is a lattice theorem only.
The raw signs in (5.1) are not middle packings.

Representation branching creates no further obstruction.  The explicit
injection in (5.5) already carries the degree-two seed signal.  For
example, if \(g=B_2w_4\), the natural down map on
\((\iota_x+\iota_y)g\) gives \(2g\).  The failure is therefore not loss
of the relevant Specht isotype.

## 6. Universal lower-shadow capacity and the second-step obstruction

### Theorem 6.1 — packing capacity

Let \(F\) be any middle-wreath packing in dimension \(M\).  Fix
\(1\le q\le M-1\) and

\[
T\in\binom{[2M+1]}{M-q}.
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
(B_{M-q}\mathbf1_F)_T
\le
L_{M,q}:=
\left\lfloor
\frac1{q+1}\binom{M+q+1}{q}
\right\rfloor.}
\tag{6.1}
\]

Every support-feasible trade \(p=\mathbf1_P-\mathbf1_N\) therefore
satisfies

\[
|(B_{M-q}p)_T|\le L_{M,q}.
\tag{6.2}
\]

#### Proof

Whenever \(T\) occurs as a cyclic \((M-q)\)-interval in one wreath,
it has exactly \(q+1\) cyclic middle extensions: add \(a\) positions
on the left and \(q-a\) on the right for \(0\le a\le q\).

For distinct rows of a middle packing, these extension families are
disjoint, because no middle target has two owners.  The total number of
middle supersets of \(T\) is

\[
\binom{(2M+1)-(M-q)}q
=\binom{M+q+1}q.
\]

Division proves (6.1).  The two loads in a support-feasible trade both
belong to \([0,L_{M,q}]\), so their difference belongs to
\([-L_{M,q},L_{M,q}]\), proving (6.2). \(\square\)

At depth two,

\[
L_{M,2}
=\left\lfloor\frac{(M+3)(M+2)}6\right\rfloor.
\tag{6.3}
\]

### Theorem 6.2 — the raw suspended effect is not support feasible from \(M=6\)

No support-feasible trade \(p\) in any dimension \(M\ge6\) satisfies

\[
B_{M-2}p=B_{M-2}z_M.
\tag{6.4}
\]

#### Proof for \(M\ge7\)

Choose one coefficient \(+1\) target in \(B_2w_4\) and one new label
from every added pair.  The corresponding coordinate in (5.5) has
magnitude

\[
A_M=\frac{(M-2)!}{2}.
\tag{6.5}
\]

At \(M=7\),

\[
A_7=60>L_{7,2}=15.
\]

Thereafter \(A_{M+1}/A_M=M-1\ge6\), whereas the ratio of the
unfloored quadratic capacities is less than \(2\).  Thus
\(A_M>L_{M,2}\) for every \(M\ge7\), contradicting (6.2).

#### Proof for \(M=6\)

Let \(E\) be a base pair with coefficient \(+1\) in (2.6), and let

\[
A=\{x_4,y_4\},\qquad B=\{x_5,y_5\}
\]

be the two fresh pairs.  For \(u\in A,v\in B\), put

\[
R_{uv}=E\cup\{u,v\}.
\]

All four targets have coefficient

\[
(B_4z_6)_{R_{uv}}=12,
\]

and

\[
L_{6,2}=\frac1{3}\binom92=12.
\]

If (6.4) held, the positive load at every \(R_{uv}\) would equal
\(12\), while the negative load would be zero.  Equality in the proof
of (6.1) forces the positive occurrences of each \(R_{uv}\) to exhaust
all \(36\) middle six-set supersets of \(R_{uv}\).

The six-set

\[
T=E\cup A\cup B
\]

contains all four \(R_{uv}\).  Its unique positive owner would therefore
have to contain all four as cyclic four-subintervals of the same cyclic
six-interval \(T\).  A six-interval has only

\[
6-4+1=3
\]

four-subintervals contained in it.  This contradiction proves the
\(M=6\) case. \(\square\)

Thus every positive recursion must disperse or renormalize the deeper
signal by the second step.  Literal multiplication by \(m-1\) cannot
continue inside the exact-factor fibre.

## 7. Exact pointed-section criterion

The sector identity is linear.  Positivity asks for a section of the gap
orbits.

Let \(N,P\) be old middle packings satisfying

\[
B_m(\mathbf1_P-\mathbf1_N)=
B_{m-1}(\mathbf1_P-\mathbf1_N)=0.
\tag{7.1}
\]

Fix an oriented representative of every row in \(N\cup P\).  For each
sign \(\epsilon\in\{-,+\}\) and each row \(C\) occurring on that sign,
choose a finite set of insertion descriptors

\[
\Xi_C^\epsilon\subseteq
\mathbb Z_{2m+1}\times\{m-1,m,m+1,m+2\}.
\tag{7.2}
\]

The chosen lifted family on sign \(\epsilon\) consists of all
\(E_{a,d}C\) with \((a,d)\in\Xi_C^\epsilon\).  This sign-specific
notation matters when a common auxiliary row is adjoined to both old
signs but lifted differently.

For an old middle target \(S\) occurring at start \(s_C(S)\), let
\(\kappa_d(a-s_C(S))\) be the four-bit incidence vector on

\[
S\cup\{x\},\quad S\cup\{y\},\quad
[2m+1]\setminus S
\quad\hbox{at new rank }m+1,
\]

and

\[
S\quad\hbox{at new rank }m.
\tag{7.3}
\]

For an old \((m-1)\)-target \(U\), define
\(\eta_d(a-s_C(U))\) to be the incidence vector on

\[
U\cup\{x,y\}\quad\hbox{at new rank }m+1,
\]

and

\[
U\cup\{x\},\quad U\cup\{y\}
\quad\hbox{at new rank }m.
\tag{7.4}
\]

### Theorem 7.1 — necessary and sufficient section test

The two selected lifted sides have equal \(B_{m+1}\)- and
\(B_m\)-incidence if and only if:

1. for every old middle target \(S\), the sums of the
   \(\kappa\)-signatures over its unique negative and positive owners are
   equal; and
2. for every old \((m-1)\)-target \(U\), the sums of all
   \(\eta\)-signatures over its negative and positive occurrence
   multisets are equal.

Assume each selected descendant family above one parent is internally a
middle packing.  Then each whole lifted sign is a middle packing if and
only if every target \(U\cup\{x,y\}\) in (7.4) is selected at most once
on that sign.

If these conditions hold and the negative lifted packing is contained in
one exact factor, replacing it by the positive lifted packing is an
exact-factor move.  It is depth-two active precisely when its
\(B_{m-1}\)-image is nonzero.

#### Proof

Every new target in the top two rows contains zero, one, or two new
labels.  Targets with zero or one new label and old core of size at least
\(m\) are exactly the four coordinates in (7.3).  Targets with old core
of size \(m-1\) are exactly the three coordinates in (7.4).  This
partitions the target rows: the only apparently omitted sector, a
new-rank-\(m\) target with two new labels and old core of size \(m-2\),
is empty for every distance in (7.2), because
\(h_d(m-1)=0\).  Hence the two signature ledgers are necessary and
sufficient.

If two descendants of different old parents share a zero- or one-new
middle target, deleting the new labels gives a common old middle target,
contradicting the old packing property.  The only remaining cross-parent
collision is a two-new target \(U\cup\{x,y\}\), and its multiplicity is
recorded by (7.4).  This proves the packing criterion.

Equal middle support lets the same residual factor complete both signs.
The last assertion is the definition of depth-two activity. \(\square\)

This theorem gives the smallest exact algebraic/combinatorial property:
a signature-compatible, packing-compatible, factor-completable section
whose next lower row is nonzero.  None of those four adjectives follows
from the other three.

### Explicit near-antipodal signatures

For \(d=m-1\), write \(z=a-s\).  The old-middle signature is

\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
z&\kappa^-(z)\\ \hline
0&(1,0,1,0)\\
1,\ldots,m-2&(1,0,0,0)\\
m-1&(1,0,0,1)\\
m&(0,0,0,1)\\
m+1&(0,1,0,1)\\
m+2,\ldots,2m-1&(0,1,0,0)\\
2m&(0,1,1,0).
\end{array}
\tag{7.5}
\]

The old-\((m-1)\) signature is

\[
\eta^-(z)=
\begin{cases}
(0,1,0),&0\le z\le m-2,\\
(0,0,0),&z=m-1,m,\\
(0,0,1),&m+1\le z\le2m-1,\\
(1,1,1),&z=2m.
\end{cases}
\tag{7.6}
\]

The coordinates in (7.6) are ordered as
\((Uxy,Ux,Uy)\).  In particular, unlike the antipodal type, the
near-antipodal type has a both-new middle sector.  Equality of only the
four-bit ledger (7.5) is not enough.

For one fixed old parent \(C\), two directed
\(d=m-1\) descendants \(E_{a,m-1}C,E_{b,m-1}C\) are middle-disjoint
if and only if

\[
b-a\equiv m\quad\hbox{or}\quad m+1\pmod{2m+1}.
\tag{7.7}
\]

Indeed, phases exposing a fixed \(S\cup\{x\}\) form an arc of length
\(m\).  Every pair at circular distance at most \(m-1\) lies in a
translate of this arc and shares such a target.  At distances \(m,m+1\),
the one-new arcs, the two-point no-new set, and the singleton both-new
state have no common phase.  Thus a fixed directed near-antipodal ray
allows at most two children above one parent, and a two-child choice has
the form

\[
E_{a,m-1}C+E_{a+m,m-1}C.
\tag{7.8}
\]

## 8. Symbolic obstruction at the seed

The \(m=4\) seed has a particularly rigid ownership-transfer table.  For
the rows \(A,B,C,D\) in (2.5), the moved old middle occurrences are

\[
\begin{gathered}
A0\leftrightarrow A1,\qquad
A2\leftrightarrow D0,\qquad
A3\leftrightarrow C0,\qquad
A4\leftrightarrow B0,\\
A6\leftrightarrow D5,\qquad
A7\leftrightarrow C5,\qquad
A8\leftrightarrow B5.
\end{gathered}
\tag{8.1}
\]

All other starts are \(\tau\)-fixed: \(A5\), and starts

\[
1,2,3,4,6,7,8
\tag{8.2}
\]

in each of \(B,C,D\).

This table is read directly from (2.5).  As unordered four-sets,

\[
\begin{aligned}
\tau(A0)&=A1=\{2,3,4,6\},&
\tau(A2)&=D0=\{1,3,4,8\},\\

\tau(A8)&=B5=\{2,4,6,9\}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{8.2A}
\]

Each occurrence in (8.2) contains either both of \(1,2\) or neither,
so it is fixed by \(\tau\).  Equations (8.2A) account for every
remaining occurrence and prove (8.1).

### 8.1 The corrected antipodal no-go

For \(d=4\), the five fibres of the simultaneous old-middle
\((B_5,B_4)\)-signature are

\[
\{0,1,2\},\quad\{3\},\quad\{4\},\quad
\{5,6,7\},\quad\{8\}.
\tag{8.3}
\]

Take the old negative/positive row pair

\[
C_0=(1,3,4,8,6,7,9,5,2),
\qquad
D_0=(1,5,9,7,6,8,4,3,2).
\tag{8.4}
\]

Three common old middle targets start in this pair at

\[
(8,6),\qquad(7,7),\qquad(1,4).
\tag{8.5}
\]

Let \(\kappa\) be the class map in (8.3), put

\[
x=a-8,\qquad \delta=b-a.
\]

Top-two equality would require

\[
\kappa(x)=\kappa(x+\delta+2),
\tag{8.6}
\]

\[
\kappa(x+1)=\kappa(x+\delta+1),
\tag{8.7}
\]

\[
\kappa(x+7)=\kappa(x+\delta+4).
\tag{8.8}
\]

Equation (8.7) gives
\(\delta\in\{0,\pm1,\pm2\}\).  The five cases are:

* \(\delta=0\): (8.6) leaves \(x=0,5\), and (8.8) fails;
* \(\delta=1\): (8.7) leaves \(x+1\in\{0,1,5,6\}\), and (8.6)
  fails;
* \(\delta=2\): (8.7) leaves \(x+1\in\{0,5\}\), and (8.6) fails;
* \(\delta=-2\): (8.7) leaves \(x+1\in\{2,7\}\), and (8.8)
  fails; and
* \(\delta=-1\): (8.7) leaves
  \(x+1\in\{1,2,6,7\}\), and (8.8) fails.

Thus a one-child antipodal section cannot preserve both rows.

Any two distinct antipodal descendants of one old parent share a new
middle target.  Let \(\pi\) push every lifted row back to its old parent
by deleting \(x,y\), retaining coefficients.  The exact deletion identity

\[
(B_4\pi h)_S
=(B_5h)_{Sx}+(B_5h)_{Sy}-(B_5h)_{[9]\setminus S}
\tag{8.9}
\]

shows that a conformal \(B_5\)-kernel subrelation propagates one common
descendant multiplicity around the connected old ownership overlay.
Packing restricts that multiplicity to \(0\) or \(1\).  Equations
(8.6)--(8.8) rule out \(1\).  Therefore there is no nonzero
support-feasible

\[
h\sqsubseteq P_4w_4
\]

with

\[
B_5h=B_4h=0.
\tag{8.10}
\]

The reverse distance \(d=5\) is equivalent by reversing the gap direction.

The \(B_4h=0\) hypothesis in (8.10) is essential to the proof.  A
previously circulated stronger wording claimed that \(B_5h=0\) alone
was impossible.  The three-target argument uses the simultaneous
\((B_5,B_4)\) signature and does not prove that stronger assertion.

### 8.2 The fixed near-antipodal rays

For \(d=3\), the four-bit signature word is

\[
\begin{array}{c|ccccccccc}

\tag{8.11}

Its fibres are

\{0\},\quad\{1,2\},\quad\{3\},\quad\{4\},\quad
\{5\},\quad\{6,7\},\quad\{8\}.
\tag{8.12}

This partition refines (8.3).  Therefore the three equations
(8.6)--(8.8) have no solution with \(\kappa_3\) either.  The relation

\[
\kappa_6(z)=\kappa_3(2-z)
\tag{8.13}
\]

gives the same conclusion for the reverse distance.

The one-child argument does not by itself exhaust a fixed near ray,
because (7.8) permits two children over one parent.  Put

\[
Q_a(C)=E_{a,3}C+E_{a+4,3}C.
\]

For an old middle target its aggregate signature has cyclic class word

\[
p(0),\ldots,p(8)=A,B,C,B,D,E,C,C,E,
\tag{8.14}
\]

with fibres

\[
\{0\},\quad\{1,3\},\quad\{2,6,7\},\quad
\{4\},\quad\{5,8\}.
\tag{8.15}

The corresponding five vectors are

\[
\begin{aligned}
A&=(1,0,1,1),&
B&=(1,1,0,1),&
C&=(1,1,0,0),\\
D&=(0,1,1,1),&

\end{aligned}
\tag{8.15A}

None is zero or one of the seven single-child vectors in (8.11).
Therefore the aggregate four-bit signature determines whether a parent
contributes zero, one, or two children.

For every nonzero shift \(\delta\in\mathbb Z_9\),

\[
|\{z:p(z)=p(z+\delta)\}|\le2.
\tag{8.16}
\]

For shifts \(1,2,3,4\), the equality counts are respectively
\(1,1,1,2\); the other four counts follow by symmetry.

Suppose the negative packets have phases \(a_i\) and the positive packets
have phases \(b_i\).  The seven fixed starts in each of \(B,C,D\),
together with (8.16), force

\[
a_i=b_i\qquad(i=B,C,D).
\]

The six nonloop edges incident with \(A\), together with its fixed start
\(5\), similarly force \(a_A=b_A\).  The loop
\(A0\leftrightarrow A1\) now gives the unique adjacent equality in
(8.14), so

\[
a_A=7.
\]

The edge \(A3\leftrightarrow C0\) forces
\(p(4)=p(a_C)\), hence \(a_C=4\).  The edge
\(A7\leftrightarrow C5\) then asks for

\[
p(0)=p(8),
\]

contradicting (8.15).  Thus the uniform directed \(d=3\) two-child
packet also fails.  The reverse \(d=6\) case follows globally.

The zero-, one-, and two-child aggregate signature sets are pairwise
disjoint.  Equality around the connected old ownership overlay therefore
propagates one common child multiplicity \(k\in\{0,1,2\}\).
The preceding one- and two-child arguments rule out \(k=1,2\).
Consequently

\[
\boxed{
\text{the uniform }P_3w_4\text{ and }P_6w_4
\text{ have no nonzero conformal support-feasible top-two piece}.}
\tag{8.17}

### 8.3 Complete one-child no-go for all four distances

It remains possible a priori to choose the two gap types and their
directions independently row by row.  Even that does not give a
one-child suspension.

All phases below are in \(\mathbb Z_9\).  For an old four-window
starting at \(s\), put \(z=a-s\), and set

\[
I_4=\{0,1,2\},\qquad C_4=\{8,0,1,2,3\}.
\]

Writing \([E]\) for the indicator of \(E\), direct inspection of the
four possible target sectors gives

\[
K_d(z)=
\bigl(
[z\in C_4,\ z+d\notin I_4],\
[z+d\in C_4,\ z\notin I_4],\
[z,z+d\in C_4],\
[z,z+d\notin I_4]
\bigr).
\tag{8.17A}
\]

The coordinates are those in (7.3).  Evaluating (8.17A) gives the two
raw four-bit words

\[
\begin{aligned}
N={}&(1010,1000,1000,1001,0001,
      0101,0100,0100,0110),\\
H={}&(1000,1000,1000,1001,0101,
      0100,0100,0100,1111),
\end{aligned}
\tag{8.18}
\]

for the near and antipodal types.  Reverse directions satisfy

\[
N_{\rm rev}(z)=N(2-z),\qquad
H_{\rm rev}(z)=H(2-z).
\tag{8.19}
\]

The corresponding three-bit old-triple words, recording respectively
\(U\cup\{x\}\) in \(B_4\), \(U\cup\{y\}\) in \(B_4\), and
\(U\cup\{x,y\}\) in \(B_5\), follow in the same way from

\[
I_3=\{0,1\},\qquad C_3=\{8,0,1,2\}.
\]

Their first two bits are obtained from (8.17A) by replacing
\((I_4,C_4)\) with \((I_3,C_3)\); their third bit is
\([\{z,z+d\}=\{8,2\}]\).  Hence

\[
\begin{aligned}
J_3&=(100,100,100,000,000,010,010,010,111),\\
J_4&=(100,100,100,000,010,010,010,010,100),\\
J_5&=(100,100,100,010,010,010,010,000,100),\\
J_6&=(100,100,111,010,010,010,000,000,100).
\end{aligned}
\tag{8.19A}
\]

Together, the \(K\)-words \(N,H\) and the \(J\)-words exhaust every
new-label sector of \(B_5\) and \(B_4\).

The nonfixed old-triple ownership table, also read directly from (2.5),
is

\[
\begin{aligned}
\tau(A0)&=B6=\{2,4,6\},&
\tau(A2)&=D0=\{1,3,4\},\\
\tau(A3)&=C0=\{1,3,8\},&
\tau(A4)&=B0=\{1,7,8\},\\
\tau(A7)&=D6=\{2,5,9\},&
\tau(A8)&=C6=\{2,6,9\}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{8.19B}
\]

Every other old triple contains both of \(1,2\) or neither.  Thus
(8.19B) is exhaustive, not merely a list of examples.

Every row choice can therefore be written as

\[
T(p+\sigma s),
\qquad
T\in\{N,H\},\quad \sigma\in\{\pm1\}.
\tag{8.20}
\]

Write \(T^\sigma(p)\) for this affine profile.  In raw gap coordinates,

\[
\begin{aligned}
N^-(p)&:(d,a)=(3,p),&
N^+(p)&:(d,a)=(6,2-p),\\
H^-(p)&:(d,a)=(4,p),&
H^+(p)&:(d,a)=(5,2-p).
\end{aligned}
\tag{8.20B}

Direct comparison of the two nine-letter words gives

\[
\begin{array}{c|cc}
&\text{nontrivial rotation}&\text{nonidentical reflection}\\ \hline
N&\le2&\le3\\
H&\le5&\le5.
\end{array}
\tag{8.20A}
\]

These bounds follow by comparing the displayed fibres: the three
singleton values of \(N\) fix a reflection once two of them agree, while
the two length-three constant arcs of \(H\) give at most five agreements.

Seven fixed starts in each of \(B,C,D\) force its negative and positive
profiles to be identical:

* cross-type agreement at seven starts is impossible, because \(N\)
  has three values absent from \(H\), while only two phases are omitted;
* distinct dihedral copies of \(N\) agree at at most three positions; and
* distinct dihedral copies of \(H\) agree at at most five positions.

The six cross-row constraints and the fixed start \(A5\) provide seven
agreements for \(A\), so its two profiles are identical as well.  Hence
the positive child over each underlying row is the \(\tau\)-image of the
negative child.

The loop \(A0\leftrightarrow A1\), after a global swap of \(x,y\) if
necessary, leaves

\[
N^+(1),\quad N^+(6),\quad
H^+(0),\quad H^+(1),\quad H^+(5),\quad H^+(6).
\tag{8.21}

For near \(A\), the old-middle equations force

\tag{8.22}

A8,\ B6,\ C5\quad(\text{and possibly }D4),
\tag{8.23}

or

\quad A3\leftrightarrow C0,\quad D0\leftrightarrow A2.
\tag{8.25}

Thus both near-\(A\) cases fail already in \(B_5\).

\begin{array}{c|c}

\boxed{

### Corollary 8.4 — complete at-most-one no-go

Suppose each of the eight signed seed rows is assigned either no child
or one order-preserving child with
\(d\in\{3,4,5,6\}\).  If the resulting conformal selection \(h\)
satisfies

\[
B_5h=B_4h=0,
\]

then \(h=0\).

#### Proof

Let \(\varepsilon_v\in\{0,1\}\) record whether the signed old row \(v\)
is active.  Fix an old middle target \(S\), and let \(v_-,v_+\) be its
unique negative and positive owners.  Its four-coordinate ledger is

\[
S\cup\{x\},\quad S\cup\{y\},\quad[9]\setminus S
\quad\hbox{in }B_5,
\qquad S\quad\hbox{in }B_4.
\tag{8.30}
\]

Deleting new labels, and complementing in the third coordinate, shows
that no old row other than the owner of \(S\) can contribute to this
ledger.  On either sign the ledger is therefore

\[
\varepsilon_v K_d(a-s_v(S)).
\tag{8.31}
\]

Every phase of each of \(K_3,K_4,K_5,K_6\) is nonzero, by
(8.18)--(8.19).  Equality of (8.30) across the signs forces

\[
\varepsilon_{v_-}=\varepsilon_{v_+}
\tag{8.32}
\]

on every old middle-ownership edge.  That eight-vertex overlay is
connected: fixed targets join the two signs of each underlying row, and
(8.1) joins \(A\) to each of \(B,C,D\) in both bipartite directions.
Thus all eight activity indicators are equal.  If they are zero, then
\(h=0\); if they are one, the theorem proved in (8.29) gives a
contradiction.
\(\square\)

This propagation uses neither packing of the children, the old-triple
ledger, \(B_3\)-activity, nor exact completion.

## 9. Exact completion is an independent semigroup condition

Let \(N',P'\) be any support-feasible selected lifted trade, and let

\[
u=B_{m+1}\mathbf1_{N'}
=B_{m+1}\mathbf1_{P'}\in\{0,1\}^{\binom{2m+3}{m+1}}.
\]

Let

\[
\mathcal S_{m+1}
=B_{m+1}\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{\Omega_{m+1}}
\tag{9.1}
\]

be the nonnegative middle-column semigroup.

### Proposition 9.1 — completion equivalence

The selected trade is applicable at an exact factor if and only if

\[
\boxed{\mathbf1-u\in\mathcal S_{m+1}.}
\tag{9.2}
\]

#### Proof

If \(H\dot\cup N'\) is exact, then

\[
B_{m+1}\mathbf1_H=\mathbf1-u,
\]

so (9.2) holds.  Conversely, any nonnegative integer solution of this
equation is automatically a packing because its right side is \(0/1\).
It gives the common residual \(H\), and the same \(H\) completes \(P'\)
because the two sides have equal middle incidence. \(\square\)

Integer-lattice membership in (9.2) is automatic: for any known exact
factor \(F_0\),

\[
\mathbf1-u
=B_{m+1}(\mathbf1_{F_0}-\mathbf1_{N'}).
\tag{9.3}
\]

Equation (9.3) is signed and does not imply membership in the
nonnegative cone or semigroup.  Neither the shadow identities nor the
Specht branching calculation proves (9.2).

## 10. Smallest surviving suspension lemma

The preceding results eliminate:

* every nonzero equivariant positive order-preserving operator;
* every fixed-distance one-child lift of the seed;
* every uniform directed near-antipodal conformal thinning, including
  its maximal two-child packet;
* every rowwise mixture of the two gap types with at most one child over
  each changed seed row, including arbitrary omissions; and
* every recursion which retains the literal factorially amplified lower
  image beyond the first step.

They do not eliminate a nonuniform packet using two children over some
parent, descendants of old completion rows, or an ownership-rerouting
preparation.

The smallest direct replacement statement is the following.

> **Mixed packet suspension \((\mathrm{MPS}_m)\) — UNPROVED.**
> Starting from an exact-factor-realizable, depth-two-active trade or
> stacked-primitive packet \(w_m\), first adjoin the same designated
> auxiliary rows from one common completion to both old signs, if
> desired.  There are sign-specific descriptor sets
> \(\Xi_C^\epsilon\) as in (7.2) on these enlarged signs such that:
>
> 1. the \(\kappa\)- and \(\eta\)-ledgers in Theorem 7.1 balance;
> 2. both selected signs are middle packings;
> 3. the new \(B_{m-1}\)-effect is nonzero; and
> 4. the negative packing satisfies the semigroup condition (9.2).

The capacity bound (6.1) then follows automatically from item 2.
Items 1--4 are exactly the positive, depth-two-active exact-factor gate
within the order-preserving two-label insertion architecture.  If a
primitive output is wanted, conformally decompose the resulting vector
in the kernel of the stacked rows
\(\binom{B_{m+1}}{B_m}\); because the total \(B_{m-1}\)-image is
nonzero, at least one stacked-Graver summand is still depth-two active,
and it remains exact-factor realizable by absorbing the unused rows into
the common residual.

If \((\mathrm{MPS}_m)\) holds successively along the trade or primitive
packet produced at the preceding step, induction gives a genuine
infinite family of exact-factor depth-two moves and, after this
extraction, stacked-primitive packets.

For the first step, Corollary 8.4 excludes every at-most-one-child
selection, including those which omit seed rows.  The uniform two-child
near ray is also excluded by (8.17).  Thus the first unresolved cases
are genuinely nonuniform multi-child packets, auxiliary rows, or
ownership rerouting, rather than another common gap or phase.

Even a proof of \((\mathrm{MPS}_m)\) would be only a local structural
advance.  It would not by itself prove MWB, common labelled ownership, or
the fixed-Gaussian-window theorem required for the contiguous-OR bound.

## 11. Adversarial audit

### Exact versus positive

The family \(z_M\) in Theorem 5.1 is an exact signed lattice family, not
an exact-factor family.  Squarefree coefficients do not imply that either
sign is a packing.  Theorem 4.1 shows directly that the natural orbit sums
repeat middle targets.

Theorem 3.2 classifies individual \(P_d\), not arbitrary signed linear
combinations of distances.  Such combinations can cancel the common
scalar multiplying (3.6).  The nonnegative equivariant cone, where that
cancellation is impossible, is the separate statement of Theorem 4.1.
The denominator condition (3.11A) is essential for the quotient map.

### The two no-go scopes

The antipodal three-target argument proves top-two impossibility, not
middle-only impossibility.  The hypothesis \(B_4h=0\) in (8.10) must not
be dropped.

The complete mixed theorem (8.29) concerns exactly one child over each
changed seed row.  Corollary 8.4 extends it to arbitrary omissions.  It
does not rule out a two-child/zero-child mixture, use of a
common-completion row, or non-order-preserving rerouting.

### Every incidence row

The classification (3.8) uses all three new-label sectors at
\(B_{m+1},B_m,B_{m-1}\).  In particular, near-antipodal insertion has
a both-new middle sector; omitting the first coordinate of (7.6) would
make the pointed criterion false.

The mixed one-child proof first balances the four coordinates attached
to every old middle target, then separately checks the old-triple
coordinates.  The candidate old-middle solutions which survive the first
ledger fail in (8.23)--(8.28).  Thus no hidden aggregation across the two
old ranks is used.

### Capacity

One occurrence of an \((M-q)\)-target consumes exactly \(q+1\)
distinct middle supersets.  The difference bound in (6.2) is
\(L_{M,q}\), not \(2L_{M,q}\), because both sign loads lie in the same
interval \([0,L_{M,q}]\).

The \(M=6\) argument does not claim a strict scalar violation:
the coefficient equals the capacity.  The contradiction comes from four
simultaneously saturated branches and the fact that one six-interval has
only three contained four-subintervals.

### Completion

Support feasibility does not imply (9.2).  Equation (9.3) gives only
signed lattice membership.  No cone or semigroup saturation theorem is
proved here.

In \((\mathrm{MPS}_m)\), auxiliary rows must be adjoined identically to
both old signs before they receive sign-specific descendants.  Capacity
is a consequence of packing, and primitivity is obtained only afterward
by conformal extraction; neither is an additional unproved gate.

## 12. Final theorem-level status

There is a genuine all-dimensional signed suspension:

\[
\boxed{
B_Mz_M=B_{M-1}z_M=0,\qquad
B_{M-2}z_M\ne0
\quad(M\ge4).}
\]

The individual constant-gap operators and the nonnegative equivariant
cone are completely classified, and representation branching carries
the signal.  Nevertheless:

\[
\boxed{
\begin{gathered}
\text{every positive equivariant insertion repeats middle targets;}\\
\text{the seed has no nonzero lift with at most one child per row;}\\
\text{the fixed near-antipodal two-child thinning also fails;}\\
\text{the raw lower effect is not support feasible from }M=6.
\end{gathered}}
\]

Therefore the requested infinite family of exact-factor circuits is not
proved.  The constant-gap, order-preserving line is exhausted up to the
precise nonuniform mixed-packet and common-completion statement
\((\mathrm{MPS}_m)\).  Any successful all-\(m\) theorem must change the
ownership topology rather than merely pad, average, or uniformly thin the
finished \(m=4\) identity.
