# Cross-cell compiler semigroups: simplex/Latin normality and a uniform finite-state conductor

Date: 2026-07-26

Method: pure mathematics only.  No computation, search, solver, or web
input is used.

## 0. Outcome

This note isolates the integral-semigroup part of a prospective transverse
status-cell construction.  Every column below is the **whole** target census
of one legal joint state; it includes both signs and every protected depth

\[
                 \mathcal C=\{(\sigma,q):\sigma\in\{-,+\},
                                      1\le q\le H\}.
\tag{0.1}
\]

No depthwise splitting of a compiler column is allowed.

There are three exact conclusions.

1.  A coherent gadget with two joint states has an exactly normal
    semigroup.  More generally, if its homogenized whole columns are
    linearly independent, the semigroup is free and normal in its physical
    group lattice.  In fact every library of at most four distinct binary
    all-depth columns is normal.  Thus a two-state antithetic gadget, a
    genuinely triangular three-state cross/large/small gadget, or a
    four-state binary packet rectangle has no semigroup-hole obstruction
    once cone and lattice membership have been checked.

2.  A common-block Latin construction has an exact network lift.  If
    \(d_{ia}\) is the whole column obtained by putting option \(a\) on
    owner block \(i\), the semigroup generated by permutation states is
    exactly

    \[
    \left\{(N,X,D(X)):
       X\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{b\times b},\quad
       X\mathbf1=X^T\mathbf1=N\mathbf1\right\}.
    \tag{0.2}
    \]

    It is normal, for arbitrary nonnegative integral target columns
    \(d_{ia}\).  This is the precise integral mechanism behind a Latin
    negative-covariance law; both signs and all depths are carried intact.

3.  Normality is false for a general dependent finite-state binary
    library.  Nevertheless, if there are \(K\) joint states and every
    literal target multiplicity in one state is at most \(\mu\), there is a
    conductor using at most

    \[
       \boxed{\kappa(K,\mu)
       =2^{K+1}K^2(K!)^2\max(1,\mu)^{2K}}
    \tag{0.3}
    \]

    gadget copies.  This bound is independent of the number of literal
    target rows.  Hence growing global union-support does not itself create
    growing semigroup holes for a fixed-state bounded-multiplicity gadget.

The last assertion is only an arithmetic theorem.  It does not prove the
weighted Farkas inequalities, negative floor covariance, or that differently
labelled rank-twisted tensor copies are column-isomorphic.  If the tensor
construction has \(R_m\) unrelated literal incidence types, the conductor
must be paid once per type unless a common Latin/network lift couples them.
The exact negligible-error condition is then

\[
                  {H\kappa(K,\mu)R_m\over N_m}=o(1),
\tag{0.4}
\]

where \(N_m\) is the total number of gadget copies of the same owner mass.

## 1. Whole-column model

Let \(\mathcal T\) be the disjoint union of all literal target sets indexed
by (0.1).  A legal joint state \(j\) of a finite multi-cell gadget has a
column

\[
                   a_j=(a_j(c,T))_{(c,T)\in\mathcal T}
                   \in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{\mathcal T}.
\tag{1.1}
\]

The coordinate is the number of occurrences of the literal target \(T\)
emitted by that state at colour \(c=(\sigma,q)\).  Put

\[
                         v_j=(1,a_j).
\tag{1.2}
\]

The first coordinate records one inseparable gadget copy.  For a library
\(A=\{v_1,\ldots,v_K\}\), define

\[
\begin{aligned}
 \mathsf S(A)&=\left\{\sum_{j=1}^K n_jv_j:n_j\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}\right\},\\
 \mathsf C(A)&=\left\{\sum_{j=1}^K t_jv_j:t_j\in\mathbb R_{\ge0}\right\},\\
 \mathsf L(A)&=\left\{\sum_{j=1}^K z_jv_j:z_j\in\mathbb Z\right\},\\
 \overline{\mathsf S}(A)&=\mathsf C(A)\cap\mathsf L(A).
\end{aligned}
\tag{1.3}
\]

Normality always means

\[
                    \mathsf S(A)=\overline{\mathsf S}(A),
\tag{1.4}
\]

that is, normality in the group lattice generated by the physical columns,
not in the full ambient integer lattice.  All support-design, cycle, and
port congruences remain encoded in \(\mathsf L(A)\).

If every state is owner-perfect on a gadget of owner mass \(M\), then

\[
             \sum_Ta_j((\sigma,q),T)=M
             \quad(\sigma\in\{-,+\},\ q\le H),
\tag{1.5}
\]

and consequently

\[
                         \|a_j\|_1=2HM.
\tag{1.6}
\]

## 2. Exact simplex normality

### Theorem 2.1 (whole-column simplex criterion)

If \(v_1,\ldots,v_K\) are linearly independent over \(\mathbb R\), then

\[
                         \mathsf S(A)=
                         \overline{\mathsf S}(A).
\tag{2.1}
\]

#### Proof

Take \(y\in\overline{\mathsf S}(A)\).  Cone membership gives a unique
real representation

\[
                         y=\sum_jt_jv_j,qquad t_j\ge0.
\tag{2.2}
\]

Lattice membership gives an integral representation

\[
                         y=\sum_jz_jv_j,qquad z_j\in\mathbb Z.
\tag{2.3}
\]

Linear independence makes the two coefficient vectors equal.  Thus
\(z_j=t_j\ge0\) for every \(j\), so \(y\in\mathsf S(A)\). \(\square\)

The criterion is simultaneous in all coordinates in (1.1); appending
further depth or sign rows cannot invalidate the proof.

### Corollary 2.2 (two and three binary states)

Every two-state gadget with distinct columns is normal.  Every three-state
gadget with three distinct binary columns

\[
                         a_j\in\{0,1\}^{\mathcal T}
\tag{2.4}
\]

is normal.

#### Proof

Two distinct points are affinely independent, which is equivalent to
linear independence of their homogenizations.

Suppose three distinct binary vectors were affinely dependent.  They would
be collinear.  On a coordinate where two endpoints differ, a strict
interior point of their line has a coordinate strictly between zero and
one, and hence is not binary.  Thus a line through two cube vertices
contains no third cube vertex.  The three binary points are affinely
independent, and Theorem 2.1 applies. \(\square\)

### Proposition 2.3 (four binary states)

Every library of at most four distinct binary target columns is normal in
its generated group lattice.

#### Proof

Only four columns require discussion.  If the four points are affinely
independent, Theorem 2.1 applies.  Three distinct cube vertices cannot be
collinear by the proof of Corollary 2.2, so the only remaining case is that
their convex hull \(P\) has affine dimension two.

No selected binary point lies in the convex hull of the others.  Indeed, if
a binary vector \(u\) is a convex combination of binary vectors, then at
every coordinate where \(u\) is zero all vectors of positive coefficient
must have coordinate zero, and similarly at every coordinate where \(u\)
is one.  All vectors of positive coefficient therefore equal \(u\).
Thus \(P\) is a quadrilateral.

Let \(\Lambda\) be the rank-two lattice generated by its vertex
differences.  Every \(\Lambda\)-point of \(P\) is an integral vector in
the unit cube, hence binary; the preceding coordinatewise argument shows
that it is one of the four vertices.  Therefore \(P\) has exactly four
boundary lattice points and no interior lattice point.  Pick's formula in
the lattice \(\Lambda\) gives normalized area two.  Either diagonal
triangulates \(P\) into two lattice triangles of positive integral
normalized area; their areas sum to two, so both triangles are unimodular.

The cones over these two homogenized unimodular triangles cover the cone
over \(P\).  Every lattice point in either cone is a nonnegative integral
sum of its three ray generators.  Hence every point of the normalized
semigroup belongs to the semigroup generated by the four vertices.
\(\square\)

This gives a useful construction rule.  If a transverse cross-cell trade
is packaged into two antithetic joint states, semigroup saturation is
automatic.  If the legal local shores are cross, large, and small and their
complete protected trace columns are distinct and binary, the resulting
three-state triangle is also arithmetically exact; the same is true after
adding one fourth binary joint state.  What remains in these cases is cone
membership, the physical lattice congruences, and the floor-covariance
estimate.

## 3. The Latin lift is a normal network semigroup

Let \(i,a\in[b]\).  Block \(i\) has a legal option \(a\) with arbitrary
whole target column

\[
                         d_{ia}\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{\mathcal T}.
\tag{3.1}
\]

For a permutation \(\pi\in S_b\), put

\[
 D(P_\pi)=\sum_i d_{i,\pi(i)},
 \qquad
 c_\pi=(1,P_\pi,D(P_\pi)),
\tag{3.2}
\]

where \(P_\pi\) is the permutation matrix.  Retaining the matrix coordinate
is essential: it records the integral block-to-label routing table.

### Theorem 3.1 (exact Latin semigroup)

The semigroup generated by the columns \(c_\pi\) is

\[
 \boxed{
 \mathsf S_{\rm Lat}
 =\left\{(N,X,D(X)):
   X\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{b\times b},\quad
   X\mathbf1=X^T\mathbf1=N\mathbf1\right\}.}
\tag{3.3}
\]

It is normal in its group lattice.

#### Proof

A sum of \(N\) permutation columns plainly has the form on the right of
(3.3).  Conversely, regard \(X\) as the multiplicity matrix of an
\(N\)-regular bipartite multigraph between block vertices and label
vertices.  Hall's inequality holds because an \(N\)-regular bipartite
multigraph sends \(N|S|\) edges out of a set \(S\), while its neighbour
set can receive at most \(N|N(S)|\) edges.  Hence it has a perfect
matching.  Remove that matching and iterate.  This decomposes \(X\) into
\(N\) permutation matrices and proves (3.3).

The real cone generated by the \(c_\pi\)'s consists of triples
\((N,X,D(X))\) with \(X\ge0\) and the same row and column sums
\(N\mathbf1\), by the Birkhoff decomposition.  If such a triple belongs to
the group lattice, then \(N\) and every entry of \(X\) are integers.
The preceding decomposition puts it in (3.3).  This is normality. \(\square\)

The target map \(D\) was arbitrary.  It may contain lower and upper
columns at every depth, and it may have extensive literal overlap between
different status cells.  Thus the Latin lift is an exact answer to the
integral grouping question **provided the fractional construction first
produces a common block-label routing table**.

### Lemma 3.2 (entrywise rounding of a transport table)

Let \(x\in\mathbb R_{\ge0}^{b\times b}\) have every row and column sum
one.  For every positive integer \(N\), there is an integral matrix \(X\)
with row and column sums \(N\) such that

\[
                         |X_{ia}-Nx_{ia}|<1
\tag{3.4}
\]

whenever \(Nx_{ia}\notin\mathbb Z\), and equality holds when
\(Nx_{ia}\in\mathbb Z\).

#### Proof

Put \(F_{ia}=\lfloor Nx_{ia}\rfloor\) and let
\(\theta_{ia}=Nx_{ia}-F_{ia}\).  The row deficits

\[
 r_i=N-\sum_aF_{ia}=\sum_a\theta_{ia}
\tag{3.5}
\]

and the column deficits \(s_a\) are nonnegative integers with equal total.
The fractional matrix \(\theta\) is a feasible bipartite flow with these
margins and capacities one, supported on the entries where \(\theta>0\).
Integral max-flow gives a zero-one matrix \(Y\), on the same support, with
row margins \(r_i\) and column margins \(s_a\).  Then \(X=F+Y\) has the
claimed margins and entrywise bounds. \(\square\)

Combining Lemma 3.2 with Theorem 3.1 gives \(N\) legal integral Latin
states whose aggregate target vector is \(D(X)\).  Moreover,

\[
 \boxed{
 \|D(X)-ND(x)\|_1
 \le\sum_{i,a}\|d_{ia}\|_1.}
\tag{3.6}
\]

Indeed, expand the difference, use the triangle inequality, and apply
\(|X_{ia}-Nx_{ia}|\le1\).

If every block has owner mass \(M\), then

\[
 \sum_{i,a}\|d_{ia}\|_1=2HMb^2.
\tag{3.7}
\]

The \(N\) Latin layers have owner mass

\[
                         W_{\rm bank}=NbM.
\tag{3.8}
\]

Thus the simultaneous all-depth occurrence error is at most

\[
             {2HMb^2\over NbM}W_{\rm bank}
             ={2Hb\over N}W_{\rm bank}.
\tag{3.9}
\]

In particular it is \(o(W_{\rm bank})\) whenever \(Hb=o(N)\).

There is also an exact floor-energy consequence.  Suppose
\(ND(x)=y\) is an integral floor-balanced vector, so each coordinate of
\(y\) is one of its two allowed floor values.  Put

\[
                  \rho=\max_{(c,T)}\sum_{i,a}d_{ia}(c,T).
\tag{3.10}
\]

Then the rounded vector \(z=D(X)\) satisfies

\[
 \boxed{
       \sum_{c}\!Q_c(z)
       \le (\rho+1)\,2HMb^2.}
\tag{3.11}
\]

To prove this, write \(e=z-y\).  Formula (3.4) gives
\(|e(c,T)|\le\rho\), while (3.6) gives
\(\|e\|_1\le2HMb^2\).  If \(y_T=c\) or \(c+1\), then

\[
 (y_T+e_T-c)(y_T+e_T-c-1)
       \le e_T^2+|e_T|
       \le(\rho+1)|e_T|.
\tag{3.12}
\]

Summation proves (3.11).  Hence this network rounding yields
simultaneous floor energy \(o(W_{\rm bank})\) under the exact quantitative
condition

\[
                         {H b(\rho+1)\over N}=o(1).
\tag{3.13}
\]

The hypothesis that the fractional vector itself is floor-balanced is
substantive.  Uniform first marginals alone do not imply it.

## 4. A uniform conductor for every fixed-state bounded column library

The simplex and Latin theorems are exact normality results.  The next
theorem shows that even when normality fails, its asymptotic cost is
uniformly bounded if the number of states and the target multiplicity are
fixed.

### Theorem 4.1 (dimension-free finite-state conductor)

Assume

\[
                 0\le a_j(c,T)\le\mu
                 \qquad(j\in[K],\ (c,T)\in\mathcal T).
\tag{4.1}
\]

Put \(\mu_0=\max(1,\mu)\) and

\[
                         \Delta=K!\mu_0^K.
\tag{4.2}
\]

There is an element \(h\in\mathsf S(A)\), of first coordinate at most

\[
                         \kappa=2^{K+1}K^2\Delta^2,
\tag{4.3}
\]

such that

\[
                         \boxed{
 h+\overline{\mathsf S}(A)\subseteq\mathsf S(A).}
\tag{4.4}
\]

The bound is independent of \(|\mathcal T|\).

#### Proof

Write \(V\) for the integer matrix with columns \(v_j\), and let its rank
be \(s\le K\).  The cone of \(V\) is pointed because the first coordinate
of every generator is one.  Triangulate it into simplicial cones generated
by linearly independent subsets of the \(v_j\)'s.  There are at most
\(2^K\) such cones.

For one simplicial cone with generator set \(J\), every
\(x\in\overline{\mathsf S}(A)\) in that cone has a unique expression

\[
                         x=\sum_{j\in J}\lambda_jv_j,qquad
                         \lambda_j\ge0.
\tag{4.5}
\]

Subtracting \(\sum_j\lfloor\lambda_j\rfloor v_j\) leaves a lattice
point in the half-open parallelepiped

\[
 \Pi_J=\left\{\sum_{j\in J}\theta_jv_j:0\le\theta_j<1\right\}.
\tag{4.6}
\]

Choose \(|J|\) coordinate rows on which the columns in \(J\) form a
nonsingular square matrix \(B_J\).  Projection to those rows is injective
on their span.  Since all entries are at most \(\mu_0\),

\[
        |\det B_J|\le |J|!\mu_0^{|J|}\le\Delta.
\tag{4.7}
\]

The projected half-open parallelepiped contains at most
\(|\det B_J|\) integer points.  Therefore there is a set
\(F\subseteq\overline{\mathsf S}(A)\) with

\[
              |F|\le2^K\Delta,qquad
              \overline{\mathsf S}(A)=F+\mathsf S(A).
\tag{4.8}
\]

Every coordinate of every \(f\in F\) is at most \(K\mu_0\).

We next bound an integral representation of a fixed \(f\in F\).  Choose
\(s\) independent rows of \(V\), obtaining a rank-\(s\) matrix \(U\),
and choose an invertible \(s\)-column submatrix \(B\).  Let
\(\delta=|\det B|\le\Delta\).  Since \(f\in\mathsf L(A)\), the equation

\[
                              Vz=f
\tag{4.9}
\]

has an integral solution.  Reduce every coefficient outside \(B\) modulo
\(\delta\) into \(\{0,\ldots,\delta-1\}\).  This preserves solvability
for the basis coefficients, because

\[
                         \delta w\in B\mathbb Z^s
                         \qquad(w\in\mathbb Z^s)
\tag{4.10}
\]

by the adjugate identity.  The resulting right side for the basis system
has sup norm at most \(K\mu_0\delta\).  Every entry of
\(\operatorname {adj}B\) is at most
\((s-1)!\mu_0^{s-1}\) in absolute value.  Cramer's rule therefore gives

\[
                         \|z\|_1\le2K^2\Delta.
\tag{4.11}
\]

The selected independent rows determine a vector in the image of \(V\),
so the solution of the reduced row system is indeed a solution of (4.9).

Write \(z=p_f-q_f\), with \(p_f,q_f\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^K\) and disjoint
supports.  By (4.11), \(\|q_f\|_1\le2K^2\Delta\).  Define

\[
                         h=V\sum_{f\in F}q_f.
\tag{4.12}
\]

Its first coordinate is at most

\[
              |F|\,2K^2\Delta
              \le2^{K+1}K^2\Delta^2=\kappa.
\tag{4.13}
\]

For a fixed \(f\in F\),

\[
 h+f
 =V\left(p_f+\sum_{g\in F,\ g\ne f}q_g\right)
 \in\mathsf S(A).
\tag{4.14}
\]

Together with (4.8), this proves (4.4). \(\square\)

### Corollary 4.2 (universal finite-copy absorber)

For every normalized lattice point \(x\in\overline{\mathsf S}(A)\), the
configuration \(h+x\) is an integral nonnegative sum of legal whole
states.  Thus at most \(\kappa(K,\mu)\) fixed gadget copies form a
universal absorber for all remaining lattice-compatible configurations,
irrespective of the number of literal target coordinates.

There is also an exact interior form.  Write

\[
                         h=\sum_j\eta_jv_j,qquad
                         \sum_j\eta_j\le\kappa.
\tag{4.15}
\]

If \(y\in\overline{\mathsf S}(A)\) has a cone representation

\[
                         y=\sum_j\lambda_jv_j,qquad
                         \lambda_j\ge\eta_j,
\tag{4.16}
\]

then \(y\in\mathsf S(A)\).  Indeed, \(y-h\) is again in the normalized
semigroup and (4.4) applies.  In particular, if a fractional law gives
every used state probability at least \(\varepsilon>0\), every
lattice-compatible aggregate of \(N\) copies on that ray is integral once

\[
                         N\varepsilon\ge\kappa.
\tag{4.17}
\]

If a state covers \(M\) owners, the absorber's complete two-sign,
all-depth incidence mass is at most

\[
                         2H\kappa M.
\tag{4.18}
\]

Against a bank of \(N\) such copies, this is

\[
                         {2H\kappa\over N}W_{\rm bank}.
\tag{4.19}
\]

For fixed \(K,\mu\), it is \(o(W_{\rm bank})\) whenever \(N/H\to\infty\).
This accounts exactly for both signs and every protected depth.  Turning
the occurrence bound (4.19) into a floor-energy bound additionally
requires bounded pointwise overlap of the absorber columns, just as in
(3.10)--(3.12); semigroup theory alone does not supply that bound.

## 5. Normality can fail: an exact binary lattice hole

The bounded conductor cannot be replaced by a general normality theorem.
Here is a binary eight-column counterexample.

Let \(G\) consist of two triangles

\[
                 12,23,31\qquad\hbox{and}\qquad45,56,64
\tag{5.1}
\]

joined by the length-two path \(37,74\).  For every edge \(e\), let

\[
                         v_e=(1,\chi_e)\in\{1\}\times\{0,1\}^7,
\tag{5.2}
\]

where \(\chi_e\) is its vertex-incidence vector.  Put

\[
                         y=(3;1,1,1,1,1,1,0).
\tag{5.3}
\]

Then

\[
 y={1\over2}(v_{12}+v_{23}+v_{31}+v_{45}+v_{56}+v_{64}),
\tag{5.4}
\]

so \(y\) lies in the cone.  It also lies in the group lattice, because

\[
                         y=v_{12}+v_{37}+v_{45}+v_{64}-v_{74}.
\tag{5.5}
\]

But \(y\) is not in the semigroup.  Its seventh target coordinate is zero,
so a nonnegative representation cannot use either path edge.  Restricted
to either triangle, producing degree one at all three vertices would
require coefficient \(1/2\) on each of its three edges, and hence has no
integral realization.  Therefore

\[
                         y\in\overline{\mathsf S}(A)
                         \setminus\mathsf S(A).
\tag{5.6}
\]

Duplicating any row of this example into lower/upper or multiple-depth
copies preserves the hole.  Thus the mere facts that columns are binary,
owner-exact, and simultaneous in all colours do not imply normality.  This
is an abstract semigroup obstruction; it is not asserted that the eight
columns are realized by the present physical packet library.

## 6. Tensoring and the exact remaining boundary

The preceding results separate three cases.

### 6.1 A fixed coherent two/three/four-state binary gadget

If its homogenized all-depth columns satisfy Theorem 2.1, every
lattice-compatible cone point is integral exactly.  No conductor, random
rounding, or depthwise repair is needed.  This is the preferred arithmetic
form of a negative-covariance gadget.

### 6.2 A common cross-cell block-label table

If the transverse construction exposes block and option indices before
projection to literal targets, Theorem 3.1 gives an exactly normal lift.
The fractional Farkas/covariance construction must output a feasible
doubly stochastic table.  Lemma 3.2 and (3.11) then give the precise
all-depth rounding cost.  Projecting away the routing table before proving
integrality is unsafe: linear images of normal semigroups need not be
normal.

### 6.3 Unrelated rank-twisted incidence types

Suppose the tensor bank splits into \(R_m\) classes, each consisting of
column-isomorphic copies of one \(K\)-state, multiplicity-\(\mu\) gadget.
Apply Theorem 4.1 in every class.  The total absorber incidence is at most

\[
                         2H\kappa(K,\mu)M R_m.
\tag{6.1}
\]

If the total owner mass is \(W_{\rm bank}=MN_m\), this is \(o(W_{\rm
bank})\) exactly under (0.4).  If every frozen carrier creates its own
literal incidence type, so that \(R_m\) is comparable with \(N_m\), this
argument gives no saving.  One then needs the common Latin/network lift or
a new absorber which moves between carrier types.

Consequently semigroup saturation is not the live obstruction for a fixed
simplex gadget or a genuine common-block Latin resolution.  For those
architectures, the remaining tasks are exactly:

1. verify the joint weighted Farkas inequalities, with the floor/ceiling
   reset polytope rather than only coordinate means;
2. prove negative floor covariance for the same integral joint states;
3. preserve the common lower/upper chronology through every \(q\le H\);
   and
4. show that the rank-twisted tensoring supplies either sufficiently many
   column-isomorphic copies or one common block-label routing table.

No coefficient-one conclusion is claimed here.  The theorem closes the
integral arithmetic gate conditionally on one of the two explicit
structures above and identifies the exact carrier-type obstruction when
neither structure is present.
