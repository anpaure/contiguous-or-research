Date: 2026-08-01  
Status: **PASS for the frozen-relative rectangle class; one scope wording correction**

## 1. Verdict

I independently reconstructed the cyclic incidence atlas, parsed the frozen
candidate1911 factor, enumerated rectangles by unordered owner pairs and
cross-facet incidence products, and recomputed every terminal matching,
rank-7/rank-10 turn load, quotient component, and component voltage from
scratch.

The numerical result is correct for the following exact class:

* each rectangle is defined relative to one of the two frozen candidate1911
  matchings;
* a same-shore packet is a pair of such base-relative rectangles with
  disjoint owner pairs;
* a mixed packet is one base-relative `D` rectangle and one base-relative
  `H` rectangle, whose owner pairs may overlap;
* all terminal matching, cross-shore edge-disjointness, palette, topology,
  and voltage conditions are evaluated after both changes.

There is no full survivor in that class.

## 3. Scope correction

The reference JSON says “every exactly-two-rectangle packet.”  Read without
a convention, that is slightly too broad.  A genuinely sequential second
same-shore rectangle may be formed relative to the intermediate matching and
share one owner with the first; its terminal change is a three-owner
assignment circuit.  Such a move is not generated by the reference script's
pair-of-base-rectangles loop.  It is closed separately by the authenticated
one-side circuit census, not by this generator.

The only other sequential overlap is a second rectangle on the same owner
pair, which crosses back to the old facet pair (possibly using parallel
incidences).  I enumerated these returns independently.  There are 17 legal
`DD` and 18 legal `HH` terminal maps in the present atlas; none gains either
named lower hole, and the best terminal debt remains exactly two lower holes
and zero upper holes.  Thus the wording issue does not expose a repair.

A proof-safe formulation is therefore:

> Every pair of rectangles defined relative to the frozen candidate1911
> matchings, with disjoint owner pairs on a common shore or arbitrary overlap
> on opposite shores, has no full survivor.  Together with the independent
> one-simple-one-side circuit census, sequential one-owner overlaps are also
> closed through its stated support bound.

No claim follows for three or more rectangles, two longer circuits, a larger
mixed exchange, residence, ranks at least 11, source/opening, or compiler.
