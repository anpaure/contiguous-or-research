# Facet-packet root incidence is linear, with explicit local 2-opt switches

## Status

The one-step facet-ring reduction turns each selected owner block into a
clique whose edges are coloured by immediate-lower roots.  This note proves
that, after the owner blocks are made disjoint, any two packet cliques share
at most one root colour.  Equivalently, the packet--root incidence graph is
`C_4`-free.  It also records the exact local root trade generated by a
Hamilton-cycle 2-opt move.

These are unconditional structural inputs for the rainbow-clique factor.
They do not prove that the required globally rainbow Hamilton cycles exist.

## 1. Facet packets

Put

\[
 n=2r-1,\qquad D=d+1,\qquad L=D+1,
\]

and assume `d>=1`.  A facet packet is a pair

\[
 K\subset H\subset[n],\qquad |H|=r+1,\quad |H-K|=L.
\]

Write `F=H-K`.  Its owner set and root-colour set are

\[
 \mathcal O(H,K)=\{H-f:f\in F\},                         \tag{1.1}
\]

and

\[
 \mathcal R(H,K)=\{H-\{f,g\}:\{f,g\}\in {F\choose2}\}. \tag{1.2}
\]

Thus `mathcal O(H,K)` is the vertex set of a `K_L`, and the edge joining
`H-f` to `H-g` has colour `H-{f,g}`.

Let `mathcal M` be any family of facet packets whose owner sets (1.1) are
pairwise disjoint.

## 2. Exact two-packet intersection theorem

### Theorem 2.1 (root-linearity)

For two distinct packets `B=(H,K)` and `B'=(H',K')` in `mathcal M`,

\[
                  |\mathcal R(B)\cap\mathcal R(B')|\le1. \tag{2.1}
\]

Equality holds precisely when

\[
 H=Q\mathbin{\dot\cup}\{x_1,x_2\},\qquad
 H'=Q\mathbin{\dot\cup}\{y_1,y_2\},                     \tag{2.2}
\]

for one rank-`r-1` set `Q`, with the four displayed labels distinct, and

\[
                 \{x_1,x_2\}\subseteq H-K,\qquad
                 \{y_1,y_2\}\subseteq H'-K'.             \tag{2.3}
\]

In that case the unique common root colour is `Q`.

#### Proof

Suppose `Q` is common.  There are pairs

\[
 \{f,g\}\subseteq H-K,\qquad \{f',g'\}\subseteq H'-K'
\]

such that

\[
              H=Q\cup\{f,g\},\qquad H'=Q\cup\{f',g'\}. \tag{2.4}
\]

If the two added pairs coincide, then `H=H'`, and the owner packets share
both facets `H-f` and `H-g`, contrary to owner-disjointness.

If the two pairs meet in exactly one label, say `g=g'`, then the two
packets share the owner

\[
                         Q\cup\{g\}=H-f=H'-f',             \tag{2.5}
\]

again a contradiction.  Hence the pairs in (2.4) are disjoint.  This is
exactly (2.2)--(2.3), and it shows in particular that

\[
                         H\cap H'=Q.                        \tag{2.6}
\]

Since `Q=H cap H'` is then forced by the two packets, a second common root
cannot exist.  Conversely, (2.2)--(2.3) puts `Q` in both root families,
proving the equality statement.  \(\square\)

### Corollary 2.2 (linear packet--root incidence)

Make a bipartite graph `J` with left shore `mathcal M`, right shore the
rank-`r-1` roots, and `B Q` an edge when `Q in mathcal R(B)`.  Then

* every packet vertex has degree `binom(L,2)`;
* the degree of a root `Q` is its colour multiplicity `t_Q`; and
* `J` is `C_4`-free.

Equivalently, the occurrences of one root `Q` pair disjoint owners
containing `Q`, while two distinct packet cliques can support at most one
common root constraint.

This is stronger than properness of the edge-colouring.  Properness says
one colour is a matching on owner vertices; root-linearity says the
colour lists of two packet variables overlap in at most one entry.

## 3. Exact local Hamilton-cycle switch

Fix one packet and identify its owner clique with the labels in `F`: the
clique vertex labelled `f` is `H-f`, and clique edge `fg` has root colour

\[
                              Q_{fg}=H-\{f,g\}.              \tag{3.1}
\]

Let a Hamilton cycle contain two vertex-disjoint edges `ab` and `cd`, in
the cyclic order

\[
                         a,b,\ldots,c,d,\ldots,a.
\]

Deleting these two edges and inserting `ac` and `bd` gives another
Hamilton cycle.  On root colours the move is the exact trade

\[
 \boxed{
   \{Q_{ab},Q_{cd}\}
       \longleftrightarrow
   \{Q_{ac},Q_{bd}\}.}                                    \tag{3.2}
\]

All four colours in (3.2) are distinct.  The move changes no owner and no
other root occurrence.

For a fixed selected cycle edge `ab`, there are exactly `L-3` choices of a
second cycle edge disjoint from it, hence `L-3` such 2-opt switches.  The
alternative cross-pairing `ad,bc` closes the two resulting paths
separately and is not a Hamilton cycle; thus (3.2) is the unique
nontrivial Hamilton-preserving reconnection for the chosen two cuts.

## 4. The sharpened global gate

The owner/q1 problem after a facet-block packing is therefore a constrained
selection problem with three unusually strong properties:

1. each variable is a Hamilton cycle of one complete graph `K_L`;
2. each root colour occurs in at most `t_Q=O(L)` variables; and
3. any pair of variables shares at most one colour.

The elementary moves (3.2) generate the Hamilton-cycle graph inside each
packet.  What remains is a global augmenting theorem: starting from one
cycle per packet, route duplicated colours to the root holes using these
local 2-opt trades without creating a new duplicate elsewhere.

Theorem 2.1 removes multi-colour pair interactions from that theorem.
Every cross-packet interaction is a single binary colour constraint; the
remaining difficulty is the long alternating routing of those constraints,
not local packet entanglement.
