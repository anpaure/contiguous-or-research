# Sparse frame harmonic gap versus component integrality

Date: 2026-07-26

Method: pure mathematics only.

## 0. Outcome

Let `n=2m`.  Averaging a legal recursive context-array factor over all
coordinate frames gives the uniform fractional target load.  There is also
a polynomial-size frame generator with a completely explicit simultaneous
spectral gap: average over all transpositions of `[n]`.  On every target
slice `binom([n],r)`, its eigenvalue on the degree-`j` harmonic is

\[
 \lambda_j=1-\frac{j(n-j+1)}{\binom n2},             \tag{0.1}
\]

so the gap on every nonconstant target harmonic is exactly

\[
                         \gamma_n=\frac{2}{n-1}.     \tag{0.2}
\]

This holds simultaneously for both signs and every protected depth.
Thus lack of a sparse fractional spectral catalogue is not the outer
obstruction.

The obstruction is integrality of the status-cell/component heat.  For
an arbitrary catalogue of perfect-matching frames, let `G` be the union
of all matching edges.

* If `G` is disconnected, every target slice has a nonconstant
  degree-one occupancy harmonic fixed pointwise by the heat operator.
  Its spectral gap is zero.
* If `G` is connected, the common middle-owner overlap has one component.
  An integral component selector therefore chooses one whole frame
  globally.  Every such frame has the fixed-matching Gaussian
  occurrence-capacity deficit
  \[
  D_{m,q}=\sum_f(T_{f,q}-V_f)_+=\bigl(\delta_A+o(1)\bigr)W
  \quad(q=A\sqrt m+o(\sqrt m)),                     \tag{0.3}
  \]
  where `delta_A>0`.  Hence its simultaneous all-depth discrepancy is
  `Omega_A(W)`, not `o(W)`.

For the transposition catalogue, the union graph is the complete graph.
It therefore realizes both sides sharply: the fractional averaging
operator has the gap (0.2), while every integral component choice is
global and suffers (0.3).

Consequently no sparse-catalogue spectral argument can by itself round the
`S_n`-average within the perfect-matching status-cell/associator component
model.  The fractional statement controls

\[
             \sup_h\;\text{defect of the averaged operator on }h,
\]

whereas integral failure is witnessed by a harmonic/cut chosen after the
global frame is selected.  The maximum and the frame average cannot be
interchanged.

This is scoped to component choices generated by perfect-matching status
cells and their exact common-owner recouplings.  It does not rule out an
exterior-moving factor whose owner components are not the exclusion
components of the frame union graph.

## 1. Target harmonics and frame averaging

For `0<r<n`, put

\[
                         \mathcal V_r
                         =\mathbb R^{\binom{[n]}r}.  \tag{1.1}
\]

The symmetric group acts unitarily by

\[
                         (\rho_r(\sigma)f)(T)
                         =f(\sigma^{-1}T).           \tag{1.2}
\]

For a symmetric probability measure `mu` on `S_n`, define

\[
                         P_{\mu,r}
                         =\sum_{\sigma}\mu(\sigma)\rho_r(\sigma).
\tag{1.3}

Constants are fixed.  The degree-one harmonic space is

\[
 \mathcal H_1^{(r)}
 =\left\{T\mapsto\sum_{i\in T}a_i:\sum_i a_i=0\right\}.       \tag{1.4}
\]

It has dimension `n-1` and is the standard representation, independently
of `r`.  More generally

\[
                         \mathcal V_r
 =\bigoplus_{j=0}^{\min(r,n-r)}\mathcal H_j^{(r)}.  \tag{1.5}
\]

The full `S_n` average is the orthogonal projection onto constants.  This
is the representation-theoretic form of the uniform fractional target
load in the moving-frame master LP.

## 2. A locality bound on every frame heat

Let `mov(sigma)=|{i:sigma i ne i}|`.

### Theorem 2.1 (degree-one trace obstruction)

Suppose `mu` is symmetric and every permutation in its support moves at
most `s` coordinates.  On every nontrivial slice,

\[
 \lambda_{\max}\left(P_{\mu,r}|_{\mathcal H_1^{(r)}}\right)
 \ge1-\frac{s}{n-1}.                                \tag{2.1}
\]

Hence the spectral gap of `P_(mu,r)` on nonconstant harmonics is at most
`s/(n-1)`, simultaneously for all `r`.

#### Proof

The character of the standard representation at `sigma` is

\[
                         \chi_{\rm std}(\sigma)
                         =\operatorname {fix}(\sigma)-1.      \tag{2.2}
\]

Therefore

\[
 \operatorname {tr}\left(P_{\mu,r}|_{\mathcal H_1^{(r)}}\right)
 =\mathbb E_\mu\operatorname {fix}(\sigma)-1
 \ge n-s-1.                                         \tag{2.3}
\]

The operator is self-adjoint because `mu(sigma)=mu(sigma^{-1})`.  Its
largest eigenvalue is at least its average eigenvalue, which is (2.1).
Since `mathcal H_1^(r)` is contained in the nonconstant space, the full
gap cannot be larger.  \(\square\)

In particular, bounded `B_4` or bounded-coordinate frame moves have only
`O(1/m)` one-step gap.  At least `Omega(m/s)` sequential heat layers are
necessary for constant contraction of the first target harmonic.

## 3. Exact spectrum of the transposition catalogue

Let `mu_tr` be uniform on the `binom(n,2)` transpositions.  Its support is
polynomial in `n` and every column is a whole coordinate conjugate of the
base legal atlas.

### Theorem 3.1 (simultaneous Johnson spectrum)

On `mathcal H_j^(r)`,

\[
                         P_{\mu_{\rm tr},r}
                         =\lambda_j I,
 \qquad
 \lambda_j=1-\frac{j(n-j+1)}{\binom n2}.            \tag{3.1}
\]

Consequently (0.2) is the exact nonconstant spectral gap on every slice.

#### Proof

For `T in binom([n],r)`, a transposition with both endpoints in `T` or
both outside `T` fixes `T`; a crossing transposition replaces one member
of `T` by one nonmember.  Hence

\[
 P_{\mu_{\rm tr},r}
 ={1\over\binom n2}
 \left[\left(\binom n2-r(n-r)\right)I+A_r\right],  \tag{3.2}
\]

where `A_r` is the adjacency operator of the Johnson graph `J(n,r)`.

The standard up-operator decomposition of the Boolean slice gives

\[
                         A_r|_{\mathcal H_j^{(r)}}
 =\bigl((r-j)(n-r-j)-j\bigr)I.                     \tag{3.3}
\]

For completeness, take a harmonic function `g` on the `j`-sets and lift
it by

\[
                         (U_{j,r}g)(T)
                         =\sum_{Z\subseteq T,|Z|=j}g(Z).       \tag{3.4}
\]

In `A_rU_(j,r)g`, first choose the removed element of `T` and the inserted
element outside `T`, then count whether a fixed `j`-set survives.  The
terms retaining it contribute `(r-j)(n-r-j)`, while the harmonic
zero-sum relation among its one-point replacements contributes `-j`.
This proves (3.3); these lifted harmonic spaces give the orthogonal
decomposition (1.5).

Substitute (3.3) into (3.2).  Since

\[
 r(n-r)-\bigl((r-j)(n-r-j)-j\bigr)=j(n-j+1),        \tag{3.5}
\]

we obtain (3.1).  Among `j>=1`, the largest eigenvalue is `lambda_1`, and

\[
                         1-\lambda_1
                         ={n\over\binom n2}={2\over n-1}.
\]

All eigenvalues in the relevant range are nonnegative, so this is also
the absolute spectral gap.  \(\square\)

After `t` sequential transposition layers, every nonconstant target mode
is contracted in `L^2` by at most

\[
                         \left(1-{2\over n-1}\right)^t
                         \le e^{-2t/(n-1)},          \tag{3.6}
\]

simultaneously at every signed depth.

## 4. Exact component structure of a matching-frame catalogue

Let `mathscr M` be any family of perfect matchings of `[n]`, and let `G`
be the graph whose edge set is their union.  A status-cell move exchanges
the occupied endpoint along one matching edge.  Let `C_1,...,C_b` be the
connected components of `G`.

### Theorem 4.1 (exclusion-component classification)

The common overlap components on the middle layer are exactly

\[
 \mathcal U_{\boldsymbol\kappa}
 =\left\{X\in\binom{[n]}m:
       |X\cap C_a|=\kappa_a\ (1\le a\le b)\right\}. \tag{4.1}
\]

#### Proof

Every elementary exchange stays inside one `C_a`, so all displayed
occupancies are invariant.  Conversely, on a connected graph the
`k`-particle exclusion graph is connected.  To see this, fix a spanning
tree and a canonical `k`-set.  Starting at the leaves, move occupied
vertices along tree edges toward their canonical positions, never changing
the particle count.  Apply this independently in every component `C_a`.
\(\square\)

### Corollary 4.2 (fixed harmonics or one global component)

If `b>1`, then for every target rank `0<r<n` and every nontrivial component
`C_a`,

\[
                         h_{a,r}(T)
 =|T\cap C_a|-{r|C_a|\over n}                     \tag{4.2}
\]

is a nonconstant degree-one harmonic fixed by every edge-generated frame
heat.  Hence the component heat has spectral gap zero.

If `b=1`, (4.1) is the whole middle layer.  An integral componentwise
selector has exactly one choice variable and must choose one whole frame
option globally.

#### Proof

When `b>1`, every edge transposition preserves each `C_a`, hence fixes
`h_(a,r)` pointwise.  When `b=1`, Theorem 4.1 leaves only the occupancy
vector `(m)`.  \(\square\)

## 5. The connected case has a state-adaptive Hall obstruction

Suppose now that `G` is connected and that every legal integral option is
one exact factor internal to one selected perfect matching `M`.  Let

\[
\begin{aligned}
 V_f&={m!\over f!^2(m-2f)!}\,2^{m-2f},\\
 T_{f,q}&={m!\over f!(f+q)!(m-2f-q)!}\,2^{m-2f-q}.
\end{aligned}                                        \tag{5.1}
\]

Here `V_f` is the number of middle owners with `f` full and `f` empty
`M`-pairs, and `T_(f,q)` is the number of signed rank-`m-q` targets in the
corresponding status class.  One internal factor supplies at most `V_f`
occurrences to those targets.  Therefore either sign has deficit at least

\[
                         D_{m,q}=\sum_f(T_{f,q}-V_f)_+.         \tag{5.2}
\]

The exact Stirling/local-central-limit calculation gives, for
`q=A sqrt(m)+o(sqrt(m))`,

\[
 {D_{m,q}\over W}\longrightarrow
 \delta_A:=e^{-A^2}\Phi(A/2)-\Phi(-3A/2)>0.          \tag{5.3}
\]

For reference, (5.3) follows by writing

\[
 {T_{f,q}\over V_f}
 =2^{-q}{f!\over(f+q)!}{(m-2f)!\over(m-2f-q)!},     \tag{5.4}
\]

putting `f=m/4+(sqrt(m)/4)z`, using Stirling uniformly on
`|z|<=m^(1/10)`, and summing the positive part against the limiting
standard-normal law of `(4f-m)/sqrt(m)`.  The inequality in the positive
part becomes `-3A/2<z<A/2`; integration gives (5.3).  Outside this window
the hypergeometric Chernoff bound is `o(W)`.  Positivity follows because
the integrand represented by `(T/V-1)_+` is positive on a nonempty
interval.

Thus every integral global frame misses `Omega_A(W)` targets at one
protected depth.  The witness may depend on the selected `M`; this is
precisely why a fractional spectral gap against each fixed harmonic does
not round the frame average.

## 6. The transposition catalogue realizes the conflict explicitly

Fix one base perfect matching `M_0` and take the frame catalogue

\[
                         \mathscr F_{\rm tr}
 =\{\tau M_0:\tau\text{ a transposition}\}.         \tag{6.1}

It has `binom(n,2)` named integral frames.  Its frame-averaging operator on
every target slice is exactly the operator in Theorem 3.1 applied to the
base load vector.

Its matching-edge union is the complete graph.  Indeed, if `{a,b}` is not
already an edge of `M_0`, let `a'` be the mate of `a`; the transposition
`(a' b)` sends `{a,a'}` to `{a,b}`.  Hence Theorem 4.1 gives one global
middle-owner component.

Every integral component choice selects one member of (6.1) globally, and
Section 5 gives it target deficit `(delta_A+o(1))W`.  On the other hand,
the fractional operator has the simultaneous gap `2/(n-1)` and its powers
converge to the uniform `S_n` average.

This proves the advertised max--average mismatch:

\[
 \sup_h\mathbb E_{\tau}\operatorname {Defect}(\tau M_0,h)
 \quad\hbox{can be small, while}\quad
 \mathbb E_{\tau}\sup_h\operatorname {Defect}(\tau M_0,h)
 \ge(\delta_A-o(1))W.                               \tag{6.2}
\]

No spectral estimate which takes the frame average before the supremum
can remove this state-adaptive integral cut.

## 7. Exact boundary

Within the perfect-matching status-cell/associator component model, the
requested conjunction is impossible:

1. disconnected frame union gives an explicit fixed nonconstant harmonic
   of eigenvalue one;
2. connected frame union gives one global integral component and every
   outcome has a Gaussian `Omega_A(W)` target deficit;
3. the polynomial transposition catalogue already has the exact
   simultaneous harmonic gap, so enlarging the fractional catalogue does
   not address the integral obstruction; and
4. bounded-coordinate frame moves have one-step gap only `O(1/m)`.

This does not obstruct arbitrary whole-route columns or exterior-moving
components.  A positive theorem must break the exclusion-component
classification (4.1), or correlate several global frames inside one
legal integral route before taking target shadows.  Ordinary component
heat, independent integral shore choices, and spectral averaging of frame
labels cannot do so.
