# The universal-shadow Catalan rotor target

Date: 2026-07-27

## 1. The global pattern left by the k=11 optimum

Let

\[
n=2m+1,
\qquad r=m+1,
\qquad W=\binom nr,
\qquad C_m=W/n.
\]

When `n` is prime, translation by `1` acts freely on every nontrivial layer
of the Boolean lattice.  Hence the rank-`r` layer has exactly `C_m`
translation orbits.

The exact `k=11` middle chronology is generated by a base block

\[
B_0,B_1,\ldots,B_{C_5-1}
\]

and one nonzero voltage `v=2`:

\[
T_{C_5t+j}=B_j+2t.
\tag{1.1}
\]

The base block visits every middle necklace once, and the lift is one cycle

## 2. Definition

A **Catalan rotor** is a cyclic sequence

\[
B=(B_0,\ldots,B_{C_m-1}),
\qquad |B_i|=m+1,
\]

such that:

1. the `B_i` represent all translation orbits of rank-`m+1` sets;
2. consecutive sets differ by one deletion and one insertion, up to a
   translation on the closing edge;
3. the accumulated closing translation `v` is nonzero modulo `n`.

For prime `n`, condition 3 makes the translated lift one Hamilton cycle of
the rank-`m+1` Johnson graph.

For a lifted cycle `T`, define

\[
L_i^{(q)}=\bigcap_{h=0}^{q}T_{i+h},
\qquad
U_i^{(q)}=\bigcup_{h=0}^{q}T_{i+h}.
\tag{2.1}
\]

The rotor is **universal-shadow** when, for every admissible `q`,

\[
\{L_i^{(q)}:|L_i^{(q)}|=r-q\}
=\binom{[n]}{r-q},
\tag{2.2}
\]

and

\[
\{U_i^{(q)}:|U_i^{(q)}|=r+q\}
=\binom{[n]}{r+q}.
\tag{2.3}
\]

For a deadline `d`, it is **d-resident** if an inserted coordinate is never
deleted in the next `d` transitions.  This implies that every window through
depth `d` has the expected intersection and union ranks.

## 3. Quotient arithmetic

At depth `q`, the number of target translation orbits is

\[
N_q^{\rm orb}={1\over n}\binom n{r-q}
              ={1\over n}\binom n{r+q-1},
\]

on the lower side, with the complementary expression on the upper side.
The rotor supplies `C_m` cyclic windows at every depth.  Therefore the mean
orbit load is exactly the original layer ratio

\[
\lambda_q={C_m\over N_q^{\rm orb}}
          ={W\over\binom n{r-q}}.
\tag{3.1}
\]

Translation quotienting does not create extra capacity; it spends phase
freedom to make every hit automatically uniform over its orbit.  The
universal-shadow property is therefore a genuine correlated covering design,
not a counting consequence.

At depth one the lower condition is exact: every rank-`m` target occurs once.
Equivalently, interleaving the intersections gives a Hamilton cycle of the
middle-levels graph.  The upper depth-one mean is

\[
{W\over\binom n{m+2}}={m+2\over m}=1+{2\over m}.
\]

The strongest finite normal form uses only loads one and two, with exactly

\[
W-\binom n{m+2}
\]

double targets.

## 5. The construction conjecture

The finite evidence motivates the following deliberately strong target.

> **Universal-shadow rotor conjecture.**  For every sufficiently large odd
> prime `n=2m+1`, there is a Catalan rotor whose lift is `d(n)`-resident and
> universal-shadow, where `d(n)` is the deadline from the exact lower bound.

This conjecture alone does not yet imply `nu(n)=B(n)`: after cutting the
cycle, the intermediate lower witnesses must be chosen compatibly with the
coordinate pins and the low-target sandwich graph must satisfy Hall.  With
that compiler condition added, Theorem 4.1 of
`MATH_EXACT_CENTRAL_COMPILER_THEOREM_20260727.md` gives the exact formula.

The next finite test is `n=13`.  Here

\[

\qquad d(13)=3,
\]

The bounded h3 follow-up did not prove either existence or nonexistence.

global `k=13` inference.

## 6. Proof routes that remain compatible with the data

1. **Recursive necklace rotor.**  Build the `C_m` quotient chronology by a
   Catalan/Dyck recursion while maintaining shadow orbit coverage as a global
   invariant.
2. **Exact design plus cycle joining.**  First choose a cap-controlled
   two-sided 2-factor by an absorption theorem, then merge quotient cycles
   with trades that preserve every already-covered shadow orbit.
3. **Carrier and deterministic correction.**  Use a middle-levels Hamilton
   rotor as a carrier and solve the shadow constraints by globally coupled
   switches; local independent repair is ruled out by the earlier capacity
   audits.

A successful proof must be global.  The exact `k=11` selector is deliberately
generic at the local level, while its full 42-step chronology is perfectly
organized across every rank.
