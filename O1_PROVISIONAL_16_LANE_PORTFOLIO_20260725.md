# Final sixteen-lane O(1) portfolio

Date: 2026-07-25

This note began as a selection analysis and was finalized after all thirty
proof-body audits landed.  It uses the
completed proof audit in `O1_30_THREAD_FULL_PROOF_AUDIT_20260725.md` and the
independent product-box, heat/switching, marked-gap, and O/P/S/V audits.  The

only after their complete proof bodies and fragile dependencies were checked.

## 1. Selection rule

A retained lane should satisfy all four tests below.

1. **Soundness:** its latest proof body has been checked, with every final
   implication still labelled conditional when its terminal lemma is open.
2. **End-to-end relevance:** the surviving gate preserves the structure that
   the coefficient-one proof actually needs: one exact integral factor or one
   literal global word, one common chronology across depths, and an additive
   rather than merely fractional error estimate.
3. **Quantifier survival:** the gate is not already killed by a theorem for
   the same quantifier order.  In particular, a fixed or prescribed overlay
   is not a substitute for an adaptive `for every F there exists tau` theorem.
4. **Portfolio nonredundancy:** when several lanes share a mechanism, retain
   the strongest live positive endpoint and merge completed no-go lemmas into
   it.  A no-go-only lane occupies a slot only if it excludes a broad natural
   seed or architecture not already excluded by a retained lane.

The purpose of a sixteen-task cap is therefore not to keep the sixteen most
impressive theorem files.  All theorem files remain in the permanent ledger.
It is to keep sixteen mathematically different next moves alive.

Only I provisionally occupies a slot.  It is the sole all-dimensional

## 4. Cross-lane chronology and consistency

### I versus N

I's independently completed insertion--erasure induction proves

\[
M_1(F_m^{\rm MSW})\ge (1/16-o(1))W
\]

and the row-distance lower bound
\((1/8-o(1))\operatorname{Cat}_m\).  N's earlier statement that this
bound was unsupported is chronologically obsolete.  No theorem in N uses
that rejection.  The two lanes are consistent: I rules out the canonical
seed and sparse repairs; N seeks a genuinely adaptive global orientation.

The same insertion--erasure certificates now have a sharper
fixed-transposition consequence.  Localizing them to targets fixed by
\((2\ 3)\) gives

\[
 M_1^{(2\,3)\text{-fix}}(F_m^{\rm MSW})
 \ge(m-3)\operatorname{Cat}_{m-2}-{2W\over m+2}
 =\left({1\over32}-o(1)\right)W.
\]

These holes are frozen at every corner of the **complete** canonical
\((2\ 3)\)-component cube.  Thus that whole fixed fibre, not merely the
private size-two atlas or the `3/8` suffix tail, is now closed for an
`o(W)` first shadow.  The proof is
`MATH_THEOREM_MSW_23_FIXED_HOLE_FLOOR_20260725.md`; adaptive recomputation
with a new bridge remains outside its scope.

### C, J, M, AB, and L

On a fixed Gaussian window, the unhalved floor energy used by C/J/M and the
factorial energy used by AB are uniformly equivalent because \(c_q=O_A(1)\).
AB7's locked high-energy cell therefore refutes J's FCE premise and every
universal fixed/prescribed-transposition energy-dependent gap.  It does not
refute:

- C's `for every F, locally minimal under every tau` endpoint;
- an adaptive `for every F there exists tau` selector;
- AB6's recomputed multibridge schedule from a special endpoint;
- L's chronology-sensitive higher-harmonic/reduced-cut program.

M's parity--Johnson identity remains useful, but its fixed-tau lane should be
merged into C/AB rather than kept.  There is no contradiction among retained
C, L, and AB once the quantifiers are stated correctly.

### F, H, T, U, and X

F's endpoint dual concerns one parent (even with ambient letters), while H
explicitly seeks global endpoint sharing across parents.  T closes confined

X's decisive intervals may cross packet seams, escaping its own
independent-packet lower bound.  Therefore F/T do not refute H/U/X; they are
constraints that specify what those live routes must exploit.

## 5. Admission and replacement rules for W, Y, Z, AA, and AC

A pending lane enters the final sixteen only after its full proof body is
verified and only if it passes the structural tests in Section 1.

### W: correlated physical-atom rounding

Admit W if it proves an additive-\(o(W)\) rounding or a genuinely sharper
correlated-atom gate that shares initialization and preserves one literal
chronology across the Gaussian window.  A fixed-uniformity nibble, fractional
cover, or isolated-reset atom theorem does not qualify.  A successful W would
normally replace **I** first; if it subsumes nonproduct packet mechanics, it
replaces **X** rather than occupying a duplicate slot.

### Y: stationary completion and spill

Admit Y if its stationary completion theorem produces one exact factor with
quantitative \(o(W)\) weighted spill, or proves O's missing singular-boundary
existence statement.  If it is only a spill identity or another conditional
stationary law, merge it into O/AD.  If it strictly subsumes O, retain Y in
place of O rather than retaining both.

### Z: exchange closure and corridor augmentation

Admit Z at high priority if it gives adaptive or multistep exchange closure
from an arbitrary exact factor with a quantitative capture/augmentation
bound.  A theorem for a prescribed overlay is already blocked by AB7.  If Z
subsumes packet rebundling, replace the weakest of **B**, **G**, or the
subsumed member of **K/R**; do not remove AB unless Z also subsumes AB6 and
AB7 with the same quantifiers.

### AA: exact-ownership signing and holonomy

Admit AA only if its holonomy invariant or signing theorem applies to the
surviving adaptive/all-transposition/multistep exact-factor problem.  A
fixed-cell or prescribed-sign no-go duplicates C/M/AB and should be merged
and archived.  If AA proves a strictly stronger adaptive structural theorem,
it may replace **C**; otherwise it should not consume a slot.

### AC: Graver/semigroup augmentation

Admit AC if its absolute augmentation gate is genuinely positive and
quantitative on the exact-factor fibre, rather than merely exposing
semigroup holes or assuming normality.  AC then replaces **B**, whose endpoint
is the same Graver/packet family.  A no-go-only AC report should be merged
into B/AB and archived.

### Default displacement order

For a sound pending lane that is live and genuinely nonredundant, the default
release order is

\[
\boxed{I\;\longrightarrow\;G\;\longrightarrow\;B}
\]

followed by the specifically subsumed member of X, R, K, C, or O.  This order
protects fifteen distinct positive routes before preserving a completed
diagnostic task.  It is not a claim that I's theorem is weaker mathematics;
it is a rule for allocating scarce active exploration slots.

nonredundant conditional routes.  AA and AC are sound but architecture-scoped
no-go/closure lanes.  Applying the predeclared displacement order

\[
 I\longrightarrow G\longrightarrow B
\]

therefore gives the final active portfolio

\[
\boxed{\{A,C,H,K,L,N,O,R,S,U,W,X,Y,Z,AB,AD\}}.

The fourteen lanes to archive are

\[
\boxed{\{B,D,E,F,G,I,J,M,P,Q,T,V,AA,AC\}}.

This is an active-task allocation decision, not a repudiation of the archived

important canonical-factor obstruction.  Every verified theorem file and all
