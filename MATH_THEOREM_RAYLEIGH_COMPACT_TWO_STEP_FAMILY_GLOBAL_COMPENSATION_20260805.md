# Global compensation in the compact two-step shoulder family

**Date:** 2026-08-05  
**Method:** pure mathematics; an exact numerical-semigroup Bellman clock,
triangular reindexing, and a dominated Riemann-sum limit; no computation,
enumeration, search, or solver  
**Status:** unconditional global closure of the explicit compact family from
`MATH_THEOREM_RAYLEIGH_COMPACT_CRITICAL_TWO_STEP_SHOULDER_OBSTRUCTION_20260805.md`
for all sufficiently large parameters.  Although that family has a genuine
positive-measure adverse derivative-prefix layer, its complete physical
shoulder is asymptotically positive of order `H`.  This proves that the local
adversity is paid by other layers in the same exact Bellman table.  It does
not close the compact shoulder for arbitrary tables.

## 1. The exact Bellman clock

Let `K` be the Rayleigh signed-tail kernel, let `zeta` be its unique
minimum, and, for an integer `H>=5`, put

\[
 N=2H,
 \qquad
 L_H=\zeta\left(1-{2\over H}\right),
 \qquad
 \lambda_H={L_H\over H},
 \qquad
 L'_H=(H+1)\lambda_H.
\tag{1.1}
\]

Use the saturated table

\[
 c_j=
 \begin{cases}
 0,&0\le j<H,\\
 L_H,&j=H,\\
 \lambda_H(H+1),&H+1\le j<2H,\\
 2L_H,&j=2H.
 \end{cases}
\tag{1.2}
\]

The cited compact two-step theorem proves that this is a nonnegative,
nondecreasing, internally superadditive, saturated first-`zeta` table, that
its complete critical set is `{H,H+1,2H}`, and that its formal clock is

\[
                         U_m=\lambda_Hm.          \tag{1.3}
\]

Let `V` be the physical Bellman clock of (1.2).

### Theorem 1.1 (semigroup-floor formula)

Write

\[
                         m=qH+r,
 \qquad q\ge0,
 \qquad0\le r<H.
\]

Then

\[
 \boxed{
 U_m-V_m=\lambda_H(r-q)_+.}
\tag{1.4}
\]

Equivalently, after dividing by `lambda_H`, the physical clock is the
largest member of the numerical semigroup

\[
                         \langle H,H+1\rangle
\]

not exceeding `m`.

#### Proof

The normalized generator cost `a_j=j-c_j/lambda_H` is

\[
 a_j=
 \begin{cases}
 j,&j<H,\\
 0,&j=H,\\
 j-(H+1),&H+1\le j<2H,\\
 0,&j=2H.
 \end{cases}
\tag{1.5}
\]

A generator `H+1+s`, `0<=s<H-1`, has the same normalized reward as one
zero-cost generator `H+1`, followed by `s` units of zero-reward filler.
Likewise every generator below `H` is pure filler, and `2H` is already in
the semigroup generated by `H`.  Therefore the least normalized cost of
an exact fill of `m` is

\[
 m-\max\{s\le m:s\in\langle H,H+1\rangle\}.       \tag{1.6}
\]

Every semigroup element has the form

\[
                         pH+b,
 \qquad p\ge b\ge0.                               \tag{1.7}
\]

If `r<=q`, then `m=qH+r` itself has the form (1.7).  If `r>q`, the
largest element with `p=q` is `qH+q`, while every element with
`p>=q+1` is at least `(q+1)H>m`.  Hence the distance in (1.6) is
`(r-q)_+`.  Restoring the factor `lambda_H` proves (1.4). `square`

## 2. Exact triangular shoulder

Put

\[
 \mathcal H_H:=\Phi(V)-\Phi(U)
 =\sum_{m\ge0}\bigl(K(V_m)-K(U_m)\bigr).          \tag{2.1}
\]

### Corollary 2.1 (triangular formula)

One has

\[
 \boxed{
 \mathcal H_H
 =\sum_{q=0}^{H-2}\sum_{s=1}^{H-q-1}
   \left[
    K(qL'_H)-K(qL'_H+s\lambda_H)
   \right].}
\tag{2.2}
\]

#### Proof

For `m=qH+r`, Theorem 1.1 says that the term changes precisely when
`q<r`.  In that case

\[
 V_m=\lambda_H(qH+q)=qL'_H,
 \qquad
 U_m=\lambda_H(qH+r).
\]

Write `r=q+s`.  The conditions `q<r<H` become
`0<=q<=H-2` and `1<=s<=H-q-1`, and the formal argument becomes
`qL'_H+s\\lambda_H`.  This is (2.2). `square`

Formula (2.2) displays the exact global compensation omitted by a
single-chain sign test.  Its `q=0` row lies wholly before the Rayleigh
minimum and is favorable.  The `q=1` row contains the adverse two-step
layers from the earlier theorem.  The later rows lie in the Gaussian
tail.

## 3. The scaled global limit

Recall the arithmetic-comb functional

\[
                         C(h)=\sum_{q=0}^{\infty}K(qh). \tag{3.1}
\]

The all-mesh arithmetic-comb theorem proves

\[
                         C(h)>0\qquad(h>0).       \tag{3.2}
\]

### Theorem 3.1 (global compensation limit)

For the exact physical family (1.2),

\[
 \boxed{
 \lim_{H\to\infty}{\mathcal H_H\over H}=C(\zeta)>0.}
\tag{3.3}
\]

Consequently

\[
                         \mathcal H_H>0           \tag{3.4}
\]

for every sufficiently large `H`, and hence

\[
                         \Phi(V)>\Phi(U)>0        \tag{3.5}
\]

throughout the tail of this compact critical family.

#### Proof

Fix `q`.  Since

\[
 L_H\longrightarrow\zeta,
 \qquad
 L'_H\longrightarrow\zeta,
 \qquad
 \lambda_H={L_H\over H},                         \tag{3.6}
\]

the `q`-th row of (2.2), divided by `H`, is a Riemann sum and satisfies

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 &{1\over H}\sum_{s=1}^{H-q-1}
 \left[K(qL'_H)-K(qL'_H+s\lambda_H)\right]\\
 &\hspace{30mm}\longrightarrow
 \int_0^1\left[K(q\zeta)-K((q+x)\zeta)\right]\,dx.
 \end{aligned}                                    \tag{3.7}
\]

The limit may be summed over `q`.  Indeed, for all sufficiently large
`H`, both `L_H` and `L'_H` are bounded below by `zeta/2`.  Once `q` is
larger than a fixed absolute constant, every argument in its row lies on
the outer Gaussian branch, and the absolute value of the normalized row
is bounded by

\[
 2\exp\left[-\left(A+{q\zeta\over2}\right)^2\right], \tag{3.8}
\]

where `A=sqrt(pi)/2`.  This is summable in `q`, uniformly in `H`; the
finitely many initial rows are bounded by continuity of `K`.  Dominated
convergence therefore gives

\[
 \lim_{H\to\infty}{\mathcal H_H\over H}
 =\sum_{q=0}^{\infty}K(q\zeta)
  -{1\over\zeta}\int_0^{\infty}K(u)\,du.          \tag{3.9}
\]

Let `R` have Rayleigh density `2r exp(-r^2)`, let
`A=E R`, and put

\[
 X=(A-R)_+,
 \qquad
 Y=(R-A)_+.
\]

The Rayleigh kernel is the signed-tail difference

\[
                         K(u)=P(X>u)-P(Y>u).
\]

Tail integration and centering give

\[
 \int_0^\infty K(u)\,du
 =E X-E Y
 =E(A-R)=0.                                      \tag{3.10}
\]

Equations (3.1), (3.9), and (3.10) prove that the limit is `C(zeta)`.
It is strictly positive by (3.2).  Therefore (3.4) holds for all
sufficiently large `H`.  The formal arithmetic clock has value
`Phi(U)=C(lambda_H)>0`, again by (3.2), proving (3.5). `square`

## 4. Exact implication and scope

The earlier compact theorem proves that a positive-measure subset of the
`q=1` shoulder layers is genuinely adverse.  Theorem 3.1 proves that the
other rows more than compensate: the net shoulder is eventually positive
and in fact grows linearly in `H` with limiting density `C(zeta)`.

Thus the explicit obstruction family is a sharp no-go only for a
**termwise** proof.  It is not asymptotically close to a negative clock.
It supplies a concrete benchmark for the live compact theorem:

> preserve the triangular/Pareto coupling strongly enough that the
> aggregate pre-minimum credit converges to an arithmetic-comb reserve,
> rather than trying to make every critical-chain prefix favorable.

Nothing here proves that an arbitrary saturated Bellman table admits the
triangular formula (2.2), or that its total compact shoulder is positive.
No universal all-price or Boolean-capacity claim is made.
