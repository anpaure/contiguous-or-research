# Multiframe packet hypergraphs: exact orbit degrees and the floor-balanced matching gate

Date: 2026-07-26

Method: pure mathematics only.  The valid recursive context-array factor is
used as an integral local state on an orientation packet.  No computation,
search, solver, web input, or crossed recursion is used.

## 0. Verdict

The connected-\(U\) quartet obstruction does **not** apply to an arbitrary
owner-disjoint choice of packets from several coordinate frames.  It applies
to a more restrictive variable: a component recoupling which must choose one
whole frame shore on every equivalence component generated by the status
cells.

The distinction is literal.  On \(J(4,2)\), the three packets

\[
\begin{aligned}
 \{12,13\},\qquad
 \{14,24\},\qquad
 \{23,34\}                                                   \tag{0.1}
\end{aligned}
\]

partition all six middle owners.  They belong respectively to frames

\[
 23|14,\qquad 12|34,\qquad 24|13,                            \tag{0.2}
\]

whose coordinate-edge union is \(K_4\), hence connected.  Thus a connected
frame union can admit a completely integral mixed-frame packet mosaic.
What fails is only the attempt to obtain (0.1) by choosing one whole shore
on the single common status component.

For the unrestricted orientation-packet hypergraph the exact scalar
statistics are favorable.  A packet is

\[
 {\cal P}(F,M)
 =\{F\cup S:S\text{ chooses one endpoint of every edge of }M\}, \tag{0.3}
\]

where \(|F|=m-r\), \(M\) is an \(r\)-matching disjoint from \(F\), and
\(n=2m\).  It has \(R=2^r\) owners.  The unindexed packet hypergraph has

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
 |\mathscr P_r|
   &={ (2m)!\over (m-r)!^2\,2^r r!},\\
 D
   &=\binom mr^2r!,\\
 D_j
   &=j!(r-j)!\binom{m-j}{r-j}^{\!2}\qquad(1\le j\le r),\\
 {D_j\over D}
   &={\binom rj\over\binom mj^2}.
\end{aligned}}                                                \tag{0.4}
\]

Here \(D_j\) is the codegree of two owners at Johnson distance \(j\);
it is zero for \(j>r\).  If \(r\le m/4\), then

\[
                         {\Delta_2\over D}={r\over m^2}.         \tag{0.5}
\]

For a recursive isometric \(2r\)-necklace rather than the whole cube,
the full indexed \(S_{2m}\)-orbit has owner degree

\[
                         D_{\rm cyc}=2r(m!)^2                   \tag{0.6}
\]

and normalized pair codegree

\[
 {D_{{\rm cyc},j}\over D_{\rm cyc}}
 =
 \begin{cases}
 2/\binom mj^2,&1\le j<r,\\
 1/\binom mr^2,&j=r,\\
 0,&j>r.
 \end{cases}                                                   \tag{0.7}
\]

These owner statistics prove that the old connected-component obstruction
cannot simply be transferred to the unrestricted packet hypergraph.  They
do not prove the augmented all-depth matching theorem.

The exact augmented gate is a **floor-balanced, target-anticorrelated packet
matching**.  If an owner-disjoint packet family covers \(S\) owners and
has target loads \(Z_{j,T}\) on a signed depth \(j\), put

\[
 c_j=\left\lfloor{S\over N_j}\right\rfloor,\qquad
 Q_j=\sum_T(Z_{j,T}-c_j)(Z_{j,T}-c_j-1).                       \tag{0.8}
\]

Then \(Q_j\ge0\), and simultaneously over all signed depths

\[
\boxed{
 \sum_j\#\{T:Z_{j,T}=0\}
 \le \sum_j(N_j-S)_+ +{1\over2}\sum_jQ_j.}                     \tag{0.9}
\]

If the owner leave is \(L=W-S\), the first term is at most \(2HL\).
Thus

\[
                         2HL+\sum_jQ_j=o(W)                    \tag{0.10}
\]

is a rigorous sufficient condition for the desired near-perfect augmented
cover.

There is an exact pair-kernel formulation of (0.10).  Let
\(A_j(P)\) be the packet's distinct depth-\(j\) targets and set

\[
                         K_j(P,P')=|A_j(P)\cap A_j(P')|.         \tag{0.11}
\]

For any owner matching \({\cal I}\),

\[
 Q_j({\cal I})
 =\sum_{\substack{P,P'\in{\cal I}\\P\ne P'}}K_j(P,P')
   -2c_jS+N_jc_j(c_j+1).                                      \tag{0.12}
\]

Consequently a distribution on fixed-size owner matchings closes the outer
gate if its joint selection probabilities \(\pi_{PP'}\) satisfy, in total
over all protected signed depths,

\[
\boxed{
 \sum_j\left[
 \sum_{P\ne P'}K_j(P,P')\pi_{PP'}
 -\bigl(2c_jS-N_jc_j(c_j+1)\bigr)
 \right]=o(W),}                                               \tag{0.13}
\]

together with \(HL=o(W)\).  Every bracket is nonnegative after expectation,
so no cancellation between depths is being hidden.

Ordinary pairwise pseudorandomness is not enough.  At the uniform
fractional orbit point, let the target load be
\(\lambda=W/N=c+\theta\), \(0\le\theta<1\), and let the owner degree be
\(D\).  A joint law which is asymptotically independent in the kernel
\(K_j\) has expected floor energy

\[
                         N(c+\theta^2)-{W\over D}+o(W)
                         =\Omega(W),                            \tag{0.14}
\]

not \(o(W)\).  Successful rounding needs a negative target-overlap
covariance of this same linear order.  Small owner codegrees, expansion,
or a spectral gap do not supply that covariance.

Thus the precise remaining constructive statement is no longer a
connectedness question:

> Construct a common all-depth distribution, or one deterministic member,
> of the multiframe packet-matching hypergraph which has owner leave
> \(o(W/H)\) and satisfies the floor-collision inequality (0.13).

This theorem neither proves nor disproves that statement.  It proves that
the connected-\(U\) no-go cannot exclude it, computes the full owner
incidence geometry, and identifies the exact second-moment property which
would suffice.

## 1. Literal orientation packets

Let

\[
                         \Omega=\binom{[2m]}m,\qquad
                         W=|\Omega|.                            \tag{1.1}
\]

Fix \(1\le r\le m\).  Choose

* a frozen-one set \(F\subset[2m]\) of size \(m-r\);
* an \(r\)-edge matching \(M\) whose \(2r\) endpoints avoid \(F\).

The remaining set

\[
                         E=[2m]\setminus(F\cup V(M))             \tag{1.2}
\]

also has size \(m-r\).  Define \({\cal P}(F,M)\) by (0.3).  Equivalently,
its Boolean indicators satisfy

\[
\begin{aligned}
 x_v&=1 &&(v\in F),\\
 x_v&=0 &&(v\in E),\\
 x_u\oplus x_v&=1 &&(\{u,v\}\in M).
\end{aligned}                                                   \tag{1.3}
\]

Therefore every member has rank \(m\), the \(r\) pair orientations are
free, and

\[
                         |{\cal P}(F,M)|=2^r.                    \tag{1.4}
\]

It is a literal physical orientation cube \(Q_r\).  Any valid recursive
context-array factor installed on this cube is an integral *state* of the
packet; it does not change the owner set (0.3).

### Proposition 1.1 (exact intersection test)

Let \({\cal P}(F,M)\) and \({\cal P}(F',M')\) be two packets.  Form the
graph \(G=M\cup M'\), and pin a vertex to \(1\) if it lies in \(F\cup F'\)
and to \(0\) if it lies in \(E\cup E'\).

The packets meet if and only if:

1. no vertex receives both pin values; and
2. in every component of \(G\), the parity of the graph distance between
   any two pinned vertices equals the xor of their pin values.

If they meet, then

\[
 |{\cal P}(F,M)\cap{\cal P}(F',M')|=2^{u},                     \tag{1.5}
\]

where \(u\) is the number of components of \(G\) containing no pinned
vertex.

#### Proof

Every edge of \(G\) imposes the equation that its endpoint bits are
opposite.  The union of two matchings has maximum degree two, and every
cycle alternates between \(M\) and \(M'\), hence is even.  Thus an
un-pinned component has exactly two complementary solutions.  A pinned
component has one solution precisely when all its pins have the parity
required by path length.  Components are independent, proving both the
criterion and (1.5). \(\square\)

This criterion, not connectivity of the union of the physical frame
matchings, is the literal disjointness law in the arbitrary packet
hypergraph.

## 2. Exact owner degrees and codegrees

Let \(\mathscr P_r\) be the simple hypergraph on \(\Omega\) containing
every packet (0.3) once.

### Theorem 2.1 (packet census)

The edge count, degree, and distance-\(j\) codegrees are exactly (0.4).

#### Proof

Choose \(F\), then the \(2r\) active vertices, then their perfect matching:

\[
\begin{aligned}
 |\mathscr P_r|
 &=\binom{2m}{m-r}\binom{m+r}{2r}{(2r)!\over2^rr!}\\
 &={ (2m)!\over (m-r)!^2\,2^rr!}.                            \tag{2.1}
\end{aligned}
\]

For a fixed owner \(X\), choose the \(r\) active vertices lying in \(X\),
the \(r\) active vertices outside \(X\), and a bijection between them.
This gives

\[
                         D=\binom mr^2r!.                       \tag{2.2}
\]

Now let \(X,Y\) have Johnson distance \(j\), so

\[
 |X\setminus Y|=|Y\setminus X|=j.                              \tag{2.3}
\]

In a common packet, every vertex of \(X\setminus Y\) must be paired with
one vertex of \(Y\setminus X\), giving \(j!\) choices.  Choose another
\(r-j\) active vertices from \(X\cap Y\), another \(r-j\) from
\([2m]\setminus(X\cup Y)\), and biject those two sets.  Hence

\[
 D_j=j!\binom{m-j}{r-j}^2(r-j)!                                \tag{2.4}
\]

for \(j\le r\), and no common packet exists for \(j>r\).  Finally,

\[
\begin{aligned}
 {D_j\over D}
 &={j!(r-j)!\over r!}
   \left({\binom{m-j}{r-j}\over\binom mr}\right)^2\\
 &={\binom rj\over\binom mj^2},                                \tag{2.5}
\end{aligned}
\]

as claimed. \(\square\)

For \(1\le j<r\), the ratio of consecutive normalized codegrees is

\[
 {D_{j+1}/D\over D_j/D}
 ={(r-j)(j+1)\over(m-j)^2}.                                    \tag{2.6}
\]

If \(r\le m/4\), this is less than one.  Thus the largest nontrivial
codegree is at \(j=1\), proving (0.5).

The rank \(R=2^r\) grows.  In the mesoscopic regime \(r=\Theta(\sqrt m)\),

\[
                         R^2{\Delta_2\over D}
                         =4^r{r\over m^2}\longrightarrow\infty. \tag{2.7}
\]

Thus a fixed-uniformity slogan of the form
\(\Delta_2=o(D)\) is not, without a uniform-in-\(R\) theorem, an augmented
matching proof.  Equation (2.7) is a limitation of that proof method, not
an owner-matching obstruction: one fixed frame already gives an
owner-near-factor outside the exponentially small low-split tail.

## 3. Recursive necklace subpackets

Let \(C\) be an isometric \(2r\)-cycle inside one packet.  Its direction
word uses each of the \(r\) active pairs once and then repeats the same
order.  From a fixed cycle owner there are exactly

\[
 a_j=
 \begin{cases}
 2,&1\le j<r,\\
 1,&j=r
 \end{cases}                                                   \tag{3.1}
\]

other cycle owners at Johnson distance \(j\).

Take the full **indexed** coordinate orbit
\(\{\sigma C:\sigma\in S_{2m}\}\), retaining repeated physical cycles with
their indices.  Double counting owner incidences gives

\[
 D_{\rm cyc}={(2m)!\,2r\over W}=2r(m!)^2.                       \tag{3.2}
\]

For an ordered pair \((X,Y)\) at distance \(j\), double count
\((\sigma,U,V)\) with \(U,V\in C\), \(d(U,V)=j\), and
\((\sigma U,\sigma V)=(X,Y)\).  There are
\(W\binom mj^2\) ordered central pairs at distance \(j\).  Therefore

\[
                         {D_{{\rm cyc},j}\over D_{\rm cyc}}
                         ={a_j\over\binom mj^2},                 \tag{3.3}
\]

which is (0.7).

The cube and necklace formulas differ for a reason: a cube owner has
\(\binom rj\) packet partners at distance \(j\), while a necklace owner
has only the \(a_j\) chronological partners in (3.1).

## 4. Why connected status support does not lock packet mosaics

We verify (0.1)--(0.2).  In \(J(4,2)\), set

\[
\begin{aligned}
 P_1&={\cal P}(\{1\},\{\{2,3\}\})=\{12,13\},\\
 P_2&={\cal P}(\{4\},\{\{1,2\}\})=\{14,24\},\\
 P_3&={\cal P}(\{3\},\{\{2,4\}\})=\{23,34\}.                    \tag{4.1}
\end{aligned}
\]

They are disjoint and exhaust \(\binom{[4]}2\).  Extend their active
matchings to the three pair frames

\[
\begin{aligned}
 M_1&=\{23,14\},\\
 M_2&=\{12,34\},\\
 M_3&=\{24,13\}.                                               \tag{4.2}
\end{aligned}
\]

Their edge union is \(K_4\).  Hence the equivalence relation generated by
all three complete frame-status cells has one middle-layer class.
Nevertheless (4.1) is an exact mixed-frame owner partition.

There is no contradiction.  A common-component recoupling variable must
choose one entire frame partition on that class.  The packet hypergraph
instead has one variable for every individual cell and imposes only that
chosen cells be disjoint.  The mosaic (4.1) is not a whole-shore choice.

Therefore:

\[
\boxed{
\begin{array}{c}
\text{connected coordinate union}\\
\not\Longrightarrow
\text{one global frame in an arbitrary packet matching}.
\end{array}}                                                  \tag{4.3}
\]

Any use of the connected-\(U\) theorem against the unrestricted packet
hypergraph must first prove an additional physical law forcing every legal
packet selection to be constant on the generated status component.  The
valid local context-array theorem supplies no such law.

## 5. General decorated-orbit degrees and codegrees

The owner formulas above extend exactly to every target decoration.
Let \(\Gamma=S_{2m}\), let

\[
                         {\cal V}=\dot\bigcup_{a\in A}{\cal V}_a \tag{5.1}
\]

be a disjoint union of transitive \(\Gamma\)-resource orbits, and put
\(N_a=|{\cal V}_a|\).  The orbits may be the middle owners and all typed
lower and upper target layers.

Let \(B\subset{\cal V}\) be one decorated packet state, with

\[
                         b_a=|B\cap{\cal V}_a|.                   \tag{5.2}
\]

Take the full indexed orbit \(B_\sigma=\sigma B\),
\(\sigma\in\Gamma\).

### Theorem 5.1 (indexed orbit census)

Every resource \(v\in{\cal V}_a\) has indexed degree

\[
                         D_a={|\Gamma|b_a\over N_a}.             \tag{5.3}
\]

For two resource orbits \({\cal V}_a,{\cal V}_b\), let
\(\omega\) be one diagonal-\(\Gamma\) orbit of ordered pairs in
\({\cal V}_a\times{\cal V}_b\), of size \(N_\omega\).  Let

\[
 b_\omega
 =|\{(u,v)\in(B\cap{\cal V}_a)\times(B\cap{\cal V}_b):
                                      (u,v)\in\omega\}|.         \tag{5.4}
\]

Every fixed ordered pair in \(\omega\) has indexed codegree

\[
                         D_\omega={|\Gamma|b_\omega\over N_\omega}.
                                                                    \tag{5.5}
\]

If physically identical indexed packets are quotiented by a common
stabilizer, every number in (5.3)--(5.5) is divided by the stabilizer
order; all normalized ratios are unchanged.

#### Proof

There are \(|\Gamma|b_a\) pairs \((\sigma,u)\) with
\(u\in B\cap{\cal V}_a\).  Their images \(\sigma u\) are equidistributed
over the transitive orbit \({\cal V}_a\), proving (5.3).

Likewise there are \(|\Gamma|b_\omega\) triples
\((\sigma,u,v)\) whose base ordered pair lies in \(\omega\).  Their image
pairs are equidistributed over the diagonal orbit \(\omega\), proving
(5.5). \(\square\)

Thus every augmented codegree question reduces exactly to the internal
two-resource orbit census \(b_\omega\) of one valid packet.  Owner
codegrees alone are only the owner--owner part of this table.  In
particular they contain no information about owner--target or
target--target overlap kernels.

## 6. The necessary balance before augmented matching

Suppose one tries to treat every resource in (5.1) as a matching vertex.
If \(L\) pairwise disjoint decorated packets cover every resource exactly
once, then necessarily

\[
                         Lb_a=N_a\qquad(a\in A).                  \tag{6.1}
\]

Hence

\[
                         {b_a\over N_a}
                         \quad\text{must be independent of }a.  \tag{6.2}
\]

The same conclusion, up to the declared relative errors, is necessary for
a simultaneous near-perfect augmented matching.

For an unthinned context-array packet, let \(R\) be its owner count.  It
has \(R\) distinct targets at each protected signed depth.  Thus

\[
 {b_{\rm own}\over N_{\rm own}}={R\over W},\qquad
 {b_{q,\epsilon}\over N_{q,\epsilon}}={R\over N_q},             \tag{6.3}
\]

and these are unequal for \(q>0\).  Therefore the unthinned decorated
packet orbit cannot have an augmented perfect matching.  This is the
conservation obstruction behind the forced target surplus.

There are two legitimate normalizations.

1. Keep all target occurrences, require only target coverage, and send the
   surplus to dump variables.
2. Mark an accepted subcollection of target claims so that, in aggregate,
   a packet has
   \[
                         b_{q,\epsilon}={RN_q\over W}             \tag{6.4}
   \]
   claims, using a rational multicover or adjacent integer packet types
   when (6.4) is not integral.

Only after (6.4), or an equivalent radius thinning, is an ordinary
augmented matching theorem dimensionally possible.

## 7. A sufficient floor-balanced packet-matching theorem

We return to the natural cover-with-surplus formulation.  Let
\({\cal I}\) be an owner-disjoint family of integral packet states which
covers exactly \(S\) owners.  At each signed depth \(j\), assume the valid
local trace map is injective inside every packet, and write

\[
                         A_j(P)\subseteq{\cal T}_j,\qquad
                         |A_j(P)|=|P|.                           \tag{7.1}
\]

Define

\[
 Z_{j,T}=\sum_{P\in{\cal I}}{\bf1}_{\{T\in A_j(P)\}},\qquad
                         \sum_TZ_{j,T}=S.                        \tag{7.2}
\]

Let \(c_j,Q_j\) be as in (0.8).

### Theorem 7.1 (floor collision implies coverage)

For every signed depth,

\[
 \#\{T:Z_{j,T}=0\}
 \le (N_j-S)_+ +{Q_j\over2}.                                    \tag{7.3}
\]

Consequently (0.9)--(0.10) hold.

#### Proof

Every summand

\[
                         (z-c_j)(z-c_j-1)                       \tag{7.4}
\]

is nonnegative for integral \(z\).  If \(c_j\ge1\), a zero load
contributes \(c_j(c_j+1)\ge2\), giving the stronger bound
\(\#\{Z=0\}\le Q_j/[c_j(c_j+1)]\).

If \(c_j=0\), then

\[
                         Q_j=\sum_TZ_{j,T}(Z_{j,T}-1).           \tag{7.5}
\]

Writing \(U_j=\#\{T:Z_{j,T}>0\}\), one has

\[
 S-U_j=\sum_{Z_{j,T}>0}(Z_{j,T}-1)
 \le {Q_j\over2}.                                               \tag{7.6}
\]

Therefore

\[
 N_j-U_j=(N_j-S)+(S-U_j)
 \le(N_j-S)_+ +Q_j/2.
\]

This proves (7.3).  Since \(N_j\le W\) and \(S=W-L\),
\((N_j-S)_+\le L\).  There are \(2H\) signed depths, proving (0.9)--(0.10).
\(\square\)

Now define the target-overlap kernel (0.11).  Local injectivity gives the
exact ordered-pair identity

\[
\begin{aligned}
 \sum_TZ_{j,T}(Z_{j,T}-1)
 &=\sum_{\substack{P,P'\in{\cal I}\\P\ne P'}}
                         K_j(P,P').                             \tag{7.7}
\end{aligned}
\]

Since

\[
 Q_j=\sum_TZ_{j,T}(Z_{j,T}-1)-2c_jS+N_jc_j(c_j+1),             \tag{7.8}
\]

equations (0.12)--(0.13) follow.

### Corollary 7.2 (probabilistic sufficient condition)

Suppose a probability law is supported on owner-disjoint packet families
covering a fixed number \(S=W-L\) of owners.  Let

\[
 \pi_{PP'}=\Pr(P,P'\in{\cal I}).                                \tag{7.9}
\]

If \(HL=o(W)\) and (0.13) holds, then some member of the support has total
signed-depth target deficit \(o(W)\).

#### Proof

By (7.7)--(7.8), (0.13) is exactly
\(\sum_j\mathbb E Q_j=o(W)\).  The random variable
\(2HL+\sum_jQ_j\) is nonnegative, so one outcome is at most its
expectation.  Apply Theorem 7.1. \(\square\)

This is a genuine all-depth statement: the law chooses one common packet
family, and the sum in (0.13) uses its joint probabilities at every depth.

## 8. Why near-independent packet rounding is fatal

Take one transitive indexed packet orbit with uniform owner degree \(D\),
and give every packet the fractional weight

\[
                         x_P={1\over D}.                          \tag{8.1}
\]

At one signed depth, the full frame average has constant target load

\[
                         \lambda={W\over N}=c+\theta,
 \qquad c=\lfloor\lambda\rfloor,\quad0\le\theta<1.               \tag{8.2}
\]

Thus every target lies in exactly \(\lambda D\) indexed packets, with the
usual interpretation that indexed orbit counts make this integer.

The independent fractional pair kernel is exactly

\[
\begin{aligned}
 \sum_{P\ne P'}K(P,P')x_Px_{P'}
 &=\sum_T\left[
       \left(\sum_{P:T\in A(P)}x_P\right)^2
       -\sum_{P:T\in A(P)}x_P^2\right]\\
 &=N\lambda^2-{W\over D}.                                      \tag{8.3}
\end{aligned}
\]

Indeed the first square is \(\lambda^2\) at every target, while the total
diagonal contribution is

\[
 \sum_P|A(P)|x_P^2={1\over D}\sum_P|A(P)|x_P={W\over D}.         \tag{8.4}
\]

Subtract the floor collision baseline from (7.8):

\[
\begin{aligned}
 &N\lambda^2-{W\over D}-2cW+Nc(c+1)\\
 &\hspace{25mm}=N(c+\theta^2)-{W\over D}.                       \tag{8.5}
\end{aligned}
\]

For \(q=A\sqrt m+O(1)\), one has

\[
                         \lambda={W\over N}=e^{A^2+o(1)},       \tag{8.6}
\]

so (8.5) is \(\Omega_A(W)\) whenever \(D\to\infty\).  The same conclusion
holds at every depth with \(\lambda\) bounded away from infinity; in
particular it already holds at depth one.

Therefore a packet-matching law satisfying only an aggregate
near-independence estimate

\[
 \sum_{P\ne P'}K(P,P')\pi_{PP'}
 =(1+o(1))\sum_{P\ne P'}K(P,P')x_Px_{P'}                     \tag{8.7}
\]

has linear expected floor energy.  To satisfy (0.13), its covariance must
instead obey

\[
\boxed{
 \sum_{P\ne P'}K(P,P')
        \bigl(\pi_{PP'}-x_Px_{P'}\bigr)
 \le -N(c+\theta^2)+o(W),}                                     \tag{8.8}
\]

where the harmless \(W/D\) correction can be retained exactly.

This is the promised sharper obstruction.  The unrestricted packet
hypergraph has very small normalized **owner** codegrees, but a successful
matching must create a linear, specifically directed negative covariance
in every physical target-overlap kernel.  Generic nibble pseudorandomness
or spectral mixing tends toward (8.7), not (8.8).

## 9. Exact boundary

The following are proved.

1. The common-status connectedness theorem does not constrain arbitrary
   multiframe packet mosaics; the exact \(J(4,2)\) partition (4.1) is a
   counterexample with connected frame union.
2. The full orientation-packet hypergraph and its necklace suborbits have
   the exact owner degrees and codegrees (0.4)--(0.7).
3. Every decorated full orbit has the exact resource degree/codegree
   formulas (5.3)--(5.5).  Computing the local pair census
   \(b_\omega\) is sufficient to recover all augmented second moments.
4. Unthinned all-depth target claims are dimensionally incompatible with
   an augmented matching; accepted-claim or radius thinning is necessary.
5. The floor-collision condition (0.13) is a rigorous simultaneous all-q
   sufficient theorem.
6. Asymptotically independent packet selection leaves
   \(\Omega(W)\) floor energy.  The missing law must have the negative
   covariance (8.8).

Accordingly the next exact statement is:

> **Floor-balanced multiframe packet matching.**  On the accepted-claim
> or cover-with-dump packet catalogue, construct one owner-disjoint family
> with \(L=o(W/H)\) whose joint packet indicators satisfy (0.13), or prove
> a target-overlap cut which makes (0.13) impossible.

This is strictly finer than coordinate-union connectedness and strictly
stronger than owner-degree/codegree pseudorandomness.  No coefficient-one
conclusion is claimed without it.
