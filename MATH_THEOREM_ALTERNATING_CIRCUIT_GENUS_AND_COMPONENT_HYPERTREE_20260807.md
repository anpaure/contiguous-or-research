# Alternating-circuit genus, safe component hypertrees, and the exact supply gap

**Date:** 2026-08-07  
**Status:** unconditional topology theorem.  It gives an exact sufficient
certificate for Hamiltonizing a protected simple two-factor by arbitrary
alternating circuits, not only `C6`/`C8` circuits.  It does not prove that
the required component-diverse circuits occur in every Middle-Levels
factor.

## 1. One directed matching exchange

Let a simple bipartite two-factor be written as the disjoint union of two
perfect matchings.  After contracting the first matching, the second is a
permutation `sigma` of the contracted vertex set; its cycles are precisely
the components of the two-factor.

Let

\[
                         \tau=(v_1\ v_2\ \cdots\ v_t)             \tag{1.1}
\]

be a directed alternating matching circuit.  Assume every new incidence is
allowed, no vertex of its support is protected, and no new incidence is an
edge of the fixed first matching.  Toggling the circuit therefore leaves
two disjoint perfect matchings and hence another protected simple
two-factor.

Let `a` be the number of old `sigma`-cycles met by the support
`S={v_1,...,v_t}`.  On `S`, define `rho(v)` to be the next member of `S`
encountered while following the old `sigma`-cycle from `v`.  Thus

\[
                         c(\rho)=a.                              \tag{1.2}
\]

After the exchange, collapse every unchanged old fragment between
successive members of `S`.  The resulting fragment permutation is

\[
                         \rho\tau^{-1}.                          \tag{1.3}
\]

Write

\[
                         b=c(\rho\tau^{-1}).                     \tag{1.4}
\]

Then the exchange replaces the `a` touched old components by exactly `b`
new components.

## 2. The exact genus formula

### Theorem 2.1 (alternating-circuit genus)

There is a nonnegative integer `g` such that

\[
                         \boxed{b=t+1-a-2g}.                     \tag{2.1}
\]

In particular,

\[
                         b\le t+1-a,                             \tag{2.2}
\]

and every circuit satisfying

\[
                         a>{t+1\over2}                           \tag{2.3}
\]

strictly reduces the number of factor components.

If the circuit is component-transversal, meaning `a=t`, then `b=1`: it
merges all `t` touched components into one and creates no split.

#### Proof

The permutation `tau` is transitive on `S`, so the group generated by
`rho,tau` has one orbit.  The standard permutation Euler inequality gives

\[
 c(\rho)+c(\tau)+c(\rho\tau^{-1})\le t+2.
\tag{2.4}
\]

Since `c(tau)=1`, equations (1.2) and (1.4) give (2.2).  Taking signs in
(1.3) gives

\[
                         a+b\equiv t+1\pmod2.                    \tag{2.5}
\]

Therefore the nonnegative difference `t+1-a-b` is even; writing it as
`2g` proves (2.1).  Inequality (2.3) implies `b<a`.  If `a=t`, (2.2)
gives `b<=1`, while `b>=1`; hence `b=1`. \(\square\)

### Exact short-circuit cases

* A directed triangle (`C6`) meeting three components merges `3 -> 1`.
  If it meets exactly two components, it leaves the component count
  unchanged.  Thus `C6` exchanges preserve component-count parity.
* A directed four-cycle (`C8`) meeting four components merges `4 -> 1`;
  one meeting three components merges `3 -> 2`.  If it meets exactly two
  components, its two possible topological types are `2 -> 1` and
  `2 -> 3`.  The former is precisely the Hamilton-safe interlacing type.

The theorem also quantifies long circuits: length itself is not the issue.
The load-bearing quantity is the number of repeated old components on the
circuit support.

## 3. A complete safe multi-circuit certificate

Let the vertices of a hypergraph `K` be the components of an initial
protected two-factor.  For every hyperedge `e` of `K`, suppose there is an
allowed directed alternating circuit that uses exactly one vertex from
each component in `e`.  Suppose also that all circuits are physically
support-disjoint, or more generally that each remains allowed after every
earlier toggle.

Call `K` a **component hypertree** when its incidence bipartite graph is a
tree after isolated unused component vertices are deleted.

### Theorem 3.1 (protected component-hypertree Hamiltonization)

If `K` is a spanning component hypertree, toggling its circuits in any
leaf-to-root order produces a protected Hamilton cycle.  Every intermediate
factor is simple, and every toggle strictly reduces its number of
components by `|e|-1`.

#### Proof

Before the circuit for `e` is used, two initial components represented in
`e` cannot already lie in the same merged block.  Otherwise the previously
used hyperedges connecting them, together with `e`, would make a cycle in
the incidence graph of `K`.  Thus the circuit is transversal to the
current factor components.  Theorem 2.1 merges its `|e|` current components
into one and changes no other component.

The incidence tree is connected, so after every hyperedge is processed all
initial components lie in one block.  Equivalently,

\[
                         \sum_{e\in E(K)}(|e|-1)
                           =|V(K)|-1.                            \tag{3.1}
\]

All toggles avoid the protected set and the current fixed-matching edges by
hypothesis, so simplicity and protection hold at every step. \(\square\)

This is the exact multi-circuit replacement for a one-shot `C6/C8` lemma.
The parity row is transparent: a hypertree made only of three-component
`C6` hyperedges can span only an odd number of initial components; an even
number requires at least one even-cardinality circuit hyperedge.

## 4. Why the closed-shore bound alone cannot supply the hypertree

The factor-boundary theorem proves a strong necessary condition on every
out-closed shore of the free exchange digraph.  That condition, even
together with large semidegree, does not force a short or
component-diverse directed circuit.

For integers `L>=5` and `D>=1`, take the digraph

\[
                         R_{L,D}=\mathbb Z_L\times[D]             \tag{4.1}
\]

and put every possible arc from layer `i` to layer `i+1`.  This digraph is
strongly connected and `D`-in/`D`-out regular.  It has no nonempty proper
out-closed shore, so every inequality whose premise is out-closed is
vacuous.  Yet every directed cycle has length divisible by `L`; in
particular there is no directed triangle or four-cycle, and `L` can be
arbitrarily large.

Moreover, colouring all layers by two old-component labels shows that even
large semidegree and arbitrarily many factor-boundary crossings do not by
themselves force the diversity condition (2.3).  This is an abstract
countermodel to an inference from the cut theorem, not a claimed
Middle-Levels counterexample: the incidence geometry and the fact that the
colours arise from one actual factor permutation are additional structure.

## 5. Exact remaining Middle-Levels lemma

For a protected Middle-Levels factor, the topology problem is now exactly:

> Construct a dynamically safe family of component-transversal alternating
> circuits whose component supports contain a spanning hypertree.

It is enough to prove this for circuits of arbitrary length.  Insisting on
one `C6` or `C8` is stronger than necessary.  Conversely, strong matching
exchange connectivity and the linear factor-boundary theorem do not prove
the required coloured-circuit supply.

For the single sharp-pivot `B+1` route this general supply lemma is no
longer needed: the separate MSW protected-Hamilton theorem embeds that one
pivot directly.  The hypertree gate remains relevant to the full growing
PBBS packet bank.
