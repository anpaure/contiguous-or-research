# AC exploration: normality and holes of the decorated-wreath semigroup

## 0. Executive verdict

There are three different affine semigroups in this question, and they must
not be conflated.

1.  The **raw decorated-wreath semigroup** is generated by the simultaneous
    middle and lower-rank incidence vectors of individual wreaths.  On its
    exactly uniform ray there is an explicit arithmetic denominator
    \(\Delta_{m,H}\), every arithmetically admissible point is in the signed
    column lattice, and the all-wreath vector gives a positive point at
    degree
    \[
    d_0=\frac{m!(m+1)!}{2}.
    \]
    Thus every missing admissible point below degree \(d_0\) would be a
    genuine semigroup hole.  Already depth one forces the packet degree to
    be divisible by \(m/\gcd(m,2)\).  Exact targetwise-uniform packets are
    therefore necessarily mesoscopic.

2.  The **mobile floor/ceiling semigroup** lets the high-quota set vary.  Its
    degree-one target is always in both the rational cone and the integer
    column lattice, and its \(d_0\)-fold multiple is always positive.  Hence
    it has a finite balanced-ray saturation index
    \[
    1\le \sigma_{m,H}\le d_0.
    \]
    Degree one is present if and only if there is a perfectly balanced exact
    factor.  Consequently, if perfect balance fails, the degree-one target
    is a genuine hole, with an explicit saturation multiplier at most
    \(d_0\).  Proving normality of this semigroup would prove perfect balance,
    a theorem strictly stronger than MWB; normality is not a soft substitute
    for the missing positive theorem.

3.  The **exact-factorization semigroup** is generated by indicators of
    exact factors.  The primitive complete-wreath vector need not be known to
    factor, but \(2W\) copies always factor by taking the full coordinate
    orbit of any exact factor.  This gives finite lattice and positive
    resolution indices, both checkable by Smith form and a bounded integral
    feasibility problem.  It also gives the decisive no-gain theorem:
    refactoring the exactly balanced orbit multicover into factors of
    Catalan-scale defect is equivalent, with exactly the same constant, to
    the existence of one factor of Catalan-scale defect.

No explicit genuine hole for the unrestricted exact-wreath semigroups is
proved here.  What is proved is a finite list of exact hole candidates, an
explicit degree-divisibility congruence, an exact mesoscopic packet lower
bound, and
a complete separation between lattice obstruction, cone obstruction, and
positive-semigroup obstruction.  No finite or computational search is used.

## 1. Notation and the three positivity levels

Unless explicitly stated otherwise, assume
\[
m\ge3,\qquad 1\le H\le m-2.
\]
Put
\[
n=2m+1,\qquad
W=\binom{n}{m},\qquad
B=\frac{W}{n}=\operatorname{Cat}_m,
\]
and let \(\Omega=\Omega_m\) be the set of unoriented cyclic orders on
\([n]\).  Thus
\[
|\Omega|=\frac{(n-1)!}{2}.
\]
For \(0\le q\le H\), write
\[
r_q=m-q,\qquad
V_q=\binom{[n]}{r_q},\qquad
N_q=|V_q|=\binom{n}{m-q}.
\]
Let \(A_q\) be the \(V_q\)-by-\(\Omega\) cyclic-interval incidence
matrix:
\[
(A_q)_{S,C}=1
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
S\text{ is a cyclic }r_q\text{-interval of }C.
\]
Write \(A=A_0\) and \(B_q=A_q\) for \(q\ge1\).  Let \(U_r\) be the
point-versus-\(r\)-set incidence matrix.

An exact middle factor is a vector
\[
x_F\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{\Omega},\qquad Ax_F=\mathbf1_{V_0}.
\]
It is automatically a \(0/1\) vector: every wreath column contains at
least one middle interval, so any coefficient at least two would violate a
middle equation.  Summing the middle equations gives
\[
|F|=\mathbf1^Tx_F=\frac{W}{n}=B.
\]
Exact factors are known to exist for every \(m\) in the present problem.

For \(q\ge1\), write
\[
W=c_qN_q+\rho_q,\qquad
c_q=\left\lfloor\frac{W}{N_q}\right\rfloor,
\qquad 0\le\rho_q<N_q.
\]
For a factor \(F\), its depth-\(q\) load is
\[
\mu_q(F)=B_qx_F\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{V_q}.
\]
A floor/ceiling quota is \(c_q\mathbf1+\mathbf1_{H_q}\), where
\(H_q\subseteq V_q\) and \(|H_q|=\rho_q\).  Define
\[
O_q(F)=
\min_{|H_q|=\rho_q}
\sum_{S\in V_q}
\bigl(\mu_q(F;S)-c_q-\mathbf1_{H_q}(S)\bigr)_+,
\]
and
\[
J_H(F)=\sum_{q=1}^H\frac{O_q(F)}{c_q}.
\]
This is the true weighted unlabelled overload objective.  It does not encode
a common nested owner resolution, so a small value of \(J_H\) is MWB data,
not the stronger labelled synchronization theorem.

We use the following terminology throughout.

* A target is **arithmetically integral** if all of its displayed
  coordinates are integers.
* It is a **lattice point** if it lies in the integer group generated by the
  semigroup columns.
* It is a **cone point** if it lies in their nonnegative rational cone.
* It is a **semigroup point** if it has a nonnegative integral column
  representation.
* A **genuine hole** is a point in cone intersect lattice which is not in
  the semigroup.

A target outside the cone is not a hole, even if it is in the signed
lattice.  If one linear inequality separates it from the cone, the same
inequality separates every positive multiple, so it has no finite
saturation multiplier.

The signed-lattice input used below is the established simultaneous selector
saturation theorem
\[
\boxed{
(B_q)_{q=1}^H\bigl(\ker_{\mathbb Z}A\bigr)
=
\bigoplus_{q=1}^H\ker_{\mathbb Z}U_{r_q}.}
\tag{1.1}
\]
The selector theorem is naturally stated first for oriented cyclic orders
modulo rotation.  Write \(\Omega^+\) for that set and
\(\mathsf A_s^+,\mathsf A_s^{\mathrm{un}}\) for the oriented and
unoriented rank-\(s\) interval-incidence matrices.  Equation (1.1) holds
integrally in the present unoriented quotient as well.  Indeed, reversal has
no fixed oriented order for \(n\ge3\), and the two reversed columns have
identical interval incidence in every rank.  Given an oriented signed lift
\(y^+\), define its
unoriented coefficient on the reversal pair \([C]=\{C,C^{\mathrm{rev}}\}\)
by
\[
y_{[C]}=y_C^++y_{C^{\mathrm{rev}}}^+.
\]
Then
\[
\mathsf A_s^{\mathrm{un}}y=\mathsf A_s^+y^+
\]
in every rank \(s\).  Thus both the middle-kernel equation and all
prescribed lower images descend with integral coefficients and with no
factor of two.

All positivity conclusions below are proved separately; (1.1) alone never
supplies a nonnegative wreath vector.

## 2. The all-wreath multiplicities

### Theorem 2.1 — exact uniform incidence of all wreaths

For every \(0\le q\le H\) and every \(S\in V_q\), the number of
unoriented wreaths in which \(S\) is a cyclic \(r_q\)-interval is
\[
\boxed{
d_q=\frac{n!}{2N_q}
=\frac{(m-q)!(m+q+1)!}{2}.}
\tag{2.1}
\]
In particular,
\[
\boxed{
d_0=\frac{n!}{2W}=\frac{m!(m+1)!}{2},
\qquad
\frac{d_q}{d_0}=\frac{W}{N_q}.}
\tag{2.2}
\]
If \(u=\mathbf1_\Omega\), then
\[
Au=d_0\mathbf1_{V_0},\qquad
B_qu=d_q\mathbf1_{V_q}.
\tag{2.3}
\]

#### Proof

Every unoriented cyclic order has exactly \(n\) cyclic intervals in every
proper rank.  The action of \(S_n\) is transitive on \(V_q\).  Double
counting pairs \((C,S)\) with \(S\) a cyclic \(r_q\)-interval of \(C\)
therefore gives
\[
N_qd_q=|\Omega|n=\frac{(n-1)!}{2}n=\frac{n!}{2}.
\]
This proves the first equality in (2.1); the factorial formula and (2.2)
follow immediately.  Equation (2.3) is the coordinate form of the same
count. \(\square\)

Thus the complete wreath system is a positive, exactly uniform middle
multicover of degree \(d_0\), simultaneously uniform at every lower rank.
This is the basic finite saturation witness.

## 3. The raw decorated-wreath incidence semigroup

Define
\[
\mathcal S^{\mathrm{raw}}_{m,H}
=
\left\{
(Ax,B_1x,\ldots,B_Hx):x\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{\Omega}
\right\}.
\tag{3.1}
\]
For an integer \(t\ge0\), the exactly uniform degree-\(t\) target is
\[
\upsilon_H(t)=
\left(
t\mathbf1_{V_0},
t\frac{W}{N_1}\mathbf1_{V_1},\ldots,
t\frac{W}{N_H}\mathbf1_{V_H}
\right).
\tag{3.2}
\]
Membership means that one nonnegative integral wreath multiset covers every
middle set exactly \(t\) times and is exactly uniform at all displayed lower
ranks.  At \(t=1\), the middle equation forces the multiset to be one exact
factor.  At \(t>1\), it need not split into \(t\) exact factors; that is the
separate colored IDP problem of Section 6.

Set
\[
\lambda_q=\frac{W}{N_q}
=\prod_{j=1}^q\frac{m+1+j}{m+1-j}
\tag{3.3}
\]
and define the exact ambient denominator
\[
\boxed{
\Delta_{m,H}
=\operatorname{lcm}_{1\le q\le H}
\operatorname{den}(\lambda_q)
=\operatorname{lcm}_{1\le q\le H}
\frac{N_q}{\gcd(N_q,W)}.}
\tag{3.4}
\]
Here every denominator is taken after reduction.

### Theorem 3.1 — arithmetic, lattice, cone, and positive status on the uniform ray

Assume \(1\le H\le m-2\).  Then:

1. \(\upsilon_H(t)\) is integral if and only if
   \(\Delta_{m,H}\mid t\).
2. For every \(t\ge0\), the rational vector in (3.2) lies in the rational
   cone of the raw columns.
3. For every arithmetically admissible \(t\), \(\upsilon_H(t)\) lies in the
   integer column lattice of the raw columns.
4. \(\Delta_{m,H}\mid d_0\), and
   \(\upsilon_H(d_0)\in\mathcal S^{\mathrm{raw}}_{m,H}\), witnessed by
   \(x=u\).

Consequently the raw uniform-ray saturation index
\[
\tau_{m,H}
=\min\{t\ge1:\upsilon_H(t)\in
\mathcal S^{\mathrm{raw}}_{m,H}\}
\tag{3.5}
\]
is finite and obeys
\[
\boxed{\Delta_{m,H}\mid\tau_{m,H},\qquad
\tau_{m,H}\le d_0.}
\tag{3.6}
\]
Every admissible \(t\) for which \(\upsilon_H(t)\) is absent from the
semigroup is a genuine hole.  In particular, if
\(\tau_{m,H}>\Delta_{m,H}\), then
\(\upsilon_H(\Delta_{m,H})\) is a genuine hole.

#### Proof

Part 1 is exactly the definition of the least common denominator.

For Part 2, take the constant nonnegative rational coefficient vector
\[
x=\frac{t}{d_0}u.
\]
Equations (2.2)--(2.3) give (3.2).

For Part 3, fix any exact factor \(F\).  Begin with the signed integral
vector \(t x_F\), which has the required middle incidence.  Define
\[
z_q=t\lambda_q\mathbf1_{V_q}-tB_qx_F.
\tag{3.7}
\]
It is integral by admissibility.  Its total is zero because every wreath has
\(n\) intervals in rank \(r_q\), whence
\[
\mathbf1^Tz_q
=t(N_q\lambda_q-\mathbf1^TB_qx_F)
=t(W-W)=0.
\]
Its point margins also vanish.  A point lies in exactly \(r_q\) of the
\(n\) cyclic \(r_q\)-intervals of one wreath, so
\[
U_{r_q}B_qx_F=r_qB\mathbf1_{[n]}.
\]
On the other hand,
\[
U_{r_q}(\lambda_q\mathbf1_{V_q})
=\lambda_q\binom{n-1}{r_q-1}\mathbf1
=\frac{W}{N_q}\frac{r_qN_q}{n}\mathbf1
=r_qB\mathbf1.
\]
Consequently
\[
U_{r_q}z_q=t(r_qB-r_qB)\mathbf1=0.
\]
Thus \(z_q\in\ker_{\mathbb Z}U_{r_q}\) for every \(q\).  The simultaneous
saturation theorem (1.1) supplies \(y\in\ker_{\mathbb Z}A\) with
\(B_qy=z_q\) for every \(q\).  Then \(tx_F+y\) is a signed integral raw
column representation of \(\upsilon_H(t)\).

Finally, (2.2) gives \(d_0\lambda_q=d_q\in\mathbb Z\), so
\(\Delta_{m,H}\mid d_0\), and (2.3) gives the claimed nonnegative integral
witness at \(d_0\).  Every semigroup target is integral, so Part 1 also
gives \(\Delta_{m,H}\mid\tau_{m,H}\).  The hole statements now follow
directly from the definitions. \(\square\)

The value of \(\tau_{m,H}\) is finite and checkable without any asymptotic
interpretation: test only the multiples of \(\Delta_{m,H}\) up to \(d_0\).
In every such system, \(0\le x_C\le t\), since any middle equation
corresponding to an interval of \(C\) contains the nonnegative term \(x_C\).

### Corollary 3.2 — an exact mesoscopic packet lower bound

If \(H\ge1\), every exactly uniform integral packet has degree divisible by
\[
\operatorname{den}\!\left(\frac{W}{N_1}\right)
=\operatorname{den}\!\left(\frac{m+2}{m}\right)
=\boxed{\frac{m}{\gcd(m,2)}}.
\tag{3.8}
\]
Hence an exactly targetwise-uniform packet has degree at least \(m/2\), and
at least \(m\) when \(m\) is odd.

This is a genuine structural obstruction to bounded-degree
symmetry-retaining packets.  It is not an MWB obstruction: a degree-one
factor is allowed to use a nonuniform floor/ceiling pattern, and those high
quota locations break the exact symmetry.

### Proposition 3.3 — the invariant witness has sharp degree

Among integral raw solutions whose wreath coefficient vector is invariant
under all coordinate relabellings, \(d_0\) is the least positive middle
degree.

#### Proof

The action of \(S_n\) is transitive on \(\Omega\), so invariance forces
\(x_C=a\) for one integer \(a\ge0\) and every \(C\).  Then
\(Ax=ad_0\mathbf1\).  The least positive possibility is \(a=1\), of degree
\(d_0\). \(\square\)

This sharpness concerns invariant witnesses only.  It does not assert
\(\tau_{m,H}=d_0\); a noninvariant positive packet may occur earlier.

## 4. The mobile floor/ceiling decorated semigroup

The exactly uniform ray is too rigid for MWB.  The correct zero-overload
system lets each target receive either its floor or its ceiling quota.

For a degree \(t\), introduce nonnegative variables
\[
x\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{\Omega},\qquad
h_q,s_q\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{V_q},
\]
and impose
\[
\begin{aligned}
Ax&=t\mathbf1_{V_0},\\
B_qx-h_q&=tc_q\mathbf1_{V_q} &&(1\le q\le H),\\
h_q+s_q&=t\mathbf1_{V_q} &&(1\le q\le H).
\end{aligned}
\tag{4.1}
\]
Let \(\mathcal M_{m,H}\) denote the integer matrix on the left of (4.1),
let \(b_{m,H}\) be the right-hand side at \(t=1\), and let
\[
\mathcal S^{\mathrm{mob}}_{m,H}
=\mathcal M_{m,H}\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{\Omega+2\sum_qN_q}.
\tag{4.2}
\]
Summing the second equations and using the middle equation shows
automatically that
\[
\mathbf1^Th_q=t\rho_q.
\tag{4.3}
\]
Indeed, \(n\mathbf1^Tx=tW\), so
\(\mathbf1^TB_qx=n\mathbf1^Tx=tW\), and the second equation gives
\(t(W-c_qN_q)=t\rho_q\).

For \(t>1\), this is again an uncolored \(t\)-fold middle cover.  It does not
assert a decomposition into \(t\) exact factors with separate high-quota
vectors.  Thus \(\sigma_{m,H}\) is a ray saturation index, not a colored
factor-resolution index.

### Lemma 4.1 — degree one is exact perfect balance

The target \(b_{m,H}\) is in \(\mathcal S^{\mathrm{mob}}_{m,H}\) if and
only if there is an exact factor \(F\) satisfying
\[
\mu_q(F;S)\in\{c_q,c_q+1\}
\quad\text{for every }q\le H\text{ and }S\in V_q.
\tag{4.4}
\]

#### Proof

At degree one, \(Ax=\mathbf1\) makes \(x\) a \(0/1\) exact-factor
indicator.  The equation \(h_q+s_q=\mathbf1\) makes \(h_q\) a \(0/1\)
vector.  The second equation says that the load is \(c_q+h_q(S)\).  The
converse uses these same variables. \(\square\)

### Theorem 4.2 — explicit finite saturation of the mobile target

For every \(m\) and \(H\) in the stated range:

1. \(b_{m,H}\) lies in the rational cone of \(\mathcal M_{m,H}\).
2. \(b_{m,H}\) lies in the integer column lattice of
   \(\mathcal M_{m,H}\).
3. \(d_0b_{m,H}\in\mathcal S^{\mathrm{mob}}_{m,H}\).

Therefore the balanced-ray saturation index
\[
\sigma_{m,H}
=\min\{t\ge1:tb_{m,H}\in
\mathcal S^{\mathrm{mob}}_{m,H}\}
\tag{4.5}
\]
is finite and satisfies
\[
\boxed{1\le\sigma_{m,H}\le d_0.}
\tag{4.6}
\]
Moreover,
\[
\boxed{
\sigma_{m,H}=1
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\text{a perfectly floor/ceiling-balanced exact factor exists}.}
\tag{4.7}
\]
If \(\sigma_{m,H}>1\), then \(b_{m,H}\) is a genuine affine-semigroup
hole.

#### Proof

For cone membership take
\[
x=\frac1{d_0}u,\qquad
h_q=\frac{\rho_q}{N_q}\mathbf1,
\qquad
s_q=\left(1-\frac{\rho_q}{N_q}\right)\mathbf1.
\tag{4.8}
\]
Since \(d_q/d_0=W/N_q=c_q+\rho_q/N_q\), equations (4.1) hold at
\(t=1\).

For lattice membership, take any exact factor \(F\) and set, now allowing
the auxiliary variables to be signed,
\[
x=x_F,\qquad
h_q=B_qx_F-c_q\mathbf1,
\qquad
s_q=\mathbf1-h_q.
\tag{4.9}
\]
These are integral and solve the degree-one equations.

For positive membership at degree \(d_0\), define
\[
\eta_q=d_q-c_qd_0
=d_0\frac{\rho_q}{N_q}.
\tag{4.10}
\]
Both \(d_q\) and \(c_qd_0\) are integers, so \(\eta_q\) is integral;
also \(0\le\eta_q<d_0\).  Take
\[
x=u,\qquad
h_q=\eta_q\mathbf1,
\qquad
s_q=(d_0-\eta_q)\mathbf1.
\tag{4.11}
\]
Equations (2.3) and (4.10) verify (4.1).  The remaining claims follow from
Lemma 4.1 and the definition of a hole. \(\square\)

### Corollary 4.3 — what normality would actually prove

If \(\mathcal S^{\mathrm{mob}}_{m,H}\) is normal relative to its integer
column lattice, then \(\sigma_{m,H}=1\), so a perfectly balanced exact
factor exists.

The converse is false as a logical implication: membership of one target
does not establish normality of the whole semigroup.  More importantly,
perfect balance is stronger than MWB, which only asks for
\(J_H(F)=o(W)\) in the appropriate windows.  Thus a global normality proof
would solve a stronger problem, not bypass the positive support issue.

### Proposition 4.4 — finite checkability

For fixed \((m,H)\), \(\sigma_{m,H}\) is determined by the finite family of
integer systems (4.1) for \(1\le t\le d_0\).  Every variable in those
systems is bounded:
\[
0\le x_C\le t,\qquad 0\le h_{q,S},s_{q,S}\le t.
\tag{4.12}
\]

#### Proof

For a fixed wreath \(C\), choose any middle interval \(M\) of \(C\).  The
nonnegative middle equation at \(M\) contains \(x_C\), so \(x_C\le t\).
The other bounds follow from \(h_q+s_q=t\mathbf1\).  Theorem 4.2 bounds the
range of \(t\). \(\square\)

This is a finite, checkable hole certificate: a proof that all systems with
\(t=1\) fail, together with (4.8), (4.9), and (4.11), would be an explicit
genuine hole certificate.  No such failure certificate is presently known
in general.

## 5. A general finite dilation exponent

The special multiplier \(d_0\) is much sharper than a generic semigroup
bound, but it is useful to record that finite integer matrices always have a
finite uniform dilation exponent.

### Proposition 5.1 — determinant saturation bound

Let \(M\) be an integer matrix of rank \(R>0\).  Retain \(R\) independent
rows, and let
\[
\mathfrak D(M)=
\operatorname{lcm}{\,|\det Q|:
Q\text{ is a nonsingular }R\times R
\text{ column submatrix of the retained matrix}\,\}.
\tag{5.1}
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{
\mathfrak D(M)
\bigl(\operatorname{cone}(M)\cap\operatorname{gp}(M)\bigr)
\subseteq M\mathbb Z_{\ge0}.}
\tag{5.2}
\]

#### Proof

Let \(z\in\operatorname{cone}(M)\cap\operatorname{gp}(M)\).  Choose a
conic representation of \(z\) with
minimal support.  Its support columns are linearly independent; extend them
to a column basis \(Q\) of the retained row space.  In the resulting basis
representation, the extra coefficients are zero and all coefficients are
nonnegative.  Since the retained coordinates of \(z\) are integral,
Cramer's rule shows that every coefficient denominator divides
\(|\det Q|\).  Multiplication by \(\mathfrak D(M)\) therefore makes all
coefficients nonnegative integers.  Equality on the retained independent
rows implies equality on every row of \(M\). \(\square\)

For the mobile matrix, a wreath column has squared norm \(n(H+1)\), an
\(h\)-column has squared norm \(2\), and an \(s\)-column has squared norm
\(1\).  Hadamard's inequality therefore gives the completely explicit but
enormous bound
\[
|\det Q|\le[n(H+1)]^{R/2},
\qquad
\mathfrak D(\mathcal M_{m,H})
\le\left\lceil[n(H+1)]^{R/2}\right\rceil!.
\tag{5.3}
\]
This proves finite saturation in a global sense but has no useful
Catalan-scale content.  On the balanced ray, Theorem 4.2 replaces it by the
exact explicit multiplier \(d_0\).

## 6. The exact-factorization semigroup and primitive IDP

Let \(\mathfrak F_m\) be the finite set of all exact factors and define
\[
\mathcal R_m
=\left\{
\sum_{F\in\mathfrak F_m}z_Fx_F:
z_F\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}
\right\}
\subseteq\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{\Omega}.
\tag{6.1}
\]
For the literal polytope/IDP formulation, homogenize:
\[
\widehat{\mathcal R}_m
=\mathbb N\{(1,x_F):F\in\mathfrak F_m\}.
\tag{6.1a}
\]
Projection \((t,x)\mapsto x\) is an integral isomorphism from
\(\widehat{\mathcal R}_m\), its group, and its cone onto
\(\mathcal R_m\), its group, and its cone, respectively.  Indeed every
such pair satisfies
\[
Ax=t\mathbf1,
\]
so \(t\) is recovered integrally from \(x\).  Thus the unhomogenized
normality language below is equivalent to the standard homogenized
polytope language.  In particular, \(ku\in\mathcal R_m\) corresponds to
\((kd_0,ku)\in\widehat{\mathcal R}_m\).

Membership of \(ku\) means that \(k\) copies of every wreath can be
partitioned into exact factors.  For \(k=1\), this is a large-set or
resolution property: the complete wreath system is partitioned into exactly
\[
\frac{|\Omega|}{B}=d_0
\tag{6.2}
\]
exact factors.

### Theorem 6.1 — universal orbit identity

For every exact factor \(F\),
\[
\boxed{
\sum_{\sigma\in S_n}x_{\sigma F}=2W\,u.}
\tag{6.3}
\]
Consequently,
\[
\boxed{2W\,u\in\mathcal R_m,}
\tag{6.4}
\]
and this factorization contains \(n!=2Wd_0\) factors, counted with
multiplicity.  The vector \(2Wu\) is simultaneously exactly uniform at
every lower rank:
\[
B_q(2Wu)=2Wd_q\mathbf1_{V_q}.
\tag{6.5}
\]

#### Proof

The left side of (6.3) is invariant under \(S_n\), which acts transitively
on \(\Omega\), so it is a constant multiple of \(u\).  Its total
coefficient sum is \(n!|F|=n!B\).  Dividing by
\(|\Omega|=(n-1)!/2\) gives the constant
\[
\frac{n!B}{(n-1)!/2}=2nB=2W.
\]
Equation (6.5) follows from (2.3). \(\square\)

Dividing (6.3) by \(n!=2Wd_0\) also gives
\[
\frac1{n!}\sum_{\sigma\in S_n}x_{\sigma F}=\frac{u}{d_0},
\tag{6.5a}
\]
so \(u/d_0\) is the universal barycenter of the exact-factor polytope.

If \(g_F=|\operatorname{Stab}_{S_n}(F)|\), summing only over distinct
factors in the orbit divides (6.3) by \(g_F\):
\[
\sum_{G\in S_nF}x_G=\frac{2W}{g_F}u.
\tag{6.6}
\]
In particular \(g_F\mid2W\).

### Definition 6.2 — positive and lattice resolution indices

Define
\[
\rho_m=\min\{k\ge1:ku\in\mathcal R_m\}
\tag{6.7}
\]
and let \(\ell_m\) be the positive generator of the ray intersection
\[
\operatorname{gp}(\mathcal R_m)\cap\mathbb Ru
=\ell_m\mathbb Zu.
\tag{6.8}
\]
The factor-resolution index \(\rho_m\) is unrelated to the rankwise quota
remainders \(\rho_q\).

The orbit identity gives
\[
\boxed{
\ell_m\mid\rho_m,qquad
\ell_m\mid2W,qquad
\rho_m\le
\min_{F\in\mathfrak F_m}\frac{2W}{g_F}
\le2W.}
\tag{6.9}
\]

The finite alternatives are exact.

* If \(\ell_m>1\), failure of \(u\) is a lattice congruence, not a hole.
* If \(\ell_m=1<\rho_m\), then \(u\) is a genuine hole of
  \(\mathcal R_m\).
* More generally, if \(\ell_mu\notin\mathcal R_m\), then \(\ell_mu\) is
  the primitive genuine hole on this ray.
* Normality of \(\mathcal R_m\) forces \(\rho_m=\ell_m\), not necessarily
  \(\rho_m=1\).

The distinction in the last bullet is important: normality fills every
lattice point in the cone, but it does not erase a genuine lattice index.

### Proposition 6.3 — finite Smith-form and ILP certificate

Let \(R_m\) be the \(|\Omega|\)-by-\(|\mathfrak F_m|\) matrix with columns
\(x_F\).  Then \(\ell_m\) is determined by the Smith normal form of
\(R_m\), and \(\rho_m\) is determined by the finite systems
\[
R_my=ku,\qquad y\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{\mathfrak F_m},
\qquad 1\le k\le2W.
\tag{6.10}
\]
More explicitly, suppose
\[
P R_m Q=\operatorname{diag}(s_1,\ldots,s_R,0),
\qquad s_i>0,
\]
is a Smith form and \(w=Pu\).  Since \(2Wu\) is in the column group,
\(w_j=0\) beyond the rank \(R\), and
\[
\boxed{
\ell_m=\operatorname{lcm}_{1\le i\le R}
\frac{s_i}{\gcd(s_i,w_i)},}
\tag{6.10a}
\]
with the term interpreted as \(1\) when \(w_i=0\).  Indeed,
\(ku\in\operatorname{im}_{\mathbb Z}R_m\) exactly when
\(s_i\mid kw_i\) for every \(i\le R\).

Each coefficient obeys \(0\le y_F\le k\), so these are bounded finite
problems.  This is a finite and independently checkable formulation; no such
calculation is performed here.

### Theorem 6.4 — conflict-graph formulation of the IDP index

Let \(G_m\) have vertex set \(\Omega\), with two wreaths adjacent when they
share a middle interval.  Then
\[
\alpha(G_m)=B,
\qquad
\chi_f(G_m)=\frac{|\Omega|}{B}=d_0.
\tag{6.11}
\]
Moreover, for every \(k\ge1\),
\[
\boxed{
ku\in\mathcal R_m
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\chi_k(G_m)=kd_0,}
\tag{6.12}
\]
where \(\chi_k\) is the \(k\)-fold chromatic number.  Thus \(\rho_m\) is
the least fold at which the optimal fractional coloring value \(d_0\) is
attained integrally.

Here a \(k\)-fold coloring assigns exactly \(k\) colors to every vertex,
with adjacent vertices receiving disjoint color sets.  The equivalent
“at least \(k\)” convention gives the same optimum after trimming.

#### Proof

An independent set consists of wreaths with pairwise disjoint middle
interval families.  Every wreath contains \(n\) middle intervals, so such a
set has size at most \(W/n=B\).  Exact factors exist and attain this bound.
The graph is vertex-transitive, hence its fractional chromatic number is
\(|\Omega|/\alpha=d_0\).  More explicitly, any fractional coloring has
total weight at least \(|\Omega|/B\), since every independent set has size
at most \(B\).  Conversely, give weight \(1/(2W)\) to each member of the
full \(S_n\)-orbit multiset of one exact factor.  Equation (6.3) says that
every vertex receives total weight one, while the total color weight is
\(n!/(2W)=d_0\).

If \(ku\) is a sum of exact-factor indicators, give one color to each
factor occurrence.  Every wreath receives \(k\) colors, adjacent wreaths
receive disjoint color sets, and the number of colors is forced to be
\(k|\Omega|/B=kd_0\).

Conversely, suppose a \(k\)-fold coloring uses \(kd_0\) colors.  Trim
assignments, if necessary, so every vertex has exactly \(k\) colors.  Every
color class is independent and has size at most \(B\).  The total number of
vertex-color incidences is
\(k|\Omega|=kd_0B\), so every one of the \(kd_0\) color classes has size
exactly \(B\).  Each is therefore an exact factor, and the color classes
sum to \(ku\). \(\square\)

This is the primitive integer-decomposition problem for the universal
barycenter \(u/d_0\) of the exact-factor polytope.

### Proposition 6.5 — analytic base case \(m=2\)

This is a separate factor-semigroup base case, outside the standing
\(H\ge1,\ H\le m-2\) range.

For \(m=2\), \(n=5\), and \(d_0=6\), the twelve wreaths are the twelve
undirected Hamilton \(5\)-cycles of \(K_5\).  The complement of every such
cycle is another Hamilton \(5\)-cycle, and the complementary pair partitions
the ten middle edges.  The six complementary pairs partition \(\Omega\) into
exact factors.  Hence
\[
\ell_2=\rho_2=1.
\tag{6.13}
\]
In fact \(G_2\) is \(K_{12}\) with a perfect matching deleted.  At depth
one, every wreath contains every singleton once, so every exact factor has
singleton load two and zero depth-one defect.

This proof is analytic and exhaustive; it is not a finite search.

## 7. What deterministic decoration can and cannot change

### Proposition 7.1 — graph decoration preserves the semigroup exactly

If one appends to each factor its deterministic lower-rank incidence, the
resulting semigroup is
\[
\widetilde{\mathcal R}_{m,H}
=\mathbb N\{(x_F,B_1x_F,\ldots,B_Hx_F):F\in\mathfrak F_m\}.
\tag{7.1}
\]
The map
\[
L:x\longmapsto(x,B_1x,\ldots,B_Hx)
\tag{7.2}
\]
is an injective integral graph embedding with integral inverse given by
projection onto the first coordinate block.  Therefore
\[
\widetilde{\mathcal R}_{m,H}=L(\mathcal R_m),
\tag{7.3}
\]
and \(L\) preserves the cone, group lattice, holes, normality, and the ray
indices \(\ell_m,\rho_m\), all relative to the column group and its real
linear span.  Off-graph integral points in the larger ambient product lattice
are not part of this semigroup normalization.

Thus deterministic shadow decoration by itself creates no new IDP theorem.
Only projection that forgets wreath ownership, or genuinely variable
decorations such as quota or owner-path choices, can change the positive
problem.

### Proposition 7.2 — independent high-token allocation adds no bare ray obstruction

Suppose \(ku\) has been decomposed into \(kd_0\) exact factors.  For every
depth \(q\), one can choose a high family \(H_{i,q}\subseteq V_q\) of size
\(\rho_q\) for each factor \(i\) so that every target \(S\in V_q\) is high
in exactly \(k\eta_q\) factors, where \(\eta_q\) is defined in (4.10).

#### Proof

It suffices to construct a simple bipartite graph with
\[
kd_0\text{ factor vertices of degree }\rho_q
\]
and
\[
N_q\text{ target vertices of degree }k\eta_q.
\]
The total degrees agree because
\[
kd_0\rho_q=N_qk\eta_q.
\]
Also \(\rho_q\le N_q\) and \(k\eta_q\le kd_0\).  A complete bipartite
unit-capacity max-flow, with the stated integral source and sink margins,
has a fractional constant solution and therefore an integral solution by
flow integrality.  Its factor neighborhoods are the required high families.
\(\square\)

This proposition allocates quota tokens only.  It does not assert
\[
B_qx_{F_i}=c_q\mathbf1+\mathbf1_{H_{i,q}}.
\]
Alignment of the high tokens with the actual lower loads is exactly the
missing positive problem.

## 8. Exact orbit refactorization and Catalan-scale defect

The full orbit vector \(2Wu\) is the canonical exactly balanced orbit
multicover.  It is exactly uniform at every lower rank by (6.5), regardless
of how it is refactored.  Aggregate balance, however, allows signed
deviations of different factors to cancel.

### Theorem 8.1 — exact no-gain theorem for invariant factor costs

Let \(J:\mathfrak F_m\to\mathbb R_{\ge0}\) be invariant under coordinate
relabeling, and put
\[
J^*=\min_{F\in\mathfrak F_m}J(F).
\]
Then every factorization of \(2Wu\) contains exactly \(n!\) factors and
\[
\boxed{
\min_{\substack{2Wu=\sum_{i=1}^{n!}x_{F_i}\\F_i\in\mathfrak F_m}}
\sum_{i=1}^{n!}J(F_i)
=n!J^*.}
\tag{8.1}
\]
Also
\[
\boxed{
\min_{\substack{2Wu=\sum_i x_{F_i}}}
\max_iJ(F_i)=J^*.}
\tag{8.2}
\]

#### Proof

The number of factors is forced by total wreath multiplicity:
\[
K B=2W|\Omega|
=2W\frac{(n-1)!}{2}=W(n-1)!,
\]
so \(K=n!\).  Every summand has cost at least \(J^*\), proving the lower
bounds in (8.1)--(8.2).  Choose a minimizing factor \(F^*\) and take all
\(n!\) coordinate relabelings.  The orbit identity (6.3) gives a
factorization of \(2Wu\), and invariance gives cost \(J^*\) for every
summand. \(\square\)

### Corollary 8.2 — invariant properties

For any \(S_n\)-invariant class \(\mathcal P\subseteq\mathfrak F_m\), the
following are equivalent:

1. some exact factor belongs to \(\mathcal P\);
2. some factorization of \(2Wu\) contains a factor in \(\mathcal P\);
3. some factorization of \(2Wu\) consists entirely of factors in
   \(\mathcal P\).

The only nontrivial implication is 1 implies 3, obtained by orbiting one
factor in \(\mathcal P\).

Take in Theorem 8.1
\[
J(F)=J_H(F)=\sum_{q\le H}\frac{O_q(F)}{c_q}.
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{
\min_{2Wu=\sum_i x_{F_i}}
\frac1{n!}\sum_iJ_H(F_i)
=\min_FJ_H(F).}
\tag{8.3}
\]
There is no loss and no gain in the constant.  For every threshold \(L\),
the exactly balanced orbit multicover has a decomposition with every factor
satisfying \(J_H(F_i)\le L\) if and only if one exact factor satisfies
\(J_H(F)\le L\).

This applies verbatim to a Catalan threshold \(L=C B\), to a window-scaled
threshold \(L=C_AHB\), or to the invariant class defined by the simultaneous
per-depth bounds
\[
O_q(F)\le C_Ac_qB\qquad(q\le H).
\tag{8.4}
\]
Thus decomposition of the exactly balanced orbit multicover into
near-balanced single factors is not a relaxation of the one-factor problem;
it is exactly equivalent to it.

In the fixed window \(H=\lceil A\sqrt m\rceil\), simultaneous per-depth
Catalan bounds of the form (8.4) give
\[
J_H(F)\le C_AHB=O_A(W/\sqrt m)=o(W).
\]
Hence such a constituent decomposition would prove fixed-window MWB, but by
the equivalence it can exist only after the same one-factor theorem has been
proved.

The scope here is separable, relabeling-invariant constituent cost, with
each constituent's quota decoration freely chosen or reoptimized.  The
theorem does not apply to an arbitrarily frozen labelled decoration, to
quota assignments tied to old orbit copies, or to one common nested owner
resolution shared across depths.

### Theorem 8.3 — decorated orbit version

For an exact factor \(F\) and high sets \(H_q\subseteq V_q\) of sizes
\(\rho_q\), put
\[
\delta_q(F,H_q)
=B_qx_F-c_q\mathbf1-\mathbf1_{H_q}
\tag{8.5}
\]
and
\[
\mathcal E_H(F,H)
=\sum_{q=1}^H\frac1{c_q}
\sum_{S\in V_q}(\delta_q(F,H_q;S))_+.
\tag{8.6}
\]
Then
\[
\min_H\mathcal E_H(F,H)=J_H(F).
\tag{8.7}
\]
Let one complete-wreath decorated block be
\[
U^{\mathrm{dec}}
=\left(u,(\eta_q\mathbf1_{V_q})_{q=1}^H\right).
\tag{8.8}
\]
For every decorated pair \((F,H)\), its full coordinate orbit is exactly
\[
\sum_{\sigma\in S_n}
\left(x_{\sigma F},
(\mathbf1_{\sigma H_q})_q\right)
=2W U^{\mathrm{dec}}.
\tag{8.9}
\]
Consequently, if
\[
J^*_{m,H}=\min_{F,H}\mathcal E_H(F,H),
\]
then
\[
\boxed{
\min_{\substack{
2WU^{\mathrm{dec}}=\sum_{i=1}^{n!}(x_{F_i},(\mathbf1_{H_{i,q}})_q)}}
\frac1{n!}\sum_{i=1}^{n!}\mathcal E_H(F_i,H_i)
=J^*_{m,H}.}
\tag{8.10}
\]
The analogous minimum of the largest summand energy is also
\(J^*_{m,H}\).

#### Proof

Equation (8.7) is the definition of balanced overload.  The wreath block in
(8.9) is (6.3).  For the high-token block, transitivity on \(V_q\) makes
the orbit sum constant, with coefficient
\[
\frac{n!\rho_q}{N_q}
=2W\frac{d_0\rho_q}{N_q}
=2W\eta_q.
\]
This proves (8.9).  Every decorated summand in any decomposition has energy
at least \(J^*_{m,H}\).  Orbiting one minimizing pair attains equality in
every summand, proving (8.10) and the maximum statement. \(\square\)

The aggregate discrepancy in (8.9) is exactly zero:
\[
2WB_qu
-n!c_q\mathbf1
-2W\eta_q\mathbf1
=2W(d_q-c_qd_0-\eta_q)\mathbf1=0.
\tag{8.11}
\]
Nevertheless every orbit summand retains the defect of the original factor.
This is the sharpest possible demonstration that aggregate exact balance is
annealed balance, not constituent balance.

For comparison, with
\(\theta_q=\rho_q/N_q\) and
\(\lambda_q=c_q+\theta_q\), every factor load satisfies
\[
\sum_{S\in V_q}
(\mu_q(S)-c_q)(\mu_q(S)-c_q-1)
=\|\mu_q-\lambda_q\mathbf1\|_2^2
-N_q\theta_q(1-\theta_q).
\tag{8.12}
\]
In a balanced multicover the signed centered histograms sum to zero, but the
sum of their squared norms can be large.  Cancellation controls the mean,
not the dispersion.

## 9. Additive invariants, cone cuts, and genuine holes

### Proposition 9.1 — signed saturation exhausts additive lower-histogram invariants

Let \(G\) be an abelian group and let
\[
\Phi:\bigoplus_{q=1}^H\mathbb Z^{V_q}\to G
\]
be a homomorphism which is constant on every signed exact-middle fiber.
Put \(U=\bigoplus_{q=1}^HU_{r_q}\).  Then there is a homomorphism
\[
\psi:\operatorname{im}U\to G
\]
such that \(\Phi=\psi\circ U\).  In this precise sense, \(\Phi\) factors
through the tuple of point-incidence maps
\[
(U_{r_q})_{q=1}^H.
\tag{9.1}
\]

#### Proof

Constancy on signed fibers says that \(\Phi\) annihilates
\((B_q)_q(\ker_{\mathbb Z}A)\).  By (1.1), it annihilates
\(\bigoplus_q\ker_{\mathbb Z}U_{r_q}\).  Hence two lower-histogram tuples
with the same point margins have the same \(\Phi\)-value, which is precisely
the well-defined induced map \(\psi\) on \(\operatorname{im}U\).  No
extension of \(\psi\) to the whole ambient point-coordinate group is
asserted. \(\square\)

For every exact factor,
\[
U_{r_q}\mu_q(F)=r_qB\mathbf1_{[n]}.
\tag{9.2}
\]
Thus there is no additional additive cyclic-shadow congruence on the full
signed fiber beyond totals and point margins.  In particular, a genuine
obstruction to a point-regular balanced target cannot come from an
undetected linear congruence of the lower histograms.  It must come from the
nonnegative cone, support feasibility, nonlinear structure, or the
factorization coloring.

The denominator \(\Delta_{m,H}\) is an explicit degree-divisibility
congruence on the exactly uniform ray, but it is only an ambient/lattice
integrality obstruction.  It is not an additional character of the signed
profile quotient in Proposition 9.1.  Failure at a degree not divisible by
\(\Delta_{m,H}\) must not be called a hole.

### Proposition 9.2 — the known prescribed-quota obstruction is outside the cone

Fix depth \(q\), put \(r=m-q\), and let
\(\mathcal U\subseteq\binom{[n]}r\).  Define its upper middle shadow
\[
\partial_q^+\mathcal U
=\{M\in\tbinom{[n]}m:
R\subseteq M\text{ for some }R\in\mathcal U\}.
\]
Every nonnegative fractional exact factor satisfies the containment cut
\[
\boxed{
\sum_{R\in\mathcal U}(B_qx)(R)
\le|\partial_q^+\mathcal U|.}
\tag{9.3}
\]

#### Proof

In one wreath, every cyclic \(r\)-interval has exactly \(q+1\) cyclic
middle-interval extensions, and every cyclic middle interval has exactly
\(q+1\) cyclic \(r\)-subintervals.  Count pairs \((R,M)\) with
\(R\in\mathcal U\), \(R\subseteq M\), and both intervals in the wreath.
This gives the columnwise inequality between the number of
\(\mathcal U\)-intervals and the number of middle intervals in
\(\partial_q^+\mathcal U\).  Sum with nonnegative wreath weights and use
\(Ax=\mathbf1\). \(\square\)

The first-wave prime-dimensional construction gives, at depth one, a
point-regular floor/ceiling target violating (9.3).  Explicitly, for
sufficiently large prime \(n=2m+1\), let
\[
a=\lceil2\log_2n\rceil,\qquad 2a<m-2,
\]
and let \(\mathcal U\) be the \((m-1)\)-sets containing a cyclic
\(a\)-block in a fixed ground cycle.  Then
\[
|\mathcal U|
\le n\binom{n-a}{m-1-a}<\frac{N_1}{n},
\tag{9.4}
\]
while
\[
|\partial_1^+\mathcal U|
\le\frac{m+2}{m-a}|\mathcal U|
<2|\mathcal U|.
\tag{9.5}
\]
For (9.4), take the union bound over the \(n\) cyclic \(a\)-blocks and use
\[
\frac{\binom{n-a}{m-1-a}}{\binom{n}{m-1}}
=\prod_{j=0}^{a-1}\frac{m-1-j}{n-j}
<2^{-a}\le n^{-2}.
\]
For (9.5), count pairs \((R,M)\) with \(R\in\mathcal U\),
\(|M|=m\), and \(R\subset M\).  Every \(R\) has \(m+2\) upper neighbors.
Every \(M\) in the shadow contains a distinguished \(a\)-block and hence at
least \(m-a\) members of \(\mathcal U\), obtained by deleting a point
outside that block.  The final strict inequality is exactly
\(2a<m-2\).

At depth one \(c_1=1\) and
\[
\rho_1=W-N_1=\frac{2N_1}{m}>|\mathcal U|.
\]
Using whole cyclic-translation orbits, \(\mathcal U\) extends to a
point-regular high family \(\mathcal H\) of size \(\rho_1\).  The target
\(\mathbf1+\mathbf1_{\mathcal H}\) assigns load two to every member of
\(\mathcal U\), contradicting (9.3) by (9.5).

Here are the arithmetic details behind that extension.  Since \(n\) is
prime, every nonempty proper subset has a cyclic-translation orbit of size
\(n\).  The family \(\mathcal U\) is a union of such orbits.  Also both
\(W\) and \(N_1\) are divisible by \(n\), so \(n\mid\rho_1\); and
\(\rho_1<N_1\).  Therefore whole unused orbits extend \(\mathcal U\) to
the required size.  Cyclic invariance makes \(\mathcal H\) point-regular.

To obtain a full \(H\)-stacked signed target, for each \(q\ge2\) choose any
union \(\mathcal H_q\) of \(\rho_q/n\) cyclic orbits.  Again
\(n\mid W,N_q\), hence \(n\mid\rho_q\).  Every profile
\[
b_q=c_q\mathbf1+\mathbf1_{\mathcal H_q}
\]
has total \(W\) and point margin
\[
U_{r_q}b_q
=\frac{r_q(c_qN_q+\rho_q)}{n}\mathbf1
=r_qB\mathbf1.
\]
It therefore has the same point margin as an exact-factor load.  Relative to
any exact factor, the simultaneous profile difference lies in
\(\bigoplus_q\ker_{\mathbb Z}U_{r_q}\), so (1.1) gives a full signed lift.

The resulting full target is in the signed lattice but outside the rational
cone, because its depth-one projection violates (9.3).  If
\(L\) is the separating functional, then
\[
L(kb)=kL(b)<0\qquad(k\ge1),
\]
so no positive multiple enters the cone.  It is therefore not a semigroup
hole and has infinite saturation along that ray.  The mobile semigroup of
Section 4 evades this example because it chooses the high family jointly
with the wreath vector instead of prescribing this bad one.

## 10. Exact finite alternatives and the remaining theorem

The normality/IDP question has now been reduced to the following finite
quantities.

### Raw exact-uniform ray

\[
\Delta_{m,H}\mid\tau_{m,H},\qquad \tau_{m,H}\le d_0.
\]
Every admissible failure is a genuine raw semigroup hole.  The depth-one
factor \(m/\gcd(m,2)\) is an explicit mesoscopic degree obstruction.

### Mobile floor/ceiling ray

\[
1\le\sigma_{m,H}\le d_0.
\]
The degree-one target is already in cone intersect lattice.  Therefore
\(\sigma_{m,H}>1\) is exactly a genuine hole, while
\(\sigma_{m,H}=1\) is exactly perfect balance.

### Exact-factorization ray

\[
\ell_m\mid\rho_m,qquad
\ell_m\mid2W,qquad
\rho_m\le2W.
\]
Smith form decides the lattice index \(\ell_m\); bounded factorization or
equivalently optimal \(k\)-fold coloring decides \(\rho_m\).  A strict gap
\(\ell_m<\rho_m\) is exactly a genuine ray hole.

### Catalan-defect decomposition

For every invariant defect threshold, including Catalan-scale thresholds,
\[
\boxed{
\text{the balanced orbit multicover decomposes entirely into good factors}
\iff
\text{one good exact factor exists}.}
\tag{10.1}
\]
For weighted overload, the minimum average constituent defect is exactly
\(J_H^*=\min_FJ_H(F)\).  Thus ordinary normality of the raw or uncolored
factor semigroup cannot prove MWB.  What would be needed is a
**norm-controlled, color-preserving integer decomposition theorem**; at the
universal orbit multiplicity, Theorem 8.1 shows that such a theorem is
literally equivalent to the original one-factor optimum.

No claim in this report upgrades unlabelled overload to a common nested
owner synchronization.  The labelled theorem remains strictly stronger
unless an additional stability theorem constructs one common nested flow.

## 11. Audit ledger

The decisive implications have the following exact status.

1. **All-wreath count and multiplier:** proved by transitive double
   counting; constants are \(d_q=n!/(2N_q)\) and
   \(d_0=m!(m+1)!/2\).
2. **Uniform-ray lattice membership:** proved only after checking both zero
   totals and zero point margins, then applying the simultaneous integral
   saturation theorem (1.1).
3. **Mobile target is a genuine hole if degree one fails:** valid because
   cone membership, lattice membership, and a positive \(d_0\)-multiple are
   all exhibited explicitly.
4. **Prescribed regular quota obstruction:** outside the cone at every
   positive multiple; explicitly not called a hole.
5. **Primitive factor IDP:** unresolved in general.  The lattice index is
   not assumed to be one.  Normality would give \(\rho_m=\ell_m\), not
   automatically \(\rho_m=1\).
6. **Orbit-refactorization theorem:** exact.  The lower bound is termwise;
   the upper bound is the full orbit of an optimizer.  The number of factors
   is forced to be \(n!\).
7. **Catalan-scale conclusion:** an equivalence, not an existence proof.
   It proves that the orbit multicover supplies no shortcut to MWB.
8. **Common-owner scope:** absent.  All semigroups here are unlabelled unless
   explicitly decorated by high tokens; independent high tokens still do
   not form a common nested resolution.

The honest unresolved positive question is therefore not whether the
balanced orbit vector exists—it exists with exact formulas—nor whether its
signed lattice is saturated—it is.  The unresolved question is whether one
can choose a positive exact factor, or a color-preserving factorization,
whose individual weighted defect is small.  Aggregate normality alone does
not control that constituent defect.
