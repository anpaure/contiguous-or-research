# Cyclic-quotient SCD lifting gives an exact selector with uniform position marginals

**Date:** 2026-08-01  
**Status:** unconditional theorem, conditional only on the established
necklace-poset SCD theorem.  It gives exact named high-target coverage and
coordinatewise zero flag flux.  At depth `d=3` this is the complete rail-
balance equation.  It does not prove statewise Hall, owner rainbow,
connectedness, or the non-all-high pull-clock rows.

## 0. Result

Let

\[
 n=2m+1,qquad 2\le d\le m,qquad
 W=\binom{n}{m},
 \tag{0.1}
\]

and let the cyclic group `C_n=<tau>` rotate the ground set.  Fix any
symmetric-chain decomposition of the necklace poset

\[
                         B_n/C_n.
 \tag{0.2}
\]

Such a decomposition exists for every `n` by the necklace-poset SCD
theorem.

### Theorem 0.1 (equivariant necklace-SCD selector)

There is one depth-`d` deletion flag

\[
 f(q)=(q;z_1(q),\ldots,z_{d-1}(q))
 \tag{0.3}
\]

at every rank-`m` root `q` with the following properties.

1. Every named target of every rank

   \[
                       m-1,m-2,\ldots,m-d+1
   \tag{0.4}
   \]

   can be marked exactly once among the suffix rows of the flags.
2. For every deletion position `j` and every coordinate `x`,

   \[
   H_j(x):=|\{q:z_j(q)=x\}|={W\over n}.
   \tag{0.5}
   \]

3. The unmarked flag table is `C_n`-equivariant: translating a root
   translates its complete deletion word.

Consequently the table has zero coordinatewise positional flux,

\[
                         H_j(x)-H_{j+1}(x)=0
 \tag{0.6}
\]

for every `j<d-1` and `x`.  At `d=3`, rail states are single coordinates,
so (0.6) is exactly the full de Bruijn rail-balance condition.

The marking used in part 1 need not be equivariant when a lower target has
a nontrivial stabilizer.  The literal flag table remains equivariant, which
is all that rail balance uses.

## 1. Freeness of the middle action

### Lemma 1.1

The rotation action on rank `m` subsets of `[2m+1]` is free.

#### Proof

Suppose a nonidentity rotation fixes a rank-`m` set.  The cycles of the
subgroup generated by that rotation have one common length `ell>1` dividing
`n`.  An invariant set is a union of those cycles, so `ell` divides `m`.
But

\[
                         \gcd(m,2m+1)=1.
 \tag{1.1}
\]

Thus `ell=1`, a contradiction.  `square`

It follows that every middle necklace has exactly `n` named members and

\[
                    |\binom{[n]}m/C_n|={W\over n}.
 \tag{1.2}
\]

The same argument gives freeness at rank `m+1`, although only rank `m` is
needed below.

## 2. Every quotient chain lifts to one literal middle flag

Every symmetric chain in `B_n/C_n` has endpoints at ranks `a` and `n-a`.
Since `n=2m+1`, it contains a unique quotient element of rank `m`.  The
quotient SCD therefore has exactly `W/n` chains, one for every rank-`m`
necklace.

Fix one quotient chain and choose a representative `Q_m` of its rank-`m`
element.  Going downward, choose representatives

\[
             Q_m\supset Q_{m-1}\supset\cdots\supset Q_a
 \tag{2.1}
\]

of its successive quotient elements.  This can be done coherently.  If an
orbit `[A]` covers `[B]`, then some representative of `[B]` lies in a fixed
representative of `[A]`; after the first choice, rotate both representatives
by the same group element and continue inductively.

Let

\[
                       z_j=Q_{m-j+1}-Q_{m-j}
 \tag{2.2}
\]

for every step available inside the quotient chain.  If the chain stops
before depth `d-1`, append arbitrary distinct elements of its minimum set
`Q_a` to the deletion word.  There are enough because the number required is

\[
 d-1-(m-a)\le a
 \tag{2.3}
\]

when `d<=m+1` (and in particular under (0.1)).  The appended suffixes are
declared unmarked.

This constructs one representative flag for every quotient chain.

## 3. Lift the entire flag, not merely its root

For every quotient-chain flag `f=(Q_m;z_1,...,z_(d-1))`, take all `n`
translates

\[
 \tau^t f=(\tau^tQ_m;\tau^tz_1,\ldots,\tau^tz_{d-1}),
 \qquad t\in\mathbb Z_n.
 \tag{3.1}
\]

By Lemma 1.1 the `n` translated roots are distinct.  Since the quotient SCD
has one chain per middle necklace, (3.1) supplies exactly one flag at every
named rank-`m` root.

For a fixed quotient chain and a fixed position `j`, the labels

\[
                         \tau^t z_j,qquad t\in\mathbb Z_n,
 \tag{3.2}
\]

run through every coordinate exactly once.  Summing over the `W/n` quotient
chains proves

\[
                         H_j(x)=W/n
 \tag{3.3}
\]

for every `j,x`, proving parts 2 and 3 of Theorem 0.1.

This argument uses freeness only at the middle root.  Deeper target orbits
may have stabilizers; those are handled by marking rather than by deleting
duplicate flags.

## 4. Exact named-target marking with stabilizers

Fix a target orbit `[S]` of rank `m-j`, where `1<=j<d`.  The quotient SCD
places `[S]` in one unique quotient chain.  Since that chain crosses rank
`m`, its lifted flag offers `[S]` at deletion depth `j`.

Let

\[
                         h=|\operatorname{Stab}_{C_n}(S)|.
 \tag{4.1}
\]

Among the `n` translated copies of the quotient flag, every named member of
the orbit `[S]` occurs exactly `h` times at depth `j`: the orbit has `n/h`
members and the translation map has fibres of size `h`.  Mark one of those
`h` occurrences for each named member and leave the others unmarked.

Do this independently for every target orbit and every depth.  Marking an
arbitrary subset of the nested suffixes of one flag is legal; there is no
requirement that every offered suffix be marked.  Since the quotient SCD
partitions its elements, no target orbit is supplied by a second quotient
chain.  Hence every named target in (0.4) is marked exactly once.

This proves part 1 and completes Theorem 0.1.  `square`

## 5. Exact consequence at depth three

For `d=3`, write each flag as

\[
                         f=(q;z_1,z_2).
 \tag{5.1}
\]

Its incoming rail state is `z_1` and its outgoing rail state is `z_2`.
The state shores are

\[
 L_x=\{f:z_2=x\},\qquad R_x=\{f:z_1=x\}.
 \tag{5.2}
\]

Equation (0.5) gives

\[
                         |L_x|=|R_x|=W/n
 \tag{5.3}
\]

for every coordinate `x`.  Thus the exact target selector satisfies the
first chronology row—rail balance—without averaging or denominator
clearing.

The remaining two exact rows are unchanged:

1. every literal state graph `G_x=(L_x,R_x)` must satisfy Hall;
2. its statewise perfect matchings must admit one global owner-rainbow
   choice.

Because the whole flag table is equivariant, all `G_x` are isomorphic under
rotation.  Hence statewise Hall at `d=3` reduces to checking/proving Hall in
one representative state graph.  Owner rainbow remains a coupled quotient
colouring problem across the states.

There is an exact quotient form of that coupling.  Fix `x=0` and colour an
edge of `G_0` by the rotation orbit of its rank-`(m+1)` owner.  The rotation
action on those owners is free because

\[
                         \gcd(m+1,2m+1)=1.
\]

Thus there are exactly `W/n` owner colours, the same as the size of either
shore of `G_0`.

### Theorem 5.1 (depth-three quotient rainbow criterion)

The equivariant flag table admits a rotation-equivariant owner-exact
literal cycle cover if and only if `G_0` has a perfect matching whose owner-
orbit colours are all distinct.

#### Proof

Translate a rainbow perfect matching of `G_0` by every rotation.  The
translates perfectly match all state graphs.  One base edge of owner colour
`[O]` produces every named owner in `[O]` exactly once.  A matching of size
`W/n` with distinct colours therefore uses every owner orbit and hence every
named owner once.  Conversely, quotienting a rotation-equivariant owner-
exact cover at state zero gives such a rainbow matching.  `square`

So the remaining depth-three common gate is one Catalan-sized rainbow
perfect matching, followed only later by connectedness and voltage.  For
`k=17`, its order is

\[
                         {\binom{17}{8}\over17}=1430.
\]

This is a genuine reduction for the first unresolved odd case `k=17`, where
the optimal depth is three.  It does not solve that case: Hall can still
fail even when the two state shores have equal size.

## 6. Why the earlier necklace obstructions do not contradict the theorem

### 6.1 Direct within-orbit chronology

The direct necklace-rotor audit proves that almost every middle root has no
Johnson-adjacent nontrivial rotation.  Therefore flags inside one translated
middle necklace cannot generally be joined to each other.

Theorem 0.1 makes no such join.  It uses cyclic translates only to build a
balanced static table.  Its future statewise matching is allowed—and in
general required—to join flags from different quotient chains/root orbits.
Thus the existing obstruction remains valid and identifies the next
cross-orbit Hall gate.

### 6.2 The full `k=17` age-factor obstruction

The direct-SCD obstruction for the `k=17` static age factor asks one fixed
necklace SCD to partition every quotient target at ranks `2,...,9` into
short age flags.  Long SCD chains then contain too many required elements.

Theorem 0.1 uses only the high slab (0.4).  Elements below that slab are not
claimed by the all-high flags and may belong to the pull/boundary sector.
Therefore the long-chain counting contradiction does not apply.

### 6.3 Lower-orbit stabilizers

The action need not be free at ranks below `m`.  The theorem does not divide
their orbit sizes by `n`.  It explicitly records the stabilizer multiplicity
`h` and marks one of the `h` offered occurrences of every named target.
Thus periodic necklaces create redundant offers, not a deficit.

## 7. What does not extend automatically to `d>3`

For general depth, the rail states are ordered `(d-2)`-words.  Equation
(0.5) balances every coordinate at every position, but it need not balance
whole words:

\[
 |\{f:(z_1,\ldots,z_{d-2})=w\}|
 \stackrel{?}{=}
 |\{f:(z_2,\ldots,z_{d-1})=w\}|.
 \tag{7.1}
\]

At the quotient level, (7.1) asks for equality of the weighted inventories
of prefix-word orbits and suffix-word orbits, with weights given by their
stabilizers.  The necklace SCD theorem alone supplies no such identity.

Therefore the general gain is exact target coverage plus all one-coordinate
flux equations.  Full rail balance follows automatically only at `d<=3`.

## 8. Sharpened frontier

For the depth-three all-high face, the common integral rows are now:

| row | status |
|---|---|
| one flag per named middle root | exact |
| every named high target once | exact |
| rail balance | exact |
| one representative state graph has Hall | open |
| owner-rainbow statewise selection | open |
| connectedness/voltage | later/open |
| non-all-high pull rows and upper/compiler guards | later/open |

The next useful theorem is therefore very specific:

> **Quotient-SCD state-Hall theorem (`d=3`).**  Choose the necklace SCD and
> the literal representatives/short-chain completions so that the single
> representative state graph has a perfect matching; then choose those
> matchings owner-rainbow.

The cyclic quotient supplies exact balance and reduces every state to one
isomorphism class.  It does not supply the cross-orbit edges or their owner
transversal.
