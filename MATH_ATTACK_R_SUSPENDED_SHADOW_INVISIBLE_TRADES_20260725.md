Method: pure mathematics only.  No web search, finite search, random

## 0. Outcome

Let \(B_r^{(m)}\) be cyclic-\(r\)-interval incidence on cyclic orders of
\([2m+1]\).  A depth-\(q\) relation is a signed vector \(z\) satisfying

\[
 B_m^{(m)}z=B_{m-1}^{(m)}z=\cdots=
 B_{m-q+1}^{(m)}z=0,
 \qquad B_{m-q}^{(m)}z\ne0.                 \tag{0.1}
\]

Thus the formulation in the question--preserve the first \(s\) lower
shadows and change depth \(s+1\)--is \(q=s+1\).  The certified \(m=4\)
four-for-four factor circuit has \(q=2\).

This report does **not** construct an all-\(m\) family of exact-factor
depth-\(q\) trades.  It proves a collection of sharp integral obstructions
which are complete for the all-pointings, common-marked-context, ordinary
Cartesian-boundary, and frozen-atlas architectures analyzed here.

1. **Complete constant-gap suspension at every depth.**  If (0.1) holds
   in dimension (m), inserting two new labels at every translate of a
   directed gap (d) preserves the same depth exactly when

   \[
    m-q+1\le d\le m+q.                       \tag{0.2}
   \]

   There are (2q) directed, or (q) unoriented, allowed gap types.  All
   have the same active map

   \[
    B_{m-q+1}^{(m+1)}P_dz
      =(m-q+1)(\iota_x+\iota_y)B_{m-q}^{(m)}z. \tag{0.3}
   \]

   Iteration gives a squarefree integral signed relation in every larger
   dimension.  This is a lattice theorem, not a factor theorem.

2. **An arbitrary-depth (q)-step impossibility theorem.**  Start at
   (m_0\ge q+2), and suppose one active coefficient has magnitude at
   least one.  After (q) successive allowed constant-gap lifts, every
   one-new-label branch has magnitude

   \[
    |a|\frac{m_0!}{(m_0-q)!}.                 \tag{0.4}
   \]

   This is at least the exact depth-(q) capacity of every middle
   packing.  Equality occurs only for

   \[
    (q,m_0,|a|)=(2,4,1),                       \tag{0.5}
   \]

   and in that case four simultaneously saturated branches would have to
   be four length-four subintervals of one length-six interval, of which
   only three exist.  Hence **no support-feasible trade, and therefore no
   exact-factor trade, can retain the literal (q)-fold suspended
   effect**.  This generalizes the dimension-six obstruction for the
   certified seed.

3. **Positive equivariant old-order-preserving two-label insertion is
   impossible.**  The nonnegative old-label-equivariant,
   old-order-preserving cone is generated by the
   (q) allowed dihedral gap orbits.  Every nonzero orbit repeats a new
   middle target at least (m-q+2\ge4) times over one old row.  Thus its
   only packing-preserving element is zero.

4. **A tagged-cut invariant kills the formal common-marked-context Dyck
   substitution.**  Appending
   any common nonempty odd context to a marked even local block exposes
   the block's odd-half deck as an integral quotient of the new middle
   shadow.  For the certified (m=4) circuit this deck is nonzero for
   every independent choice of the eight row orientations.  Consequently
   no formal common-exterior prefix/suffix substitution is even
   middle-neutral.  At a fixed
   Dyck boundary there are formally

   \[
    \operatorname{Cat}_p\operatorname{Cat}_{m-p-4}       \tag{0.6}
   \]

   candidate copies; the suffix-only count is
   \(\operatorname{Cat}_{m-4}\), a \(1/256\) asymptotic copy density.
   The actual number of legal direct-context copies is zero.  Moreover, at
   any fixed boundary, or for any chosen context middle-packing, the
   raw effects have private unit pivots, so no integer bundle inside that
   library repairs the defect.

5. **Ordinary Cartesian concatenation reaches the middle layer but stops
   at the first shadow.**  A cap factor produces
   \(\operatorname{Cat}_{M-4}\) pairwise middle-disjoint,
   support-feasible four-for-four product copies, whose partial-sign row
   count divided by \(\operatorname{Cat}_M\) tends to \(1/64\).  Each copy
   has a private first-shadow coefficient
   \(-1\).  No nonempty integer combination is first-shadow neutral.
   Exact-factor completion of even one such product copy is not proved;
   the private-pivot obstruction is independent of completion.

6. **The frozen MSW local-trade atlas is also injective at the first
   shadow.**  It contains \(\operatorname{Cat}_{m-1}\) exact-middle
   two-for-two occurrences, and a fixed boundary supplies a positive
   density of simultaneous exact-factor switches.  Their first-shadow
   vectors possess a triangular unit minor.  Hence no nonzero signed
   combination of this whole frozen atlas preserves the first lower
   shadow.

The surviving route suggested by the (m=4) seed is state-level: mix owners
across contexts and recompute interaction components after preparatory
factor switches.  Any route which retains the branch recurrence must
renormalize its active effect, and any route which retains the displayed
common-seam descendants must create linearly growing order dispersion.  A
precise exact transposition-component criterion is given below.  Satisfying
it in every dimension remains unproved.

## 1. Definitions and exact-factor scope

Let \(\Omega_m\) be the unoriented cyclic orders on \([2m+1]\), modulo
rotation and reversal.  For \(C\in\Omega_m\), let
\(\mathcal W_r(C)\) be its family of cyclic \(r\)-intervals and put

\[
 B_r^{(m)}e_C=\sum_{S\in\mathcal W_r(C)}e_S.          \tag{1.1}
\]

An exact middle factor is a vector

\[
 x\in\{0,1\}^{\Omega_m},\qquad B_m^{(m)}x=\mathbf1. \tag{1.2}
\]

Every row owns \(2m+1\) middle sets, so an exact factor has exactly

\[
 \frac1{2m+1}\binom{2m+1}{m}
   =\frac1{m+1}\binom{2m}{m}
   =\operatorname{Cat}_m                         \tag{1.3}
\]

rows.

A reduced signed vector

\[
 z=\mathbf1_P-\mathbf1_N                         \tag{1.4}
\]

is **support feasible** when \(P,N\) are middle-wreath packings and

\[
 B_m^{(m)}\mathbf1_P=B_m^{(m)}\mathbf1_N.          \tag{1.5}
\]

It is **exact-factor realizable** when there is one common residual packing
\(H\) for which

\[
 H\mathbin{\dot\cup}N,\qquad H\mathbin{\dot\cup}P \tag{1.6}
\]

are exact factors.  Integral signed incidence, support feasibility, and
exact-factor realizability are three different conditions.  Every
impossibility theorem below states which one it uses.

For \(q\ge2\), call \(z\) **depth-\(q\) active** when (0.1) holds.  It then
preserves the first \(q-1\) lower shadows and changes the \(q\)-th lower
shadow.  In the notation of the question, \(s=q-1\).

## 3. Exact transposition-component trades

The following criterion is the clean state-level target left after the
no-go theorems.

Let (F) be an exact factor in dimension (m), let
(\tau=(u\,v)), and form the bipartite ownership overlay of (F) and
(\tau F).  Its left and right vertices are factor rows; every middle set
joins its unique owner in (F) to its unique owner in (\tau F).

### Theorem 3.1 -- exact component switch

For every union (L) of left sides of ownership-overlay components,

\[
 z_L=\mathbf1_{\tau L}-\mathbf1_L                    \tag{3.1}
\]

is an exact-factor trade, and for every (r),

\[
 \boxed{B_r^{(m)}z_L=(\tau-I)B_r^{(m)}\mathbf1_L.}    \tag{3.2}
\]

Consequently (z_L) preserves the first (s) lower shadows exactly when

\[
 B_{m-j}^{(m)}\mathbf1_L
 \quad\hbox{is (\tau)-invariant for }1\le j\le s,  \tag{3.3}
\]

and it changes depth (s+1) exactly when this invariance first fails at
rank (m-s-1).

#### Proof

Each overlay component has the same middle targets on its two sides, so
replacing its left rows by its right rows preserves every middle owner.
It remains to see that the right side of a component is \(\tau\) of its
left side.  In any cyclic row the labels \(u,v\) have \(2m=2m+1-1\)
total incidences among its middle intervals.  If every middle interval
contained exactly one of them, that total would be \(2m+1\).  Hence some
middle interval contains both or neither and is fixed setwise by \(\tau\).
It gives an overlay edge joining \(C\) to \(\tau C\), so these vertices lie
in the same component.  Every overlay vertex has degree \(2m+1\), so each
component has equally many left and right vertices by counting its edges
from both sides.  The inclusion \(\tau L_K\subseteq R_K\) is therefore an
equality for every component \(K\).  This proves the asserted component
pairing and exactness of (3.1).  Equivariance of interval incidence gives
(3.2), from which (3.3) and the activity criterion follow. \(\square\)

Equivalently, at rank (r), only target pairs

\[
 R\cup\{u\},\qquad R\cup\{v\},qquad
 |R|=r-1,\quad R\cap\{u,v\}=\varnothing             \tag{3.4}
\]

need be checked: their multiplicities in the partial histogram of (L)
must agree.  This is a finite exact criterion inside one factor fibre.  It
does not assert that a nonempty qualifying component union exists.  The
(m=4) seed appears only after three preparatory factor switches change
the overlay state; the corresponding kernel is empty in the unprepared
fixed MSW cube.

## 4. Exact gap sectors

Put \(n=2m+1\).  Orient an old order \(C\), number its gaps by
\(\mathbb Z_n\), and add fresh labels \(x,y\).  Let \(E_{a,d}C\) insert
\(x\) in gap \(a\) and \(y\) in gap \(a+d\), where
\(1\le d\le n-1\).  Define the all-pointings operator

\[
 P_de_C=\sum_{a\in\mathbb Z_n}e_{E_{a,d}C}            \tag{4.1}
\]

and

\[
 h_d(\ell)=(\ell-d)_++(\ell-(n-d))_+.                \tag{4.2}
\]

### Lemma 4.1 -- sector identity

Let (T) be a new (r)-set, (t=|T\cap\{x,y\}|), and
(U=T\setminus\{x,y\}).  For (r\ge3),

\[
 (B_r^{(m+1)}P_de_C)_T=
 \begin{cases}
 h_d(n-r+1)\mathbf1_{\{U\text{ is an old }r\text{-interval}\}},&t=0,\\[1mm]
 (r-h_d(r-1))\mathbf1_{\{U\text{ is an old }(r-1)\text{-interval}\}},&t=1,\\[1mm]
 h_d(r-1)\mathbf1_{\{U\text{ is an old }(r-2)\text{-interval}\}},&t=2.
 \end{cases}                                         \tag{4.3}
\]

#### Proof

For (t=0), both insertion gaps must avoid the (r-1) internal gaps of
the old (r)-interval.  The permitted gaps form an interval of length
(n-r+1), giving (h_d(n-r+1)).  For (t=2), both insertion gaps must
lie in the (r-1) consecutive internal-or-boundary gaps around the old
((r-2))-interval, giving (h_d(r-1)).  For (t=1), the selected new
label has (r) possible placements around the old ((r-1))-interval;
exactly (h_d(r-1)) also place the other new label inside the same
window.  Subtraction gives the middle line. \(\square\)

### Theorem 4.2 -- complete depth-(q) gap classification

Assume

\[
 2\le q\le m-2,\qquad
 B_mz=\cdots=B_{m-q+1}z=0,\qquad B_{m-q}z\ne0.        \tag{4.4}
\]

Then (P_dz) is again depth-(q) active in dimension (m+1) if and
only if

\[
 \boxed{m-q+1\le d\le m+q.}                          \tag{4.5}
\]

For every allowed (d),

\[
\begin{aligned}
 B_{m+1}^{(m+1)}P_dz&=B_m^{(m+1)}P_dz=\cdots
 =B_{m-q+2}^{(m+1)}P_dz=0,\\
 B_{m-q+1}^{(m+1)}P_dz
   &=(m-q+1)(\iota_x+\iota_y)B_{m-q}^{(m)}z\ne0.
\end{aligned}                                       \tag{4.6}
\]

#### Proof

At new depth (q-1), the rank is (r=m-q+2).  The only sector in
(4.3) which can see the active old row (B_{m-q}z) is the two-new sector,
and its coefficient is

\[
 h_d(r-1)=h_d(m-q+1).                                 \tag{4.7}
\]

The new-label sectors are disjoint, so this leakage cannot cancel against
another sector.  Since (B_{m-q}z\ne0), it vanishes exactly when

\[
 d\ge m-q+1,\qquad n-d\ge m-q+1,                      \tag{4.8}
\]

which is (4.5).  At every shallower new rank the three old rows used in
(4.3) are among the zero rows in (4.4), with old complementation handling
the new middle row.

At new depth (q), (r=m-q+1).  Condition (4.5) gives
(h_d(r-1)=0).  The no-new sector uses the zero row (B_{m-q+1}z), the
both-new sector vanishes, and each one-new sector uses (B_{m-q}z) with
coefficient (r=m-q+1).  This proves (4.6). \(\square\)

There are (2q) integers in (4.5).  Reflection pairs (d) with
(n-d), leaving (q) unoriented types.  For (q=2), (4.5) is exactly

\[
 d\in\{m-1,m,m+1,m+2\}.                              \tag{4.9}
\]

### Theorem 4.3 -- positive equivariant cone obstruction

Consider nonnegative integral operators which commute with all
permutations of the old labels, preserve the old cyclic order inside every
child, and preserve the zero rows in (4.4).  Among distinct-gap insertions,
their cone is generated by the (q) dihedral orbit sums

\[
 P_d+P_{n-d},\qquad m-q+1\le d\le m.                 \tag{4.10}
\]

No nonzero operator in this cone maps even one old wreath to a new middle
packing.

#### Proof

The dihedral stabilizer of one old unoriented cycle is transitive on gap
translates and pairs (d) with (n-d).  Thus every equivariant
order-preserving operator is a nonnegative combination of the orbit sums.
At the last shadow which must remain zero, every disallowed distance
contributes the same nonzero vector
\(\iota_{xy}B_{m-q}z\) with positive scalar
\(h_d(m-q+1)\); nonnegative coefficients cannot cancel it.  Theorem 4.2
therefore leaves exactly (4.10).  A same-gap orbit has positive two-new
leakage and is also excluded.

For an old middle target (S), one full directed allowed orbit contains
the target (S\cup\{x\})

\[
 m+1-h_d(m)\ge m-q+2\ge4                              \tag{4.11}
\]

times.  Hence every nonzero orbit repeats a middle target and is not a
packing. \(\square\)

Thus any positive suspension within this order-preserving equivariant
architecture must break cyclic phase symmetry and mix different old owners.

## 5. The all-dimensional signed suspension

Let (z_{m_0}) be a squarefree depth-(q) relation, where

\[
 m_0\ge q+2.                                          \tag{5.1}
\]

At each step (j\to j+1), choose

\[
 j-q+1\le d_j\le j+q,                                \tag{5.2}
\]

use a fresh pair ({x_j,y_j\}), and set

\[
 z_{j+1}=P_{d_j}z_j,\qquad
 J_j=\iota_{x_j}+\iota_{y_j}.                         \tag{5.3}
\]

### Theorem 5.1 -- exact signed infinite family

For every (M\ge m_0),

\[
 B_Mz_M=B_{M-1}z_M=\cdots=B_{M-q+1}z_M=0,            \tag{5.4}
\]

and

\[
 \boxed{
 B_{M-q}z_M=
 \frac{(M-q)!}{(m_0-q)!}
 J_{M-1}\cdots J_{m_0}B_{m_0-q}z_{m_0}.}             \tag{5.5}
\]

If the two signs of (z_{m_0}) have (r_0) rows, then (z_M) is
squarefree and

\[
 |z_M^+|=|z_M^-|
 =r_0\prod_{j=m_0}^{M-1}(2j+1).                      \tag{5.6}
\]

#### Proof

Equations (5.4)--(5.5) follow by iterating Theorem 4.2; the scalar is

\[
 \prod_{j=m_0}^{M-1}(j+1-q)
 =\frac{(M-q)!}{(m_0-q)!}.                            \tag{5.7}
\]

Deleting the newest labelled pair from a descendant recovers its unique
parent and its two marked gaps.  If two descendants represented the same
unoriented order, an orientation reversal would replace (d_j) by
(2j+1-d_j); choosing one directed representative for each parent and
recursively deleting the fresh pairs recovers the full insertion history.
Because (2j+1) is odd, no directed gap is its own reverse.  Coefficients
therefore remain in ({-1,0,1}), and every parent has (2j+1)
descendants, proving (5.6). \(\square\)

For the certified seed (m_0=4,q=2,r_0=4),

\[
 B_{M-2}z_M=
 \frac{(M-2)!}{2}J_{M-1}\cdots J_4(B_2w_4),         \tag{5.8}
\]

and each sign has

\[
 4\prod_{j=4}^{M-1}(2j+1)                            \tag{5.9}
\]

rows.  The (2^{M-4}) fresh-label sectors in (5.8) are disjoint, so the
active image is nonzero.  Nothing here says that either sign is a middle
packing.  Indeed, Theorem 4.3 already shows why the natural orbit sum is
not positive.

## 6. Packing capacity and the (q)-step no-go

### Theorem 6.1 -- exact depth-(q) capacity

Let (F) be any middle-wreath packing in dimension (M), let
(1\le q\le M-1), and fix

\[
 T\in\binom{[2M+1]}{M-q}.
\]

Then

\[
 \boxed{
 (B_{M-q}\mathbf1_F)_T\le
 L_{M,q}:=
 \left\lfloor\frac1{q+1}\binom{M+q+1}{q}\right\rfloor.} \tag{6.1}
\]

Every support-feasible trade (p) therefore satisfies

\[
 |(B_{M-q}p)_T|\le L_{M,q}.                           \tag{6.2}
\]

#### Proof

Whenever (T) occurs as a cyclic ((M-q))-interval in one row, it has
exactly (q+1) cyclic middle extensions: add (a) positions on the left
and (q-a) on the right, (0\le a\le q).  Different rows of a middle
packing give disjoint extension families.  The total number of middle
supersets of (T) is

\[
 \binom{(2M+1)-(M-q)}q=\binom{M+q+1}q.                \tag{6.3}
\]

Division gives (6.1).  The positive and negative loads of a
support-feasible trade both lie in ([0,L_{M,q}]), so their difference
lies in ([-L_{M,q},L_{M,q}]), proving (6.2). \(\square\)

### Theorem 6.2 -- every literal depth-(q) recurrence fails within (q) lifts

Let (q\ge2), (m_0\ge q+2), and let (z_{m_0}) satisfy (0.1).  Suppose

\[
 (B_{m_0-q}z_{m_0})_E=a\ne0.                         \tag{6.4}
\]

Apply any allowed constant-gap operators for exactly (q) steps and put

\[
 M=m_0+q.                                             \tag{6.5}
\]

Then no support-feasible trade (p) in dimension (M) satisfies

\[
 B_{M-q}p=B_{M-q}z_M.                                \tag{6.6}
\]

#### Proof

Let the (q) fresh pairs be (\{x_j,y_j\}),
(m_0\le j<m_0+q).  For each
\(\sigma\in\{0,1\}^q\), let \(R_\sigma\) be \(E\) together with one
selected label from every fresh pair.  Equations (4.6) and (5.5) give the
same coefficient on all (2^q) branches:

\[
 |(B_{M-q}z_M)_{R_\sigma}|
 =A:=|a|\frac{m_0!}{(m_0-q)!}.                        \tag{6.7}
\]

At (M=m_0+q), the unfloored capacity is

\[
 \Lambda:=\frac1{q+1}\binom{m_0+2q+1}{q}.            \tag{6.8}
\]

It remains to compare \(A\) and \(\Lambda\).  First take \(|a|=1\) and put

\[
 F_q(m_0):=\frac A\Lambda
 =(q+1)q!\prod_{i=0}^{q-1}
 \frac{m_0-i}{m_0+2q+1-i}.                           \tag{6.9}
\]

Every factor in the product is strictly increasing in (m_0), so
(F_q(m_0)) is strictly increasing.  At its smallest allowed argument
(m_0=q+2),

\[
 R_q:=F_q(q+2)
 =\frac{(q+1)q!(q+2)!(2q+3)!}{2(3q+3)!}.             \tag{6.10}
\]

Now (R_2=1), while

\[
 \frac{R_{q+1}}{R_q}
 =\frac{2(q+2)(q+3)(2q+5)}
        {3(3q+4)(3q+5)}>1                            \tag{6.11}
\]

for (q\ge2), because numerator minus denominator is

\[
 q(q-1)(4q+7)>0.                                     \tag{6.12}
\]

Therefore \(A\ge\Lambda\), with equality exactly when

\[
 q=2,\qquad m_0=4,\qquad |a|=1.                      \tag{6.13}
\]

If \(|a|>1\), the inequality is strict as well.  Outside (6.13),
\(A>\Lambda\ge L_{M,q}\), contradicting (6.2).

It remains to eliminate (6.13).  Here (M=6), (A=L_{6,2}=12), and
there are four branch targets \(R_\sigma\).  After reversing signs if
necessary, equality of the signed coefficient forces positive load (12)
and negative load (0) at each branch.  Every occurrence consumes three
distinct middle supersets, and

\[
 12\cdot3=\binom{9}{2}=36,                            \tag{6.14}
\]

so all middle six-set supersets of each \(R_\sigma\) are used.  The common
six-set

\[
 T=E\cup\{x_4,y_4,x_5,y_5\}                          \tag{6.15}
\]

contains all four branches.  Its unique positive owner would have to
contain all four as cyclic four-subintervals of the same cyclic
six-interval (T).  A six-interval has only

\[
 6-4+1=3                                              \tag{6.16}
\]

contained four-subintervals.  This contradiction proves the exceptional
case and the theorem. \(\square\)

The comparison is independent of which of the (2q) allowed directed
gaps is used at each step, because all allowed gaps have the same active
map (0.3).  The theorem rules out any construction which retains the
literal branch geometry and coefficients.  It does not rule out a
state-dependent map which mixes branches and cancels or redistributes the
active mass.

### Corollary 6.3 -- amplification must be sparse

Suppose, instead, that exact-factor trades in successive dimensions obey a
branch-preserving integral recurrence

\[
 g_{j+1}=c_j(\iota_{x_j}+\iota_{y_j})g_j,\qquad
 g_j=B_{j-q}z_j,\qquad c_j\in\mathbb Z\setminus\{0\}. \tag{6.17}
\]

If (a\ne0) is a coefficient of (g_{m_0}), then capacity gives the exact
necessary condition

\[
 |a|\prod_{j=m_0}^{M-1}|c_j|
 \le L_{M,q}.                                        \tag{6.18}
\]

Hence

\[
 \#\{j<M:|c_j|\ge2\}
 \le\left\lfloor\log_2\frac{L_{M,q}}{|a|}\right\rfloor
 =O_q(\log M).                                       \tag{6.19}
\]

Thus a branch-preserving all-(m) exact suspension can amplify on only
logarithmically many steps.  On a density-one set of steps its integral
scalar must have magnitude one.  The natural scalar (c_j=j+1-q) violates
this requirement maximally.

### Corollary 6.4 -- total-variation capacity

Every support-feasible trade with (r) rows on each sign satisfies

\[
 \|B_{M-q}p\|_1\le2(2M+1)r.                          \tag{6.20}
\]

For an exact-factor trade \(r\le\operatorname{Cat}_M\), so

\[
 \boxed{\|B_{M-q}p\|_1\le2\binom{2M+1}{M}.}          \tag{6.21}
\]

The raw suspended image has

\[
 \|B_{M-q}z_M\|_1
 =2^{M-m_0}\frac{(M-q)!}{(m_0-q)!}
  \|B_{m_0-q}z_{m_0}\|_1.                           \tag{6.22}
\]

For the (m_0=4,q=2) seed, this is

\[
 2^{M-2}(M-2)!.                                      \tag{6.23}
\]

At (M=9), (6.23) is (645120), whereas the right side of (6.21) is

\[
 2\binom{19}{9}=184756.                              \tag{6.24}
\]

The suspended total variation thereafter grows by (2(M-1)), while the
binomial width grows by a factor less than (4).  Thus the inequality
continues to fail for every (M\ge9).  This weaker but coordinate-free
invariant also rules out any rearrangement which preserves the raw total
variation.

## 7. The common-context odd-half invariant

The direct MSW/Dyck concatenation and the ordinary Cartesian path product
are different functors.  This section treats the first one.  It already
fails at the middle layer.

It is convenient to use the omitted-label representation.  For a cyclic
word

\[
 q=(q_0,\ldots,q_{2m}),
\]

put

\[
 I_j^{(r)}(q)=
 \{q_{j+1},q_{j+3},\ldots,q_{j+2r-1}\},
 \qquad
 B_re_q=\sum_{j\in\mathbb Z_{2m+1}}e_{I_j^{(r)}(q)}.
 \tag{7.1}
\]

Because multiplication by \(2\) permutes \(\mathbb Z_{2m+1}\), this is
the same cyclic-interval incidence as in ordinary order, only reindexed.

Let \(a,b\ge1\).  Let

\[
 A=(a_0,\ldots,a_{2a-1})
\]

be a marked local block and let

\[
 T=(t_0,\ldots,t_{2b})
\]

be an ordered context on disjoint labels.  Write

\[
 \Sigma_T(A)=(a_0,\ldots,a_{2a-1},t_0,\ldots,t_{2b})
 \tag{7.2}
\]

and put \(m=a+b\).  Define the marked odd-half deck

\[
 D_{\rm odd}^{(a)}e_A
 =e_{\{a_1,a_3,\ldots,a_{2a-1}\}}.                   \tag{7.3}
\]

Finally define the context tag

\[
 H_T=\{t_{2b}\}\cup\{t_1,t_3,\ldots,t_{2b-3}\},       \tag{7.4}
\]

where the second set is empty when \(b=1\).  Let \(\Pi_{H_T}\) restrict a
rank-\(m\) incidence vector to targets whose intersection with the context
labels is exactly \(H_T\), and then delete \(H_T\).

### Theorem 7.1 -- tagged-cut quotient

For every integral signed vector \(z\) of marked \(2a\)-blocks,

\[
 \boxed{
 \Pi_{H_T}B_m\Sigma_Tz=D_{\rm odd}^{(a)}z.}            \tag{7.5}
\]

In particular, vanishing of the marked odd-half deck is a necessary
condition for middle neutrality under every nonempty common context.

#### Proof

Number the positions of \(\Sigma_T(A)\) by \(0,\ldots,2m\).  The positional
middle mask at cut \(j\) is

\[
 J_j=\{j+1,j+3,\ldots,j+2m-1\}\pmod{2m+1}.            \tag{7.6}
\]

Its cyclic zero-one indicator has exactly one adjacent \(00\) pair, at
positions \((j-1,j)\), and otherwise alternates.  At

\[
 j_*=2m-1
\]

one has

\[
 J_{j_*}=\{2m,1,3,\ldots,2m-3\}.                     \tag{7.7}
\]

Its local trace is \(\{1,3,\ldots,2a-1\}\), and its context trace is
exactly (7.4).  This context trace is unique among all cuts: it omits the
adjacent context positions \(2m-2,2m-1\).  If another \(J_j\) had the same
context trace, these positions would be its adjacent \(00\) pair, forcing
\((j-1,j)=(2m-2,2m-1)\), hence \(j=j_*\).

Therefore a target \(H_T\cup X\), with \(X\) using only local labels and
\(|X|=a\), occurs precisely at this one cut, and precisely when

\[
 X=\{a_1,a_3,\ldots,a_{2a-1}\}.
\]

The assertion is targetwise and integral, proving (7.5). \(\square\)

The mechanism is worth emphasizing.  The old equality \(B_a z=0\) may
cancel only after all cyclic cuts are summed.  A nonempty context tags one
particular cut and exposes \(D_{\rm odd}^{(a)}z\).  Thus literal
concatenation needs cut-resolved neutrality, which is strictly stronger
than ordinary middle neutrality.

### Theorem 7.2 -- the certified seed violates every common context

Normalize the four negative omitted-label rows of the seed by rotating the
distinguished label \(9\) to the last position and deleting it.  The
resulting marked eight-blocks are

\[
\begin{aligned}
 A_1&=(2,1,8,6,7,4,5,3),\\
 A_2&=(8,6,7,4,5,2,3,1),\\
 A_3&=(8,6,7,2,4,1,5,3),\\
 A_4&=(4,5,8,2,6,1,7,3).
\end{aligned}                                        \tag{7.8}
\]

The positive unoriented rows are their \(\tau=(1\,2)\) images.  Before
adjoining one common context \(T\), allow every one of the eight signed
rows to be reversed independently.  For every \(b\ge1\), the resulting
four-for-four families \(N_T,P_T\) in dimension \(m=4+b\) satisfy

\[
 \boxed{B_m\mathbf1_{N_T}\ne B_m\mathbf1_{P_T}.}       \tag{7.9}
\]

#### Proof

For a displayed \(A_i\), the odd-half set in (7.3) is

\[
 O_i=\{a_1,a_3,a_5,a_7\};
\]

after reversal it is the even-half set

\[
 E_i=\{a_0,a_2,a_4,a_6\}.
\]

The two choices for each negative row are

\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
 A_1&1346\ \text{or}\ 2578\\
 A_2&1246\ \text{or}\ 3578\\
 A_3&1236\ \text{or}\ 4578\\
 A_4&1235\ \text{or}\ 4678 .
\end{array}                                          \tag{7.10}
\]

The positive option list is its \(\tau\)-image:

\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
 \tau A_1&2346\ \text{or}\ 1578\\
 \tau A_2&1246\ \text{or}\ 3578\\
 \tau A_3&1236\ \text{or}\ 4578\\
 \tau A_4&1235\ \text{or}\ 4678 .
\end{array}                                          \tag{7.11}
\]

Whichever orientation is chosen for negative \(A_1\), its selected set is
\(1346\) or \(2578\), and neither set appears anywhere in (7.11).  By
Theorem 7.1, the target \(H_T\) union this local set has positive
multiplicity zero and negative multiplicity at least one.  This proves
(7.9).  The orientations on the two signs were independent throughout.
\(\square\)

The orientation claim can be stated exactly as

\[
 A_iT^{\rm rev}\sim_{\rm dihedral}A_i^{\rm rev}T:
\]

reverse the first word to \(TA_i^{\rm rev}\), then rotate.  Hence the
\(A_i/A_i^{\rm rev}\) options with one fixed \(T\) cover the two relative
orientations of the common context.

### 7.1 The formal MSW prefix/suffix substitution ansatz

At a complete Dyck boundary, the MSW concatenation law gives, up to
coordinate shifts,

\[
 q(PUR)=
 \bigl(\rho(P),\,2p+\rho(U),\,2p+8+\rho(R),\,2m+1\bigr),
 \tag{7.12}
\]

when \(U\) is a canonical semilength-four Dyck root.  The direct
prefix/suffix **ansatz** for the prepared noncanonical seed is to replace
the shifted block \(\rho(U)\) by one marked block \(A_i\), or its reversal,
while retaining the same exterior.  Cyclically rotate that marked
eight-block first.  The shifted suffix \(\rho(R)\), the distinguished
closing label, and \(\rho(P)\) then form one common ordered context of
length \(2(m-4)+1\).  Theorem 7.2 excludes every substitution of this
form.

This is not a claim that the prepared seed rows themselves are columns of
the canonical MSW factor or that MSW concatenation automatically acts on
them.  Constructing a state-dependent prefix/suffix functor for prepared
rows would be new mathematics.  The theorem rules out precisely the formal
operation which keeps the canonical exterior unchanged.

If two proposed endpoint factors agree outside such a cylinder, their
difference on the middle row is exactly the nonzero vector in (7.9).
Likewise, a sealed ownership component must have the same middle union on
both sides.  Thus a direct context is neither an exact-factor trade nor a
sealed partial trade.  Exterior-owner rerouting or row-dependent cuts are
outside this theorem.

### 7.2 Exact formal counts and private defects

Assume \(m\ge5\).  At a fixed boundary semilength
\(0\le p\le m-4\), the formal contexts are indexed by

\[
 P\in\mathcal D_p,\qquad
 R\in\mathcal D_{m-p-4},
\]

so their count is

\[
 N_{m,p}=\operatorname{Cat}_p\operatorname{Cat}_{m-p-4}.
 \tag{7.13}
\]

For fixed \(p\), their root words are distinct.  They are formal candidate
copies, not already proved simultaneous middle packings of all lifted seed
rows.

For suffix-only contexts \(p=0\), the count is
\(\operatorname{Cat}_{m-4}\), and

\[
 \frac{\operatorname{Cat}_{m-4}}{\operatorname{Cat}_m}
 =
 \frac{m(m-1)(m-2)(m+1)}
 {16(2m-1)(2m-3)(2m-5)(2m-7)}
 \longrightarrow\frac1{256}.                         \tag{7.14}
\]

Counterfactually, four changed rows per copy would occupy asymptotic
fraction \(1/64\) of a factor.  Summing the formal occurrences over all
boundaries gives

\[
 \sum_{p=0}^{m-4}
 \operatorname{Cat}_p\operatorname{Cat}_{m-p-4}
 =\operatorname{Cat}_{m-3},                           \tag{7.15}
\]

and

\[
 \frac{\operatorname{Cat}_{m-3}}{\operatorname{Cat}_m}
 =
 \frac{m(m-1)(m+1)}
 {8(2m-1)(2m-3)(2m-5)}
 \longrightarrow\frac1{64}.                          \tag{7.16}
\]

Different boundaries may overlap, so (7.15) is an occurrence count, not
one disjoint library.

There is nevertheless a fixed-boundary bundle theorem.  Let the context
orders \(T_\lambda\) form a middle packing on their \(2b+1\) context
labels.  A full concatenation middle mask which uses exactly \(a\) local
labels restricts to a middle \(b\)-mask of the context order.  Indeed, if
both context endpoints were selected, both local endpoints would be
forbidden; the remaining local path on \(2a-2\) vertices has independence
number only \(a-1\), a contradiction.  Thus the context restriction is a
maximum independent \(b\)-set of its odd context cycle.

The eight negative options in (7.10) are distinct, so the unmatched
\(A_1\) target has coefficient exactly \(-1\).  It follows that this target
is private to its context: another context row could contain it only by
owning the same context middle mask, contrary to packing.  Selecting one
private target per context gives a diagonal \(\pm1\) minor.  Projection to
these pivot coordinates is therefore an integral left inverse.  Hence the
raw common-context effects are linearly independent over every field, their
integer span is primitive, and no rational or integer bundle restores
middle neutrality.

For fixed \(p\), the compressed and cyclically rotated context word is
\(q(PR)\).  The map \((P,R)\mapsto PR\) is injective because the split
length \(2p\) is fixed.  These context cycles are therefore distinct rows
of the smaller exact MSW factor, so the context-packing hypothesis holds.

There is also a symmetric private defect.  Whichever positive \(A_1\)
choice is made, \(2346\) or \(1578\), it is absent from the entire negative
option list (7.10).  Thus, for coupled completions which retain all the
displayed local rows and repair their deficits using exterior rows, there
is one private deficit in each direction.  If the \(N_{m,p}\) defects are
distinct, at least

\[
 \left\lceil\frac{N_{m,p}}{2m+1}\right\rceil          \tag{7.17}
\]

exterior or modified rows per side are needed, because one wreath owns only
\(2m+1\) middle targets.  This is an \(O(1/m)\)-density row bound and does
not rule out a globally coupled rerouting.  If the repair deletes or
changes a displayed local row, its private pivot can disappear and this
particular charge no longer applies.

## 8. A metric obstruction to bounded seam repair

The tagged-cut theorem is exact for a common marked context.  The following
ordinary-order argument also controls bounded row-dependent modifications.

For ordinary cyclic orders \(C,D\), let \(d_\circ(C,D)\) be cyclic
adjacent-swap distance, allowing a final rotation or reversal.

### Lemma 8.1 -- packing distance

Two distinct rows in one middle-wreath packing in dimension \(M\) satisfy

\[
 d_\circ(C,D)\ge M+1.                                 \tag{8.1}
\]

#### Proof

One adjacent swap changes at most two cyclic middle intervals.  Hence

\[
 |\mathcal W_M(C)\cap\mathcal W_M(D)|
 \ge2M+1-2d_\circ(C,D).                               \tag{8.2}
\]

Rows on one side of a packing have disjoint middle wreaths.  Setting the
left side to zero and using integrality gives (8.1). \(\square\)

For the ordinary seed rows \(C,D\) in (2.1), there is an explicit six-swap
path:

\[
\begin{aligned}
 &(1,3,8,7,4,5,9,6,2)\\
 &\quad\longrightarrow
 (1,3,4,8,7,5,9,6,2)
 \quad\text{(move \(4\) left across \(7,8\))}\\
 &\quad\longrightarrow
 (1,3,4,8,6,7,5,9,2)
 \quad\text{(move \(6\) left across \(9,5,7\))}\\
 &\quad\longrightarrow
 (1,3,4,8,6,7,9,5,2)
 \quad\text{(move \(9\) left across \(5\)).}
\end{aligned}                                        \tag{8.3}
\]

This path never uses the cyclic seam \(2\mid1\).

### Theorem 8.2 -- linear dispersion is necessary

Insert any common context at the seam \(2\mid1\) of these two seed rows,
lifting them to dimension \(M\).  Then modify the two lifted rows by
\(e_C,e_D\) adjacent swaps, respectively.  If the two resulting rows lie on
one side of a middle packing, then

\[
 \boxed{e_C+e_D\ge M-5.}                              \tag{8.4}
\]

If each row is modified by at most \(b\) swaps, then

\[
 \boxed{b\ge\left\lceil\frac{M-5}{2}\right\rceil.}    \tag{8.5}
\]

In particular a literal common-seam lift already fails in dimension
\(M=6\).

#### Proof

The six swaps in (8.3) lift unchanged because none crosses the insertion
seam.  The triangle inequality gives

\[
 d_\circ(C_{\rm new},D_{\rm new})\le6+e_C+e_D.
\]

Combine this with (8.1). \(\square\)

For \(d\) row-disjoint copies, summing one such close pair per copy gives

\[
 \sum_{i=1}^d(e_{C_i}+e_{D_i})\ge d(M-5).             \tag{8.6}
\]

Thus positive-density copies which retain these two common-seam row
descendants require total adjacent-swap dispersion linear both in dimension
and in the number of copies.  An \(o(dM)\) total repair, equivalently an
\(o(M)\)-average repair per retained pair, cannot work.  Row-dependent cuts
which abandon these
descendants and fully recomputed factor states are outside the theorem.

An audit correction is necessary here.  Earlier reports quoted a seed
distance \(7\) computed in the omitted-label \(q\)-order.  Step-two
reindexing does not preserve adjacent-swap distance, while (8.2) concerns
ordinary cyclic-interval order.  The explicit path (8.3) proves the
relevant upper bound \(6\); the resulting bound (8.4) is the correct one.

## 9. Cartesian caps: positive-density partial copies with private shadows

The common-context operation in Section 7 simply appends an odd block.  An
ordinary Cartesian product of complementary paths interleaves the cap
blocks differently and can preserve the middle union.  It still cannot
preserve the first shadow.

Let \(X,Y\) be disjoint, with \(|X|=2a\), \(|Y|=2b\), and let \(z\) be a
closing label.  A complementary path on \(X\) is

\[
 P=(x_0,y_0,x_1,\ldots,y_{a-1},x_a),
 \qquad |x_i|=a,\quad |y_i|=a+1,\quad x_a=X\setminus x_0.
 \tag{9.1}
\]

Write

\[
 y_i=x_i\cup\{\alpha_i\}
     =x_{i+1}\cup\{\beta_i\}.
\]

With a suitable orientation its cut cyclic order is

\[
 (z,B(P),A(P)),
\quad
 B(P)=(\beta_0,\ldots,\beta_{a-1}),
\quad
 A(P)=(\alpha_0,\ldots,\alpha_{a-1}).                 \tag{9.2}
\]

For \(1\le h\le a-1\), define the internal depth-\(h\) deck

\[
 L_h(P)=
 \sum_{k=0}^{a-h}
 e_{\operatorname{suf}_k(B(P))
   \cup\operatorname{pre}_{a-h-k}(A(P))}.             \tag{9.3}
\]

Path reversal leaves this multiset unchanged.

For a complementary path \(Q\) on \(Y\), write its deletion and insertion
blocks as \(D(Q),C(Q)\), with lower root \(u_0(Q)=D(Q)\).  The Cartesian
product \(P\star Q\) has cut order

\[
 (z,B(P),D(Q),A(P),C(Q))                              \tag{9.4}
\]

up to reversal.

Throughout this section a complementary path, and a product path, is
identified with the cyclic wreath obtained by closing it with \(z\).
Thus \(B_r(P\star Q)\) means incidence of that closed cyclic order.

### Theorem 9.1 -- private-root projection

Put \(M=a+b\).  For every \(1\le h\le a-1\), every
\(S\in\binom{X}{a-h}\), and every \(Q\),

\[
 \boxed{
 (B_{M-h}(P\star Q))_{S\cup D(Q)}=(L_h(P))_S.}        \tag{9.5}
\]

If the \(Q\)'s form a vertex-disjoint complementary-path packing and
\(Q'\ne Q\), then the same projection of every \(P\star Q'\) is zero.
Consequently, for a signed base family \(w\),

\[
\begin{aligned}
 \Pi_{D(Q)}B_{M-h}(w\star Q)&=\Delta L_h,\\
 \Pi_{D(Q)}B_{M-h}(w\star Q')&=0\qquad(Q'\ne Q),
\end{aligned}                                        \tag{9.6}
\]

where \(\Delta L_h\) is the signed sum of the decks (9.3).

#### Proof

A \(z\)-free cyclic window of length \(M-h\) whose \(Y\)-trace is exactly
\(D(Q)\) must contain the full consecutive \(D(Q)\)-block and none of
\(C(Q)\).  It is therefore uniquely one of

\[
 \operatorname{suf}_k(B(P))\cup D(Q)\cup
 \operatorname{pre}_{a-h-k}(A(P)),
 \qquad0\le k\le a-h.                                \tag{9.7}
\]

Conversely, every window in (9.7) occurs.  This proves (9.5).  For another
path \(Q'\), a full \(b\)-letter trace in the \(D(Q'),C(Q')\) sector is a
lower vertex of \(Q'\).  It cannot equal the already owned lower vertex
\(D(Q)\) because the cap paths are vertex-disjoint.  This proves (9.6).
\(\square\)

Thus if \(\Delta L_h\ne0\), all context-copy depth-\(h\) increments have
private sectors and are linearly independent over \(\mathbb Z\).  A unit
cell gives independence over every field and a primitive integer span.

### Theorem 9.2 -- the seed product chart

Cut the certified seed at \(z=3\).  Its four negative and four positive
paths have equal path-vertex unions and can be oriented so that their
terminal multisets agree.  For the first internal deck,

\[
 \boxed{(\Delta L_1)_{\{2,4,6\}}=-1.}                 \tag{9.8}
\]

Let \(\mathcal Q_t\) be any exact complementary-path factor on \(2t\)
labels, \(t\ge1\), and put \(M=4+t\).  For every
\(Q\in\mathcal Q_t\), form the four-for-four product difference \(W_Q\).
Then:

1. every \(W_Q\) is an integral squarefree support-feasible middle trade;
2. the middle supports are pairwise disjoint across \(Q\);
3. each copy has the private first-shadow pivot

   \[
   (B_{M-1}W_Q)_{\{2,4,6\}\cup u_0(Q)}=-1;            \tag{9.9}
   \]

4. no nonempty integer combination of the \(W_Q\)'s preserves
   \(B_{M-1}\).

#### Proof

Here \(N_i,P_i\) denote the omitted-label cyclic rows, not the ordinary
orders in (2.1).  They are

\[
\begin{array}{c|l}
N_1&1,8,6,7,4,5,3,9,2\\
N_2&1,9,8,6,7,4,5,2,3\\
N_3&1,5,3,9,8,6,7,2,4\\
N_4&1,7,3,9,4,5,8,2,6
\end{array}
\qquad
\begin{array}{c|l}
P_1&1,9,3,5,4,7,6,8,2\\
P_2&1,5,4,7,6,8,9,2,3\\
P_3&1,7,6,8,9,3,5,2,4\\
P_4&1,8,5,4,9,3,7,2,6 .
\end{array}                                          \tag{9.10A}
\]

If \(z=q_j\), direct reading of the cut gives

\[
 L_1(q,z)=
 \left\{
 \{q_{j+4+2k},q_{j+6+2k},q_{j+8+2k}\}:
 0\le k\le3
 \right\},                                           \tag{9.10B}
\]

with indices modulo \(9\).  Hence the four negative \(L_1\)-decks at
\(z=3\) are

\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
N_1&578,579,159,169\\
N_2&246,124,128,178\\
N_3&457,459,569,269\\
N_4&678,679,579,259,
\end{array}                                          \tag{9.10}
\]

and the positive decks are

\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
P_1&269,259,579,578\\
P_2&278,128,124,146\\
P_3&169,569,459,457\\
P_4&159,579,679,678.
\end{array}                                          \tag{9.11}
\]

The target \(246\) appears once negatively and never positively, proving
(9.8).

The unordered endpoint pairs on the negative side are

\[
2578|1469,\quad2469|1578,\quad4578|1269,\quad4678|1259,
\tag{9.12}
\]

and, in the order \(P_2,P_1,P_3,P_4\), the positive side has the same four
pairs.  Orient matched pairs toward the same terminal.

Cutting an old middle set \(T\) at \(z\) maps it to \(T\) when
\(z\notin T\), and to

\[
 X\setminus(T\setminus\{z\})
\]

when \(z\in T\).  Therefore the old \(B_4\)-equality gives equality of the
alternating path-vertex unions.

Define

\[
 V(P)=\{x_0,y_0,\ldots,y_{a-1},x_a\},
\qquad
 A\boxplus B=A\cup B
\]

for sets on the two disjoint coordinate blocks.  The product identity

\[
 V(P\star Q)=
 [V(P)\boxplus\{u_0(Q)\}]
 \mathbin{\dot\cup}
 [\{x_a(P)\}\boxplus(V(Q)\setminus\{u_0(Q)\})]
 \tag{9.13}
\]

shows that the two product signs have equal middle union.  Disjoint cap
paths give disjoint product unions, proving support feasibility and
cross-context disjointness.  Equations (9.8) and Theorem 9.1 give the
private pivot (9.9), which proves the last assertion. \(\square\)

There are

\[
 |\mathcal Q_t|=\operatorname{Cat}_t
 =\operatorname{Cat}_{M-4}                           \tag{9.14}
\]

copies.  Their four rows per partial sign give the ratio

\[
 4\frac{\operatorname{Cat}_{M-4}}{\operatorname{Cat}_M}
 \longrightarrow\frac1{64}.                          \tag{9.15}
\]

More precisely, (9.15) is the aggregate row count of either partial sign,
divided by the row count of an exact factor; it is not a density inside a
proved endpoint factor.

These are support-feasible partial trades only.  No common exact-factor
completion is proved for even one \(W_Q\), nor for their union.  If such a
completion existed, it would cancel from the shadow difference and could
not remove the private pivots.  The theorem therefore rules out ordinary
Cartesian concatenation with unchanged completion and every cross-context
integer rebundling.  Sign-specific seam mixing or exterior-owner rerouting
remains outside scope.

## 10. The shifted MSW local two-for-two atlas has no hidden first-shadow kernel

The preceding product chart starts from the nonlocal four-for-four seed.
There is also a much larger all-\(m\) library of local two-for-two exact
middle trades.  It has positive density, but its first-shadow projection is
integrally injective.

Let \(\mathcal D_j\) be the Dyck words of semilength \(j\), let
\(\rho(W)\) be the MSW flip permutation, and use the concatenation law

\[
 \rho(UV)=\rho(U)\Vert(|U|+\rho(V)).                   \tag{10.1}
\]

Fix

\[
 0\le p\le m-2,\qquad
 P\in\mathcal D_p,\qquad
 R\in\mathcal D_{m-p-2},\qquad d=2p,                 \tag{10.2}
\]

and define the two Dyck roots

\[
 X=P1100R,\qquad Y=P1010R.                            \tag{10.3}
\]

After rotating the omitted-label words so that the four-letter block comes
first,

\[
\begin{aligned}
 q(X)&=(d+4,d+2,d+3,d+1,T_{P,R}),\\
 q(Y)&=(d+2,d+1,d+4,d+3,T_{P,R}),
\end{aligned}                                        \tag{10.4}
\]

where

\[
 T_{P,R}=(d+4+\rho(R),\,2m+1,\,\rho(P)).              \tag{10.5}
\]

Let

\[
 \tau_p=(d+2\ d+3)
\]

and put

\[
 z_{p,P,R}
 =e_{\tau_pq(X)}+e_{\tau_pq(Y)}-e_{q(X)}-e_{q(Y)}.   \tag{10.6}
\]

The exact local identity used below is recorded first.  In the universal
four-letter pattern (10.9), split the common tail \(T\) into its even and
odd step-two lists

\[
 \mathsf E=(t_0,t_2,\ldots,t_{2m-4}),
\qquad
 \mathsf O=(t_1,t_3,\ldots,t_{2m-5}),                 \tag{10.6A}
\]

and for a set \(K\) put

\[
 \partial K=e_{K\cup\{\gamma\}}-e_{K\cup\{\beta\}}.
\]

### Lemma 10.0 -- universal four-letter action

For \(2\le r\le m-1\), with \(\ell=r-1\),

\[
\begin{aligned}
 B_r(e_{C'}+e_{D'}-e_C-e_D)
 ={}&\partial\operatorname{suf}_{\ell}(\mathsf O)
    +\partial\operatorname{suf}_{\ell}(\mathsf E)\\
   &-\partial\operatorname{pre}_{\ell}(\mathsf E)
    -\partial\operatorname{pre}_{\ell}(\mathsf O).
\end{aligned}                                        \tag{10.6B}
\]

Moreover,

\[
 B_1(e_{C'}+e_{D'}-e_C-e_D)
 =
 B_m(e_{C'}+e_{D'}-e_C-e_D)=0.                       \tag{10.6C}
\]

#### Proof

Read a step-two \(r\)-window.  For \(r\le m-1\), it cannot meet both
exceptional adjacent pairs in (10.9).  A window meeting neither pair is
identical in all four rows.  If it contains both entries of one pair, the
two old and two new exceptional sets agree as multisets.  A contribution
survives only when the window contains exactly one exceptional entry.
The four possible boundary cores are, in order,

\[
 \operatorname{suf}_{\ell}(\mathsf O),\quad
 \operatorname{suf}_{\ell}(\mathsf E),\quad
 \operatorname{pre}_{\ell}(\mathsf E),\quad
 \operatorname{pre}_{\ell}(\mathsf O),
\]

with signs \(+,+,-,-\), proving (10.6B).  At \(r=1\), the four copies of
\(\partial\varnothing\) cancel.  At \(r=m\), the same four boundary
classes have full parity cores; the two \(\mathsf E\)-classes and the two
\(\mathsf O\)-classes cancel pairwise.  This is (10.6C). \(\square\)

### Theorem 10.1 -- exact positive-density local chart

Every \(z_{p,P,R}\) is a support-feasible two-for-two middle trade.  Its
negative rows belong to the exact MSW factor, so replacing them by the two
positive rows gives an exact factor.

For fixed \(p\), all

\[
 \operatorname{Cat}_p\operatorname{Cat}_{m-p-2}      \tag{10.7}
\]

trades can be switched simultaneously in that factor.  Across all boundary
positions, the total number of occurrences is

\[
 \sum_{p=0}^{m-2}
 \operatorname{Cat}_p\operatorname{Cat}_{m-p-2}
 =\operatorname{Cat}_{m-1}.                          \tag{10.8}
\]

#### Proof

The four exceptional labels in (10.4) occur in the universal order pattern

\[
\begin{aligned}
 C&=(\delta,\beta,\gamma,\alpha,T),&
 D&=(\beta,\alpha,\delta,\gamma,T),\\
 C'&=(\delta,\gamma,\beta,\alpha,T),&
 D'&=(\gamma,\alpha,\delta,\beta,T).
\end{aligned}                                        \tag{10.9}
\]

At middle rank, Lemma 10.0 says that the four step-two window multisets in
\(e_{C'}+e_{D'}-e_C-e_D\) cancel targetwise.  Hence the old and new pairs
have equal middle union.  The old pair consists of two disjoint MSW rows;
equality of the unions forces the new pair to be disjoint as well.  The
unchanged MSW residual therefore completes both sides exactly.

All four rows are distinct.  An odd cyclic order has no coordinate
transposition in its dihedral stabilizer: a nonidentity rotation or
reflection cannot fix the other \(2m-1\) labels pointwise.  If, instead,
\(\tau_p C=D\), choose a middle interval of \(C\) containing both
transposed labels or neither, whose existence is proved in Theorem 3.1.
It is fixed by \(\tau_p\) and would also belong to \(D\), contradicting
disjointness of the old pair.  The same argument covers the other
cross-equalities.  Thus the move is genuinely two-for-two.  No claim about
the length or connectedness of its odd-graph edge-symmetric-difference
cycle is needed here.

For fixed \(p\), distinct \((P,R)\) give disjoint pairs of roots in
(10.3).  Their middle unions in the MSW factor are disjoint, and the
positive unions are the same, so the switches are simultaneous.  Equation
(10.8) is the Catalan convolution. \(\square\)

The case \(p=0\) alone supplies \(\operatorname{Cat}_{m-2}\) disjoint
exact-factor switches, and

\[
 \frac{\operatorname{Cat}_{m-2}}{\operatorname{Cat}_m}
 =
 \frac{m(m+1)}{4(2m-1)(2m-3)}
 \longrightarrow\frac1{16}.                          \tag{10.10}
\]

Thus two replaced rows per trade occupy asymptotic fraction \(1/8\) of a
factor.  Positive density of exact local trades is not the missing
ingredient; shallow cancellation is.

### 10.1 Exact first-shadow pivots

For a Dyck word \(W\) of semilength \(r\), split its MSW flip permutation
as

\[
 \rho(W)=(a_0,b_0,\ldots,a_{r-1},b_{r-1}),
\quad
 \mathsf A(W)=(a_i),\quad
 \mathsf B(W)=(b_i).                                 \tag{10.11}
\]

The underlying sets of \(\mathsf A(W)\) and \(\mathsf B(W)\) are,
respectively, the down-step and up-step positions of \(W\).  For the
context \((p,P,R)\), the two parity tail lists are

\[
\begin{aligned}
 \mathsf E_{P,R}
   &=(d+4+\mathsf A(R),\,2m+1,\,\mathsf B(P)),\\
 \mathsf O_{P,R}
   &=(d+4+\mathsf B(R),\,\mathsf A(P)).
\end{aligned}                                        \tag{10.12}
\]

As sets,

\[
\begin{aligned}
 E_{P,R}
  &=(d+4+\operatorname{Down}(R))
    \cup\{2m+1\}\cup\operatorname{Up}(P),\\
 O_{P,R}
  &=(d+4+\operatorname{Up}(R))
    \cup\operatorname{Down}(P).
\end{aligned}                                        \tag{10.13}
\]

For \(j\ge1\), write \([j]=\{1,\ldots,j\}\).  For a target
\(S\subseteq[2m+1]\), define its first-negative position on
the first \(2m\) coordinates by

\[
 \eta(S)=\min\{j:2|S\cap[j]|-j<0\}.                  \tag{10.14}
\]

Let

\[
 \beta_p=2p+2,
\qquad
 x_{P,R}=\text{the first entry of }\mathsf E_{P,R},
\]

and define the rank-\((m-1)\) target

\[
 S^*_{p,P,R}
 =E_{P,R}\setminus\{x_{P,R}\}\cup\{\beta_p\}.         \tag{10.15}
\]

To display the unit coefficient explicitly, let \(x,y\) be the first and
last entries of \(\mathsf E_{P,R}\), put

\[
 K=E_{P,R}\setminus\{x,y\},
\qquad
 \gamma_p=2p+3.
\]

Up to one global sign convention, the exact first-shadow square is

\[
\begin{aligned}
 B_{m-1}z_{p,P,R}
 ={}&e_{K\cup\{x,\beta_p\}}
     -e_{K\cup\{x,\gamma_p\}}\\
   &-e_{K\cup\{y,\beta_p\}}
     +e_{K\cup\{y,\gamma_p\}} .
\end{aligned}                                        \tag{10.15A}
\]

Thus (10.15) is one of four unit cells.

### Lemma 10.2 -- pivot recovery

The target (10.15) satisfies

\[
 \eta(S^*_{p,P,R})=2p+1.                              \tag{10.16}
\]

It is absent from every first-shadow column with parameter \(p'<p\), and
among columns with parameter \(p\) it occurs only in
\(B_{m-1}z_{p,P,R}\), with coefficient \(\pm1\).

#### Proof

On coordinates \(1,\ldots,2p\), the target is exactly the up-step set of
the Dyck word \(P\), so its height is nonnegative and returns to zero.
Coordinate \(2p+1\) is absent, giving (10.16).  Every square target from a
lower boundary \(p'<p\) first becomes negative no later than
\(2p'+1\), so it cannot equal (10.15).

At fixed \(p\), a square target obtained by deleting the last
\(\mathsf E\)-endpoint removes an up-step from \(P\) and becomes negative
before the local block.  The exception \(p=0\) is distinguished by the
presence or absence of the closing label \(2m+1\).  The inserted label
\(\beta_p\), rather than \(\gamma_p\), then identifies the chosen side of
the elementary square.  The first \(2p\) coordinates recover \(P\).  If
\(R\) is nonempty, after subtracting the coordinate shift \(d+4\), the
suffix of (10.15) is

\[
 \operatorname{Down}(R)\setminus\{a_0(R)\},           \tag{10.17}
\]

where \(a_0(R)\) is the first-return down-step.  This deletion map is
injective: treat every position outside (10.17) as an up-step, let \(h'\)
be the resulting height, and recover the deleted position as

\[
 1+\max\{j:h'(j)=1\}.                                \tag{10.18}
\]

Adding that down-step back recovers \(R\).  The empty \(R\) case is marked
by deletion of the closing coordinate \(2m+1\).  Thus \(P,R\), the side,
and the column are all recovered from the pivot. \(\square\)

### Theorem 10.3 -- no frozen local Haar kernel

Assume \(m\ge3\).  The vectors

\[
 \left\{
 B_{m-1}z_{p,P,R}:
 0\le p\le m-2,\ 
 P\in\mathcal D_p,\ 
 R\in\mathcal D_{m-p-2}
 \right\}                                            \tag{10.19}
\]

are linearly independent over every field.  Their integer span is a
primitive rank-\(\operatorname{Cat}_{m-1}\) sublattice.  In particular,

\[
 B_{m-1}\sum_{p,P,R}c_{p,P,R}z_{p,P,R}=0
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 c_{p,P,R}=0\ \text{for all }(p,P,R).                \tag{10.20}
\]

#### Proof

Suppose a nonzero relation exists and choose the largest \(p\) carrying a
nonzero coefficient.  For any nonzero column at that level, its pivot
(10.15) is absent from all lower levels by Lemma 10.2, absent from every
other column at its own level, and there are no active higher levels.
Its coefficient cannot cancel.  This contradiction proves independence
over every field.  Ordering columns by decreasing \(p\) makes the
restriction to the pivot rows triangular with diagonal entries \(\pm1\),
hence unimodular.  Composing that restriction with its integral inverse
gives an integral left inverse, proving primitivity. \(\square\)

This theorem is stronger than a \(0/1\)-selection obstruction: even signed
integer or rational rebundling of the entire frozen atlas cannot preserve
the first lower shadow.  Across different \(p\)'s the supports can overlap,
so the atlas is not one simultaneous interaction cube.  The theorem is a
linear-library obstruction.  Sequential switches which change the factor
and then recompute later components are not represented by a fixed linear
combination and remain possible.

## 11. What the obstructions jointly force

The results can be summarized without conflating signed, support-feasible,
and exact-factor levels.

| Architecture | Integral shadow identity | Middle packing | Exact completion | Decisive result |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Allowed all-pointings \(P_d\) | yes, every \(q\) | no | no | repeats middle targets |
| \(q\)-fold literal active effect | yes | impossible | impossible | Theorem 6.2 |
| Formal common-exterior seed substitution | no at \(B_M\) | no | no | tagged odd-half deck |
| Bounded seam repair | not excluded algebraically | needs \(\Omega(M)\) dispersion | unproved | Theorem 8.2 |
| Cartesian cap copies | yes at \(B_M\) | yes | unproved | private \(B_{M-1}\) pivots |
| Frozen local MSW two-for-two atlas | yes at \(B_M\) | yes | yes, fixed boundary | injective \(B_{M-1}\) map |
| Recomputed transposition components | conditional | yes by construction | yes by construction | criterion (3.3), existence open |

Three conclusions are exact.

1. **A positive-density supply is not enough in the analyzed charts.**
   Sections 9 and 10 exhibit Catalan-positive families of partial or exact
   middle trades.  Their first-shadow increments have private or triangular
   unit pivots.  There is no shallow kernel to rebundle.

2. **A signed depth-preserving map is not enough.**  Sections 4--6 give the
   complete constant-gap signed map at arbitrary depth.  Positivity fails
   locally by repeated middle targets and globally by exact capacity.

3. **Every surviving recursive template must change ownership topology.**
   A repair of the ruled-out sealed-copy charts must introduce ownership
   interactions outside those copies, for example by cross-context seams
   or sign-specific auxiliary rows.  Sequentially changing the factor and
   recomputing components is one concrete surviving route, not a proved
   necessity.  A branch-preserving version must keep coefficients within
   (6.18); a common-seam-descendant version must create the dispersion
   required by (8.4).  An unrelated exact all-\(m\) family is not
   constrained by those architecture-specific statements.

### Exact remaining theorem -- UNPROVED

Fix \(s\ge1\), put \(q=s+1\), and let \(M\) be sufficiently large.  Find
an exact factor \(F_M\), a transposition, and a
nonempty union \(L_M\) of ownership-overlay components such that

\[
\begin{aligned}
 (\tau-I)B_{M-j}\mathbf1_{L_M}&=0
       &&(1\le j\le s),\\
 (\tau-I)B_{M-s-1}\mathbf1_{L_M}&\ne0.               \tag{11.1}
\end{aligned}
\]

For a positive-density theorem, require many row-disjoint such component
unions which are all compatible unions of components in the same overlay
and the same factor state, or one component union containing a
positive-density family of marked subcircuits.  Theorem 3.1 would then
immediately give exact-factor trades preserving the first \(s\) lower
shadows and changing depth \(s+1\).

The fixed MSW state does not satisfy even the first of these equations for
the local chart.  The \(m=4\) seed shows that preparatory noncommuting
switches can create a qualifying component.  What is missing is an
all-dimensional state-preparation and component-splitting theorem.

One should not replace (11.1) by a labelled common-owner condition.
Unlabelled shadow equality is the required property here; labelled
synchronization is strictly stronger and only sufficient.  Likewise, a
partial packing theorem is not an exact-factor theorem until a common
residual completion is supplied.

## 12. Adversarial audit

### 12.1 Indices and quantifiers

The depth convention is:

\[
 \text{preserve \(s\) lower shadows, change \(s+1\)}
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad q=s+1.                 \tag{12.1}
\]

The constant-gap theorem assumes \(2\le q\le m-2\), so the active old rank
\(m-q\) is at least two and every sector used in Lemma 4.1 is defined.
The allowed interval (4.5) contains exactly \(2q\) directed gaps.  Its
reflection quotient has \(q\) types.

At every step the active scalar is \(j+1-q\), not \(j-q\).  Therefore after
\(M-m_0\) steps it is exactly

\[
 \prod_{j=m_0}^{M-1}(j+1-q)
 =\frac{(M-q)!}{(m_0-q)!}.                            \tag{12.2}
\]

### 12.2 Capacity comparison

One occurrence of an \((M-q)\)-target consumes exactly \(q+1\), not \(q\),
middle supersets.  The signed coordinate bound is \(L_{M,q}\), not
\(2L_{M,q}\), because both sign loads lie in the same interval
\([0,L_{M,q}]\).

For Theorem 6.2, the comparison ratio is strictly increasing in \(m_0\).
At the lower endpoint \(m_0=q+2\), \(R_2=1\), and the difference between
the numerator and denominator in (6.11) is exactly

\[
 q(q-1)(4q+7).                                       \tag{12.3}
\]

Thus (6.13) is the sole equality case.  Its contradiction uses four
simultaneously saturated branch coordinates, not a false strict scalar
inequality.

### 12.3 Common-context audit

Every step-two middle positional mask on \(2m+1\) positions has exactly
one adjacent \(00\) pair.  This makes the context tag in (7.4) unique.
The two negative \(A_1\) decks \(1346,2578\) are absent from the complete
positive option list (7.11).  This checks the decisive seed step without
enumerating wreaths or using finite-search output beyond the displayed
certified rows.

The theorem fixes one marked cut and one common ordered exterior.  A
row-dependent rotation inside the exterior or a different marked local cut
is not covered; either is already state-dependent rerouting.  The formal
MSW-style \(P[\text{seed}]R\) substitution ansatz fixes the cut and is
covered.  A genuine state-dependent context functor for the prepared seed
rows is unproved.

### 12.4 Two concatenation functors

There is no contradiction between Sections 7 and 9.

- Direct omitted-label block concatenation has order \((A,T)\) and fails
  already at the middle layer.
- Cartesian path concatenation has order \((z,B,D,A,C)\), can preserve
  the middle union under endpoint compatibility, and fails at the first
  lower shadow by a private cap root.

Calling both operations a “suffix” without distinguishing them would
produce false implications.

### 12.5 Exactness audit

- The seed \(w_4\) and each fixed-boundary local two-for-two switch in Section
  10 are exact-factor realizable.
- The vectors \(z_M\) in Section 5 are squarefree integral lattice
  relations only; their signs are not packings.
- The Cartesian product copies in Section 9 are support-feasible partial
  trades.  Their common exact completion is unproved.
- Capacity and private-pivot obstructions remain valid even if an
  attempted completion is added, because a common residual cancels from
  every shadow difference.

### 12.6 Correct metric

The packing-distance inequality uses ordinary cyclic-interval order.
Distance in the omitted-label step-two word is a different metric.  The
explicit six-swap ordinary path (8.3) is the only seed distance used here.
It strengthens the correct common-seam requirement to \(M-5\).

### 12.7 Unsupported statements deliberately excluded

This report does not claim:

- an all-\(m\) exact-factor shallow-invisible trade;
- exact completion of the Cartesian product chart;
- that every PBBS common-core short cycle has nonzero first-shadow
  increment;
- that failure of the frozen MSW atlas rules out sequentially recomputed
  components;
- that a support-feasible partial trade implies semigroup or exact-factor
  completion;
- labelled common-owner synchronization; or
- MWB or the contiguous-OR width conjecture.

The sole open structural gate used in the final conclusion is (11.1)
together with an all-dimensional exact state-preparation theorem.

## 13. Final theorem-level status

There is a complete arbitrary-depth signed suspension:

\[
 \boxed{
 B_Mz_M=\cdots=B_{M-q+1}z_M=0,\qquad
 B_{M-q}z_M=
 \frac{(M-q)!}{(m_0-q)!}
 J_{M-1}\cdots J_{m_0}B_{m_0-q}z_{m_0}\ne0.}         \tag{13.1}
\]

There is also a complete positive obstruction for the four architectures
analyzed in this report:

\[
 \boxed{
 \begin{gathered}
 \text{no nonzero equivariant gap orbit is a packing;}\\
 \text{the literal active effect leaves the support-feasible class
       within \(q\) lifts;}\\
 \text{formal common-marked Dyck contexts fail middle neutrality;}\\
 \text{Cartesian cap copies and the frozen MSW local atlas have
       private or triangular first-shadow unit pivots.}
 \end{gathered}}                                      \tag{13.2}
\]

Therefore the desired all-\(m\), positive-density exact-factor theorem is
not obtained by the suspension or concatenation templates analyzed here.
Within those templates the obstruction is not signed incidence or lack of
Catalan supply; it is positivity inside one exact factor fibre together
with cut-resolved shallow balance.  The surviving concrete route is a
noncommutative, state-dependent ownership rerouting satisfying the exact
component equations (11.1).  The report does not rule out a structurally
unrelated exact all-\(m\) family.
