# Outer moving frames: exact target harmonics, sparse heat, and the integral-orbit obstruction

Date: 2026-07-26

Method: pure mathematics only.  The valid recursive context array of
`MATH_AUDIT_CROSSED_DOUBLE_FACTOR_FAILURE_AND_VALID_CONTEXT_ARRAY_20260726.md`
is used as a black box.  No crossed-double-factor recursion is used.

## 0. Verdict

Put \(n=2m\).  Conjugating one valid recursive context-array factor by
coordinate frames in \(S_n\) has an exact and particularly simple harmonic
description.  On the target layer \(\binom{[n]}k\),

\[
 \mathbb R^{\binom{[n]}k}
 =\bigoplus_{j=0}^{\min(k,n-k)}S^{(n-j,j)}.                 \tag{0.1}
\]

The catalogue consisting of all \(\binom n2\) transposition neighbours of
each frame is sparse compared with \(n!\), every catalogue vertex is an
integral legal factor, and its normalized heat operator has eigenvalue

\[
 \boxed{\lambda_j
 =1-\frac{j(n-j+1)}{\binom n2}}                         \tag{0.2}
\]

on the degree-\(j\) target harmonic.  Thus its exact nonconstant spectral
gap is

\[
 \boxed{\gamma_n=\frac{2}{n-1}=\frac1m+O(m^{-2}).}          \tag{0.3}
\]

The same operator and the same gap work simultaneously on every lower and
upper layer \(k=m\mp q\).  Iterating the sparse heat for \(O(n^2)\) steps
makes the *fractional* all-depth target profile \(o(W)\)-close in total
variation to the full \(S_n\)-average when \(H=O(\sqrt m)\).

This does **not** give an integral rounding theorem.  There are two exact
obstructions.

1. A global moving frame only rotates the target multiplicity vector.  Every
   integral orbit point has exactly the same number of target holes, the same
   collision energy, and the same value of every permutation-invariant
   discrepancy.  Heat reduces these quantities only after taking a
   fractional convex combination.
2. If one keeps the exact independent owner components of a disconnected
   perfect-matching atlas, every compatible global frame change preserves
   the coordinate-block relation.  On every protected target layer it fixes
   the nonconstant degree-at-most-two statistic
   \[
      R_k(T)=\sum_i\binom{|T\cap B_i|}{2}.                    \tag{0.4}
   \]
   Hence the component heat has eigenvalue \(1\) on a nonconstant target
   harmonic and has zero spectral gap.  Adding frames until this invariant
   disappears makes the coordinate union graph connected; the exact
   component theorem then leaves only one global middle-owner component, so
   obstruction 1 applies.

Consequently the requested three properties cannot be obtained merely by
sparsifying the \(S_n\)-orbit and applying heat:

\[
 \begin{array}{c}
 \text{independent integral common-owner choices},\\
 \text{gap on every nonconstant target harmonic},\\
 \text{rounding of the symmetric fractional average}
 \end{array}
 \quad\text{cannot all follow from coordinate-frame conjugation.}       \tag{0.5}
\]

The exact escape is a non-relabeling owner-preserving trade whose integral
states have genuinely different permutation-invariant target collision
energies.  A larger or better spectral-gap Cayley catalogue alone cannot
provide that missing property.

## 1. Target incidence and the frame action

Let

\[
 \Omega_k=\binom{[n]}k,\qquad V_k=\mathbb R^{\Omega_k},
 \qquad N_k=|\Omega_k|.                                      \tag{1.1}
\]

The coordinate action is

\[
 (\rho_k(g)f)(T)=f(g^{-1}T),\qquad g\in S_n.                  \tag{1.2}
\]

Fix a legal retained atlas \(a\).  At a signed depth with target rank
\(k=m\mp q\), let

\[
 b_{a,k}(T)=\#\{\text{retained owner occurrences of }a
                         \text{ having literal target }T\}.   \tag{1.3}
\]

If the retained owner mass is \(G\), then

\[
 b_{a,k}\ge0,qquad \sum_Tb_{a,k}(T)=G.                       \tag{1.4}
\]

Conjugating every physical coordinate, every owner, every local context
array, and every target by \(g\) gives another legal integral atlas \(ga\),
and exactly

\[
                         b_{ga,k}=\rho_k(g)b_{a,k}.             \tag{1.5}
\]

Since \(S_n\) is transitive on \(\Omega_k\), (1.4)--(1.5) give

\[
 {1\over n!}\sum_{g\in S_n}b_{ga,k}
             ={G\over N_k}{\bf1}.                              \tag{1.6}
\]

After the normalization \(W/G\) used in the outer fractional master
point, the constant in (1.6) is \(W/N_k\), exactly as in the full-frame
double count.  The same frame \(g\) acts in (1.5) at every signed depth;
there is no depthwise resampling.

For a probability measure \(\mu\) on frames, define

\[
 P_{\mu,k}=\sum_g\mu(g)\rho_k(g).                              \tag{1.7}
\]

If \(\mu(g)=\mu(g^{-1})\), then \(P_{\mu,k}\) is self-adjoint and

\[
 \langle f,(I-P_{\mu,k})f\rangle
 ={1\over2}\sum_g\mu(g)\|f-\rho_k(g)f\|_2^2.                \tag{1.8}
\]

Thus its eigenvalue-one space is precisely the space fixed by the subgroup
generated by \(\operatorname{supp}\mu\).  Formula (1.8) will be the
component obstruction below.

## 2. Exact harmonic decomposition

It suffices first to take \(k\le n/2\); complementation identifies
\(V_k\) with \(V_{n-k}\).  For \(0\le j\le k\), let \(U_j\) be the
inclusion operator

\[
 (U_jf)(T)=\sum_{\substack{A\subseteq T\\|A|=j}}f(A).          \tag{2.1}
\]

The nested inclusion spaces decompose orthogonally as

\[
 V_k=\mathcal H_{k,0}\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathcal H_{k,k},
 \qquad
 \dim\mathcal H_{k,j}=\binom nj-\binom n{j-1},                \tag{2.2}
\]

where \(\mathcal H_{k,j}\cong S^{(n-j,j)}\).  These are the usual
Johnson-scheme target harmonics.  One elementary realization is to take
functions \(f\) on the \(j\)-sets satisfying

\[
 \sum_{x\notin B}f(B\cup\{x\})=0
 \quad\text{for every }|B|=j-1,                               \tag{2.3}
\]

and apply \(U_j\).  The kernel rank in (2.3) gives the dimension in
(2.2), and the successive inclusion spaces exhaust \(V_k\).  All the
spaces are \(S_n\)-invariant.

For an arbitrary sparse frame measure, (1.7) consequently has the exact
block action

\[
 P_{\mu,k}|_{\mathcal H_{k,j}}
 \simeq \widehat\mu(n-j,j)
 :=\sum_g\mu(g)\rho_{(n-j,j)}(g).                              \tag{2.4}
\]

Thus a claimed all-harmonic gap is exactly the assertion

\[
 \sup_{1\le j\le\min(k,n-k)}
      \|\widehat\mu(n-j,j)\|_{\rm op}<1.                      \tag{2.5}
\]

There is no scalar eigenvalue formula for a noncentral catalogue; (2.4),
not a one-dimensional occupancy eigenvalue, is the correct test.

## 3. The sparse transposition heat and its exact spectrum

Let \(\mathcal C\) be the conjugacy class of transpositions and put

\[
 P_k={1\over\binom n2}\sum_{1\le a<b\le n}\rho_k((a\ b)).     \tag{3.1}
\]

This is a Cayley heat with \(\binom n2\) neighbours per frame.  Every
neighbour is obtained by a coordinate relabeling of the complete legal
factor, so every state, before averaging, remains integral.

### Theorem 3.1 (exact transposition eigenvalues)

On \(\mathcal H_{k,j}\), the operator (3.1) is multiplication by the
number in (0.2).

#### Proof

A transposition fixes a \(k\)-set when both transposed coordinates are in
it or both are outside it.  Otherwise it moves along one edge of the
Johnson graph \(J(n,k)\).  Hence

\[
 \binom n2P_k=
 \left[\binom k2+\binom{n-k}2\right]I+A_k,                    \tag{3.2}
\]

where \(A_k\) is the Johnson adjacency operator.

Take \(f\) satisfying (2.3) and put \(F=U_jf\).  For fixed \(T\), a
term \(f(A)\) with \(A\subseteq T\) persists in
\((k-j)(n-k)\) neighbours.  Terms with \(|A\cap T|=j-1\), followed by
(2.3), contribute

\[
 -(k-j+1)jF(T).
\]

Therefore

\[
 A_kF=\bigl((k-j)(n-k-j)-j\bigr)F.                             \tag{3.3}
\]

Substitution in (3.2) and elementary simplification give

\[
 \binom k2+\binom{n-k}2+(k-j)(n-k-j)-j
 =\binom n2-j(n-j+1),                                         \tag{3.4}
\]

which proves (0.2).  Complementation proves the same statement for
\(k>n/2\). \(\square\)

For \(n\ge4\) and \(1\le j\le n/2\), the quantity
\(j(n-j+1)\) is increasing.  All the eigenvalues in (0.2) are
nonnegative, and hence

\[
 \|P_kf\|_2\le\left(1-{2\over n-1}\right)\|f\|_2
 \quad(f\perp{\bf1}).                                        \tag{3.5}
\]

This proves the exact simultaneous gap (0.3).

### Corollary 3.2 (simultaneous fractional smoothing)

Let \(I\) be any collection of signed protected depths, \(|I|=L\), and
let \(c_i\ge0\) be the normalized target profiles of total mass \(W\).
Then

\[
 \sum_{i\in I}
 \left\|P_{k_i}^{\,t}c_i-{W\over N_{k_i}}{\bf1}\right\|_1
 \le
 L W^{3/2}\left(1-{2\over n-1}\right)^t.                     \tag{3.6}
\]

Indeed, \(N_{k_i}\le W\), the centered \(L^2\)-norm of each nonnegative
mass-\(W\) vector is at most \(W\), and Cauchy--Schwarz across both the
target and depth indices gives (3.6).

Consequently, if

\[
 t\ge{n-1\over2}
 \left({1\over2}\log W+\log L+\omega(1)\right),              \tag{3.7}
\]

then the left side of (3.6) is \(o(W)\).  For
\(L\le2(H+1)\), \(H=O(\sqrt m)\), this is

\[
                         t=(\tfrac14\log 2+o(1))n^2.           \tag{3.8}
\]

Thus there is no spectral obstruction to *fractional* all-depth
smoothing.  The obstruction begins exactly when one asks for one integral
state.

## 4. Global orbit heat cannot round

For a target profile \(b\), write

\[
 \Delta(b)=\sum_{T\in\Omega_k}(1-b(T))_+,
 \qquad
 E(b)=\sum_{T\in\Omega_k}
       \left(b(T)-{\sum_Ub(U)\over N_k}\right)^2.              \tag{4.1}
\]

These are, respectively, the literal uncovered-target count and the
centered collision energy.

### Theorem 4.1 (integral-orbit isometry)

For every coordinate frame \(g\),

\[
 \Delta(b_{ga,k})=\Delta(b_{a,k}),
 \qquad E(b_{ga,k})=E(b_{a,k}).                                \tag{4.2}
\]

More generally, every function of the unordered multiset of target loads
is constant on the integral frame orbit.

#### Proof

Equation (1.5) merely permutes the coordinates of the vector.  Both
expressions in (4.1), and every symmetric function of those coordinates,
are therefore unchanged. \(\square\)

For all protected depths simultaneously, define

\[
 \Delta_H(a)=\sum_{q,\epsilon}\Delta(b_{a,m+\epsilon q}),
 \qquad
 E_H(a)=\sum_{q,\epsilon}E(b_{a,m+\epsilon q}).                \tag{4.3}
\]

The same single frame acts at every depth, and (4.2) gives

\[
                         \Delta_H(ga)=\Delta_H(a),
 \qquad E_H(ga)=E_H(a).                                       \tag{4.4}
\]

On the other hand, \(P_k^tb_{a,k}\) is a convex combination of the
profiles in (4.2), and (3.6) can make that convex combination almost
constant.  Hence spectral contraction of the barycentre supplies no
integral member with smaller discrepancy.  The elementary extreme example
is the orbit of a singleton vector: its orbit average is uniform and its
transposition heat has the gap (0.3), while every integral orbit point
still has exactly one occupied coordinate.

Applied to a fixed physical matching frame, the occurrence-capacity cut in
`MATH_THEOREM_MULTISCALE_QUARTET_ATLAS_COMPONENT_TRANSPORT_DICHOTOMY_20260726.md`
gives, at \(q=A\sqrt m+o(\sqrt m)\),

\[
 {D_{m,q}\over W}\longrightarrow
 e^{-A^2}\Phi(A/2)-\Phi(-3A/2)>0.                              \tag{4.5}
\]

Every global conjugate has the same deficit by (4.2).  Thus the sparse
heat does not weaken that fixed-frame cut by even one target.  It only
hides the cut in the fractional average.

## 5. Independent owner components force a fixed target harmonic

Now take the exact moving-perfect-matching component framework.  Let
\(\mathcal A\) be the admitted physical matchings and let

\[
 G_{\mathcal A}=([n],\bigcup_{M\in\mathcal A}M)                \tag{5.1}
\]

have coordinate components \(B_1,\ldots,B_c\).  The exact common-owner
supports are

\[
 U_{\boldsymbol\kappa}=
 \{X\in\Omega_m:|X\cap B_i|=\kappa_i\ (1\le i\le c)\}.        \tag{5.2}
\]

This is the exclusion-component theorem; the recursive context array is
installed inside these supports and does not change them.

Suppose first that \(c\ge2\).  Any global coordinate relabeling which
permutes the cells of the whole partition (5.2) normalizes the block
relation

\[
 x\sim y\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 x,y\in B_i\text{ for some }i.                                 \tag{5.3}
\]

Indeed, for distinct coordinates \(x,y\), one has \(x\sim y\) exactly when
every middle owner containing exactly one of \(x,y\) remains in its same
cell of (5.2) after exchanging \(x\) and \(y\).  If \(x,y\) lie in
different blocks, a middle owner can be chosen so that this exchange
changes the two corresponding entries of \(\boldsymbol\kappa\).  Thus the
partition (5.2) intrinsically recovers (5.3).  In particular, any global
frame heat which can be applied without splitting an independent owner
component is supported in the normalizer of (5.3).

For \(2\le k\le n-2\), define \(R_k\) by (0.4).  It counts selected
coordinate pairs which belong to a common coordinate component.  It is
fixed by the full normalizer of (5.3).  It is nonconstant on \(\Omega_k\).
Here is a direct proof.  Choose two blocks.  Every component of a union of
perfect matchings has size at least two.  Since \(2\le k\le n-2\), there
is a feasible occupancy vector with donor occupancy at least two and
receiver occupancy at most its block size minus two: the allowed range of
total occupancies under these two inequalities is exactly \([2,n-2]\).
Perform two consecutive unit transfers from the donor to the receiver.  If
\(\sum_i\binom{t_i}{2}\) were constant, the first transfer would force
\(t_{\rm donor}=t_{\rm receiver}+1\), whereas the second would force
\(t_{\rm donor}-1=t_{\rm receiver}+2\), a contradiction.

Moreover,

\[
 R_k(T)=\sum_i\sum_{\{x,y\}\in\binom{B_i}{2}}
             {\bf1}_{\{x,y\}\subseteq T},                     \tag{5.4}
\]

so \(R_k\) lies in the inclusion space of degree at most two.  Let
\(\Pi_{k,j}\) be the orthogonal projection onto \(\mathcal H_{k,j}\).
Because every \(\Pi_{k,j}\) is \(S_n\)-equivariant, it commutes with the
block normalizer.  Since \(R_k\) is nonconstant, at least one of

\[
                 \Pi_{k,1}R_k,\qquad \Pi_{k,2}R_k              \tag{5.5}
\]

is nonzero; that vector is fixed by every compatible frame move.

The degree can be identified exactly.  Let
\(d_x=|B_i|-1\) for \(x\in B_i\), and let
\(e=\sum_i\binom{|B_i|}{2}\).  Conditional on \(x\in T\),

\[
 \mathbb E(R_k(T)\mid x\in T)
 =d_x{k-1\over n-1}
 +(e-d_x){(k-1)(k-2)\over(n-1)(n-2)}.              \tag{5.6}
\]

The coefficient of \(d_x\) is
\((k-1)(n-k)/((n-1)(n-2))>0\).  Therefore the degree-one
projection is nonzero exactly when the block sizes are not all equal.  If
all block sizes are equal, the degree-one projection vanishes; since
\(R_k\) is still nonconstant and has degree at most two, its degree-two
projection is nonzero.  Thus the residual eigenvalue-one mode is explicit,
not merely existential.

### Theorem 5.1 (component harmonic obstruction)

Every symmetric coordinate-frame heat which preserves the independent
owner-component system (5.2) has eigenvalue \(1\) on a nonconstant
degree-one or degree-two target harmonic, at every protected central rank.
In particular its nonconstant spectral gap is zero.

#### Proof

Take the nonzero fixed vector in (5.5) and apply (1.8). \(\square\)

The only way for a matching-frame catalogue to remove this invariant is to
add edges which merge the blocks in (5.1).  If the final union graph is
connected, the exact component theorem gives

\[
                         U_{(m)}=\Omega_m.                       \tag{5.7}
\]

There is then one global owner component and no independent componentwise
frame choice.  A catalogue consisting of coordinate conjugates is exactly
the global orbit of Section 4.  This proves the dichotomy announced in
Section 0.

## 6. Exact boundary

The following statements are now proved.

1. The target representation and the action of every sparse frame
   catalogue are exactly (2.1)--(2.5).
2. The \(\binom n2\)-regular transposition catalogue is a sparse legal
   integral-state heat with exact gap \(2/(n-1)\) on every nonconstant
   protected target harmonic.
3. It gives simultaneous \(o(W)\) *fractional* discrepancy after the
   explicit number of steps in (3.7).
4. No global orbit heat can improve any integral target deficit or
   collision energy.
5. Retaining nontrivial exact owner components forces a fixed target
   harmonic of degree at most two; removing it collapses the matching
   component system to one global component.

The companion construction
`MATH_THEOREM_STAR_MOVING_FRAME_SPECTRUM_AND_COMPONENT_GRAM_GATE_20260726.md`
reduces the move catalogue from \(\binom n2\) to the minimum possible
\(n-1\) transpositions and has gap \(1/(n-1)\).  Its exact component-Gram
identity refines Section 4: component rounding descends precisely when the
joined cross-component Gram is positive.  The block-fixed mode of Section 5
shows that this extra inequality cannot be inferred from frame spectrum.

Therefore a simultaneous integral all-\(q\) rounding theorem cannot be
deduced from spectral gap of moving coordinate frames.  The precise next
constructive statement must exhibit owner-preserving **non-relabeling**
switches \(a\leftrightarrow a'\) such that

\[
 P(a)=P(a')\quad\text{but}\quad
 E_H(a')<E_H(a)                                                  \tag{6.1}
\]

on every nonconstant residual harmonic, with the switches simultaneously
legal on a partition of owner space.  Condition (6.1) is impossible for
coordinate conjugates by Theorem 4.1 and is not supplied by the current
matching-status component atlas by Theorem 5.1.

This is a spectral obstruction to the proposed outer heat/rounding lane,
not an obstruction to every conceivable exterior-moving, non-orbit trade.
